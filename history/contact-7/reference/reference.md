# Contact and conflict: swap-in cards

These lessons use **ancient China in the Qin and Han period** as the worked example. If your class studies a different society, use its card with the same questions: **Why did it meet other societies, and why did it fight? What did those contacts lead to, for both sides? How did contact change it?**

Each card is a short summary to start from, not a full account. Check it against your class text.

## Ancient China (Qin and Han)

- **Contacts and conflicts:** the Qin wars of unification (230 to 221 BCE); the Han and the Xiongnu, northern herders on horseback (defeat at Baideng, 200 BCE; Emperor Wu's wars from 133 BCE); Zhang Qian's mission west (from 138 BCE); campaigns to Ferghana for horses (104 and 101 BCE).
- **Effects:** the heqin peace agreements (from about 198 BCE: a royal bride, gifts, rulers as equals); the Hexi Corridor, taken from 121 BCE; four commanderies set up over the following years (from about 111 BCE), and walls to Dunhuang; silk west, and horses, grapes and alfalfa east; costly wars and new taxes.
- **Contact and change:** silk reached Rome through traders in between (indirect contact); Buddhism reached China along the trade routes (first mentioned in 65 CE).
- **Sources to look for:** Sima Qian's history (on Zhang Qian and the Xiongnu); Han wooden slips from the frontier near Dunhuang. The Xiongnu left no writing of their own.

## Ancient Egypt

- **Contacts and conflicts:** in the New Kingdom, Egypt built an empire into Nubia and the Levant; it traded with Cyprus, Crete and Greece; it fought the Hittites at Kadesh (about 1274 BCE), where both kings claimed victory.
- **Effects:** about fifteen years after Kadesh, Ramesses II and the Hittite king Hattusili III agreed one of the oldest peace treaties known: no attacks, help against enemies, and sending back people who fled.
- **Sources to look for:** Ramesses II's account of Kadesh carved on temple walls; the treaty in Egyptian hieroglyphs at Karnak and on clay tablets from the Hittite capital, Hattusa.

## Ancient Greece

- **Contacts and conflicts:** colonisation from about 750 to 550 BCE: new towns around the Mediterranean and the Black Sea (historians point to little farmland, trade and quarrels at home); the Persian Wars (Marathon 490 BCE, Salamis 480 BCE), after Athens helped Greek cities rebelling against Persia around 499 BCE; the Peloponnesian War between Athens and Sparta.
- **Effects:** Greek culture spread around the Mediterranean; after the Persian Wars, Athens led the Delian League; Alexander the Great's conquests later carried Greek culture as far as India.
- **Sources to look for:** Herodotus on the Persian Wars; Thucydides on the Peloponnesian War (both Greek writers, with a Greek point of view).

## Ancient Rome

- **Contacts and conflicts:** three wars with Carthage (264 to 146 BCE), ending with Carthage destroyed; expansion around the Mediterranean; indirect trade with Han China for silk, and sea trade with India.
- **Effects and change:** Rome became the strongest power in the western Mediterranean; Romans took in gods from other lands, such as Isis (Egypt) and Mithras (linked with Persia); Roman coins found in southern India show trade there.
- **Sources to look for:** Polybius and Livy on the wars with Carthage; Pliny the Elder on spending on Eastern luxuries; Florus on envoys to Augustus (one writer's claim that historians treat carefully).

## Ancient India

- **Contacts and conflicts:** Persian rule in the north-west under the Achaemenid kings; Alexander the Great's campaign into the Indus region (326 BCE); the Mauryan Empire; Ashoka's conquest of Kalinga.
- **Effects and change:** after Kalinga, Ashoka turned to Buddhism and spread its teachings through edicts carved on rocks and pillars; according to a later Sri Lankan chronicle, he sent his son and daughter to Sri Lanka; trade by sea with the Roman world.
- **Sources to look for:** the Pillars and Rock Edicts of Ashoka; Roman coins and pottery found at Indian ports; Greek writers such as Megasthenes (preserved by later writers).
