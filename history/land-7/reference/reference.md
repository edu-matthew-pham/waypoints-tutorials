# Shaped by the land: swap-in cards

These lessons use **ancient China in the Qin and Han period** as the worked example. If your class studies a different society, use its card below with the same three questions: **What was the land like? What did it make possible? What did it make hard, and what did people do about it?**

Each card is a short summary to start from, not a full account. Check it against your class text, and use your teacher's sources.

## Ancient China (Qin and Han)

- **Land:** the Yellow River and the soft loess soil of the north; the Wei River valley, ringed by mountains, as the heartland; the warmer, wetter Yangtze in the south; desert and steppe to the north and north-west; the Tibetan Plateau to the west.
- **Made possible:** millet farming in the north and rice in the south; a surplus that fed officials, soldiers and workers; protection for the heartland.
- **Made hard:** floods along the Yellow River; slow travel west, squeezed through the Hexi Corridor.
- **What people did:** dykes, water works such as Dujiangyan (about 256 BCE), canals such as the Lingqu (214 BCE), walls in the north.
- **Short timeline:** millet farming along the middle Yellow River, about 7,800 years ago · Qin unites China, 221 BCE · Han dynasty, 206 BCE to 220 CE · Zhang Qian's mission west, 2nd century BCE.
- **Sources to look for:** UNESCO's pages on Dujiangyan and the Lingqu Canal; Qin bronze weights and measures; Sima Qian's history (written in the Han period).

## Ancient Egypt

- **Land:** the Nile running north through desert to the sea; a narrow strip of farmland on each bank; the delta in the north; rocky rapids (cataracts) in the south.
- **Made possible:** the yearly flood (the inundation) left water and fresh soil for crops; boats carried people and goods up and down the river; the desert on either side helped protect the valley.
- **Made hard:** a low flood meant poor harvests, and a very high one destroyed fields and villages; the cataracts blocked boats in the south.
- **What people did:** canals and basins to hold flood water; measuring the flood's height; forts near the cataracts.
- **Short timeline:** Egypt united under one ruler, about 3100 BCE · pyramids at Giza, about 2600–2500 BCE · New Kingdom, about 1550–1070 BCE.
- **Sources to look for:** surviving nilometers (structures used to measure the flood); tomb paintings of farming; the Greek writer Herodotus, who described Egypt as a gift of the river.

## Ancient Greece

- **Land:** mountains covering most of the land; small valleys and plains near the coast; many islands; a long, broken coastline.
- **Made possible:** the sea for fishing, trade and travel between communities; olives and grapes on hillsides.
- **Made hard:** little flat land for grain; travel over land between valleys was slow, so communities grew up separately as city-states.
- **What people did:** traded by sea for grain; set up new settlements (colonies) around the Mediterranean and Black Sea.
- **Short timeline:** city-states grow, about 800 BCE onwards · Athens and Sparta at their height, 5th century BCE.
- **Sources to look for:** pottery and coins found far from Greece; Thucydides' history (5th century BCE).

## Ancient Rome

- **Land:** the Tiber River and its seven hills near the coast; the fertile Po valley in the north of Italy; the Apennine mountains down the middle.
- **Made possible:** hills that were easier to defend; the river for trade and transport; fertile plains that grew a surplus.
- **Made hard:** marshy, flood-prone ground near the Tiber; clean water for a big city.
- **What people did:** drained land; built aqueducts to bring water from the hills (the first, the Aqua Appia, in 312 BCE); built roads across Italy.
- **Short timeline:** Rome's legendary founding, 753 BCE (a story, not a dated record) · Republic, about 509 BCE · first aqueduct, 312 BCE · Empire, 27 BCE.
- **Sources to look for:** surviving aqueducts and roads; Frontinus' book on Rome's water supply (about 97 CE).

## Ancient India

- **Land:** the Himalayas along the north; the plains of the Indus and Ganges rivers; monsoon rains in summer.
- **Made possible:** river plains and monsoon rain for farming; rivers for transport; the mountains as a barrier in the north.
- **Made hard:** a weak or late monsoon could ruin harvests; the Himalayas made travel from the north very hard, so contact came through north-western passes and by sea.
- **What people did:** stored water and grain; traded by sea and through the north-west.
- **Short timeline:** Indus valley cities such as Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, about 2600–1900 BCE · Mauryan empire, about 321–185 BCE.
- **Sources to look for:** the excavated Indus cities, with their planned streets, wells and drains; Greek accounts of India (such as Megasthenes, preserved by later writers).
