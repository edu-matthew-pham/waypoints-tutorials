Applying phonic knowledge in reading

AC9E1LY04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can decode unfamiliar words using developing phonic knowledge as a primary word-identification strategy.

Success criteria

  • Student attempts to decode an unfamiliar word using phonics before guessing or skipping
  • Student correctly blends phonemes in single-syllable decodable words

Prior knowledge

Students can read decodable and authentic texts using developing phonic knowledge and monitor meaning at Foundation level (AC9EFLY04). They have not yet developed consistent phrasing and fluency, or systematically used context and grammatical knowledge as monitoring strategies alongside phonics.

Width

Minimum
Use phonic knowledge to attempt to decode an unfamiliar word in a reading context.
Typical
Consistently apply developing phonic knowledge — blending, digraphs, common patterns — to decode unfamiliar words encountered in decodable and authentic texts.
Wider
  • Apply phonic knowledge to multi-syllable words, breaking them into decodable parts
  • Discuss what strategies help when phonics alone doesn't fully solve a word

Hinge

Without fluent phonic decoding, cognitive resources cannot be directed toward comprehension — students who remain word-by-word decoders cannot develop the phrasing and fluency that N2 requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPhonic decoding must become fluent and automatic before reading resources can be directed to comprehension. Repeated reading of decodable texts builds the automaticity required.
