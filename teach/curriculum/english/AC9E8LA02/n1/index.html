← AC9E8LA02 Layers of meaning through literary devices

Devices that separate surface and implied meaning

AC9E8LA02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify rhetorical devices (irony, hyperbole, parody, understatement) and explain that they create a gap between what is literally said and what is meant.

Lessons on this site

  • Layers of meaning

Success criteria

  • Names a rhetorical device and identifies where it appears in a specific text.
  • Explains the difference between the literal statement and the implied or intended meaning created by the device.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify evaluative language that expresses opinions and judgements, and understand how word choice signals attitude (AC9E7LA02). They have not yet examined how rhetorical devices such as irony, hyperbole, parody and understatement create layered meaning that separates surface statement from implied meaning.

Width

Minimum
Identify one rhetorical device (e.g. irony or hyperbole) in a provided text and explain that the surface statement and the implied meaning are different.
Typical
Identify and explain how irony, hyperbole, parody and understatement each create a gap between literal statement and implied meaning, drawing on examples from a range of texts including speeches, opinion pieces or humorous writing.
Wider
  • Compare how two different devices (e.g. understatement vs hyperbole) achieve contrasting evaluative effects on the same subject in two different texts
  • Investigate how political cartoons or satirical writing use parody and irony simultaneously to layer multiple evaluative meanings
  • Examine how First Nations or non-Western rhetorical traditions use indirect or figurative expression to convey serious social or political commentary

Hinge

Students who conflate irony with sarcasm or cannot identify understatement arrive at Node 2 unable to explain how devices layer meaning — you cannot analyse the layering of a device you cannot reliably identify. Systematic device misidentification makes Node 2's integration task incoherent.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often conflate irony with sarcasm, or miss understatement as a device. Encountering these devices across varied text types (humour, political commentary, literature) resolves device-specific misconceptions through broader exposure.
