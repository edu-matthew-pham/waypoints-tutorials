Creating with vocabulary and sentences

AC9E3LY06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 3 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create texts using topic-specific vocabulary and appropriate simple and compound sentences to express and combine ideas.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student uses at least one topic-specific vocabulary item in their text
  • Student uses both simple and compound sentences — not only one type

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning and structuring.

Width

Minimum
Create a text that includes at least two topic-specific vocabulary items and at least one compound sentence, explaining the purpose of each choice.
Typical
Use appropriate simple and compound sentences to express and combine ideas; use vocabulary including topic-specific vocabulary relevant to the text type and purpose; select vocabulary deliberately to suit the topic.
Wider
  • Compare vocabulary choices in a first draft with a revised draft and discuss how the vocabulary changes improved the text
  • Experiment with improving a generic sentence by replacing vague vocabulary with precise or topic-specific words, and evaluate the effect

Hinge

Students who have not created a text with deliberate vocabulary and sentence choices have no substantive draft to bring to N3 editing — N3 requires a composed text as its input.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCreating a text with deliberate vocabulary and sentence choices requires students to coordinate structural planning, grammatical knowledge (sentences, clauses) and vocabulary choice simultaneously. This is the composing integration that distinguishes planned, purposeful writing.
