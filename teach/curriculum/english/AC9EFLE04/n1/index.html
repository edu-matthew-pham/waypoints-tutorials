← AC9EFLE04 Explore rhythms and sound patterns

Poems and songs have rhythm

AC9EFLE04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that a poem, rhyme or song has a rhythmic beat — a repeating pattern of strong and weak sounds.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can clap or tap the beat of a familiar rhyme without losing the pattern.
  • Student can identify that a poem or song has a rhythm.

Prior knowledge

Students have heard and participated in rhymes, songs and chants in home and early childhood settings. They have not yet been asked to consciously attend to, describe or replicate the rhythmic and sound patterns that structure these texts.

Width

Minimum
Clap or tap the beat of one familiar rhyme or song.
Typical
Recognise that poems, rhymes and songs have rhythm; clap, tap or move to the beat of multiple rhymes and songs; identify rhythm as a pattern that repeats.
Wider
  • Compare the rhythms of different poem types — a nursery rhyme, a marching chant, a lullaby — and discuss how the rhythm feels different
  • Explore rhythms from First Nations Australian songs or chants and compare the rhythmic patterns with familiar English nursery rhymes

Hinge

Without recognising that rhythmic beat is a pattern, students cannot identify rhyming and sound patterns in Node 2, nor replicate them in Node 3 — replication requires holding the pattern in mind, which is impossible if the pattern has not yet been consciously perceived.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsRhythmic beat recognition must become automatic through repeated clapping and moving to diverse rhymes — students need to internalise the pattern before they can transfer it to identifying and replicating sound patterns in subsequent nodes.
