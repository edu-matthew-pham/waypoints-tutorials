← AC9EFLE04 Explore rhythms and sound patterns

Rhyme and sound patterns repeat

AC9EFLE04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Foundation English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify rhyming patterns and other sound patterns (e.g. alliteration) in poems and songs.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify rhyming words in a poem or song.
  • Student can describe the sound pattern they hear — where it repeats or changes.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Poems and songs have rhythm.

Width

Minimum
Identify the rhyming words at the end of two lines in a familiar poem or song.
Typical
Identify rhyme patterns in poems and songs; recognise that sound patterns (rhyme, repetition of sounds) contribute to the text's effect; explore poems with both rhyme and alliteration.
Wider
  • Compare how rhyme patterns differ across several poems — some rhyme every line, some alternate, some don't rhyme — and discuss the effect
  • Create a short class rhyme or chant using a familiar sound pattern as a model
  • Explore sound patterns in First Nations Australian poetry and songs — attending to patterns that may be different from typical English rhyme schemes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents may conflate 'poem' with 'rhyme' — the misconception that poems must rhyme. Multiple examples including non-rhyming poems and First Nations patterns that differ from English rhyme schemes are needed.
