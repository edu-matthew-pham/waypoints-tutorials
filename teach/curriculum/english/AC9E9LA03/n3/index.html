Subversion across modes

AC9E9LA03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine how authors subvert conventions by experimenting with the combination of spoken, written, visual and multimodal elements.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies which conventions are being subverted and in which mode(s)
  • Student explains the effect of the subversive choice on the text's meaning or audience experience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Purpose and effect of structural adaptation.

Width

Minimum
Examine a multimodal text and identify how it subverts or experiments with the combination of at least two modes (e.g. spoken and visual, written and image), explaining the effect.
Typical
Analyse how structural subversion operates across spoken, written, visual and multimodal elements in a range of texts, including how the combination itself becomes the subversive device.
Wider
  • Analyse how spoken word poetry or performance poetry subverts the conventions of both written poetry and spoken language simultaneously
  • Examine how digital storytelling experiments with navigation and reader agency as a structural subversion of linear narrative

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierExamining subversion across modes requires students to coordinate understanding of conventions in multiple modes simultaneously — written, spoken, visual — and recognise that the combination itself can be the subversive move. This integration is harder than analysing each mode separately.
