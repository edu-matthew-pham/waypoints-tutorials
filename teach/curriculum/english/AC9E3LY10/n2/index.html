← AC9E3LY10 Base words, prefixes and suffixes

Suffix generalisations

AC9E3LY10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply generalisations for adding a suffix to a base word (doubling, dropping 'e') to form plurals and past tense correctly.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly applies the doubling or 'drop e' rule when adding a suffix
  • Student can state the generalisation they applied

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Prefixes changing base word meaning.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two suffix generalisations correctly (e.g. double the final consonant before '-ing'/'-ed' for CVC words; drop the 'e' before a vowel suffix) and explain the rule applied.
Typical
Use generalisations for adding a suffix to form plurals and past tense — including doubling the final consonant and dropping the 'e'; apply to form and read multimorphemic words.
Wider
  • Find exceptions to the doubling rule and discuss why the exceptions exist — exploring what patterns predict when doubling applies
  • Compare suffix generalisations in English with known patterns in another language if applicable — discussing similarities and differences

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSuffix generalisations are precision spelling rules that require repeated application in writing before they become automatic. Students know the rules but apply them inconsistently — practice across multiple words is the remedy.
