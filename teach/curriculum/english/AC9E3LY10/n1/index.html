← AC9E3LY10 Base words, prefixes and suffixes

Prefixes changing base word meaning

AC9E3LY10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use knowledge of prefixes to change the meaning of a base word and read new multimorphemic words.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies the base word and prefix in a multimorphemic word
  • Student explains how the prefix changes the meaning of the base word

Prior knowledge

Students build morphemic word families using prefixes and suffixes (AC9E2LY12). They have not yet applied generalisations for adding suffixes to base words (e.g. doubling, dropping 'e').

Width

Minimum
Identify the base word and prefix in at least three multimorphemic words, explain how the prefix changes the meaning, and read the words correctly.
Typical
Use knowledge of prefixes to change the meaning of base words; read new multimorphemic words by recognising base + prefix structure.
Wider
  • Collect words with the same prefix (e.g. 'un-') and generalise about what the prefix consistently does to meaning
  • Explore how knowing one prefix helps decode a whole family of unfamiliar words encountered in reading

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a repertoire of common prefixes and their meanings to use morphological knowledge productively as a decoding strategy. Range of examples matters more than depth on a single prefix.
