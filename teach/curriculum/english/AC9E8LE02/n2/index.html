Evaluating opinions about style

AC9E8LE02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare and evaluate different opinions about the stylistic features of literary texts, including opinions that challenge or support their own view.

Success criteria

  • Compares at least two different opinions about a text's style, identifying the evidence each opinion relies on.
  • Evaluates which opinion is better supported, with reasoning connected to specific language features, devices or structures.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Style as the sum of language choices.

Width

Minimum
Compare two opinions about the style of a literary text (e.g. from two reviews or class discussion) and evaluate which is better supported with reference to language features, devices or structure.
Typical
Compare and evaluate a range of opinions about the stylistic features of literary texts, including opinions about characterisation, setting and plot; share and refine own opinions in response to opinions that challenge them.
Wider
  • Research critical responses to a literary text and evaluate how different critics have interpreted the same stylistic features differently, examining what assumptions underlie each reading
  • Engage in structured academic discussion or debate where opinions about literary style are challenged and defended with reference to textual evidence
  • Reflect on how one's own cultural background or experience shapes the stylistic features one notices or values in a text

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyEvaluating opinions about literary style requires a repertoire of critical perspectives and metalanguage. Encountering multiple critical viewpoints — including ones that prioritise different stylistic features — builds the evaluative range needed to hold and defend a position.
