← AC9E6LY02 Interaction skills and formality

Developing and evaluating arguments in interaction

AC9E6LY02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop and support arguments and share and evaluate information, experiences and opinions through spoken interaction.

Success criteria

  • States an argument and supports it with at least one reason or piece of evidence
  • Evaluates at least one argument or opinion from another speaker, explaining whether and why they agree or disagree

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Questioning and clarifying strategies.

Width

Minimum
Develop at least one argument orally and support it with a reason or evidence; evaluate one argument or opinion offered by another speaker.
Typical
Develop, support and extend arguments in structured spoken contexts (discussions, debates); share and evaluate information, experiences and opinions using appropriate evidence and reasoning; interrogate ideas offered by others with relevant questions.
Wider
  • Lead a structured group discussion on a topic, ensuring all participants contribute and arguments are developed and evaluated
  • Participate in a formal debate and reflect on how the structured format shaped argument development compared with informal discussion

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDeveloping arguments in real-time spoken interaction requires integrating formality awareness (Node 1), questioning strategies (Node 2), and substantive content reasoning simultaneously — the coordination challenge is the learning.
