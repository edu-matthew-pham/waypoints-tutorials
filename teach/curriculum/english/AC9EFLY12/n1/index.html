← AC9EFLY12 Write and read CVC words

Sounds can be written as letters

AC9EFLY12 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can represent each phoneme in a CVC word with the correct letter when writing.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly writes at least five CVC words, including words with different vowels.
  • Student represents each phoneme with the correct letter — not just onset and final consonant.

Prior knowledge

Students can name letters, produce letter sounds (AC9EFLY11), and segment and blend phonemes (AC9EFLY10). They have not yet been asked to connect phoneme-grapheme knowledge in both directions: encoding (writing) and decoding (reading) CVC words.

Width

Minimum
Write at least five CVC words correctly from dictation, representing each phoneme with the appropriate letter.
Typical
Write CVC words across all short vowel sounds by representing each phoneme with the appropriate letter; spell common CVC patterns reliably.
Wider
  • Write CVC words across all five short vowel sounds — ensuring the vowel discrimination is secure
  • Write CVC words in the context of real writing tasks — labelling drawings, completing simple sentences
  • Sort CVC words by vowel sound — building awareness of short vowel patterns

Hinge

CVC encoding is the prerequisite for the spelling strategy in LY13 — students who cannot reliably encode CVC words cannot extend phoneme-grapheme correspondence to unknown words. Encoding errors also propagate directly into the writing tasks in LY06.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsCVC encoding must become reliable and accurate — encoding errors cascade directly into unintelligible writing. Practice across all short vowels is required before accuracy is secure.
