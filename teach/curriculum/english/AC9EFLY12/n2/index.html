← AC9EFLY12 Write and read CVC words

Letters can be blended into words

AC9EFLY12 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read CVC words by blending the sounds associated with the letters left to right.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student reads at least five CVC words by blending, including unfamiliar words.
  • Student reads CVC words within a short decodable text rather than only in isolation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sounds can be written as letters.

Width

Minimum
Read at least five CVC words by blending letter sounds.
Typical
Blend the sounds of CVC words when reading; apply blending to new CVC words not previously seen; read CVC words in the context of simple texts.
Wider
  • Read CVC words in connected text — applying blending in the context of meaning-making
  • Read CVC words across a range of vowel patterns — not limited to familiar words
  • Identify CVC words in a familiar text and read them aloud — connecting letter-sound knowledge to reading fluency

Hinge

CVC decoding is the prerequisite for reading connected text in LY04 — students who cannot blend CVC words cannot apply phonic decoding to unknown words in authentic texts, and their reading remains dependent on memorisation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsBlending at the word level must become fluent — slow, halting blending interferes with comprehension. Accuracy first, then fluency across multiple CVC words.
