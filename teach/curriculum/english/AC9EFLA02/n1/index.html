← AC9EFLA02 Language to express preferences

Preferences can be put into words

AC9EFLA02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can express a like, dislike or preference using words, and recognise that language is one way to communicate feelings.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student uses at least one specific word or phrase to express a preference beyond 'like' or 'don't like'.
  • Student can identify that language was used to express a feeling or preference.

Prior knowledge

Students bring natural oral language for expressing feelings. They have not yet been asked to consciously identify or compare the specific language choices used to express preferences, or to recognise how these are conveyed in visual and media texts.

Width

Minimum
Express one like or dislike using a word or phrase (e.g. 'I love...', 'I don't like...').
Typical
Express preferences, likes and dislikes in words; explore different word choices for the same feeling (e.g. 'like', 'love', 'enjoy', 'prefer').
Wider
  • Collect and compare words for positive and negative preferences from classroom picture books
  • Explore vocabulary for feelings and preferences in languages other than English known to students in the class

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a range of preference language beyond the immediate 'like/don't like' binary. A vocabulary repertoire is what this node is building.
