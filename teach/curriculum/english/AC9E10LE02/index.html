Extending others' interpretations of literature

AC9E10LE02 · Year 10 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

reflect on and extend others' interpretations of and responses to literature

Prior knowledge

Students present personal responses comparing initial impressions with subsequent analysis (AC9E9LE02). They have not yet reflected on and extended others' interpretations of literature.

By the end

Students reflect on and extend others' interpretations of and responses to literature.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • debating whether a text possesses universal qualities and retains relevance
  • presenting arguments based on close textual analysis to support an interpretation of a text; for example, writing an essay or creating a set of director’s notes
  • creating personal reading lists in a variety of genres and explaining why texts qualify for inclusion on a particular list

Waypoints

  • 1. Engaging with others' interpretationsHingeStudents can identify and reflect on how others have interpreted a literary text, comparing another's interpretation with their own.
  • 2. Extending interpretations through close textual analysisStudents can extend an interpretation of a text by presenting arguments based on close textual analysis, going beyond initial responses.
