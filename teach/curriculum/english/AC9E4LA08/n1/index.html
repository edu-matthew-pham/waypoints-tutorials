← AC9E4LA08 Adverb and prepositional phrases

Circumstantial details — what, when, where, how

AC9E4LA08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 4 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that sentences can include additional information about the circumstances surrounding an activity — when it happened, where, how and why.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least three types of circumstantial detail in a sentence and name the question each answers (when? where? how?)
  • Can explain that circumstantial phrases add detail to the main activity without changing what the activity is

Prior knowledge

Students understand how different verb types and tense work in clauses (AC9E3LA07, AC9E3LA08). They have not yet examined how adverb groups/phrases and prepositional phrases provide circumstantial details of time, place, manner and cause around activities.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two different types of circumstantial detail in a provided sentence (e.g. time and place) and name what question each answers.
Typical
Identify all four types of circumstantial detail — time, place, manner, cause — across a range of sentences and explain how each adds precision to the description of an activity.
Wider
  • Compare how circumstantial detail is used differently in narrative (for atmosphere and pace) versus scientific description (for precision and replicability)
  • Examine how removing circumstantial phrases changes the meaning and specificity of a sentence across different text types

Hinge

Identifying adverb and prepositional phrases in N2 and N3 requires students to know what type of circumstantial detail they are providing — without N1's time/place/manner/cause framework, classification in N2 and N3 is impossible.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCircumstantial detail types appear across narrative and factual texts with different distributions — multiple contexts help students see how each type functions differently across purposes, before they encounter them as adverb and prepositional phrases.
