← AC9E3LY01 Purpose and audience vary texts

Audience shapes text choices

AC9E3LY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Year 3 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how texts with the same purpose are created differently for different audiences.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least two specific differences between two texts with the same purpose
  • Student connects each difference to the audience — explaining why the audience requires that particular choice

Prior knowledge

Students identify how similar topics and information are presented differently across text types (AC9E2LY01). They have not yet examined how the same purpose can produce different texts when the audience changes.

Width

Minimum
Compare two texts with the same purpose but different audiences (e.g. a safety campaign for adults vs children) and identify at least two ways the texts differ because of the audience.
Typical
Identify how the same purpose (e.g. safety, instruction, persuasion) produces different texts for different audiences — examining vocabulary, images, examples and complexity as audience-driven choices.
Wider
  • Compare instructions for assembling the same item written for different age groups, and discuss what specific changes are made to suit each audience
  • Find examples of similar-purpose texts aimed at children versus adults and evaluate whether the audience-related choices are effective

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often see text differences as arbitrary or about topic rather than audience. Comparing texts with the same purpose but different audiences — across multiple examples — builds the insight that audience is a systematic determinant of textual choices.
