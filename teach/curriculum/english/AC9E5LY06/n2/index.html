Developing ideas through language choices

AC9E5LY06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop ideas in their writing using text connectives, expanded noun groups, and specialist vocabulary, making deliberate choices about how these tools contribute to the text's development.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Uses text connectives to signal the logical relationship between ideas — not just as sentence openers.
  • Uses at least one expanded noun group and one specialist vocabulary choice and can explain the function of each in context.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning for purpose and structure.

Width

Minimum
In a draft text, identify and explain at least two deliberate language choices — text connectives, expanded noun groups, or specialist vocabulary — and explain how each develops an idea or serves the text's purpose.
Typical
Draft a text that demonstrates deliberate use of text connectives (to signal relationship and sequence), expanded noun groups (for precision and description), and specialist vocabulary (for technical accuracy and register). Explain the function of each type of language choice in developing the text's ideas.
Wider
  • Examine how text connective choices differ across text type purposes — comparing how connectives in narrative (sequence, time), argument (concession, causation), and explanation (cause, condition) serve different ideational purposes
  • Apply specialist and technical vocabulary appropriate to a specific learning area in an informative text, and compare the precision achieved with and without specialist terms

Hinge

Node 4 requires students to edit for meaning, structure, and accuracy — including reviewing their connective choices, noun groups, and vocabulary for precision. Students who have not yet made deliberate language choices in drafting have nothing substantive to edit at the craft level; their editing will default to surface-level fixes rather than the meaning-development work Node 4 demands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often use text connectives as sentence-starters rather than as tools that signal the logical relationship between ideas. Encountering connective choices across argument, explanation, narrative, and informative writing contexts helps students see that connective selection is a meaning choice, not a formula.
