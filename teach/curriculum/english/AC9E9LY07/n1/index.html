← AC9E9LY07 Plan and deliver analytical presentations

Planning for spoken analytical and critical purposes

AC9E9LY07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a spoken presentation with a clear structure that develops and expands ideas analytically or critically for a specific audience.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a plan with a clear purpose and structure suited to the spoken form
  • Student can explain at least one planning decision in terms of what the audience needs

Prior knowledge

Students plan, create and deliver presentations selecting structures, devices and voice features for purpose and audience (AC9E7LY07). They have not yet developed analytical and critical spoken modes, or deliberately coordinated literary devices, language features and voice features simultaneously.

Width

Minimum
Plan a spoken presentation with a clear purpose, a structure that develops ideas, and deliberate selection of at least one text structure suited to the purpose.
Typical
Plan a spoken presentation that organises, expands and develops ideas — selecting text structures and adapting literary devices to meet the needs of the audience when debating, presenting a seminar or creating a voiceover.
Wider
  • Compare planning strategies for a formal seminar versus a debate — identifying what structural choices differ and why the audience and form demand different organisation
  • Plan a collaborative imaginative recreation of a text or key idea, identifying how collaboration requires explicit structural negotiation

Hinge

Students who cannot plan a spoken text for audience cannot meaningfully rehearse voice features at N2 — without a planned structure there is nothing to rehearse against, and delivery at N3 is unfocused.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPlanning analytical spoken texts requires students to reason about how ideas should be organised for an audience that cannot re-read. The reasoning scaffold — what the audience needs to hear first, how to signal transitions, how to use voice to mark structure — differs from written planning and needs explicit modelling.
