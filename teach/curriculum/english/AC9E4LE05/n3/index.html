← AC9E4LE05 Create and edit literary texts

Editing — peer feedback and revision

AC9E4LE05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 4 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can give and receive specific feedback about choices in storyline, character and setting development, and revise their text in response to feedback.

Success criteria

  • Can give one specific piece of feedback about a storyline, character or setting choice in a peer's text
  • Has made at least one revision to own text in response to feedback received

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Creating with craft — sentences, dialogue and literary devices.

Width

Minimum
Share a literary text with a peer, give one piece of specific feedback about a storyline, character or setting choice, and make at least one revision to their own text based on feedback received.
Typical
Engage in structured peer editing focused on storyline, character and setting choices — identifying what is working, what could be developed, and giving specific, text-referenced feedback — and revise the text in response.
Wider
  • Compare a draft and revised version of a text and explain what changed and why the revision improves the text
  • Use a published author's editing process (e.g. from interviews or manuscript facsimiles) as a model for own revision approach

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEffective peer editing requires a structured framework — students without prompts default to 'it was good'. Explicit criteria for storyline, character and setting feedback provide the scaffold that makes editing generative.
