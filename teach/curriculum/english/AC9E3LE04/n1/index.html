Imagery and its effects

AC9E3LE04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify imagery (visual, nature, sensory) in a text and discuss the effect it creates for the reader's imagination.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies imagery in a text (naming what is being described)
  • Student discusses the effect on the reader — what the imagery makes them imagine, see or feel

Prior knowledge

Students identify, reproduce and experiment with rhythmic sound and word patterns (AC9E2LE04). They have not yet discussed the effects of named literary devices — particularly imagery and sound devices like onomatopoeia — on meaning and the reader's experience.

Width

Minimum
Identify one piece of imagery in a text and discuss what picture or sensation it creates for the reader.
Typical
Discuss the effects of imagery in texts — including imagery related to nature, senses and familiar experiences — explaining what the imagery makes readers see, feel or imagine.
Wider
  • Compare imagery used to describe the same natural phenomenon (e.g. a storm, a river) in two different texts and discuss how different choices create different effects
  • Explore how imagery in a First Nations Australian text conveys a relationship with Country/Place that goes beyond description
