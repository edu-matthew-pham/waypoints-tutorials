Storylines across contexts

AC9E4LE01 · Year 4 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise similar storylines, ideas and relationships in different contexts in literary texts by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors

Prior knowledge

Students discuss characters, events and settings across different cultural and contextual contexts in literature (AC9E3LE01). They have not yet recognised similar storylines, ideas and relationships across First Nations Australian, Australian and world literary texts.

By the end

Students can identify common storylines, themes and relationship patterns across literary texts from different cultural contexts — including First Nations Australian, Australian and world authors — and explain how context shapes the way these universal patterns are expressed.

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying recurring storylinesStudents can identify a recurring storyline pattern (e.g. journey, transformation, conflict and resolution) across two or more texts from different contexts.
  • 2. Themes and connections to experienceStudents can identify the theme of a literary text and connect it to their own experience, other texts and the wider world.
  • 3. Characters, settings and cultural contextStudents can explain how cultural context shapes the way characters, settings and relationships are portrayed in a text, even when the underlying storyline is familiar.
