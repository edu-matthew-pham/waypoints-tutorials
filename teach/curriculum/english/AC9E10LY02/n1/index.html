Analysing spoken text structures and features

AC9E10LY02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 10 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain the purposes and effects of text structures and language features in spoken and multimodal texts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one text structure feature and one language feature in a spoken text
  • Explains the effect of each feature on the audience

Prior knowledge

Students analyse how language positions listeners and use interaction skills to present and discuss opinions about spoken texts (AC9E9LY02). They have not yet explained the purposes and effects of text structures and language features in spoken texts, using interaction skills to present a developed opinion.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one text structure feature and one language feature in a spoken text and explain their effect.
Typical
Analyse the spoken and multimodal features of a media text (e.g. documentary, podcast, political speech), explaining how text structure and language features create effects such as mood, authority or persuasion.
Wider
  • Compare spoken text features across different genres (interview, documentary, political speech) and analyse how genre shapes structure and language choices
  • Examine how spoken features of First Nations Australian storytelling or oratory function differently from Western speech conventions
  • Analyse how multimodal features (combination of words and sound) in a media text create effects that neither mode achieves alone

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to present an opinion about a spoken text grounded in analysis of its features — without feature analysis, opinions are disconnected from textual evidence and the interaction skill becomes mere assertion.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSpoken text features vary substantially across genres — students who have only analysed one genre may not recognise structural and language features in unfamiliar spoken text types; genre breadth builds reliable identification.
