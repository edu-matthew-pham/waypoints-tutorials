Vowel digraphs

AC9E2LY10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 2 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read and write words containing vowel digraphs — including common and less common patterns — applying correct sound-letter matches.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly reads words containing vowel digraphs without sounding out individual letters.
  • Student correctly writes words with common vowel digraphs.

Prior knowledge

Students use short and long vowels, blends and digraphs to write words and read one- and two-syllable words (AC9E1LY11). They have not yet applied less common long vowel patterns, silent letters or multi-syllabic and compound word decoding.

Width

Minimum
Read and write words with common vowel digraphs ('ee', 'oo', 'ai', 'ay', 'ea') correctly.
Typical
Apply vowel digraph knowledge across common and less common patterns in reading and writing, including words encountered in shared texts and learning area vocabulary.
Wider
  • Compare vowel digraphs with the same sound but different spelling (e.g. 'ee' and 'ea' for the same vowel sound) and discuss how context helps choose the correct spelling
  • Identify vowel digraphs in content-area vocabulary (Science, HASS) and practise reading and writing them in context

Hinge

Vowel digraph automaticity is prerequisite for Node 3 multi-syllabic decoding — students who cannot reliably apply digraph patterns to individual syllables cannot decode multi-syllabic words where digraphs appear within a syllable.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsVowel digraph recognition and production requires repeated application across many words before it becomes automatic. Students who understand the concept still misread or misspell digraphs in new words. Practice volume builds automaticity more than breadth of pattern types at this stage.
