Identifying homophone pairs

AC9E4LY11 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify homophone pairs and distinguish the meaning of each word in the pair.

Success criteria

  • Can name at least three homophone pairs
  • Can explain the different meaning of each word in at least two pairs

Prior knowledge

Students recognise and write most high-frequency words including some homophones (AC9E3LY12). They have not yet applied contextual and syntactical clues systematically to determine correct homophone spelling.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three homophone pairs and give the meaning of each word.
Typical
Identify a range of common homophone pairs (our/hour, their/there/they're, to/too/two, hear/here, wear/ware/where) and explain the distinct meaning of each, noting part of speech where relevant.
Wider
  • Collect homophones encountered in reading across a week and build a personal homophone reference list with meanings and example sentences
  • Investigate near-homophones and confusables (e.g. affect/effect, then/than) and develop memory strategies for distinguishing them

Hinge

Using context to select the correct homophone in N2 requires students to know both words in the pair — without knowing the alternatives, students cannot apply the meaning-check that N2 requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsKnowing homophone pairs is a memory task that requires repeated encounter to become reliable — students need to recognise both words in a pair before context checking is possible.
