Evaluating language features for purpose and audience

AC9E8LY03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse language feature choices in a text and evaluate whether those choices are effective for the text's purpose and audience — forming and justifying a view.

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific language features in a text and analyses their relationship to the text's purpose and audience.
  • Evaluates whether the language choices are effective, providing reasoned justification with reference to the text.

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse how language features are shaped by purpose and audience, and identify how authors use supporting evidence in texts (AC9E7LY03). They have not yet evaluated the effectiveness of language feature choices or the use of sources and quotations — moving from analysis to evaluative judgement.

Width

Minimum
Analyse two language features in a text and evaluate whether they are effective for the text's stated purpose and audience, giving reasons for the evaluation.
Typical
Analyse and evaluate how language features — including vocabulary, tone, sentence structure and register — vary according to purpose and audience; evaluate an author's language choices and present a reasoned opinion about their effectiveness.
Wider
  • Compare how two different authors addressing the same audience for the same purpose use language features differently, evaluating which approach is more effective and why
  • Evaluate how language feature choices in a First Nations or culturally specific text are calibrated to their intended community audience — what would be gained or lost for a different audience
  • Analyse how a text changes when its intended audience changes — taking a formal report and rewriting it for a different audience — and evaluate which version is more effective for each audience

Hinge

Node 2 applies the same evaluative move to sources and quotations. The criteria-application scaffold — 'effective for what purpose, for which audience, with what reasoning?' — built at Node 1 is required for Node 2 to produce genuine evaluation rather than impressionistic reaction to source presence or absence.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluation requires students to apply criteria (effectiveness for purpose and audience) rather than simply describe what they observe. The evaluative move — 'and this is effective/not effective because' — needs to be explicitly scaffolded and practised.
