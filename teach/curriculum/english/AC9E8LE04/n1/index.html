Identifying literary intertextuality

AC9E8LE04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify an intertextual reference (allusion or quotation) in a literary text and explain what source it draws on.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific intertextual reference in a literary text.
  • Names the source text the reference draws on and explains what prior knowledge is needed to recognise it.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify connections between texts and discuss how one text draws on or responds to another (AC9E7LE04). They have not yet examined intertextuality in literary texts specifically — how allusion or quotation in written texts enables new understanding of aesthetic quality through the meaning generated by the connection between texts.

Width

Minimum
Identify one intertextual reference (allusion or direct quotation) in a literary text and name the source text it references.
Typical
Identify intertextual references through allusion or quotation in written literary texts; explain how knowledge of the source text is required to understand the full meaning of the reference.
Wider
  • Investigate how a literary text uses allusions to a specific cultural tradition (e.g. classical mythology, the Bible, a particular literary canon) and examine what knowledge that assumes of the reader
  • Examine how a contemporary author uses intertextual references to classic texts to position their work within a literary tradition
  • Compare how two authors allude to the same source text differently, examining what each achieves through the reference

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to explain how an intertextual reference enables new understanding of aesthetic quality — a task that is impossible if students cannot reliably identify references and name their source texts. Without solid identification at Node 1, Node 2 analysis is speculative rather than grounded.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkIdentifying an intertextual reference requires students to distinguish between a text simply mentioning something and deliberately alluding to it — a non-obvious interpretive move. Without explicit scaffolding of what constitutes an allusion and how to trace it to a source, students miss references or over-identify casual mentions as allusions.
