Evaluating features of spoken texts

AC9E7LY02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and evaluate the features of spoken texts — including how voice, structure and language choices serve the purpose and audience

Lessons on this site

  • Judge a spoken text

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one feature of a spoken text
  • Evaluates the feature's effect on audience understanding or engagement with a reasoned justification

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Interaction skills in discussion.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one feature of a spoken text (e.g. pace, tone, vocabulary choice, structure) and explain how it affects the audience's understanding or engagement
Typical
Evaluate the features of a spoken text across multiple dimensions — how evidence is selected and presented, how voice features (tone, volume, pitch, pace) support the message, how vocabulary and sentence structures are chosen for the audience — and form and justify an evaluative judgement
Wider
  • Identify key evidence supporting an argument in a discussion between two speakers — evaluate how effectively each speaker uses evidence and interaction moves
  • Compare the same information presented in formal and informal spoken contexts and evaluate how the language choices affect audience engagement and credibility

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents can describe spoken text features (it was fast, it was loud) without evaluating them (and this made the argument more or less effective because...). The reasoning scaffold — what feature, what effect, what evaluative judgement — is what distinguishes description from evaluation and is the core capability this node develops.
