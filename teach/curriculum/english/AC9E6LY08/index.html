Phonic knowledge for complex words

AC9E6LY08 · Year 6 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use phonic knowledge of common and less common grapheme–phoneme relationships to read and write increasingly complex words

Prior knowledge

Students can use phonic, morphemic and vocabulary knowledge to read and spell words that share common letter patterns (AC9E5LY08). They have not yet applied phonic generalisations to read and write words with uncommon letter patterns such as those in 'pneumonia', 'resuscitate' and 'vegetation'.

By the end

Students can apply phonic knowledge of common and less common grapheme–phoneme relationships to read and write increasingly complex words including those with uncommon letter patterns.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using phonic generalisations to read and write complex words with uncommon letter patterns; for example, "pneumonia", "resuscitate" and "vegetation"

Waypoints

  • 1. Common grapheme-phoneme relationships in complex wordsHingeStudents can apply phonic knowledge of common grapheme–phoneme relationships to read and write multi-syllabic complex words.
  • 2. Less common grapheme-phoneme relationshipsStudents can apply phonic generalisations to read and write words with less common and uncommon letter patterns.
  • 3. Phonic strategies in independent readingStudents can independently apply phonic knowledge to decode unfamiliar complex words encountered during reading across curriculum areas.
