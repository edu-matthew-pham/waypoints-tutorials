← AC9E6LY08 Phonic knowledge for complex words

Common grapheme-phoneme relationships in complex words

AC9E6LY08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply phonic knowledge of common grapheme–phoneme relationships to read and write multi-syllabic complex words.

Success criteria

  • Reads at least three provided complex words accurately by applying phonic strategies
  • Spells at least two provided complex words correctly

Prior knowledge

Students can use phonic, morphemic and vocabulary knowledge to read and spell words that share common letter patterns (AC9E5LY08). They have not yet applied phonic generalisations to read and write words with uncommon letter patterns such as those in 'pneumonia', 'resuscitate' and 'vegetation'.

Width

Minimum
Decode and spell at least three provided complex multi-syllabic words by applying common grapheme–phoneme relationships; explain the phonics strategy used.
Typical
Apply phonic knowledge to read and write a range of complex words; use knowledge of syllable patterns and common letter patterns to decode unfamiliar multi-syllabic words encountered in curriculum-area reading.
Wider
  • Apply phonic knowledge to decode unfamiliar subject-specific vocabulary across curriculum areas
  • Compare how the same phoneme is represented by different graphemes in a set of related words and identify the pattern

Hinge

Students who have not achieved fluent decoding of common complex patterns cannot apply phonic strategies to the less common patterns in Node 2 — the cognitive load of slow decoding prevents pattern recognition for uncommon letter combinations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsApplying phonic generalisations to complex words requires fluent automaticity — the decoding needs to be fast enough that it does not impede reading comprehension. Repeated encounter and practice with complex words builds the fluency required.
