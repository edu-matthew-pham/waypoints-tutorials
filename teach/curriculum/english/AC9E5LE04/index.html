Imagery and sound devices

AC9E5LE04 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Examine the effects of imagery, including simile, metaphor and personification, and sound devices in narratives, poetry and songs

Prior knowledge

Students can examine the use of literary devices and deliberate word play including imagery and rhythm in literary texts (AC9E4LE04). They have not yet examined how specific imagery types — simile, metaphor, personification — create meaning through comparison, or how sound devices create effects in poetry and song.

By the end

Students examine and explain the effects of simile, metaphor and personification, and sound devices in narratives, poetry and songs — explaining how each device creates meaning and what effect it has on the reader or listener.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • discussing how figurative language including simile and metaphor can make use of a comparison between different things
  • discussing how, by appealing to the imagination, figurative language provides new ways of looking at the world

Waypoints

  • 1. Imagery: comparison and new ways of seeingHingeStudents can identify simile, metaphor and personification in texts and explain how each works through comparison to create a new way of seeing something.
  • 2. Imagery creates effectStudents can explain the specific effect that a piece of imagery creates for the reader — emotional, sensory, conceptual — connecting the comparison to the author's purpose.
  • 3. Sound devices and their effectsStudents can identify sound devices in poetry and songs and explain the effects they create for the reader or listener.
