← AC9E7LE03 Literary devices create character

Influencing emotions and opinions

AC9E7LE03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how literary devices and language features are used to influence the reader's emotional response and opinions in different text types

Success criteria

  • Identifies a device or language feature used to influence emotions or opinions
  • Explains how it produces that effect on the reader or viewer

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How literary devices construct character.

Width

Minimum
In a given text, identify a device or language feature used to influence the reader's emotions or opinions; explain how it produces that effect
Typical
Examine how literary devices and language features influence emotional response and opinion across different text types — how imagery creates empathy in narrative, how dialogue reveals moral character in drama, how visual composition positions the viewer in film; explain the mechanisms of influence in each
Wider
  • Explain the impact and significance of specific language features in a text — not just what they do locally but what they contribute to the reader's cumulative emotional and evaluative response
  • Compare how the same character is presented to influence the reader's sympathy differently in two different texts or adaptations — what devices are deployed to generate different emotional responses?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe mechanisms by which devices influence emotions and opinions vary significantly across text types — dialogue works differently in drama than in narrative; imagery operates differently in poetry than in film. Seeing the influence mechanism across genuinely different text types builds understanding of how influence is constructed, not just that it happens.
