Sensing and relating verbs

AC9E3LA07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify sensing verbs (feeling, thinking, perceiving) and relating verbs (defining, describing) and explain how they construct meaning differently from doing verbs.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies a sensing or relating verb and names its process type
  • Student explains how the sensing or relating verb contributes differently to meaning than a doing verb would

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Doing and saying verbs.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one sensing verb and one relating verb in a text and explain what type of process each represents.
Typical
Explore sensing verbs (feeling, thinking, perceiving) and relating verbs (constructing definitions and descriptions) — identifying different types and explaining how they control meaning in a clause differently from doing verbs.
Wider
  • Find sensing verbs in a narrative text and discuss how they let readers understand what characters are thinking and feeling without being told directly
  • Compare a description written with relating verbs versus adjectives and discuss what each choice achieves

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSensing and relating verbs don't look like 'typical verbs' to students. Seeing them in multiple different text contexts — narrative, information, description — helps students recognise the full range of what verbs can do beyond physical action.
