Language portrayals of character and setting

AC9E3LE03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify descriptive language that creates character and setting, and discuss what the language choices reveal about traits, relationships and place.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies specific language choices (not just general description) that portray a character or setting
  • Student discusses what the language reveals — trait, relationship, place quality — not just what it describes

Prior knowledge

Students discuss how language presents characters and settings differently across texts (AC9E2LE03). They have not yet examined how language and illustrations together create mood — the atmosphere or feeling a text establishes.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two descriptive language choices that portray a character or setting, and explain what each reveals.
Typical
Identify and discuss how descriptive language creates setting, how it portrays character traits and actions, and how characters' speech and actions reveal personality — connecting language choices to their effect on the reader.
Wider
  • Compare how the same character is described at different points in the story and discuss how the language changes as the character changes
  • Find language choices that portray setting in a First Nations Australian text and discuss how the relationship with Country/Place is constructed through language differently from other settings

Hinge

Students who cannot identify and interpret descriptive language choices at N1 lack the analytical vocabulary needed to discuss how language and illustration work together to create mood at N2.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents need to encounter descriptive language choices across multiple text types — narrative, picture books, poetry — before the concept of language as a deliberate constructive choice becomes stable. A single exemplar risks anchoring the concept too narrowly.
