Characters and events across contexts

AC9E3LE01 · Year 3 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

discuss characters, events and settings in different contexts in literature by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors

Prior knowledge

Students discuss how characters and settings are connected in literature from diverse contexts (AC9E2LE01). They have not yet compared how the same character type (e.g. the wolf) or story element is portrayed differently across texts from different cultural contexts.

By the end

Students discuss characters, events and settings in different contexts in literature by First Nations Australian, Australian and world authors.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring the ways wide-ranging authors tell the same story, identifying variations in the use of characters, settings and events
  • discussing characters and their relationship with Country/Place and families in literature by First Nations Australian authors
  • discussing similarities and differences in the way the wolf is portrayed in different versions of traditional stories
  • exploring the ways Australian settings are portrayed in stories

Waypoints

  • 1. Same story, different tellingsStudents can discuss how the same story or character type is told differently by different authors, identifying variations in characters, events and settings.
  • 2. Australian settings in literatureStudents can discuss how Australian settings are portrayed in stories and identify distinctive features of how Australian places and environments are represented.
