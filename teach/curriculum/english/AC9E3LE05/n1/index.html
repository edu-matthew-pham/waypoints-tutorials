Drawing on literary texts for inspiration

AC9E3LE05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw on literary texts they have read for inspiration and ideas, adapting settings or changing endings to create their own texts.

Success criteria

  • Student creates an imaginative text that draws identifiably on at least one element from a known literary text
  • Student can explain what was adapted and what reflects their own ideas or world

Prior knowledge

Students create and edit literary texts by adapting structures and language features of familiar texts (AC9E2LE05). They have not yet focused on using or adapting characters, events and settings that reflect their own world as a source for original imaginative text creation.

Width

Minimum
Create a short imaginative text that adapts at least one element (setting, character type, event or ending) from a read text, explaining what was borrowed and what was changed.
Typical
Draw on literary texts for inspiration — adapting texts by changing setting or revising an ending; creating texts that reflect their own world through character, event or setting choices.
Wider
  • Adapt a traditional story by shifting the setting to a familiar Australian or local context, and discuss how the story changes as a result
  • Create a text that uses a character type from a read text but places them in a situation from the student's own experience, discussing how the known character behaves in the new context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyCreative text creation at Y3 is supported by having a repertoire of literary elements to draw from. Students who have encountered a range of character types, settings and event structures across their reading have more resources to adapt. This is a strand where reading breadth directly enables writing.
