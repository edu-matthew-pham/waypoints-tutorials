Drawing on encountered texts

AC9E7LE07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify specific language features and literary devices in a source text and use them as starting points for their own creative experimentation

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one language feature or literary device from a source text to experiment with
  • Explains the connection between the encountered device and their own creative attempt

Prior knowledge

Students can create and edit literary texts that adapt plot structure, characters, settings and ideas from texts they have experienced, and experiment with literary devices (AC9E6LE05). They have not yet focused on experimenting with language features and literary devices encountered in texts — the move from adapting existing elements to experimenting with specific craft techniques.

Width

Minimum
From a text they have read, identify at least one language feature or literary device to experiment with; explain what they noticed and what they will try
Typical
Examine a range of language features and literary devices encountered in texts — narrative structure, imagery, sentence variation, metaphor, word choice, narrative voice — and select at least two to experiment with in their own writing; explain the connection between what they read and what they are attempting
Wider
  • Use aspects of texts in imaginative recreations — re-situating a character from a text into a new situation, experimenting with how the device travels to the new context
  • Experiment with different narrative structures such as epistolary form, flashback, multiple narrators — drawing on structural devices encountered in reading

Hinge

If students cannot identify specific devices to attempt, Node 2's editing work loses its anchor — purposeful editing requires being able to name what was tried and evaluate whether it achieved its effect.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly recreate surface features (story about similar characters) without experimenting with specific craft techniques. Reading across a wider range of texts — poetry, narrative, drama, film — gives students a broader repertoire of devices to attempt. Breadth of reading is the precondition for breadth of creative experimentation.
