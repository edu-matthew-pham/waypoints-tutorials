Experimenting and editing

AC9E7LE07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create a literary text that experiments with language features and literary devices, and edit it with attention to the effect of their choices

Success criteria

  • Creates a literary text with at least one deliberate experiment with a language feature or literary device
  • Identifies and explains at least one editing decision made to improve the effect of a craft choice

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Drawing on encountered texts.

Width

Minimum
Create a literary text that includes at least one deliberate experiment with a language feature or literary device; identify at least one editing decision made to improve the effect of a specific choice
Typical
Create a literary text experimenting with at least two language features or literary devices; edit with attention to how each choice is working — what effect does it create, and what could be changed to improve that effect?
Wider
  • Create a prequel using an imagined series of life events of a character, presented as a series of flashbacks in a scripted monologue — experimenting with temporal structure and voice
  • Transform a familiar print narrative into a short video or film narrative — experimenting with how literary devices translate across modes and making deliberate adaptation choices
  • Experiment with imagery, sentence variation, metaphor and word choice in a literary text, making visible through annotation the intention behind each craft choice

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierLiterary creation integrates reading knowledge (what devices are available), craft knowledge (how to deploy them), and editing judgement (whether the deployment is achieving the intended effect) in a coordinated creative process. The integration across reading, planning, drafting and editing is what this node develops — each component can be practised separately but the creative act requires their coordination.
