Giving reasons with 'because'

AC9E1LA02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 1 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use 'because' and similar connectives to link a preference to a reason.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a sentence using 'because' where the reason is specific to the stated preference
  • Student can identify the reason part of a given 'because' sentence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Stating a preference.

Width

Minimum
Construct at least one sentence using 'because' to give a reason for a preference or dislike.
Typical
Use 'because' and similar connectives to provide reasons for likes, dislikes and preferences across a range of topics, with reasons that are specific to the preference stated.
Wider
  • Explore other reason-giving connectives (e.g. 'since', 'as') and compare how they feel in spoken versus written contexts
  • Examine how reasons in opinion texts are structured, linking to AC9E1LA03 text organisation

Hinge

Students who append comments rather than genuinely linking a reason with 'because' cannot produce the reasoned opinion required for AC9E1LY06 opinion writing — the connective structure is a prerequisite for coherent opinion text production.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsUsing 'because' to genuinely link a reason (not just append a comment) requires repeated practice. Students frequently produce 'I like dogs because dogs are nice' — consolidation builds the habit of a substantive, specific reason.
