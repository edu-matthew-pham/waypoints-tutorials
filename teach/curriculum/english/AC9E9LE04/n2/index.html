Evaluating aesthetic qualities and appeal

AC9E9LE04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the aesthetic qualities of an author's literary style — making and supporting judgements about what makes the style effective, distinctive or appealing.

Success criteria

  • Student makes a specific evaluative claim about the author's style (not just description)
  • Student supports the evaluation with at least two pieces of textual evidence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying authorial style.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate the aesthetic appeal of one aspect of an author's style, explaining what makes it effective and how it contributes to the overall literary experience.
Typical
Analyse and evaluate the aesthetic qualities of an author's literary style — assessing its appeal, explaining its distinctiveness, and presenting comparisons to others.
Wider
  • Present a comparison of two authors' styles, evaluating the relative aesthetic appeal of each and arguing for a judgement about which is more effective for a particular literary purpose
  • Examine how cultural background and context shape what readers find aesthetically appealing in literary style — analysing where aesthetic preferences are culturally situated

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating aesthetic quality requires students to move from 'I find this appealing' to 'this has aesthetic quality because...' — a reasoning move from subjective response to grounded evaluation. The criteria for aesthetic judgement need explicit scaffolding to produce substantiated evaluation rather than preference assertion.
