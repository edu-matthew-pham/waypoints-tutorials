← AC9E6LA08 Figurative and emotive vocabulary

Using subject metalanguage

AC9E6LA08 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use the correct vocabulary (metalanguage) to name text structures, literary devices and language features when discussing and analysing texts.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two figurative language metalanguage terms accurately when discussing a text
  • Pairs the metalanguage term with an explanation of its effect, not just its identification

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Figurative language for vivid and unexpected meaning.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two figurative language terms accurately in a written or spoken response about a text.
Typical
Discuss texts using vocabulary to name text structure, literary devices and language features accurately; use metalanguage (metaphor, simile, personification, idiom, hyperbole, imagery) fluently in written analysis.
Wider
  • Write a short analytical paragraph about a poem that uses at least three metalanguage terms accurately and explains the effect of each device
  • Compare two texts of the same type using metalanguage to discuss both similarities and differences in language choices

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate and fluent use of metalanguage in discussion and writing requires practice. Students who know the terms in isolation often revert to vague language ('it makes it more interesting') under the pressure of producing extended writing or spoken analysis.
