Evaluating sources and information

AC9E5LY05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the accuracy and currency of information from print and digital sources, and identify the research purpose before selecting and organising information.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a research purpose and explains how it guides source selection.
  • Evaluates at least two sources using specific criteria (accuracy, currency, relevance) and explains the evaluation — does not simply accept or reject sources without reasoning.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Summarising and main idea.

Width

Minimum
Identify the research purpose for a task and evaluate at least two sources for their relevance and accuracy, explaining the basis for the evaluation.
Typical
Apply research skills including identifying research purpose, locating texts, gathering and organising information, evaluating the accuracy and currency of print and digital sources, and summarising information from several sources. Explain the criteria used to evaluate source quality.
Wider
  • Examine how the same topic is covered differently across sources of different types (encyclopedia, news article, personal blog, academic reference) and discuss what each source's characteristics suggest about its reliability and purpose
  • Apply information evaluation skills to a contested topic — where sources offer different claims — and discuss how to navigate conflicting information

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating source accuracy and currency requires students to apply criteria rather than accept information at face value. The reasoning chain — 'this source is/isn't reliable because... based on criteria...' — needs to be made explicit; students without this scaffold tend to trust all sources equally or distrust all unfamiliar sources.
