← AC9E8LE06 Create and edit literary texts

Experimenting with language features and devices

AC9E8LE06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create a literary text that deliberately experiments with at least one language feature or literary device, and articulate the purpose or effect the experiment is intended to achieve.

Success criteria

  • Creates a literary text that uses at least one named literary device or language feature deliberately.
  • Can explain the purpose or intended effect of the device choice in their own text.

Prior knowledge

Students can create literary texts that use language features and literary devices to create character, setting and mood (AC9E7LE06). They have not yet focused on deliberate experimentation — using devices with conscious intentionality toward a particular effect — or on collaborative and iterative editing of literary writing with peer feedback.

Width

Minimum
Create a short literary piece (e.g. a short story opening or poem) that deliberately uses at least one literary device or language feature for a named effect, and explain the choice made.
Typical
Create literary texts that combine multiple literary devices (e.g. imagery, tone, dialogue choices) to achieve a specific purpose or evoke a specific reader response; make deliberate and reflective choices in the writing process.
Wider
  • Create a short story with a sustained use of a single dominant device (e.g. extended metaphor or unreliable narrator perspective) and reflect on how the constraint shaped the writing
  • Collaborate with a peer to write a short script focusing on dialogue choices for each character, and reflect on how the dialogue reveals character through language rather than description
  • Experiment with combining literary devices from different cultural traditions (e.g. First Nations storytelling features with Western narrative conventions) to create a distinctive hybrid literary text

Hinge

Node 2 is editing for effect — a task that requires students to have a created text to edit and, crucially, to have made deliberate device choices in that text to revisit and evaluate. Students who have not built a working repertoire at Node 1 produce undifferentiated writing with no purposeful choices to reflect on or revise.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyLiterary experimentation requires a repertoire of devices to experiment with. Students who have only analysed devices need guided practice deploying them in their own writing across multiple short tasks before the experimental relationship to craft becomes comfortable.
