Plan, create and deliver spoken and multimodal presentations

AC9E8LY07 · Year 8 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and multimodal presentations for audiences and purposes, selecting language features, literary devices, visual features and features of voice to suit formal or informal situations, and organising and developing ideas in texts in ways that may be imaginative, reflective, informative, persuasive and/or analytical

Prior knowledge

Students can plan, create and deliver spoken and multimodal presentations for audiences and purposes, selecting features of voice and language (AC9E7LY07). They have not yet focused on the full planning-rehearsal-delivery cycle with deliberate selection of literary devices and visual features, or on calibrating voice features for specific audience effects.

By the end

Students plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and multimodal presentations with deliberate selection of language, literary, visual and voice features for specific purposes and audiences.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • integrating multimodal features in a spoken presentation to support the audience's understanding
  • choosing vocabulary and sentence structures, and using literary devices such as similes, metaphors and personification to meet perceived audience needs
  • selecting features of voice, such as tone, volume, pitch and pace, with particular attention to the effects these may have on audience reaction
  • collaborating with peers to develop a persuasive advertising campaign about a contemporary issue

Waypoints

  • 1. Planning and crafting presentationsHingeStudents can plan a spoken or multimodal presentation that makes deliberate selections of language features, literary devices, visual features and voice features, organising and developing ideas for a specific purpose and audience.
  • 2. Rehearsing and delivering for effectStudents can rehearse and deliver a spoken or multimodal presentation, using features of voice — tone, volume, pitch, pace — with attention to the effects these have on audience reaction and acceptance of ideas.
