Evaluative and emotional vocabulary

AC9E3LA02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify evaluative and emotional vocabulary choices and explain how they express the speaker's or writer's feelings and opinions about characters, events or ideas.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies evaluative or emotional vocabulary and names the opinion or feeling it expresses
  • Student explains how the word choice shows the speaker's/writer's position — not just that it describes the character or event

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Modal verbs and degrees of certainty.

Width

Minimum
Identify evaluative or emotional word choices in a text (adverbs, nouns or verbs) and explain what opinion or feeling each expresses.
Typical
Recognise how adverb, noun and verb choices present different evaluations of characters and events; explore emotional language that demonstrates a range of emotions and positions; build vocabulary for expressing opinions and feelings.
Wider
  • Compare how the same character is described with different evaluative vocabulary in two versions of the same story, and discuss what this reveals about point of view
  • Create a short text about the same event from two different emotional perspectives, choosing vocabulary deliberately to show the different positions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often see emotional vocabulary as simply positive or negative. Exploring different text types — narrative, persuasion, news — and different versions of the same story reveals that evaluative language constructs a specific perspective, not just a mood.
