← AC9E9LA01 Language empowers relationships and roles

Community membership through language

AC9E9LA01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify language features that signal membership in specific communities, such as family registers, teen slang, technical jargon and classroom interaction patterns.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least two community-specific language features and names the community each belongs to
  • Student explains how each language feature signals membership or reinforces belonging in that community

Prior knowledge

Students understand how language expresses and creates personal and social identities (AC9E7LA01). They have not yet examined how language functions as a mechanism of power within communities — shaping membership, authority and social roles.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two examples of community-specific language and explain how they signal belonging to that community.
Typical
Compare language features across three or more communities (e.g. family, peer group, classroom), explaining how each signals membership and reinforces shared identity.
Wider
  • Analyse how online community language (gaming, fandoms, professional forums) constructs hierarchies of expertise through jargon and access
  • Examine how First Nations Australian authors use language, including specific greetings in language, to reinforce relationships to Country/Place and community
  • Investigate how code-switching between community registers positions speakers differently depending on context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often conflate all informal language as equivalent; examining different community types reveals that the empowering function varies — jargon in professional contexts differs qualitatively from intimacy markers in family contexts. More community contexts resolve this flattening.
