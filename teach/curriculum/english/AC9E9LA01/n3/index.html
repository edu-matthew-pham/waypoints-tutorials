Language empowerment as a mechanism

AC9E9LA01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise how language choices function as mechanisms of empowerment — not just markers of identity — actively shaping relationships and roles rather than simply reflecting them.

Success criteria

  • Student explains how a language choice actively constructs a relationship or role, not merely reflects it
  • Student uses appropriate metalanguage (e.g. register, mode, positioning) in their explanation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Language and power in roles.

Width

Minimum
Explain how a specific language choice in a text actively constructs or reinforces a relationship dynamic, rather than merely describing it.
Typical
Analyse a text (e.g. a speech, social media post, classroom exchange) and explain how multiple language choices work together to empower particular roles or relationships, using metalanguage appropriately.
Wider
  • Compare how the same relationship is constructed differently across two texts from different cultural or historical contexts
  • Analyse how First Nations Australian language use in literature creates empowerment through connection to Country and cultural knowledge, rather than through dominance
  • Examine how disrupting expected language norms can challenge established power relationships

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to coordinate understanding of community membership, role construction and the active/constitutive function of language into a single analytical move. The integration — language doesn't just reflect power, it creates it — is the core insight of the descriptor.
