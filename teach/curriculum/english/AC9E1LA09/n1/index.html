Topics have their own words

AC9E1LA09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that different learning areas use their own topic-specific vocabulary and can name examples from a familiar topic.

Success criteria

  • Student names at least three topic-specific words from a current learning area topic
  • Student explains that these words are associated with that particular topic or subject

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and develop awareness of vocabulary used in familiar contexts related to personal experiences (AC9EFLA08). They have not yet systematically recognised topic-specific vocabulary tied to learning area disciplines, or understood the cultural and contextual dimension of vocabulary such as Acknowledgement of Country.

Width

Minimum
Name at least three topic-specific words from a learning area topic currently studied and explain that these words belong to that topic.
Typical
Identify topic-specific vocabulary from at least two different learning area topics, explain that each discipline has words that belong to it, and use those words when discussing the topic.
Wider
  • Compare vocabulary from two different learning area topics and discuss what makes words 'belong' to a particular field
  • Investigate how some words are used in everyday language and in a technical sense in a learning area (e.g. 'force' in science versus everyday use)
