Punctuation and capital letters

AC9EFLA09 · Foundation English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify punctuation as a feature of written text different from letters; recognise that capital letters are used for names, and that capital letters also signal the beginning of sentences while punctuation marks signal the end

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise some letters and have encountered written text. They have not yet been asked to distinguish punctuation marks from letters or to understand the systematic roles of capital letters and end-of-sentence punctuation.

By the end

Students can identify punctuation marks as distinct from letters, recognise capital letters in names and at sentence beginnings, and identify end-of-sentence punctuation marks — understanding that these are consistent, rule-based conventions in written English.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • commenting on capital letters encountered in everyday texts; for example, "That's the letter that starts my name." "The name of my family and my town have capital letters."

Waypoints

  • 1. Punctuation is not lettersHingeStudents can identify punctuation marks in a text and distinguish them from letters.
  • 2. Capital letters signal names and sentence startsStudents can identify capital letters used for names and at the beginning of sentences, and explain the rule behind each use.
  • 3. Punctuation signals sentence endingsStudents can identify end-of-sentence punctuation marks (full stop, question mark, exclamation mark) and understand that they signal the end of a sentence.
