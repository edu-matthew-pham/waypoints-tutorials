Punctuation signals sentence endings

AC9EFLA09 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify end-of-sentence punctuation marks (full stop, question mark, exclamation mark) and understand that they signal the end of a sentence.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify a full stop, question mark and exclamation mark in a text.
  • Student can explain that each of these signals the end of a sentence.
  • Student can use a full stop in their own writing at the end of a sentence.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Capital letters signal names and sentence starts.

Width

Minimum
Identify a full stop in a text and say that it means the sentence has ended.
Typical
Identify full stops, question marks and exclamation marks as sentence-end signals; recognise the rule that punctuation marks — not new lines — signal sentence endings; apply this in reading and beginning writing.
Wider
  • Compare sentences ending with different punctuation marks and discuss how each affects how the sentence is read or spoken
  • Investigate how punctuation functions differently in digital informal writing (texts, posts) vs formal written texts
  • Sort sentences by end punctuation type and read each aloud — exploring how punctuation signals prosody

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents may know full stops but not question marks or exclamation marks as sentence-enders — each requires its own contextual exposure before the general 'punctuation ends sentences' concept is robust.
