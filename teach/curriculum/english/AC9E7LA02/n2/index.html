← AC9E7LA02 Evaluative language and substantiation

Substantiating evaluations

AC9E7LA02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can understand how evaluations are substantiated by reference to the text and to other sources

Lessons on this site

  • Judging and backing it up

Success criteria

  • Identifies whether an evaluative claim is substantiated by textual reference, outside source, or neither
  • Explains how substantiation strengthens an evaluative claim

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Vocabulary of evaluation.

Width

Minimum
Given an evaluative claim about a text, identify whether it is supported by textual reference, outside source, or neither; explain why the unsupported claim is less persuasive
Typical
Examine how evaluative language is used in a review or critical response and trace the substantiation strategy — which claims are supported by textual evidence, which by external sources, and which are bare assertions; discuss the effect of each
Wider
  • Examine how evaluative language is used to critically assess the validity of evidence and the reliability of sources — moving from evaluating literary texts to evaluating informational texts and source quality
  • Compare two evaluations of the same text that reach different conclusions and identify how different substantiation strategies produce different persuasive effects

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly produce evaluative claims without understanding that a claim requires support, or they treat any reference to the text as adequate substantiation. An explicit reasoning scaffold — what is the claim, what is the evidence, and how does the evidence support the claim — builds the connection between evaluation and substantiation that is not intuitive.
