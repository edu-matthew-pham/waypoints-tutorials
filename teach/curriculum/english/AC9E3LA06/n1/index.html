Identifying clause elements

AC9E3LA06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the verb and subject in a clause.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly identifies the verb in a clause
  • Student correctly identifies the subject that goes with that verb

Prior knowledge

Students understand how compound sentences connect ideas using coordinating conjunctions (AC9E2LA06). They have not yet examined the internal structure of a clause — specifically the subject-verb relationship — or the agreement rule.

Width

Minimum
Identify the verb and subject in at least three clauses by locating the verb first, then finding what or who the verb is about.
Typical
Identify clauses by locating verbs and the key words that link to the verbs; practise identifying subjects and verbs across different sentence types and text contexts.
Wider
  • Find clauses in different text types (narrative, information, instruction) and compare how subjects vary — from simple nouns to noun groups
  • Explore what happens to a clause when the subject is removed — discussing why subjects are necessary for meaning

Hinge

Students who cannot reliably identify the subject and verb in a clause cannot apply subject-verb agreement rules at N2 — agreement requires knowing which subject the verb must match.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsClause identification is a precision skill — knowing the definition doesn't translate automatically into reliable identification. Students need repeated practice across varied examples before subject and verb identification becomes fluent.
