Experimenting with text structures

AC9E9LE06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create a literary text that makes a deliberate structural experiment and can explain what the experiment is intended to achieve.

Success criteria

  • Student makes at least one identifiable structural experiment in the text
  • Student can articulate what effect the structural experiment is intended to create

Prior knowledge

Students create and edit literary texts experimenting with language features and literary devices (AC9E7LE07). They have not yet created hybrid texts that experiment specifically with text structures, or focused editing on structural integration of experiments.

Width

Minimum
Create a short literary text that makes one deliberate structural experiment (e.g. non-linear timeline, subverted genre opening, hybrid form) and explain the purpose of the choice.
Typical
Create and draft a literary text that experiments with at least one text structure element and one language feature or device, explaining how the choices serve purpose and audience.
Wider
  • Create a text that adapts an existing short story, poem, play or speech into a different form (e.g. print to short film, poem to spoken word piece), examining what is preserved and what must change
  • Experiment with prequel or sequel writing as a form of textual intervention — examining how the original text constrains and enables the new one

Hinge

Students who have only experienced linear prose structures default to them — without a repertoire of structural options, 'experimentation' at N1 is impossible, and N2 editing for structural coherence has nothing to edit.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyCreative experimentation requires a repertoire of structural options to choose from. Students who have only experienced linear prose structures will default to them. Building familiarity with a range of structural possibilities — non-linear, hybrid, genre-subversive — enables genuine experimentation.
