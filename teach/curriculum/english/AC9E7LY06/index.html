Select structures for purpose

AC9E7LY06 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal texts, selecting subject matter, and using text structures, language features, literary devices and visual features as appropriate to convey information, ideas and opinions in ways that may be imaginative, reflective, informative, persuasive and/or analytical

Prior knowledge

Students can plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal texts using paragraphs, complex sentences, expanded verb groups, tense, topic-specific vocabulary, punctuation and visual features (AC9E6LY06). They have not yet worked at the level of deliberately selecting text structures, language features and literary devices to convey specific purposes — the move from using these elements to selecting them purposefully.

By the end

Students plan, create and publish texts selecting structures, language features and literary devices for purpose.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • compiling a portfolio of texts in a range of modes related to a particular concept, purpose or audience; for example, a class anthology of poems or stories
  • using appropriate text conventions to create scripts for interviews, presentations, advertisements, radio segments, podcasts, digital and online reflections including vlogs
  • editing for meaning by removing unnecessary repetition, reordering sentences and varying sentence structures to refine ideas, adding or substituting words for impact, and reviewing accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation
  • tracking a word-processed document to jointly edit texts

Waypoints

  • 1. Planning with purpose in mindHingeStudents can plan a text by identifying its purpose, audience and the structural and language choices that will serve them
  • 2. Creating for purposeStudents can create a written or multimodal text, making deliberate choices about text structures, language features and literary devices to serve the purpose and audience
  • 3. Editing and publishing for purposeStudents can edit a draft text by making purposeful revision decisions, and publish in a form appropriate to the audience and purpose
