Substantiation improving cohesion

AC9E8LA04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how strengthened paragraph internal structure — particularly the inclusion of substantiation — improves overall textual cohesion by ensuring each paragraph fully develops its contribution to the text.

Success criteria

  • Produces an extended analytical paragraph that includes a clear claim, at least one piece of evidence and an explicit substantiation connecting evidence to claim.
  • Can explain how their paragraph contributes to the cohesion of a longer text.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Paragraph internal structure.

Width

Minimum
Revise or extend one underdeveloped paragraph by adding substantiation, and explain how the revision improves the paragraph's contribution to the text as a whole.
Typical
Write or revise extended analytical paragraphs that use claim, example, quotation and substantiation effectively, explaining how well-developed paragraph structure contributes to the cohesion of a longer analytical text.
Wider
  • Investigate how argument cohesion depends on each paragraph fully substantiating its claim — how a single under-substantiated paragraph disrupts the logical chain of an entire text
  • Write a multi-paragraph analytical text and review its cohesion by tracing whether each claim is fully substantiated and whether the paragraph sequence creates a coherent progression of ideas
  • Examine how formal debate speeches or published opinion writing use substantiation differently from academic essays

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsParagraph substantiation is a fluency skill — knowing the structure intellectually is not enough. Students need repeated practice writing and revising paragraphs before the pattern becomes automatic. Consolidation at this node (writing multiple paragraphs, revising for substantiation) builds the fluency that transfers to independent writing.
