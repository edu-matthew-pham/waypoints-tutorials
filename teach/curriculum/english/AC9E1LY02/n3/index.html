← AC9E1LY02 Turn-taking and active listening

Responding to and building on contributions

AC9E1LY02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 1 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can respond to what others have said — acknowledging, agreeing, questioning or extending a previous contribution.

Success criteria

  • Student explicitly acknowledges a prior contribution before making their own
  • Student's response connects to what was previously said rather than introducing an unrelated idea

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Active listening behaviours.

Width

Minimum
Respond to at least one contribution from another student, acknowledging what was said before adding their own idea.
Typical
Respond to others' contributions in discussion, using language that acknowledges what was said (e.g. 'I agree with...', 'I also think...', 'But what about...') and adds a new idea or question.
Wider
  • Practise restating another person's idea before responding — discuss how this changes the quality of the discussion
  • Explore how asking a question in response to a contribution extends the discussion in a different way than agreeing does

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkResponding to a contribution requires students to listen, hold what was said, and then connect their own response — a sequenced reasoning move. A simple scaffold ('I heard [name] say... and I think...') supports this coordination.
