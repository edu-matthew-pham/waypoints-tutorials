Spelling patterns for irregular words

AC9E2LY11 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply known spelling patterns to read and write words with unpredictable sound-spelling relationships.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly spells a set of irregular high-frequency words in their writing.
  • Student can use context to select the correct pronunciation when a word is ambiguous.

Prior knowledge

Students spell one- and two-syllable words using common letter patterns, and read and write an increasing number of high-frequency words (AC9E1LY13/14). They have not yet applied morphemic knowledge to words with irregular or unpredictable sound-spelling relationships.

Width

Minimum
Apply a known spelling pattern to write an irregular high-frequency word correctly (e.g. 'one', 'once').
Typical
Apply spelling pattern knowledge to a range of irregular words and high-frequency words; use context to select the correct form when a word has more than one plausible pronunciation.
Wider
  • Group irregular words by shared spelling pattern and discuss what the pattern reveals about English spelling history
  • Use context clues in a sentence to read a word with multiple plausible pronunciations (e.g. 'read' — present vs past tense)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSpelling irregular words correctly requires memorisation and pattern recognition built through repeated exposure and writing practice. Width is thin — the skill does not vary meaningfully across contexts. Repeated correct production in writing is the productive investment.
