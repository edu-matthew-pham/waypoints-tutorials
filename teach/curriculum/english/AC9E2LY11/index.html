Spelling patterns and morphemes

AC9E2LY11 · Year 2 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use knowledge of spelling patterns and morphemes to read and write words whose spelling is not completely predictable from their sounds, including high-frequency words

Prior knowledge

Students spell one- and two-syllable words using common letter patterns, and read and write an increasing number of high-frequency words (AC9E1LY13/14). They have not yet applied morphemic knowledge to words with irregular or unpredictable sound-spelling relationships.

By the end

Students can use spelling patterns and morphemes to read and write words whose spelling is not completely predictable from their sounds, including high-frequency words.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using known words and knowledge of spelling patterns and morphemes to write unknown words; for example, 'one', 'once', 'only' and 'lone'
  • using context to read the correct word when an unknown word has more than one plausible pronunciation

Waypoints

  • 1. Spelling patterns for irregular wordsStudents can apply known spelling patterns to read and write words with unpredictable sound-spelling relationships.
  • 2. Using morphemes to spell unknown wordsStudents can apply morphemic knowledge — recognising base words, prefixes and suffixes — to spell unknown words by analogy with known words.
