← AC9E9LE02 Personal response and subsequent analysis

Comparing initial and developed response

AC9E9LE02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can present a personal response that explicitly compares their initial impressions with their subsequent whole-text analysis, explaining what changed and why.

Success criteria

  • Student explicitly compares an initial impression with their subsequent analysis
  • Student explains what the fuller analysis revealed that the initial impression could not have accessed

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Whole-text analysis.

Width

Minimum
Present a response that identifies at least one way their initial impression changed after whole-text analysis, and explains what the analysis revealed that the initial impression missed.
Typical
Present a personal response to a literary text that traces the journey from initial impressions to developed interpretation — explaining what the analysis added, complicated or confirmed, and arriving at an independent position.
Wider
  • Examine how the process of sharing initial responses with others before detailed analysis affects subsequent interpretation — and what this reveals about reading as a social process
  • Reflect on a text where initial and final response are significantly different, and analyse what this reveals about the text's complexity or the reader's assumptions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPresenting a response that integrates initial impression and subsequent analysis — showing how one led to or was transformed by the other — requires students to hold both simultaneously and articulate the relationship. This is the core capability of the descriptor.
