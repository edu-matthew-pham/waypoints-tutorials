← AC9E9LE02 Personal response and subsequent analysis

Initial response as legitimate reading

AC9E9LE02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can articulate an initial response to a literary text and recognise that first impressions are a valid starting point for analysis.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student articulates at least one specific initial response (not just a general impression)
  • Student connects the response to a specific textual feature rather than leaving it as an unanchored feeling

Prior knowledge

Students form and justify opinions about texts, identifying agreement and difference with others (AC9E7LE02). They have not yet compared their initial impressions with a developed whole-text analysis — the dialectic between first response and considered interpretation.

Width

Minimum
Record an initial response to a literary text — including emotional reactions, questions and confusion — and explain what in the text prompted each response.
Typical
Articulate initial impressions of a literary text including what the student found compelling, confusing or significant, connecting each response to specific textual features.
Wider
  • Compare how different students' initial responses to the same text vary, and begin to hypothesise what textual features produce those different responses
  • Examine how initial responses might be shaped by the reader's cultural background or prior experiences

Hinge

Students who cannot articulate an initial response have nothing to compare against when asked to present the journey from first impression to analysis at N3 — the comparison is definitionally blocked.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents often have undifferentiated initial responses ('I liked/didn't like it'). Experiencing varied texts and being asked to record specific reactions — what surprised, confused or interested them — builds the repertoire of response types that makes comparison with later analysis meaningful.
