Evaluating information and ideas

AC9E3LY05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate information and ideas in texts — assessing relevance for a task, comparing how different texts present similar ideas, and determining importance.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student makes an evaluative judgement about a text (relevant/important/trustworthy) and gives a reason
  • Student's reason is connected to a specific feature of the text — not just a general impression

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Building literal and inferred meaning.

Width

Minimum
Make at least one evaluative judgement about a text — e.g. whether it is relevant for a task, which information is most important, or whether two texts agree — and explain the reasoning.
Typical
Compare and contrast how different texts present similar ideas or information; determine the relevance of a text for a particular task; evaluate information and ideas across texts when listening and viewing.
Wider
  • Compare two texts that present conflicting information about the same topic and discuss how to decide which to trust
  • Evaluate which source (a book, a video, a website) is most useful for a specific research purpose and justify the choice

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluation requires criteria — students need reasoning scaffolds for what makes information important, relevant or trustworthy at Y3. Without criteria, evaluation defaults to preference. The reasoning structure (relevant/important/accurate because...) needs explicit modelling.
