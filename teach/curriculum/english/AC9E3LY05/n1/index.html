← AC9E3LY05 Evaluate texts with comprehension strategies

Building literal and inferred meaning

AC9E3LY05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use comprehension strategies including prediction, questioning and inference to build both literal and inferred meaning from texts.

Success criteria

  • Student applies at least two named comprehension strategies to a text
  • Student distinguishes between information stated directly (literal) and meaning they had to work out (inferred)

Prior knowledge

Students use comprehension strategies to build literal and inferred meaning across texts (AC9E2LY05). They have not yet used comprehension strategies specifically to evaluate information and ideas — assessing relevance, importance and accuracy.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two comprehension strategies (e.g. predicting, inferring, questioning) to build meaning from a text, explaining what each strategy helped them understand.
Typical
Make predictions about a text; determine important ideas, events or details; draw inferences using text evidence and prior knowledge; learn new content and ask questions to expand understanding.
Wider
  • Compare comprehension strategies used for narrative versus informative texts, discussing how purpose changes which strategies are most useful
  • Revisit predictions made before reading after completing a text and discuss whether and why they were confirmed or revised

Hinge

Students who have not built literal and inferred meaning using comprehension strategies cannot evaluate information and ideas at N2 — evaluation is downstream of comprehension, not parallel to it.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents at Y3 benefit from building a repertoire of named strategies that they can select and apply. Familiarity with multiple strategies — rather than defaulting to one — expands their comprehension toolkit across different text types.
