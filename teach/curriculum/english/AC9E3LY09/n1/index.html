← AC9E3LY09 Multisyllabic words and phonemes

Complex consonant patterns

AC9E3LY09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Year 3 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read and write words with consonant digraphs, consonant blends and digraphs that represent different sounds.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly reads multisyllabic words with complex consonant patterns
  • Student correctly segments a multisyllabic word into syllables when writing

Prior knowledge

Students manipulate complex sounds and use vowel digraphs, consonant clusters and silent letters to read and write multisyllabic words (AC9E2LY09-10). They have not yet applied this knowledge to more complex patterns including consonant digraphs representing different sounds.

Width

Minimum
Read and write at least four multisyllabic words with complex consonant patterns (digraphs, blends) and explain the sound each pattern represents.
Typical
Read and write complex words with consonant digraphs and blends; apply knowledge to consonant digraphs that represent different sounds in different words (e.g. 'machine', 'chef' for 'ch'); use blending and segmenting across syllable boundaries.
Wider
  • Collect examples of the same digraph representing different sounds (e.g. 'ch' in chair/chef/chorus) and sort them — discussing patterns in when each pronunciation applies
  • Explore how splitting words into syllables helps decode long unfamiliar words, practising across different syllable patterns

Hinge

Students who cannot flexibly apply phoneme-grapheme knowledge for complex consonant patterns will encounter systematic decoding errors when reading multisyllabic words — this blocks accurate reading across curriculum texts and underpins fluency in LY10 and LY11 morphological work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe key challenge is that digraphs like 'ch' don't always represent the same sound. Students need exposure to multiple examples across different words before they develop flexible pattern recognition rather than applying a single rule.
