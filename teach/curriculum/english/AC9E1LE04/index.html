Sound patterns in poetry

AC9E1LE04 · Year 1 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

listen to and discuss poems, chants, rhymes and songs, and imitate and invent sound patterns including alliteration and rhyme

Prior knowledge

Students can explore and replicate the rhythms and sound patterns of literary texts such as poems and chants at Foundation level (AC9EFLE04). They have not yet specifically identified and invented alliteration, or systematically imitated and created their own sound patterns.

By the end

Students listen to and discuss poems, chants, rhymes and songs, and imitate and invent sound patterns including alliteration and rhyme.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • listening to and discussing poems including those that play with sound; for example, tongue twisters and nonsense rhymes
  • clapping and chanting with poems and experimenting with rhythm
  • imitating and experimenting with alliteration using the same initial letter or sound

Waypoints

  • 1. Recognising sound patternsStudents can identify alliteration and rhyme in poems, chants and songs and describe the sound pattern they create.
  • 2. Discussing the effect of sound patternsStudents can discuss how sound patterns such as alliteration and rhyme create effects in poems, chants and songs — how they sound and feel.
  • 3. Imitating sound patternsStudents can imitate a sound pattern from a poem or chant by creating their own example using the same pattern.
  • 4. Inventing sound patternsStudents can invent their own sound patterns — creating original alliterative phrases or rhyming pairs — and share them with others.
