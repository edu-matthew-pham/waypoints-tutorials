Colon uses

AC9E7LA09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the uses of colons and explain what each use signals to the reader

Lessons on this site

  • Colons and brackets

Success criteria

  • Identifies a colon in a given sentence
  • Explains what the colon signals in that specific context

Prior knowledge

Students understand how commas are used in lists, to separate clauses and in dialogue (AC9E6LA09). They have not yet worked with colons or brackets — punctuation marks that introduce, expand or parenthesise information rather than separating clauses.

Width

Minimum
Identify a colon in a given sentence and explain what it signals — introduction of a list, elaboration of a preceding clause, or introduction of a quotation or example
Typical
Examine colons in several sentences across different uses and explain how each signals a specific relationship between what comes before and after the colon
Wider
  • Examine the impact of information added to sentences when colons are used — compare a sentence with and without the colon-introduced elaboration and discuss how meaning changes
  • Experiment with writing sentences where a colon introduces a list, an elaboration, and a quotation — discuss how the reader's expectation changes depending on what follows the colon

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsColon usage is a precision skill with a small set of rules that students apply inconsistently — particularly confusing colon-before-list with comma-before-list. Practice across the distinct uses builds reliable application.
