← AC9EFLE03 Recognise literary text types

Literary texts have features

AC9EFLE03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify at least one key feature (character, event, beginning, or ending) in a familiar literary text.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify a character in a literary text.
  • Student can identify a key event from the story.
  • Student can identify the beginning and ending of a story.

Prior knowledge

Students have encountered stories and poems. They have not yet been asked to distinguish literary text types from each other, or to identify the structural features (beginnings, endings, characters, events) that define and differentiate them.

Width

Minimum
Name a character and an event in one story.
Typical
Identify characters, events, beginnings and endings across multiple literary texts; recognise that these are features that literary texts share.
Wider
  • Compare how different texts begin — story openings across picture books and fairytales — noting patterns and conventions
  • Map the events in a familiar story in order — from beginning to ending — using drawings or a simple timeline

Hinge

Without being able to identify characters, events, beginnings and endings, students cannot recognise the patterns that distinguish literary text types in Node 2 — type-level pattern recognition depends on feature-level identification being secure.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFeature identification must become reliable across multiple texts before students can abstract text-type patterns from those features. Practice across a range of stories builds the consistent recognition habit.
