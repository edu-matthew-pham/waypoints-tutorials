← AC9EFLY09 Recognise rhyme, alliteration and phonemes

Rhyming words share endings

AC9EFLY09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify a rhyming pair and produce a rhyming word when given a target.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify whether two words rhyme when heard.
  • Student can generate at least one rhyming word when given a target word.

Prior knowledge

Students may have heard rhymes and songs. They have not yet been asked to consciously attend to, name and generate the phonological patterns in spoken words — rhyme, alliteration, syllables and phonemes.

Width

Minimum
Identify whether two words rhyme; generate one word that rhymes with a given word.
Typical
Recognise and produce rhyming words; identify rhyming patterns in familiar stories and rhymes; generate rhyming strings.
Wider
  • Generate rhyming strings across multiple word families — exploring how many words share the same rime
  • Compare rhyming conventions in English with other languages or First Nations Australian language patterns known to students
  • Create simple rhyming couplets orally — applying rhyme generation to creative language play

Hinge

Reliable rhyme recognition signals that phoneme-level auditory discrimination is developing — without this, phoneme isolation in Node 4 and the blending and segmenting tasks in LY10 will be unreliable, as they all depend on the ability to hear and attend to sounds within words.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsReliable rhyme recognition and production is foundational to phonological awareness — errors here indicate the phoneme-level awareness needed for decoding is not yet secure. Practice across many word pairs is the remedy.
