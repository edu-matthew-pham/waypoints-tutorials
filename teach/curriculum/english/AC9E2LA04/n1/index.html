Tracking a referent

AC9E2LA04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 2 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can track how a person or thing is referred to across a section of text — by name, then by pronoun — and identify which pronoun refers to which entity.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify the referent of a personal pronoun in a given sentence.
  • Student does not default to naming the most recently mentioned noun as the referent in cases where this would be incorrect.

Prior knowledge

Students explore how repetition, rhyme and rhythm create cohesion in poems, chants and songs (AC9E1LA04). They have not yet examined how pronouns and ellipsis create cohesion in prose and other text types by tracking referents and omitting recoverable information.

Width

Minimum
In a short passage, identify the noun that a given pronoun refers back to (the referent).
Typical
Map pronoun reference chains across a paragraph — tracking how 'Isy', 'she', 'I', 'sister' all refer to the same character — in both narrative and informative texts.
Wider
  • Track reference chains across multiple characters in a narrative and identify where pronoun reference becomes ambiguous
  • Compare pronoun reference chains in first-person vs third-person narratives and discuss how each affects the reader's relationship to the story

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly assume the most recently mentioned noun is always the referent — a positional heuristic that fails with complex reference chains. Multiple textual examples across different pronouns (she/he/they/it) and referent types resolve this. More texts, not more explanation of one example.
