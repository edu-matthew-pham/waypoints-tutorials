Texts as context-shaped artefacts

AC9E8LY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Year 8 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify specific features of a text and explain how they reflect the context — social, historical, technological or cultural — in which the text was created.

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific features of a text (e.g. language choices, structure, format) and names the contextual factors that shaped them.
  • Explains the connection between at least two textual features and the context in which the text was created.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how texts are shaped by the context in which they are created and understand how digital and multimodal contexts influence text forms (AC9E7LY01). They have not yet focused on how specific contemporary text forms — social media texts and speeches for reconciliation — reflect the specific contexts of their creation.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two features of a provided text (e.g. a social media post or speech) and explain how each reflects the context in which it was created.
Typical
Identify and explain how social media texts and speeches for reconciliation reflect the contexts in which they are created; examine how platform affordances, audience, purpose and historical moment are all visible in textual features.
Wider
  • Compare how a social media text and a formal speech on the same topic reflect different contextual constraints — platform, audience, register, purpose — and what this reveals about how context shapes text
  • Investigate how speeches for reconciliation reflect specific historical and political contexts and how these contexts have shaped the language and structural choices of those speeches
  • Examine how a First Nations community text (e.g. a land rights statement or community announcement) reflects the specific cultural and political context of its creation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe abstract claim 'texts reflect contexts' needs multiple concrete examples before it becomes a generalised lens. Social media and reconciliation speeches are the ACARA examples, but encountering multiple diverse text-context pairs builds the transferable insight that this is a universal feature of all texts.
