Voice as a literary device

AC9E10LE06 · Year 10 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compare and evaluate how 'voice' as a literary device is used in different types of texts, such as poetry, novels and film, to evoke emotional responses

Prior knowledge

Students create and edit literary texts including hybrids, experimenting with structures, features and devices for purpose (AC9E9LE06). They have not yet compared and evaluated how voice as a literary device evokes emotional responses across different text types.

By the end

Students compare and evaluate how 'voice' as a literary device is used in different types of texts to evoke emotional responses.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing the “voice” of protest in a range of poems or songs and evaluating how different voices evoke a response

Waypoints

  • 1. Understanding voice as a literary deviceHingeStudents can identify how voice operates as a literary device in a text, explaining how the author constructs voice and what it contributes to the text.
  • 2. Comparing and evaluating voice across text typesStudents can compare and evaluate how voice as a literary device evokes different emotional responses across poetry, novels and film.
