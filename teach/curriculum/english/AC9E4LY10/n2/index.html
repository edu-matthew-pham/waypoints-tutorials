Morphological word families

AC9E4LY10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify word families built from the same base and use family relationships to spell unknown related words.

Success criteria

  • Can name at least three words in a word family and explain what they share
  • Can use word family knowledge to spell a related word they have not seen before

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Spelling generalisations — double letters and affixes.

Width

Minimum
Build a word family from a base word (at least 3 members) and use the family to spell a word they might not know independently.
Typical
Build and explore morphological word families across a range of base words and morphemes, explaining how meaning and spelling are preserved across the family and using family relationships as a spelling strategy.
Wider
  • Explore word families from Latin and Greek roots (e.g. tri-: tricycle, tripod, triangle) and identify how knowing the root helps spell and define multiple words
  • Research the word families of key vocabulary from a current cross-curriculum unit and use family knowledge to spell related forms

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyMorphological family knowledge is accumulative — each new family learned expands the range of words a student can spell and define. Students benefit from ongoing collection across curriculum contexts.
