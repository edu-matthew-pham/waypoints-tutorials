Spelling generalisations — double letters and affixes

AC9E4LY10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and apply spelling generalisations including rules for doubling letters when adding suffixes.

Success criteria

  • Can correctly apply a doubling rule (e.g. begin → beginning) and name the rule
  • Can apply a drop-e rule (e.g. hope → hoping) and name the rule

Prior knowledge

Students apply knowledge of base words, prefixes and suffixes to read and comprehend multimorphemic words (AC9E3LY10). They have not yet used spelling generalisations, morphological word families and word origins as systematic strategies for spelling more complex words.

Width

Minimum
Apply one spelling generalisation (e.g. doubling the final consonant before -ing: begin → beginning) to at least three words correctly.
Typical
Apply a range of spelling generalisations — doubling rules, drop-e rules, change-y-to-i rules — across a range of words when adding common affixes, explaining the rule in each case.
Wider
  • Investigate which spelling generalisations have exceptions and explore why (e.g. words of Latin origin may follow different doubling patterns)
  • Apply spelling generalisations systematically when editing own writing, developing a check habit

Hinge

Building word families in N2 requires correct application of suffixing rules (doubling, drop-e) — without N1's generalisations, students produce spelling errors when inflecting or deriving words within families.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSpelling generalisations are procedural rules that require practice across many word examples before they become reliable. Understanding a rule does not guarantee consistent application without sustained practice.
