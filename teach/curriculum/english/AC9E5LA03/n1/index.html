← AC9E5LA03 Text stages and phases

Stages serve purpose

AC9E5LA03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the characteristic stages of familiar text types and explain how each stage contributes to the text's overall purpose.

Success criteria

  • Labels the stages of a given text type correctly and explains the communicative function of each stage.
  • Explains why removing or reordering a stage would disrupt the text's purpose.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how texts across the curriculum have different language features and are organised for different purposes, and identify some characteristic stages (AC9E4LA03). They have not yet described the internal phase structure within stages, or compared how the same genre adapts across spoken, written and multimodal modes.

Width

Minimum
Identify and label the stages of at least two text types (e.g. narrative, information report) and explain what communicative work each stage does.
Typical
Map stages across a range of text types including narrative, procedure, argument, explanation, and information report. For each, explain how the sequence of stages builds toward the text's purpose, and why stages are ordered as they are.
Wider
  • Compare how the same text type (e.g. argument) is staged differently across spoken, written and multimodal forms, and explain what the structural differences reveal about audience and context
  • Examine texts where conventional stage ordering is disrupted (e.g. in medias res narrative, or delayed thesis argument) and discuss the effect of that disruption

Hinge

Stage identification and purpose-function explanation is prerequisite for Node 2's phase analysis and Node 3's language-feature mapping. Students who cannot identify and explain stages cannot locate phases within them, and cannot reason about why particular language features cluster in particular structural positions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStage identification across multiple text types requires practice to become reliable. Students who have mapped only narrative stages will apply narrative assumptions to argument or explanation text structures. Consolidation through deliberate practice across text types builds the cross-genre stage recognition that Node 2 and Node 3 depend on.
