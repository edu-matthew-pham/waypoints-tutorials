Text stages and phases

AC9E5LA03 · Year 5 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Describe how spoken, written and multimodal texts use language features and are typically organised into characteristic stages and phases, depending on purposes in texts

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how texts across the curriculum have different language features and are organised for different purposes, and identify some characteristic stages (AC9E4LA03). They have not yet described the internal phase structure within stages, or compared how the same genre adapts across spoken, written and multimodal modes.

By the end

Students describe the characteristic stages and phases of a range of text types — including narrative, procedure, argument, explanation, discussion and information report — explaining how language features within each stage serve the text's purpose, and how these structures adapt across spoken, written and multimodal modes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • becoming familiar with the typical stages and language features of text types such as narrative, procedure, arguments, explanation, discussion and informative texts, and how they can be composed in written, digital and multimedia forms
  • recognising that paragraphs vary in their function and how they are organised in a text and between different types of texts
  • describing the stages and phases, and purposes of narratives, historical recounts, procedural recounts, causal explanations, discussions, information reports, reviews and types of poems

Waypoints

  • 1. Stages serve purposeHingeStudents can identify the characteristic stages of familiar text types and explain how each stage contributes to the text's overall purpose.
  • 2. Phases within stagesHingeStudents can identify phases within stages and explain how phases build the communicative work of the stage they belong to.
  • 3. Language features signal structureStudents can identify specific language features used in different stages and phases, and explain how those features serve the communicative purpose of that structural position.
  • 4. Describing texts across modesStudents can describe how the same genre structures adapt across spoken, written and multimodal modes, identifying what changes and what remains consistent.
