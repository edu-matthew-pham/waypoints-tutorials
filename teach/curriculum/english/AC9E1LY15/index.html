Grammatical morphemes and word families

AC9E1LY15 · Year 1 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise and know how to use grammatical morphemes to create word families

Prior knowledge

Students can read and write an increasing number of high-frequency words (AC9E1LY14). At Foundation level students understand that words can be made of more than one meaningful part (AC9EFLY15). They have not yet systematically used grammatical morphemes — inflectional endings such as -s, -ed, -ing, -er — to generate and recognise word families.

By the end

Students recognise and use grammatical morphemes to create and read word families.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • building word families from common morphemes; for example, 'play', 'plays', 'playing', 'played', 'playground'
  • using morphemes to read words; for example, by recognising the base word in words such as 'walk-ed'

Waypoints

  • 1. Morphemes carry meaningStudents can identify that words can be made of more than one part (base word + morpheme) and that each part contributes meaning.
  • 2. Common grammatical morphemesHingeStudents can recognise and use common grammatical morphemes — -s, -es, -ed, -ing, -er — to create word family members.
  • 3. Using morphemes to read and spellStudents can use morpheme knowledge to read unfamiliar words by recognising base words, and to spell by applying morpheme addition.
