← AC9E1LY15 Grammatical morphemes and word families

Using morphemes to read and spell

AC9E1LY15 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 1 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use morpheme knowledge to read unfamiliar words by recognising base words, and to spell by applying morpheme addition.

Success criteria

  • Student reads a morphologically complex word by identifying the base word
  • Student correctly spells a word formed by adding a familiar morpheme to a known base

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Common grammatical morphemes.

Width

Minimum
Read an unfamiliar word by identifying the base word inside it; spell at least two words formed by adding a morpheme to a known base.
Typical
Apply morpheme knowledge to read unfamiliar words — recognising the base word and then the morpheme — and to spell words formed by adding known morphemes to known base words.
Wider
  • Discuss what happens to some base words when morphemes are added — e.g. 'hop' → 'hopped' (doubling) — at awareness level only
  • Use morpheme knowledge to self-correct reading errors — identify whether the base word was read correctly

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierUsing morphemes as a reading and spelling tool integrates knowledge of base words and morpheme function into an active decoding and encoding strategy — the endpoint of this strand's progression at Year 1.
