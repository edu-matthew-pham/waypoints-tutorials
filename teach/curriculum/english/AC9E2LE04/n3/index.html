Experimenting with new patterns

AC9E2LE04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 2 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can experiment with creating their own rhythmic sound and word patterns — inventing new words, changing syllables, substituting sounds — to produce a poem, chant or rhyming sequence.

Success criteria

  • Student produces an original rhythmic sequence with an identifiable pattern.
  • Student can explain what sound or word pattern they used and what effect they were aiming for.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Reproducing sound patterns.

Width

Minimum
Create a short original rhythmic sequence (at least two lines) that uses a deliberate sound or word pattern.
Typical
Experiment with syllable manipulation, invented words and sound substitution to create original rhythmic sequences; explore how poets use word sounds to create effects for an audience.
Wider
  • Create a new verse for an existing poem or chant that maintains the original rhythmic pattern
  • Invent nonsense words using known sound patterns and discuss how sound can create meaning even when words are invented

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExperimenting with sound patterns benefits from an explicit creative reasoning scaffold — what pattern am I using, what sound effect am I going for, does my line fit the pattern? Making this process visible helps students move from accidental to deliberate sound play.
