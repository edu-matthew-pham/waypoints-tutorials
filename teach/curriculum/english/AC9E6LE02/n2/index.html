← AC9E6LE02 Comparing literary texts on similar topics

How differences influence readers

AC9E6LE02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how differences in narrator, narrative structure, voice, and language and visual features between texts influence readers or viewers.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one difference in narrator, voice or narrative structure between two compared texts
  • Explains how that difference influences the reader's interpretation or experience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying shared and different features.

Width

Minimum
Identify one difference in narrator or voice between two texts on a similar topic and explain how that difference changes the reader's experience.
Typical
Analyse how differences in the use of narrator, narrative structure, voice, and language and visual features between two texts influence how readers interpret the topic or theme; explain which features best serve the texts' respective purposes.
Wider
  • Analyse how the choice of first-person vs. third-person narrator shapes reader identification with characters across two texts on the same theme
  • Compare how visual features in picture books vs. chapter books on the same topic influence younger vs. older reader experiences

Hinge

Students who can only identify differences without connecting them to reader effect cannot form an evaluative preference in Node 3 — the evaluation requires the difference→effect reasoning chain established here.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMoving from 'these texts are different' to 'this difference produces this reader effect' requires a reasoning scaffold. Students often identify differences without connecting them to audience experience — the reasoning chain needs to be made explicit.
