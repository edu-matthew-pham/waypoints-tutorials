← AC9E10LE08 Create and edit literary texts with sustained voice

Selecting and adapting text structures and literary devices

AC9E10LE08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select and adapt text structures and literary devices for a specific purpose and audience, explaining their choices.

Success criteria

  • Makes at least two deliberate choices of text structure or literary device for a specific purpose
  • Can explain the intended effect of each choice on the intended audience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Constructing and sustaining a voice.

Width

Minimum
In a literary text, make and describe at least two deliberate choices of text structure or literary device for a specific purpose, with an explanation of the effect intended.
Typical
Create and edit a literary text that demonstrates deliberate selection and adaptation of text structures, literary devices, and language and visual features for a specific purpose and audience, with reflection on choices made.
Wider
  • Experiment with adapting aesthetic features encountered in mentor texts into own writing, explaining what was borrowed and how it was adapted
  • Create a literary text for a specific real audience (school community, competition) and reflect on how purpose and audience shaped every structural and device choice
  • Create a literary text that incorporates or responds to a First Nations Australian text, reflecting on the choices required to do so responsibly

Hinge

Node 3 (editing) requires students to evaluate and revise their structural and device choices — without having made deliberate choices in Node 2, there is nothing purposeful to edit or reflect on.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to develop the habit of reasoning from purpose and audience backward to structural and device choices — moving from 'I want to create X effect' to 'therefore I will use Y structure/device'; this reasoning chain is the core capability move here.
