Planning — structure and content

AC9E4LY06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 4 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a written or multimodal text by selecting appropriate structure, grouping ideas into logical sections and deciding on key vocabulary.

Success criteria

  • Plan includes identified text type structure with at least two sections
  • Plan includes key vocabulary noted before writing begins

Prior knowledge

Students plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal texts using visual features, paragraphs and topic vocabulary (AC9E3LY06). They have not yet planned and created texts using complex sentences, linked ideas, synonyms/antonyms, multisyllabic word spelling and simple punctuation across all three text types.

Width

Minimum
Create a plan for a text that identifies the text type structure, groups ideas into at least two sections and notes key vocabulary.
Typical
Plan texts across all three text types — imaginative, informative and persuasive — selecting appropriate structure for each, organising ideas into logical paragraph groups and planning vocabulary including synonyms and precise terms.
Wider
  • Use digital planning tools (mind-mapping, outline software) to organise and restructure ideas before writing, comparing the outcome with a handwritten plan
  • Plan a multimodal text, considering how visual and verbal elements will work together at each stage of the text

Hinge

Creating with complex sentences and linked ideas in N2 requires a planned text structure — without a plan, students cannot make or evaluate deliberate organisational choices when drafting.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPlanning across three text types requires explicit reasoning about how structure serves purpose — selecting between narrative, report and argument structures is not intuitive at Year 4 without a scaffolded decision process.
