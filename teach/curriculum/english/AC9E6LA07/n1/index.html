← AC9E6LA07 Images, figures, tables, diagrams, maps and graphs

Sequential visual representations

AC9E6LA07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how sequential visual representations (timelines, flowcharts, procedure diagrams, life-cycle diagrams) represent order and process.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the type of sequential visual representation and names the type of information it conveys
  • Explains at least one way the visual contributes to meaning differently from the accompanying written text

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how the sequence of images in print, digital and film texts has an effect on meaning (AC9E5LA07). They have not yet examined how a range of non-narrative visual representations (tables, diagrams, maps, graphs, flowcharts) contribute to meaning in informational and procedural texts.

Width

Minimum
Explain how one type of sequential visual representation (e.g. a flowchart or procedure diagram) represents order or process in a provided text; identify what information it adds that the written text alone does not.
Typical
Identify and explain how a range of sequential visual representations (timelines, flowcharts, procedure diagrams, life-cycle diagrams, photo stories) convey sequence and process; compare how different sequential types suit different purposes.
Wider
  • Create a visual sequential representation (flowchart, timeline or diagram) for a curriculum-area process and evaluate whether it adds meaning more efficiently than prose
  • Compare how comic strips and scientific procedure diagrams both use sequential images — and identify what is the same and what differs in their conventions
