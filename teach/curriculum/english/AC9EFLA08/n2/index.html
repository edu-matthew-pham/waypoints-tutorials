← AC9EFLA08 Vocabulary in familiar contexts

Vocabulary grows through experience

AC9EFLA08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify multiple ways of encountering and learning new vocabulary — through interactions, texts, images, objects and play.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify at least two different ways they have encountered new vocabulary.
  • Student can use a recently acquired vocabulary word appropriately in speech or in a text they are creating.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Topics have words.

Width

Minimum
Identify one new word encountered in class and explain where or how they came across it.
Typical
Recognise that vocabulary can be expanded through informal interactions, planned experiences, texts, images, artefacts and objects; draw on vocabulary encountered in multiple ways — including imaginative play — to use words in context.
Wider
  • Investigate how role-play activities generate new vocabulary — comparing words used in different dramatic contexts (shopkeeper, scientist, storyteller)
  • Explore vocabulary from different cultural contexts in the class — including words from First Nations Australian languages or other community languages
  • Compare vocabulary encountered through visual texts (picture books, films) with vocabulary encountered through oral interaction and real objects

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents may think vocabulary grows only through 'word learning lessons'. Multiple sources — play, objects, images, community language — help build the broader understanding that vocabulary is encountered everywhere.
