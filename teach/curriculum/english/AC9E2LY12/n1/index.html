← AC9E2LY12 Prefixes, suffixes and word families

What prefixes and suffixes do

AC9E2LY12 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that prefixes and suffixes change the meaning or grammatical function of a base word and give at least one example of each.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify a prefix or suffix and name the base word.
  • Student can explain what the prefix or suffix adds to the base word's meaning or grammatical function.

Prior knowledge

Students use grammatical morphemes to create and recognise word families (AC9E1LY15). They have not yet systematically built word families using a range of named prefixes and suffixes.

Width

Minimum
Identify a prefix or suffix on a given word and explain what it adds to the base word (e.g. 'un-' changes meaning; '-ing' changes tense).
Typical
Identify prefixes and suffixes across a range of words, explain what each adds, and distinguish between meaning-changing prefixes (un-, re-) and grammatical suffixes (-ing, -ed, -er, -est).
Wider
  • Compare words with the same prefix or suffix across multiple examples and discuss what pattern of meaning the prefix/suffix creates
  • Identify prefixes and suffixes encountered in learning area vocabulary and discuss how knowing the affix helps with word meaning

Hinge

Understanding that prefixes and suffixes are separable, meaningful elements is conceptually prerequisite for Node 2 — students who see affixes as arbitrary word-parts cannot systematically build word families by adding or removing them.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often think 'un-' or '-ing' are just part of the word, not added elements. Multiple examples across different base words — showing that the same prefix changes different base words in the same way — resolves the misconception that affixes are arbitrary. More base word examples, not more explanation of one, is productive.
