Intertextual connections and interpretation

AC9E10LE05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how intertextual connections shape interpretations of a text, identifying how references to other texts or traditions enrich or complicate meaning.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one intertextual connection in a text
  • Analyses how the connection shapes interpretation of the text

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How language devices layer meaning.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one intertextual connection in a text and analyse how it shapes interpretation of the text.
Typical
Analyse how intertextual connections across a text — to other literary works, traditions, genres or cultural references — shape how the text can be interpreted, examining what knowledge is required to activate those connections.
Wider
  • Examine how satirical texts use intertextual references to shape interpretation and what happens to interpretation when readers lack the referenced knowledge
  • Analyse how First Nations Australian literary texts create intertextual connections to traditional narratives and evaluate how those connections shape meaning differently for different readers
  • Compare how two texts that reference the same canonical source use that intertextual connection for different interpretive purposes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepIntertextual analysis requires students to understand that interpretation is reader-positioned — different readers activate different intertextual connections; encountering texts with diverse intertextual references makes this visible.
