← AC9E9LY04 Evaluate idea organisation and its impact

Techniques for organising ideas

AC9E9LY04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify organisational techniques used in texts and explain what each technique does to the reader's experience of the ideas.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student names an organisational technique and explains its function (not just describes the content sequence)
  • Student identifies how the technique shapes what the reader experiences — not just what information is present

Prior knowledge

Students explain how ideas are structured using taxonomies, cause and effect, metaphors and chronology (AC9E7LY04). They have not yet evaluated the impact of organisational choices on meaning — moving from describing organisation to assessing its effectiveness.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two organisational techniques in a text (e.g. juxtaposition, chronological reversal, exaggeration) and explain what each does to the arrangement of ideas.
Typical
Analyse the techniques used in extended texts to organise ideas — examining comparison, contrast, exaggeration, juxtaposition, chronological manipulation, and expansion/compression of time.
Wider
  • Compare how two texts covering the same event use different organisational techniques and assess which arrangement is more effective for their respective purposes
  • Examine how a text's organisation creates emotional effects — analysing how the sequence of information is designed to evoke particular responses

Hinge

Evaluating impact at N2 requires named technique types — students without a repertoire of organisation techniques evaluate by intuition rather than evidence, which means the N2 evaluative judgement cannot be substantiated.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a repertoire of organisational technique names and functions to analyse organisation at the level of choice rather than just sequence. Building this repertoire across diverse text types enables the evaluative work at node 2.
