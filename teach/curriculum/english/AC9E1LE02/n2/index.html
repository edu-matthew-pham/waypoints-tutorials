Connecting text to personal experience

AC9E1LE02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 1 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make an explicit connection between something in a literary text and their own experience, and explain how the connection helps them understand the text.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student states an explicit connection between something in the text and their own experience
  • Student explains how the connection helps them understand the character, event or feeling

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Responding to characters and events.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one connection between a text and personal experience and explain what it helps them understand.
Typical
Make text-to-self connections across a range of literary texts, explaining how personal experience helps them understand characters, events or feelings depicted in the text.
Wider
  • Discuss what happens when a text depicts an experience the reader has not had — how do they make sense of it?
  • Compare personal connections made by different readers in a class discussion — explore why different things connect for different people

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyMaking text-to-self connections is a transferable strategy that students build through repeated practice across varied texts. The more varied the texts and experiences engaged with, the more robust the strategy becomes.
