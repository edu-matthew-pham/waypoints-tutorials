Formality as a continuum

AC9E6LA01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that language exists along a continuum from informal to formal and name the key registers (slang, colloquial, conversational, formal).

Success criteria

  • Names and correctly applies the four register labels (slang, colloquial, conversational, formal) to provided examples
  • Identifies at least one language feature (e.g. vocabulary choice, contraction use) that signals register

Prior knowledge

Students understand that language is selected for social contexts and that it helps to signal social roles and relationships (AC9E5LA01). They have not yet examined how formality and social distance interact as a continuum, or how formal address protocols function at institutional or ceremonial levels.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three points on the formality continuum using provided examples; label each as slang, colloquial, conversational or formal.
Typical
Discuss and sort a range of spoken and written examples across the full continuum; explain what features (vocabulary, sentence structure, tone) signal each register.
Wider
  • Compare the same message expressed across all four registers (e.g. a request for help) and analyse how meaning and relationship signal shift
  • Examine how digital communication (texts, emails, social media posts) creates new registers that blend formal and informal features

Hinge

Students who conflate 'polite' with 'formal' or treat formality as binary cannot correctly reason about social distance in Node 2 — the two-variable model breaks down without a working continuum.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often conflate 'polite' with 'formal' or assume formality is binary. Exposure to a range of register examples across spoken and written modes helps students build a more nuanced continuum model.
