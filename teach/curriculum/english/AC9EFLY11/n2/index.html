Letters represent sounds

AC9EFLY11 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the most common sound represented by each letter.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can produce the most common sound for each letter of the alphabet.
  • Student can connect a letter to its sound when prompted by either the letter name or the letter form.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Letters have names.

Width

Minimum
Produce the most common sound for at least fifteen letters.
Typical
Know the most common sound each letter represents; connect letter names to letter sounds; apply letter-sound knowledge in the context of simple reading and spelling.
Wider
  • Identify the most common sound for all twenty-six letters, then explore that some letters have more than one common sound (e.g. 'c' in 'cat' vs 'city')
  • Connect letter-sound knowledge to words encountered in reading — applying the knowledge in context rather than isolation
  • Explore sounds in other languages — noting that the same letter may represent different sounds in different languages

Hinge

Letter-sound associations are the prerequisite for all phonic decoding in LY04, LY12 and LY13 — without automatic letter-to-sound retrieval, students cannot blend or segment, and reading and spelling both fail at the letter level.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsLetter-sound associations require practice to automaticity — slow retrieval directly limits decoding speed. The 'most common sound' must be the automatic first response before alternative sounds are introduced.
