Letters have names

AC9EFLY11 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and name most upper- and lower-case letters when shown them.

Success criteria

  • Student can name all twenty-six upper-case letters.
  • Student can name all twenty-six lower-case letters.
  • Student can match upper- and lower-case letter pairs.

Prior knowledge

Students may recognise letters in their own name and in familiar texts. They have not yet been explicitly taught all upper- and lower-case letter names and their most common associated sounds.

Width

Minimum
Recognise and name at least fifteen lower-case letters.
Typical
Recognise and name all upper- and lower-case letters; match upper- and lower-case pairs; apply letter naming in the context of familiar words and texts.
Wider
  • Recognise letters across a range of fonts and handwriting styles — understanding that the same letter takes different visual forms
  • Identify letters in environmental print, books, signs and digital texts — building multi-context letter recognition
  • Compare the English alphabet with other alphabetic writing systems — noting similarities and differences

Hinge

Letter name knowledge is a prerequisite for letter-sound learning in Node 2 — without reliable letter identification, students cannot associate sounds with specific letters, and phonics instruction cannot proceed effectively.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsComplete and automatic letter name knowledge is a prerequisite for phonics instruction — uncertain letter names slow decoding and letter-sound learning. Practice to automaticity is required.
