Re-reading to edit

AC9E1LY06 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 1 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can re-read their own writing to identify and fix errors — adding words for meaning, correcting spelling, checking punctuation.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student re-reads their text and makes at least one deliberate edit
  • Student can explain what they changed and why

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Topic vocabulary and punctuation.

Width

Minimum
Re-read a piece of their own writing and make at least one change that improves it.
Typical
Re-read writing to edit for meaning (adding or deleting words), spelling (using resources to check words they are unsure of), and punctuation, making concrete improvements.
Wider
  • Discuss what to look for when editing — develop a simple personal editing checklist
  • Compare a first draft with an edited version and describe the improvements made

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkRe-reading to edit requires a deliberate and sequenced checking routine — students need to learn to read their own writing as a reader, not just a writer. A simple scaffold (Does it make sense? Are words spelt right? Does each sentence end with a mark?) supports the habit.
