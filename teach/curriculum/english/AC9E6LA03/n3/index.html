← AC9E6LA03 Text structure: stages, phases and adaptation

Author adaptation of text structures

AC9E6LA03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and explain when and why authors adapt or subvert the characteristic stages and language features of a text type.

Lessons on this site

  • Mixing text types

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific structural adaptation in a text and names what the conventional structure would be
  • Explains what effect the adaptation creates for the reader

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Characteristic structures across text types.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example where an author has adapted a typical text structure (e.g. a narrative that opens with a flashback rather than a scene-setting introduction) and explain the effect.
Typical
Analyse at least two examples of authors adapting text structures or language features across different text types; explain what effect the adaptation creates and why an author might choose it.
Wider
  • Compare two texts of the same type by different authors and evaluate how structural adaptations shape audience experience or argument
  • Create a short text that deliberately adapts a typical structural feature and explain the choice in an author's note

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkRecognising adaptation requires holding the conventional structure in mind simultaneously with the actual structure — a metacognitive comparison move. Scaffolding the reasoning ('What is the typical structure? What has changed? What effect does that create?') prevents students from either accepting deviation as arbitrary or missing it entirely.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates knowledge of stages (Node 1), knowledge of characteristic structures across text types (Node 2), and applies both to the analytic question of intentional adaptation.
