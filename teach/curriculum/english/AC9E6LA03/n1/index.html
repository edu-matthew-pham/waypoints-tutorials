Stages and phases within texts

AC9E6LA03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that texts are organised into stages and that stages can be further divided into phases, and name the stages and phases in a familiar text type.

Lessons on this site

  • Stages and phases

Success criteria

  • Names the stages of at least one text type accurately
  • Identifies at least one phase within a stage and explains its purpose

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how spoken, written and multimodal texts use language features and are typically organised into stages for different purposes (AC9E5LA03). They have not yet examined the internal phase structure within stages, or the ways authors deliberately adapt typical structures.

Width

Minimum
Identify the stages of one familiar text type (e.g. narrative) and name at least one phase within a stage (e.g. the hook as a phase within the introduction).
Typical
Examine a range of text types (narrative, review, discussion, historical recount) and identify their stages; identify how introductions and other stages may be divided into phases with distinct purposes.
Wider
  • Map the stage and phase structure of a film or picture book narrative, comparing visual and written structural signals
  • Compare stage and phase structures across curriculum areas (e.g. a science report vs a history recount) and identify shared and distinct structural features

Hinge

Students who cannot identify stages across a range of text types arrive at Node 2 without the structural vocabulary needed to compare characteristic structures — N2 presupposes that stage/phase concepts are stable across text types.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate examples of stage/phase structures across multiple text types before the underlying concept of structural organisation becomes generalisable. Exposure to a range of text types — not just narrative — builds the repertoire needed to recognise and explain the concept across the curriculum.
