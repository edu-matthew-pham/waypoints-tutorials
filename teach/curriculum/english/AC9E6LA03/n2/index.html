← AC9E6LA03 Text structure: stages, phases and adaptation

Characteristic structures across text types

AC9E6LA03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the characteristic stages and phases of multiple text types including reviews, discussions and historical recounts.

Success criteria

  • Names and explains the characteristic stages of at least two text types, including one non-narrative type
  • Identifies at least one language feature associated with a specific stage in a text type

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Stages and phases within texts.

Width

Minimum
Identify and explain the characteristic stages of at least two different text types, including at least one from outside narrative (e.g. review or discussion).
Typical
Explain characteristic stages and phases across the range of text types encountered in curriculum — including reviews, discussions of alternative positions, historical recounts, and narratives; identify language features that accompany each stage.
Wider
  • Examine a range of curriculum-area texts (science, history, geography) and explain how structural stages serve the discipline's purposes
  • Investigate how humour texts use stage structures differently — for example, how exaggeration functions within a structural stage

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents typically build strong understanding of narrative structure but underestimate the structural regularity of non-narrative texts. Extending across reviews, discussions and recounts prevents the misconception that text structure is primarily a literary concern.
