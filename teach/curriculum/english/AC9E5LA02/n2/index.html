Tempering moves: distancing and recruiting support

AC9E5LA02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain how distancing language (impersonal it, modal verbs) and recruited support (anonymous or general sourcing) change the persuasive character of an assertion.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two distinct tempering moves in a text and names what type of move each represents.
  • Explains how at least one tempering move changes the relationship between the writer, the claim, and the reader.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What makes a bare assertion.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two tempering moves (e.g. 'It could be said...' or 'Most people agree...') in a sample text and explain how each changes the claim's relationship to the reader.
Typical
Identify and explain the full range of distancing and recruiting-support moves from the elaboration examples. Analyse how each move positions the writer in relation to the claim and the reader, and discuss when each move is more or less appropriate.
Wider
  • Compare how academic writing, journalism, and everyday persuasive writing use these tempering moves differently, and explain what those differences signal about each genre's conventions
  • Examine political speeches or editorials and identify where and why writers choose to distance themselves from or recruit support for claims, discussing the rhetorical effect

Hinge

Node 3 requires students to compare the persuasive weight of anonymous versus specific sourcing — a comparison that only makes sense against a shared understanding of why sourcing matters at all. Students who have not grasped that distancing and recruited support are rhetorical moves with distinct effects cannot reason about the elevated credibility that named authoritative sourcing adds.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly treat all tempering moves as equivalent ('they all just say you're not sure'). Encountering the moves across different genres and contexts helps students see that each move signals a distinct relationship between writer, claim, and evidence — distancing vs. sourcing serve different rhetorical purposes.
