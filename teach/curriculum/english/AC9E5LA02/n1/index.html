What makes a bare assertion

AC9E5LA02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify a bare assertion and explain why it is insufficient as persuasive or informative language in contexts that require evidence or qualification.

Lessons on this site

  • Claims and how sure

Success criteria

  • Identifies a bare assertion in a sample text and explains what is missing (evidence, sourcing, qualification).
  • Distinguishes between a bare assertion and a qualified or evidenced claim in at least two examples.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify subjective language of opinion and feeling, and objective language of factual reporting (AC9E4LA02). They have not yet examined the rhetorical mechanisms for tempering assertions — distancing language, sourcing, generalising support — or how these choices affect a claim's persuasive weight.

Width

Minimum
Identify examples of bare assertions in texts and explain what is absent — evidence, qualification, or sourcing — that would make them more persuasive or reliable.
Typical
Distinguish bare assertions from qualified or evidenced claims across a range of texts. Explain why bare assertions may not convince a reader and what a reader might need before accepting the claim.
Wider
  • Examine advertising or social media posts where bare assertions function as persuasive technique, discussing why audiences accept or reject them
  • Limited width — this node is a conceptual foundation (what a bare assertion is) that does not vary meaningfully across contexts at this year band. Extensions connect to how bare assertions function rhetorically, explored in later nodes.

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to identify and explain tempering moves specifically as responses to what bare assertions lack — distancing compensates for unsubstantiated confidence, sourcing compensates for anonymity. Students who cannot identify what makes an assertion bare cannot analyse why or how specific tempering moves address those gaps.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStudents often confuse opinion-statements with bare assertions, and may not recognise that a claim can be factual but still function as a bare assertion if unsupported. Consolidation through multiple examples helps students develop reliable identification before moving to the tempering moves.
