← AC9E7LA06 Tense consistency

Consistency of tense for clarity

AC9E7LA06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can understand how maintaining tense consistency across a sentence or passage achieves clarity, and identify where inconsistency creates confusion

Lessons on this site

  • Keep the tense steady
  • Challenge: rework the report

Success criteria

  • Identifies a tense inconsistency in a given sentence or passage
  • Explains why the inconsistency creates confusion for the reader

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Verb tense forms.

Width

Minimum
Given a sentence or short passage with a tense inconsistency, identify the error and explain why it creates confusion for the reader
Typical
Examine how tense is maintained across compound, complex and compound-complex sentences; identify intentional tense shifts (e.g. flashback, quoted speech) and distinguish them from errors
Wider
  • Examine how tense consistency operates differently in different text types — narrative (past tense maintained), explanation (present tense maintained), and mixed texts where intentional tense shifts occur
  • Discuss cases where tense inconsistency is deliberate — stream of consciousness, historical present — and explain how context signals intentionality

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTense inconsistency in student writing is a high-frequency error that responds to practice rather than broader contexts. Students understand the concept but lose tense tracking in longer sentences and passages. Sustained practice identifying and correcting inconsistency across varied sentence structures is the remedy.
