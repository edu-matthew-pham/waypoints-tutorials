← AC9E2LY04 Read with fluency and self-correct

Using phonic and word knowledge to decode

AC9E2LY04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 2 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use phonic (sound-letter) and morphemic knowledge, and knowledge of high-frequency words, to decode unfamiliar words during reading.

Success criteria

  • Student applies a decoding strategy when encountering an unfamiliar word rather than stopping or guessing randomly.
  • Student correctly decodes a range of word types using appropriate strategies.

Prior knowledge

Students read texts with developing phrasing and fluency, monitoring meaning using context and grammar (AC9E1LY04). They have not yet self-corrected systematically using phonic and word knowledge, or re-read specifically to restore lost meaning.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least one decoding strategy — phonic, morphemic or sight word — when encountering an unfamiliar word in a text.
Typical
Use phonic knowledge, morphemic knowledge and high-frequency word recognition in combination when decoding, selecting the most appropriate strategy for different word types.
Wider
  • Identify which decoding strategy was most useful for a given unfamiliar word and explain why
  • Read texts with increasing complexity of word patterns — compound words, silent letters, vowel digraphs — applying appropriate decoding strategies

Hinge

Automatic decoding is a prerequisite for Nodes 2 and 3 — students who cannot decode without conscious effort cannot allocate attention to monitoring meaning or self-correcting. Decoding bottlenecks collapse the monitoring and self-correction work that follows.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsReliable decoding requires fluency — automatic application of phonic and word knowledge without slowing comprehension. Practice across multiple reading contexts builds the automaticity this node requires. More reading practice is more productive than widening decoding strategy types at this stage.
