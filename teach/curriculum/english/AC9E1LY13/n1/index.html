Spelling one-syllable words with known patterns

AC9E1LY13 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 1 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can spell one-syllable words using known letter patterns — including short vowels, consonant blends and digraphs.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly spells at least five one-syllable words with known patterns
  • Student can apply known patterns to spell a new, unfamiliar one-syllable word

Prior knowledge

Students can understand that letters represent multiple sounds and that syllables contain vowel sounds (AC9E1LY12). They have not yet applied this knowledge to spell a range of one- and two-syllable words using common letter patterns systematically.

Width

Minimum
Correctly spell at least five one-syllable words containing known letter patterns (e.g. short vowel, blend).
Typical
Spell a range of one-syllable words using known letter patterns — CVC, CVCC, CCVC, digraphs — applying phoneme-grapheme knowledge to spell previously unwritten words.
Wider
  • Generate and spell multiple words from the same pattern family (e.g. words ending in -st, -nd, -ng)
  • Apply spelling knowledge to write one-syllable topic vocabulary in content area writing

Hinge

Reliable one-syllable spelling is the foundation for the two-syllable strategy in N2 — students who cannot spell single syllables accurately cannot apply the 'spell each part' strategy to longer words.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSpelling requires more precision than reading — students can recognise correctly spelled words they cannot yet produce. Consolidation through varied writing contexts builds reliable spelling production.
