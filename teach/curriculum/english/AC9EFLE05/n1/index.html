← AC9EFLE05 Retell and adapt familiar texts

Retelling maintains sequence

AC9EFLE05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can retell key events from a familiar story in the correct sequence.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can retell at least three events from a familiar story in the correct order.
  • Student's retelling includes the beginning and ending of the story.

Prior knowledge

Students have encountered and enjoyed literary texts. They have not yet been explicitly asked to retell a story in sequence or to adapt one — changing elements while maintaining its recognisable structure.

Width

Minimum
Retell at least three events from a familiar story in order — orally, through drawing, or through sequenced pictures.
Typical
Retell familiar literary texts in sequence using oral retelling, drawings or sequenced pictures; include key characters and events; maintain the story's beginning, middle and end.
Wider
  • Retell the same story through two different modes — oral and sequenced pictures — and compare what each mode captures
  • Use digital tools to sequence images for a retelling and share with the class
  • Retell a First Nations Australian story with attention to its cultural sequence of events

Hinge

Sequential retelling establishes the structural knowledge of the story that adaptation in Node 3 depends on — students cannot adapt a story while maintaining its recognisable structure if they have not yet internalised that structure through accurate retelling.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSequencing events correctly is a foundational narrative literacy skill — precision matters, and errors (missing events, wrong sequence) propagate into later story writing. Practice across multiple stories builds the habit.
