Paragraphs in narrative texts

AC9E3LA04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise that paragraphs in narrative texts vary in length and serve different purposes — they do not always follow a predictable pattern formula.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies that narrative paragraphs vary in length
  • Student explains one example of why a paragraph starts or ends at a particular point in a narrative

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Paragraphs in informative texts.

Width

Minimum
Identify paragraphs in a narrative text and notice that they vary in length, explaining one example of why a short or long paragraph is used at a particular point.
Typical
Recognise that narrative paragraphs vary in length and purpose — some build tension, some shift scene, some develop character — and do not always follow a topic-sentence pattern. Examine models of effective narrative paragraphing.
Wider
  • Compare how a very short paragraph (even one sentence) is used for effect in a narrative versus a long descriptive paragraph — discuss what each achieves
  • Experiment with changing the paragraph breaks in a narrative extract and evaluate how it changes the reading experience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents taught the topic-sentence rule may misapply it to narrative. Seeing that narrative paragraphing follows different logic — and that variation itself is a tool — prevents the misconception that all paragraphs work the same way.
