Language, expression and gesture work together

AC9E1LA01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 1 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how language choices, facial expressions and gestures combine to shape how an interaction is received by others.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student gives an example showing how body language and words together affect how a message is received
  • Student explains, for at least one interaction type, how changing the gesture or expression would change the meaning

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Open and closed questions.

Width

Minimum
Describe how a speaker's words and body language together affect how their message is received in at least one interaction type.
Typical
Explain how the combination of language, facial expression and gesture shapes meaning across multiple interaction types, using examples from texts or familiar situations.
Wider
  • Discuss how the same verbal request can be received differently depending on facial expression and tone — e.g. a polite request vs an impatient demand
  • Explore representations of interaction in literature by First Nations Australian authors, discussing how visual and verbal elements together create meaning

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to coordinate their understanding of verbal and non-verbal modes together — it's not adding new content but assembling the components from nodes 1–3 into a unified explanation of how communication works.
