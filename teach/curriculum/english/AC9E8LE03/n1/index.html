How texts position readers

AC9E8LE03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that specific language and image choices in a text are designed to guide readers toward particular responses and viewpoints — not as neutral description but as positioning.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific language or image choice in a text.
  • Explains how that choice is designed to guide the reader toward a particular response or viewpoint, rather than describing the choice neutrally.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how language choices and visual features shape reader responses (AC9E7LE03). They have not yet explained the positioning function systematically — how specific language and image choices lead readers toward particular responses and viewpoints rather than others.

Width

Minimum
Explain how at least two specific language or image choices in a provided text are designed to guide the reader toward a particular response or viewpoint.
Typical
Explain how language choices (e.g. word choice, tone, narrative perspective, figurative language) and/or visual features in texts work together to position readers to respond in particular ways and form specific viewpoints.
Wider
  • Analyse how a complex picture book combines words and images to position readers to respond emotionally or morally to a character
  • Compare how two texts on the same subject use different language and visual choices to position readers toward opposing viewpoints on that subject
  • Examine how First Nations or multicultural authors use positioning strategies to invite non-Indigenous or monocultural readers to adopt unfamiliar perspectives

Hinge

Node 2 applies the positioning concept to film and multimodal texts. Students who have not established the critical stance — that specific choices are designed to guide responses — will approach film analysis as plot summary or personal reaction rather than as analysis of design choices. The conceptual shift must be established in print before it can travel to multimodal contexts.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of 'positioning' requires students to adopt a critical stance toward texts that usually feel transparent — to ask 'why is this word chosen rather than another?' Explicit scaffolding of the from-effect-to-cause reasoning is needed: notice the effect first, then reverse-engineer the choices that created it.
