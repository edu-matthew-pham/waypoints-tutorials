Positioning in film and multimodal texts

AC9E8LE03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how film and multimodal texts position viewers to respond to characters and events through the interaction of language, image and other modalities.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two specific choices in a film or multimodal text (e.g. camera angle, word choice, colour, music) and explains how each positions the viewer.
  • Explains the cumulative effect of these choices on the viewer's response to a character or event.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How texts position readers.

Width

Minimum
Share an opinion about how a film positions the viewer to respond to at least one character, identifying the specific choices (camera angle, music, dialogue, colour, etc.) that create that positioning.
Typical
Explain how film and multimodal texts use a combination of language, image, sound and structural features to position viewers to respond to characters, events and issues in particular ways.
Wider
  • Analyse how a film or TV episode uses a sustained combination of visual and linguistic choices to position the viewer to sympathise with one character and distrust another
  • Compare how the same narrative event is positioned differently in two film adaptations of the same story — examining what choices create different viewer responses
  • Investigate how documentary filmmakers use visual and language choices to position viewers toward particular conclusions about a real-world subject

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe positioning concept needs to travel across modalities — from print to image to film. Students who only practise it in print texts may struggle to apply the same critical lens to visual and multimodal texts. Multiple modality contexts build the generalised critical reading skill.
