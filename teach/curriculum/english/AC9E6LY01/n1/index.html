← AC9E6LY01 Texts reflecting context of creation

Context shapes representation

AC9E6LY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the context in which a text was created (time, place, culture, purpose) shapes how it represents ideas and events.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one specific feature of a text that reflects the context of its creation
  • Explains why a text from a different context might represent the same ideas or events differently

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the ways in which a text reflects the time and place in which it was created (AC9E5LY01). They have not yet examined media texts specifically, or analysed how context shapes representation through comparison of multiple texts from different times or places.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one way a provided text reflects the context of its creation; explain why a text from a different context might represent the same event or idea differently.
Typical
Analyse how context — including time, place, cultural values and purpose — shapes how a text represents ideas and events; discuss the relationship between creator context and text representation across a range of text types.
Wider
  • Compare news reports of the same type of event from different time periods and identify how the representations reflect their historical contexts
  • Identify how advertisements for the same product from different decades reflect changing social values and contexts

Hinge

Students who treat their own cultural context as neutral cannot analyse how creator context shapes media representation in Node 2 — the N2 analysis presupposes that context is an active force, not a neutral background.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who analyse only contemporary texts can mistake their own cultural context as neutral or universal. Comparing texts from different times and places reveals context as an active force in representation — preventing the misconception that texts simply describe reality.
