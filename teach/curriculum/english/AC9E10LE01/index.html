Representations in literary texts reflect context

AC9E10LE01 · Year 10 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse representations of individuals, groups and places and evaluate how they reflect their context in literary texts by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors

Prior knowledge

Students analyse how people and places are represented in literary texts across historical, social and cultural contexts (AC9E9LE01). They have not yet evaluated how representations reflect the specific context in which they were produced.

By the end

Students analyse representations of individuals, groups and places and evaluate how they reflect their context in literary texts by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • analysing the ways in which cultural stories may be retold and adapted across a range of contexts, such as the story of the “anti-hero”, and evaluating how the “anti-hero” reflects the context
  • analysing how stories written by First Nations Australian authors contemporise or modernise traditional stories and evaluating the responses of contemporary audiences
  • analysing how humour is used to represent the underdog in Australian texts and evaluating how the underdog reflects the context

Waypoints

  • 1. Analysing representations of individuals and groupsHingeStudents can analyse how individuals and groups are represented in a literary text, identifying specific textual choices that construct those representations.
  • 2. Evaluating representations as reflections of contextStudents can evaluate how the representations in a literary text reflect the context in which it was produced, making a supported judgement.
