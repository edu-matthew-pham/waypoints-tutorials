← AC9E10LE01 Representations in literary texts reflect context

Analysing representations of individuals and groups

AC9E10LE01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 10 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how individuals and groups are represented in a literary text, identifying specific textual choices that construct those representations.

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific textual choices that construct the representation of at least one individual or group
  • Analyses how those textual choices contribute to the representation

Prior knowledge

Students analyse how people and places are represented in literary texts across historical, social and cultural contexts (AC9E9LE01). They have not yet evaluated how representations reflect the specific context in which they were produced.

Width

Minimum
Analyse how at least one individual or group is represented in a provided literary text, identifying specific language and structural choices that construct the representation.
Typical
Analyse how individuals, groups and places are represented across a literary text, examining a range of textual choices and explaining how each contributes to the representation.
Wider
  • Analyse representations of the same group (e.g. the underdog, the outsider) across multiple literary texts from different authors, comparing how the representation differs
  • Examine representations of First Nations Australians in texts by non-First Nations Australian authors and compare with representations in texts by First Nations Australian authors
  • Analyse how representations of places in literary texts construct cultural or national identity

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to evaluate representations as reflections of context — a judgement that is only coherent if students can first identify and analyse what the representation is. Thin treatment here leaves Node 2 without the analytical foundation it depends on.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often assume that a text's representation is neutral or obvious; encountering contrasting representations of the same group from different author positions resolves this and builds critical reading of representation.
