Forming and justifying a personal response

AC9E7LE02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can form an opinion about characters, settings or events in a text and justify it by reference to the text

Success criteria

  • States a personal opinion about a character, setting or event
  • Justifies the opinion with at least one reference to the text

Prior knowledge

Students can identify similarities and differences in literary texts on similar topics, themes or plots (AC9E6LE02). They have not yet formed personal opinions about characters, settings and events and justified them — or explicitly engaged with the tension between their own and others' interpretations.

Width

Minimum
Form an opinion about a character, setting or event in a given text; justify it with at least one reference to what the text shows or says
Typical
Form opinions about multiple aspects of a text (character, setting, events) and justify each with textual evidence; explain what in the text led to each opinion
Wider
  • Compare personal opinions on texts and justify responses with reference to specific textual evidence — including reasoning about character behaviours such as integrity and loyalty
  • Establish criteria for discussing the relative merits of characters, settings and events before forming opinions — discuss how having criteria makes justification more rigorous

Hinge

If students cannot justify an opinion with textual reference, Node 2's dialogic work has no foundation — engaging with others' opinions requires first being able to anchor one's own position in the text.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents form opinions readily but rarely justify them with textual evidence — they describe their feeling (I liked/disliked the character) without connecting it to what the text actually shows. The reasoning scaffold — opinion → textual evidence → connection between evidence and opinion — is the core move this node develops.
