Making inferences

AC9E2LY05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 2 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make a supportable inference by integrating information from print, images and prior knowledge.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student makes an inference that integrates at least two sources of information.
  • Student can identify which sources of information support the inference (text, image, prior knowledge).

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Predicting and connecting.

Width

Minimum
Make one inference and identify at least two sources of information used to support it (e.g. text + image, text + prior knowledge).
Typical
Make inferences across a range of text types — narrative and informative — integrating print, image and prior knowledge; identify the main idea of a text as a form of inference.
Wider
  • Use a graphic organiser to record text evidence, image evidence and prior knowledge used to support an inference, then discuss how confident the inference is
  • Compare inferences made by different readers about the same text and discuss why they might differ

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often treat inference as guessing. The key misconception is that inferences are unsupported. Multiple text examples — where the inference is clearly grounded in combinations of evidence sources — resolve this. More text-type and evidence-source combinations is the productive investment.
