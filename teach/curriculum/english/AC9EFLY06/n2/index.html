← AC9EFLY06 Create short written texts

CVC spelling, vocabulary and punctuation

AC9EFLY06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply CVC spelling, use some learnt vocabulary, and include a capital letter at the sentence start and a full stop at the end.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student uses a capital letter at the start of a sentence and a full stop at the end.
  • Student spells at least one CVC word correctly without prompting.
  • Student uses at least one learnt vocabulary word accurately in context.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Writing communicates an idea.

Width

Minimum
Write at least one sentence with a capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end, spelling at least one CVC word correctly.
Typical
Use learnt vocabulary in writing; spell some CVC words correctly; apply basic sentence boundary punctuation (capital letter and full stop); demonstrate growing control of these conventions in short texts.
Wider
  • Write short texts across different topics, applying vocabulary and spelling knowledge to new content
  • Experiment with question marks and exclamation marks in appropriate writing contexts
  • Use topic-specific vocabulary from cross-curricular learning in written texts

Hinge

Without these conventions being at least partially established, students cannot productively participate in shared editing in Node 3 — they need something to read back and review, and the editing process presupposes that conventions have been attempted.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsCVC spelling accuracy and sentence punctuation conventions are precision skills — errors cascade into illegible or misleading texts. Practice across multiple writing tasks consolidates these as reliable conventions rather than occasional correct applications.
