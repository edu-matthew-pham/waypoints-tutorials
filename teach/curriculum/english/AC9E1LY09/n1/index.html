Phoneme segmentation in simple words

AC9E1LY09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can segment single-syllable words into all individual phonemes in sequence.

Success criteria

  • Student segments a single-syllable word into correct individual phonemes in order
  • Student counts phonemes accurately (not letters) in a given word

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and generate rhyming words, identify syllables and segment single-syllable CVC words into onset and rime at Foundation level (AC9EFLY15). They have not yet segmented words fully into individual phonemes, particularly those containing consonant blends or clusters.

Width

Minimum
Segment at least three single-syllable words (including CVC and CVCC patterns) into individual phonemes in order.
Typical
Segment single-syllable words into all phonemes in sequence, including words with initial and final consonants, across varied vowel patterns.
Wider
  • Segment words with four phonemes and confirm count
  • Compare segmentation of words with similar spelling but different phoneme counts

Hinge

Accurate single-word phoneme segmentation is definitionally prerequisite for N2 and N3 — students who cannot segment simple words cannot isolate individual consonants within blends.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate phoneme segmentation requires repeated practice — students often blend phonemes rather than cleanly separating each one. Segmentation is a foundational decoding skill that underpins spelling and must be automated.
