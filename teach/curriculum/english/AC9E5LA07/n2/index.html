Juxtaposition and relationship between images

AC9E5LA07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how placing images adjacent to each other — juxtaposition — creates meaning through contrast, comparison or connection.

Success criteria

  • Identifies two adjacent images and explains the meaning created by their juxtaposition that neither image creates alone.
  • Names the type of relationship the juxtaposition creates (contrast, connection, comparison, irony, etc.).

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Images sequence to tell or guide.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one example of juxtaposition in an image sequence and explain what meaning the pairing creates that the individual images do not.
Typical
Analyse how juxtaposition is used across several image sequences to create contrast, irony, emotional connection, or narrative development. Explain how the relationship between consecutive images generates meaning beyond what each image contains individually.
Wider
  • Examine how film editing techniques such as cut, dissolve, or match cut create meaning through the juxtaposition of consecutive shots — comparing this to how picture books use page turns to create similar effects
  • Analyse how image juxtaposition is used in advertising or persuasive texts to create implicit arguments, and discuss the ethical dimensions of that technique

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents focus on what individual images contain rather than on the relationship between consecutive images. Encountering juxtaposition across picture book, digital, and film contexts helps students see that meaning emerges from adjacency, not just content — a key conceptual shift.
