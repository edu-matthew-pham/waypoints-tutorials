← AC9E5LA07 Image sequence and meaning

Images sequence to tell or guide

AC9E5LA07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that images in texts appear in a deliberate sequence, and explain how the ordering of images guides the reader's or viewer's path through the text.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the sequence of images in a text and explains how the ordering creates a reading or viewing path.
  • Explains what would change in meaning if the sequence were altered.

Prior knowledge

Students can explore the effect of choices when framing an image, placement of elements in the image, and colour (AC9E4LA10). They have not yet examined how the ordering and sequencing of images across a text — rather than single-image composition — creates meaning.

Width

Minimum
In a picture book or image sequence, identify how the order of images creates a narrative or reading path, and explain how changing the order would change the meaning.
Typical
Analyse image sequences in wordless picture books, digital texts with hyperlink choices, and/or short films to explain how ordering guides interpretation. Identify moments where the sequence creates surprise, builds expectation, or controls information reveal.
Wider
  • Compare how image sequences function differently in print (fixed order), digital (multiple possible paths via hyperlinks), and film (time-based) — explaining what each medium's constraints do to meaning-making
  • Analyse how the sequence of images in texts by First Nations Australian authors create meaning, discussing how image ordering reflects cultural storytelling practices
