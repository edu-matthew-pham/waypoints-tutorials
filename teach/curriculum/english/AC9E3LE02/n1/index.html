← AC9E3LE02 Connect personal and character experiences

Drawing on prior knowledge to connect

AC9E3LE02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw on relevant prior knowledge and personal experience to make meaningful connections to characters' situations in texts.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student names a specific connection between their experience and a character's situation (not just 'I've been sad too')
  • Student explains how the connection helps them understand something about the character

Prior knowledge

Students identify features of literary texts and give reasons for personal preferences (AC9E2LE02). They have not yet focused specifically on drawing connections between their own experiences and those of characters — using prior knowledge and personal experience as a reading resource.

Width

Minimum
Identify one connection between a character's experience in a text and a personal or known experience, and discuss what the connection helps them understand.
Typical
Discuss relevant prior knowledge and past experiences to make meaningful connections to characters in texts — explaining how personal experience helps understand what a character might feel or do.
Wider
  • Compare connections made by different students to the same character — discussing how different experiences lead to different connections and what this reveals about reading as a personal act
  • Explore a text where a character's experience is very different from one's own, and discuss how to make connections despite the unfamiliarity

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMaking a meaningful connection requires more than naming a similarity — students need the reasoning chain: this happened to the character → something similar happened to me → this helps me understand that the character felt/did X. The 'so what' of the connection needs explicit scaffolding.
