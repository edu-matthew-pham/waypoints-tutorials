← AC9E6LY09 Spelling: word origins, morphology and letter patterns

Latin and Greek roots for spelling

AC9E6LY09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use knowledge of some Latin and Greek roots to spell and understand the meaning of words derived from those roots.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the Greek or Latin root in at least two provided words
  • Explains how knowing the root meaning helps with both spelling and word meaning

Prior knowledge

Students can build and spell new words from knowledge of known words, base words, prefixes and suffixes (AC9E5LY09). They have not yet applied specific Latin and Greek root knowledge (beyond the most common), or used etymology systematically to access the spelling and meaning of technical words.

Width

Minimum
Identify the root (Greek or Latin) in at least three provided words and explain how knowledge of the root helps with spelling and meaning.
Typical
Use a dictionary to explore word origins; apply knowledge of Greek roots (e.g. 'ath' — outstanding skill, 'pent' — five, 'dec' — ten) to spell and understand words including technical vocabulary; explain how root knowledge helps decode and spell unfamiliar words.
Wider
  • Research the meanings of five or more Greek or Latin roots and generate a family of words for each, explaining meaning connections
  • Investigate the meanings of words in languages of First Nations Australians using dictionaries or online resources from state libraries or universities

Hinge

Students without a working repertoire of root meanings cannot apply morphemic strategies to prefixes and suffixes in Node 2 — the prefix/suffix application presupposes that base word origins are recognisable.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a repertoire of root meanings before the strategy of using etymology to spell and decode becomes productive. Building a small but well-understood set of roots is more effective than a superficial encounter with many.
