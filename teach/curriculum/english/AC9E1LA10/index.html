Sentence boundary punctuation

AC9E1LA10 · Year 1 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand that written language uses punctuation such as full stops, question marks and exclamation marks, and uses capital letters for familiar proper nouns

Prior knowledge

Students can identify punctuation as a feature of written text different from letters, and recognise capital letters at sentence beginnings (AC9EFLA09). They have not yet understood the specific signalling functions of sentence-ending punctuation marks or the use of capital letters for proper nouns.

By the end

Students understand sentence boundary punctuation — full stops, question marks, exclamation marks — and use capital letters for familiar proper nouns.

Waypoints

  • 1. Punctuation marks signal sentence endingsStudents can identify full stops, question marks and exclamation marks as sentence-ending punctuation and explain that each signals a different type of sentence.
  • 2. Punctuation guides reading aloudStudents can use intonation and pauses in response to sentence-ending punctuation when reading.
  • 3. Capital letters for proper nounsHingeStudents can identify familiar proper nouns and explain that they require capital letters.
  • 4. Writing with sentence punctuationStudents can apply sentence boundary punctuation and proper noun capitalisation in their own writing.
