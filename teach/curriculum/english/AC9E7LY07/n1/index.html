Planning a presentation for purpose and audience

AC9E7LY07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a presentation by making deliberate choices about structure, content and mode based on purpose and audience

Success criteria

  • Identifies the purpose and audience for a presentation task
  • Plans at least one structural and one mode choice that serves them, with a reason

Prior knowledge

Students can plan, create, rehearse and deliver spoken and multimodal presentations that develop a theme or idea using precise vocabulary, pitch, tone, pace, volume and visual and digital features (AC9E6LY07). They have not yet worked at the level of selecting text structures, language features and literary devices deliberately for presentation purposes, or combining print, visual and audio elements to interpret ideas.

Width

Minimum
Given a presentation task, identify the purpose and audience; plan at least one structural choice and one mode choice (print, visual, audio) that serves them
Typical
Plan a presentation by mapping purpose and audience to structural, language and mode choices — what text type, what visual and audio elements, what voice approach is appropriate and why each serves the communicative goal
Wider
  • Prepare a presentation combining print, visual and audio elements to explore and interpret ideas, drawing on knowledge and research about perspectives different from students' own
  • Monitor ideas developed at each stage of creating a presentation in a blog or journal — making the planning and revision process visible

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly plan presentation content without planning how structural, language, mode and voice choices will serve the audience. The decision chain — purpose → audience → structural and mode choices → voice approach — makes the planning process explicit and purposeful.
