Voice features for audience engagement

AC9E7LY07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select and use voice features including volume, tone, pitch and pace to serve the purpose and engage the audience

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one voice feature used in a presentation and explains its effect
  • Uses at least one deliberate voice choice in their own delivery and can explain why

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning a presentation for purpose and audience.

Width

Minimum
In a delivery or viewing of a presentation, identify at least one voice feature (volume, tone, pitch, pace) and explain how it affects audience understanding or engagement
Typical
Select voice features for a specific purpose and audience — explain why a slower pace suits a complex explanation, why varied pitch maintains engagement, why tone shift signals importance — and apply these choices in delivery
Wider
  • Select features of voice for different audiences and purposes and recognise the effects these have on audience understanding and engagement — practise the same content with different voice approaches and evaluate the difference
  • Share feedback with a peer while planning, creating and rehearsing a presentation — develop the habit of using audience feedback to refine voice and delivery choices

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierEffective presentation delivery integrates content, structure, mode choices and voice features simultaneously in real time. Voice features cannot be rehearsed in isolation from content and structure — the integration is what rehearsal develops. This node is where the planned components come together in performance.
