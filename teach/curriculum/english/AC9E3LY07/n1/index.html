Planning and researching a presentation

AC9E3LY07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can research a topic and plan the sequence of ideas for a presentation using planning techniques such as storyboards.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a plan (storyboard, list or outline) that shows sequenced ideas before delivery
  • Student's plan includes enough content for a complete presentation — not just a title

Prior knowledge

Students create, rehearse and deliver short presentations for familiar audiences, varying tone, volume and pace (AC9E2LY07). They have not yet planned and researched content for presentations, or used storyboards and sequencing as planning tools.

Width

Minimum
Research a topic for a presentation, gather at least three facts or ideas, and plan the sequence using a simple storyboard or list.
Typical
Research a topic to prepare an oral or multimodal presentation; plan the sequence of ideas and information using techniques such as storyboards; explain ideas to a peer when planning.
Wider
  • Compare how planning a multimodal presentation differs from planning an oral-only one — discussing what additional decisions are needed
  • Explain the planned presentation to a peer before delivery and use their feedback to make at least one improvement to the plan

Hinge

Students who have not planned and sequenced their presentation content cannot deliver at N2 — there is no content to pace or adjust tone for.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPlanning and sequencing a presentation is a procedural skill that develops through repeated practice across different topics and presentation types. Students need multiple opportunities to plan before the process becomes reliable enough to support deliberate delivery at N2.
