Spelling for stylistic effect

AC9E9LY08 · Year 9 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand how spelling is used in texts for particular effects; for example, characterisation, humour and to represent accents and distinctive speech

Prior knowledge

Students use spelling rules and word origins including Greek and Latin roots to learn and spell new words (AC9E7LY08). They have not yet examined spelling as a deliberate authorial device used for characterisation, humour and accent representation.

By the end

Students understand how spelling is used deliberately in texts to create effects including characterisation, humour and representation of accents and distinctive speech.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring the spelling of neologisms and their effect in media texts such as online posts; for example, 'selfie' and 'Paralympics'
  • analysing how spelling is used to represent the distinctive speech of a character by noting where authors have dropped letters from words to emulate the sound of spoken words

Waypoints

  • 1. Spelling as characterisation deviceStudents can identify deliberate spelling variations used to represent character speech and explain the characterisation effect they create.
  • 2. Spelling for humour and neologismStudents can identify how unconventional spelling is used in media texts for humour or to create neologisms, and explain the effect.
