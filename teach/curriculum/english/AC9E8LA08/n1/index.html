Identifying academic vocabulary

AC9E8LA08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify vocabulary typical of academic texts — words used across disciplines to discuss evidence, reasoning and abstraction — and distinguish this from subject-specific technical vocabulary.

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific academic vocabulary words in a text and explains that they are typical of formal academic register.
  • Distinguishes academic vocabulary (cross-disciplinary) from technical vocabulary (subject-specific) with at least one example of each.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify specialist and technical vocabulary and understand how vocabulary choices shape meaning and register (AC9E7LA08). They have not yet focused specifically on the cross-disciplinary vocabulary of academic writing — words that are not subject-specific but are characteristic of formal academic register across disciplines.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least four academic vocabulary words in a provided academic text (e.g. 'evidence', 'consequence', 'contradiction', 'acknowledge') and explain that they are cross-disciplinary rather than subject-specific.
Typical
Identify academic vocabulary across a range of academic texts on different topics; compare and contrast vocabulary choices in academic texts, considering how they create precise information or convey abstract ideas; distinguish academic vocabulary from technical vocabulary.
Wider
  • Investigate how academic vocabulary differs across disciplines — comparing the language of a science report, a history essay and a literary analysis to identify shared academic vocabulary vs discipline-specific register
  • Examine how academic vocabulary items (e.g. 'evidence', 'analysis', 'significance') carry implicit disciplinary assumptions about what counts as valid knowledge
  • Compare the academic vocabulary in a text written for an expert audience with a version of the same content written for a general audience, and analyse what changes

Hinge

Node 2 requires accurate use of academic vocabulary in own writing. Students who have not grasped that academic vocabulary is cross-disciplinary will substitute subject-specific technical terms or informal equivalents — producing writing that fails to meet the academic register the descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAcademic vocabulary is best understood as cross-disciplinary — it appears in science, history and English writing alike. Students who only encounter it in English contexts may not recognise its transferable nature. Multiple disciplinary contexts build this generalised understanding.
