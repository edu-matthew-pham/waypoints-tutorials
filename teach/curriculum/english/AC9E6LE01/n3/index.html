← AC9E6LE01 Responses to characters and events across contexts

Sharing and evaluating responses

AC9E6LE01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can share and evaluate responses to characters and events in texts from diverse contexts, explaining how context shapes their own and others' readings.

Success criteria

  • Shares a personal response to a character or event and connects it to the text's context
  • Explains how a reader from a different context might respond differently to the same character or event

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Reading across diverse authors.

Width

Minimum
Share a personal response to a character or event from a text by a First Nations Australian or world author and connect the response to one aspect of the text's context.
Typical
Share and evaluate responses to texts from diverse historical, social and cultural contexts; explain how own and others' responses are shaped by context; compare responses across the class and discuss how different readers may respond differently to the same character or event.
Wider
  • Compare personal responses to a First Nations Australian text with those of a classmate and discuss how different contexts produce different readings
  • Evaluate a published review of a text from a different cultural context and analyse how the reviewer's context shapes their response

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierSharing and evaluating responses requires integrating understanding of how context shapes meaning (Node 1) and reading experience from diverse authors (Node 2) into a reflective, socially situated act of literary discussion.
