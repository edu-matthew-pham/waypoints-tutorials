Planning and organising ideas

AC9E8LY06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a written or multimodal text by organising and expanding ideas — using conceptual maps, journals or equivalent tools — and selecting appropriate text structures for the text's purpose and audience.

Success criteria

  • Produces a planning document that organises ideas and identifies a text structure suited to the purpose.
  • Can explain why the chosen structure is appropriate for the text's purpose and intended audience.

Prior knowledge

Students can plan, create, edit and publish texts for a range of purposes and audiences, making deliberate text structure and language feature choices (AC9E7LY06). They have not yet focused on expanding and organising ideas through the full planning-to-publishing cycle with academic accuracy goals, or on the deliberate use of vocabulary for positioning and persuasion.

Width

Minimum
Produce a planning document (conceptual map, outline or journal) for a written text that organises and expands at least three ideas and identifies the text structure to be used.
Typical
Plan written and multimodal texts using conceptual maps or journals to organise and expand ideas; select appropriate text structures and justify how these selections serve the text's purpose and audience; plan for the integration of language features, literary devices and visual features.
Wider
  • Compare different planning approaches (linear outline vs conceptual map vs journal drafting) and evaluate which approach produces better-organised ideas for a specific text type
  • Plan a multimodal text that requires explicit decisions about how written text, images and visual layout will work together to serve the purpose and audience
  • Investigate how published authors describe their planning process and compare with own practice — examining whether planning strategies vary by text type or purpose

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to create texts with deliberate structural and language feature selections — which presupposes a plan that has already organised ideas and identified structural choices. Thin planning means students create reactively rather than deliberately, making the 'deliberate selection' evidence invisible.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPlanning a text with deliberate structural and language feature choices requires students to separate planning decisions from execution — an unfamiliar metacognitive move. Without explicit scaffolding of what a planning document should contain (ideas, structure, feature selections), students either skip planning or treat it as an outline with no selection rationale.
