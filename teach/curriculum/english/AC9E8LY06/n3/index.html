← AC9E8LY06 Plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal texts

Editing for accuracy and meaning

AC9E8LY06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can edit own texts for grammatical accuracy, spelling and punctuation, and for clarity and precision of meaning — using experimentation with sentence structure, literary devices and vocabulary to clarify ideas.

Success criteria

  • Produces a revised version of own text with identifiable improvements to at least one grammatical feature and at least one meaning clarity issue.
  • Can explain the rationale for at least two specific editing decisions.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Creating with deliberate language and feature selection.

Width

Minimum
Edit a piece of own writing for at least one grammatical feature (sentence structure, punctuation or spelling) and for at least one meaning clarity revision, and explain the revisions made.
Typical
Edit written texts for accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation, and for meaning — experimenting with sentence structure order, literary devices and vocabulary to clarify ideas for academic or creative purposes; reflect on how editing decisions change the text.
Wider
  • Conduct a systematic editing cycle — first for accuracy, then for meaning, then for effect — and document the revision history with annotations explaining each change
  • Edit a peer's writing for meaning clarity and accuracy, providing written justifications for suggested revisions
  • Examine published author revisions (where available) and compare editorial decisions with own editing practice

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsEditing fluency requires repeated practice — the habit of reading one's own writing critically for accuracy and meaning does not develop from a single editing task. Multiple edit cycles across varied texts, with explicit attention to different editing foci, build the automaticity that makes editing reliable rather than superficial.
