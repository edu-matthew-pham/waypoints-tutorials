Rhythm and sound effects

AC9E4LE04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 4 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify rhythm and sound patterns in poetry and literary texts and explain the effect each creates on meaning and reader experience.

Success criteria

  • Can identify a rhythm or sound pattern in a poem and explain one effect it has
  • Does not describe sound patterns as just 'nice sounding' — can connect the pattern to a specific effect

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Word play — puns, ambiguity.

Width

Minimum
Identify a rhythmic or sound pattern in a poem (e.g. regular beat, alliteration pattern) and explain one effect it has on the reader.
Typical
Identify and discuss how rhythm — regular beat, varied pace, line length — and sound devices — alliteration, assonance, onomatopoeia — work together in poetry to create effects of pace, mood, emphasis and pleasure.
Wider
  • Compare the rhythm of a traditional children's poem with a contemporary free-verse poem and explain how the different rhythmic choices produce different reading experiences
  • Explore how First Nations Australian song poetry uses rhythm and sound patterning for cultural, ceremonial and storytelling purposes
