← AC9E3LA09 Images extend meaning

Images add information not in the words

AC9E3LA09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify information that an image provides that is not stated in the accompanying written text.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies specific information in the image that is not stated in the written text
  • Student explains what meaning the image adds — not just that 'it shows what's happening'

Prior knowledge

Students understand that images add to or multiply meaning in texts (AC9E2LA08). They have not yet focused on how images extend meaning specifically — adding information or dimensions that the written text alone does not provide.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one piece of information an image adds that is not in the written text, and explain what the image tells the reader.
Typical
Recognise how images extend meaning by adding information not in the words — including how the relationship between characters can be depicted through position, expression and gesture that the words don't explicitly describe.
Wider
  • Compare what a text with its images says versus the same text without them — discussing what meaning is lost when the images are removed
  • Examine how the same event is shown in an image versus described in words, and discuss what each mode does better

Hinge

Students who still treat images as illustrations of what the words say (rather than as extending meaning) lack the analytical stance needed for N2 — gaze, angle and distance analysis requires students to already be reading images as meaning-makers.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often treat images as illustrations of what the words already say. Multiple examples where the image adds genuinely new information — not just pictures of what is written — helps students see extending as distinct from illustrating.
