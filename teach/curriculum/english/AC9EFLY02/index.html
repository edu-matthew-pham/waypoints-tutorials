Listen and use voice features

AC9EFLY02 · Foundation English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interact in informal and structured situations by listening while others speak and using features of voice including volume levels

Prior knowledge

Students have developed oral interaction skills through home and play contexts. They have not yet been explicitly required to practise structured listening behaviours or to consciously vary and control voice features such as volume across different interaction contexts.

By the end

Students can interact in both informal and structured situations — listening appropriately while others speak and controlling voice features including volume — across a range of classroom and social contexts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exchanging ideas with peers in pairs and small groups, engaging in class discussions, listening to others and contributing ideas
  • showing understanding of appropriate listening behaviour, such as listening without interrupting, and looking at the speaker, if culturally appropriate
  • altering volume for inside and outside situations and when speaking to an audience
  • participating in informal interaction situations; for example, play-based experiences that involve the imaginative use of spoken language
  • listening to and following instructions

Waypoints

  • 1. Listening is activeHingeStudents can demonstrate active listening behaviours when others are speaking — attending without interrupting and showing they understand.
  • 2. Volume varies by contextStudents can adjust their speaking volume appropriately for different contexts — indoor/outdoor, one-to-one, speaking to an audience.
  • 3. Interact in structured and informal situationsStudents can participate in both informal and structured interaction situations — contributing ideas, exchanging with peers and following instructions — using appropriate listening and voice behaviours.
