Analysing language features in context

AC9E9LY01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how specific language features are used to represent individuals or groups in ways that reflect the text's context.

Success criteria

  • Student analyses specific language features (not just general register) used to represent a person or group
  • Student connects the language choices to at least one contextual factor — not just noting that the text is old or comes from a different culture

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Representations vary across contexts.

Width

Minimum
Analyse how language features (e.g. word choice, grammar, framing) are used in a text to represent a person or group in a particular way, and connect the choices to the text's context.
Typical
Analyse language features used across texts from different time periods to represent individuals or groups — identifying how language choices shift with changing contexts and what this reveals about the relationship between language and social values.
Wider
  • Analyse how language that was normalised in historical texts to represent a group is recognised as problematic today, examining what this reveals about how contexts shape representation
  • Compare the representation of the same concept (e.g. 'progress', 'freedom') across texts from different political or cultural contexts, examining how the same word carries different representational freight

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierAnalysing how language features reflect context requires students to integrate language analysis (specific features, choices) with contextual understanding (historical period, publication context, social values). These two competencies need to be coordinated rather than applied sequentially.
