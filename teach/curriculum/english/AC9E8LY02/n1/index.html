Identifying stated and implied meanings in spoken texts

AC9E8LY02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can listen to a conversation or speech, identify the point being made, and explain both the tone and manner of presentation — distinguishing stated from implied meanings.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the explicit point being made in a spoken text.
  • Identifies at least one implied meaning and explains the cues (tone, manner, word choice) that signal it.

Prior knowledge

Students can use interaction skills in a range of spoken contexts, including formal and informal discussion, and use active listening to evaluate spoken texts (AC9E7LY02). They have not yet focused specifically on using interaction skills to support or challenge the stated or implied meanings of spoken texts — engaging with the substance of what is said, not just participating in discussion.

Width

Minimum
Listen to a spoken text, identify the explicit point being made and one implied meaning, and explain how the tone or manner of presentation signals the implied meaning.
Typical
Listen to a range of spoken texts; identify stated and implied meanings; explain the tone and manner of presentation; describe how the interaction between explicit and implicit meaning shapes the overall message.
Wider
  • Analyse how a formal speech uses understatement, irony or indirect language to imply positions without stating them directly — examining what the speaker does not say as well as what they say
  • Compare how stated and implied meanings operate differently in informal conversation vs formal debate, examining how context affects the balance between explicit and implicit communication
  • Investigate how First Nations or multicultural communication styles may use indirect expression, silence or implied meaning differently from mainstream Australian English interaction conventions

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to use interaction strategies to support or challenge the implied meanings of spoken texts. Students who cannot distinguish stated from implied meaning in the first place can only engage with surface content in discussion — the interaction strategies at Node 2 have no target to work on.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe distinction between stated and implied meaning in spoken language is more challenging than in written text — the cues are prosodic and contextual rather than linguistic alone. Explicit scaffolding of what to listen for — tone, pace, word choice, what's conspicuously absent — is needed before students can do this independently.
