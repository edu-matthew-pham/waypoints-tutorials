Interaction skills for challenging meaning

AC9E8LY02 · Year 8 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use interaction skills for identified purposes and situations, including when supporting or challenging the stated or implied meanings of spoken texts in presentations or discussion

Prior knowledge

Students can use interaction skills in a range of spoken contexts, including formal and informal discussion, and use active listening to evaluate spoken texts (AC9E7LY02). They have not yet focused specifically on using interaction skills to support or challenge the stated or implied meanings of spoken texts — engaging with the substance of what is said, not just participating in discussion.

By the end

Students use interaction skills for identified purposes in a range of contexts, including when supporting or challenging the stated or implied meanings of spoken texts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • participating in pair, group, class, school and community speaking and listening situations, including informal conversations, discussions, debates and presentations
  • listening to a conversation or speech, identifying the point being made, and explaining the tone and manner of presentation
  • using effective strategies for dialogue and discussion in range of formal and informal contexts

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying stated and implied meanings in spoken textsHingeStudents can listen to a conversation or speech, identify the point being made, and explain both the tone and manner of presentation — distinguishing stated from implied meanings.
  • 2. Supporting and challenging meaning in discussionStudents can use effective interaction strategies — speaking clearly, asking questions about stated and implied ideas, restating and summarising — to support or challenge the meanings of spoken texts in presentations and discussion.
