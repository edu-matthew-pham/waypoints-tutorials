← AC9E10LA01 Inclusive and exclusive language effects

Shared assumptions and cultural knowledge

AC9E10LA01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify language that references shared assumptions and appeals to shared cultural knowledge, values and beliefs, and explain its inclusive or exclusive effect.

Success criteria

  • Identifies language that references or assumes shared cultural knowledge or values
  • Explains the inclusive effect on one audience group and the potentially exclusive effect on another

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Alignment and distancing devices.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of language appealing to shared cultural knowledge in a provided text and explain who is included and who may be excluded.
Typical
Analyse how texts use references to shared cultural knowledge, values and beliefs to construct insider and outsider positions, across at least two different text contexts.
Wider
  • Compare how texts targeting different cultural communities deploy different shared knowledge assumptions, examining what is taken for granted in each
  • Analyse examples where assumed shared knowledge marginalises particular groups — such as gendered or culturally specific references in media texts
  • Examine how First Nations Australian community communications draw on shared cultural knowledge to create solidarity and speak past mainstream gatekeeping

Hinge

Node 3 requires students to analyse how language empowers or disempowers people — a move that depends on understanding how shared assumption and exclusion operate. Students who cannot identify assumed cultural knowledge will miss the mechanism through which disempowerment occurs.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe inference required — identifying what is assumed rather than stated — needs explicit scaffolding for most students; the reasoning move from ‘what the text says’ to ‘what the text assumes’ is the core challenge here.
