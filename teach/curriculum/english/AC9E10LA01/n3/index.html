← AC9E10LA01 Inclusive and exclusive language effects

Empowerment and disempowerment through language

AC9E10LA01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can understand how language choices can empower or disempower people, identifying examples of marginalising or inclusive language and explaining their social effects.

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific language that empowers or disempowers people in a given text
  • Explains the social effect of the language choice on the people named or referenced, with reference to context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Shared assumptions and cultural knowledge.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one example of language that is inclusive or marginalising in a provided text and explain the social effect on the people named or referenced.
Typical
Analyse how language in a range of texts empowers or disempowers people, considering how naming, labelling and inclusion/exclusion choices shape social effects across different contexts.
Wider
  • Compare how empowering and disempowering language has been used historically and currently in relation to the same group — e.g. changing language around disability, First Nations identity, or gender
  • Examine political or media texts to trace how language choices have been used to disempower particular groups, and evaluate the social consequences
  • Analyse how communities have reclaimed previously marginalising language as an act of empowerment

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepWhether language is empowering or disempowering is highly context-dependent; students need to encounter multiple contexts to understand that the same word or phrase can have opposite effects depending on who uses it and in what setting.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the alignment devices and shared assumptions from nodes 1–2 with broader social analysis — students coordinate their linguistic analysis with awareness of power and social context.
