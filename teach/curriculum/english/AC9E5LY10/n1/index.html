Less common plurals and word origins

AC9E5LY10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 5 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can spell and explain less common plurals whose forms are derived from their word origins (Greek, Latin, English), explaining why these plurals differ from the regular -s pattern.

Success criteria

  • Correctly spells at least three less common plurals from different origin patterns.
  • Explains why the less common plural form differs from the regular -s pattern — connects form to origin.

Prior knowledge

Students can use and apply phonological and morphological knowledge to read and spell complex letter combinations (AC9E4LY09). They have not yet examined irregular plurals derived from word origins, or examined how suffixes systematically change the grammatical class of words.

Width

Minimum
Identify and correctly spell at least three less common plural forms (e.g. 'cacti', 'lice', 'criteria') and explain that each follows the plural pattern of its language of origin.
Typical
Explore less common plurals including Latin-origin plurals (-us/-i, -um/-a), Greek-origin plurals (-on/-a), and irregular English plurals (vowel changes such as 'louse/lice'). Explain how word origin determines the plural form and use this knowledge to interpret and spell unfamiliar plurals.
Wider
  • Investigate how less common plural forms are used in specific learning area vocabulary — scientific (e.g. 'bacteria', 'stimuli'), mathematical (e.g. 'formulae', 'radii') — and discuss how knowing the origin helps with specialist reading
  • Examine how English speakers sometimes regularise less common plurals (e.g. 'forums' alongside 'fora') and discuss what this variation reveals about how language changes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyLess common plural knowledge is a vocabulary repertoire that grows through encounter. No single plural form is a hinge; the goal is an accumulating working knowledge of plural patterns from different word origins that supports reading and spelling across learning areas.
