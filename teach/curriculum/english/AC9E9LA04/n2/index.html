Nominalisation as condensing device

AC9E9LA04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify nominalisations and explain how they condense prior information into a noun phrase that advances the text's argument or explanation.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies a nominalisation and states what process or clause it condenses
  • Student explains how condensing allows the text to carry that information forward as an established premise

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Text connectives and logical relationships.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two nominalisations in a text and explain what information each condenses and how this allows the text to move forward.
Typical
Investigate nominalisation across academic and technical texts, analysing how abstract nouns condense clauses or arguments, distil information and allow the text to treat that information as a given as it develops.
Wider
  • Compare nominalisations in scientific and humanistic writing, examining differences in how each discipline uses condensing to build knowledge
  • Analyse how nominalisation can obscure agency and examine the ideological implications of this choice in journalism or political discourse

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepNominalisation is hard to see in familiar contexts because it feels natural. Exposure to academic and technical texts from different disciplines helps students recognise nominalisation as a systematic device rather than an occasional quirk. Cross-discipline examples resolve the 'is this even a device?' confusion.
