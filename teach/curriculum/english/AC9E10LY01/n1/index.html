Analysing representations in texts

AC9E10LY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 10 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how people, places, events or concepts are represented in a text, identifying specific textual choices that construct those representations.

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific textual choices that construct a representation
  • Analyses how those choices contribute to the representation of the person, place, event or concept

Prior knowledge

Students analyse how representations of people, places, events and concepts reflect their contexts (AC9E9LY01). They have not yet evaluated how those representations are constructed and what they communicate about the context.

Width

Minimum
Analyse how at least one person, place, event or concept is represented in a text, identifying specific textual choices that construct the representation.
Typical
Analyse how a range of people, places, events and concepts are represented across a text, examining how language, visual and structural choices each contribute to the representation.
Wider
  • Compare how stereotypes of people, places or events are constructed across a range of texts and evaluate the assumptions underlying each
  • Analyse representations of events and issues in First Nations Australian media, examining how representations differ from mainstream media
  • Compare representations of the same event from texts produced for different audiences and analyse how the intended audience shapes the representation

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to evaluate how representations reflect context — a judgement that depends on first identifying the specific textual choices that construct the representation. Without analysis, evaluation has no evidential basis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to accept representations as natural or accurate; encountering contrasting representations of the same subject (especially First Nations Australian media versus mainstream media) makes the constructed nature of representation visible.
