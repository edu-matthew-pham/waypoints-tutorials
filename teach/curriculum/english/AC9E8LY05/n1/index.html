Comprehension strategies for deeper reading

AC9E8LY05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply comprehension strategies (visualising, predicting, connecting, summarising, monitoring, questioning, inferring) purposefully to interpret ideas in texts — reading for what is implied and inferred, not only what is stated.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two comprehension strategies and explains how each was used with a specific text.
  • Demonstrates inference or questioning as a tool for interpretation — surfacing implied or unstated meaning.

Prior knowledge

Students can use a range of comprehension strategies to construct meaning from texts and synthesise ideas across texts (AC9E7LY05). They have not yet applied these strategies specifically to interpretation and evaluation — using them to assess the credibility, effectiveness and significance of ideas in texts rather than merely to build comprehension.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two named comprehension strategies to a text and explain how they helped interpret ideas — specifically naming what the strategy surfaced about implied or inferred meaning.
Typical
Apply a range of comprehension strategies purposefully to interpret ideas in texts; reflect on and connect information across and between texts; demonstrate inference and questioning as deliberate tools for interpretation.
Wider
  • Apply comprehension strategies to a text from a cultural context unfamiliar to the reader — and reflect on which strategies worked, which were less effective, and what gaps in cultural knowledge affected comprehension
  • Use inferring and questioning strategies specifically to analyse what a text does NOT say — what is conspicuously absent from the text — and why
  • Compare how different comprehension strategies are required for reading a visual image vs a written text on the same subject

Hinge

Node 2 requires applying comprehension strategies specifically to evaluation — assessing credibility and effectiveness. Students who only have summarising in their repertoire cannot apply inferring, questioning or monitoring to make evaluative judgements. The full strategy repertoire built at Node 1 is what Node 2 applies in a new evaluative mode.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyComprehension strategies become truly useful when students have a full repertoire and can select the most appropriate strategy for a given reading challenge. Students who default to summarising need to experience questioning, inferring and monitoring as equally productive tools across different texts.
