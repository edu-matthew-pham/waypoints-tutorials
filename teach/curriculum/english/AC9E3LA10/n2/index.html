Words with different meanings in different contexts

AC9E3LA10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify words that have different meanings in different contexts and explain how the context determines which meaning is active.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies a word with multiple meanings and names at least two contexts
  • Student explains what the word means in each context and how they know which applies

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Topic-specific and technical vocabulary.

Width

Minimum
Identify one word that has different meanings in different contexts (e.g. 'warm' in everyday vs scientific contexts) and explain what it means in each context.
Typical
Recognise and explore words that have different meanings in different contexts — everyday meanings versus technical meanings — and use context clues to determine which meaning is active.
Wider
  • Find examples of the same word used in an everyday context and a subject-specific context in the same week of learning, and discuss how they know which meaning is intended
  • Explore how new words enter a topic vocabulary and what happens to the everyday word when a technical term takes over the same meaning

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often assume a word has one meaning. Seeing the same word used differently across contexts — maths vs everyday, science vs everyday — reveals that meaning is context-dependent. Multiple contrast examples are necessary for this insight.
