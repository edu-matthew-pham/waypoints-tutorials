Topic-specific and technical vocabulary

AC9E3LA10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and use topic-specific and technical vocabulary for their learning area topics, including measurement terms and narrative terms.

Success criteria

  • Student uses at least two topic-specific or technical words accurately in context
  • Student can explain what each word means in that learning area context

Prior knowledge

Students experiment with and begin to make conscious vocabulary choices to suit a topic (AC9E2LA09). They have not yet examined how topic-specific and technical vocabulary works across disciplines, or how words shift meaning across contexts.

Width

Minimum
Identify and use at least three technical or topic-specific words from a learning area topic and explain what each means in that context.
Typical
Identify and use technical words for specific topics across the curriculum — e.g. metric length terms, narrative feature terms (character, setting, plot); extend vocabulary by adding prefixes and suffixes to known words.
Wider
  • Compare technical vocabulary from two different subject areas (e.g. maths and science) and discuss how each subject builds its own specialised vocabulary
  • Investigate how adding a prefix or suffix to a word creates new vocabulary with related but different meaning

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyVocabulary extension is inherently repertoire-based — students need to encounter and use topic-specific words repeatedly across different topics and subject areas to build a genuinely expanding vocabulary. Range across learning areas is the growth mechanism.
