Plot, character and setting

AC9E1LE03 · Year 1 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

discuss plot, character and setting, which are features of stories

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise different types of literary texts and identify features including events and characters (AC9EFLE03). They have not yet discussed plot, character and setting as the three core structural features of stories, or explored how these features operate across different story types.

By the end

Students discuss plot, character and setting as features of stories, recognising these across different story types including traditional tales and narratives.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising similar characters and settings in different types of literary texts; for example, traditional tales, narrative poems and fables
  • discussing whether features of settings including time and place are realistic or imagined
  • retelling stories, identifying the main characters and describing their qualities and the events in sequence

Waypoints

  • 1. Stories have charactersStudents can identify characters in stories and describe what kind of character they are — their qualities, roles and relationships.
  • 2. Stories have a settingStudents can identify and describe the setting of a story — where and when it takes place — and discuss whether it is realistic or imagined.
  • 3. Stories have a plotHingeStudents can describe the plot of a story — the sequence of events including a problem and how it is resolved.
  • 4. Plot, character and setting work togetherStudents can discuss how plot, character and setting work together as features of a story — how each influences the others.
