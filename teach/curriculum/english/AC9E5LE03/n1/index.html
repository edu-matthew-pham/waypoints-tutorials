What point of view is

AC9E5LE03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify whether a text uses first or third person narration and explain what each choice means for who is telling the story.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the point of view (first or third person) in a given literary text.
  • Explains who the narrator is and what their relationship to the events of the story is.

Prior knowledge

Students can discuss how authors and illustrators make stories engaging through character, setting and plot (AC9E4LE03). They have not yet examined how narrative point of view — who tells the story and what they can or cannot know — shapes what readers have access to and how their sympathies are positioned.

Width

Minimum
Identify the point of view in a literary text as first or third person and explain who the narrator is and what their relationship to the story is.
Typical
Identify point of view across several texts, explaining the difference between a first-person narrator (character in the story, subjective, limited knowledge) and a third-person narrator (outside the story, potentially more access). Introduce the concept of narrative distance.
Wider
  • Examine second-person narration in texts (e.g. some picture books, choose-your-own-adventure formats) and discuss what effect the direct address creates for the reader
  • Limited width — identifying narrative person is a relatively narrow grammatical recognition task; the conceptual richness is in what each point of view does to reader access and sympathy, explored in later nodes.

Hinge

Correct identification of narrative person is definitionally prerequisite for Node 2 (information access analysis) and Node 3 (sympathy and interpretation). Students who confuse first and third person narration, or who cannot identify who the narrator is, cannot analyse what that narrator knows or how they position the reader.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPoint of view identification is a foundational recognition task that students need to perform reliably before analysis can proceed. Students who read first-person narration as objective or who conflate narrator with author need practice with varied examples across multiple text types until the distinction is secure.
