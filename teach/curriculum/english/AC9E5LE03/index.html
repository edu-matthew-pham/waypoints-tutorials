Point of view shapes response

AC9E5LE03 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Recognise that the point of view in a literary text influences how readers interpret and respond to events and characters

Prior knowledge

Students can discuss how authors and illustrators make stories engaging through character, setting and plot (AC9E4LE03). They have not yet examined how narrative point of view — who tells the story and what they can or cannot know — shapes what readers have access to and how their sympathies are positioned.

By the end

Students recognise and explain how the point of view in a literary text — including first and third person narration — determines what information readers can access and how it influences readers' interpretation of events and characters.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing texts narrated from a first person and third person point of view, and discussing what information the audience can access and how this influences the audience's sympathies
  • discussing why an author might choose a particular point of view

Waypoints

  • 1. What point of view isHingeStudents can identify whether a text uses first or third person narration and explain what each choice means for who is telling the story.
  • 2. Point of view controls information accessHingeStudents can explain how the choice of point of view determines what information the audience can access — what they know, when they know it, and what remains hidden.
  • 3. Point of view shapes sympathy and interpretationStudents can explain how point of view influences readers' sympathies with characters and their interpretation of events — what point of view makes readers feel and believe.
