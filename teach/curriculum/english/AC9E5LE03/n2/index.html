Point of view controls information access

AC9E5LE03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the choice of point of view determines what information the audience can access — what they know, when they know it, and what remains hidden.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two types of information that are available or unavailable to readers because of the text's point of view choice.
  • Explains why the author might have chosen to give readers the particular information access their point of view provides.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What point of view is.

Width

Minimum
Compare what a reader would know if a text's point of view were changed (e.g. from first to third), and explain at least two types of information that would become available or unavailable as a result.
Typical
Analyse how first-person narration limits reader knowledge to what the narrator observes, thinks, and feels, while third-person narration can offer broader access. Discuss why an author might choose to restrict or expand reader access to information — what narrative purposes does each choice serve?
Wider
  • Examine unreliable first-person narrators in texts and discuss what clues allow readers to understand more than the narrator tells them
  • Compare the information access available to readers in two versions or adaptations of the same story told from different points of view

Hinge

Understanding information access is directly prerequisite for Node 3's analysis of how point of view shapes sympathy and interpretation. Students who cannot explain what readers can and cannot know from a given point of view will be unable to reason about how that restricted or expanded access positions readers emotionally and interpretively.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly think that first person is 'more honest' and third person is 'more distant' without understanding that point of view shapes information access as a deliberate craft choice. Encountering the same events from multiple points of view helps students see information access as variable and purposeful.
