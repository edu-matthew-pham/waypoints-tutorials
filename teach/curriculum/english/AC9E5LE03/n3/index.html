Point of view shapes sympathy and interpretation

AC9E5LE03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how point of view influences readers' sympathies with characters and their interpretation of events — what point of view makes readers feel and believe.

Success criteria

  • Explains how the point of view positions readers to sympathise with or interpret a specific character in a particular way.
  • Identifies how a different point of view would change the reader's response to the same events or characters.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Point of view controls information access.

Width

Minimum
Identify how the point of view in a text positions readers to sympathise with or understand a particular character, and explain how a different point of view would change the reader's relationship to that character.
Typical
Discuss why an author chose a particular point of view, explaining how that choice shapes reader sympathy, moral positioning, and interpretation of events. Examine how readers can access more than the narrator tells them by reading against or beyond the narrator's perspective.
Wider
  • Compare texts narrated from a first person and third person point of view about similar events, discussing what information the audience can access and how this influences the audience's sympathies — drawing on the elaboration examples
  • Examine a text by a First Nations Australian author and discuss how point of view shapes the reader's access to and understanding of cultural context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to integrate point of view identification (node 1) and information access understanding (node 2) into an account of how readers are positioned emotionally and interpretively — the full demand of the descriptor's 'influences how readers interpret and respond' clause.
