Punctuation for referencing and citing

AC9E9LA09 · Year 9 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand punctuation conventions for referencing and citing others for formal and informal purposes

Prior knowledge

Students understand how colons and brackets support meaning (AC9E7LA09). They have not yet examined the punctuation conventions specific to referencing and citation — how to integrate others' words and ideas into their own writing with appropriate attribution.

By the end

Students understand punctuation conventions for referencing and citing others in formal and informal contexts, including when to use direct quotation and when to report sources.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • understanding who to and how to cite in essays, reviews and academic assignments, and when it is appropriate to use direct quotations or to report sources more generally

Waypoints

  • 1. Direct quotation conventionsHingeStudents can apply punctuation conventions for integrating direct quotations into their own writing.
  • 2. When to quote vs paraphrase or reportStudents can explain when direct quotation is appropriate versus paraphrasing or general source reporting, and apply this judgment in their writing.
