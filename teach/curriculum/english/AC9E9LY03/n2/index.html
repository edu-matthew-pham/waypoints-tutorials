Evaluating perspective representation

AC9E9LY03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how effectively or fairly language features represent a perspective — making and supporting judgements about language choices as representational tools.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student makes a supported evaluative judgement about a language choice as a representation tool
  • Student's evaluation goes beyond agreeing or disagreeing with the perspective — it assesses how the language works as representation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Language features as perspective devices.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least one language choice as a representation tool — making a judgement about whether it represents the perspective fairly, effectively or misleadingly, and supporting the judgement with evidence.
Typical
Evaluate how language features are used to represent perspectives across a range of text types — comparing campaign speeches, journalism and social media, and making supported judgements about the effectiveness and fairness of representational choices.
Wider
  • Evaluate how language in a historical text represents a group in ways that are now recognised as problematic, and assess the impact of such representations
  • Examine advocacy speeches from films or media and evaluate how language features influence the listener — assessing both effectiveness and ethical dimensions of the techniques used

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating language representation requires criteria for judgement — students need reasoning scaffolds for what makes a representation fair, effective or misleading. Without criteria, evaluation collapses into agreement or disagreement with the perspective rather than analysis of the representation.
