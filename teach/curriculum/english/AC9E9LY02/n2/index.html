Presenting and discussing opinions about spoken texts

AC9E9LY02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use interaction skills to present and discuss opinions about spoken texts, including speaking clearly, presenting a reasoned opinion and responding to others.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student presents an opinion about a spoken text with at least one specific piece of evidence
  • Student demonstrates at least one interaction skill (e.g. responding to another's view, inviting others' contributions, building on a point)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How language positions listeners.

Width

Minimum
Present an opinion about a spoken text, including at least one specific language feature as evidence, speaking clearly for an appropriate length of time.
Typical
Use effective dialogue and discussion strategies in formal and informal contexts — presenting opinions about spoken texts with specific evidence, listening to and responding to others' views, and speaking with clarity and appropriate pacing.
Wider
  • Conduct a formal discussion of a spoken text using structured dialogue protocols, evaluating how the structure itself affects the quality of opinion exchange
  • Compare how the same opinion about a text lands differently when presented orally versus in writing — examining what the spoken mode adds or limits

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsOral discussion skills require repeated practice under real discussion conditions. The skills — speaking clearly, responding to others, maintaining coherence under conversational pressure — develop through practice, not conceptual extension.
