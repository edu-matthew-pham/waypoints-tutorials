← AC9E6LY04 Select, navigate and read texts for purpose

Using structural features to navigate

AC9E6LY04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use structural features of texts (table of contents, glossary, chapters, headings, subheadings) to locate specific content for a purpose.

Success criteria

  • Locates specific information in a text using at least two structural features
  • Identifies which structural feature was most useful for the stated purpose

Prior knowledge

Students can navigate and read texts for specific purposes, monitoring meaning using strategies such as adjusting reading rate and re-reading (AC9E5LY04). They have not yet systematically evaluated structural features (table of contents, glossary, headings) for their effectiveness in serving navigation and comprehension purposes, or applied research skills to locate and evaluate multiple texts.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two structural features (e.g. table of contents, headings) to locate specific information in a provided text for a stated purpose.
Typical
Use knowledge of text structure to navigate a range of print and digital texts; use structural features including table of contents, glossary, chapters, headings and subheadings to locate specific literal content for a given research or reading purpose.
Wider
  • Compare navigation features in a print text vs. a digital text on the same topic and evaluate which features better serve a specific reading purpose
  • Navigate a multi-source research task using structural features across several texts and evaluate which texts best serve the purpose
