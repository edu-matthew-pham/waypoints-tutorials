Greek and Latin roots

AC9E7LY08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use knowledge of Greek and Latin roots to work out the meaning and spelling of technical and academic vocabulary

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a Greek or Latin root in a given word
  • Explains how the root contributes to the word's meaning

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Morphological knowledge as a spelling tool.

Width

Minimum
Identify a Greek or Latin root in a given technical word and explain how knowledge of the root helps with both spelling and meaning
Typical
Investigate a set of Greek and Latin roots (e.g. bio, graph, tele, port, scrib) and generate or identify words built from each; explain how the root contributes to meaning across the word family
Wider
  • Investigate how Greek roots dominate scientific and technical vocabulary while Latin roots dominate legal and academic vocabulary — explore examples from multiple subject areas
  • Build a personal glossary of Greek and Latin roots encountered across subjects and use it to decode unfamiliar technical terms

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyGreek and Latin root knowledge is a vocabulary-building resource — the more roots students know, the more unfamiliar words they can decode. Building a working repertoire of high-yield roots across subjects is more valuable than deep knowledge of a small set.
