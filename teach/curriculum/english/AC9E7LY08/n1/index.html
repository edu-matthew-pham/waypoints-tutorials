Morphological knowledge as a spelling tool

AC9E7LY08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use knowledge of base words, prefixes and suffixes to work out the spelling of unfamiliar words

Success criteria

  • Identifies the base word or affix in an unfamiliar word
  • Uses morphological knowledge to spell the word correctly

Prior knowledge

Students use knowledge of word origins including some Latin and Greek roots, base words, prefixes, suffixes, letter patterns and spelling generalisations to spell new words including technical words (AC9E6LY09). They have not yet focused on using this morphological knowledge strategically — as a generalised tool for encountering and learning unfamiliar words.

Width

Minimum
Given an unfamiliar word, identify the base word, prefix or suffix; use that knowledge to spell the word correctly and explain the strategy used
Typical
Apply morphological knowledge to a range of unfamiliar words across different word families; explain how recognising a known base word or affix helps with both spelling and meaning
Wider
  • Investigate word families built from a common base — e.g. 'graph' (photograph, biography, grapheme) — and explore how the base contributes to meaning across all derived words
  • Examine spelling rules that govern how base words change when suffixes are added (doubling, dropping e, y to i) and explain why knowing the rule is more efficient than memorising each word

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsMorphological spelling is a skill that requires regular application to become automatic. Students understand the concept of base words and affixes but do not apply the strategy consistently when encountering new words. Practice across varied word families builds the habit of morphological reasoning as a first response to an unfamiliar word.
