← AC9E5LY01 Texts reflect time and place

Social expectations in texts

AC9E5LY01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how the social expectations of the time and place of a text's creation are reflected in its content, characters, or events.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a social expectation in a text and explains how it is reflected in specific textual details.
  • Explains how that social expectation reflects the time and place of the text's creation rather than universal values.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Vocabulary marks time and place.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one social expectation embedded in a text and explain how it is reflected — through character behaviour, dialogue, plot events, or narrator attitude.
Typical
Describe how texts from different times and places reflect the social expectations of their context, examining how values, norms, and social roles are represented. Explain how those expectations differ from contemporary social expectations and what those differences reveal about social change.
Wider
  • Examine how texts from different historical periods reflect changing social expectations about gender, class, or community, and discuss what reading those texts from a contemporary perspective reveals
  • Compare how texts by First Nations Australian authors reflect the social expectations and values of their communities and the historical contexts that shaped them

Hinge

Node 3 requires students to synthesise vocabulary evidence and social expectation evidence into a coherent description of how a text reflects its context. Students who have not yet established the social expectations analysis strand will produce Node 3 descriptions that are one-dimensional — relying only on vocabulary and missing the social values dimension that the descriptor explicitly requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often read social expectations in historical texts as simply 'wrong' without understanding that those texts were written within a different social context. Encountering a range of texts from different times and places — including First Nations Australian texts — helps students understand social expectations as context-specific rather than universal.
