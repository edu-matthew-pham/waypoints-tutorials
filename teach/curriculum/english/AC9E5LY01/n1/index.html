Vocabulary marks time and place

AC9E5LY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify vocabulary in a text that signals when and where it was created, and explain how vocabulary choices reflect the language use of a particular time and place.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two vocabulary choices or expressions in a text and explains what each signals about time and/or place.
  • Explains why a specific word choice reflects the language use of a particular period or location.

Prior knowledge

Students can compare texts from different times with similar purposes and audiences to identify similarities and differences (AC9E4LY01). They have not yet examined how vocabulary, idiomatic expressions, and social expectations within texts reveal the time and place of their creation.

Width

Minimum
In a text, identify at least two vocabulary choices — including idiomatic expressions if present — that signal the time or place of the text's creation and explain what each reveals.
Typical
Analyse vocabulary choices across texts from different time periods and places, identifying how idiomatic expressions, word choices, and naming conventions signal time and place. Explain why the same concept might be described differently across texts from different times.
Wider
  • Examine how Australian English vocabulary has been influenced by time and place — including words from First Nations Australian languages and from waves of migration — and discuss what the vocabulary of texts reveals about their cultural context
  • Compare texts from the same time period but different places and discuss how vocabulary choices reflect regional and cultural variation
