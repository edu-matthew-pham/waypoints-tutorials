Complex vowel sounds in multisyllabic words

AC9E4LY09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and apply complex vowel patterns in multisyllabic words, including unstressed vowels.

Success criteria

  • Can correctly read at least five multisyllabic words with complex vowel combinations
  • Can correctly write at least five multisyllabic words with complex vowel combinations

Prior knowledge

Students apply phoneme-grapheme knowledge and syllable patterns to fluently read and write multisyllabic words (AC9E3LY09). They have not yet applied this knowledge to more complex letter combinations including a variety of vowel sounds and known prefixes and suffixes.

Width

Minimum
Identify the vowel pattern in at least two multisyllabic words with complex letter combinations and read/write them correctly.
Typical
Apply knowledge of a range of complex vowel patterns — unstressed vowels, vowel digraphs in multisyllabic words — to read and write words accurately, including words such as 'builder', 'animal', 'straightaway', 'thoughtful'.
Wider
  • Sort multisyllabic words by vowel pattern and identify rules or generalisations that help predict spelling
  • Use morphological analysis to identify how vowel sounds shift across word families (e.g. explain vs explanation)

Hinge

Adding prefixes and suffixes in N2 requires accurate reading and writing of the base word — vowel errors in the stem produce compounded errors when morphemes are attached.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsUnstressed vowels in multisyllabic words are a common source of spelling errors at Year 4. Reliable reading and writing of these patterns requires practice across many exemplars before automaticity is achieved.
