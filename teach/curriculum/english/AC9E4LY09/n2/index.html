Prefixes and suffixes in multisyllabic words

AC9E4LY09 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify known prefixes and suffixes in multisyllabic words and use this knowledge to decode and spell them.

Success criteria

  • Can identify a prefix or suffix in a multisyllabic word and name its meaning
  • Can use prefix/suffix knowledge to decode or spell an unfamiliar word

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Complex vowel sounds in multisyllabic words.

Width

Minimum
Identify a prefix or suffix in a multisyllabic word and use this morphological knowledge to read or spell the word correctly.
Typical
Apply knowledge of known prefixes (un-, re-, pre-, dis-) and suffixes (-ful, -less, -ness, -tion, -ing, -ed) to decode and spell a range of multisyllabic words, explaining how the morpheme changes or adds meaning.
Wider
  • Explore how the same prefix or suffix appears across multiple words from different word families and generalise about its meaning contribution
  • Use morphological decomposition as a decoding strategy for unfamiliar long words in subject texts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyMorphological awareness grows through accumulating prefix and suffix families across contexts — each new morpheme learned expands the decoding and spelling range transferably across vocabulary domains.
