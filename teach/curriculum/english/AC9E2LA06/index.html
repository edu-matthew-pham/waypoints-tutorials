Compound sentences

AC9E2LA06 · Year 2 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand that connections can be made between ideas by using a compound sentence with 2 or more independent clauses usually linked by a coordinating conjunction

Prior knowledge

Students understand that a simple sentence is a single independent clause representing one event or idea (AC9E1LA06). They have not yet connected independent clauses into compound sentences or understood how coordinating conjunctions signal the relationship between clauses.

By the end

Students can understand that compound sentences connect two or more independent clauses using coordinating conjunctions, and can identify the relationship between ideas that each conjunction signals.

Waypoints

  • 1. Independent clauses can be joinedStudents can identify that a compound sentence contains two or more complete ideas, each of which could stand alone, joined by a linking word.
  • 2. Conjunctions signal relationshipsStudents can identify how 'and', 'but' and 'so' each signal a different relationship between ideas — addition, contrast and consequence — and choose the correct conjunction for a given pair of clauses.
  • 3. Constructing compound sentencesHingeStudents can construct their own compound sentences, connecting independent clauses with appropriate coordinating conjunctions to express related ideas clearly.
