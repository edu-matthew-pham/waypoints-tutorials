Idea organisation structures

AC9E8LY04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the organisational structure a text uses (e.g. proposition and support, cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution) and explain how that structure shapes the text's meaning.

Success criteria

  • Names the organisational structure used in a text (proposition/support, cause/effect, compare/contrast, or problem/solution).
  • Explains how that structural choice shapes what the reader understands or is persuaded of.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how authors structure texts and organise information to achieve purpose (AC9E7LY04). They have not yet analysed the specific organisational logics — proposition and support, cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution — and how these structures shape the meaning a text produces.

Width

Minimum
Identify the organisational structure used in one provided text (e.g. a news article or argument) and explain how that structure shapes what the reader understands from the text.
Typical
Analyse how texts structured according to proposition and support, cause and effect, compare and contrast, or problem and solution each shape meaning differently; identify examples of each structure and explain the effect the structural choice has on a reader.
Wider
  • Compare how a news article and an academic essay organise the same information differently and analyse how the structural difference shapes the meaning and credibility each text produces
  • Investigate how a problem-solution structure can be used persuasively — examining what is at stake in the problem framing and whether the proposed solution addresses all aspects of the defined problem
  • Analyse how the organisation of a webpage (e.g. a news site or advocacy site) uses non-linear structure and shapes meaning differently from a linearly structured print text

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to analyse how organisational choices shape and limit meaning — a task that requires being able to name and classify the organisational structure in question. Students who cannot identify whether a text uses cause-effect or problem-solution logic cannot analyse the meaning implications of that structural choice.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents may know proposition-support from English but not recognise the same organisational logic appearing as cause-effect in other subjects or as problem-solution in design. Encountering the structures across multiple text types builds a transferable analytical vocabulary.
