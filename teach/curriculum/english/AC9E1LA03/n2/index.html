Text stages differ by type

AC9E1LA03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 1 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the typical stages of at least two text types and explain how those stages serve the text's purpose.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies the stages of a given text and names at least the opening and purpose-serving stages
  • Student explains what a specific stage does for the reader (e.g. 'the orientation tells you who and when')

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Texts have a purpose.

Width

Minimum
Identify the opening, middle and end of two different text types and describe what each stage does.
Typical
Describe the typical stages of familiar text types — recount (orientation, events, reorientation), narrative (orientation, complication, resolution), procedure (goal, steps) — and explain how stages serve the purpose.
Wider
  • Compare the stages of a recount and a narrative to identify where they overlap and where they differ
  • Examine informative texts to identify stages (introduction, information sections, conclusion/summary)

Hinge

Students who apply the beginning-middle-end narrative schema to all texts cannot distinguish the stages of recounts, procedures or informative texts — this misconception, if uncorrected, flows into N3's multimodal organisation work and into AC9E1LY06 writing-for-purpose.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents readily grasp that stories have a beginning, middle and end but often apply this schema to all texts. Encountering multiple distinct text types with different stage names helps resolve the misconception that all text structure is narrative structure.
