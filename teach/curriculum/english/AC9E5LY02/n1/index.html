Paraphrasing to clarify

AC9E5LY02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can paraphrase what a speaker has said to check understanding and signal attention, and use this as a clarification strategy in discussion.

Success criteria

  • Paraphrases a speaker's contribution accurately — captures the meaning in own words without significantly distorting it.
  • Uses paraphrasing as a clarification strategy — checks understanding rather than just replaying what was said.

Prior knowledge

Students can listen for key points and information and contribute to discussions by expressing opinions and making judgements (AC9E4LY02). They have not yet applied paraphrasing as a clarification strategy, or developed systematic approaches to questioning for meaning-making in discussion.

Width

Minimum
In a discussion context, paraphrase at least one contribution from another speaker to check understanding, and explain what paraphrasing does for the conversation.
Typical
Use paraphrasing to check understanding and signal engagement across discussion contexts. Explain the difference between paraphrasing (capturing meaning in own words) and summarising (condensing) and echo (repeating). Apply paraphrasing as a strategy to keep conversations moving and build shared understanding.
Wider
  • Practise paraphrasing in paired and group contexts with explicit feedback on accuracy — developing awareness of what is captured and what is lost in a paraphrase
  • Compare how paraphrasing functions differently in formal discussion versus informal conversation contexts — discussing when the strategy is most useful

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsParaphrasing is a skill that requires practice to become automatic. Students often confuse paraphrasing with repeating or summarising. Consolidation through deliberate practice in structured discussion contexts builds reliable deployment before paraphrasing is integrated with questioning and justification.
