Present and justify opinions

AC9E5LY02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can present an opinion clearly and support it with reasons, making connections to their own experience where relevant and choosing vocabulary suited to the context.

Success criteria

  • Presents an opinion with at least two supporting reasons in a discussion context.
  • Responds to at least one other speaker — through paraphrasing, questioning, or building on their point — demonstrating interaction rather than turn-taking.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Purposeful questioning for meaning.

Width

Minimum
Present an opinion in a discussion context with at least two reasons supporting it, and respond to at least one contribution from another speaker in the discussion.
Typical
Present and justify opinions across formal and informal discussion contexts, choosing vocabulary and sentence structures appropriate to purpose and formality. Make connections between their own contributions and those of others. Respond constructively to challenge or different perspectives.
Wider
  • Participate in a structured debate or formal discussion, applying all interaction skills (paraphrasing, questioning, justifying) in an integrated performance and reflecting on what worked and what could be improved
  • Apply different registers — formal and informal — to presenting the same opinion in different contexts, and discuss how register choice affects persuasiveness and appropriateness

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to integrate paraphrasing (node 1), purposeful questioning (node 2), and opinion presentation into a coherent interactive performance — the full capability demand of the descriptor. Phase collapse here means students default to opinion-delivery without the interactive skills that make discussion productive.
