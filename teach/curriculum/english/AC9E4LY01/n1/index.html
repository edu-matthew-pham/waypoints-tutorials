← AC9E4LY01 Texts across time and context

Same purpose, different times

AC9E4LY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify two texts from different time periods with the same purpose and explain what is similar and different about them.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify two texts from different periods with the same purpose
  • Can describe at least one similarity and one difference in their depictions

Prior knowledge

Students recognise how the same purpose can produce different texts when audiences differ (AC9E3LY01). They have not yet compared texts from different time periods to identify how historical context shapes depictions of events.

Width

Minimum
Identify two texts from different times with the same purpose and describe one similarity and one difference.
Typical
Compare texts from different periods across at least two dimensions — language choices, visual conventions, what is included or excluded — and explain how each difference relates to the time period in which the text was made.
Wider
  • Compare news coverage of the same type of event (e.g. a major sporting event) from different decades, examining how the depiction has changed
  • Compare family communication texts (letters, postcards, emails, texts) across different periods and explain how technology has changed the form while the purpose (staying in contact) remains similar

Hinge

N2's analysis of how historical context shapes depiction requires students to have already identified two comparable texts from different periods — N1 provides the comparative base N2 builds from.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents need to see multiple cross-period comparisons before the pattern of contextual shaping becomes visible — a single pair of texts is insufficient to reveal that depiction is always contextual rather than occasionally different.
