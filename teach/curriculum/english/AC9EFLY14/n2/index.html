← AC9EFLY14 Read and write high-frequency words

High-frequency words can be written

AC9EFLY14 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can write at least some high-frequency words correctly from memory.

Success criteria

  • Student can write at least five high-frequency words correctly from memory.
  • Student uses high-frequency words in independent writing without needing to copy them.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Some words are recognised at sight.

Width

Minimum
Write at least five high-frequency words correctly without copying.
Typical
Read and write an increasing number of high-frequency words; write familiar words from memory in the context of independent writing tasks.
Wider
  • Use high-frequency words in independent writing and self-check for correct spelling
  • Build a personal word card collection — connecting reading recognition with written production
  • Apply known high-frequency words as anchors in independent writing — using them alongside phonemic spelling strategies for unknown words

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsWritten production of high-frequency words must be automatic — looking up or sounding out every common word in writing impedes composition. Practice to accuracy across multiple words is required.
