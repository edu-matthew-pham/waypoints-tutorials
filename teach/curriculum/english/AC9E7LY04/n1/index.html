Idea structure patterns

AC9E7LY04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and name the idea structure being used in a given text and explain how it organises the ideas

Success criteria

  • Identifies the idea structure in a given text (taxonomy, cause-effect, extended metaphor, or chronology)
  • Explains how the structure organises the ideas in that text

Prior knowledge

Students can select, navigate and read texts for a range of purposes, monitoring meaning and evaluating structural features (AC9E6LY04). They have not yet explicitly explained how ideas themselves are structured — the logical or rhetorical patterns (taxonomy, cause-effect, metaphor, chronology) that organise thinking within and across texts.

Width

Minimum
In a given text, identify the idea structure being used (taxonomy, cause-effect, extended metaphor, or chronology) and explain how it organises the information or argument
Typical
Examine idea structures across different text types — taxonomies in scientific explanations, cause-effect in arguments and explanations, chronology in recounts and histories — and explain how the structure serves each text's purpose
Wider
  • Identify cause and effect in explanations and analyse how causal structure is used to convince an audience of a course of action — tracing how the reasoning moves from cause to effect to recommended response
  • Examine how extended metaphors organise ideas in literary and persuasive texts — how a sustained comparison shapes the way readers understand a concept

Hinge

If students cannot recognise and name idea structures, Node 2's work on how structure serves purpose is inaccessible — explaining why a structure was chosen requires first being able to identify what structure is present.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a working repertoire of idea structure patterns to recognise them across texts. Building knowledge of the four named structures — taxonomy, cause-effect, extended metaphor, chronology — across varied text examples gives students the analytical tools to explain how ideas are organised, not just describe what a text is about.
