Idea structures serve purpose

AC9E7LY04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the choice of idea structure in a text serves its purpose and shapes the reader's understanding

Success criteria

  • Explains why a specific idea structure suits the purpose of a given text
  • Identifies how the structure shapes the reader's understanding of the topic

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Idea structure patterns.

Width

Minimum
Explain why a specific idea structure is well-suited to a text's purpose — e.g. why cause-effect suits an explanation that aims to convince, or why chronology suits a recount
Typical
Compare two texts on related topics that use different idea structures and explain how the different structures produce different understandings — what does a taxonomic organisation make visible that a chronological one obscures, or vice versa?
Wider
  • Examine how a writer uses cause-effect structure not just to explain but to persuade — tracing how the causal chain is constructed to make a recommended action seem inevitable
  • Analyse how the same sequence of events can be structured as chronology (neutral recount) or cause-effect (persuasive argument) and discuss how the choice of structure shapes the reader's interpretation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents can identify an idea structure without being able to explain why a writer chose it over alternatives. The reasoning scaffold — what does this structure make salient, what does it obscure, and how does that serve the purpose — builds the explanatory connection between structure choice and meaning-making that the descriptor requires.
