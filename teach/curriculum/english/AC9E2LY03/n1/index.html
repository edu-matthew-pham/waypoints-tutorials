Identifying text purpose

AC9E2LY03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the purpose of a given text — to entertain, to inform, to persuade — and connect this to the type of text.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly identifies the purpose of an imaginative, an informative and a persuasive text.
  • Student can name at least one text feature that signals the purpose of each text.

Prior knowledge

Students describe similarities and differences between imaginative, informative and persuasive texts (AC9E1LY03). They have not yet identified specific intended audiences and distinguished these from general purposes.

Width

Minimum
Identify the purpose of one imaginative, one informative and one persuasive text.
Typical
Identify and discuss the purposes of a range of texts across all three types, including texts by First Nations Australian authors, explaining what the purpose is and what text features signal it.
Wider
  • Identify texts written by First Nations Australian authors and discuss their purposes — noting where purposes may include preserving cultural knowledge, sharing stories of Country, or engaging with specific communities
  • Identify advertisements and signs as persuasive texts and discuss what they are trying to persuade the audience to do or believe
