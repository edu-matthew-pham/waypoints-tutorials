Purpose drives structure

AC9E7LA03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how a text's purpose determines the structural choices a writer makes

Lessons on this site

  • Purpose shapes the text
  • Challenge: build an argument

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one structural difference between two texts with different purposes
  • Explains how the structure serves the purpose of each text

Prior knowledge

Students understand how texts are organised into characteristic stages and phases depending on purpose, and how authors adapt text structures and language features (AC9E6LA03). They have not yet worked at the level of identifying and describing structural variation across a wider range of text types including reviews, persuasive texts, and analytical responses.

Width

Minimum
Given two texts with different purposes (e.g. a recount and a persuasive text), identify at least one structural difference and explain how each structure serves its purpose
Typical
Compare the structural logic of three or more text types — how the stages of a review (context → summary → judgement) differ from those of a persuasive text, and why each structure serves its purpose
Wider
  • Examine how the structure of a book or film review moves from description of context to summary to judgement — and why reversing that order would undermine the purpose
  • Identify the social purpose of a text type and trace how that purpose is embedded in its structural stages

Hinge

If students cannot connect purpose to structure, Node 2's work on language feature variation loses its anchoring principle — explaining why language features differ requires first understanding that purpose drives all textual choices.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly describe structural features without connecting them to purpose — they can list 'introduction, body, conclusion' without explaining why that sequence serves the text's goal. Seeing the purpose-structure connection across several distinct text types helps students internalise the principle rather than memorising formats.
