Complex sentences

AC9E4LA06 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand that complex sentences contain one independent clause and at least one dependent clause typically joined by a subordinating conjunction to create relationships, such as time and causality

Prior knowledge

Students understand that clauses contain subjects and verbs that must agree (AC9E3LA06). They have not yet examined complex sentences with independent and dependent clauses joined by subordinating conjunctions to create time or causal relationships.

By the end

Students can identify independent and dependent clauses in complex sentences, name the subordinating conjunctions that join them, and explain what relationship — time, cause, condition, concession — the dependent clause adds to the main clause.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • creating richer, more specific sentences by including information about place, time, manner, cause or condition in a dependent clause; for example, "Denise found a cocoon." becomes "Denise decided to keep an eye on the cocoon until the butterfly emerged."
  • creating more precise and detailed sentences by adding adverbial clauses; for example, "They crossed the mountain range." becomes "Although the path was overgrown, they crossed the mountain range."

Waypoints

  • 1. Independent and dependent clausesHingeStudents can identify the independent clause and at least one dependent clause in a complex sentence.
  • 2. Subordinating conjunctions and relationshipsHingeStudents can identify subordinating conjunctions and explain the relationship each creates between clauses.
  • 3. Using complex sentences for richer meaningStudents can create complex sentences that use dependent clauses to add information about time, cause, manner or condition, making sentences more specific and detailed.
