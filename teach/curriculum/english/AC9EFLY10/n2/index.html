Blending: sounds into words

AC9EFLY10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can blend a sequence of separately spoken phonemes into a recognisable word.

Success criteria

  • Student blends three separately spoken phonemes into a recognisable word.
  • Student can blend words of varying CVC, CVCC and CCVC patterns.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sentences are strings of words.

Width

Minimum
Blend three phonemes into a CVC word when given the sounds separately (e.g. /l/-/i/-/p/ → 'lip').
Typical
Blend phonemes of single-syllable spoken words — including CVC, CVCC and CCVC patterns; produce the target word correctly from the phoneme sequence.
Wider
  • Blend phoneme sequences across a wide range of single-syllable word types — varying vowels, clusters and codas
  • Apply blending to words containing digraphs once grapheme knowledge is established
  • Practise blending through word games, oral riddles and chants — building fluent blending as a reading tool

Hinge

Blending fluency is the core decoding mechanism in LY04 and LY12 — students who cannot reliably blend phonemes into words cannot read connected text using phonics, and their decoding will remain halting and inaccurate.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsBlending fluency is the core decoding mechanism — it must become fast and accurate. Slow or inaccurate blending limits reading. Practice to fluency across word types is required before width adds value.
