← AC9E4LY02 Acknowledge and extend in discussion

Listening for key points

AC9E4LY02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify key points and information in what others say in a discussion and use these in their own contribution.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least one key point from another person's contribution
  • Can reference that point in their own response

Prior knowledge

Students use interaction skills to contribute to conversations and discussions, sharing information and ideas (AC9E3LY02). They have not yet practised acknowledging another's opinion and linking responses to the topic while extending ideas.

Width

Minimum
After listening to a peer's contribution, identify one key point and reference it in a follow-up response.
Typical
Listen actively across a discussion, taking notes or tracking key points, and reference what others have said in own contributions to demonstrate listening and build coherence in the discussion.
Wider
  • Practise active listening in structured discussion formats (Socratic seminar, fishbowl) where referencing others' ideas is required
  • Reflect on one's own listening after a discussion, identifying what was easy and difficult to track and why

Hinge

Acknowledging another's opinion in N2 requires students to have identified key points from that contribution — N2 cannot be performed without N1's active listening.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsActive listening while tracking key points is a fluency habit requiring repeated practice across different discussion contexts before students can reliably identify what to reference.
