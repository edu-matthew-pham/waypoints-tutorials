← AC9E6LY06 Plan, create, edit and publish written and multimodal texts

Editing for structure, meaning and accuracy

AC9E6LY06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can re-read and edit their own and others' work against negotiated criteria for text structure, meaning, grammar, spelling and punctuation, and explain editing choices.

Success criteria

  • Makes at least two edits to a piece of writing and identifies what criterion each addresses
  • Explains at least one editing choice in terms of how it improves the text's purpose, meaning or accuracy

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Creating with varied complex sentences and precise vocabulary.

Width

Minimum
Re-read own writing and make at least two edits against a stated criterion (e.g. punctuation accuracy, vocabulary precision); explain at least one editing choice.
Typical
Re-read and edit own and others' work using negotiated criteria across text structure, meaning, accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation; explain editing choices; use knowledge of comma rules, embedded clauses and word choice to improve writing.
Wider
  • Edit a provided piece of writing against multiple criteria and write a brief editor's note explaining each significant change and why it improves the text
  • Use the editing process to deliberately introduce a literary device and evaluate whether it improves the text

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsEditing accuracy — particularly comma use, embedded clause structure and spelling — requires repeated practice to become reliable. Students who understand the rules conceptually still need editing practice to apply them consistently under production pressure.
