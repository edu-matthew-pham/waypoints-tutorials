← AC9EFLA01 Language use at home and school

Asking, expressing and requesting appropriately

AC9EFLA01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use context-appropriate language when asking for things, expressing opinions or making requests, and can explain why they chose those words.

Success criteria

  • Student can make an appropriate request or express an opinion in a school context using suitable words and tone.
  • Student can explain why they chose the language they used for that context.
  • Student can identify at least one adjustment they would make to the same request in a different relationship context.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Relationships shape language choices.

Width

Minimum
Use appropriate language to make one request or express one opinion in a school context.
Typical
Ask relevant questions, express requests and share opinions in ways that suit the context; reflect on why the language chosen was appropriate for that context.
Wider
  • Role-play the same request (e.g. asking for help) in three different contexts — with a teacher, a classmate, and a family member — and compare the language used
  • Explore how characters in stories ask for things and evaluate whether their language was appropriate for the relationship
  • Investigate how language used in requests differs across culturally diverse contexts represented in classroom texts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierStudents must bring together: knowing the relationship context, choosing appropriate register, and producing the language — three elements that now need to work together in performance. This is where the concept and the capability unite.
