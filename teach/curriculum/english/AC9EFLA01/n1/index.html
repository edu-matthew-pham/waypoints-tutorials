Language varies by context

AC9EFLA01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that different situations call for different language — that how you speak changes depending on where you are.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can name at least one situation where they would speak differently (e.g. talking to a teacher vs talking to a friend).
  • Student can say why the language is different in those two situations.

Prior knowledge

Students bring informal oral language from home, family and community contexts. They have not yet been asked to consciously observe and reflect on how language changes across different relationship contexts.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of language that is used at school but not at home, or vice versa.
Typical
Identify and discuss examples of language used differently at school versus home, noting that the setting affects how we speak.
Wider
  • Compare language used in multiple school settings — in the classroom, at lunch, on the playground — and discuss how it shifts across each
  • Compare language used in multiple home settings — with parents, siblings, grandparents — and identify what changes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents at Foundation may initially treat 'school language' as a fixed thing — the misconception is that language is constant rather than contextually variable. More settings help break the exemplar convergence on a single school/home contrast.
