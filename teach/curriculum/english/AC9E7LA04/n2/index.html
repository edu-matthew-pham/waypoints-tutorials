Structural cohesion devices

AC9E7LA04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can understand how overviews, initial paragraphs and concluding paragraphs function as cohesion devices that guide readers through the whole text

Success criteria

  • Identifies the overview/introduction and conclusion in a given text
  • Explains how each structural device guides the reader

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Cohesion at the paragraph level.

Width

Minimum
Identify the overview or introductory paragraph and the concluding paragraph in a given text; explain what job each does in guiding the reader
Typical
Examine how the introduction (overview of what's coming), body paragraphs (developed with topic sentences), and conclusion (synthesis or restatement) work as a cohesive system — and how each part signals its function to the reader
Wider
  • Compare and analyse the structure of media texts such as digital news sites or print media — how do structural devices create cohesion in texts that may not have traditional paragraph structure?
  • Examine how the absence or disruption of cohesion devices affects readability — what happens when a text omits an overview or has no concluding paragraph?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly understand paragraph-level cohesion but don't extend it to whole-text structural logic. Seeing how overviews and conclusions function across different text types — including non-traditional structures like digital news — helps students understand cohesion as a principle rather than a formula.
