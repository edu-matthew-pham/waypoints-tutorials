Cohesion at the paragraph level

AC9E7LA04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how topic sentences and paragraph structure guide readers through a text

Lessons on this site

  • Tell the reader what's coming

Success criteria

  • Identifies the topic sentence in a given paragraph
  • Explains how the topic sentence signals the paragraph's direction and connects to the text

Prior knowledge

Students understand how cohesion can be created through intentional repetition and word associations (AC9E6LA04). They have not yet worked at the level of structural cohesion devices — how overviews, topic sentences, and concluding paragraphs function to guide readers through a text's logic.

Width

Minimum
In a given text, identify the topic sentence of at least two paragraphs; explain how each signals what the paragraph is about and connects to the text's overall direction
Typical
Examine how topic sentences, sentence openers and text connectives work together to create cohesion across paragraphs — how a reader can follow the text's logic by reading the opening of each paragraph
Wider
  • Identify how authors foreshadow how a text will unfold through sentence openers and text connectives — trace the signposting across a whole text
  • Compare two versions of the same text — one with strong topic sentences, one without — and discuss the difference in readability

Hinge

If students cannot reliably identify topic sentences at the paragraph level, Node 2's whole-text structural logic is inaccessible — recognising how overviews and conclusions function requires first being able to track cohesion at the paragraph level.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTopic sentence identification is a precision skill students use repeatedly across their schooling. Students commonly identify any first sentence as a topic sentence, or miss topic sentences that don't appear first. Practice with varied paragraph structures builds reliable recognition.
