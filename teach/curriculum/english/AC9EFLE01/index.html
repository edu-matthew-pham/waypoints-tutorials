Share ideas about literature

AC9EFLE01 · Foundation English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

share ideas about stories, poems and images in literature, reflecting on experiences that are similar or different to their own by engaging with texts by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors and illustrators

Prior knowledge

Students have heard and interacted with stories at home and in early childhood settings. They have not yet been explicitly asked to reflect on how literary experiences connect to or differ from their own lives, or to engage with literature from a culturally diverse range of authors.

By the end

Students can share ideas about stories, poems and images from a diverse range of authors — connecting literary experiences to their own lives and identifying cultural features that reveal characters' worlds.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • engaging with texts that reflect the social and cultural groups to which students belong
  • comparing experiences depicted in stories by wide-ranging world authors with own experiences
  • viewing stories by First Nations Australian storytellers from print, visual, digital and multimodal sources
  • engaging with texts that portray Australian family life in different settings across Australia; for example, suburban and remote settings
  • identifying some features of culture that are revealed by characters and events in stories; for example, dress, food and daily routines

Waypoints

  • 1. Literature reflects experiencesStudents can connect events or characters in a story to their own experiences, identifying at least one similarity or difference.
  • 2. Cultural details reveal characters' worldsHingeStudents can identify cultural features in stories — dress, food, daily routines — and explain what these reveal about characters' lives.
  • 3. Literature from diverse authors and illustratorsStudents can engage with texts from First Nations Australian, Australian and world authors and illustrators — sharing ideas that reflect awareness of diverse authoring voices.
