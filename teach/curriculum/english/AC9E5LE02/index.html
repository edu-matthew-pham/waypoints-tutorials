Present opinion with literary terms

AC9E5LE02 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Present an opinion on a literary text using specific terms about literary devices, text structures and language features, and reflect on the viewpoints of others

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the effects of text structures and language features in literary texts (AC9E4LE02). They have not yet used specific literary terminology to support and present an opinion, or reflected on how others' viewpoints differ from their own.

By the end

Students present an opinion on a literary text supported by specific references to literary devices, text structures and language features using relevant terminology, and reflect on how the viewpoints of others complement, challenge, or extend their own interpretation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • posing and discussing questions such as 'Should characters have behaved as they did?' and beginning to make judgements about the dilemmas characters face
  • identifying language features such as use of dialogue and rich descriptive language, and presenting an opinion about their effect on readers

Waypoints

  • 1. Opinion supported by textual evidenceStudents can state an opinion about a literary text and support it with specific references to the text, identifying the textual evidence for their view.
  • 2. Using specific literary termsHingeStudents can use specific terms about literary devices, text structures and language features accurately when discussing a literary text and explaining their opinion.
  • 3. Engaging with the viewpoints of othersStudents can reflect on viewpoints expressed by others about a literary text, explaining how those viewpoints are the same as, different from, or extend their own interpretation.
