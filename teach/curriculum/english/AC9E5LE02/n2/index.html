← AC9E5LE02 Present opinion with literary terms

Using specific literary terms

AC9E5LE02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use specific terms about literary devices, text structures and language features accurately when discussing a literary text and explaining their opinion.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two specific literary terms correctly and with explanation — not just appended as labels.
  • The term is used accurately — the feature it names is present in the text at the place cited.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Opinion supported by textual evidence.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two specific literary terms correctly in presenting an opinion about a literary text, explaining how the feature named by each term contributes to the effect being discussed.
Typical
Use a range of specific literary terms — including terms for literary devices, text structural features, and language features — accurately in presenting a literary opinion. Apply terms from the Y5 literature curriculum including those from AC9E5LE03 (point of view), AC9E5LE04 (imagery, simile, metaphor, personification, sound devices) where appropriate.
Wider
  • Compare how using specific vs. vague language when presenting a literary opinion changes the precision and persuasiveness of the opinion — examining what is gained by using exact terminology
  • Research and apply one literary term not yet studied, using it correctly in presenting an opinion and explaining what it adds to the discussion

Hinge

Node 3 requires students to integrate textual evidence, terminology, and reflection on others' viewpoints into a single discussion move. Students who cannot deploy literary terms accurately will be unable to participate meaningfully in the reflective discussion that Node 3 demands — the shared language of literary terms is the medium through which viewpoints are compared and extended.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyLiterary terminology is a vocabulary repertoire that grows across the year and across the curriculum. Students need encounter with terms across multiple texts and discussion contexts to develop reliable accurate use. No single term is a hinge; the goal is expanding the working repertoire of correctly used terms.
