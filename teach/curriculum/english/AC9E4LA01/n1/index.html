Register and formality

AC9E4LA01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 4 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that language changes depending on whether a situation is formal or informal, and give examples of the same idea expressed in each register.

Success criteria

  • Can identify a formal and an informal version of the same communicative act and name at least one language feature that marks the difference
  • Does not treat formal language as simply 'more correct' — can explain that both registers serve different purposes

Prior knowledge

Students understand how cooperation depends on shared social conventions that vary with formality (AC9E3LA01). They have not yet explored how language relationships vary across formal/informal contexts and how cultural background influences interaction.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two examples of formal and informal language in a provided text or scenario and explain why the register differs.
Typical
Compare formal and informal language across two or more familiar contexts (e.g. playground vs classroom report) and identify specific features — vocabulary choices, sentence structure, tone — that signal formality.
Wider
  • Compare written formal and informal registers in the same genre across different purposes, such as a formal letter vs a text message covering the same request
  • Examine how digital communication contexts (email, chat, social media) sit on the formality spectrum and what signals register in each

Hinge

N2's analysis of how factors like age and expertise shape language choices requires students to understand register as contextually appropriate — without dispelling the formal=correct misconception, N2 analysis produces distorted conclusions about why language varies.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often conflate formal language with 'correct' language and informal with 'wrong'. Encountering multiple contexts where informal register is appropriate — and where formal register would be odd — helps displace this misconception.
