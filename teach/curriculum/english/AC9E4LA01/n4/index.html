← AC9E4LA01 Language in formal and informal contexts

Cultural conventions in interaction

AC9E4LA01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how greeting conventions and respectful forms of address vary across cultures, including First Nations Australian practices.

Success criteria

  • Can describe at least one First Nations Australian convention for greeting or addressing Elders and explain its cultural significance
  • Does not treat one cultural convention as the default — can explain that conventions reflect cultural values

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Inclusive language.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one cultural convention that shapes how people greet or address each other formally.
Typical
Compare greeting and address conventions across at least two cultural contexts, including First Nations Australian conventions for respecting Elders, and explain how these reflect cultural values about relationships.
Wider
  • Explore First Nations Australian protocols for greeting, introducing and acknowledging Country and Elders, and compare these with conventions in other cultural groups represented in the class community
  • Investigate how formal greetings and terms of address differ across a range of cultures represented in Australian society and discuss what these differences reveal about values and relationships

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepWithout encountering diverse examples, students default to a single cultural norm as the standard. Multiple cultural contexts help students understand conventions as culturally situated rather than universal.
