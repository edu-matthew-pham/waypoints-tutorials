Create texts for familiar audiences

AC9E2LY06 · Year 2 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

create and edit short imaginative, informative and persuasive written and/or multimodal texts for familiar audiences, using text structure appropriate to purpose, simple and compound sentences, noun groups and verb groups, topic-specific vocabulary, simple punctuation and common 2-syllable words

Prior knowledge

Students create and edit short written and multimodal texts using simple sentences, topic vocabulary and sentence boundary punctuation (AC9E1LY06). They have not yet created persuasive texts, used compound sentences, extended noun groups or verb groups, or edited for vocabulary precision.

By the end

Students can create and edit short imaginative, informative and persuasive written or multimodal texts for familiar audiences using appropriate structure, grammar, vocabulary and punctuation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • creating written texts so that readers follow the sequence of ideas or events
  • using vocabulary, including topic-specific vocabulary, appropriate to the purpose
  • using digital tools to create texts
  • using simple and compound sentences correctly and flexibly
  • editing by adding, deleting or changing vocabulary to improve a text
  • reviewing sentences for grammatical accuracy

Waypoints

  • 1. Selecting structure for purposeHingeStudents can select or plan a text structure appropriate to their purpose — imaginative, informative or persuasive — before writing.
  • 2. Constructing sentences with noun and verb groupsHingeStudents can use simple and compound sentences with extended noun groups and verb groups correctly and flexibly in their writing.
  • 3. Editing for vocabulary and accuracyStudents can edit their own writing by making deliberate vocabulary replacements and reviewing grammatical accuracy.
