← AC9E4LA09 Past, present and future tense

The three tenses — identification

AC9E4LA09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify past, present and future tense in sentences and name the verb forms that signal each.

Success criteria

  • Can correctly identify past, present and future tense in at least three different sentences
  • Can name the verb form change that signals tense (e.g. 'walked' for past, 'will walk' for future)

Prior knowledge

Students understand that verbs are anchored in time through tense (AC9E3LA08). They have not yet examined the three-tense system and the impact of tense choices on meaning across different text types.

Width

Minimum
Identify the tense of at least three sentences and name the verb form used in each.
Typical
Identify past, present and future tense across a range of sentences and texts, noting the different verb forms used to express each tense (simple, progressive, perfect forms where they appear).
Wider
  • Examine how tense varies within a single text (e.g. a narrative that uses past tense for events but present tense for dialogue or reflection) and explain the shifts
  • Compare how different languages mark tense and explore whether tense is always marked by verb form

Hinge

Explaining why text types use particular tenses in N2 requires students to reliably identify the tense being used — tense misidentification in N1 produces incorrect pattern analysis in N2.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsReliable identification of tense — especially in complex verb groups — requires practice. Students commonly mis-identify tense when auxiliary verbs are involved.
