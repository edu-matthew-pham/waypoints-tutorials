Synonyms and antonyms

AC9E4LA11 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

expand vocabulary by exploring a range of synonyms and antonyms, and using words encountered in a range of sources

Prior knowledge

Students extend topic-specific vocabulary and understand that words can have different meanings in different contexts (AC9E3LA10). They have not yet explored synonyms and antonyms as vocabulary-expanding tools with shades of meaning.

By the end

Students can identify synonyms and antonyms for words, explain how synonyms differ in shade of meaning, and apply new vocabulary from a range of sources — including First Nations Australian language words — in their own writing and discussion.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • building vocabulary from research about technical and learning area-specific topics; for example, gravity and magnetic forces
  • determining or clarifying the shades of meaning of synonyms and antonyms
  • using words encountered in texts that are formed from a First Nations Australian language; for example, Woomba Woomba or Toowoom was the place referred to by First Nations Australians on the Darling Downs and then the name was referred to as Toowoomba by the drovers

Waypoints

  • 1. Synonyms and shades of meaningStudents can identify synonyms for a word and explain how synonyms differ in intensity, formality or connotation.
  • 2. AntonymsStudents can identify antonyms and explain how antonym pairs help clarify meaning through contrast.
  • 3. Encountering and using new vocabularyStudents can use words encountered in reading and research — including technical, learning-area and First Nations Australian vocabulary — in their own spoken and written language.
