Experimenting with storylines, characters and settings

AC9E5LE05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw on storylines, characters and settings from texts they have experienced as source material for their own literary writing, making deliberate choices about what to adapt, extend, or transform.

Success criteria

  • Creates a literary text that incorporates at least one element drawn from a model text and demonstrates how it has been transformed.
  • Can explain the creative choices made — what was borrowed, what was changed, and why.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Experimenting with figurative language.

Width

Minimum
Create a literary text that draws on at least one element — storyline, character, or setting — from a text they have experienced, explaining what they borrowed and how they transformed or extended it.
Typical
Create a literary text that uses model texts as creative source material — adapting storylines, borrowing character types, transforming settings — and explain the creative choices made. Demonstrate awareness of what was taken from models and how it has been made their own.
Wider
  • Experiment with retelling a story from a different point of view or in a different setting, discussing how the change transforms the narrative and what the experiment reveals about the original
  • Create a literary text inspired by texts from culturally different contexts — adapting story structures, characters or settings from those texts and reflecting on what the adaptation requires

Response modes
