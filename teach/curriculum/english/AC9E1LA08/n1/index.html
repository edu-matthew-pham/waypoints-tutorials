Images carry meaning

AC9E1LA08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe what meaning an image contributes to a text, independent of the words.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student describes at least one piece of meaning that an image carries independently of the words
  • Student identifies whether the image repeats, extends or contrasts the meaning of the accompanying words

Prior knowledge

Students can explore the contribution of images and words to meaning in stories and informative texts (AC9EFLA07). They have not yet compared how images function differently across different text types, or how image sequences build meaning, or how images can carry meaning not present in the verbal text.

Width

Minimum
Cover the words in a given illustrated text and describe what the image alone communicates.
Typical
Identify and describe the meaning carried by images in texts — what information, emotion or story detail the image communicates — and discuss how this meaning relates to the accompanying words.
Wider
  • Examine texts where the image and words tell different things — discuss what each contributes and why the author might have made that choice
  • Compare a cartoon image, a photograph and a diagram of the same subject and discuss how each conveys meaning differently

Hinge

Students who treat images as mere word-repetition cannot engage with N2's analysis of image sequences or N3's comparison of image types — both depend on students already accepting that images carry independent meaning.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often treat images as illustrations that simply repeat what words say. Encountering examples where images carry distinct or additional meaning — diagrams that explain what words only describe, images that show emotion not stated — resolves this misconception.
