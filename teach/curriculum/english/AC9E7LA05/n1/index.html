← AC9E7LA05 Complex and compound-complex sentences

Clause types and relationships

AC9E7LA05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify independent and dependent clauses and explain the relationship between them in a complex sentence

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the independent and dependent clause in a given complex sentence
  • Explains what the dependent clause contributes to the meaning of the sentence

Prior knowledge

Students understand how embedded clauses can expand complex sentences to elaborate and explain ideas (AC9E6LA05). They have not yet worked with compound-complex sentences — the combination of coordination and subordination in a single sentence — or examined how sentence structure choices serve rhetorical purposes (elaborating, extending, explaining).

Width

Minimum
Label the independent and dependent clauses in a given complex sentence; explain what the dependent clause adds to the independent clause
Typical
Identify clause relationships across several complex sentences; explain how the dependent clause elaborates, extends or qualifies the main idea in each case
Wider
  • Examine how the position of the dependent clause (before or after the independent clause) changes emphasis — 'Although it was raining, we went ahead' vs 'We went ahead, although it was raining'
  • Identify clause relationships in sentences from a range of text types (narrative, explanation, argument) and discuss how subordination serves different purposes in each

Hinge

Clause identification errors here propagate directly into Nodes 2 and 3 — students who cannot reliably distinguish independent from dependent clauses cannot identify compound-complex structure or explain elaboration/extension/explanation relationships.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsClause identification is a precision skill with a high error rate — students commonly mislabel the dependent clause as the main idea, or treat any long clause as 'complex'. Fluency here is the prerequisite for everything that follows; practice with varied sentence structures (not just textbook examples) builds the reliable recognition the later nodes depend on.
