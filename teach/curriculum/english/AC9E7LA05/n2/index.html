Compound-complex sentence structure

AC9E7LA05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 English · Language

Checkpoint

Students can identify and construct compound-complex sentences, distinguishing them from complex sentences

Success criteria

  • Identifies the independent and dependent clauses in a compound-complex sentence
  • Distinguishes a compound-complex sentence from a complex sentence and explains the structural difference

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Clause types and relationships.

Width

Minimum
Given a compound-complex sentence, identify its independent clauses and at least one dependent clause; explain how it differs from a complex sentence
Typical
Construct compound-complex sentences by combining coordination and subordination; discuss how adding a second independent clause changes what the sentence can express compared to a complex sentence alone
Wider
  • Compare a complex and compound-complex version of the same idea — 'When dinosaurs roamed the earth, vegetation depleted' vs 'When dinosaurs roamed the earth, weather patterns shifted significantly and as a result vegetation depleted' — and explain what the compound-complex version adds
  • Identify compound-complex sentences in published texts and discuss why the author chose that structure over a simpler alternative

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly confuse compound-complex sentences with long or complicated sentences generally. The distinction between coordination and subordination — and what happens when both operate in the same sentence — only becomes clear when students see the structure across enough varied examples to isolate the pattern. A single textbook example produces surface recognition; breadth across sentence types and contexts builds the structural understanding.
