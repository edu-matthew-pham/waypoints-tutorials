Text stages and language features

AC9E4LA03 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify how texts across the curriculum have different language features and are typically organised into characteristic stages depending on purposes

Prior knowledge

Students describe how language features and structures in curriculum texts vary according to purpose (AC9E3LA03). They have not yet identified the characteristic stages of specific text types and how stage organisation serves social purposes.

By the end

Students can identify the characteristic stages and language features of a range of curriculum text types — including narratives, information reports, explanations and arguments — and explain how stage organisation and language features together serve each text's social purpose.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the typical stages and language features of texts such as narratives, factual recounts, imaginative recounts, biographies, information reports, explanations, book talks, poetry and arguments for a particular purpose
  • understanding how and why text structure is important in texts such as sequential and causal explanations, and comparative and part-whole information reports
  • recognising that poems have different purposes that influence the organisation into characteristic stages; for example, poems that tell stories, poems that describe and poems that reflect on aspects of life
  • recognising the difference between a text's form such as a poster, email or list and its organisation into stages depending on its social purpose

Waypoints

  • 1. Stages and purposeHingeStudents can explain that texts are organised into stages and that stages serve the text's social purpose.
  • 2. Language features by text typeHingeStudents can identify specific language features associated with particular text types and explain how these features serve the text's purpose.
  • 3. Explanations and reports — structural differencesStudents can distinguish sequential and causal explanations from comparative and part-whole information reports by their stages and language features.
  • 4. Form vs stage organisationStudents can distinguish between a text's form (poster, email, list) and its stage organisation, explaining that different forms can realise the same stages.
