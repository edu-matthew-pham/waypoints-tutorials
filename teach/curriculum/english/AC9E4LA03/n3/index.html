← AC9E4LA03 Text stages and language features

Explanations and reports — structural differences

AC9E4LA03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish sequential and causal explanations from comparative and part-whole information reports by their stages and language features.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify whether a text explains a process or reports information, and name a structural feature supporting that identification
  • Does not confuse sequential explanation (how something happens) with information report (what something is)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Language features by text type.

Width

Minimum
Identify whether a given text is an explanation or an information report and name one structural feature that supports the identification.
Typical
Compare sequential/causal explanation structures with comparative/part-whole report structures across two or more texts, identifying the stages and language features that distinguish them.
Wider
  • Locate examples of sequential, causal and comparative structures in texts from different curriculum areas (science, HASS, maths) and explain how the structure suits the subject content
  • Create a short explanation and a short report on the same topic and compare the structural choices made

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDistinguishing explanation from report structure requires explicit attention to the logic of each — cause-effect chains vs classification-description hierarchies. A reasoning scaffold helps students see why the structures differ, not just that they do.
