Sentence patterns creating tone

AC9E8LE05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how sentence-level choices — including active/passive voice, simple vs complex syntax, sentence length and pattern — create tone in a literary text.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific sentence-level feature (e.g. passive voice, terse syntax) in a literary text.
  • Explains how that feature creates a specific tone, using appropriate metalanguage.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how literary devices and language features create effects in literary texts (AC9E7LE05). They have not yet analysed how sentence-level grammatical choices (active/passive voice, simple vs complex syntax) systematically create tone, or how this connects to the broader operation of imagery in meaning-making.

Width

Minimum
Identify how one sentence-level choice (e.g. active vs passive voice, terse vs elaborate syntax) creates a specific tone in a provided literary passage.
Typical
Analyse how sentence patterns — including active and passive voice, simple and complex syntax, and varied sentence length — create tone across a literary text; explain how different sentence-level choices produce formal, intimate, urgent or detached tones.
Wider
  • Compare how a short story writer and a poet create contrasting tones using different approaches to sentence structure and length
  • Examine how First Nations Australian authors use sentence patterns that reflect oral storytelling rhythms to create a tone distinct from written Western prose tradition
  • Analyse how a single paragraph changes in tone when rewritten using active instead of passive voice, or terse instead of complex syntax

Hinge

Node 2 analyses how imagery creates meaning and effect — a task requiring the feature→effect reasoning chain built at Node 1. Students who identify sentence features without connecting them to tone arrive at Node 2 unable to analyse images for meaning rather than just label them as devices.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often identify sentence features (e.g. 'short sentences') without connecting them to tone (e.g. 'which creates urgency'). The reasoning scaffold — feature → tone → effect — needs to be made explicit and practised before it becomes automatic.
