Imagery creating meaning and effect

AC9E8LE05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how literary devices including imagery — and specifically how simile, metaphor and sensory imagery — create meaning and effect in literary texts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific image in a literary text and names the device (simile, metaphor, personification, sensory image).
  • Analyses the meaning or effect the image creates, explaining what it suggests beyond its literal content.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sentence patterns creating tone.

Width

Minimum
Identify an image (simile, metaphor or sensory image) in a literary text and analyse what meaning it creates — what it suggests beyond its literal content.
Typical
Analyse how imagery and related literary devices create meaning and effect in literary texts; explain how writers use terse or elaborate language choices and how these influence both the meaning created by images and the tone of the text overall.
Wider
  • Analyse how sustained imagery (an extended metaphor or a consistent pattern of images) creates cumulative meaning across a literary text
  • Compare how imagery operates differently in poetry vs prose — examining how compressed imagery in poetry creates different effects from extended imagery in narrative
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian authors use imagery connected to Country, place and natural cycles to create meaning not available through imagery drawn from other traditions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only encounter imagery in poetry may not transfer the analytical skill to prose. Encountering imagery across poetry, prose fiction and drama — and seeing how it operates differently in each — builds a genuinely transferable analytical capability.
