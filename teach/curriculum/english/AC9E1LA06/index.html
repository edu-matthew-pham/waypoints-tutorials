Simple sentences

AC9E1LA06 · Year 1 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand that a simple sentence consists of a single independent clause representing a single event or idea

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that sentences are key units for expressing ideas (AC9EFLA05). They have not yet understood the internal structure of a simple sentence — that it consists of a single independent clause with a process (verb), participants (noun groups) and optional circumstances (adverbs).

By the end

Students understand that a simple sentence is a single independent clause representing one event or idea, containing a process, participants and optional circumstances.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • knowing that a single event or idea can include a process, a happening or a state (verb), the participant or who or what is involved (noun group/phrase), and the surrounding circumstances (adverb group/phrase)
  • understanding that simple sentences answer questions such as 'what is happening?' and 'who or what is involved?' along with details such as 'where?', 'when?', 'how?'

Waypoints

  • 1. A sentence expresses one event or ideaHingeStudents can identify that a sentence expresses one complete event or idea and recognise when a sentence is complete.
  • 2. Sentences have participants and processesStudents can identify the participant (who or what) and the process (what is happening) in a simple sentence.
  • 3. Circumstances add detailStudents can identify circumstantial detail (when, where, how) in simple sentences and explain that these details are optional additions to the core event.
  • 4. The clause as a unitStudents can explain that a simple sentence is a single independent clause — one complete event or idea expressed with a participant, process and optional circumstances.
