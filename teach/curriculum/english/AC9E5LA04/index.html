Starting point and prominence

AC9E5LA04 · Year 5 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Understand how texts can be made cohesive by using the starting point of a sentence or paragraph to give prominence to the message and to guide the reader through the text

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how text connectives including temporal and conditional words, and topic sentences, add cohesion to texts (AC9E4LA04). They have not yet examined how the starting position of a sentence signals what the sentence is about, or how writers manipulate theme position to guide readers.

By the end

Students explain how sentence and paragraph starting points signal to readers what the text is about and how it is developing, identifying how writers use theme position to create cohesion and give prominence to the message.

Waypoints

  • 1. Sentence openers signal topicHingeStudents can identify the sentence opener as signalling what the sentence is about, and explain that the rest of the sentence typically provides new information about that starting point.
  • 2. Theme position creates cohesionStudents can explain how consistent or shifting sentence opener choices create cohesion and guide the reader through the development of a text.
  • 3. Giving prominence through starting positionStudents can explain how placing particular information at the start of a sentence or paragraph gives that information prominence, and identify why a writer has made that choice.
