← AC9E5LA04 Starting point and prominence

Sentence openers signal topic

AC9E5LA04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the sentence opener as signalling what the sentence is about, and explain that the rest of the sentence typically provides new information about that starting point.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the sentence opener in at least three consecutive sentences and explains what topic or frame each one sets up.
  • Explains the relationship between the sentence opener and the new information that follows it.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how text connectives including temporal and conditional words, and topic sentences, add cohesion to texts (AC9E4LA04). They have not yet examined how the starting position of a sentence signals what the sentence is about, or how writers manipulate theme position to guide readers.

Width

Minimum
In a sample text, identify the starting element of several sentences and explain what topic or frame each one signals to the reader.
Typical
Analyse a text to map how sentence openers establish what each sentence is about, and explain how the sequence of openers tracks the development of the text's topic for the reader.
Wider
  • Compare how sentence opener choices differ between narrative, information report and argument texts — explaining why different genres favour different types of starting elements
  • Experiment with rewriting sentence openers in a sample text and discuss how those changes shift the reader's path through the text

Hinge

Node 2's cohesion analysis and Node 3's prominence explanation both depend on students recognising the sentence opener as a deliberate positional choice with a signalling function. Students who do not see the opener as a topic-signal cannot trace how opener sequences create cohesion or reason about why a writer has positioned a specific element first.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often focus on the content of sentences rather than their structural organisation. Encountering sentence openers across different genres — narrative (time or character), report (topic noun), argument (stance or connector) — helps students see that the opener is a deliberate positional choice, not just where a sentence happens to start.
