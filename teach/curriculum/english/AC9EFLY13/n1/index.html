← AC9EFLY13 Use letters and sounds to spell

Spelling is a strategy, not just memory

AC9EFLY13 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Foundation English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can attempt to spell an unknown word by applying letter-sound knowledge — producing a plausible spelling attempt rather than random letters.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student makes a plausible spelling attempt — each phoneme in the word is represented by a letter.
  • Student uses the spelling strategy without needing to be told 'try to spell it'.
  • Student's spelling attempt reflects the sounds in the word, even if not conventionally correct.

Prior knowledge

Students can write CVC words (AC9EFLY12) and have letter-sound knowledge. They have not yet been explicitly asked to apply letter-sound knowledge independently as a self-directed spelling strategy across unknown words.

Width

Minimum
Produce a plausible letter-sound attempt for at least one unknown word encountered in writing.
Typical
Use knowledge of letters and sounds to make plausible spelling attempts; understand that spelling unknown words involves applying phoneme-grapheme knowledge; produce attempts where each phoneme is represented.
Wider
  • Apply the spelling strategy to unknown topic words in different learning areas — practising transfer of the skill
  • Compare a plausible phonemic spelling attempt with the conventional spelling — identifying where letter-sound rules were applied correctly and where other knowledge is needed
  • Use the spelling strategy in independent writing tasks across different text purposes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents may apply the spelling strategy only in explicit phonics tasks and not in independent writing. Multiple writing contexts — across different purposes and topics — help generalise the strategy so it is used independently.
