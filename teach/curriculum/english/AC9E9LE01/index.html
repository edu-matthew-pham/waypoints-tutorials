Representations of people and places across contexts

AC9E9LE01 · Year 9 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse the representations of people and places in literary texts, drawn from historical, social and cultural contexts, by First Nations Australian, and wide-ranging Australian and world authors

Prior knowledge

Students identify and explore ideas, viewpoints and issues in literary texts across historical, social and cultural contexts (AC9E7LE01). They have not yet analysed how representations of people and places are constructed — examining the choices authors make and what those choices reveal about context.

By the end

Students analyse how representations of people and places in literary texts reflect and are shaped by historical, social and cultural contexts, across First Nations Australian, Australian and world authors.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring and comparing representations of values of characters; for example, exploring the values associated with authority, community and family in literature drawn from different cultures and times
  • exploring how texts by First Nations Australian authors reflect unique ways of being, knowing, thinking and doing
  • exploring the way wide-ranging Australian novels, poems and films represent water and characters' relationships with water

Waypoints

  • 1. Representation as constructionHingeStudents can identify that representations of people and places in literary texts are constructed — the product of authorial choices — not neutral or natural.
  • 2. Context shaping representationStudents can analyse how historical, social and cultural contexts shape how people and places are represented in literary texts.
