Framing choices

AC9E4LA10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how framing choices — close-up, mid-shot, wide shot — affect what the viewer sees and feels about an image.

Success criteria

  • Can identify the framing type of an image and link it to at least one effect on the viewer
  • Can explain that framing is a deliberate choice, not an accident

Prior knowledge

Students identify how images extend the meaning of a text (AC9E3LA09). They have not yet examined how framing, element placement and salience are deliberate compositional choices that influence viewer response in still and moving images.

Width

Minimum
Identify the framing of an image (close-up, mid or wide shot) and explain one effect the framing has on the viewer.
Typical
Compare framing choices across a range of still and moving images, explaining how close-up frames emphasise detail and emotion, mid shots show action and relationship, and wide shots establish setting and scale.
Wider
  • Compare how the same subject photographed with different framings creates different emotional responses, using a sequence of images
  • Examine how framing choices in film or television advertising position the viewer in relation to the product or character
