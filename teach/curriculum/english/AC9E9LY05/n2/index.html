Comparing and contrasting across texts

AC9E9LY05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 English · Literacy

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare and contrast ideas and opinions in and between texts, identifying positions, evaluating evidence and summarising the relationship between them.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies specific ideas or opinions from each text and explains how they compare
  • Student's comparison goes beyond listing — it explains what the similarities and differences reveal about the texts' positions or purposes

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Comprehension strategies for comparison.

Width

Minimum
Compare ideas or opinions from two texts on the same topic, identifying how they are similar, how they differ, and what the difference reveals about the texts' positions.
Typical
Compare and contrast representations of an event in print and digital sources — summarising qualities, identifying opinions and analysing evidence — and compare articles representing a current event, contrasting ideas and opinions across texts.
Wider
  • Compare visual representations of the same issue across online news sources and evaluate how the visual choices support or contradict the verbal argument
  • Compare three or more texts on the same issue and map the ideological positions they represent — evaluating whether any positions are absent

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCross-text comparison requires students to hold multiple texts in mind simultaneously, coordinate their respective positions, and synthesise a view of their relationship. This integration — across texts, modes and positions — is the core challenge of the descriptor.
