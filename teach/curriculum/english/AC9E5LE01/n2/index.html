← AC9E5LE01 Historical and cultural contexts

First Nations Australian literary contexts

AC9E5LE01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify aspects of First Nations Australian literary texts that convey information about historical context, cultural practice, or the impact of events on community life.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two aspects of a First Nations Australian literary text and explains what historical or cultural context they convey.
  • Explains how the author's perspective or cultural position shapes the representation of context in the text.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Literary details carry context.

Width

Minimum
In a text by a First Nations Australian author, identify at least two aspects — images, language, dialogue, character description — that convey information about historical or cultural context, and explain what those aspects reveal.
Typical
Explore and discuss texts by First Nations Australian authors about events that shaped or had an impact on their lives, identifying how literary choices represent historical context. Explain how First Nations Australian authors position readers in relation to those contexts.
Wider
  • Explore historical context in texts by First Nations Australian authors about specific events (e.g. the Stolen Generations, land rights, community history) and discuss how literary choices make those events present for readers
  • Compare how the same historical events are represented in texts by First Nations Australian authors and by other Australian authors, examining what is foregrounded, backgrounded, or absent in each

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents may not have background knowledge about the historical and cultural contexts in First Nations Australian literature, making the literary details opaque. Engaging with a range of texts — and with contextual information about events they represent — helps students develop the interpretive knowledge needed to read these texts with understanding rather than surface recognition.
