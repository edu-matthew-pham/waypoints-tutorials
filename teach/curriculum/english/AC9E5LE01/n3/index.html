← AC9E5LE01 Historical and cultural contexts

Ballads and historical context across Australian literature

AC9E5LE01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 5 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify aspects of Australian literary texts — including ballads from different times — that convey historical and social context, and discuss how those contexts are represented through literary choices.

Success criteria

  • Identifies aspects of an Australian literary text that convey its historical period and explains what those aspects reveal about the social world of the text.
  • Makes a connection between a specific literary choice (language, image, character detail) and the historical or social context it represents.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: First Nations Australian literary contexts.

Width

Minimum
In an Australian literary text (including a ballad), identify at least two aspects that convey historical or social context and explain what those aspects reveal about the time or community represented.
Typical
Explore a range of Australian literary texts including ballads from different time periods, identifying how literary choices — character, setting, language, imagery — represent the historical and social contexts of their time. Compare how different periods' concerns and values are made visible through literary choices.
Wider
  • Compare how two Australian ballads from different historical periods represent the values, concerns and social conditions of their respective times — examining what has changed and what has stayed constant in Australian social life
  • Examine how contemporary Australian authors represent historical events from earlier periods, discussing how temporal distance affects literary representation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuilding familiarity with texts from different periods and cultural contexts — First Nations Australian, colonial, contemporary Australian, and world literature — is the long-term goal. Each text adds to the repertoire of contextual knowledge students bring to reading. Breadth of text encounter is the investment signal here.
