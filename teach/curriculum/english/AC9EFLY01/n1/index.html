← AC9EFLY01 Identify familiar texts and purpose

Texts have features we can see

AC9EFLY01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation English · Literacy · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify observable features of a familiar text — noting what is on the page or screen beyond just the words.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can point to at least two distinct features in a familiar text.
  • Student can name what each feature is (e.g. 'That's a list', 'That's a heading').

Prior knowledge

Students encounter texts in home and community settings daily. They have not yet been explicitly asked to identify text features that signal purpose, or to connect specific features (ingredients list, numbered steps, images) to the purpose of the text.

Width

Minimum
Name at least one visible feature of a familiar text beyond words alone (e.g. pictures, a list, numbers).
Typical
Identify multiple visual and structural features of familiar texts — headings, images, lists, numbered steps, labels; discuss what these features look like across different text types.
Wider
  • Compare the features of two different text types side by side — noting what is the same and what is different
  • Collect and sort examples of text features (lists, headings, pictures, numbered steps) from a range of classroom and everyday texts

Hinge

Without being able to observe and name specific text features, students cannot connect those features to purpose in Node 2 — feature identification is the perceptual prerequisite for the feature-purpose reasoning that follows.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFeature recognition must become reliable across multiple text types before students can reason about what those features signal. Practice across diverse texts builds the naming habit.
