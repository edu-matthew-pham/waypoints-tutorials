Subjective and objective language

AC9E4LA02 · Year 4 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify the subjective language of opinion and feeling, and the objective language of factual reporting

Prior knowledge

Students understand how evaluative and emotive language including modal verbs can vary in forcefulness (AC9E3LA02). They have not yet distinguished subjective language of opinion/feeling from objective language of factual reporting as distinct registers with different purposes.

By the end

Students can identify subjective language (opinion, feeling, thinking verbs) and objective language (factual reporting, summary verbs) in texts, explain the different purposes each serves, and recognise when each is appropriate.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying ways thinking verbs are used to express opinions; for example, "I think", "I believe", and ways summary verbs are used to report findings; for example, "we concluded"
  • comparing statements that have similar information presented objectively and subjectively; for example, "The man has 6 cats." "The man has too many noisy cats."

Waypoints

  • 1. Language of opinion and feelingHingeStudents can identify thinking verbs and emotive vocabulary that signal a subjective point of view in a text.
  • 2. Language of factual reportingHingeStudents can identify summary verbs and neutral vocabulary that signal objective, factual reporting in a text.
  • 3. Purpose and context of each registerStudents can explain why writers choose subjective or objective language depending on the purpose and context of their text.
