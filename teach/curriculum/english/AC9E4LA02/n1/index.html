Language of opinion and feeling

AC9E4LA02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 4 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify thinking verbs and emotive vocabulary that signal a subjective point of view in a text.

Lessons on this site

  • Fact or feeling?

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least two thinking verbs in a text and explain that they signal personal opinion
  • Can distinguish emotive vocabulary (e.g. 'wonderful', 'terrible') from neutral descriptors

Prior knowledge

Students understand how evaluative and emotive language including modal verbs can vary in forcefulness (AC9E3LA02). They have not yet distinguished subjective language of opinion/feeling from objective language of factual reporting as distinct registers with different purposes.

Width

Minimum
Identify thinking verbs (e.g. 'I think', 'I believe', 'I feel') in a provided text and explain that these signal the writer's personal view.
Typical
Identify a range of subjective language features across texts — thinking verbs, emotive adjectives, evaluative adverbs — and explain how these position the reader to share or consider the author's viewpoint.
Wider
  • Compare the use of subjective language across different opinion text types — a review, a letter to the editor, a persuasive speech — and identify which features are consistent and which vary by genre
  • Explore how First Nations Australian authors use subjective voice to position readers in relation to cultural knowledge and experience

Hinge

Identifying objective language in N2 requires students to know what subjective features look like — the contrast between thinking verbs/emotive vocabulary (N1) and summary verbs/neutral vocabulary (N2) is how students distinguish the two registers.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSubjective language features vary widely across opinion text types — students need multiple examples before the pattern is distinguishable from genre-specific features, and before the subjective/objective contrast is reliably seen.
