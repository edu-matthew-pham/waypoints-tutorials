← AC9E4LA02 Subjective and objective language

Language of factual reporting

AC9E4LA02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 4 English · Language · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify summary verbs and neutral vocabulary that signal objective, factual reporting in a text.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least one summary verb and explain that it reports a finding rather than expressing an opinion
  • Can distinguish 'The man has 6 cats' (objective) from 'The man has too many noisy cats' (subjective) and name the feature that marks each

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Language of opinion and feeling.

Width

Minimum
Identify summary verbs (e.g. 'we concluded', 'the report found', 'results showed') in a provided text and explain that these signal reported facts rather than personal views.
Typical
Identify objective language features across informational texts — summary verbs, precise nouns, impersonal constructions, present tense for facts — and explain how these create an effect of factual authority.
Wider
  • Compare how objective language is used differently in scientific reports, news articles and encyclopaedia entries — same information, different degrees of formality
  • Examine how news reporting attempts objectivity and identify moments where subjective language creeps in

Hinge

N3's explanation of why writers choose each register requires students to accurately characterise what each register consists of — the misconception about objective language from N2 produces inaccurate purpose analysis in N3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStudents commonly conflate objective language with 'no adjectives' or 'short sentences'. Precision about what objective language features are — summary verbs, impersonal constructions — requires consolidation through multiple examples.
