Cohesion: repetition and word associations

AC9E6LA04 · Year 6 English · Language

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

understand that cohesion can be created by the intentional use of repetition, and the use of word associations

Prior knowledge

Students understand how texts can be made cohesive by using the starting point of a sentence or paragraph as a signal of its theme (AC9E5LA04). They have not yet examined how repetition of key words and word associations (general/specific substitution, synonyms, antonyms) build cohesion across a text.

By the end

Students understand how cohesion is built through intentional repetition and word associations, and can identify these devices and explain their effect in texts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • noting how a general word is often used for a more specific word already mentioned (word association); for example, "Look at those apples. Granny Smiths are my favourite."
  • recognising how cohesion can be developed through repeating key words or by using synonyms or antonyms

Waypoints

  • 1. Repetition as a cohesion deviceStudents can identify repetition of key words and synonyms in a text and explain how this repetition creates cohesive links across sentences.
  • 2. Word associations and lexical cohesionHingeStudents can identify word associations — where a general word stands in for a more specific word previously mentioned — and explain how this creates cohesion.
  • 3. Using cohesive devices in own writingStudents can use repetition, synonyms and word associations intentionally to build cohesion in their own writing.
