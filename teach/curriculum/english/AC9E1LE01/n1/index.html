← AC9E1LE01 Language and images create meaning in literature

Characters are constructed through language and image

AC9E1LE01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 English · Literature

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in English: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can discuss how an author or illustrator uses specific words or images to create a character — what they look like, how they feel, what kind of person or creature they are.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least two specific language or image choices and links each to what it tells the reader about the character
  • Student uses the language of author/illustrator craft (e.g. 'the author chose... to show that...')

Prior knowledge

Students can share ideas about stories, poems and images in literature, reflecting on experiences (AC9EFLE01). They have not yet discussed specifically how language choices and image choices work to construct characters, settings and events, or engaged with the diversity of literature from First Nations Australian, Australian and world authors.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two language or image choices in a text and explain what each tells the reader about a character.
Typical
Discuss how authors use word choices (describing words, dialogue, actions) and illustrators use image choices (appearance, expression, colour) to create characters, using specific examples from familiar texts.
Wider
  • Compare how the same type of character (e.g. an animal with human qualities) is created differently across two texts from different authors or cultures
  • Discuss how characters in literature by First Nations Australian authors and illustrators are depicted, exploring what the choices tell us about the character and their world

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents need to encounter diverse author and illustrator choices — not just one familiar style — to understand that character construction is a crafted decision, not a natural consequence. Texts from different cultural contexts make this especially visible.
