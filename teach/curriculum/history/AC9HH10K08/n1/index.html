The White Australia policy — logic, operation, and dismantling

AC9HH10K08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the ideological basis and operation of the White Australia policy and analyse the causes of its gradual dismantling between the late 1960s and 1973.

Success criteria

  • Explains the ideological basis of the White Australia policy — not merely that it was discriminatory
  • Identifies at least two distinct causes of its dismantling and explains the mechanism of each
  • Traces the key steps in dismantling from Holt (1966) to Whitlam (1973)

Prior knowledge

Students have examined successive migration waves and their effects on Australian society in K07. They understand the 'White Australia' policy as a boundary condition and know that it was dismantled between the late 1960s and 1973. They have not yet examined the specific causes of attitudinal change toward migration, the arguments over multiculturalism, or how contemporary immigration policy debates evolved.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the logic of the White Australia policy; identify at least two causes of its dismantling.
Typical
Students examine the White Australia policy's ideological foundations (racial hierarchy, fear of Asian labour competition, British identity); its operation through the Immigration Restriction Act 1901 and the dictation test; the arguments for and against the policy as it came under pressure (economic development needs, Cold War alliance politics, Asian diplomatic relationships, changing global norms post-Holocaust and UDHR); trace its gradual dismantling — Holt's 1966 changes, Whitlam's final abolition in 1973.
Wider
  • Examine the role of Cold War diplomacy in eroding the White Australia policy — analysing how Australia's relationships with non-white Asian allies (South Korea, South Vietnam, Malaysia) made overt racial exclusion increasingly untenable
  • Investigate the arguments of those who defended the policy to the end — analysing what values and fears they expressed, and what this reveals about the gap between official ideology and social attitudes

Hinge

The White Australia policy's logic and dismantling is the structural context for understanding all subsequent attitudinal change toward migration. Students who do not understand why the policy existed and how it was argued away cannot analyse the causes of changing perspectives — they can only describe that attitudes changed.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat the White Australia policy as obviously wrong and its dismantling as obviously inevitable. The scaffold is: what was the internal logic of the policy for those who supported it? what specific forces eroded that logic? This makes the change analytically explicable rather than morally self-evident.
