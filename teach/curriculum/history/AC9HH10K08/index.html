Changing perspectives on migration

AC9HH10K08 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the causes of changes in perspectives, responses, beliefs and values on migration that have influenced Australian society since 1945

Prior knowledge

Students have examined successive migration waves and their effects on Australian society in K07. They understand the 'White Australia' policy as a boundary condition and know that it was dismantled between the late 1960s and 1973. They have not yet examined the specific causes of attitudinal change toward migration, the arguments over multiculturalism, or how contemporary immigration policy debates evolved.

By the end

Students analyse the causes of changes in perspectives on migration since 1945, including the dismantling of the White Australia policy, the contribution of migration to Australia's changing identity, and the evolution of contemporary immigration debates.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using sources to analyse the changing attitudes to the arrival of migrants in the 1950s, 1960s, 1970s and 1980s
  • analysing the arguments for and against the “White Australia” policy between 1964 and 1973 or the Child Migration scheme 1922–1967
  • discussing the contribution of migration to Australia’s changing identity as a nation; for example, the influence of different cultural and religious groups with the arrival of different waves of migrants, such as those from Europe in the 1950s–1960s, from different parts of Asia in the 1970s–2000s, from the Middle East in the 1980s–1990s, from India in the 1990s–2000s or from Africa in the 2000s
  • discussing the debate over multiculturalism that arose in the 1980s
  • discussing how debates over different aspects of Australia’s immigration and border protection policies have changed over time in light of international events, including consideration of issues such as mandatory detention of asylum seekers, humanitarian migration, business and skilled migration, and temporary protection visas, from the early 1990s to the present day

Waypoints

  • 1. The White Australia policy — logic, operation, and dismantlingHingeStudents can explain the ideological basis and operation of the White Australia policy and analyse the causes of its gradual dismantling between the late 1960s and 1973.
  • 2. Changing attitudes to migrants — decade by decadeStudents can analyse how Australian public attitudes to migration changed across the 1950s–1980s, using sources to identify shifts in perspective and explain their causes.
  • 3. Migration and Australian identity — the multiculturalism debateStudents can explain the contribution of migration to Australia's changing national identity and analyse the debate over multiculturalism that emerged in the 1980s.
  • 4. Contemporary immigration debates — continuity and change in attitudesStudents can explain how immigration and border protection debates have evolved from the 1990s to the present, connecting contemporary debates to the longer history of Australian attitudes to migration.
