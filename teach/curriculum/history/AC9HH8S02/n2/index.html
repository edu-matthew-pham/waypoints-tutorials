Locating sources digitally

AC9HH8S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can retrieve relevant information from multiple digital sources — including repositories of primary sources, maps and photographs of artefacts — using advanced search functions to refine results.

Success criteria

  • Retrieves relevant sources from at least two different digital repositories or source types
  • Uses at least one advanced search technique (filters, date ranges, specific search terms) and explains how it improved the search results

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Categorising sources.

Width

Minimum
Use of at least two different digital source types with at least one example of using advanced search functions (e.g. filters, date ranges, search terms) to refine results.
Typical
Systematic use of digital repositories to locate primary sources; use of online platforms hosting maps, photographs of culturally significant artefacts, and academic databases; understanding of how to assess the relevance of retrieved sources before committing to using them.
Wider
  • Compare two searches on the same topic using different search strategies (broad vs specific terms, with vs without filters) to examine how search design affects the quality of retrieved sources
  • Investigate how to locate primary sources from non-European medieval societies (e.g. Islamic manuscripts, Chinese diplomatic records, Icelandic sagas) using digital repositories, examining how the availability of digitised sources varies by culture and period

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDigital source retrieval is a procedural skill that requires practice with real repositories. Students need hands-on experience with multiple searches before they can reliably locate relevant sources and assess their relevance.
