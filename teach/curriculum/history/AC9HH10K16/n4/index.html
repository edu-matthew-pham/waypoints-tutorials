The environmental movement as a global influence

AC9HH10K16 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the causes of the environmental movement and analyse its effects on changing public opinion about environmental protection.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two causes of the environmental movement with evidence
  • Describes how the movement changed public opinion about environmental protection
  • Gives at least one Australian example of the environmental movement's influence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: American and British cultural influences — popular culture transformation.

Width

Minimum
Students explain at least two causes of the environmental movement and describe how it changed public opinion about environmental protection.
Typical
Students examine the causes of the environmental movement — rapid population growth, urbanisation, industrial pollution, species extinction, nuclear testing and fallout, Rachel Carson's Silent Spring; examine how the movement changed from a marginal concern to a mainstream political issue; examine Australian manifestations — the Franklin Dam controversy, the Great Barrier Reef campaigns, the Australian Conservation Foundation; connect to K14's content on changing perspectives on sustainability.
Wider
  • Examine the Franklin Dam controversy as a pivotal Australian environmental campaign — analysing how it changed the political dynamics of environmentalism in Australia and contributed to the emergence of the Greens as a political force
  • Investigate how anti-nuclear activism in Australia connected to the broader environmental movement — examining the Roxby Downs uranium mining debate and the Rainbow Warrior affair as cases where international and domestic environmental politics intersected

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to explain the environmental movement as a response to specific conditions — not as a natural and inevitable development. Connecting each cause to a specific change in public opinion or policy applies the causal analysis framework from N1.
