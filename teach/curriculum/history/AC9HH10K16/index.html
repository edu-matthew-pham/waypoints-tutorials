Major global influences on post-war Australia

AC9HH10K16 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

causes and effects of the significant events and developments of the major global influences on Australia in the post-Second World War period

Prior knowledge

Students have examined Australia's post-war reconstruction and strategic realignment (K05). They understand the Cold War context from general knowledge. They have not yet examined the Cold War's specific domestic effects on Australia, the long boom's economic character, or the cultural transformation of the 1950s–60s in detail.

By the end

Students explain the causes and effects of major global influences on Australia in the post-war period, including the Cold War, the long boom, and cultural transformation through American and British popular culture.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating how the fear of communism and the emergence of the Cold War impacted on Australian society, particularly in relation to political debates, immigration and some aspects of popular culture including music, film and sport
  • examining the “the long boom” under prime minister Robert Menzies, in particular the expansion of primary industry exports (wool and iron ore) and the growth of protected manufacturing industries such as steel, textiles, electrical goods and automobiles, and the promotion of home ownership
  • analysing the causes of cultural development and the effects on Australian society such as the post–Second World War baby boom and the resulting effect of the development of youth culture in the 1960s
  • investigating America’s cultural influences, such as the arrival of television for the Melbourne Olympics (1956) and Bill Haley’s Australian tour (1957), and British popular influences, such as the Mersey sound and The Beatles’ tour of 1964
  • comparing views on the values and beliefs of rock ‘n’ roll, film and television across time, age and gender, such as issues of conservatism and rebellion, the challenge to established ideas and national identity
  • analysing the causes and conditions that led to the environment movement and its effects on changing public opinion about protecting the environment, such as rapid population increase, urbanisation, increases in industrial pollution and waste, and species extinction

Waypoints

  • 1. The Cold War's domestic effects on AustraliaHingeStudents can explain how the fear of communism and the emergence of the Cold War impacted on Australian society — in political debates, immigration, and popular culture.
  • 2. The long boom — economic prosperity and its characterStudents can explain the long boom under Menzies, describing the character of post-war economic prosperity and its social effects.
  • 3. American and British cultural influences — popular culture transformationStudents can explain the American and British cultural influences on Australia in the post-war period and analyse how popular culture transformed Australian society.
  • 4. The environmental movement as a global influenceStudents can explain the causes of the environmental movement and analyse its effects on changing public opinion about environmental protection.
