The long boom — economic prosperity and its character

AC9HH10K16 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the long boom under Menzies, describing the character of post-war economic prosperity and its social effects.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two features of the long boom with evidence
  • Explains how post-war prosperity contributed to the baby boom and the development of youth culture
  • Connects the long boom to the social conditions that shaped the 1960s

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The Cold War's domestic effects on Australia.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least two features of the long boom and explain the social effects of post-war prosperity.
Typical
Students examine the long boom's economic character — expansion of primary industry exports (wool, iron ore), growth of protected manufacturing (steel, textiles, autos), the promotion of home ownership, full employment; explain the baby boom as a demographic consequence of post-war optimism; examine how prosperity created the conditions for a new youth culture in the 1960s — higher disposable income, longer secondary education, the teenage consumer; connect the long boom to the social stability that made subsequent cultural challenges seem more disruptive.
Wider
  • Examine the economic nationalism of the Menzies era — analysing how tariff protection created domestic manufacturing that was later exposed when protectionism was dismantled, and what this reveals about the limits of the long boom model
  • Investigate the regional distribution of long boom prosperity — examining how the economic growth of the 1950s–60s was distributed across Australia and what groups were excluded from its benefits

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a solid picture of the long boom's character before the cultural transformation of N3 makes sense — the baby boom creating youth culture, prosperity enabling consumer culture, and stability providing the contrast against which 1960s challenges seemed radical.
