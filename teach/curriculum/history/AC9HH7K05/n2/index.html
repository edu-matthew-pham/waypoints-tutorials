Food production and procurement systems

AC9HH7K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how food production and procurement systems — including agriculture, aquaculture, nomadism, and hunter-gatherer/forager practices — were developed by early First Nations Australians in response to different environments.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two distinct food production or procurement systems and connects each to a specific environmental context.
  • Explains that different environments produced different food systems — not a single, uniform approach across the continent.
  • Identifies that these systems reflect sophisticated ecological knowledge, not simply opportunistic foraging.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Stone tool technologies.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two distinct food production or procurement systems and connect each to an environmental context (e.g. maritime technologies in coastal areas, nomadic strategies in arid environments).
Typical
Describe a range of food production and procurement systems across different environmental contexts — maritime technologies on Torres Strait Islands and Cape York, nomadic lifestyles in arid environments, forager strategies, and sustainable harvesting practices — explaining how each system reflects sophisticated knowledge of local environments and resources.
Wider
  • Investigate the Mithaka People's sustainable harvesting practices in Queensland's Channel Country as a specific case study — examining what these practices involved, how they were organised, and what they reveal about ecological knowledge.
  • Explore the distinction between hunter-gatherer/forager practices and agriculture, examining whether the binary is adequate for describing First Nations Australians' sophisticated food systems, some of which involved deliberate cultivation and landscape management.
  • Examine the development of maritime technologies — canoes, fishing implements, tidal fish traps — in coastal and island communities, and how these technologies enabled access to marine resources that extended food security.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly apply a single, undifferentiated mental model of hunter-gatherer societies to all First Nations Australians — missing the diversity and sophistication of different food systems across different environments. Exposure to multiple, geographically distinct examples (Torres Strait maritime, Channel Country sustainable harvesting, arid-zone nomadism) specifically resolves this generalisation and builds understanding of environmental-technological diversity.
