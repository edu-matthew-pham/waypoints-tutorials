Stone tool technologies

AC9HH7K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the development of stone tool technologies by early First Nations Australians — including ground-edge tools, hafted axes, and millstones — and explain what these technologies reveal about cultural and technological sophistication over time.

Success criteria

  • Names and describes at least two stone tool technologies and their primary uses.
  • Explains stone-knapping as a skilled craft requiring specialist knowledge, not simply 'chipping rocks'.
  • Identifies that tool technologies developed and were refined over time — they were not static.

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how First Nations Australians responded to environmental processes and changes over time, including specific adaptive strategies involving water management and seasonal movement (AC9HH7K04). They have not yet examined the technological achievements of early First Nations Australians as a category in their own right, including the development and sophistication of stone tools, food production systems, and land and water management technologies.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two stone tool technologies (e.g. ground-edge tools, hafted axes, millstones) and explain what each was used for.
Typical
Describe the development and refinement of stone tool technologies over time — including the shift from core tools to more refined technologies such as ground-edge tools, hafted axes, and millstones — and explain how stone-knapping as a skill represents a body of knowledge transmitted and refined across generations.
Wider
  • Investigate the chronology of stone tool development — from early core and flake tools to the microliths and backed artefacts of later periods — examining what the changes reveal about cognitive and cultural development.
  • Examine specific stone-knapping techniques and the knowledge required to produce particular tool types, including how regional variation in tool technology reflects both available materials and local needs.
  • Explore how stone tool technologies have been dated and what they tell us about the distribution of populations and cultural exchange networks across the continent.

Hinge

Students who lack a concrete repertoire of specific stone tool technologies cannot engage meaningfully with N3's integration task — the descriptor's claim about technological contribution to daily life requires specific examples as raw material for the synthesis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a repertoire of specific technology examples before they can reason about development across places or contribution to daily life. Without knowing what ground-edge tools, hafted axes, and millstones actually are and do, the broader claims about technological achievement are abstractions. Building a concrete inventory of technologies is the prerequisite for the integration work in later nodes.
