Social, cultural, economic and political changes in Australian society

AC9HH9K02 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the key social, cultural, economic and political changes and their significance in the development of Australian society during the period

Prior knowledge

Students can identify significant events, developments and turning points in earlier periods and describe causes and effects of change (AC9HH8K03, AC9HH8K07). They have not yet examined the specific development of the Australian colonies — the different colonial contexts, the economic foundations (pastoral, agricultural, mining), or the trajectory from colonial settlement to Federation.

By the end

Students examine the different colonial contexts and explain their influences on development; describe the development of Australia's economy from settlement to WW1, including agricultural and pastoral industries; and examine key social, cultural, economic and political changes and their significance in the development of Australian society.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the different contexts of the Australian colonies and explaining their influences; for example, analysing and evaluating the effects of physical isolation on the development of the Swan River Colony in Western Australia, the deliberate exclusion of convicts in the colonisation of South Australia, the discovery of gold on the development of Victoria, and the expansion across the Blue Mountains in New South Wales
  • examining the development of Australia's economy from the early days of settlement up to the First World War, in particular agricultural and pastoral industries, and the key role played by the "squattocracy" and figures such as Elizabeth Macarthur

Waypoints

  • 1. The colonial contexts — different origins, different developmentsHingeStudents can describe the different circumstances in which Australian colonies were established and explain how those circumstances shaped their early development.
  • 2. Economic foundations — pastoral, agricultural and mining industriesStudents can describe the development of Australia's economy from early settlement to WW1, focusing on pastoral and agricultural industries, the role of key figures and groups, and the significance of gold.
  • 3. Significance of changes for Australian society's developmentStudents can examine key social, cultural, economic and political changes in the period and explain their significance for the development of Australian society — identifying what changed, what persisted, and what these changes made possible.
