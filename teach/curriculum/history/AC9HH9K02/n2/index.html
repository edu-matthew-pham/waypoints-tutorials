Economic foundations — pastoral, agricultural and mining industries

AC9HH9K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the development of Australia's economy from early settlement to WW1, focusing on pastoral and agricultural industries, the role of key figures and groups, and the significance of gold.

Success criteria

  • Describes the wool/pastoral industry and the role of the squattocracy in colonial economic development
  • Explains the significance of the gold rushes for Victoria's development, including demographic and economic effects

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The colonial contexts — different origins, different developments.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the wool industry as the economic backbone of colonial Australia, explain the role of the squattocracy in pastoral expansion, and describe the impact of the gold rushes on Victoria's development.
Typical
Students describe the wool and pastoral industries — the expansion of sheep runs, the role of squatters in occupying land beyond surveyed boundaries, the political conflict between squatters and small selectors; the gold rushes of the 1850s — their demographic effects (massive immigration), economic effects (capital, infrastructure), and social effects (class mixing, Chinese immigration and anti-Chinese sentiment); agricultural development in the late 19th century (wheat, dairy); the interconnection of pastoral wealth with political power and land policy; figures like Elizabeth Macarthur as examples of individual agency in economic development.
Wider
  • Examine the political conflict between squatters and selectors — what did land policy disputes reveal about whose interests the colonial governments served?
  • Investigate the role of Chinese miners on the Victorian goldfields — examining their experience of discrimination and contribution to colonial economic life, connecting to AC9HH9K06

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly focus on the gold rushes as the dominant economic story without understanding the longer trajectory — the wool economy as the colonial foundation, agricultural diversification, and the connections between economic change and political power.
