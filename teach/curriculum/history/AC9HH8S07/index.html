Explain historical interpretations

AC9HH8S07 · Year 8 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explain historical interpretations about significant events, individuals and groups

Prior knowledge

Students explain historical interpretations about significant events, individuals and groups (AC9HH7S07). They can identify differing interpretations and explain some reasons for them. They have not yet distinguished short-term triggers from longer-term causes within competing interpretations, or used interpretations to describe change in terms of quality, type, speed and impact.

By the end

Students explain historical interpretations about significant events, individuals and groups — identifying differing interpretations and accounting for their differences, including how interpretations of cause distinguish short-term triggers from longer-term causes, and how interpretations can be used to describe change in relation to quality, type, speed and impact.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying differing historians' interpretations on the short-term triggers and longer-term causes of a significant event
  • identifying different interpretations of an event or development, such as the influence of scientific ideas or the status of women, and accounting for the differences
  • using historical interpretations to describe change in relation to quality, type, speed and impact

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying differing interpretationsHingeStudents can identify differing historians' interpretations of a significant event, individual or group, and describe what each interpretation claims.
  • 2. Accounting for differencesStudents can account for why historians have reached different interpretations — including how short-term triggers and longer-term causes are weighted differently, and how differences in evidence, perspective and context produce different conclusions.
  • 3. Using interpretations to describe changeStudents can use historical interpretations to describe change in relation to quality, type, speed and impact — explaining what each dimension reveals about the nature of historical change.
