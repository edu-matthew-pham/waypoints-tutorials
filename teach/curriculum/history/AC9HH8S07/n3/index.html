Using interpretations to describe change

AC9HH8S07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use historical interpretations to describe change in relation to quality, type, speed and impact — explaining what each dimension reveals about the nature of historical change.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one historical interpretation to describe change in terms of at least two dimensions (quality, type, speed, impact)
  • Explains what these dimensions reveal about the nature of the historical change being interpreted

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Accounting for differences.

Width

Minimum
Use of at least one interpretation to describe change in terms of at least two dimensions (quality, type, speed or impact).
Typical
Historical interpretations used to describe the multi-dimensional character of change: quality (how complete or partial was the change?), type (was it political, social, economic, cultural?), speed (was it gradual or sudden?), impact (how deeply did it affect different groups?). Application across different types of change — e.g. the Reformation as sudden vs gradual, the Renaissance as rupture vs continuity with medieval scholasticism.
Wider
  • Apply the four dimensions of change to competing interpretations of the same event — e.g. the Black Death — to show how different interpretations emphasise different dimensions (speed: sudden pandemic vs longer-term demographic shift; impact: catastrophic for some groups, less so for others)
  • Examine how interpretations of Polynesian expansion describe change differently depending on which dimension is emphasised — the speed of settlement, the quality of navigational knowledge, the type of cultural transformation that settlement produced

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierUsing interpretations to describe change integrates interpretation identification (Node 1), accounting for differences (Node 2) and the continuity-change analytical framework from S05. Invest in helping students see the four dimensions of change as a tool for making interpretive comparisons more precise.
