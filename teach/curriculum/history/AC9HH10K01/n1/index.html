← AC9HH10K01 Causes of the Second World War

Inter-war settlement and its instabilities

AC9HH10K01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the post-WW1 settlement — the Treaty of Versailles and the League of Nations — created structural conditions of resentment, economic punishment, and failed collective security that formed the necessary preconditions for WW2.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two specific terms of the Treaty of Versailles and explains the link between each term and conditions of grievance or instability in Germany
  • Explains one structural weakness of the League of Nations and why it undermined collective security in the 1930s
  • Distinguishes between the Treaty of Versailles as a necessary condition and the outbreak of WW2 as a later outcome — articulating that the settlement created conditions that were subsequently exploited, not an inevitable trajectory

Prior knowledge

Students have explained the causes of World War One using the MAIN framework (Militarism, Alliance systems, Imperialism, Nationalism) and can distinguish long-term structural causes from short-term triggers (AC9HH9K08). They have examined the effects of WW1 on Australian society including conscription debates and Australia's relationship with Britain (AC9HH9K11), and have encountered the Treaty of Versailles as a post-war settlement — though depth of treatment of its specific terms is variable (AC9HH9K12 elaboration). They have not yet examined the inter-war period (1919–1939) as a causal field, the rise of fascism as a structural phenomenon, the diplomatic sequence leading to WW2, or Australia's political decision-making around entry into WW2.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least two specific terms of the Treaty of Versailles and explain why each created conditions of instability or grievance in Germany; explain one structural weakness of the League of Nations.
Typical
Students investigate the Treaty of Versailles across its major provisions (territorial concessions, reparations, war guilt clause, military restrictions); explain how each provision produced specific grievances; explain the structural weaknesses of the League of Nations that prevented collective security from functioning; connect the Great Depression to the deepening of economic grievance; distinguish necessary conditions from sufficient causes — the settlement created the conditions but did not make war inevitable.
Wider
  • Examine how the Treaty's terms affected parties beyond Germany — Austria-Hungary successor states, Ottoman successor states, colonial mandate peoples — showing that the grievances enabling instability were systemic, not uniquely German
  • Investigate the Russian Revolution as a parallel inter-war destabilising force — examining how the emergence of the Soviet Union reshaped European political alignments and contributed to the conditions in which fascism grew
  • Compare the Paris Peace settlements (Versailles, St Germain, Trianon, Sèvres) as a system — evaluating whether collective security was structurally possible given the contradictions built into the settlement

Hinge

Students who leave this node treating WW2 as simply caused by WW1 — or by Hitler alone — carry a mono-causal framework into every subsequent causal claim. The multi-causal structural conditions established here underpin Nodes 2 and 3: fascism rises in specific soil (N2), and appeasement responds to specific regime character shaped by that soil (N3). Thin treatment here produces systematically flat causal analysis across the unit.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly collapse complex causation into single-cause explanations (WW1 → Treaty → Hitler → WW2). Structured cause-mapping — categorising causes as economic, political, ideological, structural — builds the multi-causal framework before students are asked to weigh causes. Explicitly distinguishing necessary conditions from sufficient causes is the key scaffold.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExtending Treaty of Versailles analysis across multiple affected parties — and examining how Depression-era conditions produced different political outcomes in different countries — demonstrates that the preconditions for fascism were systemic rather than uniquely German. This corrects the tendency to treat WW2 causes as a specifically German story.
