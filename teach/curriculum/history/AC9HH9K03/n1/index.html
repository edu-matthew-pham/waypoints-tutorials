The legal and ideological basis of colonisation — terra nullius

AC9HH9K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the concept of terra nullius and its role in the legal framework of Australian colonisation, and describe what Cook's instructions reveal about British intentions.

Success criteria

  • Explains terra nullius as a legal concept and describes its consequences for First Nations Australians
  • Describes what Cook's instructions reveal about British intentions toward existing inhabitants

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the effects of contact and colonisation on societies in earlier periods and examine perspectives of colonisers and colonised (AC9HH8K09, AC9HH8K14). They have not yet examined the specific legal and ideological framework of Australian colonisation (terra nullius), the full scope of colonisation's effects on First Nations Australians, or the range of First Nations responses including the Stolen Generations.

Width

Minimum
Students define terra nullius as the legal fiction that Australia was uninhabited or unclaimed before British arrival, explain that this was used to justify taking land without treaty, and note Cook's instructions as evidence of British intentions.
Typical
Students examine Cook's secret Admiralty instructions (1768) and what they reveal about the tension between consent and possession; explain terra nullius as a legal concept that denied existing ownership by First Nations Australians; explain how this differs from colonisation in North America and New Zealand where treaties were negotiated; describe the consequences — no treaty, no recognition of prior ownership, no compensation — for First Nations Australians across the following two centuries.
Wider
  • Examine the legal challenge to terra nullius culminating in Mabo v Queensland (1992) — tracing the continuity between the original legal fiction and its eventual overturning
  • Compare the British approach (terra nullius) with treaty-based colonisation in New Zealand (Treaty of Waitangi, 1840) — examining what difference the legal framework made in practice

Hinge

Terra nullius is the foundational legal-ideological context for all subsequent effects of colonisation on First Nations Australians. Students who do not understand why land was taken without treaty cannot understand frontier conflict (N2) — the violence was produced by a system that refused to recognise First Nations ownership. It is also prerequisite for understanding the Stolen Generations (N4) as part of an ongoing colonial project.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe colonisation effects without understanding the legal framework that made them possible. Making terra nullius explicit — as a deliberate legal fiction with specific consequences — is the scaffold for understanding why First Nations Australians lost land, faced violence, and were subject to colonial laws and policies.
