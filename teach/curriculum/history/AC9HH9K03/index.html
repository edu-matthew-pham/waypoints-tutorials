European contact, settlement and impact on First Nations peoples

AC9HH9K03 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the causes and effects of European contact and extension of settlement, including their impact on the First Nations Peoples of Australia

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the effects of contact and colonisation on societies in earlier periods and examine perspectives of colonisers and colonised (AC9HH8K09, AC9HH8K14). They have not yet examined the specific legal and ideological framework of Australian colonisation (terra nullius), the full scope of colonisation's effects on First Nations Australians, or the range of First Nations responses including the Stolen Generations.

By the end

Students investigate the causes and effects of European contact and settlement in Australia — including the legal fiction of terra nullius, frontier conflict, disease, industry, and the forcible removal of children — and examine how First Nations Australians responded to and were impacted by colonisation.

Waypoints

  • 1. The legal and ideological basis of colonisation — terra nulliusHingeStudents can explain the concept of terra nullius and its role in the legal framework of Australian colonisation, and describe what Cook's instructions reveal about British intentions.
  • 2. Frontier conflict, disease and dispossessionStudents can describe the effects of European settlement on First Nations Australians — including frontier warfare, massacres, disease, and removal from land — and explain how these effects operated.
  • 3. First Nations responses to colonisationStudents can describe the range of ways First Nations Australians responded to colonisation — including resistance, adaptation, and contribution to colonial industries — and explain what shaped each response.
  • 4. The Stolen Generations — forcible removal of childrenStudents can describe the forcible removal of First Nations children from their families, including the motivations for removal, the practices and laws in place, and the experiences of those separated.
