Evaluating interpretations

AC9HH8K11 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate competing interpretations of the chosen empire or expansion — identifying which is better supported by evidence and explaining the reasoning behind that judgement.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least one historical interpretation of the chosen empire or expansion using evidence
  • Explains why the interpretation is well-supported or limited, with reference to specific sources or reasoning

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Competing interpretations.

Width

Minimum
An evaluation of at least one interpretation using evidence to assess its strengths or limitations.
Typical
Evaluation of competing interpretations using source evidence; how the perspective of subject peoples vs rulers produces different interpretive frameworks; the role of archaeological evidence in confirming or revising written-source interpretations (e.g. Viking settlements at L'Anse aux Meadows vs saga accounts).
Wider
  • Evaluate the 'Gold, God and Glory' framework against primary source evidence of conquistador motivations, identifying where the interpretation is well-supported, where it oversimplifies, and what it leaves out
  • Examine how post-colonial perspectives on the Spanish conquest have changed historical interpretations — particularly around the experiences of Indigenous peoples — and evaluate how this represents an improvement in historical understanding

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierEvaluation integrates source analysis (Node 1) and competing interpretation identification (Node 2). Invest in helping students move from noting that interpretations differ to making a reasoned judgement about which is better supported — this is the full demand of the descriptor.
