Australian popular culture — development and international contribution

AC9HH10K17 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate American and Asian cultural influences on Australian popular culture and explain the development of Australia's own creative industries and international contribution.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two international cultural influences on Australian popular culture with evidence
  • Describes at least one Australian contribution to international popular culture
  • Explains how satellite broadcast changed the dynamics of cultural transmission

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Environmental and nuclear influences — global events and Australian responses.

Width

Minimum
Students explain at least two international cultural influences on Australia and describe at least one Australian contribution to international popular culture.
Typical
Students examine American cultural influences (Hollywood, television, music genres) and Asian influences (Bollywood, Japanese and Chinese animation) on Australian popular culture; examine the development of the Australian film and television industry from the 1970s — the Australian New Wave in film, the 10BA tax incentives, the development of a distinctive Australian cultural voice; examine Australia's contribution to international popular culture in music (INXS, AC/DC, Kylie Minogue), film (Crocodile Dundee, Priscilla, Mad Max), and television; explain the role of satellite broadcast in transforming television from a national to a global medium.
Wider
  • Examine the Australian New Wave in film as a cultural project — analysing how filmmakers like Peter Weir and Bruce Beresford deliberately developed a distinctively Australian cinematic language in contrast to Hollywood
  • Investigate how digital media and social platforms have changed the dynamics of Australian cultural production — examining whether they have increased Australian cultural influence internationally or accelerated cultural homogenisation toward American norms

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a working picture of both the international influences on and Australian contributions to popular culture before the K19 identity analysis is meaningful. Building the repertoire across music, film, and television provides the evidential base.
