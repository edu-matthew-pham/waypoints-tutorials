Changing conditions and a major global influence

AC9HH10K17 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

changing social, political, economic, cultural, environmental and technological conditions, and the causes of a major global influence in Australia

Prior knowledge

Students have examined post-war migration waves (K07–K08), cultural transformation through American and British influences (K16), and the environmental movement (K14, K16). They have not yet examined the Vietnam War's specific domestic effects on Australia, multiculturalism as a policy framework, or post-war population growth in data form.

By the end

Students explain the changing conditions of post-war Australia and analyse the causes of a major global influence, including the Vietnam War's domestic effects, multiculturalism, and the environmental and cultural changes of the second half of the 20th century.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • describing the impact of the Vietnam War on Vietnam and how the communist victory in Vietnam in 1975 resulted in the arrival of refugees into Australia
  • investigating policies of multiculturalism since the 1970s and the concepts of cultural heritage and assimilation
  • analysing post–Second World War population growth and the development of Australia’s culturally diverse society, using different types of graphs
  • recognising the historic impact of the pictures of Earth taken during the Apollo 8 mission and how they influenced people’s view of the world
  • explaining events such as the struggle over American, British and French nuclear weapon testing in the Pacific from 1946 to 1996 or the sinking of the Rainbow Warrior in 1985
  • evaluating the effectiveness of international protocols and treaties such as Kyoto (1997), the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (since 1992) and the Washington Declaration (2007)
  • identifying American and Asian influences on Australian popular culture since the Second World War, such as through mainstream Hollywood and Bollywood films, and the animation film industry in China and Japan
  • investigating the changing contribution of the Australian rock ‘n’ roll, film and television industries to Australian culture and identity through the development and export of music, film and television

Waypoints

  • 1. The Vietnam War as a domestic turning pointHingeStudents can explain the impact of the Vietnam War on Australian society — as a source of political division, a driver of Vietnamese refugee migration, and a catalyst for challenging traditional values.
  • 2. Multiculturalism — policy, concept, and cultural diversityStudents can explain multiculturalism as both a social reality and a government policy framework, and analyse post-war population growth and cultural diversification.
  • 3. Environmental and nuclear influences — global events and Australian responsesStudents can explain significant global environmental and nuclear events and their effects on Australian perspectives and policy.
  • 4. Australian popular culture — development and international contributionStudents can investigate American and Asian cultural influences on Australian popular culture and explain the development of Australia's own creative industries and international contribution.
