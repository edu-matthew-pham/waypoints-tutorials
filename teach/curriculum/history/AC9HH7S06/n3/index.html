Absent perspectives — who is missing from the historical record?

AC9HH7S06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify groups whose perspectives are absent or underrepresented in historical sources, explain why this is so, and describe what limited evidence can still reveal about those groups.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two groups underrepresented in historical sources and explains why.
  • Explains what limited evidence about underrepresented groups can still reveal — does not conclude that absence of evidence means absence of significance.
  • Identifies specific types of evidence that can partially recover absent perspectives.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Attitudes and values of the past — reading the world behind the source.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one group whose perspective is underrepresented in historical sources from the ancient world, and explain why their perspective is difficult to access.
Typical
Identify multiple groups underrepresented in the historical record — women, slaves, conquered peoples, non-literate populations — explain why their perspectives are absent or limited (who controlled the production of written records, what types of evidence survive), and describe what limited evidence can still reveal about power structures and the lives of these groups.
Wider
  • Investigate specific techniques historians use to recover absent perspectives — reading against the grain, using material culture, analysing archaeological evidence from non-elite contexts, oral traditions — and apply these to sources from the History Y7 units.
  • Examine how the absence of evidence about particular groups is itself historically significant — what the absence reveals about power, literacy, and who determined what was recorded.
  • Explore how historians and communities have worked to recover and centre marginalised perspectives — including the work of First Nations historians and communities in recovering and asserting First Nations historical narratives.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates perspective identification (Node 1) and attitudes/values reading (Node 2) into a complete account of how perspective operates in historical sources — including the structurally important insight that perspective analysis must also account for whose perspective is absent. Students must coordinate whose perspective is present, what it reveals, and whose is missing and why, to reach the descriptor's full demand.
