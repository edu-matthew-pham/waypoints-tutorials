Identify past perspectives

AC9HH7S06 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify perspectives, attitudes and values of the past in sources

Prior knowledge

Students can discuss perspectives related to objects, people, places, and events (AC9HS6S04). They have not yet applied perspective analysis specifically to historical sources — distinguishing the perspective embedded in a source from the events the source describes, and examining how attitudes and values of a historical period are expressed in its sources.

By the end

Students identify perspectives, attitudes, and values of the past in sources — recognising that sources express the viewpoints of their producers, that some groups are underrepresented in historical sources, and that reading sources for perspective is a distinct historical skill from reading them for factual information.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying perspectives in a historical source, such as the saying of Confucius, 'Women and underlings are especially difficult to handle', and discussing the values and attitudes of the society that produced it
  • identifying that, while evidence may be limited for a particular group of people such as women, slaves, peoples living in newly conquered areas and ethnic groups, such evidence can provide useful insights into the power structures of a society
  • identifying the possible meanings of, and attitudes and values shown by, images and symbols in primary sources, such as funerary texts or religious manuscripts

Waypoints

  • 1. What is a perspective in a historical source?HingeStudents can explain what a perspective is in the context of a historical source, distinguish between the events a source describes and the perspective of the source's producer, and identify the perspective embedded in a given source.
  • 2. Attitudes and values of the past — reading the world behind the sourceStudents can identify the attitudes and values of the historical period expressed in a source — reading the source as a window on the society that produced it, not just the events it describes.
  • 3. Absent perspectives — who is missing from the historical record?Students can identify groups whose perspectives are absent or underrepresented in historical sources, explain why this is so, and describe what limited evidence can still reveal about those groups.
