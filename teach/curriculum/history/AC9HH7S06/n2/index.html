← AC9HH7S06 Identify past perspectives

Attitudes and values of the past — reading the world behind the source

AC9HH7S06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the attitudes and values of the historical period expressed in a source — reading the source as a window on the society that produced it, not just the events it describes.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two attitudes or values of the past expressed in a source.
  • Explains how the source expresses these values — not just states they exist but shows how they appear in the source.
  • Does not simply judge past attitudes by contemporary standards — demonstrates some attempt to understand them in historical context.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What is a perspective in a historical source?.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one attitude or value of the past expressed in a historical source, and explain how the source reveals that attitude or value.
Typical
Identify multiple attitudes and values of the past expressed in primary sources from both the Deep Time and Ancient units — including attitudes toward gender, authority, death, the divine, and social hierarchy — and explain how these attitudes and values are expressed through the source's content, imagery, language, and what it takes for granted.
Wider
  • Examine a source that expresses attitudes toward women or enslaved people (e.g. Confucius: 'Women and underlings are especially difficult to handle') and discuss what it reveals about the attitudes and values of the society, and how a historian uses such a source as evidence of those values.
  • Investigate how images and symbols in primary sources — funerary texts, religious manuscripts, coins — express attitudes and values about power, identity, and belief that may not be explicitly stated.
  • Explore the concept of 'presentism' — judging the past by contemporary values — and examine how historians balance empathy (understanding past perspectives on their own terms) with ethical critique.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly have difficulty identifying attitudes and values in sources without explicit statements — they can find Confucius's explicit attitude toward women but miss the implicit values about hierarchy in an administrative document or the implicit cosmology in a funerary text. Multiple examples across source types and attitudes — explicit and implicit, text and image, different social groups — build the generalised skill of reading sources for the values they express.
