What is a perspective in a historical source?

AC9HH7S06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what a perspective is in the context of a historical source, distinguish between the events a source describes and the perspective of the source's producer, and identify the perspective embedded in a given source.

Success criteria

  • Identifies whose perspective is represented in a source and describes it as a viewpoint, not a fact.
  • Distinguishes between what the source says (content) and what perspective it expresses.
  • Explains how the producer's social position shapes the perspective in the source.

Prior knowledge

Students can discuss perspectives related to objects, people, places, and events (AC9HS6S04). They have not yet applied perspective analysis specifically to historical sources — distinguishing the perspective embedded in a source from the events the source describes, and examining how attitudes and values of a historical period are expressed in its sources.

Width

Minimum
Identify the perspective embedded in a given historical source — whose viewpoint it represents — and distinguish this from the events the source describes.
Typical
Explain what a perspective is in a historical source — that it reflects the attitudes, values, and viewpoint of the producer, shaped by their position, social group, culture, and time period. Identify the perspective in a range of sources, distinguishing whose perspective is represented and whose is absent.
Wider
  • Examine how the same event is described from different perspectives in different sources — for example, accounts of the same battle from opposing sides, or descriptions of the same social practice by elite and non-elite sources — and analyse what each perspective reveals and conceals.
  • Investigate how perspective operates in non-textual sources — what perspective is embedded in a commemorative monument, a piece of imperial propaganda art, or a religious artefact.
  • Explore the concept of 'positionality' — how a source producer's social position (ruler, slave, priest, woman) shapes what they observe, record, and value — and how historians use this concept in source analysis.

Hinge

Students who read sources for factual content rather than producer perspective cannot identify attitudes and values of the past in N2 — the 'world behind the source' reading requires the perspective/content distinction to already be reliable.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents frequently conflate a source's perspective with its factual content, reading what a source says rather than what it reveals about its producer's viewpoint. The reasoning scaffold — who made this → what position did they occupy → how does that position shape what they would notice and value → what perspective does the source therefore express — needs to be made explicit before students can reliably distinguish perspective analysis from content description.
