How interpretations change over time

AC9HH10S07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how historians have changed their interpretations over time and construct a historical argument that identifies different interpretive possibilities.

Success criteria

  • Describe how a specific historical interpretation has changed over time and explain what drove the change
  • Construct a historical argument that identifies different interpretive possibilities while clearly presenting one position
  • Explain how new sources, new methodologies, or changing social contexts can change historical interpretation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying and evaluating competing interpretations.

Width

Minimum
Describe how at least one historical interpretation has changed over time, explaining what drove the change; construct a historical argument that acknowledges different interpretive possibilities while presenting a clear position.
Typical
Analyse how historians have changed the way they interpret an event under investigation over time — such as a change in view with the discovery of more sources, changes in methodology, or changes in the social context of historical writing; construct a historical argument that identifies different interpretive possibilities, organises ideas to build an argument, presents a particular point of view with consistent reference to available evidence, and uses evaluative language.
Wider
  • Trace the full historiography of a contested topic across at least three decades — mapping how the dominant interpretation has shifted as new evidence, new methodologies, and changing social contexts have influenced historical writing
  • Compare how the 'history wars' debate in Australia (over colonial history and the Stolen Generations) developed and was resolved or remained contested, examining what the debate reveals about the relationship between historical interpretation and political context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the interpretation identification (Node 1) with a more sophisticated understanding of how interpretations are produced and how they change — bringing together source analysis skills (S03–S04), perspective analysis (S06), and interpretation analysis (Node 1) into a full historiographical understanding.
