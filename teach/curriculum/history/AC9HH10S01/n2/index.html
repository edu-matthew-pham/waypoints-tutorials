Modifying and scoping questions

AC9HH10S01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can modify historical questions in response to source suitability and evaluate whether questions are appropriately scoped for the investigation.

Success criteria

  • Modify at least one historical question in response to source availability or scope constraints
  • Explain why the modification was necessary and what the revised question enables
  • Evaluate whether a given question is appropriately scoped for a specific investigation, providing a reasoned judgment

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Developing questions that require historical argument.

Width

Minimum
Modify at least one question in response to source availability or scope constraints and explain the reason for the modification.
Typical
Modify key questions and sub-questions in response to the suitability of available sources; determine whether questions are too broad or too narrow given the requirements of the investigation and the sources available; practise iterative question refinement — adjusting scope, focus, or framing in response to what sources can actually answer.
Wider
  • Practise question modification across topics of different scale — from local history to national to global — examining how scale affects what questions are researchable and what sources exist
  • Develop and refine questions for a topic where key sources are missing or inaccessible (e.g. oral history topics, First Nations history before written records), examining how historians adapt their questions to work with the available evidence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsQuestion modification requires iterative practice. Use think-aloud modelling of the refinement process — showing students how a question changes across multiple drafts as the historian tests it against available sources — before expecting students to do this independently.
