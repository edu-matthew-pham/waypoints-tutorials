Origin, content and context

AC9HH10S03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the historical context of a source, identify its origin and content, and identify any gaps or inaccuracies.

Success criteria

  • Identify the origin of at least one source (who created it, when, in what context)
  • Explain the historical context in which the source was produced
  • Identify at least one gap or potential inaccuracy in the source and explain what might have produced it

Prior knowledge

Students can identify the origin and content of sources and explain the purpose and context of primary and secondary sources in a Year 9 inquiry context.

Width

Minimum
For at least one source, explain its historical context, identify its origin and content, and identify at least one gap or potential inaccuracy.
Typical
Explain the historical context of sources and identify gaps or inaccuracies — asking what the source cannot tell us as well as what it can; identify the origin (who created it, when, where) and content (what it contains, what it describes) of primary and secondary sources across a range of types used in Year 10 History.
Wider
  • Apply origin and context analysis to a source whose historical context has been contested or misrepresented — examining how knowing the correct context changes the source's meaning
  • Analyse sources produced by or about First Nations Australians — examining how understanding the cultural context of production (a petition, an oral account, a sacred object) requires different analytical approaches than standard documentary analysis

Hinge

Understanding context is the prerequisite for purpose analysis in Node 2. Students who analyse purpose without first establishing context typically produce circular or superficial purpose statements — 'the author wrote this to inform readers' — rather than historically grounded purpose analysis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse the OPVL (Origin, Purpose, Value, Limitation) or similar structured framework as a scaffold for context-before-purpose analysis. Explicitly require students to establish context before attempting purpose — model what changes in the purpose analysis when context is established.
