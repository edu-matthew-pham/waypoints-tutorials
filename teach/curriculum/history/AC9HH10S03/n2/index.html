Purpose and stylistic elements

AC9HH10S03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse the intent of the author and the features of a source to determine its purpose, and describe stylistic elements to inform the meaning of sources.

Success criteria

  • Analyse the intent of the author of at least one source and explain its purpose in relation to its historical context
  • Describe at least one stylistic element of a source (artistic, architectural, or textual) and explain what it contributes to meaning
  • Apply purpose analysis across at least two different source types and compare what each type reveals

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Origin, content and context.

Width

Minimum
Analyse the intent of the author of at least one source and explain its purpose; describe at least one stylistic element of a source and explain what it contributes to meaning.
Typical
Analyse the intent of the author and the features of a source to determine purpose — asking why it was made, for what audience, and to what effect; describe stylistic elements of sources such as artistic technique, architectural features, or textual structure to inform the meaning of sources; apply this analysis across both primary and secondary source types.
Wider
  • Apply purpose analysis to a source whose purpose has been contested or misread — such as a propaganda image or a manipulated photograph — examining how knowledge of intent changes interpretation
  • Analyse a non-documentary source type (photograph, artwork, material artefact, oral testimony) and compare the purpose/stylistic analysis process with that used for written documents — examining what each source type reveals and conceals

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApply purpose and stylistic analysis across multiple source types within one inquiry — written document, photograph, material artefact, oral testimony — to build a repertoire of analytical approaches. Each source type reveals different dimensions of purpose.
