← AC9HH9S08 Create historical descriptions, explanations and arguments

Selecting appropriate formats and using historical concepts and terms

AC9HH9S08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select appropriate formats to communicate a historical argument and use historical concepts and terms accurately throughout.

Success criteria

  • Communicates a historical argument in a format appropriate to the audience and purpose, with accurate use of at least three historical terms or concepts
  • Constructs the argument using selected evidence from sources, not only general claims

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Developing a historical argument that acknowledges interpretation.

Width

Minimum
Communicate a historical argument in an appropriate format, using at least three historical terms or concepts correctly.
Typical
Select appropriate formats to communicate a historical argument — including written essays, oral presentations, scripts, visual displays, or structured reports; develop explanations and arguments using historical concepts and terms such as cause, effect, change, continuity, significance, perspective, and contested historical interpretations; construct historical arguments using selected evidence from sources.
Wider
  • Construct a historical argument in an unfamiliar format (e.g. a museum exhibit text, a podcast script, a documentary treatment) — adapting historical reasoning to a specific audience and purpose
  • Develop historical arguments across multiple formats within the same unit (e.g. a written essay and an oral presentation on related but distinct questions) — practising the skill of adapting argument to communication context
  • Produce a historical argument for a non-specialist audience (e.g. a Year 7 student or a community website) — examining how communicating history to different audiences requires different choices about language, structure, and level of detail

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPractising historical argument construction across multiple formats — written, oral, visual — and multiple audiences develops the skill as genuinely communicative rather than a formula for one output type.
