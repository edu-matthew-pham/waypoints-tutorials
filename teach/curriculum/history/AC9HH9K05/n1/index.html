Continuity and change as analytical tools

AC9HH9K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the concepts of continuity and change as analytical lenses — that continuity and change always coexist, that what changes and what persists are both historically significant — and apply this framework to a simple example.

Success criteria

  • Distinguishes continuity from change and explains why both require historical explanation
  • Applies the continuity-change framework to identify one continuity and one change in a given domain

Prior knowledge

Students can describe causes and effects of change and identify continuities in earlier periods (AC9HH8K03, AC9HH8K08, AC9HH8K13). They have not yet examined living conditions and material culture in Australia around 1900, the specific legal reforms of the new Federal Parliament, or how to systematically apply the concepts of continuity and change across multiple domains simultaneously.

Width

Minimum
Students distinguish continuity (what persisted) from change (what transformed) and explain that both require explanation — it is not enough to describe change, continuities must also be explained and vice versa.
Typical
Students apply continuity-and-change thinking to a familiar domain (e.g. transport or housing); explain that the rate and extent of change varies across different aspects of life (some things transform rapidly, others persist across generations); recognise that what counts as continuity or change depends on what time period and what aspects of life are being examined; understand that the same period can show rapid change in some domains and strong continuity in others.
Wider
  • Apply the concept retroactively — identify one aspect of life in Australia that changed dramatically between 1800 and 1900 and one that showed remarkable continuity, and explain what drove each pattern

Hinge

The analytical framework is the prerequisite for the content nodes. Students who cannot hold continuity and change as coexisting analytical categories will produce accounts that only describe change, missing the significant continuities in living conditions, social structure and cultural life that the descriptor specifically requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat 'continuity and change' as simply 'what changed' — omitting continuities as though they are less historically interesting. Making both equally requiring explanation is the analytical scaffold for this descriptor.
