Continuities and changes around the turn of the 20th century

AC9HH9K05 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

continuities and changes and their effects on ways of life and living conditions, political and legal institutions, and cultural expression around the turn of the 20th century in Australian society

Prior knowledge

Students can describe causes and effects of change and identify continuities in earlier periods (AC9HH8K03, AC9HH8K08, AC9HH8K13). They have not yet examined living conditions and material culture in Australia around 1900, the specific legal reforms of the new Federal Parliament, or how to systematically apply the concepts of continuity and change across multiple domains simultaneously.

By the end

Students examine living and working conditions in Australia around 1900, compare different settings, identify features of housing, sanitation, transport, education and industry, compare these with conditions around 1800, and explain how federal laws such as the Harvester Judgment affected living and working conditions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the living and working conditions in Australia around 1900 and making comparisons between different settings (for example, rural versus urban) using sources such as photographs, paintings, diaries and newspapers
  • examining the social and political impact of religious sectarianism between Catholics and Protestants in the formation of the Australian nation
  • identifying the main features of housing, sanitation, transport, education, agriculture and industry that influenced living and working conditions in Australia around 1900 and comparing them with early colonisation around 1800
  • explaining how laws made by the federal parliament, such as those resulting from the Harvester Judgement or the introduction of pensions, affected working conditions and standards of living

Waypoints

  • 1. Continuity and change as analytical toolsHingeStudents can explain the concepts of continuity and change as analytical lenses — that continuity and change always coexist, that what changes and what persists are both historically significant — and apply this framework to a simple example.
  • 2. Living and working conditions around 1900 — comparison across settingsStudents can describe living and working conditions in Australia around 1900, compare different settings (rural vs urban, rich vs poor), and identify the main features of housing, sanitation, transport, education and industry.
  • 3. Change over time — comparing 1800 and 1900Students can compare living and working conditions around 1900 with those around 1800, identifying what changed, what persisted, and explaining what drove the changes.
  • 4. Legal reforms and their effects on living conditionsStudents can explain how laws made by the new federal parliament — including the Harvester Judgment and the introduction of pensions — affected working conditions and standards of living.
