Significant individuals in empire

AC9HH8K10 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the role and achievements of a significant individual and/or group connected to the empire and/or expansion

Prior knowledge

Students explain the role and achievements of a significant individual in an ancient society (AC9HH7K13) and understand significance as a historical concept (AC9HH8K05). They understand the social and cultural features of the chosen empire (AC9HH8K07) and its turning points (AC9HH8K08). They have not yet applied significance analysis to individuals specifically connected to medieval empire and expansion.

By the end

Students explain the role and achievements of a significant individual or group connected to the chosen empire or expansion — describing what they did, the conditions that enabled their actions, and the impact and legacy of their achievements on the empire and beyond.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the similarities in leadership styles and successes of Genghis Khan and Kublai Khan
  • comparing historians' interpretations of the achievements of individuals such as Selim I in establishing the empire and capturing Jerusalem, or Suleiman the Magnificent in expanding the empire to Belgrade in Europe
  • outlining Erik the Red's development of Viking settlements in Eastern and Western Greenland in 985 CE
  • comparing the artefacts discovered at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland (Canada) with Viking artefacts as possible evidence that the Vikings had journeyed to North America 500 years before Christopher Columbus
  • The Spanish conquest of the Americas
  • explaining the significance of key chronological events in the lives of individuals such as Columbus, Balboa, Cortés, Montezuma II and/or Pizarro

Waypoints

  • 1. The individual in historical contextStudents can describe the historical context in which the chosen individual operated and explain the conditions that enabled their rise to significance.
  • 2. Achievements and their significanceHingeStudents can explain the key achievements of the chosen individual and evaluate their significance — describing both what was achieved and why it mattered for the empire and for later history.
  • 3. Legacy and historical judgementStudents can evaluate the legacy of the chosen individual — explaining what endured from their achievements, how historians have judged their significance, and what their actions mean for our understanding of empire and expansion.
