Legacy and historical judgement

AC9HH8K10 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the legacy of the chosen individual — explaining what endured from their achievements, how historians have judged their significance, and what their actions mean for our understanding of empire and expansion.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least one lasting legacy of the chosen individual and connects it to the empire's or expansion's longer-term history
  • References at least one historians' judgement and evaluates it against the evidence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Achievements and their significance.

Width

Minimum
An explanation of at least one lasting legacy and a reference to how the individual is regarded by historians.
Typical
Legacy evaluated across multiple criteria; differing historians' perspectives on the individual's significance; connections between the individual's achievements and ongoing historical developments. Examples: the Mongol legacy in trade connections and cultural exchange; the Viking legacy in settlement and navigation; the contested legacy of Spanish conquest for Indigenous populations and for the modern world.
Wider
  • Examine how historians' interpretations of conquistadores such as Cortés or Pizarro differ — from heroic explorers to colonial oppressors — as a case study in how the same individual can be judged very differently depending on whose perspective is centred
  • Compare the achievements of Genghis Khan and Kublai Khan across military conquest and cultural administration to evaluate which form of achievement has left a more enduring legacy

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierLegacy assessment integrates contextual understanding (Node 1) and achievement analysis (Node 2). Invest in helping students articulate a coherent historical judgement that draws on both, rather than treating legacy as a separate summary exercise.
