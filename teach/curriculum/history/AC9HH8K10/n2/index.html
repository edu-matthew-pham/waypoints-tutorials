Achievements and their significance

AC9HH8K10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the key achievements of the chosen individual and evaluate their significance — describing both what was achieved and why it mattered for the empire and for later history.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two achievements of the chosen individual
  • Explains why each achievement was significant — not just what happened, but what it changed or enabled

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The individual in historical context.

Width

Minimum
At least two achievements of the chosen individual with an explanation of their significance.
Typical
Multiple achievements analysed for immediate and longer-term significance. Examples: Genghis Khan's decimal military organisation, codified laws and policies; Selim I or Suleiman the Magnificent's territorial expansion and governance; Erik the Red's development of Viking settlements in Greenland; key chronological events in the lives of Columbus, Cortés or Pizarro and their significance for the Americas.
Wider
  • Compare historians' interpretations of Selim I and Suleiman the Magnificent's achievements to show how the same individual's record can be assessed differently depending on the interpretive lens
  • Examine Erik the Red's development of Viking settlements in eastern and western Greenland (985 CE) as evidence of expansion beyond the known world — and compare with L'Anse aux Meadows to evaluate the extent of Viking achievement

Hinge

Achievement analysis is the core demand of this descriptor. Students who cannot explain what was achieved and why it mattered — rather than merely narrating events — have not met the standard. Legacy analysis in the next node depends on solid achievement analysis here.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often narrate a biography rather than analyse achievements. Scaffold the distinction: an achievement is not just something that happened but something that had consequence. Each achievement needs an explicitly reasoned explanation of why it mattered.
