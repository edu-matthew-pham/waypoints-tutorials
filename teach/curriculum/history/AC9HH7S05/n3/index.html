Continuity and change — explaining what persisted and what shifted

AC9HH7S05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain continuities and changes within a historical period or across periods, using chronological evidence to support their explanations and identifying that continuity and change can coexist.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one continuity and one change within a period and supports each with evidence.
  • Explains that continuity and change coexist — does not treat them as mutually exclusive.
  • Uses chronological evidence (dated sources, timelines) to support continuity/change claims rather than asserting them without evidence.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Causes and effects — multi-causal historical reasoning.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one continuity and one change within a historical period, and use at least one piece of chronological evidence (e.g. a dated source or event) to support each.
Typical
Explain continuities and changes within or across historical periods, using primary sources and timelines to sequence and support explanations. Identify that continuity and change coexist — the same period can show stability in some dimensions and change in others. Explain why identifying both is important for understanding historical periods accurately.
Wider
  • Examine a historical period from the K-strand units and develop a continuity-and-change analysis that identifies specific dimensions where continuity is evident and specific dimensions where change occurred — building a nuanced account rather than simply 'things changed'.
  • Investigate how the rate of change varied within the ancient society — identifying periods of rapid change and periods of relative stability, and explaining what drove variation in the pace of change.
  • Explore how the same development can be understood as both continuity and change depending on the timescale and perspective — examining how the framing of a continuity/change question shapes the answer.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly treat continuity and change as exclusive — a period is either stable or changing. Examining multiple dimensions of the same period (technology, social structure, beliefs, political organisation) across multiple ancient and deep-time contexts specifically resolves this. Breadth across examples from both the Deep Time and Ancient units builds the understanding that continuity and change always coexist and that the interesting historical question is which dimensions are stable and which are shifting.
