Evaluating interpretations

AC9HH8K06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate historical interpretations by examining the evidence they rest on and identifying factors that might limit or strengthen them.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least one historical interpretation by identifying evidence that supports or limits it
  • Explains how factors such as source availability, perspective or historical context affect the strength of an interpretation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Competing interpretations and why they differ.

Width

Minimum
An evaluation of at least one interpretation using evidence to assess its strengths or limitations.
Typical
Evaluation of competing interpretations using source evidence; how discoveries of science are linked to ideas about the free exercise of human reason; how the importance or significance of the Scientific Revolution or Enlightenment is highlighted or questioned depending on the interpretive lens used.
Wider
  • Examine how the interpretations of the Enlightenment and Scientific Revolution highlight their importance to contemporary society, evaluating how much weight to give modern relevance as a criterion in judging historical significance
  • Analyse how modern historians' interpretations of the Crusades differ depending on whether they focus on European, Islamic or Byzantine perspectives — and evaluate which interpretation is best supported by the available evidence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a method for evaluating interpretations — not just noting that they differ but asking which is better supported, by what evidence, and why. Scaffold the evaluation process explicitly: identify the claim, identify the evidence, assess the strength of the link.
