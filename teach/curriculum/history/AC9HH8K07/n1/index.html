Way of life and social organisation

AC9HH8K07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the way of life and social organisation of the chosen group — including how their environment, economy and social structure shaped their daily existence.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two features of the chosen group's way of life using historically specific detail
  • Explains how at least one environmental or economic factor shaped the group's social organisation

Prior knowledge

Students explain the significant social, religious, cultural, economic and political features of ancient societies (AC9HH7K09, AC9HH7K11). They understand how to describe the organisation of a society across multiple dimensions. They have not yet examined these features in the context of medieval empires and expansionist societies — the Mongols, Ottomans, Vikings or the Spanish conquest of the Americas.

Width

Minimum
A description of at least two features of the chosen group's way of life (e.g. social roles, food, housing, gender roles) grounded in the specific historical context.
Typical
Multiple features of daily life across social, economic and environmental dimensions, grounded in primary source evidence where possible. Examples: the nomadic nature of Mongol life and its role in enabling Genghis Khan's rise; the role of the coffee house, bazaar and Sultan's authority in Ottoman society; Viking living conditions, farming, raiding, and the significance of honour; pre-Columbian Aztec society including nobility, slavery, religious beliefs and Tenochtitlan.
Wider
  • For the Mongols: examine how facets of Mongol life (horses, camels, yurts, dried meat) were incorporated into Chinese life after conquest, showing how expansion transformed both the conquerors and the conquered
  • For the Vikings: investigate how the environment of Scandinavian Viking lands directly influenced the activities — farming limitations, coastal access, shipbuilding — that drove expansion
  • For the Ottomans: use primary sources to track changes in way of life across different times or locations within the Ottoman Empire, showing variation within the empire

Hinge

Without understanding the way of life of the chosen group — how their environment, economy and social structure shaped them — students cannot explain why they expanded, how they governed, or what the effects of expansion were. The social foundation is prerequisite to all subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate specific, concrete details of the chosen group's way of life before they can generalise about social features. One or two facts are insufficient — build a rich picture across social, economic, environmental and cultural dimensions.
