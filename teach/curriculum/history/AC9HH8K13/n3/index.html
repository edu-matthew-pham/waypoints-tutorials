Effects and continuity

AC9HH8K13 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the effects of the chosen turning point on the Asia-Pacific society — describing what changed for different groups and what remained the same — and evaluate its historical significance.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least one change and one continuity resulting from the turning point, with reference to specific evidence
  • Identifies how the turning point affected at least one social group and evaluates its historical significance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Causes and features.

Width

Minimum
At least two effects (one change and one continuity) for at least one affected group.
Typical
Multiple effects across domains for different groups. Examples: Khmer decline effects on different classes; the continuity of Shogunate power in trade, resource use and social classes under the Tokugawa alongside the change represented by Japan's isolation; Polynesian expansion effects — successful settlement of diverse Pacific island environments; the significance of different theories of expansion (accidental vs intentional) for how we understand Pacific peoples' capabilities.
Wider
  • For feudal Japan: explain the reasons for Japan's closure to foreigners under the Tokugawa Shogunate and the impact of US Commodore Perry's visit (1853) as a turning point — examining both the continuities maintained by isolation and the rapid change it ultimately failed to prevent
  • For Polynesian expansion: outline different theories (west-east vs east-west, accidental vs intentional) and evaluate which is better supported by archaeological and environmental evidence, examining how theories about expansion affect how we understand Polynesian identity and capability

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAsia-Pacific turning points span environmental, political, military and cultural domains. Using multiple domains — and noting what continued as well as what changed — builds a more complete analysis of historical significance.
