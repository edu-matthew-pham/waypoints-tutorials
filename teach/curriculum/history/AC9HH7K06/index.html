Social organisation and cultural practice

AC9HH7K06 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the social organisation and cultural practices of early First Nations Australians, and their continuity and change over time

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the technological achievements of early First Nations Australians across different environments, including food systems and land management (AC9HH7K05). They have not yet examined the social organisational structures — moieties, totems, kinship — or the cultural practices and their transmission mechanisms as topics in their own right.

By the end

Students describe the social organisation systems of early First Nations Australians — including moieties, totems, and kinship groups — explain how these systems determine roles and relationships, and describe how cultural practices including oral traditions, lore, rites of passage, and funerary customs have been transmitted and have continued and changed over time.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the social organisation systems of First Nations Australians, such as moieties, totems and kinship groups, and how they determine roles and relationships to others, including how they underpin marriage laws, and their development and significance in areas such as Central Australia during increasing desertification
  • examining how aspects of social organisation and cultural practices of early First Nations Australians are known because they have been passed on through oral traditions and supported by media such as song, dance, music and visual art representations such as rock paintings; for example, narratives or visual representations related to lore, kinship structures, and responsibilities to the land, seas, waterways, sky and universe
  • investigating important cultural practices of early First Nations Australians and their continuity and change over time; for example, lore, rites of passage, and the antiquity and types of funerary customs and burial practices such as the early example of cremation at the Willandra Lakes in New South Wales, the tombstone openings of the Torres Strait Islands and the log coffins used by the Yolngu Peoples of Arnhem Land
  • exploring the existence of defined land, sea and sky territories, and the social and political systems that governed early First Nations Australians' societies, such as land tenure systems, delineation and reciprocal access rights, and the trade and bartering of items such as ochre, medicine and trepang

Waypoints

  • 1. Social organisation — kinship, moieties, and totemsHingeStudents can describe the social organisation systems of early First Nations Australians — including moieties, totems, and kinship groups — and explain how these systems determine roles, relationships, and responsibilities within communities.
  • 2. Cultural transmission — oral traditions and expressive artsStudents can describe how cultural knowledge — including lore, kinship structures, responsibilities to Country, and historical events — has been transmitted through oral traditions supported by song, dance, music, and visual art, and explain what this reveals about cultural continuity.
  • 3. Cultural practices — lore, rites of passage, and funerary customsStudents can describe specific cultural practices of early First Nations Australians — including lore, rites of passage, and funerary customs — and explain how these practices have shown both continuity and change over time.
