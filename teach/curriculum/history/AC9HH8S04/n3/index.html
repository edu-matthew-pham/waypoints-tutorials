← AC9HH8S04 Assess source accuracy and usefulness

Usefulness for specific inquiry

AC9HH8S04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the usefulness of a source for a specific historical inquiry question — explaining how its accuracy, origin and purpose bear on what it can contribute to answering the question.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates the usefulness of at least one source for a specific inquiry question
  • Explains how the source's accuracy, origin and purpose affect what it can contribute — distinguishing between reliability in general and usefulness for this inquiry

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Distinguishing fact from opinion.

Width

Minimum
An evaluation of at least one source's usefulness for a specific inquiry question, explaining what it can and cannot contribute.
Typical
Source usefulness evaluated against specific inquiry demands: a source may be accurate but not useful for a particular question; a biased source may be useful as evidence of attitudes even if not reliable as factual record. Cross-referencing multiple sources to assess how they support, challenge or complement each other.
Wider
  • Evaluate the usefulness of Matteo Villani's diary, Boccaccio's Decameron and Ibn Khaldun's recollections for the same inquiry question about the Black Death — examining how different sources are useful in different ways for the same question
  • Examine how the usefulness of a source depends on the inquiry question: Boccaccio's Decameron may be more useful for understanding contemporary attitudes to the Black Death than for reconstructing mortality statistics

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierUsefulness evaluation integrates validity assessment (Node 1), fact-opinion distinction (Node 2) and source analysis from S03. Invest in helping students articulate why a source is or is not useful for their specific inquiry question — not just whether it is reliable in general.
