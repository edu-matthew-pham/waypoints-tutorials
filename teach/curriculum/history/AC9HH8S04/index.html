Assess source accuracy and usefulness

AC9HH8S04 · Year 8 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify and describe the accuracy and usefulness of primary and secondary sources as evidence

Prior knowledge

Students identify and describe the accuracy and usefulness of primary and secondary sources as evidence (AC9HH7S04). They can ask basic validity questions about sources. They have not yet systematically distinguished fact from opinion using linguistic strategies, or evaluated usefulness with reference to the specific demands of a historical inquiry.

By the end

Students identify and describe the accuracy and usefulness of primary and secondary sources as evidence — examining validity through source analysis questions, distinguishing fact from opinion using linguistic strategies, and evaluating how the accuracy and usefulness of a source depends on the specific inquiry question being asked.

Waypoints

  • 1. Examining validityHingeStudents can examine the validity of a source by asking systematic validity questions — where does it come from, what information is provided, is the information factual or opinion, what other sources are needed — and explain what those questions reveal about the source's reliability.
  • 2. Distinguishing fact from opinionStudents can distinguish between factual statements and opinion or interpretation in historical sources — using linguistic strategies to detect opinion including conditional language and hedging words.
  • 3. Usefulness for specific inquiryStudents can evaluate the usefulness of a source for a specific historical inquiry question — explaining how its accuracy, origin and purpose bear on what it can contribute to answering the question.
