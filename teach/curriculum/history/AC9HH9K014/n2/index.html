Industrialisation and urbanisation — the factory system and city growth

AC9HH9K014 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how the factory system and the development of the steam engine created demand for urban labour and drove the growth of industrial cities.

Success criteria

  • Describes how the factory system and steam power concentrated production in cities
  • Explains how industrialisation created demand for urban labour and drove city growth

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Agricultural change and rural displacement.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the factory system as concentrating production in cities, explain that steam power enabled factories and railways, and describe the resulting growth of industrial cities as workers moved to find employment.
Typical
Students explain the factory system as an organisational and technological shift from cottage industry; the role of steam in driving factories, mills and railways; how railways enabled movement of goods and people; the rapid growth of industrial cities (Manchester, Birmingham, Glasgow); the conditions in industrial cities — overcrowding, poor sanitation, child labour; connect factory demand to the rural displacement from N1 as complementary push and pull.
Wider
  • Examine the growth of a specific industrial city using census data or visual sources — tracking how population size, occupational structure and neighbourhood composition changed over decades
  • Investigate the role of railways in reshaping settlement patterns — not just connecting existing cities but enabling new towns to grow around railway junctions and industrial sites

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat urbanisation as a single undifferentiated movement rather than as two complementary forces — rural push and urban pull — operating together. Explicitly connecting N1 and N2 as push-pull mechanisms consolidates the causal framework.
