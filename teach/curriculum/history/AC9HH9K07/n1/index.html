Federal social legislation and its effects — Australia in comparative context

AC9HH9K07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the major social legislation of the new Federal Government and explain how it affected living and working conditions, situating Australia's approach in comparative international context.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least three pieces of Federal social legislation and explains the effect of each
  • Situates Australian social legislation in comparative context — explaining in what ways it was internationally notable

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the development of the new Federal Parliament and its social legislation (AC9HH9K04, AC9HH9K05). They have not yet examined how Australian Federal social legislation compared internationally, how women's roles and rights developed in the new federation, or how Australia's position in the world — as a British dominion, as a Pacific nation — shaped its identity by 1914.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least three pieces of Federal social legislation (Harvester Judgment, Immigration Restriction Act 1901, old-age pension, Defence Act 1903) and explain the effect of each on living and working conditions.
Typical
Students describe the Harvester Judgment (minimum wage), the Immigration Restriction Act 1901 (White Australia policy), invalid and old-age pensions (state welfare), maternity allowance, the Defence Act 1903 (military structure); explain how each affected living and working conditions; situate these in international context — Australia was among the first nations to implement old-age pensions and a minimum wage, making it a noted social laboratory; explain how the White Australia policy reflected and reinforced racial attitudes about who the nation was for.
Wider
  • Examine how Australia was perceived internationally as a 'social laboratory' — what foreign observers (including American and British progressives) found notable about Australian social legislation
  • Evaluate the Immigration Restriction Act — examining its intent, its mechanism (the dictation test), and the contradictions between 'progressive' social legislation and racially exclusive nationhood

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly examine individual pieces of legislation without understanding the combined picture — an internationally notable social experiment in state-regulated capitalism operating within a racially exclusive national framework. Multiple examples build this complex portrait.
