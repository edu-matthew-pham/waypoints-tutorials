The role of the Church

AC9HH8K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the role of the Church as a social, political and cultural institution in medieval Europe — including its authority over spiritual and everyday life, its provision of education and welfare, and its relationship with secular rulers.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two distinct functions of the Church in medieval society (spiritual, educational, political, economic or welfare)
  • Describes the relationship between Church authority and secular feudal authority, including at least one point of overlap or tension

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The feudal hierarchy.

Width

Minimum
At least two distinct roles of the Church (e.g. spiritual authority, education, political power) with a brief explanation of why the Church held such influence.
Typical
The Church as a parallel power structure to secular feudalism — its hierarchy, economic power (tithes, land), role in literacy and law, provision of social services, and tensions between papal and royal authority.
Wider
  • Examine the role of monasteries as centres of learning, agriculture and community welfare — particularly the preservation of ancient texts — to see how the Church's institutional functions extended beyond religious practice
  • Investigate a specific Church-state conflict (e.g. the Investiture Controversy) as a case study in how Church and secular authority negotiated power

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to limit the Church to religious functions. Varying contexts — education, land ownership, courts, social welfare, illuminated manuscripts — helps them grasp the Church as a multidimensional institution with social and political as well as spiritual authority.
