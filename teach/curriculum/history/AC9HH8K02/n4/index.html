Emerging classes and the Scientific Revolution

AC9HH8K02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the emergence of merchant and artisan classes, the spread of literacy and new ideas, and the contribution of key theories to the Scientific Revolution, and explain how these developments modified medieval social and intellectual structures.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one new social or intellectual development in late medieval or early modern Europe and explains how it modified existing structures
  • Describes the contribution of at least one key individual or theory to the Scientific Revolution and connects it to broader social change

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Continuity and change in daily life.

Width

Minimum
An explanation of how at least one new class or intellectual development (e.g. merchants, the printing press, Copernicus) changed the social or intellectual landscape of late medieval and early modern Europe.
Typical
The emergence of a merchant class and guild system; the growth of universities, scientific academies and debating societies; the contribution of Copernicus, Galileo and Newton to overturning traditional views; the printing press as a vehicle for disseminating new ideas.
Wider
  • Compare the similarities and differences of daily life between the popolo minuto (common people) and popolo grasso (wealthy merchant class) in Renaissance Italy as a lens on class differentiation
  • Examine how new commodities from trade (e.g. from the Americas and Asia) drove economic change and modified the costs and quality of basic foodstuffs, connecting economic expansion to daily life

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to see the connection between merchant class growth, the spread of literacy, new institutions and the Scientific Revolution as a chain of enabling conditions — not separate events. Scaffold the logic from economic change to institutional change to intellectual change.
