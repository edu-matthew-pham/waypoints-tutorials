Historical significance

AC9HH8K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the historical significance of the chosen turning point — including its connection to the development of the modern world — and evaluate why historians regard it as significant.

Success criteria

  • Explains the historical significance of the chosen turning point using at least one criterion of significance
  • Connects the turning point to at least one feature of the modern world, explaining the link rather than merely asserting it

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Effects: what changed and what continued.

Width

Minimum
An explanation of the turning point's significance using at least one criterion of significance (e.g. scale, duration, legacy for the modern world).
Typical
Significance evaluated across multiple criteria; explicit connection to at least one feature of modern life (political, religious, cultural or economic); reference to why the turning point is remembered or commemorated.
Wider
  • Examine how the impact of the printing press on the rise of literacy represents a turning point whose significance compounds over time — its immediate effects were limited, but its long-term legacy reshaped European intellectual life
  • Investigate how the Enlightenment's ideas about human freedom and constitutional government connect to modern political structures, tracing significance through to the present

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierSignificance assessment requires integrating causal analysis (Node 2), continuity and change analysis (Node 3) and the turning point concept (Node 1). Invest in helping students draw these threads together rather than treating significance as a separate task.
