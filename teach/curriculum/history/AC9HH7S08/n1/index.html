Historical description — communicating knowledge with evidence

AC9HH7S08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create a historical description that uses historical knowledge and subject-specific terms and refers to evidence from sources — a text that accurately describes a historical topic with appropriate disciplinary language.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two subject-specific historical terms correctly in context.
  • References at least one piece of evidence from a source — naming the source or evidence type.
  • The description is accurate — it correctly represents historical knowledge without factual errors.

Prior knowledge

Students can explain historical interpretations about significant events, individuals, and groups, and develop reasoned arguments about why interpretations differ (AC9HH7S07). Students can identify and describe the accuracy and usefulness of sources, identify perspectives, and explain causes, effects, continuities, and changes (AC9HH7S03–S07). They have not yet produced formal historical descriptions, explanations, and arguments — texts that integrate historical knowledge, disciplinary concepts and terms, and source references into coherent historical communication.

Width

Minimum
Write a description of a historical topic that uses at least two historical terms correctly and refers to at least one piece of evidence from a source.
Typical
Create a historical description of a topic from the Y7 units — for example, describing the social structure of an ancient society, or the evidence for First Nations Australians' cultural continuity — that uses historical knowledge accurately, applies subject-specific terms (continuity, evidence, source, ancient, artefact), and references specific evidence from sources.
Wider
  • Produce a description in a non-traditional format — a visual display with annotations, a timeline with explanatory text, a diagram with captions — that demonstrates the same historical knowledge, terms, and evidence use as a written description.
  • Compare two descriptions of the same topic written at different levels of historical knowledge — identifying what distinguishes a strong description from a thin one in terms of specificity, evidence use, and term accuracy.
  • Investigate how professional historians write descriptions — examining the use of evidence, the precision of terms, and the density of knowledge in published historical writing — and apply these observations to improve a student description.

Hinge

Students who use historical terms vaguely, reference evidence without explaining its relevance, or substitute narrative for description cannot construct the explanatory reasoning in N2 — explanation builds on description, and imprecise description produces imprecise explanation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate description with correct terminology and source reference is a precision skill requiring practice. Students commonly use historical terms vaguely, reference evidence without explaining its relevance, or substitute narrative for description. Regular feedback on the precision of term use, the specificity of evidence reference, and the distinction between description and storytelling builds the disciplinary writing habits that explanations and arguments build on.
