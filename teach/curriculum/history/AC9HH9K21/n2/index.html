Significant events, individuals and movements in the chosen society

AC9HH9K21 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe significant events, individuals, groups and movements in the development of the chosen Asian society, explaining the role each played and what it achieved.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two significant individuals or events in the chosen society's development
  • Explains the role each played and what it achieved or failed to achieve

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The central tension — tradition vs modernisation under colonial pressure.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least two significant individuals or events from the chosen society and explain the role each played in the society's development.
Typical
For China: the Self-Strengthening Movement, Christian missionaries, Empress Dowager Cixi, the Boxer Rebellion and Society of Righteous Harmony; for Japan: Emperor Mutsuhito (Meiji), Commodore Perry's arrival, the Satsuma Rebellion, samurai resistance to modernisation; for India: the British East India Company, the Sepoy Rebellion, Indian National Congress, Gokhale, Naoroji, Tilak, early Gandhi; for Indonesia: Diponegoro, Java War, Budi Utomo, Sarekat Islam, Sukarno, Hatta, Sjahrir; describe what each figure or movement sought to achieve, what strategies they used, and what they accomplished.
Wider
  • Examine the Boxer Rebellion (or equivalent chosen society event) from both Chinese and European perspectives — analysing how the same events were understood and justified differently
  • Investigate a figure from the chosen society in depth — examining their motivations, strategies, achievements and legacy

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly focus on the most dramatic or well-known event or figure without building a picture of the range of responses — from accommodation to resistance to modernisation — that characterised the chosen society's development.
