← AC9HH8S05 Explain continuity and change

Sequencing events and identifying patterns

AC9HH8S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 History · Historical skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can sequence historical events to identify broader patterns of cause and effect, and change and continuity, across a society or period.

Success criteria

  • Sequences at least three events and identifies the broader pattern of cause/effect or change/continuity they reveal
  • Explains observations about the pattern with reference to at least two of: key events, individuals, themes, or sources

Prior knowledge

Students describe causes and effects, and explain continuities and changes (AC9HH7S05). They can identify causes, effects and continuities. They have not yet sequenced events to identify broader patterns across a society, or distinguished the different qualities of continuity — lack of deviation, widespread similarity — from the continued significance of a short-term event or development.

Width

Minimum
A sequence of at least three events with an identification of the causal or continuity pattern they reveal.
Typical
Historical events sequenced to reveal causal chains and patterns of change across multiple domains — political, social, economic, religious. The capacity to see that individual events are not isolated but part of broader patterns: e.g. decline of Rome → feudalism → growth of towns → merchant class → Renaissance → Scientific Revolution as a causal sequence.
Wider
  • Apply sequencing to both change and continuity: identify a pattern of continuity (e.g. the persistence of religious authority across the medieval period) alongside a pattern of change (e.g. the gradual erosion of feudal structures)
  • Use multiple types of evidence — events, individuals, themes, sources — to explain observations about patterns, rather than relying on a single causal account

Hinge

Sequencing events to reveal patterns is the foundation of causal and continuity analysis. Students who cannot see individual events as part of broader patterns cannot explain causes and effects at the level required, nor can they distinguish structural continuity from short-term disruption.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often list events chronologically without identifying the causal or continuity pattern. Scaffold the analysis: what does this sequence reveal about how society was changing or remaining the same? What is the underlying pattern?
