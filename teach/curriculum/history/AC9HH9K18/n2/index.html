← AC9HH9K18 Significant individuals and groups in promoting ideas

Responses to ideas — Darwin, capitalism and socialism

AC9HH9K18 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how different groups responded to specific ideas — including how religious groups responded to Darwin's evolutionary theory, and how workers responded to capitalism and socialism.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one group's response to a specific idea, explaining what the response was and why
  • Explains how values, interests or worldviews shaped how a group received a particular idea

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Historical agency — how individuals and groups promote ideas.

Width

Minimum
Students describe one case of a group responding to an idea — e.g. religious responses to Darwin, workers embracing socialism — and explain what the response was and why it was made.
Typical
Students describe Darwin's On the Origin of Species (1859) and the spectrum of religious responses — outright rejection, theological accommodation, revision of literal interpretation, enthusiastic secular embrace; describe working-class responses to capitalism (trade unionism as resistance, socialist movement as alternative) and to socialism (enthusiasm among industrial workers, fear among skilled workers, rejection by others); explain what motivated each response — what values, interests or worldviews shaped how different groups received and responded to the same idea; examine how responses shaped what ultimately happened to each idea.
Wider
  • Examine the theological debate within Christianity about evolution — from initial rejection to the accommodation most mainstream churches eventually reached — as a case study in how institutions adapt to challenging ideas
  • Compare the reception of socialism in Britain (reformist, parliamentary) with Russia (revolutionary) — examining how different social and political conditions produced different responses to the same idea

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly describe responses to ideas as uniformly positive or negative without recognising the diversity of responses within any given group. Multiple examples across different ideas and different responding groups build the nuanced picture the descriptor requires.
