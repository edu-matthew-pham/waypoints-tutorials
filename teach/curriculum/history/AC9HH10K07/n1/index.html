Push and pull factors as an analytical framework

AC9HH10K07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply a push-pull framework to explain why migration occurs, distinguishing factors that drive people from their home countries from factors that attract them to Australia.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explains the push-pull framework and distinguishes the two types of factor
  • Applies it to at least one post-war migration context with at least two push and two pull factors
  • Explains why push-pull analysis is more useful than listing migration events

Prior knowledge

Students have examined post-war reconstruction and Australia's strategic reorientation in AC9HH10K05, including the 'populate or perish' migration drive and the Colombo Plan. They understand push and pull factors in migration from general geographic study. They have not yet examined the specific waves of post-war migration in detail, the policy mechanisms governing migration, or the push factors driving each wave.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the push-pull framework and apply it to at least one post-war migration wave with at least two push and two pull factors.
Typical
Students establish push factors (war, economic collapse, political persecution, social upheaval) and pull factors (Australia's peaceful democracy, economic opportunity, established migrant communities, government recruitment); apply the framework to the post-war European migration context; explain why push-pull analysis is more analytically powerful than simply listing migration events.
Wider
  • Examine how push and pull factors interact — analysing cases where the same global event (e.g. the Holocaust, the Vietnam War) operates as a push factor for some migrants and shapes Australia's pull factors through policy responses
  • Investigate how the relative weight of push versus pull factors varies across migration waves — and what this reveals about the different kinds of migration (refugee, economic, family reunion)

Hinge

The push-pull framework is the analytical tool N2–N4 apply to successive migration waves. Students who lack it approach migration as a list of events rather than a pattern of causes. Without the framework, they cannot explain why each wave occurred when it did or compare waves analytically.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe migration as simply 'people moving to Australia' without causal analysis. Making the push-pull distinction explicit — and practising its application to a clear case before applying it to successive waves — is the scaffold for N2–N4.
