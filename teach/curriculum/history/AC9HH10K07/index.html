Post-War global influences on Australian society

AC9HH10K07 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the effects of significant post–Second World War world events, ideas and developments on Australian society

Prior knowledge

Students have examined post-war reconstruction and Australia's strategic reorientation in AC9HH10K05, including the 'populate or perish' migration drive and the Colombo Plan. They understand push and pull factors in migration from general geographic study. They have not yet examined the specific waves of post-war migration in detail, the policy mechanisms governing migration, or the push factors driving each wave.

By the end

Students analyse the effects of significant post-war world events and developments on Australian society, including the causes and nature of successive waves of migration and the government policies that shaped them.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • analysing the causes and conditions that led to increased migration to Australia and changing public opinion and government policy, such as the publication of the mass murder of the Holocaust or the plight of refugees in war-ravaged Europe
  • describing the main features of a government policy that affected migration to Australia, such as the government’s “populate or perish” policy and the “White Australia” policy
  • investigating the nature of the waves of migration (for example, from Europe in the 1950s–1960s, from different parts of Asia in the 1970s–2000s, from the Middle East in the 1980s–1990s, from India in the 1990s–2000s or from Africa in the 2000s), the numbers of migrants from those countries since the Second World War and the reasons for those migrations, including push factors such as the effects of war, economic downturns or social upheaval, and pull factors such as Australia’s peaceful democracy and economic and educational opportunities

Waypoints

  • 1. Push and pull factors as an analytical frameworkHingeStudents can apply a push-pull framework to explain why migration occurs, distinguishing factors that drive people from their home countries from factors that attract them to Australia.
  • 2. Post-war European migration — causes and government policyStudents can explain the causes of post-war European migration to Australia and describe the government policies that shaped it, including the 'populate or perish' and 'White Australia' policies.
  • 3. Successive migration waves — changing sources and causesStudents can describe the nature of successive post-war migration waves and explain the changing push factors and policy shifts that produced each wave.
  • 4. Effects of migration on Australian societyStudents can analyse the effects of successive migration waves on Australian society — economic, social, cultural, and demographic — evaluating the overall significance of migration to Australia's post-war development.
