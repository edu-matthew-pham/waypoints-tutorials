← AC9HH9K13 Causes and effects of the industrial revolution

Technology and the factory system — steam, iron and textiles

AC9HH9K13 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how key technological changes — steam-driven machinery, iron and steel production, railways — led to the development of factories and industrialised cities.

Success criteria

  • Describes steam power and at least one other key technology, explaining how each enabled industrial development
  • Explains the connection between factory growth and the development of industrial cities

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Preconditions for industrialisation — why Britain first.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the steam engine as the core technology, explain how it enabled factories and railways, and describe the resulting growth of industrial cities.
Typical
Students describe the development of steam power (Watt's improvements, stationary engines in factories, mobile engines in railways and ships); the role of iron and steel production in enabling machinery, railways and buildings; the textile industry (spinning jenny, power loom) as the first industrialised factory sector; how railways connected industrial cities and created integrated national markets; how steam power in ships enabled global trade expansion; explain the city-factory connection — factories concentrated workers in urban areas, cities grew to house and supply them.
Wider
  • Examine a specific technological innovation in depth — e.g. the Rocket locomotive (Stephenson, 1829) — and trace its effects across multiple domains (transport, manufacturing, settlement patterns, trade)
  • Investigate the spread of steam technology beyond Britain to Europe, the USA and Japan — examining how technology transfer happened and what adapted or changed in different national contexts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe the steam engine as important without understanding the mechanism — how it enabled the factory system (replacing human and water power), how railways created integrated markets, how the combination of factory and city growth is causally connected. Making these connections explicit builds the causal picture the descriptor requires.
