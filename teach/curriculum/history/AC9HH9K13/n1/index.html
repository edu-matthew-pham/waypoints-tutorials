Preconditions for industrialisation — why Britain first

AC9HH9K13 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the preconditions that enabled industrialisation to begin in Britain and explain why industrialisation began there rather than elsewhere.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three preconditions for industrialisation and explains how each contributed
  • Explains why industrialisation began in Britain rather than other European nations

Prior knowledge

Students can describe significant developments and their causes and effects in earlier periods (AC9HH8K03, AC9HH8K07). They have not yet examined the Industrial Revolution as a multi-causal, multi-domain transformation — the specific role of technology, the Agricultural Revolution preconditions, the spread of industrialisation across Europe, or how to evaluate the relative significance of multiple causes.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least three preconditions for industrialisation in Britain (e.g. Agricultural Revolution, access to coal and iron, commercial infrastructure, Enlightenment ideas, imperial trade) and explain how each contributed.
Typical
Students describe the Agricultural Revolution as releasing labour from the land; access to coal and iron ore as raw material foundations; the existing commercial infrastructure (banks, insurance, joint-stock companies); the legal framework for property rights and patents; Enlightenment ideas about progress and experimentation; the imperial trade network as both a source of capital and a market for manufactured goods; a growing population creating both labour supply and demand; explain why these conditions combined in Britain rather than France, the Netherlands or China.
Wider
  • Evaluate the relative weight of different preconditions — which was most important? Develop a supported argument and anticipate objections
  • Examine why China, which had many of the technological preconditions, did not industrialise first — exploring what this comparison reveals about the relative importance of social, political and economic conditions vs purely technological ones

Hinge

Understanding why industrialisation began in Britain rather than elsewhere is the prerequisite for understanding both the technology node (N2) and the significance evaluation in N4. Students who treat the Industrial Revolution as simply 'machines were invented' cannot explain why it was transformative or how it spread, nor can they engage the multi-causal analysis the descriptor demands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat the Industrial Revolution as a story of invention (steam engine → factories) without understanding the social, economic and political preconditions that made invention commercially viable and transformative. Making the preconditions explicit is the scaffold for multi-causal analysis throughout the standard.
