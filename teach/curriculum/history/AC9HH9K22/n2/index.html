Continuities and changes across multiple domains

AC9HH9K22 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how foreign influence and imperialism changed the chosen Asian society — and what remained the same — across ways of life, political institutions, and cultural expression around the turn of the 20th century.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two changes and one continuity in the chosen society around the turn of the 20th century
  • Applies the continuity-change framework across at least two domains

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The chosen society's position in the world around 1900.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least two changes and one continuity across different domains in the chosen society, applying the continuity-change framework.
Typical
Students apply the continuity-change framework across all three domains named in the descriptor — ways of life and living conditions, political and legal institutions, cultural expression; describe changes from European influence — railways, telegraph, Western education, factory goods, administrative reforms; continuities — traditional family structures, religious practices, artistic traditions, language, local economies that persisted despite colonial disruption; examine specific policy systems as case studies (Cultivation System vs Ethical System in Dutch East Indies; Company rule vs Crown rule in India); explain how different social groups within the chosen society experienced change and continuity differently.
Wider
  • Examine the Cultivation System and Ethical System in the Dutch East Indies as contrasting colonial policy approaches — evaluating what each prioritised and what effects each produced for Indonesian society
  • Investigate how the building of railways in India (or equivalent infrastructure in the chosen society) simultaneously produced economic integration, cultural displacement, and nationalist consciousness

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.
