Significant individual in ancient society

AC9HH7K13 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the role and achievements of a significant individual in an ancient society

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the causes and effects of contacts and conflicts within ancient societies and explain how they drove historical change (AC9HH7K12). They have accumulated understanding of the ancient society's environment (K09), social structure (K10), beliefs and practices (K11), and dynamics of change (K12). They have not yet examined how a specific individual's role and achievements reflect, shaped, and were shaped by the historical context built across K09–K12.

By the end

Students describe the role and achievements of a significant individual in the ancient society, explain how the individual's actions influenced the society, and connect the individual's story to the broader historical context of the society's environment, structure, beliefs, and dynamics of change.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • developing questions to establish the significance of key individuals such as Homer (800–701 BCE), Draco (7th century BCE), Socrates (470–399 BCE), Aspasia (470–400 BCE), Plato (428–348 BCE), Aristotle (384–322 BCE), Archimedes (287–212 BCE), Hippocrates (460–370 BCE), Alexander the Great (356–323 BCE), and/or Pericles (495–429 BCE)
  • examining the historical context, early life and achievements of a significant historical figure, such as Leonidas or Pericles from ancient Greece, and how they were perceived by their contemporaries
  • describing the importance of a key group such as patricians, plebeians, women or slaves in the maintenance of the Roman empire
  • examining the historical context, early life and achievements of a significant historical figure such as Julius Caesar, Augustus, Agrippina the Younger, Marcus Aurelius or Jesus Christ, and how they were perceived by their contemporaries
  • analysing the impact of Constantine's reign on society and religion in the Roman Empire
  • evaluating the significance of Rameses II, including military victories, expansion of the Egyptian empire and architectural endeavours
  • examining the historical context, early life and achievements of a significant historical figure from ancient Egypt, such as Hatshepsut or Akhenaten, and how they were perceived by their contemporaries
  • examining the historical context, early life and achievements of key individuals such as Chandragupta Maurya or Ashoka
  • describing the social, political and cultural impact of Confucius on ancient Chinese society
  • examining the historical context, early life and achievements of a significant historical figure such as Confucius or Qin Shi Huang, and how each was perceived by their contemporaries

Waypoints

  • 1. Context — who the individual was and why they matterHingeStudents can identify the significant individual and describe their position and role within the ancient society — including when they lived, what position they held, and why they are considered historically significant.
  • 2. Role and achievementsHingeStudents can describe the key roles and achievements of the significant individual, using evidence from sources, and explain how these achievements changed or shaped the ancient society.
  • 3. Individual and context — legacy and historical significanceStudents can explain the historical significance of the individual — connecting their role and achievements to the broader history of the ancient society — and describe the legacy of their actions within and beyond their lifetime.
