Role and achievements

AC9HH7K13 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the key roles and achievements of the significant individual, using evidence from sources, and explain how these achievements changed or shaped the ancient society.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two specific achievements with reference to evidence.
  • Explains the mechanism by which each achievement changed or shaped the society — not just that it had impact but how.
  • Uses at least two types of evidence to support the description.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Context — who the individual was and why they matter.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two specific achievements of the significant individual and explain how each changed or shaped the society.
Typical
Describe the range of the individual's roles and achievements — military, political, cultural, intellectual, or social — with reference to specific events and evidence from sources. Explain how each achievement contributed to changing or shaping the ancient society, and what the society looked different because of this individual's actions.
Wider
  • Examine specific events associated with the individual in detail — a particular military campaign, political reform, cultural patronage, or intellectual contribution — analysing the evidence and what it reveals about the individual's methods, values, and impact.
  • Investigate how the individual's achievements connected to and drew on the social structure, beliefs, and resources of the society described in K09–K11 — examining how the context shaped what was possible.
  • Explore the unintended consequences of the individual's achievements — what they set in motion that they did not intend or could not foresee.

Hinge

Students who describe achievements without explaining the mechanism of change cannot construct N3's significance argument — legacy claims require understanding how the individual's actions actually produced different outcomes, not merely that they were important.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe what an individual did without explaining the mechanism of change — how their actions produced different outcomes. The reasoning scaffold — action → mechanism → change in society — needs to be made explicit: not just 'Caesar conquered Gaul' but 'this conquest added territory, resources, and military prestige that shifted the balance of power in Rome, eventually enabling the end of the Republic'. The move from achievement to impact requires scaffolded reasoning.
