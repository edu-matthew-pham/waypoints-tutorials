First Nations Australian men and enlistment

AC9HH9K08 n6 · waypoint 6 of 6 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the distinctive circumstances of First Nations Australian men who enlisted — including the legal and social barriers they faced — and explain what their enlistment reveals about identity, belonging and the nature of Australian society in 1914.

Success criteria

  • Describes that First Nations men enlisted despite legal and social barriers, with at least one specific example
  • Explains what First Nations enlistment reveals about identity and belonging in Australian society in 1914

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 5: Reasons for Australian enlistment — the motivational framework.

Width

Minimum
Students describe that First Nations Australian men enlisted despite facing legal restrictions and social discrimination, give at least one named example or account, and explain what this tells us about their relationship to Australian and Imperial identity.
Typical
Students examine the specific legal barriers (many First Nations men were formally excluded from enlisting and required individual exemptions); the motivations that led men to enlist despite these barriers (economic, community, personal); the experience of discrimination within the military and on return; how their stories challenge a simple 'all Australians rallied together' narrative of enlistment.
Wider
  • Investigate the story of a specific First Nations soldier — e.g. Douglas Grant, Reg Saunders' father Charlie Saunders, or an individual from a local nation — to ground the general pattern in a particular human experience
  • Examine how First Nations soldiers' experiences of racial discrimination both overseas and on return connects to the broader picture of racial attitudes in Australia in this period (AC9HH9K07, AC9HH9K11)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need encounter with multiple perspectives — individual stories, legal records, post-war experiences — to build a picture of First Nations enlistment as a phenomenon with its own distinct texture, rather than treating it as a footnote to the general enlistment narrative.
