The women's movement

AC9HH10K12 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the significant events, individuals and groups in the women's movement in Australia, and how they have changed the role and status of women

Prior knowledge

Students have examined women's wartime roles and the change-versus-continuity pattern in K04 — including that most wartime gains were reversed post-war. They have studied suffrage from earlier year levels. They have not yet examined the post-1945 women's movement as a sustained organised campaign, the specific legal changes that followed, or the ongoing gap between formal equality and lived experience.

By the end

Students investigate significant events, individuals and groups in the Australian women's movement and evaluate how they changed the role and status of women.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating events connected to the changing roles and rights of women in the workforce since 1945 such as the repeal of the “marriage bar”, the Sex Discrimination Act 1984, the changing nature of participation in employment and the gender pay gap
  • examining the contributions of significant female leaders in Australian public life; for example, political leaders and activists, social reformers, sporting identities, artists and entertainers

Waypoints

  • 1. The post-war domestic settlement and its limitsHingeStudents can explain the post-war domestic settlement — the social and economic pressures that returned women to domestic roles — and why this settlement produced the conditions for organised feminist activism.
  • 2. Key events and campaigns — legal and workplace changeStudents can explain significant events connected to women's rights in the workforce since 1945, including the repeal of the marriage bar, equal pay campaigns, and the Sex Discrimination Act.
  • 3. Significant individuals and their contributionsStudents can explain the contributions of significant individuals in the women's movement, evaluating how their contributions advanced the movement.
  • 4. How the women's movement changed roles and status — evaluationStudents can evaluate how the women's movement changed the role and status of women in Australia, identifying what changed, what limits remain, and what ongoing campaigns address.
