← AC9HH10K12 The women's movement

Key events and campaigns — legal and workplace change

AC9HH10K12 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain significant events connected to women's rights in the workforce since 1945, including the repeal of the marriage bar, equal pay campaigns, and the Sex Discrimination Act.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least three events or campaigns connected to women's workforce rights
  • Evaluates what each change produced — not merely describes it
  • Identifies at least one way formal legal change has not produced full practical equality

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The post-war domestic settlement and its limits.

Width

Minimum
Students explain at least three events or campaigns connected to women's workforce rights and evaluate what each changed.
Typical
Students trace key events: the 1966 repeal of the marriage bar in the Commonwealth Public Service; the equal pay case (1969 and 1972 ACTU cases); the introduction of maternity leave; the Sex Discrimination Act 1984; the changing nature of women's participation in employment from the 1960s to present; the persistence of the gender pay gap despite formal equality legislation; explain how each change was achieved — who campaigned, through what means, and what opposition was faced.
Wider
  • Examine the equal pay cases in depth — analysing the arguments made, the industrial tribunal's reasoning, and why equal pay in principle has not produced equal pay in practice
  • Investigate the gender pay gap today — using current data to evaluate whether the legislative changes of the 1970s–80s have produced genuine workforce equality, and what structural factors maintain the gap

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a working knowledge of the key legislative and workplace milestones before evaluation of the movement's overall impact is meaningful. Building the repertoire systematically prevents the reductive focus on one or two famous events.
