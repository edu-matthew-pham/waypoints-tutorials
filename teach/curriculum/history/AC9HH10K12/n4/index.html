How the women's movement changed roles and status — evaluation

AC9HH10K12 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how the women's movement changed the role and status of women in Australia, identifying what changed, what limits remain, and what ongoing campaigns address.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates how the women's movement changed roles and status — identifying at least two durable changes
  • Identifies at least one significant area where formal change has not produced practical equality
  • Connects the historical movement to ongoing campaigns, explaining the continuity

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Significant individuals and their contributions.

Width

Minimum
Students construct an evaluative claim about how the women's movement changed roles and status, identifying at least two changes and at least one significant limit.
Typical
Students draw on N1–N3 to evaluate change across domains — workforce participation, legal rights, political representation, cultural attitudes; identify what changed durably, what changed formally but not practically (gender pay gap, domestic violence rates), and what remains contested; connect to K13's content on continuing struggles; evaluate whether the movement's achievements represent a transformation or an incremental improvement in women's status.
Wider
  • Evaluate the contemporary #MeToo movement as a continuation of the women's movement — analysing what it achieved, what it revealed about the limits of earlier legislative change, and how social media changed the dynamics of feminist activism
  • Compare women's status in Australia with another comparable country — evaluating whether Australia's women's movement produced more or less change than comparable societies

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node draws together the context (N1), legislative and workplace changes (N2), and individual contributions (N3) into an overall evaluation. Tasks requiring students to argue about the extent and limits of change across multiple domains work well here.
