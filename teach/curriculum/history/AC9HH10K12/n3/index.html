Significant individuals and their contributions

AC9HH10K12 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the contributions of significant individuals in the women's movement, evaluating how their contributions advanced the movement.

Success criteria

  • Explains the contributions of at least two individuals across different domains
  • Applies the evaluation framework to assess the impact of each contribution
  • Identifies at least one contribution from outside formal politics

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Key events and campaigns — legal and workplace change.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the contributions of at least two significant individuals across different domains (political, activist, cultural) and evaluate each.
Typical
Students examine contributions across domains: political leaders (Gough Whitlam government's women's policy agenda, Joan Child as first female Speaker); activists and organisers (Women's Electoral Lobby, women's refuges movement); cultural contributors (Germaine Greer, Dale Spender); legal reformers; explain what each contributed, through what means, and what opposition they faced; apply the K10 evaluation framework — what type of change, how durable, how broad.
Wider
  • Examine the contributions of significant Indigenous women leaders — analysing how their work navigated both gender and racial discrimination, and how their contributions have been recognised relative to non-Indigenous women leaders
  • Investigate the role of men who supported women's rights — examining what motivated male allies, what they contributed, and how their involvement affected the movement's direction

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExamining individuals across political, activist, and cultural domains gives a fuller picture than focusing on political leaders alone. The movement operated across multiple spheres simultaneously; covering multiple domains reflects this.
