Global influences on national identity

AC9HH10K19 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the effects of global influences on Australia's changing identity as a nation and its international relationships

Prior knowledge

Students have examined post-war foreign policy (K05, K18), cultural transformation (K16, K17), and Australia's evolving relationship with Britain, the USA, and Asia. They understand how migration transformed Australia's cultural composition (K07–K08). They have not yet examined how specific prime ministers' foreign policy shaped national identity, or how Australia's contribution to international popular culture connects to national identity.

By the end

Students explain the effects of global influences on Australia's changing identity as a nation — including how post-war foreign policy shaped national identity, Australia's contribution to and absorption of international popular culture, and the rising influence of China and India.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigate how the broad foreign policy and international relations policies of prime minister Gough Whitlam (1972–1975), prime minister Malcolm Fraser (1975–1983), prime minister Bob Hawke (1983–1991) in relation to cultural exchange, migration, trade and defence shaped Australia’s national identity
  • discussing the changing nature of the music, film and television industries in Australia during the post-war period, including the influence of overseas developments such as rock ‘n’ roll, disco, punk, grunge, indie and other music genres; Hollywood, Bollywood and the animation film industry in China and Japan; and the development of satellite broadcast on television
  • investigating Australia’s contribution to international popular culture in music, film, television and sport, and the recognition of Australian creative and performing arts internationally
  • discussing the rising economic and political influence of countries such as China and India since the end of the Cold War
  • examining the role of digital media, including social media, in shaping Australian society and connecting to global relationships

Waypoints

  • 1. Foreign policy and national identity — Whitlam, Fraser, HawkeHingeStudents can explain how the foreign policies of Whitlam, Fraser, and Hawke shaped Australia's national identity and international relationships.
  • 2. Australia's contribution to international popular cultureStudents can investigate Australia's contribution to international popular culture in music, film, television, and sport, and explain how this contribution shaped Australian national identity.
  • 3. The rising influence of China and IndiaStudents can explain the rising economic and political influence of China and India since the end of the Cold War and analyse its effects on Australia's international relationships and identity.
  • 4. Digital media and social media — shaping identity and global connectionStudents can examine the role of digital media, including social media, in shaping Australian society and connecting to global relationships.
