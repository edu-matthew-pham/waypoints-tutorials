The rising influence of China and India

AC9HH10K19 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the rising economic and political influence of China and India since the end of the Cold War and analyse its effects on Australia's international relationships and identity.

Success criteria

  • Explains the rise of China or India as a global power with evidence of their economic and political influence
  • Identifies at least two effects of this rise on Australia's international relationships
  • Explains the structural tension in Australia's relationship with China — economic interdependence versus strategic competition

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Australia's contribution to international popular culture.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the rise of China or India as a global economic and political power and explain at least two effects on Australia's international relationships.
Typical
Students examine China's economic rise since the 1990s — its transformation into the world's second-largest economy, its emergence as Australia's largest trading partner, the tensions between economic interdependence and strategic competition; examine India's emergence as a major economic and demographic power; examine how the simultaneous US alliance and Chinese economic relationship creates a structural tension in Australian foreign policy; connect to K18's Keating–Howard contrast — each approach carries different implications for managing the China relationship.
Wider
  • Examine the Australia-China relationship since 2017 — analysing how strategic tensions (South China Sea, Huawei, COVID-19 origins, trade disputes) transformed a relationship built on economic complementarity, and what this reveals about the limits of economic interdependence as a basis for stable relationships
  • Investigate how Australia's relationship with India has developed — examining what strategic logic drives the Quad (Australia, USA, Japan, India) and whether India represents a viable counterbalance to China in Australia's strategic calculations

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to understand the economic-strategic tension in Australia's China relationship as a structural feature, not just a recent political dispute. Scaffolding the tension explicitly — economic interdependence vs strategic competition, the ANZUS alliance obligation vs trading relationship — produces genuine analytical engagement rather than simple description.
