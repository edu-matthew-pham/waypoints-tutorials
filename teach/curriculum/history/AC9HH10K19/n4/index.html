Digital media and social media — shaping identity and global connection

AC9HH10K19 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine the role of digital media, including social media, in shaping Australian society and connecting to global relationships.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two ways digital media has shaped Australian society
  • Explains at least one way digital media has changed Australia's global connections
  • Identifies at least one tension digital media creates for Australian national identity

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: The rising influence of China and India.

Width

Minimum
Students explain at least two ways digital media has shaped Australian society and at least one way it has changed Australia's global connections.
Typical
Students examine how the internet and digital media transformed information access, communication, and cultural consumption from the 1990s; examine how social media platforms have changed how Australians form communities, debate public issues, and connect with global trends; examine how digital media has both strengthened and complicated Australian national identity — providing new platforms for Australian cultural expression while accelerating exposure to global (predominantly American) cultural content; examine how social media has changed the dynamics of political debate and activism.
Wider
  • Examine how digital media has changed the dynamics of civil rights and social movements — connecting to K11's reconciliation content and K12's women's movement content to examine how social media transformed advocacy
  • Investigate how digital media has affected Australia's international relationships — examining whether it has strengthened connections with Asia or reinforced Anglo-American cultural ties through platform dominance

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat social media as obviously transformative without examining the specific mechanisms through which it shapes identity and relationships. Scaffolding the mechanisms — how does algorithmic curation shape what Australians know about the world? how does platform dominance affect whose culture is amplified? — produces analytical engagement rather than description.
