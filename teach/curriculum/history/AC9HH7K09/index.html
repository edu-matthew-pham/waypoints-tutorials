Environment and ancient development

AC9HH7K09 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how the physical environment and geographical features influenced the development of the ancient society

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the methods and sources of evidence used by historians and archaeologists to investigate early societies, and explain the importance of conservation (AC9HH7K08). They have not yet examined how the physical environment and geography specifically shaped the development of an ancient society — the cause-and-effect relationship between landscape and civilisation.

By the end

Students describe how the physical environment and geographical features of an ancient society's location influenced its development — including how landforms, waterways, climate, and resources shaped settlement patterns, agriculture, trade, and cultural development.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the role of topography in providing barriers of defence for city-states, such as the surrounding seas, mountain ranges and river systems; for example, the Axios (Vardar), Strimonas (Struma) and Nestos rivers
  • explaining the role of the fertile soil of the Po and Tiber River regions in enabling trade through surplus production
  • describing Roman methods to manage resources; for example, water supply through aqueducts and plumbing systems
  • explaining how the Nile River enabled the establishment of ancient Egyptian society through farming (including the role of the inundation), transportation, defence (for example, the cataract fortifications) and religion (for example, Hapi, god of the inundation; Khnum, god of the Nile; and Satet, goddess of Nile floods and the Nile cataracts)
  • identifying how the environment and harmonious relationships with the natural world were reflected in belief systems such as Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism
  • examining the role of climate in enabling the establishment and expansion of agriculture around the Yellow River and how this supported the ancient society
  • describing the impact of topographic features, such as the Himalayas, rivers and seas, on contact with other societies, including trade and warfare

Waypoints

  • 1. Physical geography of the ancient society's locationHingeStudents can describe the key geographical features of the region where the ancient society developed — including major landforms, waterways, climate, and resources — and explain why this location was viable for settlement.
  • 2. Environment and agriculture — the foundation of ancient societyHingeStudents can explain how the physical environment supported the development of agriculture in the ancient society, and describe how agricultural surpluses enabled population growth, specialisation, and the development of complex social structures.
  • 3. Environment and settlement, trade, and cultural developmentStudents can explain how the physical environment shaped the ancient society's settlement patterns, trade networks, and cultural development — including how geographical features created opportunities and constraints that defined what the society became.
