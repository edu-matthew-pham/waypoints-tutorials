Explain historical interpretations

AC9HH7S07 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explain historical interpretations about significant events, individuals and groups

Prior knowledge

Students can identify perspectives, attitudes, and values of the past in sources (AC9HH7S06). They have not yet applied the concept of historical interpretation — understanding that historians construct different interpretations of the same events, that interpretations are argument-based rather than simply 'what happened', and that explaining why different interpretations exist is a distinct historical skill.

By the end

Students explain historical interpretations about significant events, individuals, and groups — demonstrating understanding that interpretations are constructed from evidence and argument, that different interpretations of the same event can coexist, and that developing a historical argument about why something is interpreted in different ways is a key historical skill.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining different interpretations to explain short-term triggers and/or turning points of the decline of an empire or society, such as failing economy, political upheaval, foreign invasion or conflict
  • developing a historical argument about why an event, individual or group is interpreted in different ways

Waypoints

  • 1. What is a historical interpretation?HingeStudents can explain what a historical interpretation is — that it is a constructed argument about the past based on evidence, not a simple statement of fact — and distinguish between a historical fact and a historical interpretation.
  • 2. Different interpretations of the same event or individualStudents can describe how the same historical event, individual, or group can be interpreted in different ways by different historians, and explain what causes these differences — including different evidence, different questions, and different values.
  • 3. Developing a historical argument about interpretationStudents can develop a historical argument about why an event, individual, or group is interpreted in different ways — constructing a reasoned explanation that accounts for the different interpretations and the factors that produce them.
