Developing a historical argument about interpretation

AC9HH7S07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop a historical argument about why an event, individual, or group is interpreted in different ways — constructing a reasoned explanation that accounts for the different interpretations and the factors that produce them.

Success criteria

  • Develops a reasoned argument — not just a description — about why a historical event or individual is interpreted in different ways.
  • Identifies at least two interpretations and at least two factors producing the difference.
  • The argument uses evidence to support its claims, not just assertion.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Different interpretations of the same event or individual.

Width

Minimum
Develop a basic argument explaining why a specific historical event or individual is interpreted in different ways, identifying at least two factors that produce the different interpretations.
Typical
Develop a historical argument explaining why a specific historical event, individual, or group is interpreted in different ways — identifying the different interpretations, explaining the evidence and reasoning behind each, and constructing a reasoned account of why interpretive diversity exists for this case. Connect the argument to the factors identified in Node 2 (evidence, questions, values).
Wider
  • Develop an argument about which of two competing interpretations is better supported by evidence — constructing a case for one interpretation while acknowledging the strengths of the alternative.
  • Investigate how historical arguments about interpretation function as a genre — examining the structure, evidence use, and reasoning in a published historical argument, and applying those structural insights to student argument development.
  • Explore how developing arguments about historical interpretation connects to the S08 communicating skill — examining how the argument structure developed in this descriptor supports the creation of historical writing.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates understanding of what interpretations are (Node 1) and why they differ (Node 2) into the descriptor's full demand — developing a historical argument about interpretation. Students must coordinate interpretation identification, causal analysis of interpretive diversity, and argument construction into a coherent explanatory claim. This is the most demanding node in the S-strand to this point, and directly prepares S08.
