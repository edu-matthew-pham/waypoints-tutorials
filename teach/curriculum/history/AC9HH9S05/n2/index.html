Evaluating patterns of continuity and change

AC9HH9S05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate patterns of continuity and change across a period, making judgements about the significance of changes and the persistence of continuities.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least one pattern of change and one continuity, making a judgement about the significance of each
  • Explains why at least one change was significant or why one continuity persisted, with reference to historical evidence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Analysing cause-and-effect relationships.

Width

Minimum
Identify one significant pattern of change and one continuity across a period being studied, explaining why each is significant.
Typical
Organise primary sources and perspectives chronologically to support historical argument about continuities and changes; evaluate whether changes were rapid or gradual, widespread or limited, reversible or permanent; assess which continuities were most significant and which changes had the greatest long-term impact.
Wider
  • Apply continuity and change analysis to two different social groups experiencing the same historical period (e.g. colonisers and colonised) — examining how the same period produced different patterns for different groups
  • Evaluate continuity and change across three different domains (social, political, economic) for the same period — assessing whether change was more or less rapid in different domains and explaining why
  • Construct a historical argument about whether a particular period (e.g. Federation, the Industrial Revolution) represented genuine transformation or primarily continuity — supporting the argument with evidence from multiple source types

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApplying the continuity/change framework to multiple groups, multiple domains, or multiple topics builds the skill as a genuine analytical capacity rather than a template applied once.
