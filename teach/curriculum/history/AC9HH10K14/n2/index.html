Technological development and its effects

AC9HH10K14 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify key technological developments since 1945 and explain how each changed the world — in households, travel and trade, and communications.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three technological developments across at least two domains
  • Explains the effect of each on everyday life — not merely notes it occurred
  • Explains how technological change changed what was possible rather than merely what was available

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Changing perspectives as a historical concept.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least three technological developments across at least two domains and explain the effect of each on everyday life.
Typical
Students examine household technology (television, appliances, central heating/cooling) and its effects on domestic life and gender roles; travel and trade technology (shipping containers, passenger jets, logistics) and its role in enabling globalisation; communications and computing (microchip, satellites, digital systems, the internet) and its transformation of information, work, and connection; explain how each wave of technology changed what was possible and how people understood their world.
Wider
  • Examine the social effects of television's arrival in Australia — connecting to K16's content on American cultural influence and the Melbourne Olympics (1956) as the catalyst for TV adoption
  • Investigate how communications technology has changed the dynamics of political movements — comparing how the civil rights movements of the 1960s used television with how contemporary movements use social media

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a working repertoire of technological developments across domains before the perspectives analysis in N4 is meaningful. Covering household, travel, and communications systematically prevents the common reduction to digital technology alone.
