← AC9HH7S01 Develop historical questions

Questions that inform inquiry — scope and focus

AC9HH7S01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop historical questions that are appropriately scoped for inquiry — not so broad as to be unanswerable and not so narrow as to require only recall — and explain how their question would guide the selection of evidence.

Success criteria

  • Develops a historical inquiry question that is appropriately scoped — investigable, not a simple recall or overly broad question.
  • Identifies what types of evidence would be needed to investigate the question.
  • Explains how the question would guide the investigation — what it would require the student to look for.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Developing questions using historical concepts.

Width

Minimum
Develop a historical inquiry question and explain in basic terms what types of evidence would be needed to investigate it.
Typical
Develop a historical inquiry question that is appropriately scoped for the available evidence, explain what types of evidence would be needed to investigate it, and demonstrate awareness of how the question's scope (breadth, time period, focus) shapes what counts as a relevant answer.
Wider
  • Refine a broad question into a more focused, investigable form — demonstrating the process of moving from initial curiosity to a researchable question — and explain what was gained and lost in the refinement.
  • Develop a set of sub-questions that break a complex inquiry question into manageable components, and explain how each sub-question contributes to answering the main question.
  • Examine how historians phrase their central questions in published works, and analyse what makes those questions effective as guides for inquiry.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the concept of a historical question (Node 1) and the skill of generating questions using concept frames (Node 2) into the descriptor's full demand — developing questions that genuinely inform inquiry. Students must coordinate the disciplinary requirements of historical questions with appropriate scope and evidence awareness, producing questions that are both historically focused and practically investigable.
