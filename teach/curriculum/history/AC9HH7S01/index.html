Develop historical questions

AC9HH7S01 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop historical questions about the past to inform historical inquiry

Prior knowledge

Students can develop questions about objects, people, places, and events in the past and present (AC9HS6S01). They have not yet distinguished historical inquiry questions from general questions — specifically the disciplinary requirements of historical questions: they must be investigable using evidence, focused on historical significance, causation, continuity/change, or perspective, and they must be open enough to require analysis rather than simple recall.

By the end

Students develop historical questions that can inform historical inquiry — questions that are focused on historically significant issues, investigable using historical evidence, and framed around historical concepts such as causation, continuity and change, perspective, or significance.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • developing a key question such as 'How were the pyramids at Giza built?' and understanding that there may not be a definitive answer; identifying related questions to inform the inquiry, including 'What evidence is there?' and 'What theories have been developed?'
  • developing questions using historical concepts such as cause, effect, change, continuity, perspectives, interpretations and significance; for example, 'What were the effects of rising sea levels on the movements of early First Nations Australians?'

Waypoints

  • 1. What makes a question historical?HingeStudents can distinguish between a general question about the past and a historical inquiry question, and explain what makes a question suitable for historical inquiry — investigable using evidence, focused on historical significance or causation.
  • 2. Developing questions using historical conceptsHingeStudents can develop historical inquiry questions using the key historical concepts — causation, continuity and change, perspective, and significance — as question frames.
  • 3. Questions that inform inquiry — scope and focusStudents can develop historical questions that are appropriately scoped for inquiry — not so broad as to be unanswerable and not so narrow as to require only recall — and explain how their question would guide the selection of evidence.
