← AC9HH9K11 Effects of WW1 on Australian society

Returned soldiers — physical, psychological and social experiences

AC9HH9K11 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the effects of war on returned soldiers — including physical and psychological trauma, reintegration challenges, social and racial discrimination, and the policy responses — including the experiences of First Nations Australian soldiers.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least three distinct types of effects experienced by returned soldiers
  • Explains the specific additional challenges faced by First Nations Australian returned soldiers, including racial discrimination and unequal access to repatriation benefits

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Australia's changing relationship with Britain.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least three distinct types of effects on returned soldiers (e.g. physical wounds/disability, shell shock/psychological trauma, employment difficulties) and describe one policy response (e.g. Soldier Settlement Scheme, pensions).
Typical
Students describe the scale and nature of physical casualties (wounded, disabled, disfigured); explain shell shock as a documented psychological consequence — what symptoms were observed, how it was understood and often dismissed at the time; examine employment reintegration challenges — many returned to a labour market that had changed; the Soldier Settlement Scheme — land allocation to returned soldiers, why many schemes failed; pension access and disputes; the experience of returned soldiers seeking social recognition of their service; apply these patterns specifically to First Nations Australian soldiers' experiences of additional racial discrimination, wage inequality, and exclusion from some repatriation benefits.
Wider
  • Examine personal accounts from returned soldiers — diaries, letters, oral histories — to ground the general patterns in specific human experiences, applying source analysis skills
  • Investigate the experience of a specific First Nations returned soldier — service record, post-war life, obstacles faced — to make concrete the additional layer of discrimination documented in elaboration 4

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly reduce the returned soldier experience to either heroic recognition or simple suffering. The full range — physical, psychological, economic, social, racial — produces a more complete picture that the descriptor requires.
Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyIndividual case studies — including First Nations soldiers — build the accumulated picture of returned soldier experience that allows the pattern to be understood as systemic, not exceptional.
