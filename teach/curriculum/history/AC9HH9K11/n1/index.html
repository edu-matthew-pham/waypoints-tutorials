The home front — how the war reshaped Australian society

AC9HH9K11 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe what the 'home front' means — the ways in which the war affected Australian society beyond the battlefield — and identify the key areas of social change it produced.

Success criteria

  • Describes the concept of the home front and explains how the war affected Australian society beyond the battlefield
  • Identifies the four main areas of social change named in the descriptor

Prior knowledge

Students can describe Australia's relationship with Britain and Empire identity by 1914 (AC9HH9K08) and the nature of warfare and the war's major turning points (AC9HH9K09, AC9HH9K10). They have not yet examined how the war reshaped Australian domestic society — including the political crisis over conscription, the changed role of women, the experience of returned soldiers, and what the war revealed about the changing relationship with Britain.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that the war had major effects on Australian society at home — on labour, women's roles, politics, and culture — and identify the four main areas the descriptor names (women's roles, conscription debates, Empire relations, returned soldiers).
Typical
Students describe the scale of Australian mobilisation (over 400,000 enlisted from a population of under 5 million); how this created labour shortages and opened new roles for women; how casualty lists arrived in communities and created grief and pressure; how the war dominated political life; how returned soldiers reintegrated into a society that had changed in their absence; frame the concept of the 'home front' as distinct from but connected to the battlefield.
Wider
  • Examine local community records — enlistment rolls, casualty notices, local newspapers — to connect the national picture to a specific town or suburb's experience of the home front
  • Compare the Australian home front with the British home front — what was similar and what was different about societies with and without direct experience of enemy attack?

Hinge

The home front concept frames the subsequent nodes — students who do not understand that a war reshapes domestic society as well as battlefields will approach the conscription debate (N3) as purely a political argument rather than a lived social crisis, and the returned soldiers node (N4) as merely a list of problems rather than a structural social consequence.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly partition the war into 'what happened at Gallipoli/the Western Front' and 'Australia'. Making the home front concept explicit — the war was fought on multiple fronts simultaneously, including social and political ones at home — reframes the structure for all subsequent nodes.
