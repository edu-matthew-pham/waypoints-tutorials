Effects of WW1 on Australian society

AC9HH9K11 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the effects of the First World War on Australian society, such as the role of women, political debates about conscription, relationships with the British Empire, and the experiences of returned soldiers

Prior knowledge

Students can describe Australia's relationship with Britain and Empire identity by 1914 (AC9HH9K08) and the nature of warfare and the war's major turning points (AC9HH9K09, AC9HH9K10). They have not yet examined how the war reshaped Australian domestic society — including the political crisis over conscription, the changed role of women, the experience of returned soldiers, and what the war revealed about the changing relationship with Britain.

By the end

Students investigate examples of the war's effects on Australian society — including changes to women's roles, the conscription referenda debates, continuities and changes in Australia's relationship with the British Empire, and the physical, psychological, economic and social experiences of returned soldiers including First Nations Australian soldiers.

Waypoints

  • 1. The home front — how the war reshaped Australian societyHingeStudents can describe what the 'home front' means — the ways in which the war affected Australian society beyond the battlefield — and identify the key areas of social change it produced.
  • 2. Women's roles — change and continuityStudents can describe the changes to women's roles in Australian society during the war and identify what changed compared to before the war, and what continuities persisted after it.
  • 3. The conscription debates — political crisis and social divisionHingeStudents can describe the two conscription referenda, identify the main groups who supported and opposed conscription and their reasons, and explain why the conscription issue divided Australian society and the Labor Party.
  • 4. Australia's changing relationship with BritainStudents can examine the continuities and changes in Australia's relationship with the British Empire as a result of the war, including how the war both affirmed and strained the imperial relationship.
  • 5. Returned soldiers — physical, psychological and social experiencesStudents can explain the effects of war on returned soldiers — including physical and psychological trauma, reintegration challenges, social and racial discrimination, and the policy responses — including the experiences of First Nations Australian soldiers.
