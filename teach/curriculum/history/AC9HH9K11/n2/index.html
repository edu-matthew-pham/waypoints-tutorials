← AC9HH9K11 Effects of WW1 on Australian society

Women's roles — change and continuity

AC9HH9K11 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the changes to women's roles in Australian society during the war and identify what changed compared to before the war, and what continuities persisted after it.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two specific changes to women's roles during the war
  • Identifies what changed compared to before the war and whether those changes persisted after 1918

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The home front — how the war reshaped Australian society.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least two specific changes to women's roles during the war (e.g. entry into previously male workplaces, nursing roles, voluntary organisations) and note whether these changes persisted after the war ended.
Typical
Students examine the specific roles women took on in paid work (manufacturing, transport, office work), in the voluntary sector (Red Cross, comfort funds, war savings), and as nurses in military hospitals; compare these to women's roles and employment patterns before 1914; examine what happened when men returned — which changes persisted, which reversed; consider what the experience of wartime work meant for women's expectations about employment and rights; connect to the broader suffrage context from AC9HH9K04 and K05.
Wider
  • Examine primary sources from Australian women during the war — letters, diary entries, newspaper reports of women workers — to ground the general pattern in individual voices and perspectives
  • Investigate how white feather campaigns created social pressure on men to enlist — examining how women were recruited as instruments of conscription pressure even without formal enlistment — and what this reveals about gender and patriotism

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly frame women's wartime roles as simple liberation — 'women got to work' — without examining the complexity of change and continuity, or the coercive aspects of women's wartime participation (white feather campaigns). Multiple examples across different domains of activity prevent oversimplification.
