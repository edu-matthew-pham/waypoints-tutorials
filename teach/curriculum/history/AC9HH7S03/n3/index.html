Integrating OCCP — what can this source tell us?

AC9HH7S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can integrate origin, content, context, and purpose analysis to explain what a source can and cannot tell us about the past, and apply this analytical framework to sources from the Deep Time and Ancient units.

  • What can this source tell us?

Success criteria

  • Applies all four dimensions (origin, content, context, purpose) to a source systematically.
  • Draws a reasoned conclusion about what the source can and cannot tell us about a specific historical question.
  • Does not treat OCCP as four separate boxes — synthesises the dimensions into an integrated interpretive conclusion.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Context and purpose — the interpretive questions.

Width

Minimum
For a given source, apply all four analytical dimensions (origin, content, context, purpose) and draw a conclusion about what the source can tell us about a specific historical question.
Typical
For a primary or secondary source from the History Y7 units, systematically apply origin, content, context, and purpose analysis and synthesise the four dimensions into a conclusion about what the source can tell us, what it cannot tell us, and why. Apply to source types from both the Deep Time unit (artefacts, oral tradition, rock art) and the Ancient unit (inscriptions, artefacts, artworks, historical texts).
Wider
  • Apply the OCCP framework to a source that is challenging or ambiguous — such as the Kimberley Bradshaw paintings (disputed origin), ancient oral traditions (uncertain authorship and transmission), or propaganda inscriptions (purpose that complicates reliability) — and explain how the challenge affects what can be concluded.
  • Compare OCCP analysis of two sources about the same event or person, examining how different origins and purposes produce different accounts and what a historian can conclude from the comparison.
  • Examine how the OCCP framework connects to the broader historical skill of corroboration — why multiple sources analysed through OCCP produce more reliable interpretations than any single source.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates origin/content (Node 1) and context/purpose (Node 2) into the descriptor's full demand — applying all four analytical dimensions together to reach a conclusion about what a source can and cannot tell us. Students must coordinate the four dimensions, not treat them as separate boxes to fill in, and synthesise them into an interpretive conclusion.
