Context and purpose — the interpretive questions

AC9HH7S03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Historical skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the historical context in which a source was produced and identify its purpose — why it was made — and describe how context and purpose affect what a source reveals about the past.

Lessons on this site

  • Why was it made, and what was going on?

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the historical context of a source's production — not just 'it's from ancient times' but specific circumstances.
  • Identifies the purpose of the source — ceremonial, propagandistic, administrative, commemorative — with reasoning.
  • Explains how context and purpose affect what the source reveals and what it omits.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Origin and content — the basic analytical questions.

Width

Minimum
For a given source, explain the historical context in which it was produced and identify its purpose (why it was made).
Typical
For a given primary or secondary source, explain the historical context — the period, events, social conditions, and circumstances that surrounded its production — and identify its purpose: was it ceremonial, propagandistic, administrative, commemorative, communicative, artistic? Explain how the context and purpose shape what the source includes, excludes, and emphasises.
Wider
  • Examine how political context shapes the purpose of sources — how rulers commissioned art, monuments, and texts for specific purposes (legitimation, propaganda, commemoration) — and what this means for reading them as historical evidence.
  • Investigate how the intended audience for a source shapes its content and purpose — a public inscription vs a private letter vs an official administrative record each have different audiences and therefore different purposes and omissions.
  • Explore how context and purpose interact in complex sources — a funerary text that is simultaneously religious, commemorative, administrative, and personal — and how identifying multiple purposes enriches source interpretation.

Hinge

Students who conflate context with setting ('it's from Ancient Egypt') and purpose with content cannot produce integrated OCCP conclusions in N3 — the synthesis node requires all four dimensions to be clearly differentiated before they can be coordinated into an interpretive conclusion.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkContext and purpose are more abstract than origin and content, and students frequently conflate them ('the context is Ancient Egypt' conflates setting with the specific circumstances of production; 'the purpose is to show X' conflates content with intent). The reasoning chain — who made this → for what audience → under what circumstances → with what intent → therefore it shows/omits X — needs explicit scaffolding before students can apply it reliably.
