← AC9HH7K10 Roles and groups in ancient society

Slaves and other groups — power structures and social influence

AC9HH7K10 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the role of slaves and other marginalised groups in the ancient society, explain how these groups contributed to and influenced the society, and describe how the relationships between social groups changed over time.

Success criteria

  • Describes the role of slaves in the ancient society with reference to specific types of work or contributions.
  • Explains that the social structure involved interdependent groups — not just powerful groups on top of passive groups below.
  • Describes at least one way in which the relationships between social groups changed over time.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Women's roles and their influence.

Width

Minimum
Describe the role of slaves in the ancient society and explain how the slave system contributed to the society's functioning.
Typical
Describe the role of slaves and the structural conditions of slavery in the ancient society — how people became enslaved, what work they performed, how they were treated, and how the slave system underpinned the economy and social structure. Describe how other non-elite groups (artisans, merchants, farmers) also contributed to and influenced the society. Explain how the composition and relationships of these groups changed over time.
Wider
  • Examine the evidence for how slaves experienced, responded to, and sometimes resisted their condition — including examples of revolt, self-purchase, manumission, and legal status changes — challenging a purely passive account of enslaved people.
  • Investigate how the slave system shaped other aspects of the society — architecture, cultural production, military capacity, economic specialisation — examining the ways in which slaves' contributions were foundational rather than peripheral.
  • Explore how the composition and size of enslaved populations changed over time in the ancient society — through conquest, trade, debt — and how changes in these numbers affected social stability.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the social hierarchy (Node 1) and women's roles (Node 2) into a complete account of the social structure of the ancient society — including how power relationships between all groups influenced and changed the society. Students must coordinate multiple social groups and their interactions into a coherent picture of how the society was organised and how it changed, reaching the descriptor's full demand.
