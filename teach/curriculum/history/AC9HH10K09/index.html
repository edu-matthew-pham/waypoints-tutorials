First Nations rights campaigns before 1965

AC9HH10K09 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the causes of First Nations Australians' campaigns for rights and freedoms before 1965, such as discriminatory legislation and policies, the 1938 Day of Mourning and the Stolen Generations

Prior knowledge

Students have examined First Nations Australians' wartime service and the contradiction between service and denied citizenship (AC9HH10K04). From Y9 they have studied colonisation and its effects on First Nations peoples (AC9HH9K03, K06). They have not yet examined the specific legislative and policy framework that governed First Nations Australians' lives in the 20th century, the early organised campaigns for rights, or the Stolen Generations as a distinct historical phenomenon.

By the end

Students explain the causes of First Nations Australians' campaigns for rights and freedoms before 1965, including the discriminatory legislative and policy framework, the significance of the 1938 Day of Mourning, and the impact of the Stolen Generations on the civil rights movement.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating the political and societal impacts that contributed to First Nations Australians’ campaigns for rights and freedoms, such as legal status at the Commonwealth and various state/territory levels, segregation and treatment on missions, and the policy of assimilation adopted in 1937 by the Aboriginal Welfare Conference of Commonwealth and State Authorities
  • explaining the significance of the 1938 Day of Mourning in the campaigns of First Nations Australians for rights and freedoms, including the significance of 26 January 1938 (150 years since the arrival of the First Fleet) and the national Indigenous rights meeting
  • exploring accounts of the past experiences of First Nations Australians who were members of the Stolen Generations and how these experiences influenced the civil rights movement in Australia from the 1960s through to the present day

Waypoints

  • 1. The legislative and policy framework of exclusionHingeStudents can describe the legal and policy framework that governed First Nations Australians' lives in the early-to-mid 20th century, explaining how it structured inequality and motivated campaigns for change.
  • 2. Early organised resistance — the AAPA, AAL, and the 1938 Day of MourningStudents can explain the significance of early organised campaigns for First Nations rights, including the AAPA, the Australian Aborigines League, and the 1938 Day of Mourning.
  • 3. The Stolen Generations — policy, experience, and lasting impactStudents can explain the policy basis for the removal of First Nations children, describe the experiences of those removed, and explain how the Stolen Generations influenced the civil rights movement.
