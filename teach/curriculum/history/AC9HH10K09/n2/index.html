Early organised resistance — the AAPA, AAL, and the 1938 Day of Mourning

AC9HH10K09 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the significance of early organised campaigns for First Nations rights, including the AAPA, the Australian Aborigines League, and the 1938 Day of Mourning.

Success criteria

  • Explains the significance of the 1938 Day of Mourning — including the choice of date and the demands made
  • Identifies at least two early organisations involved in First Nations rights campaigns and explains each organisation's approach
  • Explains how early campaigns established the model for later organised activism

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The legislative and policy framework of exclusion.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the significance of the 1938 Day of Mourning — what it was, why 26 January 1938 was chosen, and what it demanded.
Typical
Students examine the Australian Aboriginal Progressive Association (AAPA) under Fred Maynard as an early organised political movement; examine William Cooper and the Australian Aborigines League; explain the 1938 Day of Mourning — its organisation by Jack Patten and William Cooper, the choice of 26 January 1938 (150th anniversary of British arrival) as the occasion, the demands made, and its significance as the first national Indigenous rights meeting; explain how these movements established the model of organised political campaigning that later movements built on.
Wider
  • Examine the international influences on early First Nations political organising — including the influence of Marcus Garvey's pan-African movement on Fred Maynard and the AAPA — analysing how global movements for racial equality shaped Australian First Nations activism
  • Investigate William Cooper's 1938 petition to the German consulate protesting Kristallnacht — examining what this reveals about how First Nations activists connected their own struggle to international human rights concerns

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExamining multiple early organisations — AAPA, AAL, Day of Mourning — prevents the reduction of pre-1965 activism to a single event. Building a repertoire of early campaigns shows that organised resistance had a long history before the 1960s Freedom Rides, which is the key correction to the common misconception that rights activism began in the 1960s.
