Challenges to traditional values — Vietnam, feminism, and the Whitlam dismissal

AC9HH10K18 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how Australians in the 1960s and 1970s began to question traditional notions, examining conscription and the Vietnam War, the Women's Liberation Movement, and the Whitlam dismissal as key challenges.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least two of the three challenges disrupted specific traditional values
  • Connects each challenge to a specific value being questioned — not merely describes the event
  • Explains the Whitlam dismissal as a crisis of democratic values — what was contested and why

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Traditional Australian values and their post-war character.

Width

Minimum
Students explain how at least two of the three challenges — Vietnam/conscription, Women's Liberation, Whitlam dismissal — disrupted traditional values, and identify which values each challenged.
Typical
Students examine how the Vietnam War and conscription challenged notions of duty to authority and imperial loyalty; examine how the Women's Liberation Movement challenged gender roles embedded in traditional Australian identity; examine the Whitlam dismissal (1975) as a crisis of democratic values — the Governor-General's use of reserve powers, the constitutional crisis, and the political polarisation it produced; explain how each challenge exposed the tension between the ideal of Australian values and the reality of power, authority, and exclusion.
Wider
  • Examine the Whitlam government's reform agenda as an attempt to remake Australian identity — analysing how its policies on equal pay, Aboriginal land rights, no-fault divorce, and Asian engagement represented a deliberate challenge to the traditional post-war settlement
  • Investigate the 1975 constitutional crisis in depth — examining the competing interpretations of whether the dismissal was constitutionally proper or a subversion of democratic norms, and how this debate continues

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to connect each challenge to a specific value being disrupted — not just describe the events. The scaffold is: what value did conscription challenge? (imperial loyalty, democratic choice over military service) what value did the Women's Liberation Movement challenge? (gendered division of labour embedded in egalitarianism) what value did the Whitlam dismissal challenge? (democratic accountability).
