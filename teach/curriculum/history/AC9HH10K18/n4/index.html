Asia-Pacific engagement — Keating versus Howard

AC9HH10K18 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare the Asia-Pacific engagement policies of the Keating and Howard governments, evaluating how each reflected different approaches to Australian identity and values.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one key feature of each government's approach to Asia-Pacific engagement
  • Explains the values difference each approach reflects — not merely the policy difference
  • Evaluates how each approach understood Australian identity — with evidence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Changing religious landscape.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least one key feature of each government's approach to Asia-Pacific engagement and explain the values difference each approach reflects.
Typical
Students examine Keating's (1991–1996) Asia-Pacific engagement agenda — APEC leadership, Redfern Speech, republican movement, engagement with Asia as Australia's economic and strategic destiny; examine Howard's (1996–2007) approach — stronger re-emphasis on US alliance and 'Anglosphere' ties, more cautious engagement with Asia, the Tampa affair and Pacific Solution as expressions of border sovereignty; evaluate how each approach reflected different understandings of Australian identity — Keating's post-British Asia-facing identity versus Howard's more traditional Anglo-American alignment.
Wider
  • Examine how the Keating–Howard contrast played out in specific policy decisions — comparing their approaches to East Timor, the republic referendum, and APEC to evaluate which approach better served Australia's long-term interests
  • Investigate how Australia's relationship with China has evolved since the end of the Cold War — examining how economic integration and strategic competition have created a distinctive tension in Australian foreign policy

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to understand the Keating–Howard contrast as a genuine values debate, not merely a left-right policy difference. Scaffolding the underlying identity question — what does it mean for Australia to be 'part of Asia' versus 'aligned with the Anglosphere'? — makes the policy comparison analytically meaningful.
