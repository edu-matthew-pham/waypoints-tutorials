Commemoration, Anzac legend and historical interpretations

AC9HH9K12 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the commemoration of the First World War, including different historical interpretations and debates about the nature and significance of the Anzac legend and the war

Prior knowledge

Students can identify that historical interpretations differ and explain why (AC9HH8K06, AC9HH8K11). They have studied the Gallipoli campaign, the Western Front experience, and the effects of the war on Australian society (AC9HH9K09, AC9HH9K11). They have not yet examined what the 'Anzac legend' claims as a set of values and ideals, how commemoration functions as a cultural practice, or how historians have debated and contested the legend's nature and significance over time.

By the end

Students investigate the ideals associated with the Anzac tradition and how and why WW1 is commemorated in Australian society; discuss similarities and differences in historians' views of the Anzac legend over time; identify differences between commemoration and glorification; and evaluate the fairness of post-war treaties on the defeated powers, including the Treaty of Versailles on Germany.

Waypoints

  • 1. The Anzac legend — what it claims and what values it assertsHingeStudents can describe the Anzac legend as a set of specific values and ideals — mateship, courage, larrikinism, egalitarianism, endurance — and explain where these ideals came from and what they claim about Australian national character.
  • 2. Commemoration — how and why WW1 is rememberedStudents can describe how the First World War is commemorated in Australian society and explain the purposes that commemoration serves — including the distinction between commemoration and glorification.
  • 3. Historiographical debate — how historians' views of the Anzac legend have differed over timeStudents can discuss similarities and differences in historians' views of the Anzac legend over time, identifying key interpretive positions and explaining what grounds the debate.
  • 4. The Treaty of Versailles — fairness and consequencesStudents can evaluate the fairness of the Treaty of Versailles on Germany, identifying its main terms and assessing the grounds on which it can be considered just or unjust.
