The Treaty of Versailles — fairness and consequences

AC9HH9K12 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the fairness of the Treaty of Versailles on Germany, identifying its main terms and assessing the grounds on which it can be considered just or unjust.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the main terms of the Treaty of Versailles and describes the German reaction
  • Evaluates the fairness of the treaty using at least two different perspectives and supports the judgement with evidence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Historiographical debate — how historians' views of the Anzac legend have differed over time.

Width

Minimum
Students identify the main terms imposed on Germany by the Treaty of Versailles (war guilt clause, reparations, territorial losses, military restrictions), describe the German reaction to the treaty, and make a judgement about whether the treaty was fair, supported by at least one reason.
Typical
Students describe the 'Big Three' negotiating positions (Wilson's Fourteen Points, Clemenceau's punitive aims, Lloyd George's middle position); the specific terms imposed; the German delegation's exclusion from negotiations and their response (the 'diktat'); the war guilt clause (Article 231) as both legally and morally contested; the scale of reparations and territorial loss; evaluate the treaty using multiple criteria — the Allied perspective (Germany started the war and must pay), the German perspective (the terms were unprecedented and punitive), and a longer-term perspective (did the treaty's severity contribute to the conditions for WW2?); connect to the 'stab-in-the-back' myth from AC9HH9K10.
Wider
  • Compare the Treaty of Versailles with Wilson's Fourteen Points — how much of Wilson's vision was actually implemented, and why did the final treaty diverge from it?
  • Evaluate the claim that the Treaty of Versailles caused WW2 — is this a defensible historical interpretation or an oversimplification? Apply the concept of turning point from AC9HH9K10 to this question

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly judge the Treaty of Versailles as 'unfair' without engaging with the Allied perspective on why it was constructed as it was. Evaluating fairness requires holding multiple perspectives simultaneously — what counts as fair from each party's position — before reaching a reasoned judgement.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExamining the treaty from German, Allied, and longer-term historical perspectives builds the multiperspective understanding the evaluation task requires.
