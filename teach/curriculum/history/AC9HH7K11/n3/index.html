← AC9HH7K11 Beliefs, values and practices

Beliefs, values, and practices as a window on the society

AC9HH7K11 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the beliefs, values, and practices of the ancient society — including their chosen focus area — reveal what the society valued, how it organised itself, and what distinguished it from other ancient societies.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least two beliefs or values revealed through the focus area reflect the society's broader worldview.
  • Identifies at least one distinctive feature of this society's beliefs or practices — something that sets it apart.
  • Moves beyond description to explanation — not just what the society did, but what it reveals about what they valued.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Focus area — everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customs.

Width

Minimum
Explain how at least two beliefs or values revealed in the focus area reflect the society's broader priorities or worldview.
Typical
Explain how the beliefs, values, and practices examined across Nodes 1 and 2 together reveal what the ancient society valued, how it understood itself and the world, and how these beliefs and values shaped its organisation. Identify at least one way in which this society's beliefs or practices were distinctive — different from other ancient societies or from modern perspectives.
Wider
  • Examine how beliefs and values evident in the focus area connect to the society's social structure (K10) and its environmental context (K09) — developing an integrated account of how the society's physical and social worlds reflected and shaped its beliefs.
  • Investigate how the society's beliefs and practices were represented and interpreted differently by different groups within it — what the elite record says vs what archaeological evidence from non-elite contexts reveals.
  • Explore how historians and archaeologists have interpreted and reinterpreted the society's beliefs and practices as new evidence has emerged — examining how historical interpretations of ancient belief systems can change.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates religious beliefs (Node 1) and focus area practices (Node 2) into a synthetic account of what the beliefs, values, and practices reveal about the society as a whole. Students must move from describing specific practices to explaining what they reveal — connecting the particular to the general. This integration move is the descriptor's full demand.
