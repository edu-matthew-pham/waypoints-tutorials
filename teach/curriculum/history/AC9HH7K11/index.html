Beliefs, values and practices

AC9HH7K11 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

key beliefs, values and practices of an ancient society, with a particular emphasis on one of the following areas: everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customs

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the organisation and roles of key groups in the ancient society, including how groups influenced and changed society (AC9HH7K10). They have not yet examined the beliefs, values, and practices that shaped how the society understood the world and organised cultural life — the religious, ideological, and practical dimensions of daily existence.

By the end

Students describe the key beliefs, values, and practices of the ancient society with particular emphasis on one focus area (everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customs), explain how these beliefs and values shaped the society's practices, and use evidence from sources to support their descriptions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • describing the role of the Delphic Oracle in informing decision-making regarding future warfare
  • investigating significant beliefs and values associated with warfare, such as the heroic ideals revealed in the Iliad, and military practices such as army organisation, the hoplite phalanx and naval warfare
  • investigating significant beliefs associated with daily life (for example, the evidence of household religion) and practices (for example, the use of public amenities such as baths, and the forms of entertainment in theatres and amphitheatres)
  • analysing the role of the military in the organisation of Roman society and politics, in both the Republic and the early Empire
  • investigating beliefs associated with death, such as belief in an afterlife, and funerary customs and practices (for example, burial in tombs and techniques of mummification)
  • describing how the prominence of male and female gods changed based on locality and time
  • developing criteria to evaluate the significant beliefs, values and practices of Indian society associated with, for example, the role of the family and religious ceremonies, such as rites of passage for boys and men, and rites of passage for girls and women, and marriage rites
  • formulating questions to analyse how Vedism and later, Brahmanism shaped death and funerary customs
  • identifying how the Mandate of Heaven assisted people in understanding and justifying periods of warfare
  • investigating the significant beliefs, values and practices of Chinese society associated with daily life; for example, irrigation and the practice of agriculture, the teachings of Confucius, the evidence of daily life from the Han tombs

Waypoints

  • 1. Religious and cosmological beliefsHingeStudents can describe the key religious and cosmological beliefs of the ancient society — including its deities, creation stories, or understanding of the afterlife — and explain how these beliefs shaped the society's values and practices.
  • 2. Focus area — everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customsStudents can describe the specific beliefs, values, and practices of the ancient society in their chosen focus area (everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customs), using evidence from sources to support their account.
  • 3. Beliefs, values, and practices as a window on the societyStudents can explain how the beliefs, values, and practices of the ancient society — including their chosen focus area — reveal what the society valued, how it organised itself, and what distinguished it from other ancient societies.
