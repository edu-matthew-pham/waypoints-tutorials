← AC9HH7K11 Beliefs, values and practices

Focus area — everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customs

AC9HH7K11 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the specific beliefs, values, and practices of the ancient society in their chosen focus area (everyday life, warfare, or death and funerary customs), using evidence from sources to support their account.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least three specific practices in the chosen focus area with reference to evidence.
  • Connects at least two practices to beliefs or values that shaped them — not just describes what people did but why.
  • Uses at least two types of evidence (artefact, written source, visual source) to support the description.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Religious and cosmological beliefs.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least three specific practices in the chosen focus area and connect each to a belief or value that shaped it.
Typical
Describe the key practices of the chosen focus area in detail — drawing on multiple types of evidence (artefacts, written sources, visual sources) — and explain how the society's beliefs and values shaped those practices. For everyday life: food, housing, family structures, work, recreation. For warfare: motivations, tactics, weapons, treatment of enemies, warrior culture. For death and funerary customs: burial practices, beliefs about the afterlife, tomb construction, treatment of the dead.
Wider
  • Examine how practices in the focus area varied across different social groups within the society — how did elite and non-elite everyday life differ? How did warfare affect soldiers differently from rulers? How did funerary customs differ by social status?
  • Investigate specific artefacts or sites associated with the focus area in detail — a specific battle account, a tomb complex, an architectural representation of daily life — and examine what they reveal that general descriptions cannot.
  • Explore how practices in the focus area changed over time within the ancient society — identifying what was stable (continuity) and what shifted in response to political, environmental, or social changes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe choice of focus area within this descriptor means students can explore one area in depth — but breadth within that area (across social groups, across evidence types, across time periods within the society) is what produces genuine understanding rather than a flat description. Without breadth across these dimensions, students produce a generalised account that misses the differentiation, change, and complexity that makes the area historically interesting.
