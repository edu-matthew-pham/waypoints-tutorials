Environmental causes of migration

AC9HH7K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how environmental changes — including climate change and desertification — triggered the movement of early humans out of Africa and into other parts of the world.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one specific environmental change (e.g. desertification, climate shift) and explains how it created conditions that triggered migration.
  • Identifies that environmental change caused early humans to move in search of resources — not simply that they 'chose' to migrate.
  • Places early human migration out of Africa in a plausible chronological context (hundreds of thousands of years ago).

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Theories of human evolution.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one major environmental trigger (e.g. climate change, desertification) that caused early humans to migrate out of Africa.
Typical
Describe how a range of environmental changes — climate change, desertification, and shifting resource availability — combined to push and pull early human populations across different regions, including movement toward and eventually into Australia.
Wider
  • Examine specific geographical routes proposed for early human migration out of Africa (e.g. the coastal route, the northern route through the Levant), and how environmental conditions shaped which routes were viable.
  • Investigate how the concept of 'push and pull factors' used in modern migration studies applies to early human migration — connecting deep-time history to contemporary geographic and historical frameworks.
  • Explore how rising and falling sea levels during glacial periods created and closed land bridges, affecting where early humans could and could not travel.

Hinge

Students who cannot connect environmental change to migration as a causal mechanism will treat K02's arrival causes as disconnected facts rather than an extension of the same causal chain — the push-pull logic that explains departure from Africa is the same logic that explains arrival in Sahul.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe causal chain from environmental change → resource pressure → population movement is not self-evident to Year 7 students. They need the reasoning scaffold made explicit: what changed in the environment, how that affected survival, and why movement was a response. Without this scaffold, students list environmental changes without connecting them to migration as a human response.
