Identifying origin, content and context

AC9HH9S03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the origin, content and context of primary and secondary sources, including whether a source was produced at the same time as the event or retrospectively.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the origin, content and context of at least one source with accuracy
  • Distinguishes between whether the source was produced contemporaneously with the event or retrospectively, and explains why this matters

Prior knowledge

In Year 7 students identified origin, content, context and purpose of sources (AC9HH7S03). In Year 8 they continued this (AC9HH8S03). Y9 extends this by adding explanation of context (when/where produced, contemporaneous vs retrospective) and analysis of literal and symbolic features, purpose, and inferences from visual sources.

Width

Minimum
For one source, identify: who created it (origin), what it contains (content), and when and where it was produced (context).
Typical
Identify the context of sources — when and where produced, and whether contemporaneous with the event or retrospective; identify origin (author, organisation, context of creation) and content (what the source contains, both literal and implied); apply this across a range of source types (photographs, diaries, newspapers, government records, secondary accounts).
Wider
  • Compare a contemporaneous source with a retrospective account of the same event — examining how time, distance, and changed context shaped what each source includes and how it frames events
  • Analyse sources from both coloniser and colonised perspectives on the same colonial event — practising origin/content/context analysis across very different source types and social positions
  • Apply origin/content/context analysis to a digital source (e.g. a digitised newspaper from Trove) and reflect on how digitisation affects the source's accessibility and the way it is read

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents frequently conflate content (what the source says) with purpose (why it was created) — explicit modelling of the distinction between these categories is necessary. The contemporaneous/retrospective distinction is new at Y9 and needs direct instruction.
