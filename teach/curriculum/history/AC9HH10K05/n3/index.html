Australia and the founding of the United Nations

AC9HH10K05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe Australia's role in founding the United Nations and explain the significance of this involvement for Australia's post-war international standing.

Success criteria

  • Describes Australia's role in founding the UN, identifying at least one specific Australian contribution
  • Explains the significance of this involvement in terms of Australia's post-war international aspirations
  • Identifies at least one Australian individual and their specific contribution to the UN founding

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: The strategic pivot — ANZUS and the shift from Britain to the USA.

Width

Minimum
Students describe Australia's involvement in founding the UN, identifying at least one specific Australian contribution; explain why this involvement was significant.
Typical
Students examine Australia's role in founding the United Nations — HV Evatt's role as President of the UN General Assembly, Jessie Street's contribution to the charter's human rights language; explain how Australia's wartime experience motivated its engagement with collective security architecture; evaluate the significance of this involvement as evidence of Australia's post-war aspiration to be a middle power with international influence rather than a dependent of great powers.
Wider
  • Examine HV Evatt's role at the UN as a case study in middle-power diplomacy — analysing how a relatively small country can shape international institutions, and what this reveals about Australia's post-war self-conception
  • Investigate Jessie Street's contribution to the UN charter's human rights language — examining how her advocacy intersected with the broader post-war human rights moment driven by the Holocaust

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat Australia's UN founding role as a footnote to great-power history. Framing it as evidence of post-war middle-power aspiration — Australia wanting a rules-based international order as protection for smaller states — gives it analytical weight and connects it to the broader significance theme.
