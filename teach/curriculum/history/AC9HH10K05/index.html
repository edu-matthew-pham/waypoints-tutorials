Post-War significance for Australia

AC9HH10K05 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the significance of the Second World War to Australia's immediate post-war economic, political and social development, and Australia's international relationships in the 20th century

Prior knowledge

Students have established in AC9HH10K01 that the war accelerated Australia's strategic pivot from Britain to the USA and that this was a political choice. From K04 they have examined the home front effects. They understand Australia's pre-war relationship with Britain from WW1 study (AC9HH9K11). They have not yet examined post-war reconstruction, ANZUS, Australia's role in the UN, or the mechanisms of Australia's post-war engagement with Asia.

By the end

Students evaluate the significance of WW2 to Australia's post-war development — economic, political, and social — and to Australia's international relationships, including the shift from Britain to the USA and the deepening engagement with Asia.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the role of Prime Minister Ben Chifley in post-war reconstruction
  • evaluating the significance of the Second World War to Australia’s changing relationship with Britain and the USA, particularly the shift in importance from the one to the other, such as the creation of the ANZUS alliance
  • evaluating the short- and long-term effects of the Second World War on the emergence of Australia’s deeper engagement with Asia, such as the development of SEATO, the South Pacific Forum, APEC, the ASEAN Regional Forum and various trade alliances
  • describing the involvement of Australia in the founding of the United Nations, such as the roles played by HV Evatt and Jessie Street in drafting the charter of the United Nations
  • investigating the impact of the significant wave of post-war European and Asian migration, including the Colombo Plan, and views about race, including attitudes towards First Nations Australians

Waypoints

  • 1. Post-war reconstruction — economic and social agendaStudents can describe the major features of Australia's post-war reconstruction and explain how wartime experience shaped the policy agenda.
  • 2. The strategic pivot — ANZUS and the shift from Britain to the USAHingeStudents can evaluate the significance of WW2 to Australia's changing relationship with Britain and the USA, explaining ANZUS as the institutional expression of the Curtin pivot.
  • 3. Australia and the founding of the United NationsStudents can describe Australia's role in founding the United Nations and explain the significance of this involvement for Australia's post-war international standing.
  • 4. Australia's deepening engagement with AsiaStudents can evaluate the short- and long-term effects of WW2 on Australia's engagement with Asia, explaining the mechanisms through which that engagement was built.
