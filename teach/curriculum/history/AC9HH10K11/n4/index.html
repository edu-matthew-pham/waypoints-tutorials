Extent of change — methods, outcomes, and limits

AC9HH10K11 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the extent to which significant events and methods contributed to change — analysing which methods were most effective, what types of change resulted, and what limits remain.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates the effectiveness of at least two different methods, with evidence
  • Explains why legal or formal change did not automatically produce social change — with a specific example
  • Constructs an overall judgement about the extent of change since 1962, acknowledging both what changed and what limits remain

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Key events — reconciliation and recognition, 1992–present.

Width

Minimum
Students construct an evaluative argument about the extent of change, covering at least two different methods and two different types of change produced.
Typical
Students draw on N1–N3 to evaluate: which methods (direct action, legal challenge, political negotiation, media strategy, international pressure) produced which types of change; why legal change (1967 Referendum, Mabo) did not automatically produce social change; why symbolic change (Apology) did not resolve structural inequality; evaluate the extent of change overall — what has changed since 1962, what structural inequalities persist, and why.
Wider
  • Compare the effectiveness of different methods across the timeline — arguing which approach produced the most durable change and why
  • Evaluate the civil rights movement using the closing-the-gap data — assessing whether the political and legal changes documented in N2–N3 have translated into measurable improvements in health, education, employment, and life expectancy outcomes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the US influence context (N1), the legal-political chronology (N2), and the reconciliation phase (N3) into an overall evaluation of the movement's effectiveness. Tasks requiring students to evaluate across multiple methods and types of change work well here.
