The rise of extremism and the post-9/11 world

AC9HH10K20 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate the rise of political and religious extremism and explain the impact of 9/11 and the 2002 Bali bombings on Australian society and immigration.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two conditions that contributed to the rise of political or religious extremism
  • Explains the impact of the 2002 Bali bombings on Australians — distinguishing the event from its political and social consequences
  • Explains how 9/11 and Bali affected Australian immigration policy and social attitudes toward Muslim Australians

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Global rights movements — interpretations and debates.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the causes of the rise of political or religious extremism; explain the impact of 9/11 and the Bali bombings on Australian immigration policy and social attitudes.
Typical
Students examine the conditions that produced the rise of political and religious extremism in the late 20th century — globalisation's discontents, political marginalisation, ideological vacuum, the role of media; examine the 9/11 attacks and their global consequences; examine the 2002 Bali bombings as the attack that brought terrorism directly home for Australians (202 dead, 88 Australians); examine the effects on Australian immigration — increased scrutiny of Muslim migrants, changes to visa and surveillance procedures, the securitisation of migration; examine the domestic effects on Muslim Australians — discrimination, community surveillance, the pressure to demonstrate loyalty.
Wider
  • Examine how the post-9/11 securitisation of migration intersected with Australia's existing asylum seeker debate — analysing whether terrorism concerns provided a new rationale for restrictions that already existed
  • Investigate the debate over counter-terrorism legislation and civil liberties — examining how Australia's post-9/11 laws balanced security imperatives against rights protections, and whether the balance was appropriate

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to understand the rise of extremism as a product of specific conditions — not as inexplicable evil or as a clash of civilisations. Scaffolding the structural causes alongside the specific events prevents both the 'evil actors' framing and the cultural essentialism that blocks genuine historical analysis.
