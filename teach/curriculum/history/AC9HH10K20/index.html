Historical interpretations and debates

AC9HH10K20 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

different historical interpretations and debates during the second half of the 20th century

Prior knowledge

Students have examined historical interpretation and debate throughout the unit — in commemoration (K06), civil rights (K10–K11), migration (K08), and environmental movements (K14, K17). They have encountered the concept of historical significance as contested. They have not yet examined these interpretive debates as a connected set, or examined the global movements for rights, the rise of extremism, nuclear energy, and climate change debates as distinct historiographical topics.

By the end

Students discuss different historical interpretations and debates during the second half of the 20th century, including global rights movements, extremism, nuclear energy, and climate change.

Waypoints

  • 1. Historical interpretation as a practice — how and why interpretations differHingeStudents can explain why historical interpretations differ — identifying the sources of interpretive disagreement — and apply this to at least one case from the unit.
  • 2. Global rights movements — interpretations and debatesStudents can discuss significant global rights movements and the interpretive debates surrounding them, including First Nations rights in Australia, the US civil rights movement, and the South African anti-apartheid movement.
  • 3. The rise of extremism and the post-9/11 worldStudents can investigate the rise of political and religious extremism and explain the impact of 9/11 and the 2002 Bali bombings on Australian society and immigration.
  • 4. Nuclear energy and climate change — evolving debatesStudents can discuss the global debate over nuclear energy and the evolution of the climate change debate, examining how each debate has changed over time from the 1960s to the present.
