← AC9HH8K08 Turning points in empire and expansion

Effects on continuity and change

AC9HH8K08 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the effects of the turning point on the empire or expansion — describing what changed and what continued — for different groups including rulers, subject peoples and affected societies.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least one change and one continuity resulting from the turning point, with reference to specific evidence
  • Describes how the turning point affected at least two different groups differently

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Causes and features.

Width

Minimum
At least two effects (one change and one continuity) with reference to at least one affected group.
Typical
Multiple effects across social, economic, religious and political domains; different effects for different groups; longer-term consequences. Examples: Mongol expansion effects — connections between Europe and Asia, paper money, European merchant travel to China; Spanish conquest effects — spread of disease, introduction of American crops to Europe, the Encomienda system, deforestation, longer-term inequality and cultural displacement of Indigenous populations.
Wider
  • For the Spanish conquest: analyse longer-term effects on Indigenous populations — unequal land distribution, slavery, political inequality, supremacy of Spanish culture and language — to show how a medieval turning point produced modern inequalities
  • For the Vikings: examine the decline of Viking power (Battle of Standford Bridge, treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte, changing climate, spread of Christianity) as a multi-causal chain of continuity and change
  • For the Mongols: analyse the effects of Mongol expansion including life in China before, during and after conquest to show how the same turning point produced different continuities and changes in different places

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEffects of empire and expansion are always unequal — what changed for the conquerors often differed dramatically from what changed for the conquered. Using multiple group perspectives (rulers, subject peoples, distant affected societies) builds a fuller analysis.
