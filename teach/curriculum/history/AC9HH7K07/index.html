Cultural preservation and heritage

AC9HH7K07 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the cultural obligations of First Nations Australians about significant heritage sites, including ancestral remains, material culture and artefacts, and the role of collaboration between First Nations Australians and other individuals and groups to ensure cultural preservation

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the social organisation and cultural practices of early First Nations Australians, including funerary customs, and explain how cultural knowledge has been transmitted through oral traditions and expressive arts (AC9HH7K06). They have not yet examined cultural obligations about heritage sites specifically, or the role of collaboration and institutional frameworks in cultural preservation.

By the end

Students explain the cultural obligations of First Nations Australians regarding significant heritage sites, ancestral remains, and material culture; describe how the Lake Mungo case illustrates issues of ownership, cultural protocols, and repatriation; and explain the role of collaboration between Traditional Owners, researchers, and institutions in cultural preservation, including world heritage frameworks.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising how the human remains of early First Nations Australians are the ancestors of contemporary First Nations Peoples of Australia and, as such, any unauthorised disturbance is taken as a serious breach of cultural protocol
  • exploring how the ancestral remains found at Lake Mungo (Willandra Lakes region) illustrate the issues of ownership, cultural protocols, research and repatriation
  • examining the importance of consultation with multiple stakeholders when conserving culture and heritage sites; for example, the collaboration between the Traditional Owners and researchers
  • investigating world heritage criteria for the listing of significant sites such as the Budj Bim and Willandra Lakes regions, and cultural landscapes such as Uluru-Kata Tjuta and Kakadu
  • examining the role of national and state/territory galleries, libraries, archives, museums, historical societies and field-sites in curating, conserving and showcasing First Nations Australians' histories and cultures

Waypoints

  • 1. Cultural obligations and ancestral remainsHingeStudents can explain what cultural obligations First Nations Australians hold regarding significant heritage sites and ancestral remains, and describe why the disturbance of ancestral remains is a serious breach of cultural protocol.
  • 2. Lake Mungo — ownership, repatriation, and collaborationStudents can describe how the Lake Mungo case illustrates the issues of ownership, cultural protocols, research, and repatriation that arise when ancestral remains are discovered, and explain the collaborative processes involved in resolving these issues.
  • 3. Collaboration and institutional roles in preservationStudents can explain the roles of multiple stakeholders — Traditional Owners, researchers, government bodies, and institutions — in cultural preservation, and describe how collaboration and world heritage frameworks support the protection of significant sites.
