Corroboration and question-dependent usefulness

AC9HH10S04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can determine the usefulness of sources by corroborating with other sources and explain how the usefulness and reliability of a source depends on the questions asked of it.

Success criteria

  • Corroborate information from at least two sources and explain what the comparison reveals
  • Explain how the usefulness of a specific source changes depending on which question it is asked to answer
  • Identify at least one inconsistency between sources and explain what it suggests about the reliability of one or both

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Bias, intent, and absent perspectives.

Width

Minimum
Corroborate information from at least two sources and explain how the usefulness of each changes depending on the question asked.
Typical
Determine usefulness by corroborating with other sources of information — verifying or questioning claims by triangulating across multiple sources; examine that the reliability and usefulness of a source depends on the questions asked of it — for example, an account with a particular historical perspective can be useful for revealing past prevailing attitudes even if unreliable as a factual record; determine whether information in one source can be verified by information in another; compare a range of sources and identify similarities, differences and inconsistencies in interpretations.
Wider
  • Apply corroboration across sources that present contradictory accounts of the same event — examining how historians navigate evidentiary conflict and what it means when sources cannot be reconciled
  • Apply question-dependent usefulness analysis to a propaganda source — examining how its reliability as a factual record is low but its usefulness as evidence of the attitudes of its producers or audience is high

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCorroboration and question-dependent usefulness bring together origin/context analysis (S03) and bias/perspective analysis (Node 1) into a fully integrated source evaluation framework. This is where the individual analytical skills combine into professional-level historical source work.
