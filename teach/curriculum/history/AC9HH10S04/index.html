Usefulness and reliability of sources

AC9HH10S04 · Year 10 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explain the usefulness of primary and secondary sources, and the reliability of the information as evidence

Prior knowledge

Students can explain the usefulness and reliability of primary and secondary sources as evidence in a Year 9 historical inquiry, identifying bias and perspective.

By the end

Students explain the usefulness and reliability of primary and secondary sources as evidence, identifying absent perspectives, explaining bias and intent, corroborating across sources, and comparing sources for inconsistencies.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying perspectives absent from sources and the extent to which this may affect usefulness
  • explaining the bias of the author and the intent of a source, and the extent to which this may affect reliability
  • determining the usefulness of sources by corroborating with other sources of information
  • examining that the reliability and usefulness of a source depends on the questions asked of it; for example, an account having a particular historical perspective and therefore being of use in revealing past prevailing attitudes
  • determining whether or not the information in one historical source can be verified by information in another historical source
  • comparing a range of historical sources and identifying similarities, differences and inconsistencies in interpretations

Waypoints

  • 1. Bias, intent, and absent perspectivesHingeStudents can identify perspectives absent from sources, explain the bias of an author and the intent of a source, and evaluate how each affects usefulness and reliability.
  • 2. Corroboration and question-dependent usefulnessStudents can determine the usefulness of sources by corroborating with other sources and explain how the usefulness and reliability of a source depends on the questions asked of it.
