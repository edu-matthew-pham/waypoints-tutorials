Locating sources in databases

AC9HH10S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can locate relevant and useful historical sources from databases such as Trove and state libraries, using effective search strategies.

Success criteria

  • Locate at least two relevant sources using a specific database
  • Explain the search strategy used and why it was appropriate for the inquiry question
  • Evaluate the relevance of each source found against the inquiry question

Prior knowledge

Students can locate, identify and compare primary and secondary sources for historical inquiry, selecting sources relevant to a Year 9-level investigation.

Width

Minimum
Locate at least two relevant sources using a specific database (e.g. Trove or a state library) and explain why each is relevant to the inquiry question.
Typical
Select relevant and useful historical sources from data bases such as Trove and state libraries; develop effective search strategies for locating sources (keyword selection, date filtering, source type filtering); evaluate the relevance of sources found against the inquiry question.
Wider
  • Locate and compare sources across multiple platforms and formats (digitised archives, physical library collections, oral history repositories, museum databases), evaluating how platform and format affect what can be found
  • Practise source location for a topic with sparse records — such as local First Nations history or women's history before the 20th century — developing strategies for working with incomplete evidentiary records

Hinge

Students who cannot effectively locate sources cannot begin the comparison and selection work of Node 2 or the analysis work of S03 and S04. Database search skills are the entry condition for independent historical inquiry.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDatabase searching requires practice with specific platforms. Model the search process in Trove — including how to filter by source type, date, and format — and give students structured practice with a defined inquiry question before expecting independent location.
