Establishing historical significance

AC9HH8K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what makes an individual or group historically significant — using criteria such as influence, scale of impact and legacy — and apply these criteria to identify the chosen subject as significant.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two criteria of historical significance and applies them to the chosen individual or group
  • Explains why the chosen subject qualifies as historically significant rather than merely notable

Prior knowledge

Students explain the role and achievements of a significant individual in an ancient society (AC9HH7K13). They understand how to investigate an individual's significance using evidence. They have not yet applied this to a medieval or early modern European context — including the institutional role of the Catholic Church, Renaissance figures, or individuals who shaped political and religious reform.

Width

Minimum
Application of at least two criteria of significance to the chosen individual or group.
Typical
Multiple criteria of significance (influence on contemporaries, scale of impact, legacy for later periods) applied to the chosen subject; initial identification of what the individual or group achieved and why it mattered.
Wider
  • Compare the significance of two candidates (e.g. Galileo vs Martin Luther) using the same criteria to develop a defensible judgement about which was more historically significant
  • Examine how historical significance is constructed — why some individuals are remembered and others forgotten — using the Church's influence in determining which figures were celebrated or condemned

Hinge

Without a working understanding of historical significance as a concept with criteria, students cannot distinguish between an individual being interesting and being historically significant. All subsequent analysis of role and achievement depends on this frame.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often assume that famous = significant. Scaffold the reasoning explicitly: significance is about influence, scale, duration and legacy — not celebrity. Apply the criteria to the chosen subject step by step.
