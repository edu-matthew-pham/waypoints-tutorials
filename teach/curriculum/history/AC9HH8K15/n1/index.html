Role and context

AC9HH8K15 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the role of the chosen individual or group in their Asia-Pacific society and explain the conditions that enabled their significance.

Success criteria

  • Describes the role of the chosen individual or group in their Asia-Pacific society
  • Identifies at least one condition that enabled their significance

Prior knowledge

Students explain the role and achievements of significant individuals in ancient and imperial contexts (AC9HH7K13, AC9HH8K05, AC9HH8K10). They understand the social and cultural features of the chosen Asia-Pacific society (AC9HH8K12). They have not yet applied significance analysis to individuals specifically connected to Asia-Pacific societies — Khmer rulers, Japanese shoguns, or Polynesian groups.

Width

Minimum
A description of the chosen individual or group's role with reference to the historical conditions that enabled it.
Typical
The chosen individual or group situated within their society's context. Examples: Jayavarman VII — his rule, expansion of the Khmer Empire, expulsion of the Chams, and the scale of the Angkor construction program; Tokugawa Ieyasu as founder of the Edo Shogunate — the conditions that enabled his consolidation of power; Polynesian groups (Māori, Samoan, Tahitian, Tongan, Rapa Nui) and their achievements in settlement, navigation and cultural development.
Wider
  • For the Khmer: examine Jayavarman VII's rule as both military leader (expelling the Chams) and builder (Angkor's construction program) — and consider whether these two roles were connected or in tension
  • For Polynesia: compare the achievements of different Polynesian groups across navigation, settlement and cultural development to understand how similar social structures and technologies enabled diverse achievements across the Pacific

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to contextualise the individual or group's role before assessing their achievements. Scaffold the question: what conditions made this person's or group's actions possible, and why were they positioned to act as they did?
