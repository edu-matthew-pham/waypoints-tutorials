The Gallipoli campaign — context, experience and significance

AC9HH9K09 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the Gallipoli campaign — its objectives, what happened, and the experience of ANZAC soldiers — and explain why it holds particular significance in Australian national memory.

Success criteria

  • Describes the strategic objective of the Gallipoli campaign and what happened
  • Describes the conditions experienced by ANZAC soldiers at Gallipoli

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Geography of WW1 theatres — where Australians fought.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the strategic objective of the Gallipoli landings, describe the conditions experienced by ANZAC soldiers (cliff terrain, entrenched positions, heat, disease, supply difficulties), and note the eventual evacuation and its significance.
Typical
Students describe the April 25 landing and its immediate difficulties; the eight-month stalemate at Anzac Cove; key events (the August Offensive, Battle of Lone Pine, Chunuk Bair); the evacuation; the high casualty rates relative to tactical gain; explain why Gallipoli — a military failure — became a foundational national story.
Wider
  • Examine the experience from the Ottoman Turkish perspective using sources such as Atatürk's 1934 statement about the ANZACs — how has Turkey's own commemoration of Gallipoli evolved?
  • Investigate specific personal accounts — letters, diaries, photographs — of Gallipoli to ground the general experience in individual voices, applying source analysis skills (AC9HH9S03)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly have prior cultural knowledge of Gallipoli (ANZAC Day, family stories, media) that is often heroic and oversimplified. Examining it as a specific military operation with a specific experience — rather than purely as national myth — prepares the ground for the commemoration debates in AC9HH9K12.
