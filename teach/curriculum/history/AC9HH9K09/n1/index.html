Geography of WW1 theatres — where Australians fought

AC9HH9K09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the major theatres where Australians fought and locate key battlefields on a map — Gallipoli, the Western Front (Somme, Pozières, Passchendaele, Fromelles, Bullecourt, Villers-Bretonneux) and the Middle East (Sinai, Gaza, Beersheba).

Success criteria

  • Identifies and locates the three major theatres on a map
  • Names at least two specific battlefields in each theatre

Prior knowledge

Students can describe historical turning points and examine the experiences and perspectives of different groups in earlier historical periods (AC9HH8K03, AC9HH8K04). They have not yet examined the specific geography of WW1 theatres, the nature of industrialised warfare (trench warfare, military technology), or the contrast between experiences on the Western Front and in the Middle East.

Width

Minimum
Students name and locate the three major theatres (Gallipoli, Western Front, Middle East) and identify at least two named battlefields in each.
Typical
Students locate all named battlefields on a map of Europe and the Middle East; describe the approximate dates and sequence of Australian involvement in each theatre; explain why the Western Front was the dominant theatre for Australian forces after 1915; connect theatre geography to strategic objectives.
Wider
  • Investigate the strategic logic of the Gallipoli campaign — the Allied plan to knock Ottoman Turkey out of the war and open a supply route to Russia — to understand why Australia was fighting in the Dardanelles rather than France
  • Map the movement of the ANZAC forces through the Sinai and Palestine campaign chronologically, tracing the shift from defensive to offensive operations

Hinge

Geographic grounding is the prerequisite for all subsequent nodes. Students who cannot locate the theatres and battlefields cannot make sense of the conditions and experiences described in N2–N3 — 'trench warfare' only makes sense in the Western Front context, and the Light Horse experience only makes sense in the Palestinian desert context. Theatre geography sets up the comparison in N4.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly conflate all WW1 combat with Gallipoli or treat the Western Front as a single undifferentiated place. Explicit map work — locating theatres and individual battlefields — builds the spatial scaffold the comparison node (N4) requires.
