Deep time as a historical concept

AC9HH7K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what 'deep time' means in a historical context, describe the Before Present (BP) dating system, and place First Nations Australians' cultural history on a deep-time scale.

Success criteria

  • Explains what 'deep time' means as a historical concept and why it requires different chronological frameworks.
  • Describes the BP (Before Present) dating system and can correctly interpret a BP date (e.g. 30,000 BP = 30,000 years before 1950).
  • Correctly places First Nations Australians' cultural history in a deep-time context, using a scale that conveys the meaning of 65,000+ years.

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the arrival of early First Nations Australians on the continent, identify evidence for migration routes, and describe effects of that arrival including the establishment of diverse cultures (AC9HH7K02). They have not yet examined the evidence for cultural continuity over deep time, nor the concept of 'deep time' as a historical framework.

Width

Minimum
Explain that 'deep time' refers to the vast timescales of human prehistory (tens of thousands of years) and describe the BP (Before Present) dating system as the convention used by archaeologists for deep-time events.
Typical
Explain the concept of deep time and why it requires different chronological conventions (BP vs BCE/CE), describe how the BP system works, and place First Nations Australians' cultural continuity in context as a history spanning at least 65,000 years — longer than any other documented cultural continuity on Earth.
Wider
  • Compare the deep-time timeline of First Nations Australians' cultural continuity with other ancient civilisations (e.g. ancient Egypt at ~5,000 years, Mesopotamia at ~6,000 years) to establish the significance of 65,000+ years of continuous culture.
  • Explore how the Pleistocene and Holocene epochs used by geologists and archaeologists map onto First Nations Australians' cultural history, and how geological time and cultural time intersect.
  • Investigate the dating conventions used across different disciplines — BP for archaeology, BCE/CE for written history — and discuss why different conventions serve different purposes.

Hinge

Students who cannot correctly use BP dating and place events on a deep-time scale cannot anchor continuity or change evidence in N2 and N3 chronologically — claims about what persisted or changed across 65,000 years become unanchored without the convention to express and compare those timescales.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsChronological reasoning across orders of magnitude (65,000 years vs 200 years vs 6,000 years) is a precision skill that students commonly mishandle. The BP system, its relationship to BCE/CE, and the capacity to place events on a deep-time scale all require fluency through practice. This is a procedural-conceptual threshold before students can engage meaningfully with evidence of continuity and change.
