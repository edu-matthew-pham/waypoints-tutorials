Continuity over deep time

AC9HH7K03 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how First Nations Australians are the world's oldest continuing cultures, displaying evidence of both continuity and change over deep time

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the arrival of early First Nations Australians on the continent, identify evidence for migration routes, and describe effects of that arrival including the establishment of diverse cultures (AC9HH7K02). They have not yet examined the evidence for cultural continuity over deep time, nor the concept of 'deep time' as a historical framework.

By the end

Students explain how First Nations Australians represent the world's oldest continuing cultures, identify and describe specific evidence of both continuity and change across deep time, and explain what 'deep time' means as a historical concept and how it is investigated using methods such as the Before Present (BP) dating system and archaeological sites.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating dating methods used by archaeologists for the study of early First Nations Australians; for example, the use of the 'Before Present' (BP) dating system
  • exploring how we know about continuities and changes in First Nations Australians' cultures; for example, the archaeological research showing evidence of occupation and the lives of First Nations Australians during the Pleistocene epoch at sites such as Lake Mungo and Kutikina Cave
  • exploring evidence of continuities and changes in the ancient period at specific sites; for example, dating the creation of pictographs and the carving of petroglyphs at sites such as the Dampier Archipelago, Grampians National Park, Kakadu National Park, Koonalda Cave, Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park, Preminghana, and the West Kimberley
  • exploring evidence of continuing culture that was evident in the ancient period, such as the use of ochre
  • examining First Nations Australians' views on creation and changes in the landscape that have been passed down through oral tradition, such as the existence of megafauna, changes from wetter to drier climates and changes in vegetation in central Australia

Waypoints

  • 1. Deep time as a historical conceptHingeStudents can explain what 'deep time' means in a historical context, describe the Before Present (BP) dating system, and place First Nations Australians' cultural history on a deep-time scale.
  • 2. Evidence of continuityStudents can identify and describe specific archaeological and cultural evidence that demonstrates the continuity of First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time, including evidence from key sites.
  • 3. Evidence of change over deep timeStudents can identify and describe evidence of change in First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time, and explain that the concept of 'oldest continuing culture' encompasses both continuity and change — not a static or unchanging society.
