Evidence of change over deep time

AC9HH7K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe evidence of change in First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time, and explain that the concept of 'oldest continuing culture' encompasses both continuity and change — not a static or unchanging society.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one specific example of change in First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time with reference to evidence.
  • Explains that continuity and change coexist — 'oldest continuing culture' does not mean unchanging or static.
  • Does not treat change as evidence against continuity — correctly understands continuity as cultural persistence, not cultural stasis.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Evidence of continuity.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one example of change in First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time (e.g. changes in technology, responses to environmental change, shifts in cultural practice).
Typical
Describe multiple examples of change across deep time — environmental responses, technological development, shifts in settlement patterns — and explain how these changes coexist with cultural continuity, challenging the misconception that 'oldest continuing culture' means 'unchanging culture'.
Wider
  • Examine specific documented changes: the development and refinement of stone tool technologies, changes in population distribution in response to aridification, and adaptations to rising sea levels — connecting each change to its environmental or social context.
  • Explore how First Nations Australians' oral traditions capture and transmit knowledge of change — such as stories of sea-level rise, the existence of megafauna, and changes in landscape — as evidence that change was recognised, documented, and culturally processed.
  • Investigate how change within continuity is a distinctive feature of First Nations Australians' cultures compared to ancient civilisations that were disrupted, conquered, or ended — and what this comparison reveals about resilience and adaptation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepA persistent misconception is that 'oldest continuing culture' implies a culture frozen in time. Multiple examples of change — technological, environmental, social — across different periods and regions specifically dislodges this. Breadth of examples (different types of change, different periods, different regions) is what resolves the misconception, not deeper analysis of a single change.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the continuity evidence (Node 2) with change evidence into the descriptor's full demand — that First Nations Australians' cultures display evidence of BOTH continuity and change. Students must hold both together: same cultures persisting, adapting, innovating across 65,000+ years.
