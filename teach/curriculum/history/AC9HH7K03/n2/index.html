Evidence of continuity

AC9HH7K03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe specific archaeological and cultural evidence that demonstrates the continuity of First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time, including evidence from key sites.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two specific pieces of evidence from different sites or evidence types that demonstrate cultural continuity over deep time.
  • Explains what each piece of evidence demonstrates about continuity — not just lists evidence but connects it to the claim.
  • Uses at least one specific site name and places it geographically and chronologically.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Deep time as a historical concept.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one specific piece of evidence — from an archaeological site or cultural practice — that demonstrates the continuity of First Nations Australians' cultures over deep time (e.g. ochre use, rock art at Kakadu, occupation at Lake Mungo).
Typical
Describe multiple forms of evidence for cultural continuity — archaeological evidence from sites such as Lake Mungo, Kutikina Cave, and rock art sites (Dampier Archipelago, Kakadu, Grampians, Uluru-Kata Tjuta), and cultural continuity such as ongoing use of ochre — explaining what each piece of evidence demonstrates about continuity.
Wider
  • Investigate specific rock art sites in detail — such as the Bradshaw/Gwion Gwion paintings, petroglyphs at the Dampier Archipelago, or ochre paintings at Koonalda Cave — examining the dating methods used and what the art reveals about cultural continuity.
  • Explore how oral traditions have preserved knowledge of environmental events (megafauna, sea-level changes) over tens of thousands of years, and what this reveals about the cultural mechanisms of continuity.
  • Examine the archaeological evidence at Lake Mungo and Kutikina Cave in detail — what was found, how it was dated, and what it tells us about daily life, cultural practice, and continuity across the Pleistocene epoch.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly treat continuity as an abstract claim rather than something demonstrated by specific, varied evidence. Exposure to multiple evidence types and sites — rock art, ochre use, artefacts, oral traditions — each showing continuity in different ways, helps students build a genuine understanding that cultural continuity is evidenced, not merely asserted. Single-site focus risks students treating continuity as a local rather than continent-wide phenomenon.
