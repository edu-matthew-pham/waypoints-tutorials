Difficulty of identifying origin and purpose

AC9HH8S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can discuss the difficulty of identifying the origin and purpose of some sources, and explain how this uncertainty affects the source's usefulness in a historical inquiry.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one source where origin or purpose is genuinely uncertain and explains why
  • Explains how this uncertainty affects what the source can and cannot be used to argue in a historical inquiry

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Using internal clues to infer provenance.

Width

Minimum
At least one example of a source where origin or purpose is difficult to determine, with an explanation of how this affects its usefulness.
Typical
Cases where origin and purpose are uncertain or contested: anonymous manuscripts, oral traditions committed to writing long after the events they describe, artefacts without context, sites of unknown function. The specific case of Icelandic sagas — oral traditions later written down — as sources whose origin is ambiguous and whose purpose blends history and literature.
Wider
  • Examine how the difficulty of identifying the origin and purpose of Polynesian oral traditions affects their use as historical evidence — and how historians address this by cross-referencing with archaeological evidence
  • Discuss the monastic scribes' preservation of ancient texts — medieval manuscripts of Greek, Roman and Arab literary works — as sources whose purpose (preservation vs commentary vs distortion) affects how they should be used

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the four-question framework (Node 1) and provenance inference (Node 2) into a higher-order analysis: what happens when those tools cannot resolve uncertainty? Invest in helping students see incomplete provenance as a feature of historical work, not just a gap.
