Nature and causes of European contact — trade, competition and control

AC9HH9K20 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how European contact with the chosen Asian society developed — from trade to colonisation — and explain the European motivations driving increasing engagement.

Success criteria

  • Describes how European contact with the chosen society developed over time
  • Identifies at least two European motivations for engagement and explains how each drove increasing contact

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the key features of the chosen Asian society (AC9HH9K19) and describe the effects of European imperial expansion (AC9HH9K01). They have not yet examined how European contact specifically transformed the chosen Asian society — what changed, what persisted, and what the society's internal dynamics brought to the encounter.

Width

Minimum
Students describe how European contact developed over time (from trade to greater control) and identify at least two motivations driving European engagement.
Typical
Students describe the development of contact — the East India Companies as commercial and then political actors; European competition for trade access (British, Dutch, French, Portuguese); the transformation from commercial relationship to territorial control; explain European motivations — access to goods (spices, silk, tea, opium), strategic positioning, missionary activity, Enlightenment curiosity; describe the specific mechanism of increasing control in the chosen context (opium trade in China, Company rule in India, Dutch VOC in Indonesia).
Wider
  • Examine the opium trade as a case study in how commercial exploitation could be backed by military force — and what this reveals about the relationship between capitalism and imperialism
  • Compare the mechanisms of European control in two different Asian societies — examining why different societies experienced different types and intensities of European intervention

Hinge

Understanding how contact developed from trade to control is the prerequisite for understanding both the effects (N2) and continuity/change analysis (N3). Students who see European domination as sudden cannot explain the differential experiences of Asian societies or the varied responses to contact.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat European colonisation as a sudden imposition rather than as a gradual process with commercial origins. Making the developmental trajectory explicit — trade → commercial dominance → political control — is the scaffold for understanding how the process unfolded and how it could be resisted at different stages.
