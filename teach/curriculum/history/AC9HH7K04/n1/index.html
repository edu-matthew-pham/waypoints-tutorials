Environmental changes — sea level and aridification

AC9HH7K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the major environmental changes that affected the Australian continent over deep time — including rising sea levels between 18,000 and 7,000 years ago and the aridification of landscapes such as the Willandra Lakes region — and explain their geographical effects.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two specific environmental changes that affected the continent over deep time (e.g. rising sea levels, aridification).
  • Identifies at least one concrete geographical consequence of each change (e.g. loss of land bridge to Tasmania, drying of Lake Mungo).
  • Places these changes in a broad chronological context (Pleistocene/Holocene, or thousands to tens of thousands of years ago).

Prior knowledge

Students can explain that First Nations Australians' cultures display both continuity and change over deep time, and describe evidence of cultural adaptation including responses to environmental change (AC9HH7K03). They have not yet examined specific environmental processes in detail — rising sea levels, aridification, megafauna — or how particular communities responded to each.

Width

Minimum
Describe rising sea levels as a major environmental change that affected the continent, including at least one specific geographical effect (e.g. loss of the land bridge to Tasmania, formation of the Torres Strait Islands).
Typical
Describe both rising sea levels and aridification as significant environmental processes, explaining specific geographical consequences: the loss of the land bridge to Tasmania, the formation of the Torres Strait Islands, the drying of Lake Mungo and the Willandra Lakes region, and how these changes reshaped the continent's geography and ecology.
Wider
  • Map the changes to the coastline and land area of Australia between the Last Glacial Maximum (~20,000 BP) and the present, examining what land was lost and how that affected settlement.
  • Explore the estimated scale of sea-level rise (over 120 metres since the Last Glacial Maximum) and what that means for the coastlines and communities that existed in now-submerged areas.
  • Investigate the Holocene epoch as a period of dramatic environmental change following the Pleistocene, and how the transition shaped the continent that contemporary Australians inhabit.

Hinge

Students who cannot connect geological-scale environmental changes to their geographic and human consequences lack the analytical foundation for N3's adaptive responses — they will list adaptive strategies without grounding them in the specific challenges those strategies were responding to.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents struggle to connect geological-scale environmental change to its human consequences. The reasoning chain — sea levels rose → land bridges submerged → communities separated or displaced — requires explicit scaffolding. Without it, students list environmental changes without understanding how they produced specific social and demographic effects.
