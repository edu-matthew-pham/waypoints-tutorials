Effects of arrival

AC9HH7K02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the effects of early First Nations Australians' arrival on the continent, including the establishment of cultures across diverse environments and the beginnings of the world's oldest continuing civilisations.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one concrete effect of early First Nations Australians' arrival and dispersal across the continent.
  • Connects the diversity of environments across the continent to the development of culturally diverse societies.
  • Does not treat the arrival as a single event at a single location — recognises it as an extended process of dispersal across a large continent.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Migration routes across the continent.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one effect of the arrival of early First Nations Australians on the continent — for example, the establishment of diverse cultures adapted to different environments across Sahul/Australia.
Typical
Describe multiple effects of the arrival and dispersal of early First Nations Australians: the development of culturally and environmentally diverse societies across the continent, the beginnings of cultural practices that have continued for tens of thousands of years, and the relationship between geographical dispersal and cultural diversity.
Wider
  • Examine how early First Nations Australians' dispersal into diverse environments — coastal, desert, tropical, temperate — led to the development of distinct languages, technologies, and cultural practices adapted to each environment.
  • Investigate how the arrival and dispersal of early peoples may have intersected with the megafauna extinction debate — examining the evidence and the competing interpretations.
  • Explore how the effects of early arrival are still visible in contemporary First Nations cultures — in language diversity, in kinship systems, and in the oral traditions that preserve knowledge of ancient events.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the causes of arrival (seafaring, environmental context from K01–K02) with what that arrival produced. Students must coordinate the geographical, temporal, and cultural dimensions of the arrival story — connecting where people came from, how they got here, where they dispersed, and what they built — to reach the descriptor's full demand.
