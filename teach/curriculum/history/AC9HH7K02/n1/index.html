Seafaring and arrival — causes

AC9HH7K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the causes of early First Nations Australians' arrival on the continent, including that their ancestors were seafarers who crossed water to reach Sahul, and identify the geographical context of that crossing.

Success criteria

  • Identifies that early First Nations Australians' ancestors were seafarers who crossed a water gap to reach Sahul.
  • Names Sahul as the mega-continent including present-day Australia and describes its geographical extent during periods of lower sea level.
  • Describes the arrival as deliberate and skilled, not accidental drift.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify competing theories of early human evolution and migration, describe how environmental changes drove movement out of Africa, and identify how different evidence types inform interpretations (AC9HH7K01). They have not yet studied the specific causes, routes, or effects of the arrival of early peoples on the Australian continent.

Width

Minimum
Describe that the ancestors of early First Nations Australians were seafarers who crossed a water gap to reach the mega-continent known as Sahul, and that this was a deliberate and skilled migration.
Typical
Describe the geographical context of the arrival — the mega-continent Sahul, the water gaps between island-hopping points, and the seafaring capability required — and explain why this migration is significant as one of the earliest long-distance ocean crossings in human history.
Wider
  • Investigate the estimated timeframe of arrival (at least 65,000 years ago) and examine what evidence — radiocarbon dating, TL dating, genetic research — underpins these estimates.
  • Explore the geographical relationship between Sunda (Southeast Asia landmass during lower sea levels) and Sahul, and how changing sea levels during glacial periods affected the size and location of the water gap.
  • Examine how the concept of 'seafaring' challenges older assumptions about early human capability, and why this discovery changed historical and archaeological interpretations of early migration.

Hinge

Students who cannot reason from sea-level context → land configuration of Sahul/Sunda → remaining water gap → deliberate water crossing lack the geographic foundation for N2's route evidence and N3's effects discussion — both depend on understanding the physical reality of the arrival.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often assume early migration was overland or accidental. The reasoning chain — sea levels during glacial period → land configuration of Sahul/Sunda → remaining water gap → deliberate boat crossing — needs to be made explicit, otherwise students cannot connect the geographical evidence to the seafaring conclusion.
