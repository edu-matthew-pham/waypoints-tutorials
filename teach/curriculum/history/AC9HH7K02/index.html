Arrival on the Australian continent

AC9HH7K02 · Year 7 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

theories about the causes and effects of the arrival of early First Nations Australians on the Australian continent and their migration routes across the continent

Prior knowledge

Students can identify competing theories of early human evolution and migration, describe how environmental changes drove movement out of Africa, and identify how different evidence types inform interpretations (AC9HH7K01). They have not yet studied the specific causes, routes, or effects of the arrival of early peoples on the Australian continent.

By the end

Students identify and describe theories about why and how early First Nations Australians arrived on the Australian continent, explain likely migration routes across the continent using geographical and chronological evidence, and describe the effects of that arrival on the continent.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating how scientific techniques have been used to establish the arrival of humans to the mega-continent known as Sahul; for example, use of radiocarbon (C14) and Thermoluminescence (TL) dating techniques used to date archaeological sites related to early human occupation such as Lake Mungo, and genetic research to map the movement of people
  • investigating how the ancestors of the early First Peoples of Australia were seafarers and examining the likely effects this had on the movement of people to the continent
  • examining online maps and information to investigate the relationship between geographical features and the location of known ancient sites on the mega-continent known as Sahul; for example, investigating the vegetation types and water sources in different parts of the continent and how they reflect the location of ancient sites
  • investigating the techniques used to predict the chronology and the routes taken in the dispersal of early First Nations Australians across the continent; for example, using online information that explains how research into ancient landscapes, waterways, terrain and important early First Nations Australians' habitation sites can model the movement of people across the continent, or how DNA taken from hair samples can track the genetic connections of First Nations Australians back to specific parts of Australia as far back as tens of thousands of years ago

Waypoints

  • 1. Seafaring and arrival — causesHingeStudents can describe the causes of early First Nations Australians' arrival on the continent, including that their ancestors were seafarers who crossed water to reach Sahul, and identify the geographical context of that crossing.
  • 2. Migration routes across the continentStudents can describe likely migration routes taken by early First Nations Australians across the continent, and identify the types of evidence — ancient sites, geographical features, genetic research — used to map those routes.
  • 3. Effects of arrivalStudents can describe the effects of early First Nations Australians' arrival on the continent, including the establishment of cultures across diverse environments and the beginnings of the world's oldest continuing civilisations.
