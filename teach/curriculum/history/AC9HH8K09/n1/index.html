How rulers exercised power

AC9HH8K09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how rulers in the chosen empire exercised power — including through governance systems, military force, religious authority and symbolic legitimacy — and explain how these mechanisms sustained their rule.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two mechanisms through which the chosen ruler exercised power and explains how they sustained authority
  • Distinguishes between how power was established (conquest) and how it was maintained (governance)

Prior knowledge

Students understand how power and authority operated in medieval Europe (AC9HH8K04) and the social and cultural features of the chosen empire (AC9HH8K07). They have not yet analysed the specific power structures and relationships between rulers and subject peoples in the context of empire and expansion.

Width

Minimum
At least two mechanisms through which the chosen ruler exercised power, with a brief explanation of how they sustained authority.
Typical
Multiple mechanisms of imperial rule. Examples: Kublai Khan as first Mongol emperor of China (Yuan Dynasty) — use of administrators from diverse backgrounds, growth of Chinese culture; the Ottoman Sultan's responsibility for justice; the millet system's governance of non-Muslim subjects; Cortés and the siege of Tenochtitlan as a display of military power transforming political authority.
Wider
  • For the Mongols: examine the role of Marco Polo and the development of trade as evidence of how Mongol rulers used economic connections as well as military conquest to consolidate power
  • For the Ottomans: analyse the millet system — which regarded non-Muslim people as subjects but not subject to Muslim law — as a case study in how religious plurality was managed as a governance strategy

Hinge

Understanding how power was exercised is prerequisite to understanding how subject peoples experienced and responded to it. Students who cannot explain the mechanisms of imperial rule cannot analyse the perspectives of those ruled.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often conflate conquest with governance. Scaffold the distinction: how did rulers maintain power after expansion? The mechanisms of ongoing governance (administration, law, religious tolerance, trade) are different from the mechanisms of military conquest.
