Interactions between power and resistance

AC9HH8K09 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how subject peoples responded to imperial power — through accommodation, resistance, or cultural exchange — and explain how these interactions shaped the development of the empire or expansion.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one example of how subject peoples responded to imperial power
  • Explains how that response — accommodation, resistance or exchange — affected the development of the empire

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Experiences of subject peoples.

Width

Minimum
At least one example of how subject peoples responded to imperial power (accommodation, resistance or exchange) with an explanation of its effect.
Typical
Responses across accommodation, resistance and exchange. Examples: the forging of connections between Europe and Asia through Mongol trade (Marco Polo); the survival and transformation of Irish society through Viking and Norman contact; the Aztec responses to Cortés including the siege of Tenochtitlan; Viking settlements in Dublin (Ireland) and Jorvik (York) as evidence of cultural integration as well as conquest.
Wider
  • Examine the remains of Viking settlements such as Dublin and Jorvik as archaeological evidence of cultural integration — settlements showing how 'conquerors' and 'subject peoples' created hybrid societies
  • For the Spanish conquest: investigate the encounters between Cortés and the Aztecs, examining how Aztec responses to Spanish power — including alliances with rival indigenous groups — shaped the outcome of the conquest

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents tend to frame subject-empire interactions as purely one-directional (conquerors act, subjects suffer). Scaffold the analysis to include how subject peoples' responses shaped the empire — trade relationships, cultural borrowing, resistance that forced governance adaptation.
