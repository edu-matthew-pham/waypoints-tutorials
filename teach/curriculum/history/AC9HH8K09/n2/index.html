Experiences of subject peoples

AC9HH8K09 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the experiences and perspectives of subject peoples under the chosen empire — including how conquest, governance and cultural contact affected their daily lives — and explain how these experiences differed across groups.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Describes the experience of at least one subject group under the chosen empire using historically specific detail
  • Identifies at least one way in which the experience of different subject groups differed within the same empire

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How rulers exercised power.

Width

Minimum
A description of the experience of at least one subject group with reference to how imperial rule affected their daily life.
Typical
Varied experiences of subject peoples across different groups. Examples: Ottoman tolerance of Christians and Jews vs the constraints of dhimmi status; Viking attacks on monasteries (Lindisfarne 793 CE, Iona 795 CE) experienced from the monks' written perspective; Aztec subjects under Spanish conquest — introduction of disease, horses, gunpowder, loss of resources; survival of Iron Age society in early medieval Ireland transformed by Christianity, Vikings and Anglo-Norman conquest.
Wider
  • For the Vikings: examine the written accounts of monks that contributed to the Vikings' reputation for pillaging — and question how this single-perspective source shapes historical understanding of Viking-subject encounters
  • For the Spanish conquest: investigate the impact of conquest on Indigenous populations (disease, horses, gunpowder, loss of natural resources) and the introduction of American crops to Europe, examining both sides of the colonial exchange

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe experience of subject peoples is highly variable across empires and within them. Using multiple subject groups (e.g. Ottoman dhimmi vs Aztec commoners under Spanish rule) prevents students from generalising from a single subordinate experience.
