Contributions to First Nations rights

AC9HH10K10 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the contributions of significant individuals and groups in the campaign for the recognition of the rights of First Nations Australians and the extent to which they brought change to Australian society

Prior knowledge

Students have examined the legislative framework of exclusion, early organised campaigns, and the Stolen Generations in K09. They understand the model of organised political activism established by the AAPA, AAL, and the 1938 Day of Mourning. They have not yet examined the specific contributions of individuals across the broader campaign period, the role of allied groups (Christian, union, labour), or the extent-of-change question.

By the end

Students investigate the contributions of significant individuals and groups in the campaign for First Nations rights and evaluate the extent to which they brought change to Australian society.

Waypoints

  • 1. Framework for evaluating individual and group contributionsHingeStudents can apply a framework for evaluating the contribution of individuals and groups to social change — distinguishing types of contribution and criteria for assessing extent of change.
  • 2. Significant individuals — selected contributionsStudents can explain the contributions of at least three significant individuals to the campaign for First Nations rights, applying the evaluation framework from N1.
  • 3. Significant groups — organisations, allied movements, and coalitionsStudents can explain the contributions of significant groups — First Nations organisations and allied groups — and evaluate how coalition-building contributed to the effectiveness of the campaign.
  • 4. Extent of change — evaluation and limitsStudents can evaluate the extent to which individuals and groups brought change to Australian society — identifying what changed, how durably, and what limits remained.
