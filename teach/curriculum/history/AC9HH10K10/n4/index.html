Extent of change — evaluation and limits

AC9HH10K10 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the extent to which individuals and groups brought change to Australian society — identifying what changed, how durably, and what limits remained.

Success criteria

  • Constructs an evaluative claim about the extent of change — identifying what changed, how durably, and what limits remained
  • Uses evidence from at least two individuals or groups to support the claim
  • Identifies at least one way formal change (legislation, referendum) did not translate into full practical change

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Significant groups — organisations, allied movements, and coalitions.

Width

Minimum
Students construct an evaluative claim about the extent of change produced by the campaign, identifying at least one significant change and one significant limit.
Typical
Students draw on N1–N3 to evaluate the overall extent of change — what legislative, political, and social changes resulted from individual and group contributions; what limits remained (ongoing inequality, structural racism, incomplete land rights recognition); evaluate whether changes were durable or partial; connect to K11's content on specific events and methods, and K13's content on continuing struggles — using these to calibrate the extent-of-change judgement.
Wider
  • Evaluate the 1967 Referendum as the clearest single measure of the campaign's success — analysing what it actually changed constitutionally and what it did not change in practice
  • Assess the long-term impact of specific individuals on Australian society — arguing which contribution had the most durable effect and why, using the N1 criteria

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node draws together the individual contributions (N2), group contributions (N3), and the evaluation framework (N1) into a coherent judgement about extent of change. Structured argument tasks requiring students to use evidence from all three prior nodes work well here.
