Significant events, turning points and nature of warfare

AC9HH9K10 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

significant events and turning points of the war and the nature of warfare, including the Western Front Battle of the Somme and the Armistice

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the theatres where Australians fought, the conditions of trench warfare, and the development of military technology (AC9HH9K09). They have not yet examined the concept of a military turning point, evaluated the significance of strategic-level events (Russian Revolution, American entry, Armistice), or worked with opposing-perspective sources to construct historical understanding of specific battles.

By the end

Students investigate a major battle (including its location, techniques and the nature of warfare), examine the perspectives of those who fought on both sides using primary sources, and evaluate the significance of the Russian Revolution, American entry into the war and the Armistice of November 1918 in ending the war.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating one major battle and examining locations, techniques and warfare
  • examining the perspectives of those who fought on both sides using sources such as diaries, letters and newspapers
  • evaluating the significance of the Russian Revolution, American entry into the war and the Armistice of November 1918 in ending the war

Waypoints

  • 1. What makes an event a turning point — the conceptHingeStudents can explain what distinguishes a turning point from a significant event — that a turning point marks a shift in momentum or trajectory, not merely a notable occurrence — and apply this distinction to a historical example.
  • 2. Investigating a major battle — location, techniques, and the nature of warfareStudents can investigate a major battle, describing its location, the techniques and technologies employed, and what it reveals about the nature of warfare on that front.
  • 3. Turning points — Russian Revolution, American entry, and the ArmisticeStudents can evaluate the significance of the Russian Revolution, American entry into the war, and the Armistice of November 1918 in ending the war, explaining what each changed and why each can be considered a turning point.
