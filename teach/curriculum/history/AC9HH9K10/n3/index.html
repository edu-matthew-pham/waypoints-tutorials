Turning points — Russian Revolution, American entry, and the Armistice

AC9HH9K10 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the significance of the Russian Revolution, American entry into the war, and the Armistice of November 1918 in ending the war, explaining what each changed and why each can be considered a turning point.

Success criteria

  • Describes what changed militarily and strategically as a result of each of the three turning points
  • Evaluates which turning point most significantly affected the war's end and supports the judgement with evidence

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Investigating a major battle — location, techniques, and the nature of warfare.

Width

Minimum
Students describe what each event was, explain what it changed in the balance of the war (Russia's withdrawal released German divisions; American entry added fresh troops and resources; the Armistice ended fighting), and assess which they consider the most significant turning point and why.
Typical
Students explain the Russian Revolution's effect on the Eastern Front and Germany's ability to redeploy forces for the Spring Offensive 1918; American entry in 1917 — the reasons, the scale of forces eventually deployed, the psychological effect on German morale; the German Spring Offensive's initial success and ultimate failure; the Hundred Days Offensive and the military collapse of Germany; the Armistice terms and how they differed from unconditional surrender; evaluate which turning point had the greatest effect on the war's end and defend the judgement.
Wider
  • Examine the German 'stab-in-the-back' myth that emerged after the war — the claim that Germany was not militarily defeated but betrayed — and evaluate whether the Armistice terms supported or undermined this claim, connecting to AC9HH9K12's Treaty of Versailles question
  • Compare the German military's assessment of the war's end with the Allied political narrative — was Germany defeated in the field, or did internal collapse drive the Armistice?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe each turning point in isolation without connecting them into a coherent explanation of how the war ended. Building an argument about significance — one event as more decisive than others — requires the analytical scaffold from N1 and the weighing of competing evidence.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEncountering the war's end from multiple perspectives — German military, Allied political, Russian revolutionary — prevents the assumption that the Armistice was simply an Allied victory without complexity.
