Experiences and perspectives — colonisers, settlers and First Nations Australians

AC9HH9K06 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

different experiences and perspectives of colonisers, settlers and First Nations Australians and the impact of these experiences on changes to Australian society's ideas, beliefs and values

Prior knowledge

Students can examine perspectives of rulers and subject peoples and describe how contact shaped societies (AC9HH8K04, AC9HH8K09, AC9HH8K14). They have not yet examined the specific diversity of colonial Australian society — convict, free settler, and non-European experiences — or the contested language around colonisation and its ongoing significance.

By the end

Students investigate the experiences of specific groups in colonial Australia — convicts, free settlers, non-Europeans, and First Nations Australians — examining how their experiences differed and how those experiences shaped changes to Australian society's ideas, beliefs and values, including the ongoing contestation of terms such as 'invasion', 'colonisation' and 'settlement'.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating the experiences of a specific group of arrivals to Australia, such as convicts in Sydney, Hobart or Brisbane, or free settlers in Melbourne, Adelaide, Perth or Darwin
  • examining how convicts transported to Australia were able to begin new lives away from the rigid class structures of English society
  • describing the impact of changes brought about by non-indigenous groups on First Nations Australians
  • examining the experiences of non-Europeans in Australia prior to the 1900s
  • exploring the perspectives and experiences of First Nations Australians, including discussing terms in relation to Australian history such as 'invasion', 'colonisation' and 'settlement'

Waypoints

  • 1. Convict experiences and their contribution to colonial societyStudents can describe the experience of convicts transported to Australia and examine how convicts contributed to colonial society — including how transportation offered social mobility unavailable in Britain.
  • 2. Non-European experiences in colonial AustraliaStudents can describe the experiences of non-European groups in colonial Australia before 1900 — including Chinese miners, Japanese pearlers, South Sea Islanders, and Afghan cameleers — and explain how they contributed to and were treated by colonial society.
  • 3. First Nations perspectives and contested languageStudents can describe First Nations Australian perspectives on and experiences of colonisation, and explain why terms such as 'invasion', 'colonisation' and 'settlement' continue to be contested in Australian society.
