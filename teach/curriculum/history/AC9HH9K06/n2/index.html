Non-European experiences in colonial Australia

AC9HH9K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the experiences of non-European groups in colonial Australia before 1900 — including Chinese miners, Japanese pearlers, South Sea Islanders, and Afghan cameleers — and explain how they contributed to and were treated by colonial society.

Success criteria

  • Describes the experiences of at least two non-European groups in colonial Australia, including their contributions and the discrimination they faced
  • Explains how the treatment of non-European groups connected to the development of White Australia ideology

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Convict experiences and their contribution to colonial society.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the experience of at least two non-European groups in colonial Australia, including their contribution and the discrimination they faced.
Typical
Students describe Chinese miners on the Victorian and NSW goldfields — their scale of presence, contribution to the mining economy, the anti-Chinese violence and legal discrimination (restricted entry, special taxes); Japanese pearlers in northern Australia; South Sea Islander workers on Queensland sugar plantations (blackbirding and labour exploitation); Afghan cameleers in central Australia; explain how the treatment of non-European groups shaped the 'White Australia' policy that emerged at Federation; examine how these groups maintained cultural identity under discrimination.
Wider
  • Examine the blackbirding of South Sea Islanders — the coercive recruitment of labour for Queensland sugar plantations — and evaluate where it sits on the spectrum from voluntary migration to forced labour
  • Investigate the legacy of a specific non-European community in Australia today — tracing the connection between colonial-era migration and contemporary Australian multiculturalism

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly treat colonial Australia as a binary settler-First Nations society without recognising the significant non-European presence. Multiple examples across different groups and regions build the more complex and accurate picture the descriptor requires.
