Continuing struggles for rights

AC9HH10K13 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the continuing efforts to create change in the civil rights and freedoms in Australia, for First Nations Australians, migrants and women

Prior knowledge

Students have examined First Nations rights campaigns and civil rights events (K09–K11), the women's movement (K12), and changing attitudes to migration (K08). They have evaluated the extent of change in each domain and identified limits. They have not yet examined the three domains together as a connected pattern of ongoing struggle, or examined the international frameworks that support continuing rights claims.

By the end

Students explain continuing efforts to create change in civil rights and freedoms for First Nations Australians, migrants, and women, and examine Australia's responsibilities under international human rights frameworks.

Waypoints

  • 1. The gap between formal rights and lived realityHingeStudents can explain the concept of the gap between formal legal rights and lived experience, and apply it to all three groups as a unifying analytical framework.
  • 2. Continuing struggles — First Nations AustraliansStudents can identify the focus areas of continuing civil rights action for First Nations Australians and explain why ongoing action remains necessary.
  • 3. Continuing struggles — women and migrantsStudents can explain the continuing efforts to advance women's rights and migrants' rights, connecting each to the formal-rights gap and to relevant legislative frameworks.
  • 4. International frameworks and Australia's obligationsStudents can explain the role of international human rights frameworks in supporting continuing rights claims, including UNDRIP, the Racial Discrimination Act, and Australia's responsibilities as a signatory to international conventions.
