How power was exercised and justified

AC9HH8K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how rulers exercised and justified their power in medieval Europe — through law, religion, punishment and symbolic authority — and describe how different groups experienced that power differently.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two instruments through which rulers exercised power in medieval Europe and explains how they were justified
  • Describes how the experience of power differed for at least two social groups

Prior knowledge

Students explain the organisation and roles of key groups in ancient society (AC9HH7K10) and understand the feudal hierarchy from AC9HH8K02. They have not yet examined how power and authority were experienced differently by rulers and subject peoples, or how those relationships were contested and changed across the medieval and early modern period.

Width

Minimum
At least two ways power was exercised (e.g. law, religious authority, punishment) and one example of how different groups experienced power differently.
Typical
The multiple instruments of medieval power — legal systems, religious sanction, military force, symbolic display — and how the experience of power varied by social group; different punishments for different groups (e.g. trial by combat for nobility, ducking stools for women) as evidence of power differentials.
Wider
  • Examine primary sources such as Matteo Villani's diary, Boccaccio's Decameron or Ibn Khaldun's recollections to understand how the Black Death disrupted the usual exercise of power and authority
  • Analyse the differing levels of political involvement in city-states such as guilds in Florence and Libro d'Oro in Venice as examples of how power was distributed and contested within urban contexts

Hinge

Understanding how power was exercised and justified is prerequisite to understanding how it was contested and reformed. Students who cannot distinguish between the mechanisms of power and the justifications for it cannot analyse political change in subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often conflate power (the ability to act) with authority (the legitimacy to act). Make the distinction explicit and scaffold analysis of specific examples — punishments, laws, religious justifications — so students can identify both the mechanism and the justification.
