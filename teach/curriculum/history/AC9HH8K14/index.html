Power and authority in Asia-Pacific

AC9HH8K14 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the experiences and perspectives of rulers and of subject peoples, and the interaction between power and/or authority in the Asian-Pacific society

Prior knowledge

Students understand how power and authority operated in medieval European and imperial contexts (AC9HH8K04, AC9HH8K09) and the social hierarchy of the chosen Asia-Pacific society (AC9HH8K12). They have not yet analysed how power and authority specifically operated in Asia-Pacific contexts — including the Khmer god-king, the Tokugawa feudal system, or chiefly authority in Polynesian societies.

By the end

Students explain the experiences and perspectives of rulers and subject peoples in the chosen Asia-Pacific society — describing how power was exercised and legitimised, how subject peoples experienced authority, and how interactions between rulers and subjects shaped the society's development.

Waypoints

  • 1. How rulers exercised authorityHingeStudents can describe how rulers in the chosen Asia-Pacific society exercised authority — including the basis of their legitimacy, the mechanisms of their rule, and how they mobilised or controlled subject peoples.
  • 2. Experiences of subject peoplesStudents can describe the experiences and perspectives of subject peoples in the chosen Asia-Pacific society — including how social position, class and cultural context shaped their daily life under authority.
  • 3. Interactions and responses to authorityStudents can describe how subject peoples responded to authority in the chosen Asia-Pacific society — including accommodation, resistance or cultural negotiation — and explain how these interactions shaped the society.
