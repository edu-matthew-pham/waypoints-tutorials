Comparing perspectives across sources

AC9HH9S06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can determine whether information in one source can be verified by another and compare the range of perspectives surrounding a historical event.

Success criteria

  • Compares the perspectives in at least two sources, identifying specific differences in what each presents
  • Identifies at least one voice or group whose perspective is absent or underrepresented in the available sources, and explains why

Prior knowledge

In Year 7 students identified perspectives, attitudes and values in sources (AC9HH7S06). In Year 8 they continued with identification (AC9HH8S06). Y9 lifts the demand — from identify to compare, and adds the new requirement to explain how perspectives are influenced by events, ideas, location, beliefs and values. Students must now move from recognising different perspectives to explaining what shaped them.

Width

Minimum
Compare the perspectives in at least two sources about the same event, identifying at least one specific difference.
Typical
Determine whether information in one source can be verified by another; compare and contrast the range of perspectives surrounding a historical event, including voices that may be absent from dominant sources (women, First Nations peoples, minority groups, colonised peoples); discuss whether an individual perspective is representative of a majority or minority view at the time.
Wider
  • Compare perspectives from sources representing absent or marginalised voices (First Nations Australians, women, colonised peoples) alongside dominant-perspective sources — examining whose voices are systematically absent and why
  • Investigate one historical event using sources from three or more different national or social perspectives — practising the expansion of perspective beyond the binary
  • Examine how the same event is narrated in sources produced at three different times (contemporaneous, 50 years later, contemporary) — assessing how temporal distance influences perspective and what new perspectives emerge over time

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepComparing perspectives across multiple sources — especially including absent voices — is the defining task here. Students who only compare two easily accessible perspectives (e.g. two Allied perspectives on WWI) are not meeting the full demand of the descriptor.
