Explaining what influences perspective

AC9HH9S06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how a person's perspective is influenced by significant events, ideas, location, beliefs and values, demonstrating understanding of why people in the same period held different views.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two factors that influenced the perspective shown in one source, connecting each factor to specific evidence from the source
  • Explains how perspectives are shaped differently depending on the social position, beliefs, or experiences of the author

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Comparing perspectives across sources.

Width

Minimum
Explain at least two factors (from: events, ideas, location, beliefs, values) that influenced the perspective shown in one source.
Typical
Explain how perspectives are shaped by significant events, ideas, location, beliefs and values — connecting each source's perspective to the specific circumstances of its author; discuss whether an individual's perspective is representative of a majority or minority at the time; apply this across multiple sources and source types from the topics being studied.
Wider
  • Apply perspective influence analysis to contrasting topic contexts — e.g. explaining what influenced a WWI soldier's perspective and what influenced a First Nations leader's perspective on colonisation — demonstrating the skill across different subject areas
  • Construct a structured comparison of two perspectives using an explicit framework: for each perspective, identify the events, ideas, location, beliefs and values that shaped it — then assess which factors were most influential
  • Examine how a person's perspective changed over time within their own lifetime (e.g. a politician's views on conscription before and after the referendum) — applying perspective analysis longitudinally

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApplying perspective influence analysis across multiple contexts (different topics, different source types) develops genuine analytical transferability — the skill should work for WWI sources, colonial sources, and industrial era sources alike.
