← AC9HH9K19 Key features of an Asian society — 18th and early 19th century

Social structure, economy and cultural expression

AC9HH9K19 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the social structure, economic foundations, and significant cultural features of their chosen Asian society.

Success criteria

  • Describes the social hierarchy and economic foundations of the chosen society
  • Describes at least one major cultural feature and explains how it reflected the society's values

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The Asian society chosen — political structure and authority.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the social hierarchy, the economic basis (trade, agriculture, tribute), and at least one major cultural feature (art, architecture, literature) of the chosen society.
Typical
Students describe the social hierarchy in their chosen society — the scholar-gentry in Qing China, the caste system under Mughal administration, the rigid samurai-commoner hierarchy of Tokugawa Japan; the economic foundations — tributary systems, agricultural taxation, trade controls; significant cultural features — the Confucian examination system and its cultural role in China, Mughal art and architecture (Taj Mahal, miniature painting), Japanese Noh and Kabuki theatre, samurai aesthetic culture; explain how cultural expression reflected and reinforced political and social values.
Wider
  • Examine a specific cultural artefact from the chosen society — a painting, building, text, or ceremonial object — as a historical source that reveals something about the society's values and worldview
  • Investigate the role of religion in the chosen society — Confucianism in China, Islam in the Mughal Empire, Buddhism and Shinto in Japan — and how religious institutions shaped social and political life

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need sufficient encounter with the chosen society's features across multiple domains — political, social, economic, cultural — before they can engage the contact-and-change analysis that K20 requires. Surface familiarity with one feature is insufficient.
