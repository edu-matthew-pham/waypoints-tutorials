The Asian society chosen — political structure and authority

AC9HH9K19 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the political structure and basis of authority in their chosen Asian society — explaining who held power, how authority was organised, and what legitimised rule.

Success criteria

  • Describes the political structure of the chosen society with at least two specific features
  • Explains what legitimised rule in the society's own terms

Prior knowledge

Students can describe the key features of societies across multiple domains (AC9HH8K07, AC9HH8K12). They have not yet examined Asian societies in the modern period — the political structures, cultural systems, and economic foundations of Qing China, Mughal India, or Tokugawa Japan before European contact became transformative.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the political structure of the chosen society and the basis of its ruler's authority — e.g. the Qing Emperor's Mandate of Heaven, the Mughal Emperor's divine and military authority, the Tokugawa Shogun's feudal control — with at least two specific features.
Typical
Students describe the full political apparatus — the Emperor/Shogun's role and powers, the administrative hierarchy beneath them, the mechanisms of control (bureaucracy, military, law, ideology); explain what legitimised rule in this society's own terms; describe the stability and instability of the political system; examine the role of specific rulers or political decisions in shaping the society during this period.
Wider
  • Compare the political structure of the chosen Asian society with European political structures of the same period — absolute monarchy, parliamentary systems — identifying similarities and differences
  • Examine how political authority in the chosen society dealt with internal challenge — rebellion, succession disputes, regional autonomy — to understand the limits of central power

Hinge

Political structure is the foundation for understanding social and cultural features (N2) and — critically — for understanding how the society responded to European contact in K20. Students who do not understand the political architecture cannot explain why European contact produced different responses in different Asian societies.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe Asian rulers as powerful without understanding the specific structures and legitimating ideologies — Mandate of Heaven, Shogunal feudalism, Mughal divine kingship — that organised political authority. Making these explicit is the scaffold for the contact-and-change analysis in K20.
