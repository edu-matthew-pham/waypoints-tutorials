Renaissance and new ideas

AC9HH8K01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the emergence of Renaissance ideas — including humanism and new approaches to art, science and learning — and identify the role of significant individuals in driving intellectual change.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one Renaissance idea or significant individual and explains how it represented a departure from medieval thought
  • Connects the emergence of Renaissance ideas to at least one prior development in the period (e.g. rediscovery of classical texts, printing press)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Feudalism, towns and trade.

Width

Minimum
At least one Renaissance idea or significant individual with an explanation of how it represented a shift from medieval thinking.
Typical
The Renaissance as a broader intellectual and cultural movement: humanism, the rediscovery of classical texts, new approaches to art and science, and the role of significant individuals (e.g. da Vinci, Copernicus); the emergence of the Scientific Revolution as an extension.
Wider
  • Examine the role of Islamic scholarship in preserving and transmitting classical Greek and Roman knowledge to Renaissance Europe, connecting Node 2 to intellectual transformation
  • Explore the significance of the printing press for the spread of Renaissance ideas, connecting technological change to intellectual transformation across Europe

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe Renaissance risks being taught as a purely European phenomenon. Widening to include Islamic scholarly transmission or the printing press helps students see it as a connected, multicausal development.
