Feudalism, towns and trade

AC9HH8K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the development of the feudal system, the growth of towns and cities, and the role of trade in transforming medieval European society.

Success criteria

  • Describes the feudal hierarchy and explains how it organised political and economic relationships in medieval Europe
  • Identifies at least one way in which the growth of towns or trade contributed to change in medieval society

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Spread of Christianity and Islam.

Width

Minimum
A basic description of the feudal hierarchy and one example of how towns or trade contributed to social and economic change.
Typical
The feudal system as a response to post-Roman disorder; the growth of towns as centres of commerce and culture; trade networks connecting Europe to wider worlds; the Magna Carta as an example of feudal tensions producing early constitutional ideas.
Wider
  • Trace the connection between the Magna Carta (1215) and the Westminster system to explore how feudal tensions produced lasting political structures
  • Examine how Islamic trade networks across the Mediterranean shaped European economic development, connecting religious and economic transformation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often describe feudalism as a fixed system rather than an adaptive response to specific historical conditions. Scaffold the causal logic: what problem did feudalism solve, who benefited, and how did towns and trade eventually undermine it?
