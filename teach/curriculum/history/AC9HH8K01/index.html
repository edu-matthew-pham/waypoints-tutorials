Transformation to early modern world

AC9HH8K01 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the transformation of the ancient world to the early modern world, from the decline of the Roman Empire in western Europe through Medieval, Renaissance or pre-modern Europe

Prior knowledge

Students explain the causes and effects of contacts and conflicts within and between ancient societies (AC9HH7K12), and the organisation and roles of key groups in ancient society (AC9HH7K10). They have not yet examined the large-scale structural transformation from the ancient to early modern world — the collapse of Roman order, the rise of new religious and political systems, and the emergence of Renaissance ideas.

By the end

Students explain the key transformations from the ancient world to the early modern world — including the decline of the Roman Empire, the spread of Christianity and Islam, the development of feudalism, the growth of towns and trade, and the emergence of Renaissance ideas — and describe how these developments connect to the organisation of modern political, religious and cultural life.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the key events that have shaped the modern era, such as the decline of ancient empires and societies; the spread of Christianity in Europe; the spread of Islam through the Arab world, Mediterranean coast, Egypt, North Africa and Persia; the development of the feudal system; the growth of towns and cities; trade; the migration of peoples and the emergence of new ideas
  • describing the connection between key events or developments of this period to modern day life, such as religious holidays and political systems; for example, the expansion of the Ottoman Empire, the Magna Carta and the Westminster system
  • investigating the emergence of ideas about the world such as those formed during the Renaissance, and the place of significant individuals and people that caused change
  • explaining the historical significance of selected key events such as the conversion to Christianity of the Emperor Constantine in 312 CE and the invasion of England by William the Conqueror in 1066

Waypoints

  • 1. Decline of the ancient worldHingeStudents can identify key events and processes that marked the decline of the ancient world — including the fall of the western Roman Empire and the migration of peoples — and describe what these meant for the organisation of European society.
  • 2. Spread of Christianity and IslamHingeStudents can explain the spread of Christianity through Europe and Islam through the Arab world, Mediterranean coast, Egypt, North Africa and Persia, and describe how both shaped the political and cultural organisation of the early medieval world.
  • 3. Feudalism, towns and tradeStudents can describe the development of the feudal system, the growth of towns and cities, and the role of trade in transforming medieval European society.
  • 4. Renaissance and new ideasStudents can explain the emergence of Renaissance ideas — including humanism and new approaches to art, science and learning — and identify the role of significant individuals in driving intellectual change.
  • 5. Connections to the modern worldStudents can explain how key developments of the medieval and early modern period connect to features of modern political, religious and cultural life.
