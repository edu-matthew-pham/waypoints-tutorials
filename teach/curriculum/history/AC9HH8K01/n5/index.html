Connections to the modern world

AC9HH8K01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how key developments of the medieval and early modern period connect to features of modern political, religious and cultural life.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one connection between a medieval or early modern development and a feature of modern political, religious or cultural life
  • Uses historical concepts such as continuity, change and significance when explaining connections between the medieval period and the modern world

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Renaissance and new ideas.

Width

Minimum
At least one connection between a medieval or early modern development and a modern institution or practice (e.g. religious holidays, parliamentary systems, legal traditions).
Typical
Multiple connections across political, religious and cultural domains — e.g. the Westminster system, the Magna Carta, the Church calendar, secular humanism — showing the ongoing relevance of the medieval-to-modern transformation.
Wider
  • Trace a contemporary political or legal institution (e.g. the Australian parliamentary system, rule of law) back through medieval developments to illustrate historical significance
  • Explore continuity and change by identifying aspects of medieval life that have not persisted into modernity, asking why some features transformed and others did not

Response modes
