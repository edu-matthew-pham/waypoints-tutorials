Commemoration and historical debate

AC9HH10K06 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the commemoration of the Second World War, including different historical interpretations and debates

Prior knowledge

Students have examined WW1 commemoration and the Anzac legend, including the distinction between commemoration and glorification (AC9HH9K12). From K02 they have examined Kokoda's strategic significance and its role in reinforcing the Anzac tradition. They can apply the concept of historical interpretation. They have not yet examined specifically WW2 commemoration debates, how the Anzac legend was extended from WW1 to WW2, the Battle for Australia controversy, or the ethical questions around Kokoda trekking.

By the end

Students analyse different historical interpretations and debates about the commemoration of WW2, including the Battle for Australia debate and the Kokoda commemoration controversy.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • analysing the debate over the Battle for Australia 1942 and its commemoration since 2008
  • discussing the commemoration of Kokoda, including the debate about the appropriateness of hiking the Kokoda trail today as a form of commemoration

Waypoints

  • 1. Commemoration as historical and political practiceHingeStudents can explain that commemoration involves interpretive choices — decisions about what to remember, how, and why — and that these choices reflect the values, politics, and identity needs of the commemorating community.
  • 2. The Anzac legend extended — WW2 in Australian national memoryStudents can explain how the Anzac legend developed through WW1 was extended to WW2, and how specific WW2 campaigns — particularly Kokoda — were incorporated into Australian national memory.
  • 3. The Battle for Australia debateStudents can analyse the debate over the Battle for Australia 1942 — including what is claimed, why it is contested, and how it has been commemorated since 2008.
  • 4. The Kokoda commemoration controversyStudents can analyse the debate about the commemoration of Kokoda — including the appropriateness of commercial trekking — from multiple perspectives including Papua New Guinean communities.
