Holocaust — scale, significance, and long-term impacts

AC9HH10K03 n5 · waypoint 5 of 6 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the scale of the Holocaust using evidence, evaluate its historical significance, and explain its long-term impacts on the Jewish community and on international human rights frameworks.

Success criteria

  • Describes the scale of the Holocaust using specific evidence — not only states that it was large
  • Explains at least one long-term impact on the Jewish community with evidence
  • Explains the Holocaust's role in motivating international human rights frameworks, connecting to at least one specific institution or document

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Holocaust — ideology, context, and escalation.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the scale of the Holocaust using at least one specific piece of evidence; explain one long-term impact on the Jewish community and one on international human rights frameworks.
Typical
Students investigate the scale using primary and secondary sources — survivor testimonies, statistical records, photographic and documentary evidence; explain why scale matters to historical significance; discuss long-term impacts on the Jewish community (displacement, migration to Israel, Australia, and the USA, creation of Yad Vashem and other memorial institutions); explain the Holocaust's role in motivating the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the Genocide Convention; connect to K15's content on the UDHR.
Wider
  • Examine the post-war Nuremberg trials as the founding moment of international criminal law — analysing how the Holocaust forced a rethinking of state sovereignty and individual accountability that still shapes international law
  • Explore how Holocaust memory and commemoration has evolved — examining debates about representation, the role of survivor testimony, and how digital archives are changing access to this history

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepUsing survivor testimonies and multiple source types — statistical, personal, institutional — gives students a richer and more accurate picture of scale and experience. This also builds source analysis skills required in AC9HH10S03 and S04.
