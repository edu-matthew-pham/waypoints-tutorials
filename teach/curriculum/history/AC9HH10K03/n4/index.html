Holocaust — ideology, context, and escalation

AC9HH10K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 6 · Year 10 History · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the historical context in which the Holocaust occurred — including anti-Semitism as a longstanding European phenomenon, Nazi race theory as its political weaponisation, and the escalation from persecution to genocide.

Success criteria

  • Explains anti-Semitism as a pre-existing European phenomenon that the Nazi Party weaponised rather than invented
  • Describes at least three stages in the escalation from legal persecution to organised genocide
  • Explains why the Holocaust requires a distinct analytical framework — not merely treatment as one war atrocity among others

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Turning points — Asia-Pacific theatre.

Width

Minimum
Students explain at least two ideological or political preconditions for the Holocaust and describe the escalation from legal persecution to organised genocide.
Typical
Students examine anti-Semitism as a pre-existing European phenomenon that preceded the Nazi Party; explain how Nazi race theory weaponised anti-Semitism as a political programme; trace the escalation — legal persecution (Nuremberg Laws, 1935), organised violence (Kristallnacht, 1938), forced displacement, ghettoisation, and the shift to systematic mass murder from 1941 (Einsatzgruppen, Wannsee Conference, death camps); explain why the Holocaust requires a distinct analytical framework rather than being treated as just another war atrocity.
Wider
  • Examine the range of perpetrators, bystanders, and rescuers — analysing what conditions enabled ordinary people to participate in or resist the Holocaust, and what this reveals about the relationship between ideology, authority, and individual agency
  • Investigate the experiences of groups other than Jewish people targeted by Nazi persecution — Roma, disabled people, political opponents, homosexuals — examining how the Holocaust's ideological logic extended beyond its primary target

Hinge

The ideological and contextual grounding established here is the prerequisite for N5's scale and significance analysis. Students who encounter the Holocaust without understanding anti-Semitism's pre-existing character, Nazi race theory's specific logic, or the escalation process cannot explain why the Holocaust happened — they can only describe that it did. This produces the 'incomprehensible evil' framing that shuts down historical analysis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to understand the Holocaust as the product of specific ideological, institutional, and social conditions — not as an inexplicable aberration. The scaffold is: what were the pre-existing conditions? how did Nazi ideology operationalise them? how did escalation occur step by step? This prevents both trivialisation and the framing that forecloses historical analysis.
