← AC9HH10S08 Creating historical arguments

Extended historical text for purpose and audience

AC9HH10S08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 History · Historical skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop an extended historical text that explains, analyses and evaluates a historical event, development or movement for a specific purpose and audience, and can present historical arguments in a range of forms.

Success criteria

  • Develop an extended historical text that explains, analyses and evaluates a historical topic — not merely describing it
  • Demonstrate a clear purpose and audience orientation in the organisation and communication of the text
  • Present the historical argument in a chosen form with appropriate structure, evidence, and use of historical concepts and terms

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Constructing a historical argument.

Width

Minimum
Develop an extended text that explains, analyses and evaluates a historical topic for a specific purpose and audience; present a historical argument in at least one chosen form.
Typical
Develop an extended text that explains, analyses and evaluates a historical event, development or movement for purpose and audience — organising ideas and information to present relevant historical knowledge about the impact of a global event on a community to be presented to peers; present a historical argument in a chosen form: essay, oral presentation, debate, interactive digital or non-digital display, online conference or forum incorporating evidence.
Wider
  • Produce a historical argument in an unfamiliar form — such as a museum exhibit text panel, a public lecture script, or a documentary voiceover — adapting historical communication to a specific purpose and audience different from a standard essay
  • Present a historical argument on a topic central to the course (e.g. the significance of Reconciliation, or the effects of post-war migration on Australian identity) using a form that requires the argument to be communicated to a non-specialist audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates all S-strand skills — question development (S01), source work (S02–S04), causal and continuity analysis (S05), perspective analysis (S06), interpretation analysis (S07), and argument construction (Node 1) — into a complete, purposeful historical text. It is the culminating performance of the Year 10 historical skills sequence.
