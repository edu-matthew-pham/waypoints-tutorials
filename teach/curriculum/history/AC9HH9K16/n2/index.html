← AC9HH9K16 Perspectives and experiences during the industrial revolution

Gendered and age-based experiences — men, women and children

AC9HH9K16 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare the different experiences of men, women and children in industrial workplaces and domestic life, and explain how technological change affected each group's way of life.

Success criteria

  • Describes the distinct industrial experience of each of the three groups — men, women and children
  • Explains how at least one group's experience changed from pre-industrial to industrial conditions

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Class differentiation — how industrialisation affected rich and poor differently.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least one distinct experience of each group (men, women, children) in the industrial context and explain how it differed from pre-industrial experience.
Typical
Students describe men's roles in heavy industry, mining, railways — the physical danger, long hours, and how factory discipline transformed working life from craft to machine-paced labour; women's industrial employment — textile mills, domestic service, limited wage levels, the tension between industrial work and domestic responsibilities; children's experiences — as cheap labour in mills, mines and chimneys, the debates about child labour and the Factory Acts; examine how steam, gas and electricity affected domestic life differently for rich and poor households; explain how industrialisation both drew women and children into paid labour and gradually created pressure for their protection from it.
Wider
  • Examine the Parliamentary Select Committee reports and Factory Acts as sources — analysing what the testimony of child workers revealed about conditions and what drove legislative reform
  • Investigate the contradictions in middle-class attitudes to working-class women and children — the domestic ideal vs economic necessity — and what these contradictions reveal about class and gender values in industrial society

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly focus on one group (often children, as the most dramatic case) without building the full differentiated picture across men, women and children. Multiple examples across all three groups are required.
