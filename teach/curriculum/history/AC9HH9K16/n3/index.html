Trade unions — collective response to industrial conditions

AC9HH9K16 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 History · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in History: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the growth of trade unions as a response to industrialisation's impacts on the working class, and explain what problems trade unions addressed and what they achieved.

Success criteria

  • Describes trade unions as a collective response to industrial conditions and explains what problems they addressed
  • Explains why collective action was necessary when individual workers lacked bargaining power

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Gendered and age-based experiences — men, women and children.

Width

Minimum
Students describe trade unions as worker organisations formed to negotiate collectively for wages and conditions, explain what industrial conditions made them necessary, and describe at least one thing they achieved.
Typical
Students describe the development of trade unions from illegal combinations to recognised organisations; the specific grievances they addressed — long hours, dangerous conditions, child labour, arbitrary dismissal, low wages; early forms of collective action (strikes, mutual aid societies); the legal battles (Combination Acts, their repeal, the Tolpuddle Martyrs); what unions eventually achieved — shorter working hours, improved safety, minimum wage negotiations, political representation through labour parties; explain why collective action was necessary when individual workers had no bargaining power.
Wider
  • Examine the Tolpuddle Martyrs case as a turning point in trade union history — and what it reveals about the balance of power between workers, employers and the state in early industrial Britain
  • Trace how British trade unionism influenced the Australian labour movement — connecting the Industrial Revolution strand to the Australian Nation strand (AC9HH9K02, K05)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat trade unions as a given feature of industrial society without understanding why collective organisation was necessary — why individual workers had no bargaining power against employers. Making the power asymmetry explicit is the scaffold for understanding why collective action emerged and what it could achieve.
