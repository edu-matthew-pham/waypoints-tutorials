Water distribution and sustainability

AC9HG7K02 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the location and distribution of water resources in Australia, their implications, and strategies to manage the sustainability of water

Prior knowledge

Students have completed AC9HG7K01 — they can classify water as a renewable/continuous/non-renewable resource, trace water movement connecting places, and explain environmental, economic and social effects. They can locate Australia broadly but have not previously examined the spatial distribution of Australia's water resources or the concept of sustainability applied specifically to water.

By the end

Students explain the location and distribution of water resources in Australia, their implications for people, and strategies to manage water sustainability.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • representing the location of Australia's water resources, such as surface water and groundwater
  • describing the distribution of Australia's water resources, and its implications for people; for example, limited access to water for people in rural and remote places and its implications
  • identifying the causes of variability in water resources or water scarcity; for example, an absolute shortage of water (physical cause), inadequate development of water resources (economic cause), or the ways water is used (such as farming, industry, drinking, washing or watering)
  • explaining the factors that contribute to variability in water resources or water scarcity; for example, location, climate, topography, seasonality or evaporation
  • examining why water is a difficult resource for communities to manage and sustain; for example, because of its shared and competing uses, and variability of supply over time
  • examining how a strategy may manage the sustainability of water resources; for example, recycling ('grey water'), stormwater harvesting and re-use, desalination, inter-regional transfer of water and trade in virtual water, and reducing water consumption

Waypoints

  • 1. Locating and representing Australia's water resourcesHingeStudents can represent the location of Australia's major water resources (surface water and groundwater) and describe the pattern of their distribution.
  • 2. Causes and factors of water variability and scarcityHingeStudents can distinguish between physical and economic causes of water scarcity, and explain factors that contribute to variability in water resources.
  • 3. Implications of distribution for peopleStudents can explain implications of Australia's water distribution for people in different settlement types, including people in rural and remote places.
  • 4. Strategies for water sustainabilityStudents can explain how a water management strategy may improve the sustainability of water resources, identifying the problem it addresses and trade-offs involved.
  • 5. Integration: distribution, implications and managementStudents explain the location and distribution of water resources in Australia, their implications for people, and strategies to manage water sustainability — integrating spatial, social and management analysis.
