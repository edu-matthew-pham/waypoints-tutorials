Economic conditions and urban change

AC9HG8K06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how changes in economic conditions affect the characteristics of urban places, including growth, decline and renewal.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least one economic condition has changed the characteristics of a named urban place.
  • Identifies whether the economic change produced growth, decline or transformation in the urban place.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Scales of urbanisation.

Width

Minimum
Students explain one way that economic conditions have changed the characteristics of an urban place.
Typical
Students explain how economic conditions (tourism boom, resource decline, deindustrialisation, urban renewal investment) affect the characteristics of urban places, using examples of population growth in coastal communities, decline in regional areas, and urban renewal districts.
Wider
  • Compare the urban character of a growth community and a declining community in the same country.
  • Examine gentrification as an economic-urban process — its causes and the differential effects on existing residents.
  • Investigate how government policy (infrastructure investment, zoning) shapes economic-driven urban change.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepUsing multiple economic scenarios — growth, decline, renewal — prevents students from treating economic urbanisation as only a growth story and builds capacity to explain divergent urban trajectories.
