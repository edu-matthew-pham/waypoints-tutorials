Urbanisation and its impacts

AC9HG8K06 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

causes of urbanisation and its impacts on places and environments, drawing on a study from a country such as the United States of America, and its implications

Prior knowledge

Students explain factors that influence where people live including liveability and environmental quality (AC9HG7K05), and strategies to enhance liveability (AC9HG7K08). They have not yet examined urbanisation as a process with causes and spatial impacts.

By the end

Students explain the causes of urbanisation and its impacts on places and environments, drawing on a country study, and explain the implications of these impacts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the difference between urban growth and urbanisation, and how push-pull forces contribute to internal and international population movements and increase the size of urban areas
  • distinguishing between large cities and the rise of megacities at the national scale, including the growth of large capital cities in Australia, Los Angeles as a megacity, or Boston – Washington as an urban corridor in the United States of America
  • explaining how changes in economic conditions affect the characteristics of urban places, including population growth in a tourist community in coastal New South Wales such as Byron Bay, population decline in a regional area such as Carnarvon Shire, or old industrial areas evolving into areas of urban renewal, and urban villages in Green Square and Barangaroo, Sydney
  • explaining the connections between urbanisation and economic and social opportunities; for example, the location of universities, sporting stadiums or parliaments in capital cities
  • explaining how urbanisation can positively or negatively affect the quality of the environment; for example, increases in carbon emissions or increases in water consumption

Waypoints

  • 1. Urban growth vs urbanisationHingeStudents can distinguish between urban growth and urbanisation, and explain push-pull forces that drive population movement toward urban areas.
  • 2. Scales of urbanisationStudents can distinguish between large cities and megacities, and explain urban corridor formation, drawing on examples at the national scale.
  • 3. Economic conditions and urban changeStudents can explain how changes in economic conditions affect the characteristics of urban places, including growth, decline and renewal.
  • 4. Impacts of urbanisationStudents can explain how urbanisation produces positive and negative impacts on the quality of the environment and on economic and social opportunities.
