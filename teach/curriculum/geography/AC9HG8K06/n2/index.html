Scales of urbanisation

AC9HG8K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between large cities and megacities, and explain urban corridor formation, drawing on examples at the national scale.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Defines megacity and names at least one example, explaining what distinguishes it from a large city.
  • Locates urban settlements at the national scale and describes the broad pattern of urban concentration.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Urban growth vs urbanisation.

Width

Minimum
Students define megacity and distinguish it from a large city, with at least one located example.
Typical
Students distinguish between large cities and megacities (population ≥10 million), describe their growth patterns and explain urban corridor formation (e.g., Boston–Washington corridor), drawing on Australian capital city growth and a US case study.
Wider
  • Compare megacity growth patterns in the Global North (e.g., Los Angeles) with those in the Global South (e.g., Lagos, Mumbai).
  • Investigate what problems emerge specifically at megacity scale that are not present in large cities.
  • Examine urban corridor formation as a distinct urban form — what drives it and what are its infrastructure implications?
