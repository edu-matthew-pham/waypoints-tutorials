Urban growth vs urbanisation

AC9HG8K06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between urban growth and urbanisation, and explain push-pull forces that drive population movement toward urban areas.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Correctly distinguishes urban growth from urbanisation with examples of each.
  • Identifies and categorises at least two push factors and two pull factors driving urbanisation.

Prior knowledge

Students explain factors that influence where people live including liveability and environmental quality (AC9HG7K05), and strategies to enhance liveability (AC9HG7K08). They have not yet examined urbanisation as a process with causes and spatial impacts.

Width

Minimum
Students define and distinguish urban growth from urbanisation, and identify at least two push-pull factors.
Typical
Students explain the difference between urban growth (increase in the size of an urban area) and urbanisation (increase in the proportion of population living in urban areas), and explain how push-pull forces contribute to internal and international population movements that drive urbanisation.
Wider
  • Examine how the relative weighting of push vs pull factors differs between internal and international migration contexts.
  • Compare urbanisation trajectories in a developed country (USA) and a developing country, identifying structural differences.
  • Investigate how digital connectivity is changing the traditional push-pull model of urbanisation.

Hinge

Students who conflate urban growth with urbanisation, or treat population movement as purely economic, will produce shallow explanations of urbanisation impacts. The conceptual distinction and the push-pull model underpin all subsequent analysis in this standard.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe urban growth / urbanisation distinction requires clear definitional work, and the push-pull model needs systematic unpacking so students can apply it analytically rather than listing factors without structure.
