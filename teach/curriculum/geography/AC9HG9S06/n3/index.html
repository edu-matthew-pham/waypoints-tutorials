← AC9HG9S06 Creating geographical descriptions and explanations

Tone, audience and structured argument

AC9HG9S06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create explanations that apply tone appropriate to the audience and present a reasoned argument

Success criteria

  • Applies a consistent tone appropriate to an identified audience throughout
  • Structures the response with a clear position, reasoning and conclusion
  • Integrates geographical concepts and research-based evidence into the argument

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Using representations and research findings as evidence.

Width

Minimum
Create a response with a consistent tone appropriate to an identified audience; structure the response with a clear position, supporting reasoning and a conclusion
Typical
Create an explanation with audience-appropriate tone (e.g. authoritative tone for a management proposal, accessible tone for a public information piece) and reasoned argument — for example, a case for reducing food wastage or a management plan for a tourist hotspot
Wider
  • Produce two versions of the same response for different audiences — explain how the tone, vocabulary and structure of each differs
  • Evaluate a published geographical response for tone-audience fit — identify where tone choices strengthen or undermine the argument
  • Draft a response where the tone must balance authority (for a professional audience) with accessibility (for a general public audience) — explain the trade-offs

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates geographical concepts (Node 1), research-grounded evidence (Node 2), and structured argument with audience awareness. The response is the whole product — nodes 1 and 2 supply the content and evidence, this node supplies the communicative form. Teaching investment should ensure the three elements work together, not appear as separate layers in the response.
