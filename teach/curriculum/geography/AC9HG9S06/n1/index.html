Applying geographical concepts and terms in responses

AC9HG9S06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Geography · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop responses using geographical concepts and terms accurately

Success criteria

  • Uses at least three geographical concepts correctly in a response
  • Applies concepts as analytical frameworks, not just as vocabulary
  • Uses geographical terminology accurately throughout

Prior knowledge

Students can create descriptions and explanations using geographical knowledge and tools (Y7-8). At Y9, the moves are: applying geographical concepts and terms in structured responses; using data representations (maps, graphs, images) and research findings as evidence; and calibrating tone appropriately to audience.

Width

Minimum
Use at least three geographical concepts (e.g. scale, place, interconnection, sustainability, environment) and five geographical terms correctly in a response
Typical
Develop a response using geographical concepts (scale, place, environment, interconnection, change, sustainability) and correct geographical terminology; apply concepts as organising frameworks rather than as vocabulary inserts
Wider
  • Evaluate a peer response for conceptual accuracy — identify where concepts are used correctly as frameworks and where they are name-dropped without conceptual function
  • Use two related concepts together in one explanation (e.g. scale and interconnection; environment and sustainability) — explain how the concepts interact rather than using each independently
  • Apply the same concept across two different content areas (e.g. use 'interconnection' to analyse both biome modification and trade) — explain what the concept reveals in each case

Hinge

Geographical concepts are the organising framework that distinguishes geographical responses from general descriptions. Students who cannot use concepts correctly as analytical tools rather than as vocabulary produce responses that meet the description command but not the geographical knowledge requirement. This blocks the explanation and research-incorporation moves in Nodes 2-3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuilding a working vocabulary of geographical concepts and terms requires repeated use across different content areas, not memorisation in one context. Applying concepts across biome, interconnection and trade content builds the transferable conceptual toolkit the descriptor requires.
