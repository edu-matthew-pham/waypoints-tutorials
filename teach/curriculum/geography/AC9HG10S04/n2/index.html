Evaluating the justification for a conclusion

AC9HG10S04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 10 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine the reasons given for a specific decision or conclusion and explain how these reasons have or have not justified the conclusion.

Success criteria

  • Students evaluate whether reasons given for a conclusion are adequate, identifying at least one strength and one gap or weakness
  • Students explain how interconnected factors (at least two types) were or were not accounted for in the justification

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Conclusions grounded in geographical concepts.

Width

Minimum
Examine one conclusion or decision and evaluate whether the reasons given adequately justify it, identifying at least one strength and one weakness in the justification.
Typical
Examine the reasoning behind decisions related to sustainability management or wellbeing improvement; evaluate whether the reasons given are sufficient, appropriate and internally consistent; identify where interconnected factors (environmental, economic, social, political, technological) are accounted for or overlooked.
Wider
  • Evaluate a geographical decision from a policy document or case study — tracing the explicit and implicit reasoning, identifying assumptions, and assessing whether the conclusion follows from the evidence and arguments given
  • Construct an alternative conclusion from the same data and evidence, explaining what different reasoning steps would be required to reach it
  • Investigate a case where a decision was made and later revised — examining what new evidence or reasoning caused the revision and what that reveals about the original justification

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPractising justification evaluation across both sustainability and wellbeing contexts builds the transferability of the critical reasoning skill — students who have only evaluated one type of conclusion may not apply the skill to a new context.
