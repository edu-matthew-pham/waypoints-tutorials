Environmental factors and agricultural productivity

AC9HG9K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how environmental factors — climate, soil, landform, water and hazards — support or constrain agricultural production

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three environmental factors and explains how each affects productivity
  • Gives specific examples from Australia and/or an Asian country
  • Explains how at least one environmental factor limits production in a named location

Prior knowledge

Students can describe factors that affect land use and explain why places have particular characteristics (Y7-8). They have not examined the interaction between environmental, economic and technological factors on agricultural productivity comparatively across two countries, nor analysed how innovations reduce environmental limitations.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three environmental factors (climate, soil, landform, water, hazards) and explain how each affects agricultural production; give examples from Australia or Asia
Typical
Explain how multiple environmental factors combine to support high agricultural production of a staple crop (e.g. wheat in Australia, rice in Asia); identify where and why environmental factors limit production
Wider
  • Compare how environmental factors operate differently for rice production in a monsoon climate (Asia) versus wheat production in a semi-arid climate (Australia)
  • Analyse how natural hazards (e.g. drought, flood, cyclone) interact with other environmental factors to reduce yield — examine a case study from Australia and one from Asia
  • Investigate the relationship between soil type and landform in determining agricultural suitability — explain using a specific case (e.g. the Murray-Darling Basin)

Hinge

Environmental factors are the baseline against which economic and technological factors operate — technologies exist specifically to address environmental limitations, and economic viability depends partly on environmental endowment. Students who cannot identify specific environmental constraints cannot explain why particular technologies are used or why productivity differs across locations in Nodes 2 and 3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEnvironmental factors produce different effects in different climates and crop systems. Comparing Australia (wheat, semi-arid) with an Asian country (rice, monsoon) from the start ensures students understand that the same factor (water) operates differently in different biome-crop combinations.
