Factors affecting agricultural productivity

AC9HG9K03 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the environmental, economic and technological factors that impact agricultural productivity, in Australia and a country in Asia

Prior knowledge

Students can describe factors that affect land use and explain why places have particular characteristics (Y7-8). They have not examined the interaction between environmental, economic and technological factors on agricultural productivity comparatively across two countries, nor analysed how innovations reduce environmental limitations.

By the end

Students examine how environmental, economic and technological factors — and their interconnections — impact the yield of specific crops in Australia and a country in Asia.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining how environmental factors, such as climate, soil, landform, water and hazards, support higher agricultural production, such as wheat, rice and maize, in Australia and a country in Asia
  • examining how economic factors such as available land, labour, finance and enterprise, and technological factors such as biotechnology and use of Geographical Information Systems (GIS) software, affect agricultural production in Australia and a country in Asia; for example, increased labour supply or access to storage, transportation and markets
  • examining how agricultural innovations have reduced environmental limitations on food production in Australia and a country in Asia; for example, increased food production due to research into and development of high-yielding and genetically engineered pest resistant varieties, construction of drip irrigation systems, and the use of stubble mulching, intercropping, agroforestry and crop rotation
  • explaining the impact of the interconnections between environmental, economic and technological factors on the yield of a particular crop, such as wheat, rice or maize, in Australia

Waypoints

  • 1. Environmental factors and agricultural productivityHingeStudents can explain how environmental factors — climate, soil, landform, water and hazards — support or constrain agricultural production
  • 2. Economic and technological factorsStudents can examine how economic factors — land, labour, finance, enterprise — and technological factors — biotechnology, GIS — affect agricultural production
  • 3. Agricultural innovations reducing environmental limitationsStudents can examine how agricultural innovations have reduced environmental limitations on food production in Australia and an Asian country
  • 4. Interconnections between factors and crop yieldStudents can explain the impact of interconnections between environmental, economic and technological factors on the yield of a particular crop in Australia
