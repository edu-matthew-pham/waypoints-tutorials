Agricultural innovations reducing environmental limitations

AC9HG9K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine how agricultural innovations have reduced environmental limitations on food production in Australia and an Asian country

Success criteria

  • Names at least two innovations and explains what environmental limitation each reduces
  • Links the innovation back to the specific environmental factor it addresses (Node 1 connection)
  • Gives examples from both Australia and an Asian country

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Economic and technological factors.

Width

Minimum
Name at least two innovations (e.g. high-yielding varieties, drip irrigation, stubble mulching); explain how each reduces a specific environmental limitation
Typical
Explain how innovations such as high-yielding and pest-resistant crop varieties, drip irrigation, stubble mulching, intercropping and agroforestry reduce specific environmental limitations (e.g. water scarcity, soil degradation, pest pressure) in Australia and an Asian country
Wider
  • Evaluate a specific innovation — what environmental limitation does it address, does it create new limitations, and what is the net effect on productivity sustainability?
  • Compare how the same innovation (e.g. high-yielding varieties) has affected productivity differently in an Australian and an Asian context — explain using environmental and economic factors from Nodes 1-2
  • Investigate crop rotation as an innovation that works with environmental processes rather than overriding them — explain the ecological mechanisms

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates environmental factors (Node 1) with economic/technological factors (Node 2) — innovations are technological and economic responses to environmental limitations. Teaching investment should ensure students connect the specific limitation an innovation addresses back to the environmental factor that creates it, producing explanatory chains rather than lists.
