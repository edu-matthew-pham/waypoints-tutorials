Human activity and erosion/sedimentation

AC9HG8K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the interconnections between specific human activities (tourism, land clearing, mining, river regulation) and the quality of geomorphological environments.

Success criteria

  • Explains the interconnection between a named human activity and a specific geomorphological process, describing the resulting environmental effects.
  • Uses location-specific evidence to support the explanation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Interconnection as a concept.

Width

Minimum
Students explain one human activity and its effects on a geomorphological environment, identifying the process(es) modified.
Typical
Students explain the interconnections between at least two types of human activity (e.g., overuse of tourist tracks, land clearing for palm oil, mining, river regulation/dams) and the quality of geomorphological environments, identifying the processes affected and the environmental consequences.
Wider
  • Compare the scale of environmental impact of different human activities — which types of activity produce the most significant geomorphological change?
  • Investigate how the effects of human-modified erosion and sedimentation are distributed unevenly, affecting some communities more than others.
  • Examine specific case studies: Three Gorges Dam effects on the Yangtze River system, or Murray-Darling management.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepUsing multiple activity types and contexts builds a repertoire of interconnection examples, preventing students from treating tourism overuse as the only mechanism of human-landscape modification.
