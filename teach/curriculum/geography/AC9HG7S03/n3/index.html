Inferring geographical relationships

AC9HG7S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can infer relationships in geographical data and information, explaining the relationship and the evidence base for the inference.

Success criteria

  • Infers at least one relationship from geographical data and identifies the evidence that supports the inference.
  • Notes at least one limitation of the inference or an alternative explanation that the data cannot rule out.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Explaining geographical patterns and trends.

Width

Minimum
Students infer at least one relationship from geographical data and explain the evidence that supports the inference.
Typical
Students infer relationships in data and information collected — e.g. using surveys and interviews to identify community attitudes about service distribution in cities vs remote communities — explaining the relationship inferred, the evidence base, and the degree of confidence warranted.
Wider
  • Distinguish between correlation (two things vary together) and causal relationship (one causes the other) in geographical data, and test this distinction on real examples
  • Infer a relationship from two different data types (quantitative and qualitative) for the same phenomenon and consider whether the two data types produce the same inference
  • Examine an apparent relationship in data that has an alternative explanation — practise generating and evaluating rival explanations

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often treat correlation as causation. The reasoning scaffold — pattern observed → relationship inferred → evidence for inference → confidence level → possible alternative explanation — builds epistemic care about inference that is central to geographical analysis.
