Integration: interpret and analyse geographical data

AC9HG7S03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students interpret and analyse geographical data — identifying similarities and differences, explaining patterns and trends, and inferring relationships — in the context of a geographical inquiry.

Success criteria

  • Produces a data analysis that demonstrates at least two of: identifying similarities/differences, explaining patterns/trends, inferring relationships.
  • Grounds each analytical claim in specific data evidence.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Inferring geographical relationships.

Width

Minimum
Students produce an analysis of geographical data that demonstrates at least two of the three analytical moves (compare, explain, infer).
Typical
Students produce an integrated analysis of a geographical dataset that identifies similarities and differences, explains at least one spatial pattern or trend, and infers at least one relationship — presented as a connected analytical argument rather than three separate exercises.
Wider
  • Produce a full data analysis section for a geographical inquiry report that applies all three analytical moves to multiple data sources, evaluates the reliability of the inferences, and identifies gaps
  • Peer-review another student's data analysis using the three-move framework — provide specific feedback on whether each move is demonstrated with sufficient evidence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe three analytical moves — compare, explain, infer — need to be applied sequentially and cumulatively in a real analysis context. Integration practises moving through all three in response to a geographical dataset rather than applying each in isolation.
