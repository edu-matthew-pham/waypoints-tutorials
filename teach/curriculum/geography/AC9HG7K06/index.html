Services, facilities and liveability

AC9HG7K06 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the location and distribution of services and facilities, and implications for liveability of places

Prior knowledge

Students have completed K05 — they understand liveability as a concept, can distinguish objective and subjective measures, and know that factors including environmental quality influence residential decisions. They can represent distribution of resources from K02 work. This standard applies distribution analysis specifically to services and facilities and connects it to liveability outcomes.

By the end

Students explain the location and distribution of services and facilities and their implications for liveability.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing the distribution and location of a range of services and facilities, such as access to clean water, sanitation, education and health services, between different types of settlements (urban, rural, remote and very remote)
  • explaining the role transport and technologies play in people's ability to access services and participate in activities in their local area
  • analysing the distribution of services and facilities in different types of settlements (for example, using aerial images of contrasting places in Australia such as inner and outer suburbs, or rural and remote) to identify implications for people, such as access to services and availability of facilities

Waypoints

  • 1. Comparing services and facilities across settlement typesHingeStudents can compare the distribution and location of services and facilities between different settlement types — urban, rural, remote and very remote.
  • 2. Implications of service distribution for liveabilityStudents can explain implications of service and facility distribution for the liveability of places, including for people with limited access.
  • 3. Transport and technology as service access enablersStudents can explain the role transport and technologies play in people's ability to access services and participate in activities in their local area.
  • 4. Integration: distribution, access and liveabilityStudents explain the location and distribution of services and facilities and their implications for liveability — integrating distributional analysis, access factors and liveability outcomes.
