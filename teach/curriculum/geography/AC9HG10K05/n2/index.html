← AC9HG10K05 Measuring human wellbeing and development

Measurement methods: GDP, HDI, PQLI and their trade-offs

AC9HG10K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare different measurements of human wellbeing — including GDP, HDI and PQLI — explaining what each measures, how they differ, and what rankings of selected countries reveal.

Success criteria

  • Students describe what GDP, HDI and at least one other index measure, identifying the dimensions each captures
  • Students compare rankings of at least two countries across two different indices and explain the reason for a ranking difference

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Concepts of human wellbeing and development.

Width

Minimum
Describe what GDP and at least one composite index (HDI or PQLI) measure and compare rankings for two countries, explaining one difference between the rankings.
Typical
Compare GDP, HDI and PQLI for a range of countries including Australia, India and a Pacific country; explain trends in different measurements and explain why a country may rank differently across indices.
Wider
  • Evaluate more recent composite measures such as the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI), Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI) or Happy Planet Index against GDP and HDI, assessing what each adds
  • Investigate anomalies — countries that rank much higher on HDI than GDP (or vice versa) — and explain the political, historical or social factors that produce the divergence
  • Examine gender-disaggregated wellbeing data (e.g. Gender Development Index, GII) and evaluate what the disaggregation reveals that composite indices obscure

Hinge

If students cannot explain why different measures produce different rankings and what that difference means, they cannot interpret spatial variation data in K06 or explain intra-national inequality in K07.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often treat indices as interchangeable and conclude that a 'better' measure is simply a more accurate version of the same thing. An explicit comparison framework showing what each index includes/excludes and why is essential before spatial analysis.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepComparing indices across a range of countries (including countries that diverge in their relative rankings) is the primary way to develop understanding of what each index captures and what it misses.
