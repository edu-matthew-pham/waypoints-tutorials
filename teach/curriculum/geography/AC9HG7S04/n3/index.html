← AC9HG7S04 Draw geographical conclusions

Conclusions as answers to the inquiry question

AC9HG7S04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce a geographical conclusion that directly responds to the inquiry question, synthesising analytical findings into a defensible claim.

Success criteria

  • Produces a conclusion that directly responds to the inquiry question.
  • Supports the conclusion with at least two analytical findings, using geographical terminology.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Explaining impacts on people, places and environments.

Width

Minimum
Students produce a conclusion that directly answers the inquiry question and is supported by at least two pieces of analytical evidence.
Typical
Students produce a geographical conclusion that: directly responds to the inquiry question, synthesises key analytical findings, uses a geographical concept as organiser, and explains impacts on people, places or environments — presented as a coherent claim rather than a list of findings.
Wider
  • Write a conclusion at two different confidence levels — a strong conclusion when evidence is clear and a hedged conclusion when evidence is partial — practising appropriate epistemic language
  • Develop a counter-argument to a classmate's conclusion using the same data — examine what the same evidence can support if analysed differently
  • Compare the conclusion reached through quantitative data analysis with the conclusion suggested by qualitative data (e.g. survey results) for the same inquiry — examine whether they agree

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsWriting a conclusion as a coherent claim rather than a list of findings requires practice. Students need repeated opportunities to draft, receive feedback, and revise conclusions — distinguishing between 'here is what I found' (finding) and 'here is what this means' (conclusion).
