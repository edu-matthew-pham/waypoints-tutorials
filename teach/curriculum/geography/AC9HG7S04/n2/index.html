Explaining impacts on people, places and environments

AC9HG7S04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the impacts of a geographical phenomenon or challenge on people, places and environments, drawing on analysis results.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least one impact on people and one impact on places or environments, connecting each to the geographical phenomenon studied.
  • Uses analysis evidence to support each impact claim.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Geographical concepts as conclusion organisers.

Width

Minimum
Students explain at least one impact on people AND one impact on places or environments from their analysis, connected to the geographical phenomenon studied.
Typical
Students explain the impacts of a geographical phenomenon or challenge on people, places and environments — e.g. impacts of water scarcity on individuals, communities and government; or impacts of declining water quality on people and liveability of places — connecting each impact to the analytical evidence.
Wider
  • Trace a cascade of impacts: initial geographical phenomenon → first-order impact → second-order impact → systemic consequence — e.g. drought → reduced farm income → community service decline → population loss
  • Compare impacts at different scales — individual, community, government — and explain how scale shapes the nature and severity of impact
  • Examine cases where impacts are distributed unequally across groups — who bears the impact and why — connecting to broader geographical concepts of equity and sustainability

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExplaining impacts across people, places AND environments — not just one category — requires deliberate widening. Students default to human impacts. Using case studies where environmental impacts are primary (water quality, habitat loss) builds fuller impact analysis.
