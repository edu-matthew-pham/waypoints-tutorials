Draw geographical conclusions

AC9HG7S04 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

draw conclusions based on the analysis of the data and information

Prior knowledge

Students have prior evidence-based conclusion skills from F–6 HASS (AC9HS6S05: develop evidence-based conclusions). This standard requires them to draw conclusions specifically in a geographical inquiry context — using geographical concepts (place, space, environment, interconnections, sustainability, scale, change) as organisers, and connecting conclusions to the impacts of geographical phenomena on people, places and environments.

By the end

Students draw conclusions based on the analysis of geographical data and information.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • drawing on the results of an analysis and using at least one of the concepts of place, space, environment, interconnections, sustainability, scale or change as an organiser to respond to a question; for example, using an analysis of the distribution of water resources to form conclusions about the sustainability of farming, or an analysis of the location of services to form conclusions about interconnections between people, place, environment and liveability
  • explaining the impacts of a geographical phenomenon or challenge on people, places and environments; for example, impacts of water scarcity on individuals, communities and government, or the impacts of declining water quality on people and the liveability of places

Waypoints

  • 1. Geographical concepts as conclusion organisersHingeStudents can use at least one geographical concept (place, space, environment, interconnections, sustainability, scale or change) to organise and frame a geographical conclusion.
  • 2. Explaining impacts on people, places and environmentsStudents can explain the impacts of a geographical phenomenon or challenge on people, places and environments, drawing on analysis results.
  • 3. Conclusions as answers to the inquiry questionStudents can produce a geographical conclusion that directly responds to the inquiry question, synthesising analytical findings into a defensible claim.
