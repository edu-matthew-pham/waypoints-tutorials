Evaluating data: generalisations, patterns and relationships

AC9HG9S03 · Year 9 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

evaluate geographical data and information to make generalisations and predictions, explain patterns and trends and infer relationships

Prior knowledge

Students can identify patterns and trends in geographical data (Y7-8). At Y9, the moves are: making and justifying generalisations from data, making predictions from trends, and inferring causal relationships between variables.

By the end

Students evaluate geographical data to make justified generalisations and predictions, explain patterns and trends with reference to geographical factors, and infer relationships between causes and impacts represented in data.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • making generalisations about trends; for example, using questionnaires or interviews to identify people's perspectives on live food fish trade in Australia or people's access to the internet in the local area
  • explaining a pattern; for example, using the current Global Hunger Index and the updated Food and Agricultural Organization's Low-Income Food-Deficit Countries (LIFDCs) to identify locations of food scarcity and malnutrition, or comparing maps showing transport networks with survey responses on personal mobility
  • explaining relationships between causes and impacts of factors represented in data; for example, the impact of the use of Global Positioning System (GPS) and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) on the way farmers control the dispersion of fertilisers and pesticides to produce higher yields and limit run-off, or the effects of the use of GPS to construct maps on how tourists use different transport systems to visits popular places in Australia

Waypoints

  • 1. Making generalisations from dataHingeStudents can make generalisations about trends from geographical data
  • 2. Explaining patterns and trendsStudents can explain a geographical pattern or trend with reference to geographical factors
  • 3. Inferring relationships between causes and impactsStudents can explain relationships between causes and impacts of factors represented in data
