Explaining patterns and trends

AC9HG9S03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Geography · Skills

Checkpoint

Students can explain a geographical pattern or trend with reference to geographical factors

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one pattern or trend in geographical data
  • Explains the pattern with reference to at least one geographical factor
  • Uses geographical data vocabulary (trend, distribution, pattern) correctly

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Making generalisations from data.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one pattern or trend in data; explain it with reference to at least one geographical factor
Typical
Explain geographical patterns and trends in data (e.g. locations of food scarcity from hunger index maps, transport network distribution from survey data) using relevant geographical factors; connect the explanation to the inquiry question
Wider
  • Compare pattern explanations across scales — explain why the same trend appears differently at local versus global scale
  • Use multiple data sources to triangulate a pattern explanation — explain how different sources support or complicate the explanation
  • Explain a pattern that has an unexpected feature — identify what additional factor or exception the data reveals beyond the main trend

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPattern explanation requires students to move from 'I can see X in the data' to 'X occurs because of geographical factor Y'. Students who describe patterns without explaining them have not reached this node. The causal step — connecting the pattern to a geographical mechanism — needs explicit scaffolding.
