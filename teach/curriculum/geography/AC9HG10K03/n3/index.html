First Nations models of sustainability

AC9HG10K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain First Nations Australians' models of sustainability, including specific practices such as fire management, land care, species protection and community capacity building, and explain how these contribute to broader conservation.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least two First Nations sustainability practices in concrete terms, not just as labels
  • Students explain how First Nations models of sustainability contribute to conservation outcomes beyond the immediate community

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: First Nations Park Rangers and cultural knowledge in practice.

Width

Minimum
Explain at least two specific sustainability practices from First Nations models and describe how each contributes to environmental outcomes.
Typical
Explain First Nations models of sustainability including obligations to Country/Place, fire management, removal of invasive species, protection of threatened species and capacity building; connect each to broader conservation outcomes and explain how these contribute to national and international sustainability frameworks.
Wider
  • Evaluate how formal recognition of First Nations sustainability models (e.g. in national park co-management agreements, state environmental legislation) has changed management outcomes in specific regions
  • Compare First Nations sustainability models in Australia with Indigenous environmental management approaches in other countries (e.g. Māori kaitiakitanga in Aotearoa New Zealand)
  • Investigate the evidence base for specific First Nations practices — e.g. mosaic burning effects on biodiversity — and evaluate how Western science is engaging with and validating this knowledge

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates custodial responsibility, Ranger practice and sustainability frameworks into a coherent account of First Nations environmental management as a knowledge system that contributes to conservation. The teaching signal is to make the integration explicit — not to treat practices as isolated examples.
