← AC9HG10S05 Develop, evaluate and recommend strategies

Recommending a strategy with predicted impacts

AC9HG10S05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recommend a strategy, explain the predicted impacts, and reflect on how personal values and attitudes influence the strategy choice.

Success criteria

  • Students recommend a strategy and explain at least two predicted impacts (one intended, one possible unintended or trade-off consequence)
  • Students reflect on at least one personal value that influenced their strategy choice, explaining the connection

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Evaluating strategies against criteria.

Width

Minimum
Recommend one strategy, explain at least two predicted impacts, and reflect on how at least one personal value influenced the choice.
Typical
Recommend a strategy supported by reasons from the evaluation; explain predicted environmental, economic and social impacts; reflect on how personal values and attitudes — including sustainability values, social justice values or cultural values — influenced the strategy choice.
Wider
  • Develop a recommendation that explicitly addresses the trade-offs identified in Node 2 — explaining how the recommended strategy prioritises some criteria over others and why
  • Reflect on how a different value position would lead to a different strategy recommendation — making the value-strategy connection explicit and non-deterministic
  • Apply sustainable design principles to an urban or built environment strategy and explain how the design principles reflect specific values about wellbeing, environment and community

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierA strategy recommendation integrates criteria evaluation, predicted impact analysis and value reflection into a coherent geographical argument. The teaching signal is to make each component explicit in the recommendation — not a bare recommendation, but one that shows the evaluative reasoning and value position behind it.
