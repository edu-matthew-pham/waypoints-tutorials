Develop, evaluate and recommend strategies

AC9HG10S05 · Year 10 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop and evaluate strategies using environmental, economic or social criteria; recommend a strategy and explain the predicted impacts

Prior knowledge

At Y9, students developed and evaluated strategies using environmental, economic or social criteria, recommended a strategy and explained predicted impacts (AC9HG9S05). At Y10 the same descriptor applies with greater complexity: explicit reflection on personal values, evaluation of collective as well as individual action, and integration with First Nations traditional practices.

By the end

Students develop and evaluate both individual and collective strategies using environmental, economic and social criteria; recommend a strategy with explained predicted impacts; reflect on personal values and their influence on strategy choices.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • proposing individual action and supporting the strategy with reasons; for example, reducing their ecological footprint by reducing the amount of food packaging included in a packed lunch, or becoming volunteers for non-government organisations such as the Red Cross or Red Crescent to increase social connectedness
  • proposing collective action and evaluation of actions; for example, identifying ways to improve the wellbeing of remote communities of First Nations Australians and evaluating the actions proposed and implemented by the community members
  • evaluating the effectiveness of a strategy in relation to environmental, economic or social criteria; for example, reflecting on whether environment degradation has been reduced and human wellbeing improved
  • explaining reasons for decisions and choices, such as the traditional use of firestick farming by First Nations Australians to control fires, or grassroots decisions on implementation and effectiveness of aid projects
  • reflecting on personal values and attitudes and how these influence strategies; for example, applying sustainable design principles to urban redevelopment projects that provide green, open spaces for citizens, or supporting non-government organisations that reflect personal values

Waypoints

  • 1. Developing strategies — individual and collectiveStudents can propose both individual and collective strategies for addressing a geographical challenge, supporting each with reasons.
  • 2. Evaluating strategies against criteriaHingeStudents can evaluate the effectiveness of a strategy using environmental, economic and social criteria, reflecting on whether the strategy achieves its aims.
  • 3. Recommending a strategy with predicted impactsStudents can recommend a strategy, explain the predicted impacts, and reflect on how personal values and attitudes influence the strategy choice.
