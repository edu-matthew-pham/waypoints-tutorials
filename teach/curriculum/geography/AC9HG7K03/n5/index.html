← AC9HG7K03 Values of water

Integration: multiple values in tension and context

AC9HG7K03 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students explain the economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic value of water for people — applying the four-value framework to a specific case and recognising where values coexist or conflict.

Success criteria

  • Applies at least three of the four value types to a specific water feature or management case, explaining each.
  • Identifies at least one point of tension between different values held by different groups for the same water feature.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Aesthetic and cultural value of water: heritage and tourism.

Width

Minimum
Students apply at least two value types to a specific water-related context, explaining each value's source and how they relate.
Typical
Students produce an integrated explanation of a specific water feature or water management case that identifies economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic values, explains how these are expressed by different groups, and considers whether and how they coexist or conflict.
Wider
  • Analyse a water management decision (e.g. a proposed dam, a river restoration project) using the four-value framework — identify which values are advanced, which are compromised, and for which communities
  • Apply the framework to a case involving First Nations Australians and non-Indigenous water users — e.g. the Darling/Baaka River, the Daintree, or a local river — to assess whose values inform decision-making and whose are marginalised

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe four value types are taught separately but the descriptor requires students to apply them together. The integration node practises the analytical move of seeing a single water context through all four lenses simultaneously.
