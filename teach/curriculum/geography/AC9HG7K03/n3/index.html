Economic value of water: irrigation and food production

AC9HG7K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the economic value of water for irrigation-based communities and industries, using a comparison between an Australian and an Asian example.

Success criteria

  • Explains the economic role of water in an irrigation-based region using a specific Australian example.
  • Compares at least one similarity and one difference in how irrigation shapes economic life in an Australian and an Asian context.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: First Nations Australians and multilayered water values.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the economic value of water for one irrigation case study.
Typical
Students examine and compare places in Australia and a country in Asia that have economies and communities based on irrigation — e.g. the Murrumbidgee Irrigation Area in NSW and the Mekong Delta in Vietnam — identifying similarities and differences in how irrigation shapes economic and community life.
Wider
  • Analyse the virtual water embedded in food exports from irrigation regions — calculate the 'water cost' of traded agricultural products and consider what this means for water sustainability
  • Compare the economic value of irrigation water with the economic value of water for urban use — how are competing economic values prioritised and by whom?
  • Examine historical case studies where irrigation dramatically changed regional economies and societies (e.g. development of the Ord River, transformation of the MIA) and evaluate the costs and benefits

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepComparing Australian and Asian irrigation cases prevents students from treating economic water value as a purely domestic story. The Mekong Delta case also shows water's economic value in a different national and cultural context.
