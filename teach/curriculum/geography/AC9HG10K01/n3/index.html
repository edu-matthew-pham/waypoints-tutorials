Conflicting perspectives on sustainable practices

AC9HG10K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain the tensions between conflicting perspectives of individuals, communities, governments and businesses regarding the use of sustainable practices.

Success criteria

  • Students identify at least two stakeholder groups with differing perspectives on a sustainability practice and explain the basis of the disagreement
  • Students explain why resolving sustainability tensions is complex, with reference to at least one specific conflict example

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Sustainability as a concept — environmental functions.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two different stakeholder perspectives on one sustainability issue, identifying the source of tension between them.
Typical
Identify and explain tensions between the perspectives of individuals, communities, businesses and governments on sustainable practices across at least two contexts; explain why perspectives differ (values, priorities, economic interests, legal frameworks).
Wider
  • Analyse a real governance dispute (e.g. state vs federal conflict over water allocation, community vs developer conflict over land use) to trace how institutional power shapes which perspectives prevail
  • Compare how different legal systems resolve sustainability conflicts — for example, rights-based approaches vs market-based mechanisms vs regulatory approaches
  • Investigate cases where community-led sustainability initiatives succeeded or failed in shifting government policy, identifying leverage points

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExposure to multiple sustainability conflicts across different stakeholder configurations (individual/community, community/government, government/business) prevents students from assuming one conflict type is representative of all.
