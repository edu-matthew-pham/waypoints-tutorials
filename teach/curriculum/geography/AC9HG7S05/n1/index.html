Individual action in response to geographical phenomena

AC9HG7S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can propose individual action in response to a geographical phenomenon or challenge, supporting the proposal with geographical reasoning.

Success criteria

  • Proposes at least one individual action in response to a geographical phenomenon or challenge.
  • Supports the proposal with geographical reasoning that connects the action to the identified problem.

Prior knowledge

Students have prior skills proposing actions from F–6 HASS (AC9HS6S06: propose actions or responses to issues or challenges and use criteria to assess the possible effects). This standard extends that to the geographical context — proposing individual and collective action in response to geographical phenomena, explaining potential impacts, and reflecting on how personal values influence explanation of impacts.

Width

Minimum
Students propose at least one individual action in response to a geographical challenge and explain why it would be effective.
Typical
Students propose individual actions in response to geographical phenomena or challenges — e.g. reducing individual water footprint, choosing active transport for liveability — supporting each proposal with reasons connected to the geographical analysis conducted.
Wider
  • Evaluate the potential impact of scaling individual action — if many individuals took the same action, would it produce meaningful change? What scale of uptake would be needed?
  • Compare individual action to collective action for the same challenge and evaluate which is better suited to addressing the geographical cause
  • Examine cases where well-intentioned individual actions have unintended negative impacts — e.g. increased water recycling in one area reducing river flows to downstream users

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkIndividual action proposals often lack connection to the geographical analysis. The reasoning scaffold: geographical finding → mechanism connecting individual action to that finding → why this action addresses the identified cause or condition → expected impact.
