Integration: strategy, impact and values

AC9HG7S05 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students identify a strategy for action in relation to a geographical challenge and explain its potential impacts — integrating individual and collective dimensions and value-awareness.

Success criteria

  • Produces a strategy proposal with at least two potential impacts explained.
  • Reflects on at least one value dimension that shapes the impact explanation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Personal values and explanation of potential impacts.

Width

Minimum
Students produce a strategy proposal (individual or collective) and explain at least two potential impacts, noting one value dimension that influences the impact explanation.
Typical
Students produce an integrated strategy proposal that: identifies a geographical challenge, proposes individual and/or collective action, explains potential impacts across multiple dimensions, and reflects on how values shape the impact explanation — presented as a coherent, reasoned proposal rather than separate components.
Wider
  • Develop a strategy proposal that includes both individual and collective components, explains potential impacts at multiple scales, and explicitly acknowledges competing values among different stakeholders
  • Evaluate the strategy using criteria from K08 (effectiveness, feasibility, equity, environmental impact) and revise the proposal based on the evaluation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe standard brings together individual action, collective action and value-awareness into a single strategy proposal with impact explanation. Integration practises producing this as a connected geographical argument rather than three separate tasks.
