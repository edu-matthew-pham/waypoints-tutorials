Effects of migration on places

AC9HG8K08 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the effects of internal and international migration on places of origin and destination, including the relationship between migration and urban concentration.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two effects of migration on a named place of origin or destination.
  • Interprets population data to describe the relationship between migration and urban concentration in at least one country.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Reasons for migration.

Width

Minimum
Students explain one effect of migration on a place of origin or destination.
Typical
Students explain the effects of migration on places of origin and destination — population growth in mining regions, cultural diversity in cities, urban concentration patterns — and interpret population data to describe the relationship between international migration and urban concentration in Australia, and internal migration and urban concentration in China.
Wider
  • Examine the brain drain effect — how migration of skilled workers affects places of origin.
  • Investigate how migrant communities reshape the cultural character of destination places (language, cuisine, religious infrastructure).
  • Compare how the effects of internal migration in China differ from international migration into Australia, considering scale and policy context.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to bring together migration typology, reasons and spatial patterns into an explanation of effects on places — including using population data as evidence for the migration-urbanisation relationship.
