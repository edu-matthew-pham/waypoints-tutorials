Interpret geographical data

AC9HG8S03 · Year 8 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret and analyse geographical data and information to identify similarities and differences, explain patterns and trends and infer relationships

Prior knowledge

Students interpret and analyse geographical data to identify similarities and differences, explain patterns and trends and infer relationships (AC9HG7S03). At Y8, more complex data sets and multi-scale analysis are expected.

By the end

Students interpret and analyse geographical data to identify similarities and differences, explain patterns and trends, and infer relationships from primary research and complex secondary sources.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying similarities and differences from data and information; for example, comparing source and destination of international migration for Australia in 2 different time periods
  • explaining patterns and trends; for example, comparing compound graphs or census data for different time periods to identify patterns in urban concentration in Australia and the United States of America, or trends in international migration
  • inferring relationships from data and information collected during primary research; for example, using observations, field sketches, field measurements, questionnaires or interviews to explain the distribution of population in your local area and suggesting possible causes, effects and trends

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying similarities and differencesStudents can identify and describe similarities and differences from geographical data and information.
  • 2. Explaining patterns and trendsHingeStudents can explain patterns and trends in geographical data, using data representations such as compound graphs and census data.
  • 3. Inferring relationshipsStudents can infer relationships from primary and secondary geographical data, using observations, measurements and other evidence to suggest causes, effects and trends.
