← AC9HG8S02 Collect, organise and represent data

Representing data using digital tools

AC9HG8S02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 8 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can represent relevant and reliable data and information in appropriate formats using digital tools and cartographic conventions.

Success criteria

  • Represents geographical data in at least one appropriate format using a digital tool.
  • Applies basic cartographic conventions (title, scale, legend, orientation) to the representation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: First Nations consultation protocols.

Width

Minimum
Students represent data in at least one appropriate digital format conforming to basic cartographic conventions.
Typical
Students represent spatial distributions and other geographical data using appropriate formats (annotated diagrams, digital mapping tools, distribution maps at different scales) that conform to cartographic conventions, combining data from primary and secondary sources.
Wider
  • Create a multi-layer digital map combining demographic, topographic and qualitative data.
  • Evaluate the cartographic choices made in a published map — what does the choice of projection, classification or symbology communicate?
  • Investigate how animated or interactive maps can represent change over time.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierRepresenting data requires students to select, evaluate and organise findings from multiple sources into a coherent cartographic product — integrating methods, source evaluation and geographic knowledge.
