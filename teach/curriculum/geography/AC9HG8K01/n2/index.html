Endogenic processes

AC9HG8K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how tectonics, volcanism, folding and faulting produce major landforms.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Names and explains at least two endogenic processes (e.g., folding, volcanism) and links each to a named landform.
  • Distinguishes endogenic processes (driven by internal Earth energy) from surface processes.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Landscape diversity.

Width

Minimum
Students explain one endogenic process (tectonic, volcanic, fold or fault) with a named landform example.
Typical
Students explain how plate tectonics drives volcanism, folding and faulting, linking each process to a named landform example (e.g., MacDonnell Ranges — folding; Great Sumatran Fault — faulting; Krakatoa — volcanism).
Wider
  • Compare landforms produced by convergent vs divergent plate boundaries.
  • Investigate how the same tectonic setting can produce different landform types (e.g., both mountains and rift valleys).
  • Examine how volcanic activity produces landscape diversity beyond just volcanic cones — lava plains, calderas, geothermal landscapes.

Hinge

If students conflate endogenic and exogenic processes, or treat all landforms as surface erosion products, they cannot accurately explain the formation of major landscapes in Node 3 or the hazard origins in AC9HG8K05.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a clear conceptual model distinguishing internal (endogenic) from external (exogenic) forces before they can correctly attribute landform origins. A process-to-landform reasoning chain — plate boundary type → stress/movement → resulting structure — prevents conflation.
