Landscape diversity

AC9HG8K01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe the diversity of landscape types at the global and national scale, including wetlands, grasslands, forests, and deserts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies and names at least three distinct landscape types with a located example for each.
  • Describes at least one distinguishing feature that differentiates each landscape type.

Prior knowledge

Students explain how water connects and changes places as it moves through environments (AC9HG7K01), and the causes and impacts of atmospheric/hydrological hazards (AC9HG7K04). They have not yet encountered tectonic, volcanic, or rock-weathering processes at this scale.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least three landscape types with one example location each.
Typical
Students describe a range of landscape types (wetlands, grasslands, forests, hot and cold deserts) with national and international examples, explaining what distinguishes each type visually and environmentally.
Wider
  • Compare landscape distributions across multiple continents, identifying climate–landscape relationships.
  • Examine how landscape classification systems differ (e.g., ecological vs geomorphological framings).
  • Investigate transition zones between landscape types and why boundaries are rarely sharp.
