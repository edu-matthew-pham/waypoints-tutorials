Geomorphological processes

AC9HG8K01 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

geomorphological processes that produce different landscapes and significant landforms

Prior knowledge

Students explain how water connects and changes places as it moves through environments (AC9HG7K01), and the causes and impacts of atmospheric/hydrological hazards (AC9HG7K04). They have not yet encountered tectonic, volcanic, or rock-weathering processes at this scale.

By the end

Students explain geomorphological processes — including tectonics, volcanism, folding, faulting, weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition — that produce different landscapes and significant landforms.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the diversity of landscapes, such as wetlands, grasslands, forests, cold and hot deserts, and landforms at the national scale; for example, mountains – Himalayan Mountains, Nepal; grasslands – the Steppe, central Asia; forests – Daintree, Australia; cold deserts – Gobi, China
  • explaining how tectonics, volcanism, folding, faulting, chemical weathering and physical weathering such as erosion, transportation and deposition shape places; for example, folding – MacDonnell Ranges, Northern Territory, Australia; faulting – Great Sumatran Fault (Semangko Fault), Indonesia; volcanism – Krakatoa, Indonesia
  • explaining the effects of rock type on a selected landform at the local scale; such as Fraser Island, Queensland or Twelve Apostles, Victoria; for example, sedimentary – igneous and metamorphic; chemical weathering – oxidation and solution; physical weathering – exfoliation and frost wedging
  • explaining the effects of erosion, transportation and deposition of water and wind on a selected landform at the local scale; for example, Fraser Island, Queensland, formed by wind, waves and ocean currents; the Twelve Apostles, Victoria, formed by erosion, tides and ocean currents

Waypoints

  • 1. Landscape diversityStudents can identify and describe the diversity of landscape types at the global and national scale, including wetlands, grasslands, forests, and deserts.
  • 2. Endogenic processesHingeStudents can explain how tectonics, volcanism, folding and faulting produce major landforms.
  • 3. Weathering processesHingeStudents can explain how chemical and physical weathering break down rock to shape landforms, including the role of rock type.
  • 4. Erosion, transportation and depositionStudents can explain how erosion, transportation and deposition by water and wind shape landforms, using a selected local-scale example.
  • 5. Process integrationStudents can explain how multiple geomorphological processes interact to produce landscapes, selecting and applying appropriate process explanations to named landforms at different scales.
