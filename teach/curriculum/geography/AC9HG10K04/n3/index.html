← AC9HG10K04 Environmental change: causes, effects and management strategies

Proposing and comparing management strategies

AC9HG10K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can propose strategies to manage the effects of environmental change and compare strategies in terms of environmental, economic and social criteria, including cross-national comparison where appropriate.

Success criteria

  • Students propose at least one management strategy and evaluate it against at least two criteria (environmental, economic, social)
  • Students explain a trade-off involved in their proposed strategy and acknowledge why different stakeholders may evaluate the trade-off differently

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: World views and strategy development.

Width

Minimum
Propose at least one management strategy for the studied context and evaluate it against at least one criterion (environmental, economic or social).
Typical
Propose and evaluate multiple strategies using environmental, economic and social criteria; compare strategies in Australia with strategies in another country for human-induced environmental change, using stated criteria and acknowledging trade-offs.
Wider
  • Compare three strategy types — environmental (marine reserves, national parks), spatial (corridors, urban planning) and holistic (addressing past and present causes) — evaluating the criteria each type is best suited to address
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of a real implemented strategy over time using before/after evidence, identifying what was achieved and what remained unresolved
  • Develop and justify a prioritised strategy recommendation that explicitly addresses the different criteria and trade-offs involved, including acknowledgment of First Nations approaches where relevant

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates cause-effect analysis, world view awareness and multi-criteria evaluation into a strategy proposal. The teacher signal is to make the connections explicit: a good strategy proposal flows directly from the cause-effect analysis (Node 1) and accounts for the stakeholder world views identified in Node 2.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStrategy comparison requires applying consistent criteria across cases — students often compare incomparable things (effectiveness vs cost vs acceptability) without acknowledging they are using different criteria. Scaffold the criteria framework before asking for comparison.
