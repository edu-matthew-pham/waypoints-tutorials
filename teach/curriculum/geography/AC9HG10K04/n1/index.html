← AC9HG10K04 Environmental change: causes, effects and management strategies

Identifying context and describing causes

AC9HG10K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify a specific environmental change context, describe its causes, and explain the impacts on the sustainability of the environment's functions.

Success criteria

  • Students identify a specific environmental change context and describe at least two causes with their mechanisms
  • Students explain the impact of the change on at least one environmental function using correct geographical terminology

Prior knowledge

Students understand human-induced environmental changes and sustainability (AC9HG10K01), environmental world views (AC9HG10K02), and First Nations custodial approaches (AC9HG10K03). They have engaged with sustainability strategies for biomes (AC9HG9K04) and urban places (AC9HG8K09), providing a prior framework for evaluating management strategies.

Width

Minimum
Describe the causes of environmental change in one identified context and explain impacts on at least one environmental function (resource, service or spiritual).
Typical
Describe the causes of environmental change in a studied context across local, national or global scale as appropriate; explain impacts on multiple environmental functions including resource provision, ecological services, and cultural or spiritual value.
Wider
  • Investigate a complex multi-causal environmental change case (e.g. Murray-Darling Basin water stress, Great Barrier Reef bleaching) tracing the interaction of natural and human factors at different scales
  • Analyse how scale of study changes the apparent causes and effects — for example, localised agricultural runoff vs national water policy vs global climate change as causes of the same environmental change
  • Examine an environmental change case in a developing country context and compare the causes and affected environmental functions with an equivalent change in Australia

Hinge

Without an accurate and specific account of causes and affected environmental functions in their studied context, students cannot evaluate whether proposed strategies actually address the right problem — the entire evaluation chain (nodes 2–3) depends on the cause-effect analysis being correct.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often list causes without tracing causal mechanisms or explaining which environmental functions are affected and how. Scaffolding the cause → mechanism → function impact chain explicitly prevents superficial cause listing.
