Collect, represent and compare geographical data

AC9HG10S02 · Year 10 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

collect, represent and compare data and information from primary research methods, including fieldwork and secondary research materials, using geospatial technologies and digital tools as appropriate

Prior knowledge

At Y9, students collected, represented and compared data using primary and secondary methods, geospatial technologies and digital tools (AC9HG9S02). At Y10 the additional complexity involves evaluating sources for relevance, reliability and bias, respecting First Nations consultation protocols, and representing multi-variable data using geospatial and digital tools.

By the end

Students collect data from primary and secondary research methods, evaluate sources for relevance, reliability and bias, respect First Nations consultation protocols where relevant, and represent multi-variable data using geospatial technologies and digital tools.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying primary research methods, including fieldwork, to collect original materials; for example, survey and interviews regarding perspectives on environmental management at the local scale, or strategies to improve human wellbeing of First Nations Australians at the national and local scale
  • collecting relevant secondary research materials online using targeted criteria; for example, "allintext: critically endangered list by country" or "allintext: targets for reducing hunger or increasing access to health care"
  • identifying and respecting protocols for consultation with communities of First Nations Australians when planning and conducting investigations; for example, acknowledging their earth-centred world view and how their traditional knowledges contribute to environmental management projects, or considering cultural and spiritual wellbeing of First Nations Australians when implementing programs to reduce economic and social inequality
  • evaluating primary or secondary research materials for relevance (for example, "Does the information reflect current thinking?"), reliability (for example, "Who is/are the author/s? Does the author reference other experts in the field?") and bias, such as information bias presenting one side of an issue, or selection bias presenting information on the positive aspects of foreign aid with cultural and social issues not considered
  • comparing findings from primary research with those from secondary research materials for relevance and reliability
  • representing multi-variable data using geospatial technologies; for example, using scatterplots to visually represent data for countries to demonstrate the correlation between 2 variables, such as comparing adult literacy with GDP per capita in United Arab Emirates or Bhutan
  • representing multi-variable data using digital tools; for example, generating pie graphs showing threats to biodiversity; using digital photographs to indicate differences in material goods between people and places

Waypoints

  • 1. Primary research methods including fieldworkStudents can identify and use primary research methods, including fieldwork, to collect original materials relevant to their inquiry.
  • 2. Evaluating sources for relevance, reliability and biasHingeStudents can evaluate primary and secondary research materials for relevance, reliability and bias, including identifying different types of bias.
  • 3. Representing multi-variable dataStudents can represent multi-variable data using geospatial technologies and digital tools.
