Identifying liveability enhancement strategies

AC9HG7K08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify strategies implemented in Australia or Europe to improve the liveability of a place and explain what aspect of liveability each targets.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two distinct liveability enhancement strategies from Australia or Europe, naming the strategy and the liveability problem it addresses.
  • Explains what aspect of liveability (physical, social, environmental, access) each strategy targets.

Prior knowledge

Students have completed K05–K07 — they can explain factors influencing residential decisions, analyse service distribution and its liveability implications, and explain how cultural connectedness shapes belonging and place perceptions. This standard is the synthesis point of the liveability unit — applying all prior conceptual work to the evaluation of strategies that improve liveability for specific community groups.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least two liveability strategies from Australia or Europe and explain what liveability problem each addresses.
Typical
Students identify strategies implemented in Australia or a country in Europe to improve liveability — including improving public spaces, walkability, transport connections, water disposal and recycling — explaining what each targets and considering applicability to their own locality.
Wider
  • Research a specific European city known for liveability enhancement (e.g. Vienna, Copenhagen, Amsterdam) and analyse which strategies contribute to its high liveability ratings and whether they are transferable to Australian cities
  • Compare strategies targeting physical infrastructure (transport, public space) with strategies targeting social infrastructure (community programs, cultural facilities) — which approach has greater liveability impact and for whom?
  • Investigate strategies in Australian cities that have improved liveability for specific groups — e.g. 20-minute neighbourhood planning, age-friendly city programs, disability access audits

Hinge

Students who cannot identify a range of strategy types will evaluate liveability improvement narrowly (defaulting to infrastructure). This limits the evaluation capacity needed in node 3, where assessing applicability requires understanding which strategies address which liveability dimensions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepWidening to European exemplars alongside Australian cases gives students a richer strategy repertoire and allows them to see liveability enhancement as a field of active planning practice rather than an abstract aspiration.
