Integration: liveability strategies and diverse communities

AC9HG7K08 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students explain strategies used to enhance the liveability of a place for diverse community groups — integrating strategy identification, needs analysis and evaluation.

Success criteria

  • Produces an analysis of a liveability strategy that addresses community group needs, strategy design, and applicability evaluation.
  • Draws on at least two liveability concepts from K05–K07 in the analysis.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Evaluating strategy applicability.

Width

Minimum
Students produce an analysis of a liveability strategy that identifies targeted groups, explains how the strategy addresses their needs, and evaluates its applicability.
Typical
Students produce an integrated analysis of a liveability strategy that: identifies the place context, explains the diverse community needs being addressed, describes the strategy and how it targets those needs, evaluates its effectiveness and applicability to the local context, and draws on at least one Australian or European comparative example.
Wider
  • Develop an original liveability improvement proposal for a specific local place — including community needs assessment, strategy design, criteria evaluation and applicability argument — drawing on K05–K08 content throughout
  • Compare two Australian cities' liveability enhancement approaches (e.g. Melbourne vs Brisbane, or a capital vs a regional city) and evaluate which approach better addresses the needs of the most disadvantaged community groups

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierK08 is the synthesis standard for the entire liveability unit. The integration node requires students to draw on all liveability concepts — measurement, distribution, cultural connectedness, diverse needs — in a single coherent analysis of a place-based strategy.
