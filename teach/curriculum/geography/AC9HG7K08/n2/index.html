← AC9HG7K08 Strategies for liveability

Diverse community needs and strategy design

AC9HG7K08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop or describe a strategy to improve an aspect of liveability that takes into account the needs of diverse groups, including young people, the aged, people with disability, or Traditional Owners.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the specific liveability needs of at least two different community groups for a proposed or analysed strategy.
  • Explains how the strategy design addresses or should address those specific needs.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying liveability enhancement strategies.

Width

Minimum
Students explain how at least one community group's specific needs have been or could be addressed by a liveability strategy.
Typical
Students develop a strategy (or examine an existing one) to improve an aspect of liveability at the local scale, taking into account the needs of at least two diverse groups — including young people, elderly residents, people with disability, or Traditional Owners — and explaining how the strategy addresses group-specific needs.
Wider
  • Develop a bilingual or culturally-responsive signage proposal for a local area in consultation with First Nations Australians or other cultural communities — examine what this signals about whose belonging is recognised
  • Design an inclusive public space that addresses the specific needs of young people, elderly residents and people with disability simultaneously — where do needs align and where do they create design tensions?
  • Research Universal Design principles and evaluate how well an existing local space meets them, proposing specific improvements

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDesigning for diverse groups requires students to adopt multiple perspectives simultaneously. Using fieldwork in a real local space makes the design challenge concrete and prevents abstract generalisation.
