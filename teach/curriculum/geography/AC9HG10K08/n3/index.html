Evaluating program effectiveness

AC9HG10K08 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the effectiveness of programs to improve wellbeing, connecting program outcomes to the causes of wellbeing inequality identified in earlier standards.

Success criteria

  • Students evaluate a program against its stated objectives, identifying at least one strength and one limitation with evidence
  • Students connect the program's approach to at least one identified cause of wellbeing inequality, explaining whether or not the program addresses that cause

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Programs and their objectives.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate the effectiveness of one program against its stated objectives, identifying at least one strength and one limitation.
Typical
Evaluate programs in terms of whether they address the identified causes of wellbeing inequality; consider whether outcomes match objectives; acknowledge limitations of evaluation data (selectivity, short-term framing, aid dependency risks).
Wider
  • Apply a consistent evaluation framework (e.g. OECD-DAC criteria: relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, impact, sustainability) to compare two programs in the same region
  • Investigate a program that has been independently evaluated and compare self-reported outcomes with external evaluation findings — examining the gap and what produces it
  • Explore critiques of aid and development programs more broadly — evaluating whether program approaches address structural causes of inequality or focus on symptoms

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierProgram evaluation integrates understanding of wellbeing causes (K05-K07) with program analysis — a good evaluation requires students to connect the program's approach to the actual drivers of the wellbeing issue it addresses. The teaching signal is to make this connection explicit rather than treating evaluation as stand-alone.
