Organisational responses to improve human wellbeing

AC9HG10K08 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

responses of international and national government and non-government organisations to improve human wellbeing in Australia, within India and another country in the Pacific

Prior knowledge

Students understand causes and consequences of wellbeing variation at global scale (AC9HG10K05), within a country in Asia (AC9HG10K06), and within Australia including for First Nations Australians (AC9HG10K07). They are positioned to evaluate organisational responses against the causes identified in those earlier standards.

By the end

Students identify and explain responses of government and non-government organisations to improve human wellbeing in Australia, India and a Pacific country, evaluating programs against their objectives and outcomes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying and describing a national, state or community program to reduce regional inequalities in human wellbeing in a country such as Papua New Guinea or Indonesia
  • explaining the objectives and outcomes of an overseas economic and social development program by the Australian Government (for example, AusAID) or a non-government overseas aid program (for example, World Vision) in India or a country in the Pacific
  • identifying and explaining ways to improve the wellbeing of remote communities of First Nations Australians, including ways proposed by the communities

Waypoints

  • 1. Types of organisations and their approachesStudents can identify the types of organisations that respond to wellbeing issues — international (e.g. UN agencies), national government (e.g. AusAID equivalents), and non-government (e.g. World Vision, Red Cross) — and explain how their mandates and approaches differ.
  • 2. Programs and their objectivesStudents can identify and explain the objectives and outcomes of specific programs to reduce wellbeing inequality in Australia, India and a Pacific country.
  • 3. Evaluating program effectivenessStudents can evaluate the effectiveness of programs to improve wellbeing, connecting program outcomes to the causes of wellbeing inequality identified in earlier standards.
