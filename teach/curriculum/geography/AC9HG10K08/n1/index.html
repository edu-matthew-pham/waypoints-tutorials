← AC9HG10K08 Organisational responses to improve human wellbeing

Types of organisations and their approaches

AC9HG10K08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the types of organisations that respond to wellbeing issues — international (e.g. UN agencies), national government (e.g. AusAID equivalents), and non-government (e.g. World Vision, Red Cross) — and explain how their mandates and approaches differ.

Success criteria

  • Students distinguish between at least two types of organisations (government, NGO, international) by mandate and approach
  • Students explain how an organisation's type shapes what it can and cannot do to improve wellbeing

Prior knowledge

Students understand causes and consequences of wellbeing variation at global scale (AC9HG10K05), within a country in Asia (AC9HG10K06), and within Australia including for First Nations Australians (AC9HG10K07). They are positioned to evaluate organisational responses against the causes identified in those earlier standards.

Width

Minimum
Describe the role of at least two different types of organisation (e.g. one government, one NGO) and explain how their approach to improving wellbeing differs.
Typical
Identify and describe the different mandates, funding sources, target populations and methods of international government organisations, national government programs and NGOs; explain how the type of organisation shapes the approach taken.
Wider
  • Examine tensions between different organisational types — for example, where government programs and NGO programs in the same country or community have conflicting priorities or methods
  • Investigate how organisations coordinate (or fail to coordinate) in complex humanitarian or development contexts — examining the UN coordination architecture for a specific crisis
  • Research the growth of social enterprise and community-led organisations as a third approach between government and NGO models, with examples from Australia's First Nations sector

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often conflate government programs and NGOs, or assume all aid organisations do the same thing. An explicit framework distinguishing types by mandate, funding, accountability and target population prevents blurring that undermines evaluation in Node 2.
