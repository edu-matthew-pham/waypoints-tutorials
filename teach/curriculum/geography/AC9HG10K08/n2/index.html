Programs and their objectives

AC9HG10K08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain the objectives and outcomes of specific programs to reduce wellbeing inequality in Australia, India and a Pacific country.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Students identify at least one specific program in each of the three contexts (Australia, India/regional, Pacific) and describe its objectives
  • Students explain what each program targets and who the intended beneficiaries are

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Types of organisations and their approaches.

Width

Minimum
Describe one specific program in at least one country context, identifying its objectives and what it targets.
Typical
Identify and explain programs in each of the three mandated contexts (Australia — including First Nations wellbeing; India or regional inequalities; Pacific country); describe program objectives, target populations and intended outcomes for each.
Wider
  • Research how AusAID's objectives and methods evolved over time and compare its approach to wellbeing improvement with that of an equivalent bilateral aid agency (e.g. USAID, DFID/FCDO)
  • Investigate the evidence base for a specific NGO program (e.g. World Vision's Zonal Health Program, Red Cross WASH programs) by examining reported outcomes data
  • Examine community-proposed ways to improve First Nations wellbeing in Australia and compare these with government-initiated programs, identifying points of alignment and divergence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe descriptor requires three country contexts — students need genuine engagement with programs in each, not a single case study applied across all three. The teaching signal is to ensure coverage of all three contexts with sufficient specificity.
