Causes of spatial inequality

AC9HG10K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe the economic, social, technological, political and environmental causes of spatial variations in wellbeing within India or another Asian country compared to Australia.

Success criteria

  • Students identify at least two types of causes (drawing from economic, social, technological, political or environmental) of spatial inequality in the studied country
  • Students explain how each identified cause contributes to the spatial pattern of wellbeing variation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Spatial patterns of wellbeing within a country.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two causes of spatial inequality in wellbeing within the studied country and explain how each contributes to the spatial pattern observed.
Typical
Describe economic (uneven investment, industrial concentration), social (education access, healthcare distribution), technological (connectivity gaps), political (historical marginalisation, policy focus) and environmental (climate, resource access) causes of spatial wellbeing inequality; compare causes in India or another Asian country with causes in Australia.
Wider
  • Investigate how colonial history shaped current spatial inequalities — examining how British colonial infrastructure development in India produced long-lasting geographic economic concentrations
  • Analyse a specific policy or investment decision (e.g. Special Economic Zones, infrastructure corridors) and evaluate its effects on the spatial distribution of wellbeing
  • Examine intersectionality in causes — how caste, gender and geography interact in India to compound wellbeing disadvantage in specific regions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCause identification is enriched by comparison — seeing that different countries have different spatial inequality profiles driven by different cause combinations builds understanding of causation as complex and contextual, not universal.
