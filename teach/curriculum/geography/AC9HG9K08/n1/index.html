Effects of production on people, places and environments

AC9HG9K08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine the effects on people, places and environments of mining, farming, forestry and the production of manufactured goods

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two production types and their effects on places
  • Distinguishes between environmental, economic and social effects
  • Names specific affected groups or places rather than speaking in generalities

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how production and consumption affect places (Y7-8). They have examined trade interconnections in K07. They have not analysed full supply chains end-to-end, evaluated the environmental effects across a supply chain, or examined sustainability management strategies for production and consumption impacts at scale.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two production types (mining, farming, forestry, manufacturing); explain one environmental, economic or social effect of each on the place of production
Typical
Explain the environmental, economic and social effects of mining, farming, forestry or manufactured goods production on the places where they occur; identify who is affected and how
Wider
  • Compare the effects of two different production types (e.g. mining vs forestry) on the same type of place — explain what differs in their environmental footprints
  • Examine the cumulative effects of multiple production activities in a single region — explain how effects interact
  • Investigate how the effects of production in low-income countries differ from those in high-income countries — explain using regulation, labour conditions and environmental standards

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe descriptor lists four production types (mining, farming, forestry, manufactured goods). Students who examine only one have an incomplete picture of the range of impact types. Multiple production contexts ensure the node's building block function for the supply chain analysis in Node 2.
