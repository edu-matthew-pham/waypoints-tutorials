Collect, organise and represent geographical data

AC9HG7S02 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

collect, organise and represent data and information from primary research methods, including fieldwork and secondary research materials, using geospatial technologies and digital tools as appropriate

Prior knowledge

Students have prior data collection and organisation skills from F–6 HASS (AC9HS6S02: locate, collect and organise information and data from primary and secondary sources in a range of formats). This standard introduces geospatial technologies, specialist digital tools, cartographic conventions and the distinction between primary fieldwork methods and secondary research materials in a specifically geographical context.

By the end

Students collect, organise and represent geographical data from primary and secondary sources using appropriate geospatial technologies, digital tools and cartographic conventions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying appropriate primary research methods, including fieldwork, and using specialised digital tools to collect original materials; for example, interview and survey data, photographs of streetscapes, annotated field sketches, diagrams and statistics related to liveability
  • collecting relevant secondary research materials, such as print and online publications, photographs and images, using advanced search functions; for example, 'allintitle: community opinion on water scarcity in Australia' or 'Australia's most liveable city'
  • representing relevant data and information in appropriate formats to combine ideas; for example, applying primary research to the design of a questionnaire or survey on what is meant by liveability, with results presented in a table or graph
  • representing spatial distribution of different types of geographical phenomena by constructing appropriate maps at different scales that conform to cartographic conventions, for example using computer mapping to show the spatial distribution of impacts of hydrological hazards on environments

Waypoints

  • 1. Primary fieldwork methods for geographical dataHingeStudents can identify appropriate primary research methods for a geographical inquiry, including fieldwork, and use specialised digital tools to collect original materials.
  • 2. Secondary research: finding and selecting relevant materialsStudents can collect relevant secondary research materials using advanced search functions, evaluating relevance and reliability.
  • 3. Representing data in appropriate geographical formatsHingeStudents can represent geographical data and information in formats appropriate to the data type and inquiry question — including tables, graphs, and geospatial representations.
  • 4. Integration: collect, organise and represent in an inquiry contextStudents collect, organise and represent data from primary and secondary sources using appropriate geospatial and digital tools — producing a coherent evidence base for a geographical inquiry.
