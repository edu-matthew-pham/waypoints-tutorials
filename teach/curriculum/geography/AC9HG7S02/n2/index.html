Secondary research: finding and selecting relevant materials

AC9HG7S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can collect relevant secondary research materials using advanced search functions, evaluating relevance and reliability.

Lessons on this site

  • Where to look, and how to search

Success criteria

  • Locates at least two secondary sources relevant to a geographical inquiry question and explains the criteria used to assess each source's relevance.
  • Uses at least one advanced search method to find sources and reflects on how it improved search results.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Primary fieldwork methods for geographical data.

Width

Minimum
Students locate at least two relevant secondary sources for a geographical inquiry and explain why each is relevant.
Typical
Students collect relevant secondary research materials — print and online publications, photographs, images, statistical datasets — using advanced search functions (e.g. specific field search operators) and explain the criteria used to assess relevance and reliability.
Wider
  • Compare two secondary sources on the same topic (e.g. two reports on water scarcity in a region) and evaluate their reliability, currency and perspective
  • Examine the difference between primary and secondary use of the same material — e.g. an aerial photograph used as primary evidence of current land use vs used as secondary evidence from a historical archive
  • Practise advanced search operators to narrow results to relevant sources and evaluate whether the results are genuinely more useful

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsReliable source selection requires repeated practice with explicit criteria. Students need multiple search exercises with structured reflection on what was found and why it was or was not suitable.
