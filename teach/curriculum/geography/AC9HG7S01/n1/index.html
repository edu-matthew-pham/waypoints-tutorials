Geographical questions vs general questions

AC9HG7S01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Geography · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish a geographical inquiry question from a general question, identifying the geographical phenomenon, concept or challenge it targets.

Lessons on this site

  • Can this be investigated?

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two features that make a question geographical and applies these to classify example questions.
  • Transforms at least one general question into a geographical inquiry question, explaining what geographical dimension has been added.

Prior knowledge

Students have developed general inquiry questions in F–6 HASS (AC9HS6S01: develop questions to investigate people, events, developments, places and systems). This standard requires them to apply question development specifically within a geographical inquiry framework — questions must be oriented to geographical phenomena, use geographical concepts as organisers, and be plannable as a geographical investigation.

Width

Minimum
Students identify what makes a question geographical (targets a spatial phenomenon, process, distribution or place relationship) and classify examples as geographical or non-geographical.
Typical
Students explain the features that make a question geographical — it investigates a spatial phenomenon, process, place relationship, environmental challenge or human-environment interaction — and practise transforming general questions into geographical questions by grounding them in place, distribution or environmental process.
Wider
  • Examine a set of real geographical inquiry questions from ACARA exemplars or published geographical research and identify what makes each question productive as a geographical inquiry starter
  • Compare geographical questions at different scales (local, national, global) and explain how scale shapes what evidence is needed to answer the question
  • Develop a question that uses one of the ACARA geographical concepts (place, space, environment, interconnections, sustainability, scale, change) as an explicit organiser and explain how the concept shapes the question

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish geographical from general questions will develop inquiry questions that do not orient the investigation geographically. The resulting investigation plan (node 2) will lack geographical focus, making all subsequent skills application disconnected from geographical inquiry.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe distinction requires explicit attention to what kind of answer a question invites. Geographical questions invite spatial, environmental or place-based analysis — not just any investigation. Scaffolding: what place/process/distribution does this question target? What evidence would answer it?
