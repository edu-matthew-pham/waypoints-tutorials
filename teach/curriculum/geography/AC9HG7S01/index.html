Develop geographical inquiry questions

AC9HG7S01 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop questions for a geographical inquiry related to a phenomenon or challenge

Prior knowledge

Students have developed general inquiry questions in F–6 HASS (AC9HS6S01: develop questions to investigate people, events, developments, places and systems). This standard requires them to apply question development specifically within a geographical inquiry framework — questions must be oriented to geographical phenomena, use geographical concepts as organisers, and be plannable as a geographical investigation.

By the end

Students develop questions to guide a geographical inquiry into a phenomenon or challenge.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • developing questions to investigate why a geographical phenomenon has changed or a challenge may arise; for example, the causes of water scarcity in different places, or measuring the liveability of a place and the factors affecting the liveability of a place
  • planning an investigation of a geographical phenomenon or challenge being studied, using digital planning tools; for example, analysing statistics on variation in water quantity and quality over time in Central Australia, or using fieldwork to survey perceptions of the liveability of a local place

Waypoints

  • 1. Geographical questions vs general questionsHingeStudents can distinguish a geographical inquiry question from a general question, identifying the geographical phenomenon, concept or challenge it targets.
  • 2. Developing questions for a specific phenomenon or challengeHingeStudents can develop questions to investigate why a geographical phenomenon has changed or a challenge may arise, producing questions that are specific, investigable and geographically grounded.
  • 3. Planning a geographical investigationStudents can plan a geographical investigation, identifying the evidence needed, how it will be collected, and appropriate digital planning tools.
