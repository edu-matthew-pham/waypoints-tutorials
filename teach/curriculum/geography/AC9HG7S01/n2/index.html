← AC9HG7S01 Develop geographical inquiry questions

Developing questions for a specific phenomenon or challenge

AC9HG7S01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Geography · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop questions to investigate why a geographical phenomenon has changed or a challenge may arise, producing questions that are specific, investigable and geographically grounded.

Lessons on this site

  • Build a question from a big idea

Success criteria

  • Develops at least two geographical inquiry questions about a specific phenomenon or challenge that are specific and investigable with real evidence.
  • Explains what geographical phenomenon, process or concept each question targets.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Geographical questions vs general questions.

Width

Minimum
Students develop at least two investigable geographical questions about a specific phenomenon or challenge, each of which could be pursued with real evidence.
Typical
Students develop questions to investigate a geographical phenomenon or challenge — e.g. the causes of water scarcity in different places, or the factors affecting liveability — producing questions that are specific, investigable with available evidence, and oriented to geographical concepts (cause, distribution, change, interconnection, sustainability).
Wider
  • Develop a question sequence — an initial question and follow-up questions that deepen or extend the inquiry based on what answers to the initial question might reveal
  • Develop questions at different scales for the same phenomenon (e.g. water scarcity questions at local, national and global scale) and explain how different questions require different evidence
  • Compare questions that investigate causes vs questions that investigate impacts vs questions that investigate responses — examine how question type shapes what an investigation can reveal

Hinge

The quality of inquiry questions determines the quality of the investigation. Vague or non-investigable questions produce investigations that cannot generate meaningful evidence or conclusions. All subsequent skills (S02–S06) depend on having a sharp inquiry question to work from.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDeveloping investigable questions requires iterative refinement — students produce a draft question, test it for specificity and investigability, refine it, and test again. Consolidation through peer critique and teacher feedback cycles builds the precision needed.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPractising question development across both water and liveability contexts — the two major content units — prevents question development being tied to a single topic.
