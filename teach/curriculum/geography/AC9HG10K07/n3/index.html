← AC9HG10K07 Spatial variations in wellbeing in Australia

Place, interconnection and wellbeing outcomes

AC9HG10K07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how a person's wellbeing is influenced by where they live, with reference to interconnections of environmental, economic, social and technological factors in at least two different Australian places.

Success criteria

  • Students explain how at least two interconnected factors shape a person's wellbeing in a specific Australian place
  • Students identify how the wellbeing experience differs between at least two different Australian places and explain the geographical basis of that difference

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Causes of wellbeing variation — environmental, economic, social, technological.

Width

Minimum
Explain how interconnections between at least two factors (e.g. economic and social) shape wellbeing outcomes for people in one specific Australian place.
Typical
Explain how a person's wellbeing is shaped by their place through the interconnection of environmental, economic, social and technological factors; demonstrate this for at least two different Australian places, including consideration of ways to improve wellbeing of remote First Nations communities including community-proposed approaches.
Wider
  • Develop a detailed interconnection web for a specific place showing how multiple factors interact and compound — identifying which factor interactions are most significant for wellbeing outcomes
  • Investigate and evaluate specific strategies proposed or implemented by First Nations communities themselves to improve wellbeing, assessing how community agency shapes outcomes
  • Compare how two different government policy approaches to regional wellbeing in Australia have fared — for example, comparing place-based investment strategies with targeted individual support schemes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates spatial pattern reading, factor analysis and the geographical concept of place into a holistic account of wellbeing as place-shaped. Students need to connect the factors identified in Node 2 into a coherent explanation of how interconnections produce a place-specific wellbeing outcome.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe descriptor requires at least two different places — the comparison is integral, not optional. Teaching should ensure students have genuine comparative material, not just a single case study.
