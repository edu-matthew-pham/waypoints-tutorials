← AC9HG10K07 Spatial variations in wellbeing in Australia

Causes of wellbeing variation — environmental, economic, social, technological

AC9HG10K07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the environmental, social, economic and technological factors that influence wellbeing and create spatial variation across Australian places, including factors specific to First Nations Australians' wellbeing.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least two factors from different domains (environmental, economic, social, technological) that contribute to wellbeing variation between named Australian places
  • Students explain at least one factor specific to First Nations Australians' wellbeing, connecting it to a historical or structural cause

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Spatial patterns of wellbeing in Australia.

Width

Minimum
Identify and explain at least two factors from different domains (e.g. one economic, one social) that contribute to wellbeing variation between two Australian places.
Typical
Explain how environmental (access to resources, climate), social (health and education services), economic (employment, income) and technological (ICT access) factors interact to create wellbeing variation across Australian places; explain the additional and intersecting factors that shape First Nations Australians' wellbeing, including Closing the Gap initiatives.
Wider
  • Investigate how historical policies (stolen generations, land alienation, forced relocation) created structural disadvantages that persist as causes of current wellbeing gaps for First Nations Australians
  • Examine the concept of the 'tyranny of distance' as a driver of regional disadvantage in Australia and evaluate policy responses that have attempted to address it
  • Analyse a specific remote community's wellbeing profile and trace the full factor set — geographical, historical, economic, social — that produces that profile

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFactor analysis is enriched by comparing two very different Australian places — e.g. a remote First Nations community vs an outer suburban area vs a regional town — to show that the same factor domain (e.g. economic) operates very differently in different geographic contexts.
