Spatial variations in wellbeing in Australia

AC9HG10K07 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

reasons for, and consequences of, spatial variations in human wellbeing in Australia, including for First Nations Australians

Prior knowledge

Students understand spatial wellbeing variations at the regional/national scale using international cases (AC9HG10K06) and are familiar with wellbeing measurement tools (AC9HG10K05). They have engaged with First Nations custodial approaches (AC9HG10K03) and Australia's internal migration and urban/rural dynamics (AC9HG8K06, AC9HG8K08).

By the end

Students explain the reasons for and consequences of spatial variations in human wellbeing in Australia, including identifying causes for First Nations Australians' wellbeing data and explaining how a person's wellbeing is influenced by where they live, with reference to interconnections of environmental, economic, social and technological factors in at least two different places.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the environmental factors (access to resources – fossil fuels, water, fertile soils), the social factors (adequate food, health and education services), the economic factors (employment, income) and the technological factors (information and communications technology) that influence human wellbeing and development between and within countries
  • interpreting and analysing similarities, differences, patterns and trends in human wellbeing data for communities of First Nations Australians compared to non-Indigenous Australians, and explaining the links between human wellbeing and Closing the Gap initiatives
  • explaining how a person's wellbeing is influenced by where they live, with reference to interconnections of environmental, economic, social and technological factors in at least 2 different places in Australia, such as urban and remote places

Waypoints

  • 1. Spatial patterns of wellbeing in AustraliaHingeStudents can describe the spatial pattern of wellbeing variation within Australia, identifying areas of higher and lower wellbeing and recognising the particular pattern for First Nations Australians.
  • 2. Causes of wellbeing variation — environmental, economic, social, technologicalStudents can explain the environmental, social, economic and technological factors that influence wellbeing and create spatial variation across Australian places, including factors specific to First Nations Australians' wellbeing.
  • 3. Place, interconnection and wellbeing outcomesStudents can explain how a person's wellbeing is influenced by where they live, with reference to interconnections of environmental, economic, social and technological factors in at least two different Australian places.
