← AC9HG9K04 Challenges to sustainable food production

Economic, social and First Nations food security impacts

AC9HG9K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine economic and social impacts of changes to food production, including impacts on First Nations Australians' access to traditional food sources

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two economic or social impacts of changes to food production
  • Explains how modifications to biomes have affected First Nations Australians' access to traditional food sources
  • Names at least one specific First Nations food source and explains the impact on its availability

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Environmental challenges to food production.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two economic or social impacts of changes to food production; identify at least one impact on First Nations Australians' access to traditional food sources
Typical
Explain economic impacts (competing land uses — urban, industrial, recreational) and social impacts of changes to food production; explain how modifications to biomes have reduced First Nations Australians' access to traditional food sources such as myrrnong, cycads or bunya nuts
Wider
  • Analyse how competing land uses create different types of food security challenges — urban expansion versus agricultural degradation affect different populations differently
  • Investigate a specific First Nations food source (e.g. myrrnong/yam daisy in Victoria) — trace how biome modification reduced its availability and explain the cultural and nutritional significance of this loss
  • Examine how First Nations food sovereignty movements are reclaiming access to traditional food sources — explain what 'food security' means in a First Nations context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFood security impacts are experienced differently across economic, social and cultural dimensions. Addressing economic and social impacts without the First Nations dimension produces an incomplete picture. Multiple contexts ensure students understand that food security is not a single, uniform challenge.
