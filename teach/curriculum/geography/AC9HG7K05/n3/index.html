Local access and perceptions of liveability

AC9HG7K05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare their own access to and use of places in their local area and evaluate how this affects perceptions of liveability.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two local area features that influence perceptions of liveability, distinguishing between objective access and subjective perception.
  • Compares their own liveability perception with at least one other perspective, explaining what accounts for any difference.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Liveability: concept and measurement.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least two aspects of their local area that affect their perception of its liveability.
Typical
Students compare their own access to and use of places and spaces in their local area with classmates and community members, evaluating how different levels of access to the same physical environment produce different perceptions of liveability.
Wider
  • Survey classmates on liveability perceptions and map variation — examine what predicts difference in perception (age, mobility, transport access, cultural background)
  • Compare a local area's liveability as perceived by young people, elderly residents and people with disability — what spaces are accessible or inaccessible to each group?
  • Conduct a walkability audit of the local area and relate findings to liveability perceptions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMoving beyond personal experience to compare perceptions across different population groups in the same place makes visible how access determines perception — the central insight of this node.
