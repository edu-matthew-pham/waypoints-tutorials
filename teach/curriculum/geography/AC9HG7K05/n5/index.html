Integration: factors, liveability and environmental quality

AC9HG7K05 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students explain factors that influence residential decisions — integrating economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic factors with liveability measurement and environmental quality analysis.

Success criteria

  • Produces an explanation of a residential decision that addresses multiple factor types and explicitly applies the liveability concept.
  • Distinguishes objective and subjective elements in the liveability analysis.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Environmental quality and residential decisions.

Width

Minimum
Students produce an explanation of a residential decision context that addresses at least two factor types and applies the liveability concept.
Typical
Students produce an integrated explanation of why people make specific residential decisions — combining factor type analysis, objective and subjective liveability measures, and environmental quality considerations — applied to a specific place or population group.
Wider
  • Apply the integrated framework to a complex case: e.g. the decision of a family to move from urban Sydney to a regional town — map all factor types, liveability measures and environmental quality considerations, and explain the decision
  • Evaluate how the factors that drive residential decisions differ for First Nations Australians (where spiritual and cultural factors may be primary) vs recently arrived migrants (economic and cultural safety factors) in the same city

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe standard brings together factor types, liveability measurement, local perception and environmental quality. Integration practises weaving these into a coherent explanation of a real residential decision rather than listing four separate considerations.
