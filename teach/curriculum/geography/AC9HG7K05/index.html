Liveability decisions

AC9HG7K05 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

factors that influence the decisions people make about where to live, including perceptions of the liveability of places and the influence of environmental quality

Prior knowledge

Students enter the liveability unit having completed the water unit (K01–K04). They understand that places have environmental, economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic dimensions of value, and that water distribution creates differential access for populations in different settlement types. They have not previously encountered the concept of 'liveability' or the objective/subjective measurement distinction.

By the end

Students explain factors that influence where people live, including perceptions of liveability and the influence of environmental quality.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining how the economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic value of places influence choices about where to live; for example, economic – working for industries located in remote and very remote places; culture – connections for cultural groups; spiritual – meanings attributed to places; aesthetic – 'bright lights' attraction or tree change
  • discussing the concept of liveability and the ways it is measured, and comparing objective measures, such as transportation infrastructure with subjective measures, such as people's perceptions
  • comparing students' access to and use of places and spaces in their local area, and evaluating how this affects perceptions of liveability
  • examining the influence of environmental quality on decisions people make about where to live; for example, clean land, air and water, views, recreation facilities and favourable climate

Waypoints

  • 1. Factors influencing residential location decisionsHingeStudents can identify and explain multiple types of factors (economic, cultural, spiritual, aesthetic) that influence decisions about where to live.
  • 2. Liveability: concept and measurementHingeStudents can explain the concept of liveability, distinguish between objective and subjective measures, and compare examples of each.
  • 3. Local access and perceptions of liveabilityStudents can compare their own access to and use of places in their local area and evaluate how this affects perceptions of liveability.
  • 4. Environmental quality and residential decisionsStudents can explain how environmental quality influences decisions about where to live, using examples of specific environmental quality factors.
  • 5. Integration: factors, liveability and environmental qualityStudents explain factors that influence residential decisions — integrating economic, cultural, spiritual and aesthetic factors with liveability measurement and environmental quality analysis.
