Environmental, economic and social impacts of tourism

AC9HG9K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the environmental, economic and social impacts of tourism on places and predict how future trends may affect these impacts

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one environmental, one economic and one social impact of tourism
  • Explains the mechanism of each impact rather than just naming it
  • Makes a prediction about how a future tourism trend will affect a place

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Causes and growth of global tourism.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one environmental, one economic and one social impact of tourism on a named place; explain the mechanism of each impact
Typical
Explain the environmental (habitat degradation, pollution, carbon footprint), economic (income, employment, dependency) and social (cultural change, displacement, commodification) impacts of tourism on places popular with tourists; predict how space tourism or post-COVID recovery may affect places
Wider
  • Examine pilgrimage tourism (e.g. Mecca, Vatican City, Varanasi) as a distinct case — explain how religious tourism produces different impact profiles compared with mass leisure tourism
  • Analyse how overtourism affects a specific place — identify which impacts are reversible and which are not
  • Predict how space tourism could affect near-Earth orbital environments — extend the impact framework to a new frontier

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish environmental, economic and social impacts — and explain their mechanisms — cannot evaluate whether management strategies address the right impact type in Node 3. Conflating all impacts as generically 'bad for the environment' prevents strategy evaluation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkImpact categorisation (environmental/economic/social) is the organising framework for this descriptor and for S05 strategy evaluation. Students who cannot distinguish impact types with mechanism explanations produce undifferentiated impact lists. Scaffolding the three-category structure with mechanism for each ensures students have the analytical framework Node 3 requires.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPilgrimage and space tourism are both named in the elaborations as prediction contexts. These extend the impact framework beyond the familiar leisure tourism case and give advancing students genuinely different impact profiles to analyse.
