Human alteration of biomes

AC9HG9K02 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the effects on environments of human alteration of biomes to produce food, industrial materials and fibres

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how people modify environments for agriculture and other purposes (Y7-8) and can describe some effects of these modifications. They have not analysed how biome alteration affects flows of nutrients, water and biodiversity systematically, nor compared natural and agricultural ecosystems structurally.

By the end

Students identify biomes that produce food and industrial materials, explain differences between natural and agricultural ecosystems in nutrient and water flows and biodiversity, and explain how agricultural innovations have increased productivity through biome alteration in Australia and Asia.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the biomes in Australia and a country in Asia that produce some of the foods and plant material people consume
  • explaining the differences between natural and agricultural ecosystems in flows of nutrients and water, and in biodiversity; for example, the tropical rainforest biome in Indonesia produces food such as fruit, grains, nuts, vegetables and spices, and non-food products such as wood, rubber, coffee, chocolate and palm oil
  • explaining how human alteration of biomes (for example, drip irrigation, fertilisers, pesticides, genetically modified seeds, agrobiotics, terracing, and controlling erosion and overgrazing) has increased agricultural productivity in Australia and a country in Asia

Waypoints

  • 1. Biomes as sources of food and industrial materialsStudents can identify the biomes in Australia and a country in Asia that produce major food and non-food products
  • 2. Natural vs agricultural ecosystems: nutrient flows, water and biodiversityHingeStudents can explain differences between natural and agricultural ecosystems in nutrient flows, water flows and biodiversity
  • 3. Agricultural innovations and productivityStudents can explain how human alteration of biomes through specific agricultural innovations has increased productivity in Australia and a country in Asia
