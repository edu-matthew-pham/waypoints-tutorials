Hydrological and atmospheric hazards

AC9HG7K04 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the causes and impacts of an atmospheric or hydrological hazard, and responses from communities and governments

Prior knowledge

Students have completed K01–K03 — they understand water as an environmental resource, can explain water distribution and sustainability, and know that water movement causes environmental change. They have basic knowledge of climate and weather from primary school but have not previously studied hazard processes in depth or examined governmental and community response frameworks.

By the end

Students explain the causes and impacts of an atmospheric or hydrological hazard and responses from communities and governments.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the environmental processes that cause a hazard, such as drought, storms, tropical cyclones or floods
  • explaining how the impacts of a hazard on people and the environment are influenced by environmental, social or economic factors
  • identifying examples of responses to a hazard from the community and the government at the local scale, and identifying practices that increased effectiveness
  • reflecting on the principles of prevention, mitigation and preparedness in responses from the community and the government to explain how the impact of a hazard can be reduced

Waypoints

  • 1. Hazard environmental processes and causesHingeStudents can explain the environmental processes that cause an atmospheric or hydrological hazard, using accurate geographical terminology.
  • 2. Factors influencing hazard impactsHingeStudents can explain how the impacts of a hazard on people and the environment are influenced by environmental, social or economic factors.
  • 3. Community and government responsesStudents can identify and describe examples of community and government responses to a hazard at the local scale, explaining what made some responses effective.
  • 4. Prevention, mitigation and preparedness frameworkStudents can apply the prevention, mitigation and preparedness framework to explain how community and government responses can reduce hazard impact.
  • 5. Integration: causes, impacts and responsesStudents explain the causes and impacts of an atmospheric or hydrological hazard and responses from communities and governments — integrating process analysis, impact factors and response framework.
