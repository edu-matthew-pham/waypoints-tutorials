Digital access and the digital divide

AC9HG9K05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret differences in people's access to the internet between and within countries and explain how technologies connect people to information, services and others

Success criteria

  • Identifies that internet access varies between and within countries
  • Describes at least two factors contributing to the digital divide
  • Explains how internet access affects connection to at least two types of services or information

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Transport and information networks: supply chains.

Width

Minimum
Identify that internet access varies between and within countries; describe at least two factors that produce the digital divide; explain how internet access affects connection to services and information
Typical
Interpret data showing differences in internet access between and within countries (including rural Australia and an Asian country); explain how technologies bridge or widen the digital divide; discuss how communications infrastructure enables collaboration
Wider
  • Analyse how the digital divide affects specific population groups differently — compare rural Australians, urban Australians and people in low-income Asian countries using real data
  • Discuss how communications infrastructure enables Indigenous and marginalised communities to maintain cultural connections across distances (e.g. Masai, Inuit, Yanomami) — what does connectivity mean in this context?
  • Investigate mobile-first internet access as a bridging technology in low-income countries — explain how mobile penetration outpaces fixed broadband in parts of Asia

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe digital divide is only visible when comparing across contexts — within Australia (rural/urban), between Australia and Asia, and across income levels. A single context produces an incomplete picture. The elaboration's reference to rural areas across Australia and an Asian country signals the need for multi-context treatment.
