Transport, technology and connections to services

AC9HG9K05 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the ways changing transportation and technologies are used to connect people to services, information and people in other places

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how technologies connect people and places and identify that transport and communication systems vary (Y7-8). They have not examined how changing transportation and technologies shape differential access to services across population groups, nor examined supply-chain logistics or the digital divide specifically.

By the end

Students examine how transport and information networks connect people to goods, services and other people, interpret differences in access to the internet between and within countries, and explain how technologies connect people across places including globally distributed business services.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • discussing how access to transportation affects the ways people perceive, use and are connected to specific services or opportunities in a place; for example, regional flights to travel to capital cities and to international destinations
  • identifying and describing how transport and information networks function to connect people to goods and services (for example, from cotton crop to t-shirts or from farm to table), including how supply-chain logistics influence these connections
  • discussing how communications infrastructure and networks support people to collaborate; for example, local people working for social sustainability and inclusion such as the Masai in Kenya, Berber in North Africa, San or Kalahari Bushmen in southern Africa, Uyghurs in China, Ainu in Japan, Inuit in Greenland and Yanomami in the Amazon Basin
  • interpreting differences in people's access to the internet between and within countries, such as in rural areas across Australia and across the world, including a country of Asia, and explaining how technologies are used to connect people to information, services and other people
  • examining how technologies have made it possible for places to provide a range of global business services, such as businesses operating call centres in India and the Philippines

Waypoints

  • 1. Transport access and perceptions of placeStudents can discuss how access to transportation affects the ways people perceive, use and are connected to specific services or opportunities
  • 2. Transport and information networks: supply chainsHingeStudents can identify and describe how transport and information networks function to connect people to goods and services, including how supply-chain logistics influence these connections
  • 3. Digital access and the digital divideStudents can interpret differences in people's access to the internet between and within countries and explain how technologies connect people to information, services and others
  • 4. Global business services and technological interconnectionStudents can examine how technologies have made it possible for places to provide a range of global business services
