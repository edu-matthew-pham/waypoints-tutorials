Integration: water as classifier, connector and change agent

AC9HG7K01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how water is classified as an environmental resource and how its movement through environments connects and changes places — applying the classification framework, connectivity concept and change processes to a specific geographical context.

Lessons on this site

  • Putting it together: classify, connect, change

Success criteria

  • Produces an explanation of a specific water-related context that applies the resource classification framework, explains a connection between places, and identifies a change process.
  • Uses accurate geographical terminology throughout and names specific places, water forms and processes.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Moving water as an agent of landscape change.

Width

Minimum
Students produce an explanation that draws on at least two of the three concepts (classification, connection, change) for a specific water-related geographical context.
Typical
Students produce an integrated explanation of a water-related geographical context that applies the resource classification framework, traces the connectivity between places, and identifies a change process — making explicit the relationship between the three concepts.
Wider
  • Apply all three concepts simultaneously to a complex case: e.g. the Murray-Darling Basin as a system where water classification (competing uses and scarcity), connectivity (rivers linking states and ecosystems) and change (salinity, erosion, altered flow regimes) operate together
  • Evaluate a real or proposed water management intervention using the three-concept framework to assess what it changes about classification (access), connectivity (flow alteration) and physical change (erosion, sedimentation)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe three concepts in this descriptor — classification, connection and change — are typically taught as sequential nodes but the descriptor requires them to work together in explanation. The integration node is where students practise moving between the three simultaneously rather than applying each in isolation.
