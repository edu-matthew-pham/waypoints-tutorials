Developing geographical responses

AC9HG8S06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop a response that uses representations of data and information to support actions and conclusions.

Success criteria

  • Develops a response that integrates at least one data representation to support a conclusion or action.
  • Uses appropriate geographical terms, concepts and reference sources within the response.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Constructing geographical explanations.

Width

Minimum
Students develop a response that includes at least one data representation to support a conclusion or action.
Typical
Students develop a response using representations of data and information — e.g., a map showing the location of iconic landforms, a flow map showing international refugee movement — to support proposed actions and conclusions, demonstrating appropriate use of geographical knowledge, methods, concepts, terms and reference sources.
Wider
  • Integrate multiple data representations (map, graph, diagram) into a coherent response.
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of different representation types for communicating the intended geographic message.
  • Develop a multimodal response that combines written, visual and cartographic elements.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDeveloping a full geographical response integrates description (Node 1), explanation (Node 2) and data representation into a coherent product that demonstrates the full range of geographical skills from the inquiry process.
