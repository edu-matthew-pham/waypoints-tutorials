Inclusion, exclusion and sense of belonging in places

AC9HG7K07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify different places where people feel included or excluded and explain how this affects perceptions of liveability.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one example of inclusion and one of exclusion in a specific place, naming the group affected in each case.
  • Explains how feelings of safety, threat, inclusion or exclusion affect perceptions of a place's liveability.

Prior knowledge

Students have completed K05 and K06 — they understand liveability as concept, know that cultural factors influence residential decisions, and can analyse implications of service distribution. From K03 they understand multilayered values (including spiritual/cultural) of water for First Nations Australians. This standard extends that understanding to place identity, belonging and inclusion/exclusion more broadly.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least one example of a place where a group feels included and one where a group feels excluded, explaining how each affects liveability perception.
Typical
Students identify different places where people feel included or excluded, safe or threatened, and explain how these experiences of belonging or exclusion affect perceptions of a place's liveability — recognising that the same physical place can produce different belonging experiences for different groups.
Wider
  • Examine how the built environment signals inclusion or exclusion — e.g. bilingual signage, accessible design, culturally appropriate facilities — and consider what physical changes signal whose belonging matters
  • Analyse a specific case of a place that has become associated with inclusion for one group and exclusion for another (e.g. a park that young people use for gathering but where elderly residents feel unsafe)
  • Research how gentrification alters belonging experiences — long-term residents may be economically displaced from places central to their cultural identity

Hinge

Understanding that belonging is place-specific and group-specific is the foundation for understanding First Nations connectedness to Country/Place in node 2. Without grasping that the same place produces different belonging experiences for different groups, students cannot engage with the specificity and depth of First Nations place relationships.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents tend to treat safety and inclusion as universal — if a place is safe/inclusive for them, it is for everyone. Explicitly scaffolding the perspective-taking required to understand differential belonging experiences is the key teaching move here.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepUsing multiple examples across age groups, cultural groups and settlement types ensures students understand that inclusion/exclusion in place is a widespread geographical phenomenon, not an edge case.
