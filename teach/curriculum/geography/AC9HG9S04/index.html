Justifying conclusions

AC9HG9S04 · Year 9 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

evaluate data and information to justify conclusions

Prior knowledge

Students can draw conclusions from geographical data (Y7-8). At Y9, the moves are: drawing conclusions about the impact of a geographical challenge on people, places and environments; and justifying conclusions by reflecting on the perspectives and reasons behind those perspectives.

By the end

Students evaluate data and information to draw and justify conclusions about geographical challenges, reflecting on environmental, economic and social perspectives and the reasons for these perspectives.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • drawing conclusions about the impact of a geographical challenge on people, places and environments; for example, investigating the causes of a decline in food species, its impacts on food security and the establishment of the Svalbard Global Seed Vault, or the effects of cyberattacks on technological interconnections and implementation of international laws related to cyber security
  • justifying conclusions by reflecting on perspectives identified and reasons for these perspectives; for example, considering environmental, economic and social factors when challenging disappearing arable land converted from food production to non-food crops, or promoting ecotourism that impacts on people and places

Waypoints

  • 1. Drawing conclusions about geographical challengesHingeStudents can draw conclusions about the impact of a geographical challenge on people, places and environments
  • 2. Justifying conclusions through perspective reflectionStudents can justify conclusions by reflecting on perspectives identified and the reasons for these perspectives
