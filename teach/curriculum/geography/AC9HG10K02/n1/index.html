Human-centred and earth-centred world views

AC9HG10K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between human-centred (anthropocentric) and earth-centred (ecocentric) environmental world views and explain what each implies about the relationship between humans and the environment.

Success criteria

  • Students define both human-centred and earth-centred world views using correct terminology
  • Students identify at least one management approach associated with each world view and explain the connection

Prior knowledge

Students can explain conflicting stakeholder perspectives on sustainability (AC9HG10K01) and understand human-induced environmental change types. They have engaged with interconnections between human activity and environments (AC9HG9K02, AC9HG9K08) and understand that different people have different relationships to place.

Width

Minimum
Define both world view types and describe one management approach associated with each.
Typical
Explain the philosophical basis and practical implications of human-centred and earth-centred world views; link each to concrete management approaches such as resource extraction vs conservation, economic development vs protected areas.
Wider
  • Investigate intermediate world views such as stewardship ethics or biocentric ethics that complicate the binary, evaluating how each positions human responsibility
  • Examine how legal frameworks in different countries encode world views — for example, rights of nature legislation in Ecuador or New Zealand vs resource-rights regimes
  • Compare how Indigenous world views differ from both human-centred and earth-centred Western frameworks, particularly regarding relational ontologies

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish these world views cannot explain why different stakeholders choose different management methods in K04, nor can they discuss whether environmental change is 'necessarily a problem' — a question whose answer depends entirely on one's world view.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe human-centred/earth-centred distinction is conceptually slippery — students often assume everyone shares their own world view. Use explicit comparison frameworks (value source, moral standing of nature, justification for management) to make the distinction concrete before applying it.
