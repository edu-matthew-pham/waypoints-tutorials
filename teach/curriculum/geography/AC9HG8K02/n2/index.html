What makes landscapes iconic

AC9HG8K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe what makes Australian landscapes and landforms iconic, including their distinctive visual and environmental characteristics.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two characteristics that make a selected landscape or landform distinctive or iconic.
  • Compares at least one Australian landscape type with an equivalent landscape in another country.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating Australian landscapes.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least one iconic landscape or landform and explain what distinguishes it.
Typical
Students identify and describe the characteristics that make selected Australian landscapes and landforms iconic — physical form, rarity, scale, distinctiveness — and compare the distribution of Australian landscapes with those of another country.
Wider
  • Examine how 'iconicity' is socially constructed — what criteria make a place iconic and who decides?
  • Compare iconic landscape features in Australia with analogous features internationally (e.g., Australian desert vs Mongolian steppe).
  • Investigate how tourism industries shape and amplify the iconic status of certain landscapes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExamining iconic landscapes across multiple contexts — different regions of Australia and internationally — prevents students from treating one example as definitively 'what iconic looks like' and builds capacity for comparative geographic thinking.
