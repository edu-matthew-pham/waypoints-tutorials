← AC9HG9K07 Trade interconnections at all scales

National, regional and global trade interconnections

AC9HG9K07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine how and why places are interconnected nationally, regionally and globally through trade in goods and services

Success criteria

  • Identifies trade interconnections at national, regional and global scales
  • Explains at least two reasons why places trade at different scales
  • Gives specific examples from Australia's trade relationships

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Local trade: businesses selling and buying across places.

Width

Minimum
Identify how places are interconnected through trade at national, regional and global scales; explain at least two reasons why places trade across scales
Typical
Explain why places specialise in particular goods or services and how comparative advantage, resource endowment and market demand drive trade interconnections at national, regional and global scales; give examples from Australia and its trading partners
Wider
  • Analyse why Australia exports particular goods (iron ore, coal, agricultural products) but imports others — explain using resource endowment and comparative advantage
  • Examine regional trade agreements (e.g. ASEAN, Pacific trade agreements) — explain how agreements shape the geography of trade interconnections
  • Investigate the role of global cities (e.g. Singapore, Hong Kong) as trade hubs — explain how their location and infrastructure concentrate trade interconnections

Hinge

Understanding why places trade — comparative advantage, resource endowment, market demand — is prerequisite for explaining how interconnections change over time in Node 3 and why production impacts fall where they do in K08. Students who can only describe that trade happens without explaining why cannot trace the logic of changing interconnections.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe 'how and why' framing in the descriptor signals that description alone is insufficient. Students who list trade examples without explaining the mechanisms (why does Australia export iron ore rather than steel?) cannot answer the 'why' component. Scaffolding the comparative advantage and resource endowment logic builds the explanatory depth the descriptor requires.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCovering local, national, regional and global scales prevents students from conflating scale levels. Each scale has different trade logic, and students who understand only one level cannot generalise.
