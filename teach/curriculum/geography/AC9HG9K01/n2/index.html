← AC9HG9K01 Distribution and characteristics of biomes

Global distribution and mapping

AC9HG9K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Geography · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the distribution of major terrestrial and aquatic biomes and map their patterns at a global and Australian scale

Success criteria

  • Correctly locates at least five major biomes on a world map
  • Describes the broad latitudinal distribution pattern of terrestrial biomes
  • Identifies major biomes present in Australia and one country in Asia

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Biome concept and classification.

Width

Minimum
Identify the location of major biomes on a world map and a map of Australia; describe broad latitudinal patterns in biome distribution
Typical
Explain latitudinal and continental patterns in biome distribution using climate factors; identify the major aquatic and terrestrial biomes of Australia compared with a country in Asia
Wider
  • Analyse why the same latitudinal band can contain different biomes (e.g. coastal versus interior) — explain using rainfall and continentality
  • Use digital mapping tools (e.g. Google Earth, GIS) to explore biome boundaries and transitions (ecotones) at regional scale
  • Compare biome distribution maps at different time periods to identify shifts attributable to climate change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDistribution is best understood through multiple spatial contexts. Mapping at global, regional and Australian scales — and comparing with an Asian country — builds the spatial literacy that the elaborations require. Students who only see one map produce overgeneralised pattern descriptions.
