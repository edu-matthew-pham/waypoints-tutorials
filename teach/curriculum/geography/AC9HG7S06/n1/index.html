Geographical descriptions with concepts and terminology

AC9HG7S06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create geographical descriptions that use accurate terminology, reference geographical concepts, and connect the audience to the topic.

Success criteria

  • Produces a geographical description that uses at least three geographical terms accurately.
  • References at least one geographical concept explicitly in the description.

Prior knowledge

Students have prior communication skills from F–6 HASS (AC9HS6S07: present descriptions and explanations, drawing ideas, findings and viewpoints from sources, and using relevant terms and conventions). This standard raises the bar specifically for geographical communication — incorporating geographical methods (maps, graphs, data representations), geographical concepts, specialised terminology, and reference to sources as integrated elements of geographical writing and presentation.

Width

Minimum
Students produce a geographical description that uses at least three accurate geographical terms and references at least one geographical concept.
Typical
Students create descriptions that connect the audience to a geographical topic and use geographical concepts and terms accurately — e.g. describing a preferred liveability strategy using terms such as distribution, accessibility, walkability, sustainability, and concepts such as place or interconnections.
Wider
  • Compare a geographical description written for a specialist audience (planners, geographers) with one written for a general community audience — examine how terminology use and concept explanation differ
  • Evaluate the precision of geographical terminology in a description — identify any terms used imprecisely and revise for accuracy
  • Analyse a published geographical description (e.g. from a geography journal, planning document) and identify how concept use and terminology contribute to its persuasiveness

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate and consistent use of geographical terminology requires practice and feedback. Students frequently use technical terms loosely. Consolidation through targeted vocabulary exercises and peer review of terminology precision builds reliable usage.
