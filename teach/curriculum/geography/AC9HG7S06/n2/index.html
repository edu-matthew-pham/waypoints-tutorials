← AC9HG7S06 Create geographical descriptions and explanations

Geographical explanations supported by research findings

AC9HG7S06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 Geography · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Geography: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct geographical explanations that use research findings (data, maps, statistics) to support ideas.

Lessons on this site

  • Explain with evidence

Success criteria

  • Produces a geographical explanation that integrates at least two research findings as evidence within the explanation (not just appended).
  • Explains how each piece of evidence supports the geographical idea being argued.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Geographical descriptions with concepts and terminology.

Width

Minimum
Students produce an explanation that uses at least two research findings as evidence and connects them to the geographical idea being explained.
Typical
Students construct explanations using research findings to support ideas — e.g. data on water usage over time and at different places, or information about liveability indexes — ensuring that evidence is integrated into the explanation rather than listed separately.
Wider
  • Construct an explanation using evidence from at least three different source types (quantitative data, map, qualitative interview/survey) and evaluate how the diversity of evidence types strengthens the explanation
  • Revise an explanation that makes claims without evidence — add appropriate research findings and evaluate how the revision changes the persuasiveness of the explanation
  • Analyse the role of maps as evidence in a geographical explanation — examine how spatial data is different from statistical data as an evidence type

Hinge

Students who cannot integrate research findings into explanations produce claims-without-evidence, which fails the basic requirement of geographical communication. This is the skill that distinguishes geographical explanation from opinion, and it is required by all subsequent assessment tasks.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsThe move from presenting evidence and then making a claim (list-then-argue) to integrating evidence within an explanation (evidence-embedded argument) requires explicit practice. Consolidation through multiple explanation drafting and revision cycles builds this integration.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPractising evidence integration across both water and liveability content — and across different evidence types (maps, statistics, survey data) — prevents students from treating evidence integration as specific to one topic or data format.
