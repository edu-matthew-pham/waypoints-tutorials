How data travels through a network

AC9TDI6K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 6 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Networks and security

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how data is structured into packets and transmitted through multiple devices from source to destination

Lessons on this site

  • Messages in pieces

Success criteria

  • Explains that data is broken into packets for network transmission
  • Describes that packets travel through multiple devices to reach the destination

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How separate systems connect to form networks.

Width

Minimum
Explain that data is broken into packets for transmission and these packets travel from the source through multiple devices to the destination
Typical
Describe how data is broken into packets, transmitted through multiple intermediate devices, and reassembled at the destination; explain why breaking data into packets is useful (e.g. packets can take different routes, errors affect only one packet)
Wider
  • Investigate what happens when a packet is lost or delayed — how does the destination device detect missing data and request retransmission?
  • Compare how packet size and number of intermediate devices (hops) affects transmission speed and reliability in different network types

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe packet model is counter-intuitive — students expect data to travel as a single stream like water through a pipe. Tracing a message as packets through a diagrammed network (source → router → router → destination) makes the multi-step transmission legible and challenges the 'direct connection' mental model.
