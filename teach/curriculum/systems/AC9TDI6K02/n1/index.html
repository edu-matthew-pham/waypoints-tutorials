How separate systems connect to form networks

AC9TDI6K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 6 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Networks and security · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how separate digital systems can be connected in different ways to exchange data

Lessons on this site

  • Joining computers

Success criteria

  • Names at least two ways a device can connect to a network
  • Explains that connecting devices allows data to be exchanged between them

Prior knowledge

Students can explore the transmission of different types of data between digital systems (AC9TDI4K02). They understand that data can move between devices. They have not yet examined how networks are structured, how devices connect to form them, or how data is structured for transmission (packets).

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two ways a device can connect to a network (e.g. cable, Wi-Fi) and explain that connecting devices allows them to exchange data
Typical
Explain how a laptop or device can connect to a network via a physical cable or radio waves (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth); compare when each type of connection is more appropriate
Wider
  • Investigate how satellite phones provide network access where mobile phone networks are unavailable, such as in remote Arnhem Land communities — explaining why different network infrastructure is needed in these locations
  • Compare how the same device (e.g. a laptop) uses different connection types in different situations — wired at school, Wi-Fi at home, mobile data travelling — and why the network experience differs

Hinge

Students who have not grasped that separate devices connect to form networks cannot follow the packet transmission model in N2 — the path a packet travels requires understanding there is a network of connected devices for it to traverse.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly confuse the connection type (cable vs wireless) with the network itself — thinking the wire is the internet rather than a path to it. Multiple connection contexts (home, school, remote area) help students see that the network is the shared infrastructure, not the physical medium.
