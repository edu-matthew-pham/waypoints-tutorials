Data types and representations

AC9TDI4K03 · Year 4 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Digital systems

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise different types of data and explore how the same data can be represented differently depending on the purpose

Prior knowledge

Students can represent data using pictures, symbols, numbers and words (AC9TDI2K02). They have explored data as objects and symbols at Foundation level. They have not yet explored how the same data can be represented in multiple ways or why one representation might suit a purpose better than another.

By the end

Students recognise different types of data (numbers, text, symbols, images) and explain how the same data can be represented in different ways, giving reasons why particular representations suit particular purposes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • describing different types of data and how they can be used, for example numbers, letters, symbols and pictures
  • explaining how the same data can be represented in different ways and why some representations are better than others in certain contexts, for example four vs 4 vs IV vs I I I I vs quatre and that numerals are better for calculation than words
  • explaining that the same information can be represented differently, for example the term 'stop' can also be represented with an octagon-shaped red sign or a hand icon
  • identifying rock paintings and other cultural expressions to understand that images are used to encode and represent ethnobotanical knowledge, for example the representation of plant use in the Kimberley cave paintings and ancient engravings including important data on medicinal and food plant classification and their usable parts

Waypoints

  • 1. Types of dataStudents can recognise and name different types of data — numbers, text, symbols and images — and give examples of each
  • 2. Multiple representations of the same dataHingeStudents can give examples of the same data shown in different ways and explain that different representations exist for the same underlying information
  • 3. Why representation depends on purposeStudents can explain why some representations suit a purpose better than others, with reference to a specific example
