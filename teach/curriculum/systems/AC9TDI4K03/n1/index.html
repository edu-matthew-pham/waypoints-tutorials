Types of data

AC9TDI4K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 4 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Digital systems

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and name different types of data — numbers, text, symbols and images — and give examples of each

Success criteria

  • Names at least three distinct types of data correctly
  • Matches each type to at least one concrete example

Prior knowledge

Students can represent data using pictures, symbols, numbers and words (AC9TDI2K02). They have explored data as objects and symbols at Foundation level. They have not yet explored how the same data can be represented in multiple ways or why one representation might suit a purpose better than another.

Width

Minimum
Name and give one example each of at least three types of data (e.g. numbers, letters, symbols, pictures)
Typical
Identify numbers, text, symbols and images as distinct data types; describe how each is used in familiar digital and non-digital contexts (e.g. numerals in a calculator, letters in a message, icons on a phone)
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian rock paintings use images to encode knowledge about plants and animals — discuss how images are a data type that carries structured information
  • Explore emoji as a data type — discuss whether they are symbols, images or a distinct category, and what kinds of meaning they can and cannot convey
