← AC9TDI10K01 Managing and securing networked systems

Access control mechanisms

AC9TDI10K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 10 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Networks and security · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe elements of access control — including authentication and permissions — and explain why they are necessary in networked systems.

Lessons on this site

  • Who are you, and what may you do?
  • Who can read what

Success criteria

  • Distinguishes authentication from authorisation/permissions correctly
  • Explains what a specific access control mechanism protects against in a networked context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Network addressing and routing.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one access control mechanism (e.g. passwords/authentication OR file permissions) and explain what it protects against.
Typical
Explain the difference between authentication (proving identity) and permissions/authorisation (what an authenticated user may do); describe how each operates in a networked system with multiple users.
Wider
  • Investigate how role-based access control (RBAC) is implemented in enterprise systems and why it is more scalable than individual permission assignment
  • Examine how misconfigured permissions have caused real data breaches — what access control failure occurred and what should have been in place

Hinge

Conflation of authentication and authorisation cascades into N5 — students who cannot distinguish 'proving identity' from 'granting access rights' cannot analyse how a networked system's security components coordinate.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often conflate authentication and authorisation. A reasoning scaffold — who are you? (authentication) → what are you allowed to do? (authorisation) → what should the system block? (enforcement) — helps students distinguish these systematically rather than mixing up the terms.
