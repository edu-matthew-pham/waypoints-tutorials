The principle: all data as integers

AC9TDI8K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Data representation · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that digital systems represent all data types — text, images, audio — as integers, and that different encoding schemes map integers to different kinds of information

Lessons on this site

  • Everything is numbers

Success criteria

  • States that all data types are represented as integers in digital systems
  • Gives at least one example of how a number represents non-numeric information

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how digital systems use numbers to represent all types of data (AC9TDI6K03) and how data is represented using binary through off and on states (AC9TDI6K04). They understand the general principle that all data is numbers, but have not yet investigated the specific encoding schemes for text, image, or audio.

Width

Minimum
State that text, images and audio are all stored as numbers in a digital system; give one example of how a number could represent non-numeric data
Typical
Explain that different data types use different encoding schemes — the same integer means different things depending on whether the system is interpreting it as a character, a colour value, or a sound amplitude
Wider
  • Limited width — the encoding-scheme principle does not vary meaningfully across contexts. Reasoning about why a universal scheme would not work goes further conceptually rather than broadening the application of the principle.

Hinge

Students who do not understand that the same integer means different things in different encoding contexts will treat Nodes 2, 3 and 4 as three unrelated memorisation tasks rather than three instances of the same principle. Without this anchor, the three encoding schemes cannot be integrated at the standard level.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepIf students don't grasp that integers are mapped to meaning through encoding schemes, the three data types (text, images, audio) appear as disconnected facts. Seeing the principle applied across all three types IS the width that resolves the misconception.
