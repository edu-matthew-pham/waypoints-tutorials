Why binary — circuits and logic gates

AC9TDI8K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 8 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Data representation

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why digital systems use binary by connecting electronic on/off states to how circuits perform operations using logic gates

Success criteria

  • Explains that binary maps to the two reliable states of electronic components
  • Demonstrates the output of AND and OR gates for given inputs
  • Connects logic gates to why digital systems are built on binary

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Bits, bytes and range.

Width

Minimum
Explain that computers use binary because electronic components have two reliable states (on/off); identify that circuits with switches can represent AND or OR operations
Typical
Demonstrate how a 2-switch circuit represents AND and OR gates; explain the output for each combination of inputs; connect this to why binary is the foundation for all computation
Wider
  • Limited width — logic gates and circuit-level operations do not vary meaningfully across contexts. Extensions such as adder circuits or the history of binary vs analogue computing go further conceptually rather than broadening the application.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe why-binary question requires students to reason through a chain: electronic components have two reliable states → those states can be encoded as 0/1 → circuits can combine those states using Boolean logic → therefore binary is the natural foundation. Students who skip this reasoning and accept 'computers use binary' as a given cannot explain why binary was chosen over other number bases.
