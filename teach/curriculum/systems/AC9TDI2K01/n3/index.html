Components working together for a purpose

AC9TDI2K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 2 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Digital systems

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explore how components of a digital system work together to serve a specific purpose

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two components used in a specific digital system
  • Explains how those components work together to achieve the system's purpose

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Components of digital systems.

Width

Minimum
For one familiar digital system, identify the components used and explain how they work together to achieve the system's purpose
Typical
Explain how components (input, output, processing, storage) work together across different digital systems for different purposes — for example how a camera, screen and storage combine to let you take and keep a photo
Wider
  • Compare how a tablet and a desktop computer achieve the same purpose (e.g. writing a message) using different component combinations — explain why the components differ
  • Investigate how digital systems provide communities with services — for example how input, processing and output components combine to allow TV to reach remote communities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierComponent identification and purpose identification are component understandings. At this node students must coordinate both simultaneously — identifying which components are active and explaining how their coordination produces the purpose. This is the integration of the descriptor's two demands: 'identify components' and 'for a purpose'.
