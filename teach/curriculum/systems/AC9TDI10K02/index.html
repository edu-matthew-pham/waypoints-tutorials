Content, structure and presentation separation

AC9TDI10K02 · Year 10 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Web documents

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

represent documents online as content (text), structure (markup) and presentation (styling) and explain why such representations are important

Prior knowledge

Students can use a range of digital tools to create and communicate content (AC9TDI8P11, AC9TDI8P12). They have not yet formally encountered the three-layer model of web documents or the rationale for separating content, structure and presentation.

By the end

Students represent online documents by separating content (text), structure (HTML markup) and presentation (CSS styling), and explain why this separation is important for design, maintenance and accessibility.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • representing documents by separating the content (the text in the document), the structure (the document structure such as headings and paragraphs) and presentation (how the document is laid out and styled)
  • writing web pages using HyperText Markup Language (HTML) for the content and structure and Cascading Style Sheets (CSS) for styling the page and explaining how HTML tags separate content from structure
  • explaining how representing content, structure and presentation separately allows each of them to be designed, edited, manipulated and stored independently of the others and why this is important

Waypoints

  • 1. The three layers of a web documentHingeStudents can identify and distinguish the three layers of an online document: content, structure and presentation.
  • 2. Representing documents with HTML and CSSHingeStudents can write web pages using HTML for content and structure and CSS for styling, and explain how HTML tags separate content from structure.
  • 3. Why separation mattersStudents can explain why separating content, structure and presentation is important — specifically how it allows each to be designed, edited and stored independently.
