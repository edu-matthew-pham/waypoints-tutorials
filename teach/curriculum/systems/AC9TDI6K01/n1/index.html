Digital systems are made of parts

AC9TDI6K01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 6 Digital Technologies (knowledge) · Digital systems · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (knowledge): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that digital systems are made up of distinct internal components, each performing a specific function

Success criteria

  • Names at least two internal components of a digital system
  • States the function of each named component in plain language

Prior knowledge

Students can explore and describe a range of digital systems and their peripherals for a variety of purposes (AC9TDI4K01). They understand that digital systems have input and output components. They have not yet investigated what happens inside a digital system — internal components and their specific functions remain unexplored.

Width

Minimum
Name at least two internal components of a digital system and state what each does
Typical
Identify the main internal components (CPU, RAM, storage, input/output) of a familiar digital system such as a tablet or desktop; describe the function of each component in plain language
Wider
  • Compare internal components across different digital systems — a smartphone versus a gaming console versus a laptop — identifying which components are shared and which differ
  • Investigate the internal components of a telehealth system used to access ultrasound imagery in remote communities, identifying which components support sending and receiving large image files

Hinge

Students who cannot identify what each component does cannot reason about how components coordinate in N2 — the 'how they work together' task requires knowing each part's role as a prerequisite.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents at this node commonly confuse 'the device' with 'the component' — treating the whole system as a single thing. Seeing the same component (e.g. a CPU) appear in different devices (tablet, console, laptop) with different physical forms but the same function helps students separate the component from any single device form factor.
