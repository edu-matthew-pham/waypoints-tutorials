First Nations businesses and competitive advantage

AC9HE9K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain processes used by First Nations Australian businesses to outperform competitors and innovate in global markets, including managing cultural and intellectual property.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two processes First Nations Australian businesses use to create or maintain competitive advantage
  • Explains how cultural and intellectual property management contributes to competitive advantage for First Nations businesses
  • Uses relevant terms (e.g. 'competitive advantage', 'intellectual property', 'cultural expression')

Prior knowledge

From Y8: how businesses adapt to market opportunities and the changing nature of work (AC9HE8K02); how First Nations Australian businesses and entrepreneurs develop market opportunities (AC9HE8K03). From Y7: entrepreneur characteristics and how these influence business success (AC9HE7K03). Students understand why businesses exist and how entrepreneurs operate but have not yet studied the specific processes used to create and maintain competitive advantage in global markets.

Width

Minimum
Identify one process used by a First Nations Australian business to create competitive advantage (e.g. brand image, cultural IP licensing) and explain how it differentiates the business.
Typical
Identify and explain the range of processes First Nations Australian businesses use to build competitive advantage: promoting brand image and uniqueness of experiences; managing cultural and intellectual property through copyright and licensing; maintaining, controlling and protecting cultural expressions in products and services (art licensing, music licensing, cultural site consulting); and collaborating through the First Nations Chamber of Commerce and Industry to share resources and build market presence.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific First Nations Australian business operating in global markets and analyse which competitive advantage processes it uses, explaining what makes its approach distinctive
  • Examine how First Nations cultural intellectual property differs from mainstream IP in Australian law and explain how this shapes the strategies businesses use to protect competitive advantage
  • Compare how First Nations businesses in different sectors (tourism, food, art, consulting) use different combinations of the identified processes to build competitive advantage

Hinge

The First Nations IP and cultural property management context is substantively different from mainstream competitive advantage processes and must be treated on its own terms before students can make meaningful comparisons. Students who are taught generic competitive advantage first and then receive First Nations content as an 'add-on' develop a distorted understanding of the processes involved.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMultiple First Nations business examples across different sectors (art, tourism, food, consulting) show that the IP-protection and branding processes are adapted to different contexts, not a single formula.
