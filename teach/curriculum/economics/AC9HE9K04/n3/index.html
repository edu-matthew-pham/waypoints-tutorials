← AC9HE9K04 Competitive advantage

Building connections as a competitive process

AC9HE9K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain processes businesses use to build connections that support competitive advantage, including industry collaboration, government partnerships and developing professional talent.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two types of connection processes businesses use to build competitive advantage
  • Explains how each connection type creates or maintains advantage
  • Uses concrete examples of connection processes in Australian contexts

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Why businesses seek competitive advantage.

Width

Minimum
Identify and explain one process businesses use to build connections (e.g. industry cost-sharing, export partnerships with government) and explain how it supports competitive advantage.
Typical
Explain the processes businesses use to build connections that support competitive advantage: working with other businesses within the industry to share promotion costs and market access; working with government to increase exports (e.g. trade missions, export grants, bilateral agreements); developing highly skilled professionals and leaders across different industries to sustain competitive capability over time. Explain how each connection process creates advantage.
Wider
  • Investigate an Australian industry association or export council and analyse how collective action through these bodies creates competitive advantage that individual businesses could not achieve alone
  • Examine how Australian universities and research institutions partner with businesses to develop human capital, and explain how this contributes to long-term competitive advantage in knowledge-intensive industries
  • Analyse how government-backed trade promotion programs (e.g. Austrade) support Australian businesses in building international connections, including evaluation of their effectiveness

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyEach connection type (industry, government, talent development) requires separate examples. Building a repertoire of specific Australian programs and partnerships — rather than treating connections generically — prepares students for evidence-based analysis tasks.
