← AC9HE9K04 Competitive advantage

Cost reduction and innovation processes

AC9HE9K04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the processes businesses use to produce goods and services at lower cost and to innovate and differentiate products and services from competitors.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least one process businesses use to reduce production costs
  • Explains at least one process businesses use to differentiate products or services from competitors
  • Connects each process to competitive advantage using correct economic terminology

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Building connections as a competitive process.

Width

Minimum
Explain one process a business uses to reduce production costs (e.g. R&D, outsourcing) and one process used to differentiate products from competitors (e.g. advertising, unique attributes).
Typical
Explain the processes businesses use to produce goods and services at lower cost: research and development to improve efficiency; improving efficiency in development, production or delivery processes; utilising local resources; and outsourcing labour in the global economy. Explain the processes businesses use to innovate and differentiate: developing identifiable marketable attributes; using advertising and social media to build brand recognition and customer loyalty. Connect each process to how it creates or maintains competitive advantage.
Wider
  • Investigate how a specific Australian business uses R&D investment to create competitive advantage — examining what it invests in, what outcomes it achieves, and how this compares to international competitors
  • Examine the ethical implications of using outsourced labour in global supply chains for competitive advantage — considering impacts on workers, communities and the business's reputation
  • Analyse how social media has changed the cost of differentiation for small businesses, and compare how a First Nations small business uses social media for competitive differentiation versus a multinational corporation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates all prior competitive advantage processes — First Nations approaches (Node 1), reasons for seeking advantage (Node 2), and connection-building (Node 3) — into a full picture of how businesses create and maintain advantage. Students should see cost reduction and innovation as additions to the toolkit, not replacements for earlier processes.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCost reduction and innovation operate very differently across sectors (manufacturing vs services vs digital). Multiple sector examples prevent students from applying a manufacturing-only mental model to all businesses.
