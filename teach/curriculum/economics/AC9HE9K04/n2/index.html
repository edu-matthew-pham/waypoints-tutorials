Why businesses seek competitive advantage

AC9HE9K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why businesses seek to build competitive advantage, including meeting changing market demands and improving profit margins.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two reasons why businesses seek competitive advantage
  • Connects the reasons to conditions in competitive or global markets
  • Uses terms such as 'profit margin', 'competitive market', 'global market'

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: First Nations businesses and competitive advantage.

Width

Minimum
Explain two reasons why businesses seek competitive advantage (e.g. to meet competitive market demands, to improve profitability).
Typical
Explain the reasons businesses seek to build or create competitive advantage: to meet the changing demands of a competitive global market (which shifts preferences, lowers barriers, and creates new entrants); to improve profit margins (by achieving premium prices or reducing costs relative to competitors); to sustain long-term viability in dynamic markets. Connect these reasons to the pressures businesses face in the global economy.
Wider
  • Examine why competitive advantage is harder to sustain in digital markets (e.g. software, media) than in physical goods markets, and explain what this means for how businesses approach advantage-building
  • Investigate how competitor actions in global supply chains have forced Australian businesses to change their competitive advantage strategies — using a specific industry example
  • Analyse how shifting consumer values (e.g. sustainability, ethical sourcing) are changing the reasons businesses seek certain types of competitive advantage

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents benefit from reasoning through the competitive pressure logic: market → competitive entry → price/quality pressure → need to differentiate → processes to achieve differentiation. This causal chain makes the 'why' of competitive advantage concrete rather than a list of reasons.
