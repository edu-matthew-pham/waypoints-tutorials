Interpreting indicator data

AC9HE10K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read and interpret indicator data — identifying trends, describing what a change in direction signals, and using appropriate economic vocabulary.

Success criteria

  • Correctly describes the trend shown in a provided indicator dataset (rising, falling, stable, volatile)
  • Uses at least one specific economic term accurately when describing what the trend signals

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What economic indicators are.

Width

Minimum
Read a provided graph or table for one indicator; describe the trend and state what a rise or fall signals.
Typical
Interpret data for multiple indicators across a time period; describe trends, identify turning points, and use terms such as economic growth, recession, inflation, unemployment rate accurately.
Wider
  • Analyse a period of economic volatility (e.g. GFC, COVID-19) using multiple indicator datasets to explain what happened and in what sequence
  • Compare Australian indicator trends with those of a trading partner to identify divergence and consider its implications
  • Evaluate the limitations of a specific indicator — what it misses, who it undercounts, or what it obscures

Hinge

The rest of the descriptor requires students to explain how indicators *influence* decisions — this is only possible if they can read what the indicators are saying. Students who cannot interpret a trend cannot explain why a government might respond.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPractise reading graphs of different indicators with varied time periods. Common errors include misreading rate-of-change (confusing a slowing growth rate with a decline), or misinterpreting unemployment — knowing fluency here prevents errors in later analysis.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExpose students to data from different economic events and time periods so they can apply interpretation skills across varied contexts rather than pattern-matching to a single example.
