← AC9HE7K04 Work, income and identity

How individuals derive an income

AC9HE7K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain the different ways people derive an income, including wages, salaries, business ownership, dividends, rental income, and social security.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three different income sources with a concrete example for each
  • Explains the difference between at least two income sources (e.g. wage vs. business profit)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Types of work.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three ways of deriving income with a concrete example for each; explain the difference between a wage and a salary.
Typical
Describe the ways people derive income — working for a wage or salary, owning a business, being a shareholder (dividends), providing a rental service, receiving social security; explain how each income source relates to a different economic role.
Wider
  • Investigate how a First Nations community enterprise generates income for its members and community — examining income streams that combine commercial revenue, grant funding and cultural services
  • Explore how passive income sources (rental, dividends) differ from active income in terms of economic effort and risk
  • Examine how gig economy work (e.g. delivery platforms, freelancing) creates income that is neither wage nor salary — and what this means for financial security

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents benefit from a growing set of concrete income-source examples before integrating them with work types. Build a class matrix pairing work types with income sources — e.g. volunteer work → no income; rental property owner → rental income.
