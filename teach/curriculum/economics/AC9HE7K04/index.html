Work, income and identity

AC9HE7K04 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the reasons individuals work, the types of work they are involved in, and how they may derive an income

Prior knowledge

Students have explored consumer choices and personal financial decision-making (AC9HS6K08). They have encountered the idea that people earn income but have not formally examined the full range of work types, income sources, or the social and identity dimensions of work — including unpaid, volunteer and First Nations community contributions.

By the end

Students explain the reasons individuals work, the types of work they are involved in, and how they may derive an income.

Waypoints

  • 1. Types of workHingeStudents can identify and describe different types of work — full-time, part-time, casual, at home, paid, unpaid, volunteer — and explain that not all work is formally recognised or remunerated.
  • 2. How individuals derive an incomeStudents can identify and explain the different ways people derive an income, including wages, salaries, business ownership, dividends, rental income, and social security.
  • 3. Reasons for working: identity, community and wellbeingStudents can explain the multiple reasons individuals work beyond earning income, including identity, self-esteem, community contribution and material and non-material living standards.
