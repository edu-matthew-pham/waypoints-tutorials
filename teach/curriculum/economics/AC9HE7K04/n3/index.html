Reasons for working: identity, community and wellbeing

AC9HE7K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the multiple reasons individuals work beyond earning income, including identity, self-esteem, community contribution and material and non-material living standards.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two reasons individuals work beyond earning income
  • Uses relevant terms (identity, living standards, material, non-material, volunteer) accurately in explanation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: How individuals derive an income.

Width

Minimum
Explain two reasons individuals work other than earning income, using a specific example for each.
Typical
Analyse the contribution work makes to individuals' identity and community roles — including earning income, self-esteem, happiness, community support through volunteering, and material and non-material living standards; apply to a range of work contexts including paid, unpaid and culturally significant work.
Wider
  • Examine how continuity of cultural practices and management of Country/Place contributes to and sustains First Nations communities — analysing how this work contributes to identity, wellbeing and economic sustainability through regional tourism ventures, mentoring and entrepreneurship
  • Investigate a case where a person chose lower-income work for personal or community reasons — exploring the trade-offs between material and non-material living standards
  • Analyse how the reasons people work have changed across generations, using evidence from older family members, community data or historical examples

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents default to income as the only reason people work. Scaffold explicitly: income is one reason; what else does work provide that money alone cannot? Work through identity, purpose, community belonging and cultural connection as distinct contributions.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations community work — cultural maintenance, mentoring, land management — provides a particularly strong context for seeing non-income motivations as genuine and substantial, not supplementary.
