Constructing reasoned arguments with appropriate tone

AC9HE9S05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can present a reasoned argument in relation to an economic or business issue, applying tone appropriate to the purpose and audience.

Success criteria

  • States a clear position on the economic or business issue
  • Provides at least one evidence-based reason that supports the position
  • Applies a tone appropriate to the specified audience and purpose

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Explaining and supporting decisions with data representations.

Width

Minimum
Write one reasoned argument on an economic or business issue — stating a position, providing at least one evidence-based reason, and using a tone appropriate to a specified audience.
Typical
Present a reasoned argument in relation to an economic or business issue applying tone appropriate to purpose — using the elaboration example (authoritative tone when explaining trends in data to peers or business representatives). The argument should: state a clear position on the issue; provide evidence-based reasons that support the position; address the issue's complexity; apply an authoritative, substantiated tone (not casual, not assertive without evidence); and acknowledge research findings. Distinguish an argument from an explanation — an argument stakes a position; an explanation describes how something works.
Wider
  • Write arguments from competing stakeholder positions on the same economic issue (e.g. free trade vs protectionism from an industry perspective vs a consumer perspective) — demonstrating how evidence selection and framing differ by position
  • Evaluate two arguments on the same economic issue for reasoning quality — identifying which makes stronger use of evidence, more valid causal reasoning, and more appropriate tone
  • Write an argument that explicitly anticipates and addresses a counter-argument — explaining why acknowledging the counter-argument strengthens rather than weakens the overall argument

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe argument structure (position → reason → evidence → explanation of how evidence supports reason) is new at Y9 and must be made explicit. Students commonly produce explanations that state conclusions without staking a position, or staking positions without evidence — neither is an argument.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepArguments in Economics and Business vary in tone and evidence demands across contexts (academic, business, policy, consumer). Practising across contexts — not just in one academic format — builds tone flexibility and evidence-selection judgment.
