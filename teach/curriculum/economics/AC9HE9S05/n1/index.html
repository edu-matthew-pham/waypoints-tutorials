Orientating the audience using concepts and terms

AC9HE9S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop responses that orient the audience to an economic or business issue using relevant concepts and terms.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the target audience explicitly or through audience-appropriate vocabulary choices
  • Uses at least two relevant economic or business terms or concepts in the orientation
  • The orientation establishes what the issue is and why it matters to the specified audience

Prior knowledge

From Y8 AC9HE8S05 (direct predecessor): create descriptions and explanations using economic and business knowledge, concepts and terms, referencing information and data from sources. Students can create descriptions and explanations with source references. Y9 adds two moves: (1) arguments — a new text type requiring a reasoned position with evidence; and (2) acknowledge research findings — moving from referencing to formal acknowledgement of sources. Y9 also adds audience orientation and appropriate tone.

Width

Minimum
Write an opening section for an economic or business response that orients a specified audience to the issue using at least two relevant economic or business terms or concepts.
Typical
Develop a response to an issue that orients the audience (peers or business representatives) using relevant economic and business terms and concepts from the elaboration list: interdependence, trade, comparative advantage, competitive advantage, globalisation, supply chain, financial risks and rewards, return on investment, debt, savings buffer, scams, financial landscape, corporate social responsibility, financial planning, insurance. The orientation should establish what the issue is, why it matters to the audience, and the key concepts that frame the analysis.
Wider
  • Write audience-oriented openings for the same economic issue for two different audiences (e.g. peers and business representatives) — demonstrating how vocabulary, assumed knowledge and framing shift between audiences
  • Evaluate a provided economic response opening and revise it to better orient a specific audience — explaining each revision decision
  • Write an orientation to a First Nations economic issue that appropriately contextualises the issue for a non-specialist audience without oversimplifying or appropriating

Hinge

Audience orientation determines whether the subsequent explanation and argument are accessible to the reader. A response that fails to orient the audience creates a comprehension gap that undermines the force of the argument regardless of its technical accuracy.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEffective orientation requires three decisions: who is the audience, what do they already know, and what do they need to be told to follow what comes next. Making these decisions explicit before drafting prevents students from writing only for themselves.
