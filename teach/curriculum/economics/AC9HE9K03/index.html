Australia-Asia trade

AC9HE9K03 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the reasons Australia trades with other nations, and the patterns of trade between Australia and Asia

Prior knowledge

From AC9HE9K02 (direct predecessor): how economic decision-making involves interdependence of consumers, businesses, financial sector and government, including globalisation and supply chains. From AC9HE9K01: Australia's financial sector and its global role. Students understand economic interdependence but have not yet specifically investigated why countries trade or analysed Australia's trade relationship with Asia.

By the end

Students explain the reasons Australia trades with other nations and the patterns of trade between Australia and Asia.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the reasons countries trade, such as variations in the quantity and quality of economic resources within and between countries, to sell surplus goods and services, or to buy goods and services required by individuals and businesses
  • identifying the benefits of trade; for example, economic benefits (such as increased production, specialisation, increased household incomes, increased employment), benefits for individuals and businesses (such as opportunities to sell surpluses, competition encouraging greater choice, connections for inventors and entrepreneurs with future discoveries), and national benefits (such as relationships between countries, access to health and education services, and diversified sources of national income)
  • analysing patterns of trade between Australia and the countries of Asia over time; for example, total value of trade each year, composition of trade (such as category, goods, services), direction of trade (such as exports to which countries, imports from which countries), percentage change over a decade (such as increase or decrease)
  • identifying similarities and differences or trends in the composition and direction of trade between Australia and Asia now and 50 years ago
  • explaining the interdependent nature of trade between Australia and the countries of Asia

Waypoints

  • 1. Why countries tradeHingeStudents can explain the reasons countries trade, including resource variations, comparative advantage, surplus goods and specialist goods.
  • 2. Benefits of tradeStudents can identify and explain the economic, individual, business and national benefits of trade for Australia.
  • 3. Patterns of Australia-Asia tradeHingeStudents can analyse patterns of trade between Australia and Asian countries over time, including total value, composition and direction of trade.
  • 4. Similarities, differences and interdependence in Australia-Asia tradeStudents can identify similarities and differences in Australia-Asia trade patterns over time and explain the interdependent nature of this trade relationship.
