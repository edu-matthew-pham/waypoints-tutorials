Similarities, differences and interdependence in Australia-Asia trade

AC9HE9K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify similarities and differences in Australia-Asia trade patterns over time and explain the interdependent nature of this trade relationship.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one similarity and one difference in Australia-Asia trade patterns across different time periods
  • Explains how both Australia and Asian trading partners are interdependent in the trade relationship
  • Connects trade patterns to the underlying economic reasons for the relationship

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Patterns of Australia-Asia trade.

Width

Minimum
Identify one similarity and one difference in Australia's trade relationship with Asia now compared with 50 years ago, and explain one way the relationship is interdependent.
Typical
Identify similarities and differences or trends in the composition and direction of trade between Australia and Asia now and 50 years ago (e.g. what goods/services have remained dominant, what has changed, which partners have risen or fallen in importance). Explain the interdependent nature of Australia-Asia trade — how Australian producers depend on Asian markets and Asian economies depend on Australian resources, commodities and services.
Wider
  • Investigate how Australia's economic dependence on Asian demand for resources creates vulnerability — using a specific commodity and examining the consequences of demand fluctuation for Australian industries and regions
  • Examine the interdependence between Australian universities and international student income from Asian countries, analysing how COVID-19 border closures exposed this dependency
  • Analyse how new trade corridors (e.g. India, Vietnam, Indonesia) are diversifying Australia's trade interdependence and explain what is driving these shifts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates reasons for trade (Node 1), benefits of trade (Node 2), and pattern analysis (Node 3) into an explanation of the Australia-Asia interdependence. Students should connect: why trade exists → what patterns have developed → how these create mutual dependence.
