Benefits of trade

AC9HE9K03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain the economic, individual, business and national benefits of trade for Australia.

Success criteria

  • Identifies benefits of trade from at least two categories (economic, individual/business, national)
  • Explains each benefit with reference to an Australian example
  • Distinguishes between different types of trade benefits rather than listing them as equivalent

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Why countries trade.

Width

Minimum
Identify and explain two benefits of trade — one economic benefit (e.g. increased production or household income) and one national or individual benefit.
Typical
Explain the full range of trade benefits across the elaboration categories: economic benefits (increased production, specialisation, increased household incomes, increased employment); benefits for individuals and businesses (opportunities to sell surpluses, competition encouraging greater choice, connections for inventors and entrepreneurs with future discoveries); and national benefits (relationships between countries, access to health and education services, diversified sources of national income). Use Australian examples for each category.
Wider
  • Evaluate the distribution of trade benefits within Australia — examining which industries, regions and demographic groups gain most from trade liberalisation and which may be disadvantaged
  • Investigate how services trade (education, tourism, financial services) creates benefits for Australia that differ in nature from goods trade benefits
  • Examine how Indigenous-owned businesses in Australia participate in trade (domestic and international) and what barriers or opportunities shape their experience of trade benefits

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need examples across all three benefit categories (economic, individual/business, national) to address descriptor-level tasks. Systematically building the repertoire with Australian examples before analysis tasks prevents students from relying on one category only.
