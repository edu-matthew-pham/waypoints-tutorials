Patterns of Australia-Asia trade

AC9HE9K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse patterns of trade between Australia and Asian countries over time, including total value, composition and direction of trade.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the composition and direction of trade between Australia and at least one Asian country
  • Describes at least one trend in Australia-Asia trade patterns over time
  • Uses trade data vocabulary (exports, imports, total value, composition, direction)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Benefits of trade.

Width

Minimum
Describe the current direction of trade between Australia and one Asian country (what Australia exports and imports) and identify one trend in this relationship over the past decade.
Typical
Analyse patterns of trade between Australia and the countries of Asia over time using the elaboration dimensions: total value of trade each year; composition of trade (category of goods and services — resources, agricultural products, manufactured goods, services); direction of trade (exports to which countries, imports from which countries); and percentage change over a decade. Identify trends and changes in these patterns.
Wider
  • Compare Australia's trade composition with Asia now versus 50 years ago, explaining what the shifts in composition reveal about changes in comparative advantage and economic development
  • Investigate the growth of services trade between Australia and Asia (education, tourism, financial services) alongside goods trade, and explain why services have grown as a share of the total
  • Analyse how China's economic development has changed its role in Australia's trade patterns — from a smaller trading partner to Australia's largest — and explain the economic factors driving this shift

Hinge

Analysing trade patterns requires being able to read multi-variable data (value, composition, direction, time) simultaneously. Students who cannot disaggregate pattern dimensions cannot address the analytical tasks in AC9HE9S03, which uses Australia-Asia trade as a primary example context.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkTrade pattern analysis involves multiple simultaneous variables (value, composition, direction, change over time). A structured data-reading framework — what is traded, with whom, how much, how has this changed — prevents students from treating pattern analysis as a single-variable description.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDifferent Asian trading partners (China, Japan, South Korea, India, ASEAN) show different pattern characteristics. Using multiple partner contexts reveals the variation within 'Australia-Asia trade' and builds more accurate pattern understanding.
