← AC9HE7S01 Develop inquiry questions

Using economics concepts in question construction

AC9HE7S01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop questions using economic and business concepts and terms such as scarcity, opportunity cost, market, income, goods and services.

Success criteria

  • Develops at least one investigable question that correctly uses an economics or business concept
  • The question is answerable using accessible data or sources

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What makes a question investigable.

Width

Minimum
Develop one question using at least one economic or business concept correctly; the question must be investigable.
Typical
Develop questions using a range of economic and business concepts and terms — scarcity, opportunity cost, market, income, business, goods and services, costs and benefits — framed as investigable questions about contemporary issues; refine questions to ensure they are answerable using accessible data or sources.
Wider
  • Develop a set of linked sub-questions that unpack a complex economic issue — practising how a main investigation question can be broken into component investigable questions
  • Peer-review a partner's question set using an explicit criterion checklist for economic vocabulary, investigability and contemporary relevance
  • Develop questions specifically about a First Nations economic context (e.g. 'How does the Gumatj Corporation decide how to allocate its resources?') and evaluate their investigability

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need practice generating and revising questions across multiple economic topics before the skill becomes transferable. Provide question stems that prompt concept use ('How does... affect...?', 'What is the opportunity cost of...?') and have students adapt them to different issues.
