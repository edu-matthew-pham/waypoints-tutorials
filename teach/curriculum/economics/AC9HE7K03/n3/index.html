Characteristics and business success

AC9HE7K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can combine entrepreneurial knowledge, skills and attitudes with observations of businesses to explain the factors that contribute to success, including opportunity recognition, initiative and innovation.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least two specific entrepreneurial characteristics contributed to a business's success
  • Uses evidence from the business's story to support the explanation
  • Uses relevant terms (entrepreneur, opportunity, initiative, innovation, stakeholder) accurately

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Values and entrepreneurial decision-making.

Width

Minimum
Explain how at least two entrepreneurial characteristics contributed to the success of a specific business; use evidence from the business's story.
Typical
Combine knowledge, skills and attitudes demonstrated by entrepreneurs with observations of successful local businesses to explain factors contributing to success, including opportunity recognition, initiative and innovation; apply to at least two entrepreneurs from different business or cultural contexts.
Wider
  • Compare how entrepreneurial success is defined differently across cultural contexts — for example, contrasting a First Nations entrepreneur whose success is measured by community benefit with a corporate entrepreneur measured by financial return
  • Evaluate a failed business venture and identify which entrepreneurial characteristics were absent or underdeveloped, explaining how this contributed to the outcome
  • Research a local or regional business and develop an original framework for what characteristics contributed most to its success, using evidence from public sources

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe final node integrates skills, values and attitudes into a cohesive explanation of business success. Students need to move beyond listing characteristics to constructing a causal argument: these characteristics, in this context, produced this outcome.
