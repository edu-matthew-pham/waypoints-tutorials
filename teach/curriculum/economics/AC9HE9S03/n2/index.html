Interpreting multi-variable data for trends

AC9HE9S03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret multi-variable data to explain direction and time period for a change, including changes in Australia's trade patterns.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two variables in a multi-variable data source
  • Explains the direction and time period of change for each variable
  • Describes what the combined pattern of change reveals about the economic issue

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Interpreting to explain economic issues.

Width

Minimum
Interpret a graph or table showing change over time to explain the direction and time period of one change.
Typical
Interpret multi-variable data to explain direction and time period for change — applying the elaboration example (change in direction and composition of Australia's trade over 10, 20 or 50 years). For multi-variable data: identify what variables are shown, describe the direction of change for each (increase, decrease, stable, fluctuating), identify the time period, and explain what the combined pattern reveals about the underlying economic issue.
Wider
  • Compare trend interpretations from graphs using different time scales (10-year vs 50-year) for the same data, explaining how time scale affects which trends are visible
  • Interpret trade data showing multiple simultaneous trends (total value increasing, composition shifting, direction changing) and explain what the combined picture reveals that single-variable reading would miss
  • Identify a misleading data visualisation in an economic or business context and explain specifically what is misleading about the trend it appears to show

Hinge

Multi-variable trend interpretation is required for AC9HE9K03 (trade patterns) and for prediction tasks in Node 4. Students who can only read single-variable trends cannot perform the combined-pattern reading that makes trade data analysis meaningful.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMulti-variable data interpretation requires a systematic reading sequence: what variables → what direction each → what time period → what the combined pattern means. Without this structure, students read the most visually prominent trend and miss the others.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPractising multi-variable interpretation across different data contexts (trade data, scam loss statistics, unemployment rates) builds transferable data-reading skill rather than context-specific familiarity.
