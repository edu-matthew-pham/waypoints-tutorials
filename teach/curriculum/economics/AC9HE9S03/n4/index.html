Making predictions about consumer and financial impacts

AC9HE9S03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain trends and make predictions about who will be affected and how, including in consumer and financial contexts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a trend and makes a specific, grounded prediction about who will be affected
  • Explains the mechanism connecting the trend to the predicted impact
  • The prediction is based on the trend data rather than general opinion or speculation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Explaining cause-and-effect relationships.

Width

Minimum
Use a trend from a data source to make one prediction about who will be affected and how, explaining the reasoning from trend to prediction.
Typical
Explain trends to make predictions about who will be affected and how — applying the elaboration examples (trend for money lost in scams and age groups most affected; predictions about how it will affect behaviour of individuals and businesses). For each prediction: identify the trend, name the group(s) most likely to be affected, explain the mechanism connecting trend to predicted impact, and state the predicted behavioural or financial change. Predictions should be grounded in the trend data, not speculative.
Wider
  • Make competing predictions from the same trend data by varying assumptions about how actors will respond — explaining what different responses by individuals vs businesses might produce different outcomes
  • Evaluate the quality of an economic prediction made previously (e.g. from a report 5 years ago) against what actually happened, analysing what the prediction got right, wrong, and why
  • Apply prediction-making to a First Nations economic context — identifying a trend relevant to First Nations communities and making a grounded prediction about impacts, acknowledging the specific factors that shape outcomes in this context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPrediction integrates trend interpretation (Node 2), causal reasoning (Node 3), and explanatory framing (Node 1). Students should build predictions by: reading the trend → reasoning through the causal mechanism → naming who is affected → predicting the specific impact. This is the full capability the descriptor demands.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPrediction-from-trend is often performed as guessing. A structured prediction argument (trend → mechanism → affected group → predicted change → caveats) helps students produce defensible rather than speculative predictions.
