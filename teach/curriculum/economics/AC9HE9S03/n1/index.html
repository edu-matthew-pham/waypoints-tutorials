Interpreting to explain economic issues

AC9HE9S03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret information to explain the nature and extent of economic and business issues, including how Australian businesses operate in the global economy.

Success criteria

  • Interprets a source to explain what a named economic or business issue involves
  • Explains how the issue operates — naming the mechanism or actors driving it
  • Goes beyond identification to provide an explanation of the issue's nature

Prior knowledge

From Y8 AC9HE8S03 (direct predecessor): interpret information and data to identify economic and business issues, trends and economic cause-and-effect relationships. Y9 extends the capability in two key directions: (1) explaining rather than merely identifying (the move to explicit explanation of relationships and mechanisms); and (2) making predictions about consumer and financial impacts — a new move requiring projection from trends to future outcomes.

Width

Minimum
Interpret one source (text or data) to explain the nature of a named economic issue — going beyond identifying the issue to explaining what it involves and how it operates.
Typical
Interpret information to explain the nature and extent of economic and business issues — applying the elaboration example (the way Australian businesses operate in the global economy). Explain what the issue involves (its components and actors), how it operates (the mechanisms at work), and its extent (how widespread or significant it is). Use the source explicitly to support each element of the explanation.
Wider
  • Interpret information from multiple source types (statistical data + case study + expert commentary) to build a richer explanation of the same issue, identifying what each source type adds
  • Apply the nature-and-extent interpretation framework to a First Nations economic issue, explaining what makes this context require different framing assumptions
  • Interpret a contested economic issue where sources frame the nature differently (e.g. trade liberalisation as opportunity vs risk) and explain how framing affects the account of 'nature and extent'

Hinge

Students who treat interpretation as identification ('the issue is X') will not produce the causal explanations required by subsequent nodes or by AC9HE9S05. The explain-not-identify distinction is the primary cognitive advance from Y8, and it must be established before prediction work begins.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe nature-extent explanation framework (what it involves → how it operates → how significant it is) scaffolds interpretation as a three-part explanatory act rather than a description. Making this structure explicit prevents students from treating interpretation as description.
