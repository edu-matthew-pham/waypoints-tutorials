Interpret data and make predictions

AC9HE9S03 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret information and data, explaining economic and business issues, trends and economic cause-and-effect relationships, and make predictions about consumer and financial impacts

Prior knowledge

From Y8 AC9HE8S03 (direct predecessor): interpret information and data to identify economic and business issues, trends and economic cause-and-effect relationships. Y9 extends the capability in two key directions: (1) explaining rather than merely identifying (the move to explicit explanation of relationships and mechanisms); and (2) making predictions about consumer and financial impacts — a new move requiring projection from trends to future outcomes.

By the end

Students interpret information and data, explaining economic and business issues, trends and economic cause-and-effect relationships, and make predictions about consumer and financial impacts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • interpreting information to explain the nature and extent of economic and business issues; for example, the way Australian businesses operate in the global economy
  • interpreting multi-variable data to explain direction and time period for a change; for example, change in the direction and composition of Australia's trade in the past 10, 20 or 50 years
  • explaining relationships between the actions of individuals and businesses; for example, the way businesses operate in the global economy and implications for the Australian market
  • explaining trends to make predictions about who will be affected and how; for example, the trend for money lost in scams and age groups most affected, and predictions about how it will affect the behaviour of individuals and businesses

Waypoints

  • 1. Interpreting to explain economic issuesHingeStudents can interpret information to explain the nature and extent of economic and business issues, including how Australian businesses operate in the global economy.
  • 2. Interpreting multi-variable data for trendsHingeStudents can interpret multi-variable data to explain direction and time period for a change, including changes in Australia's trade patterns.
  • 3. Explaining cause-and-effect relationshipsStudents can explain cause-and-effect relationships between the actions of individuals and businesses, and between economic actors in different contexts.
  • 4. Making predictions about consumer and financial impactsStudents can explain trends and make predictions about who will be affected and how, including in consumer and financial contexts.
