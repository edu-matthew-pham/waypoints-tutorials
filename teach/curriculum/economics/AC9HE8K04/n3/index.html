Types of tax and their effect on prices

AC9HE8K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify examples of taxes paid by individuals and businesses and explain how they may influence spending and pricing decisions.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two distinct types of taxes and who pays each
  • Explains, with a specific example, how one tax type influences a decision made by an individual or business

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: How and when individuals pay tax.

Width

Minimum
Identify two different types of taxes (e.g. income tax, GST, excise duty) and explain how one of them influences a spending or pricing decision.
Typical
Identify examples of taxes paid by individuals and businesses — including income tax, GST, import duties and excise duties — and explain how each may influence decision-making about spending and pricing. Use specific product examples (e.g. how excise on alcohol or petrol affects consumer purchasing).
Wider
  • Investigate how the GST (Goods and Services Tax) operates as a value-added consumption tax, including which goods and services are exempt and why (fresh food, health, education) and the distributional consequences of a flat consumption tax
  • Research how tariffs and import duties are used as both revenue-raising measures and policy instruments to protect domestic industries, and how these affect consumer prices and business sourcing decisions
  • Examine how businesses factor taxation (company tax, payroll tax, GST obligations) into pricing decisions, and why the incidence of a tax (who ultimately bears the cost) may differ from who formally pays it

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDifferent tax types have qualitatively different effects on decisions — income tax affects labour supply decisions, excise affects consumption of specific goods, import duties affect sourcing decisions. Each context type needs representation to reach the descriptor's full scope.
