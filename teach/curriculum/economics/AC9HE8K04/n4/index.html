Taxation, wellbeing and decision-making — integrated

AC9HE8K04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the importance of Australia's taxation system — connecting revenue purpose, individual obligations, tax types and their effects on decisions — and explain how the system supports individual and community wellbeing.

Success criteria

  • Explains why Australia's taxation system is important, referencing both revenue purposes and the services it funds
  • Explains how at least one feature of the taxation system affects decision-making by an individual or a business

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Types of tax and their effect on prices.

Width

Minimum
Explain how Australia's taxation system affects a decision made by a young person (e.g. whether and how much to work, what to spend money on) and connect this to how the system funds services that support wellbeing.
Typical
Explain the importance of the Australian taxation system by connecting: why taxation exists and what it funds; how individuals meet their tax obligations; how different types of taxes affect spending and pricing decisions; and how the system collectively supports individual human and financial wellbeing, communities and Australian society.
Wider
  • Evaluate whether the current Australian tax mix is well-designed to achieve its goals — examining arguments for and against changes (e.g. raising or lowering income tax, extending or narrowing the GST base, introducing new taxes on wealth or carbon)
  • Research how Australia's taxation revenue as a proportion of GDP compares with comparable countries (OECD nations) and what this implies about the scope of government services each provides
  • Investigate a case where a tax has been deliberately used to change behaviour (e.g. carbon pricing, tobacco excise, sugar tax proposals) and evaluate evidence on whether it achieved its intended effect

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe integration task should require students to connect taxation's purpose (funding services) with its mechanics (how it is collected) and its effects (how it shapes decisions) — not treat these as separate items. A real scenario (a young person starting work, a business setting a price) is a useful integration frame.
