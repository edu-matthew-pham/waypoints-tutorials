← AC9HE9S02 Locate, select and analyse sources

Selecting for relevance

AC9HE9S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select information and data from sources by asking and applying questions about relevance to their economic or business investigation.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Applies at least one explicit relevance criterion to evaluate a source
  • Explains how selected information helps answer a specific economic or business question
  • Distinguishes between relevant and irrelevant information from the same source

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating sources using contextually specific search strategies.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least one relevance criterion (e.g. 'Does this data help answer my question?') to select information from a provided source, explaining the selection decision.
Typical
Select and analyse information and data for relevance by applying the elaboration questions: 'How will the data or information help answer this economic or business question?' and 'How will the data or information inform a response to an economic or business issue?' Apply these criteria to select from a range of sources for a specific investigation, explaining selection and rejection decisions explicitly.
Wider
  • Apply relevance selection across sources of varying types (statistical data, case study, policy document, news report) for the same investigation, explaining how relevance criteria apply differently to each type
  • Practise the 'relevance decision' under conditions of abundance — given 8-10 possible sources, narrow to 3-4 and justify each inclusion and exclusion with specific relevance reasoning
  • Apply relevance criteria to a First Nations economic context — locating and selecting sources that are relevant to, and respectful of, community-specific economic and business issues

Hinge

Students who cannot discriminate between relevant and irrelevant sources will produce investigations crowded with loosely related information. This blocks meaningful analysis in AC9HE9S03 and undermines the quality of arguments in AC9HE9S05.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkRelevance requires explicit reasoning against the inquiry question — it is not a 'feels relevant' judgement. Teaching students to articulate the connection (this data shows X, which addresses Y in my question) prevents acceptance of superficially related sources.
