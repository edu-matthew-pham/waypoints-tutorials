Modifying questions to improve focus

AC9HE9S01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the focus and scope of an inquiry question and modify it to improve its usefulness for investigating an economic or business issue.

Success criteria

  • Modifies at least one inquiry question and explains what was improved by the modification
  • The modified question is more focused, better scoped, or uses more precise economic terminology than the original
  • The modification does not introduce a new tangent — it sharpens the original question's focus

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Developing questions for complex economic issues.

Width

Minimum
Take an existing inquiry question and modify it to improve its focus — explaining what was weak about the original and how the modification improves it.
Typical
Develop and modify questions to improve the focus of an investigation using economic and business concepts and terms. Apply the elaboration examples as models: narrowing a broad question ('How can consumers protect themselves from risk?' → 'What strategies do Australian consumers use to manage scam risk?'), improving terminology precision, adjusting scope to match available evidence, and checking that modified questions genuinely advance the investigation rather than introducing a new tangent.
Wider
  • Practise question modification through a structured peer feedback cycle — develop a question, receive structured critique on its focus and concept use, modify and re-evaluate
  • Apply modification to questions across different issue types (trade, consumer protection, competitive advantage) to develop transferable question-quality judgement
  • Evaluate a set of provided questions of varying quality for the same economic issue and rank them with justification — explaining what makes the best question better than the weaker ones

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsThe modification move requires repeated practice with explicit feedback before it becomes reliable. Students tend to 'modify' by making questions longer rather than sharper. Practise-and-refine cycles with explicit criteria build the precision needed.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPractising modification across different economic issue contexts — trade, consumer risk, competitive advantage — builds transferable question-quality judgement rather than issue-specific question templates.
