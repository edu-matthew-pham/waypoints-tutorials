Superannuation and broader financial decision-making

AC9HE10K04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the existence of superannuation affects other consumer and financial decisions — including career choices, savings behaviour, insurance, and planning for retirement.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one way that superannuation influences a financial decision outside of super itself
  • Explains how employment type (e.g. casual, part-time, permanent) affects superannuation outcomes

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Super investment decisions.

Width

Minimum
Give one example of how superannuation affects a non-super financial decision (e.g. how super balance affects decisions about additional voluntary contributions or salary sacrifice).
Typical
Explain how superannuation shapes broader consumer and financial decision-making — including how people think about retirement income, whether to salary sacrifice, how to balance super with other savings goals, and how employment decisions (e.g. casual vs permanent work) affect super outcomes.
Wider
  • Investigate the interaction between superannuation and the Age Pension means test — how super balance affects eligibility and what this implies for optimal drawdown strategy
  • Evaluate the financial implications of taking paid vs unpaid parental leave on super and lifetime retirement savings
  • Examine how superannuation death benefits and estate planning interact, and why beneficiary nominations matter
