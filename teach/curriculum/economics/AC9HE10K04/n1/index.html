What superannuation is and why it exists

AC9HE10K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what superannuation is, why Australia introduced a compulsory super system, and the dual purpose it serves for individuals and government.

Success criteria

  • Explains what superannuation is in terms of how it works (regular contributions, investment, access at retirement)
  • States both the individual wellbeing and government fiscal rationale for compulsory super

Prior knowledge

Students understand factors that influence major consumer and financial decisions and their short- and long-term consequences (AC9HE10K03). They can analyse how financial commitments accumulate over time and consider the role of savings. Students have also explored how governments redistribute income through transfers and services (AC9HE10K02).

Width

Minimum
Explain what superannuation is and state at least two reasons why Australia has a compulsory superannuation system.
Typical
Explain the importance of Australia's superannuation system in relation to human and financial wellbeing in retirement, and explain why the system also serves the government's ability to maintain a welfare payment system — reducing future pension dependency.
Wider
  • Investigate how Australia's superannuation system compares internationally — examining countries with defined benefit vs accumulation models and what trade-offs each involves
  • Analyse the history of superannuation in Australia — why it was introduced in the 1990s, at what rate, and how it has evolved to today's rate
  • Examine how the ageing population creates pressure on government welfare payments and evaluate how superannuation is intended to reduce this pressure

Hinge

Students who see superannuation only as savings for retirement — without understanding the government's fiscal rationale — cannot explain the system's design features (compulsion, tax treatment, preservation rules). The dual rationale is foundational.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkBuild both rationales explicitly: individual retirement income security AND reduced government pension liability. Students commonly grasp the individual rationale but miss the systemic one. The systemic rationale explains compulsion and tax incentives.
