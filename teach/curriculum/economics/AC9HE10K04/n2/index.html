How a super fund works

AC9HE10K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how an individual superannuation fund operates — who contributes, how it is taxed, and how the fund grows over time.

Success criteria

  • Identifies employer and employee as contributors and describes the employer's compulsory obligation
  • Explains at least one tax feature of superannuation (e.g. concessional tax on contributions or earnings)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What superannuation is and why it exists.

Width

Minimum
Identify who contributes to a superannuation fund and explain at least one tax feature of the system.
Typical
Explain how individuals manage a personal superannuation fund: who contributes (employer, employee, government co-contribution), how contributions are taxed, how to consolidate funds, how to find lost super, and how to track account performance.
Wider
  • Model the compound growth of a superannuation account over a working life, varying contribution rates and investment options to show the effect of early vs late contributions
  • Investigate the implications of employment gaps (e.g. career breaks, casual work, unpaid care) on superannuation balance at retirement and who is most affected
  • Examine the gender superannuation gap — why women retire with significantly less super than men — and evaluate policy responses

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary the worked examples — different employment types, contribution levels, and life stages — so students understand that super outcomes vary significantly depending on individual circumstances.
