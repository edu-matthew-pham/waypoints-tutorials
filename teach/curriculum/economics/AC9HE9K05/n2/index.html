Consumer reward programs and financial rewards

AC9HE9K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can discuss examples of consumer reward programs and financial rewards, including loyalty schemes and investment returns.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two types of consumer or financial reward with examples
  • Explains how each reward mechanism functions
  • Uses terms such as 'loyalty scheme', 'investment returns', 'savings'

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Types of consumer and financial risk.

Width

Minimum
Describe one consumer reward program and one type of financial reward (e.g. investment return) and explain how each benefits individuals or businesses.
Typical
Discuss examples of consumer rewards from the elaboration range: innovative products and services that reward early adopters; benefits accrued through loyalty schemes (frequent flyer points, cashback, member discounts); and rewards for building savings and making investments (interest on term deposits, dividends from shares, capital growth from home ownership). Explain how each rewards mechanism functions and who benefits.
Wider
  • Investigate the economics of loyalty programs — examining how businesses profit from them while appearing to reward consumers — and evaluate whether loyalty programs are genuinely beneficial for consumers or primarily for businesses
  • Examine how the rewards from home ownership vary across income levels and geographic locations in Australia, and discuss what this reveals about equity in financial reward distribution
  • Compare investment rewards available to young Australians (micro-investing, superannuation, first home saver accounts) and analyse which reward mechanisms are most accessible

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepReward mechanisms differ significantly across loyalty programs, savings products and investment assets. Multiple examples prevent students from conflating reward types and support a balanced risk-reward discussion in the final node.
