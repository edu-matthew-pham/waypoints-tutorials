Types of consumer and financial risk

AC9HE9K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and discuss examples of consumer and financial risk to individuals and businesses, including scams, identity theft and fraudulent transactions.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two distinct types of consumer or financial risk
  • Describes how each risk type affects individuals or businesses
  • Distinguishes between risk types rather than treating them as equivalent

Prior knowledge

From Y8 (direct predecessor AC9HE8K05): processes individuals and businesses use to plan and budget to achieve short- and long-term financial objectives. From Y7 (AC9HE7K05): consumer rights and responsibilities of individuals and businesses relating to financial products and services. Students understand planning, budgeting and consumer rights but have not yet studied active risk management strategies — the move from understanding what rights exist to understanding how to manage active threats.

Width

Minimum
Identify and describe two types of consumer or financial risk (e.g. scams, identity theft) and explain how each affects individuals or businesses.
Typical
Identify and discuss the range of consumer and financial risks to individuals and businesses from the elaboration examples: scams (phishing, investment scams, social engineering), identity theft (unauthorised use of personal information for financial gain), and fraudulent transactions (unauthorised payments, card fraud). Explain how each type affects individuals and businesses differently in terms of financial loss, reputational damage and emotional harm.
Wider
  • Investigate current scam types targeting Australian consumers and businesses (using ACCC Scamwatch data) and analyse which demographic groups are most affected and why
  • Examine how cybersecurity incidents in businesses create consumer financial risk — using a real Australian data breach case to trace consequences for both the business and affected customers
  • Analyse how social media and digital platforms have created new categories of consumer and financial risk that did not exist a decade ago

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish between risk types will be unable to match appropriate management strategies to specific risks in subsequent nodes — applying insurance to a scam, for example, is not meaningful risk management and reflects category confusion.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyRisk categories (scams, identity theft, fraud) each have distinct mechanisms. Building a repertoire of current, realistic examples from Australian contexts — not abstract definitions — makes the subsequent strategy-matching work genuine.
