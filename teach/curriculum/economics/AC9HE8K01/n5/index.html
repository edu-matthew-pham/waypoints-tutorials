← AC9HE8K01 Markets and price signals

Markets, prices and resource allocation — integrated

AC9HE8K01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how markets influence decisions about the allocation of resources to the production of goods and services, connecting participant interactions, the three allocation questions, price factors, and price signals into a coherent account.

Success criteria

  • Explains how a market scenario connects participant interaction, pricing factors, and resource allocation decisions
  • Uses at least three economics terms accurately in an extended explanation of how markets influence resource allocation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Price as resource allocation signal.

Width

Minimum
Explain how a change in market conditions (e.g. a rise in consumer demand for a product) flows through the market system — affecting price, sending signals to producers, and ultimately redirecting resources. Use appropriate economic terms throughout.
Typical
Explain how markets allocate resources through the price mechanism, drawing on all four building blocks: who participates and how, the three allocation questions, what drives prices, and how price signals direct resource flows. Apply to at least one extended example.
Wider
  • Compare how the market allocates resources in a sector experiencing rapid technological change (e.g. energy, transport) versus a more stable sector, examining how the price signals differ in strength and speed
  • Investigate a case where market price signals have led to outcomes that are efficient for producers and consumers but problematic for society (e.g. environmental costs not captured in price), and explain why the market allocation is incomplete
  • Evaluate how First Nations Australian businesses use market signals alongside non-market values (Country stewardship, community benefit) when making production and resource allocation decisions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe four building blocks from earlier nodes must be connected into a single coherent explanation. Students should demonstrate they can trace a cause from market conditions through to resource allocation, not treat each concept as a separate item.
