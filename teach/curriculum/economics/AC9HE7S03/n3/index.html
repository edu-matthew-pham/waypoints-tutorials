Cause-and-effect relationships

AC9HE7S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret visual displays of multi-variable data to identify a cause-and-effect relationship within an economic issue, such as the relationship between income and saving or spending behaviour.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a cause-and-effect relationship in a visual data display
  • Explains the direction of the relationship using evidence from the data
  • Uses economic terms (trend, cause, effect, relationship, variable) accurately

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identifying trends using multi-variable data.

Width

Minimum
Identify one cause-and-effect relationship in a visual display of economic data; explain which variable is the cause and which is the effect.
Typical
Interpret visual displays of multi-variable data to identify cause-and-effect relationships — for example, the relationship between income and levels of saving and spending; explain the direction and nature of the relationship using evidence from the data; distinguish correlation from causation.
Wider
  • Analyse a case where an apparent economic cause-and-effect relationship is complicated by a third variable — practising the distinction between correlation and causation
  • Investigate a cause-and-effect relationship in the context of a First Nations community economic issue — for example, how access to financial services affects saving behaviour in remote communities
  • Compare cause-and-effect relationships in consumer behaviour data across different demographic groups, identifying what additional variables might explain differences

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCause-and-effect reasoning in economics requires students to hold the relationship structure in mind: 'X changes, which causes Y to change, because...' The 'because' is the economic reasoning. Students need scaffolded practice constructing the full chain, not just labelling the correlation.
