← AC9HE10S02 Locate, select and analyse information

Evaluating sources for relevance and reliability

AC9HE10S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate economic information and data sources for relevance and reliability, using explicit criteria including who collected the data, when, and for what purpose.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Applies at least two named criteria to evaluate a source's relevance or reliability
  • Identifies at least one assumption or missing information in a source that could affect its reliability

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating sources using contextually specific search.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two relevance or reliability criteria to evaluate a provided economic source.
Typical
Select and analyse economic information and data for relevance (asking: How will this help answer the inquiry question?) and reliability (asking: How and when was the data collected? Who collected it? For what purpose?); explain assumptions or missing information that may affect reliability.
Wider
  • Compare two sources presenting conflicting economic data on the same issue (e.g. different unemployment measures) and evaluate which is more reliable for a specific inquiry purpose
  • Investigate how government statistical agencies (ABS) and advocacy groups may present different data on the same economic phenomenon and explain how purpose shapes data collection and presentation
  • Evaluate a secondary source (news article, commentary) against its primary data source and identify what has been changed, emphasised or omitted in the reporting

Hinge

The reliability evaluation move is what the descriptor's 'analyse' demand requires. Students who accept sources uncritically cannot complete the inquiry work the descriptor specifies — their findings will be built on unstated assumptions or unreliable data.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMake the evaluation criteria explicit and practise applying them to real economic sources. Students commonly evaluate 'quality' vaguely — the structured criteria (who, when, purpose, assumptions) need to become habitual.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApply reliability evaluation to different source types — ABS data, Reserve Bank reports, media articles, advocacy group reports — so students develop differentiated criteria rather than applying the same checklist uniformly.
