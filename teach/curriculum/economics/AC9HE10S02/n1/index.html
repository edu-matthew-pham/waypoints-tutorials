Locating sources using contextually specific search

AC9HE10S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use contextually specific search strategies to locate relevant economic and business sources, including using appropriate terminology and criteria.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two contextually specific search terms derived from the inquiry question
  • Locates at least two sources that are relevant to the inquiry question

Prior knowledge

Students can locate, select and analyse information and data from a range of sources at Year 9 level (AC9HE9S02). They have practised searching for economic information, evaluating sources for relevance and reliability, and using digital tools to organise data. Y10 builds on this with more complex sources, more contextually specific search strategies, and explicit attention to assumptions and missing information in sources.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two contextually specific search terms or criteria (e.g. 'global markets', 'trade agreements Australia case studies') to locate sources relevant to an inquiry question.
Typical
Locate online sources using contextually specific terms (e.g. 'global markets', 'fiscal policy Australia 2024') and criteria (e.g. key words to find 'case studies related to trade agreements'); explain how search strategy affects the quality and relevance of sources found.
Wider
  • Compare the results of searching the same topic using a general search engine vs an academic database or government statistical portal and evaluate the difference in source quality
  • Develop a search protocol for a complex economic inquiry — including Boolean operators, date filters and domain restrictions — and evaluate what the protocol is designed to achieve
  • Investigate how algorithmic filtering in online search affects what economic information people find and evaluate implications for informed decision-making

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPractise the search strategy move across multiple economic topics. Students need to internalise the connection between inquiry question → relevant search terms → source quality before they can reliably find usable sources.
