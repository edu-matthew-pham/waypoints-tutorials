Locating and evaluating sources

AC9HE8S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can locate information from a range of sources using advanced search strategies and evaluate the relevance of sources by asking whether the data or information helps answer the inquiry question.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one search strategy beyond a basic keyword search to locate relevant information
  • Identifies whether a source is relevant to the inquiry question and explains the reason

Prior knowledge

Students can locate, select and organise information and data from a range of sources (AC9HE7S02). At Year 8, the expectation shifts toward more advanced search strategies, explicit source evaluation, and using specialised digital tools to organise data.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one advanced search strategy (e.g. specifying site type or search terms in quotation marks) to locate information on an economic or business issue, and explain why the source found is or is not relevant to the inquiry question.
Typical
Locate information and data from a range of source types (government departments, non-government organisations, news media, statistics agencies) using advanced search functions. Evaluate sources by asking 'How will this data or information help answer the question?' and filter out sources that do not contribute to the investigation.
Wider
  • Compare the same economic issue as reported by different source types (e.g. ABS data, a business news article, an NGO report) and evaluate what each source type can and cannot reliably tell you
  • Investigate how to assess source credibility for economic and business data — examining indicators of authority, currency, methodology and potential bias
  • Research how economists and researchers use primary data sources (ABS, RBA, ASIC) versus secondary commentary, and practise tracing a claim in a secondary source back to its primary data

Hinge

If students cannot locate credible and relevant sources, they cannot produce evidence-based analysis in subsequent nodes. Poor source selection undermines every downstream skill — interpretation, response development and communication all depend on the quality of what has been found.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSearch strategies are procedural and benefit from repeated practice with explicit feedback. Students often over-rely on first-result web searches and need to practise the habit of source type targeting (government statistics, NGO reports, industry data) before it becomes automatic.
