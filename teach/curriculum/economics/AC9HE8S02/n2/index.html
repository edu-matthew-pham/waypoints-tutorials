Selecting and organising data

AC9HE8S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select relevant information and data from located sources and organise it into appropriate formats — including diagrams or tables — using specialised digital tools.

Success criteria

  • Selects information from at least two sources and explains why each was selected as relevant
  • Organises the selected data in a format (table, diagram, or other) that makes relationships or comparisons visible

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating and evaluating sources.

Width

Minimum
Select relevant information from at least two sources and organise it into one format (e.g. a table or diagram) that makes the relationship between the data clear.
Typical
Select relevant information and data from multiple sources and organise it using appropriate formats — including diagrams modelling economic relationships (e.g. market interactions) and tables comparing features of different business types or strategies. Use specialised digital tools (e.g. spreadsheet, diagram tool) where appropriate.
Wider
  • Construct a relational diagram (e.g. circular flow model, supply and demand diagram, market ecosystem map) that accurately represents the economic or business relationships in the data found
  • Organise data from multiple sources into a comparative table that enables direct comparison of features across multiple cases (e.g. features of different business models, tax rates across countries, wage trends by industry)
  • Evaluate two different organisational formats for the same data set (e.g. table vs diagram) and justify which is more useful for the investigation's inquiry question

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyDifferent economic inquiry questions require different organisational formats. Students benefit from practising multiple formats — comparison tables, flow diagrams, data summaries — to build a repertoire they can select from based on what the investigation requires.
