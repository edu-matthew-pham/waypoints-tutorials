Explaining economic issues from data

AC9HE10S03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret information to explain the nature, extent and importance of an economic or business issue.

Success criteria

  • States what a piece of data shows about an economic issue (description)
  • Explains what this means about the nature or extent of the issue (interpretation beyond description)

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret information and data, explain economic and business issues, trends and cause-and-effect relationships, and make predictions at Year 9 level (AC9HE9S03). They have practised interpreting single-variable economic data, identifying trends and articulating cause-and-effect logic. Y10 builds on this with multi-variable data, more complex trend interpretation and more explicit prediction reasoning.

Width

Minimum
Interpret a provided data source about an economic issue and explain in at least two sentences what the data shows about the nature or extent of the issue.
Typical
Interpret information to explain the nature, extent and importance of an economic or business issue — for example, the relationship between standards of living, infant mortality and access to health services; explain what the data reveals and why the issue matters.
Wider
  • Interpret data about a complex multi-dimensional issue (e.g. housing affordability involving income, interest rates, housing supply and geographic distribution) and explain the interaction between factors
  • Evaluate how different data sources on the same issue can support different interpretations, and explain what this means for evidence-based decision-making
  • Investigate a global economic issue (e.g. supply chain disruption, climate-related economic risk) using international data and explain its implications for Australia

Hinge

If students cannot move from data to explanation — from 'this is what the data shows' to 'this is what it means about the issue' — they cannot produce the analytical outputs the descriptor requires. Trend identification without interpretation is a persistent failure mode.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMake the explanation move explicit: what does this data show → what does this mean about the issue → why does this matter? Students who stay at description need scaffolding to take the interpretive step.
