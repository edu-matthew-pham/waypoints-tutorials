← AC9HE10S03 Interpret and predict economic impacts

Cause-and-effect analysis

AC9HE10S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse causes and effects of an economic issue — explaining why the issue arises and what its consequences are for individuals, businesses or the broader economy.

Lessons on this site

Success criteria

  • States a cause-and-effect relationship for an economic issue with at least one intermediate mechanism explained
  • Identifies at least one group affected by the effect and explains why they are affected

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Interpreting multi-variable trends.

Width

Minimum
For one economic issue, state at least one cause and one effect, explaining the connection between them.
Typical
Analyse the causes and effects of an economic issue on individuals or businesses — for example, the reasons for and implications of government intervention in the economy to improve living standards; explain the chain of causation and identify who is affected and how.
Wider
  • Map a multi-step causal chain (e.g. rising inflation → RBA cash rate increase → higher mortgage repayments → reduced consumer spending → slower growth) and evaluate where the chain might break
  • Analyse how the same economic cause produces different effects on different groups (e.g. rising interest rates affect renters, mortgage-holders and savers differently)
  • Investigate an economic issue where the cause-and-effect relationship is contested and evaluate competing explanations using evidence

Hinge

Cause-and-effect reasoning is the core analytical demand of this descriptor. Students who can describe trends but cannot explain why they happen — or what happens as a result — cannot produce the causal analysis the descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkBuild causal chains explicitly: cause → mechanism → effect. The mechanism step is where students most often default to assertion. Modelling multi-step chains with identifiable mechanisms is the key scaffold.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApply causal analysis to varied economic issues — government intervention, consumer decisions, trade — so students generalise the analytical move rather than memorising one causal chain.
