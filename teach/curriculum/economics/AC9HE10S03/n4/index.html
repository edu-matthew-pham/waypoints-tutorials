Making predictions about consumer and financial impacts

AC9HE10S03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse trends and cause-and-effect relationships to make reasoned predictions about who will be affected and how, with reference to economic evidence.

Success criteria

  • Makes a specific prediction about consumer or financial impacts connected to a named trend or causal relationship
  • Provides explicit reasoning for the prediction — the mechanism that links the trend to the predicted outcome

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Cause-and-effect analysis.

Width

Minimum
Based on a provided trend or causal analysis, make one prediction about consumer or financial impacts and explain the reasoning behind it.
Typical
Analyse trends to make predictions about who will be affected and how — for example, trends in productivity and implications for businesses; explain the reasoning underpinning the prediction and acknowledge conditions under which it might not hold.
Wider
  • Make a conditional prediction — 'if [economic condition], then [consumer/financial impact], because [mechanism]' — and evaluate the quality of the prediction against evidence of what actually happened
  • Compare predictions made by different economic commentators about the same trend and evaluate whose reasoning was most sound in retrospect
  • Investigate how businesses use economic trend data to make investment and staffing predictions and evaluate how prediction quality affects business outcomes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPrediction integrates interpretation, trend analysis and causal reasoning into a single forward-looking claim. This is the descriptor's full demand — the ability to use economic evidence to reason about likely future impacts.
