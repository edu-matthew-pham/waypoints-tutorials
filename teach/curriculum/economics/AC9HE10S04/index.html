Evaluate and decide using cost-benefit

AC9HE10S04 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop and evaluate a response to an economic and business issue, using cost-benefit analysis or criteria to decide on a course of action

Prior knowledge

Students can develop and evaluate responses to economic and business issues using cost-benefit analysis or criteria at Year 9 level (AC9HE9S04). They have practised identifying and weighing costs and benefits to justify a decision, and have used criteria-based evaluation in business contexts. Y10 builds on this with more complex financial calculations, multi-variable cost-benefit analysis, and the explicit use of opportunity cost reasoning.

By the end

Students develop and evaluate a response to an economic and business issue using cost-benefit analysis or criteria-based evaluation to justify a recommended course of action.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • evaluating the short- and long-term financial implications of a decision; for example, calculating the costs of different purchasing methods and considering ability to opt out if circumstances change
  • evaluating a response, using a cost-benefit analysis to explain the reasons for a course of action; for example, comparing different types of loans to estimate the short- and long-term financial costs and benefits of each
  • evaluating a response, using criteria such as efficiency, profitability or equity to explain the reasons for a course of action; for example, use of indicators of economic performance to identify variations within the Australian economy
  • examining the opportunity cost consumers and businesses may have to consider when deciding on a course of action; for example, employing more staff instead of upskilling existing staff to improve productivity

Waypoints

  • 1. What cost-benefit analysis involvesHingeStudents can explain what cost-benefit analysis involves and identify the types of costs and benefits relevant to economic and business decisions.
  • 2. Opportunity costHingeStudents can identify the opportunity cost in a decision and explain how it affects the evaluation of a course of action.
  • 3. Evaluating short- and long-term financial implicationsStudents can evaluate the short- and long-term financial implications of a decision, including calculating costs across time and considering flexibility to exit.
  • 4. Evaluating using criteriaStudents can evaluate a response to an economic issue using explicit criteria (e.g. efficiency, profitability, equity) and explain the reasoning behind a recommended course of action.
  • 5. Developing and justifying a responseStudents can develop a response to an economic or business issue and justify it using cost-benefit analysis and/or criteria-based evaluation, incorporating opportunity cost reasoning.
