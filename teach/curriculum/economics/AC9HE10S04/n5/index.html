← AC9HE10S04 Evaluate and decide using cost-benefit

Developing and justifying a response

AC9HE10S04 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop a response to an economic or business issue and justify it using cost-benefit analysis and/or criteria-based evaluation, incorporating opportunity cost reasoning.

Success criteria

  • Produces a specific recommended course of action, not just a description of options
  • Provides at least two pieces of cost-benefit or criteria-based reasoning that justify the recommendation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Evaluating using criteria.

Width

Minimum
Develop a recommended course of action for one economic issue and provide at least two pieces of cost-benefit or criteria-based reasoning to justify it.
Typical
Develop and evaluate a response to an economic and business issue, using cost-benefit analysis or criteria to justify a recommended course of action; incorporate opportunity cost reasoning and address both short- and long-term implications.
Wider
  • Develop a response to a real current economic policy issue (e.g. housing affordability, energy costs) and evaluate it against multiple criteria, acknowledging trade-offs
  • Present a justified response to a business decision scenario and explain how the recommendation would change under different economic conditions
  • Critically evaluate a published response to an economic issue (e.g. a policy proposal, a business strategy) using cost-benefit analysis and identify what the original evaluation missed or overstated

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates CBA, opportunity cost, time-horizon analysis and criteria-based evaluation into a single justified recommendation. It is the descriptor's full demand — not just evaluating, but developing and justifying a response.
