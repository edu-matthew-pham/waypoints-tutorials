Locating sources using targeted search

AC9HE7S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can locate relevant economics and business sources using targeted search techniques, including advanced search functions.

Lessons on this site

  • Where to look, and how to search

Success criteria

  • Locates at least two relevant sources for an economic topic
  • Describes the search terms or strategy used and explains why they were chosen

Prior knowledge

Students can locate, collect and organise information from primary and secondary sources in a range of formats (AC9HS6S02). At Year 7, the demand shifts to selecting from a wider range of source types — including government and NGO websites and digital tools — and to organising using more specialised formats such as tables, graphs and visual displays.

Width

Minimum
Locate two relevant sources for an economic topic using a search engine; describe the search terms used.
Typical
Locate online sources using targeted search techniques — advanced search functions ('define: market'), targeted criteria ('allintext: successful Australian entrepreneurs'), government department and NGO websites; explain how the search approach was tailored to the question.
Wider
  • Compare the results of a broad versus a targeted search for the same economic topic — evaluating which approach yields more relevant, reliable sources
  • Collaborate with a peer to exchange information online with an entrepreneur or industry contact to gather primary source data
  • Explore how to locate First Nations economic and business sources — including community organisations, AIATSIS and First Nations business directories — and evaluate their reliability and relevance

Hinge

If students cannot locate sources reliably using targeted search, their entire information base will be too broad, biased or unreliable to support a meaningful investigation. Weak locating cascades through selection and organisation phases.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTargeted search is a precision skill that degrades when students default to broad keyword searches. Targeted practice with economics-specific search techniques — including 'define:' and 'allintext:' operators — builds reliable searching habits that carry through the investigation.
