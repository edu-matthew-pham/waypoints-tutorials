Selecting relevant information

AC9HE7S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select information and data from sources by evaluating its relevance to their investigation question.

Lessons on this site

  • Keep what answers your question

Success criteria

  • Selects at least two pieces of information from a source with a stated reason for relevance
  • Discards or sets aside information identified as not relevant to the investigation question

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating sources using targeted search.

Width

Minimum
Select two pieces of information from a source and explain how each helps answer the investigation question.
Typical
Select relevant information and data from a range of sources by applying the question 'How will this data or information help answer my question?'; distinguish relevant from irrelevant information; apply to websites of government departments and non-government organisations.
Wider
  • Evaluate conflicting information from two sources on the same economic issue — explaining which is more useful and why
  • Practise selective use of a complex multi-variable dataset: identify only the variables needed to answer a specific question and exclude the rest
  • Evaluate the relevance and reliability of information provided by an entrepreneur contacted online, and identify what follow-up questions would strengthen the data

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish relevant from irrelevant information will collect indiscriminately, producing disorganised and unfocused responses. The selection criterion — 'does this help answer my question?' — is the discipline that makes subsequent organisation and interpretation meaningful.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a practical decision rule they can apply at source level: for each piece of information, ask 'Does this directly help answer my question?' Scaffold this as a routine check, not a post-hoc judgement.
