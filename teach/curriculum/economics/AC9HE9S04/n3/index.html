Developing response processes using CBA results

AC9HE9S04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop processes that incorporate the results of a cost-benefit analysis to formulate a response to an economic or business issue.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Develops at least one process that is grounded in a cost-benefit analysis
  • Explains how the CBA results support the proposed process
  • The process includes specific steps, not just a general recommendation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Opportunity cost in business decision-making.

Width

Minimum
Use the results of a cost-benefit analysis to recommend one process a business could use to remain competitive or address an issue, explaining how the CBA supports the recommendation.
Typical
Develop processes that consider the results of a cost-benefit analysis — applying the elaboration example (processes used by businesses to remain competitive in the global market). Show how CBA outputs (which option has higher net benefit) translate into actionable processes: what steps the business should take, in what sequence, and why. The process should be grounded in the CBA findings, not generic recommendations.
Wider
  • Develop competing response processes for the same business issue from different stakeholder perspectives (e.g. management cost-minimisation process vs worker welfare process) and compare what each CBA prioritises
  • Apply the process-from-CBA approach to a consumer decision context — developing a decision-making process for managing a specific financial risk that incorporates CBA reasoning at each step
  • Develop a response process for a complex multi-stakeholder issue (e.g. a business deciding whether to exit a global market) where different actors' CBA results point in different directions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe connection between CBA findings and actionable processes is easier to see in multiple contexts. Business competitiveness, consumer financial risk, and government resource allocation all require this connection but the process steps look different in each context.
