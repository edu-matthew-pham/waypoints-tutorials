Selecting and justifying a preferred response

AC9HE9S04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select a preferred response to an economic or business issue and justify it using criteria such as consumer and financial risk considerations.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one criterion used to justify the preferred response
  • Applies the criterion explicitly to the available options
  • Explains why the preferred response performs better against the criterion than alternatives

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Developing response processes using CBA results.

Width

Minimum
Select one response option and justify it using at least one named criterion (e.g. consumer risk, financial cost, feasibility).
Typical
Select and justify a preferred response using criteria — applying the elaboration example (criteria such as consideration of consumer and financial risk). For the justification: name the criteria used; explain why these criteria are appropriate for this issue; apply each criterion to the available options; and explain why the preferred response performs better against the criteria than the alternatives. The justification should be explicit about the criteria-to-decision reasoning.
Wider
  • Evaluate two competing responses to the same issue using different criteria sets — explaining how the choice of criteria affects which response is preferred, and discussing what this reveals about whose interests different criteria serve
  • Apply multi-criteria evaluation to a policy issue (e.g. a trade agreement or consumer protection regulation) from both business and consumer perspectives, explaining where the preferred responses diverge and why
  • Justify a preferred response to a First Nations economic issue using criteria that include community wellbeing, cultural continuity and sovereignty alongside conventional economic criteria — explaining how this changes the justification

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates CBA (Node 1), opportunity cost (Node 2), and process development (Node 3) into a complete evaluative response. Students should trace: issue → CBA of options → opportunity cost of choices → process recommendation → criteria-based justification of preferred response.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCriteria-based justification requires students to be explicit about the criteria, apply them systematically to each option, and explain the comparative verdict. Without this structure, justifications default to assertion ('I chose X because it is better').
