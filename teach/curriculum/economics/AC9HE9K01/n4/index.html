Forces shaping Australia's financial landscape

AC9HE9K01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the forces that shape and affect Australia's financial landscape, including deregulation, technology, economic conditions and consumer sentiment.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two forces that shape Australia's financial landscape
  • Explains how each named force affects the behaviour of financial institutions or their customers
  • Uses relevant economic terms (e.g. deregulation, sentiment, capital, competition)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Debt, savings and financial management.

Width

Minimum
Identify and explain one force that has shaped Australia's financial landscape (e.g. financial deregulation or technological change) and describe its effect on financial sector behaviour.
Typical
Explain the range of forces shaping Australia's financial landscape: financial deregulation (opening competition, reducing barriers), technological change (digital banking, fintech, open banking), economic and business activity cycles (how economic conditions affect lending and investment), and consumer and business sentiment (how confidence affects spending, saving and borrowing). Explain how each force affects the behaviour of financial institutions and their customers.
Wider
  • Investigate Australia's financial deregulation in the 1980s — what changed, which institutions were affected, and how it reshaped competition and consumer access to financial products
  • Examine how the Royal Commission into Misconduct in the Banking, Superannuation and Financial Services Industry (2018) changed regulation of Australia's financial sector and its effects on consumer protection
  • Analyse the emergence of open banking in Australia and explain how data-sharing between institutions is changing competitive dynamics and consumer decision-making

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEach force (deregulation, technology, cycles, sentiment) represents a distinct explanatory context. Using multiple real Australian examples — the 1980s deregulation, the GFC, the rise of fintechs — prevents students from learning only one causal story.
