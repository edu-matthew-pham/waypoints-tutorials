Structure of the financial sector

AC9HE9K01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the main types of organisations within Australia's financial sector and describe their core functions.

Success criteria

  • Names at least three types of organisations within Australia's financial sector
  • Describes the core function of each named organisation type
  • Distinguishes between types (e.g. bank vs credit union) based on ownership or structure

Prior knowledge

From Y8: how markets influence resource allocation decisions and the effect of prices (AC9HE8K01); how businesses adapt to market opportunities and changing work (AC9HE8K02); First Nations Australian businesses and entrepreneurs in markets (AC9HE8K03). Students understand basic market mechanisms and business types but have not yet studied the financial sector's specific role as an intermediary.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two types of financial organisations (e.g. banks, credit unions) and describe one core function each (e.g. collecting deposits, lending funds).
Typical
Identify the range of organisations within Australia's financial sector — banks, credit unions, building societies, insurance companies, superannuation funds — and explain their distinct functions including collecting deposits, pooling savings, lending, providing insurance, and managing long-term savings.
Wider
  • Compare the ownership and governance models of mutual organisations (credit unions, building societies) with shareholder-owned banks, examining how structure affects decision-making and consumer outcomes
  • Investigate the role of non-bank financial institutions (e.g. fintech lenders, buy-now-pay-later providers) and how they have changed the structure of Australia's financial sector
  • Examine how First Nations Community Development Finance Institutions (CDFIs) provide financial services to underserved communities, comparing their functions with mainstream banks

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish organisational types from functions will conflate what a financial institution is with what it does, causing persistent confusion when explaining how the sector affects decision-making in subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a structured table: institution type → ownership → core function → who it serves. The category-function distinction is non-obvious and benefits from explicit visual scaffolding before moving to effects.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMultiple institution types (banks, credit unions, super funds, insurance companies) in different contexts help students see that the sector is a system of specialised roles, not just 'banks'.
