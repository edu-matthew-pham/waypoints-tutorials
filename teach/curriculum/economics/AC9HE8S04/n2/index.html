Developing a reasoned response

AC9HE8S04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop a response to an economic and business issue that combines known knowledge with new ideas, identifies and describes reasons informing a recommendation, and addresses the potential costs and benefits.

Success criteria

  • Develops a response that includes a position or recommendation on an economic or business issue
  • Identifies and describes at least one reason supporting the response and at least one potential cost and benefit of the recommended course of action

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying costs and benefits.

Width

Minimum
Develop a response to one economic or business issue that includes: a stated position or recommendation, at least one reason supporting it, and acknowledgement of at least one cost and one benefit.
Typical
Develop a response to an economic and business issue that combines known knowledge with new ideas, identifies and describes the reasons informing a recommendation (e.g. why the government collects taxation revenue and provides services), and identifies the potential costs and benefits of the recommended course of action.
Wider
  • Develop a response that evaluates two or more alternative courses of action against the same set of cost-benefit criteria, and justifies a recommendation by comparing across the alternatives
  • Apply a personal budget and savings plan to an issue (e.g. whether to purchase a good or service) as the basis for a response — demonstrating that a response can be grounded in personal financial planning processes
  • Develop a response to an issue that explicitly acknowledges whose costs and benefits are included and whose may be underweighted or omitted, and reflects on what that means for the quality of the response

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyResponse development improves with practice across different issue types. Students benefit from developing responses to market pricing issues, business strategy issues, and personal financial issues before the full inquiry sequence is assessed.
