Develop responses to issues

AC9HE8S04 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop a response to an economic and business issue, identifying potential costs and benefits

Prior knowledge

Students can develop a response to an economic and business issue identifying potential costs and benefits (AC9HE7S04). At Year 8, the expectation develops toward responses that combine known knowledge with new ideas and that require explicit identification and description of costs and benefits.

By the end

Students develop a response to an economic and business issue, identifying potential costs and benefits and explaining the reasoning behind their response.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • developing a response to an issue by combining known knowledge with new ideas; for example, using a personal budget and savings plan to decide whether to purchase a good or service
  • identifying and describing reasons informing a recommendation; for example, the reasons the government collects taxation revenue and provides services to individuals and communities
  • identifying the potential costs and benefits of a decision; for example, doing casual work involves identifying benefits such as money earned, and costs such as less certainty about work hours each week
  • explaining that making an economic and business decision involves choice and foregoing an alternative want; for example, the goods and/or services they must forgo or give up in order to satisfy a particular want

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying costs and benefitsHingeStudents can identify the potential costs and benefits of a decision, including non-financial dimensions, and explain what is foregone when a choice is made.
  • 2. Developing a reasoned responseStudents can develop a response to an economic and business issue that combines known knowledge with new ideas, identifies and describes reasons informing a recommendation, and addresses the potential costs and benefits.
