Opportunity cost

AC9HE7K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the concept of opportunity cost — what is given up when a choice is made — in relation to a specific allocation decision.

  • Opportunity cost - what you give up

Success criteria

  • Defines opportunity cost accurately in own words
  • Identifies the specific alternative forgone in a given resource allocation example

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Economic scarcity.

Width

Minimum
Define opportunity cost; identify the trade-off in one personal or community resource decision (e.g. time, money, land).
Typical
Explain opportunity cost as the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made; apply to personal time and money decisions and to community or business resource allocation decisions; explain that opportunity cost is not the price paid but the value of the best alternative not chosen.
Wider
  • Analyse opportunity costs in government spending decisions; for example, if state funds are directed to roads rather than hospitals, what is the opportunity cost?
  • Investigate how opportunity cost applies to non-monetary resources such as time, environmental capacity or cultural land use
  • Compare how First Nations communities weigh opportunity costs when balancing economic development against environmental and cultural custodianship

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need exposure to a range of decision contexts — personal, household, community, government — to distinguish opportunity cost from merely 'what you didn't buy'. Each new context reinforces that opportunity cost is always a real alternative forgone, not an abstract calculation.
