What government intervention means

AC9HE10K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why markets alone do not always produce desirable outcomes and why governments intervene in the economy.

Success criteria

  • States at least two reasons why governments intervene in the economy
  • Connects a reason for intervention to a category of government tool (e.g. inequality → redistribution)

Prior knowledge

Students understand how economic decision-making involves the interdependence of consumers, businesses, the financial sector and government (AC9HE9K02). They have explored how Australia trades with other nations and its patterns with Asia (AC9HE9K03). They can explain how different actors in the economy interact and how decisions ripple across the system. Students have also begun working with AC9HE10K01 — how economic indicators signal the need for government response.

Width

Minimum
State at least two reasons why the Australian Government intervenes in the economy (e.g. to address market failure, improve living standards, reduce inequality).
Typical
Explain the rationale for government intervention — market failure, inequality, unemployment and poverty — and identify the general categories of government tools available (fiscal policy, income redistribution, regulation).
Wider
  • Examine a specific market failure (e.g. pollution, public goods provision, monopoly) and evaluate why the market alone cannot address it
  • Compare the case for and against government intervention using a contemporary Australian policy debate (e.g. housing affordability, energy markets)
  • Investigate how views on the appropriate scope of government intervention have changed over time in Australia

Hinge

Students who do not understand that intervention is a response to a problem — not an arbitrary action — will describe policies without explaining their purpose. The rationale is the connective tissue linking indicator signals (K01) to specific policy tools.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkBuild the logic chain: what the market produces without intervention → what problem this creates → what the government can do about it. This scaffold prevents students from treating policies as a list to memorise rather than responses to economic conditions.
