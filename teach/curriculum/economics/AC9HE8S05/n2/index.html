← AC9HE8S05 Create economic explanations

Using data and sources to support explanations

AC9HE8S05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create a description or explanation that uses data or information from sources in appropriate formats to support decisions and conclusions about an economic or business issue.

Success criteria

  • Creates an explanation that uses at least one piece of data or information from a source
  • Explains the connection between the cited data or information and the conclusion it supports

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Orienting an audience to an issue.

Width

Minimum
Create an explanation of one economic or business issue that references at least one piece of data or information from a source, and explains how that data supports a conclusion.
Typical
Create a description or explanation using data or information presented in appropriate formats to support decisions and conclusions about an economic and business issue. Reference sources accurately, explain the connection between the data and the conclusion, and use economic and business vocabulary accurately throughout.
Wider
  • Create an explanation that integrates both qualitative information (case study, expert commentary) and quantitative data (statistics, trend data) — and analyses what each type of evidence contributes to the explanation
  • Produce an explanation in a data visualisation format (e.g. an annotated graph, an infographic with supporting text) and evaluate whether the format communicates the issue and evidence more or less effectively than prose
  • Evaluate an economic explanation produced by someone else (a news article, a student sample) against criteria: Does it orient the audience? Does it use concepts accurately? Does the evidence support the conclusion? Provide written feedback using those criteria

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyCreating evidence-referenced explanations improves through repeated practice across different issue types and formats. Students benefit from producing explanations of market issues, business strategy issues, and taxation/financial planning issues before formal assessment.
