Business planning processes

AC9HE8K05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain business processes used to manage finances and plan in the short and long term, including business plans, borrowing from financial institutions and building savings through interest.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two business processes used for financial planning or management
  • Explains how one of these processes helps a business work toward a short-term or long-term financial objective

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Business financial records and decision-making.

Width

Minimum
Identify one short-term and one long-term business financial planning process and explain how each helps a business manage its finances toward an objective.
Typical
Identify and explain business processes used for short-term and long-term financial planning: devising a business plan, borrowing from financial institutions to fund growth or operations, and building savings through earned interest. Explain how each process serves a different planning horizon or financial need.
Wider
  • Investigate the components of a business plan and why banks and investors require them before providing finance — examining what a business plan signals about financial preparedness and risk
  • Research how small businesses in Australia access finance through different channels (bank loans, microfinance, crowdfunding, government grants) and the advantages and constraints of each
  • Examine how a business uses break-even analysis as a planning tool — calculating the volume of sales needed to cover costs and what this means for short-term survival and long-term investment decisions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepBusiness planning processes are easiest to understand through concrete cases — a start-up seeking a bank loan, an established business building a reserve fund, a growing business with a multi-year investment plan. Different scenarios illustrate different planning horizons.
