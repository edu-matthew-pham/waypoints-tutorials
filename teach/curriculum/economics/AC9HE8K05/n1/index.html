Personal financial objectives and planning tools

AC9HE8K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify ways that short-term and long-term personal financial objectives can be achieved, including through developing a budget and savings plan.

Success criteria

  • Distinguishes between a short-term and long-term personal financial objective
  • Explains how a budget or savings plan can be used to work toward a specific financial objective

Prior knowledge

Students understand consumer rights and financial responsibilities (AC9HE7K05). They understand income sources and the reasons individuals work (AC9HE7K04). They have context from AC9HE8K04 on how the taxation system affects individual financial decisions.

Width

Minimum
Identify one short-term and one long-term personal financial objective and explain one process (e.g. a budget or savings plan) that could help achieve each.
Typical
Identify ways that short-term and long-term personal financial objectives can be achieved, explaining how tools such as a personal budget and savings plan work, and how they can be adapted to different income and expenditure situations.
Wider
  • Investigate how interest rates affect personal saving and borrowing decisions — calculating the effect of compound interest on a savings target over different timeframes
  • Examine how life stage affects financial planning priorities: comparing the financial objectives and planning tools relevant to a young person starting work versus someone approaching retirement
  • Research how financial literacy interventions in schools affect long-term financial wellbeing outcomes, and evaluate what financial skills are most important to develop early

Hinge

The personal financial planning node establishes the conceptual frame (short-term vs long-term objectives; planning as a process not a one-off decision) that the business financial planning node builds on. Students who have only encountered budgeting as a static document rather than a dynamic planning process will not connect it to the business tools in the next node.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsBudget construction is a procedural skill with common errors (confusing income and expenditure categories, conflating savings with surplus). Students benefit from practising with real-number scenarios before the concept is extended to business contexts.
