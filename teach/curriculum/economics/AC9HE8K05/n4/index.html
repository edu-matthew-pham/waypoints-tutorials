Planning and budgeting — integrated

AC9HE8K05 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how individuals and businesses use planning and budgeting processes to achieve short-term and long-term financial objectives, drawing on appropriate financial records and planning tools.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explains the difference between a short-term and long-term financial objective in a realistic scenario
  • Identifies and explains at least two planning or budgeting processes an individual or business could use to achieve those objectives

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Business planning processes.

Width

Minimum
Explain how either an individual or a business would use at least two planning or budgeting processes together to work toward a stated financial objective.
Typical
Explain how individuals and businesses use a connected set of planning and budgeting processes — combining personal budgets, savings plans, business financial records and business planning tools — to achieve both short-term and long-term financial objectives. Apply to a scenario involving real financial decisions.
Wider
  • Analyse a scenario where short-term and long-term objectives are in tension (e.g. spending now to grow a business versus building financial resilience) and evaluate how planning tools help manage that trade-off
  • Investigate how financial planning processes differ for a First Nations community-owned enterprise, where objectives include community wellbeing and cultural maintenance alongside financial sustainability
  • Research financial planning challenges faced by young Australians entering the workforce today (housing affordability, superannuation, study debt) and evaluate which planning tools are most relevant

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe integration task should require connecting personal financial planning (budget, savings plan) with business financial planning (records, business plan) around a concrete scenario, making explicit that both use similar principles at different scales.
