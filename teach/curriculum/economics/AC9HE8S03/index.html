Interpret economic data

AC9HE8S03 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret information and data to identify economic and business issues, trends and economic cause-and-effect relationships

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret information and data to identify economic and business issues, trends and cause-and-effect relationships (AC9HE7S03). At Year 8, the expectation develops toward interpreting multi-variable data and identifying cause-and-effect relationships within economic and business issues.

By the end

Students interpret information and data — including tables, graphs and multi-variable data — to identify economic and business issues, trends and cause-and-effect relationships.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • interpreting information to identify economic and business issues; for example, inferring a change in the price of a key commodity as a signal about scarcity of resources or increasing consumer expenditure
  • interpreting data displayed in tables and graphs to identify trends and answer questions such as, 'For a 10-year period, what is the trend in the percentage of people over 60 paying income tax?'
  • interpreting multi-variable data to identify a cause-and-effect relationship within an economic and business issue; for example, an increase in income earned by an individual and taxation paid, or when the supply of a good and service increases, the price adjusts

Waypoints

  • 1. Interpreting to identify issues and signalsHingeStudents can interpret information to identify economic and business issues, including inferring what a change in price or expenditure signals about an underlying economic condition.
  • 2. Reading trends from dataStudents can interpret data displayed in tables and graphs to identify trends over time and use those trends to answer a specific inquiry question.
  • 3. Interpreting multi-variable cause-and-effectStudents can interpret multi-variable data to identify a cause-and-effect relationship within an economic or business issue.
