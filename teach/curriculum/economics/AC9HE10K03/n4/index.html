← AC9HE10K03 Major financial decisions

Factors and consequences — integrated analysis

AC9HE10K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse factors influencing a major financial decision and connect these to predicted short- and long-term consequences, demonstrating integrated understanding of the descriptor.

Success criteria

  • Analyses a named major financial decision by identifying at least two factors and at least one consequence per factor
  • Distinguishes between short- and long-term consequences and explains at least one trade-off in the decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Short- and long-term consequences.

Width

Minimum
For one major financial decision, explain two influencing factors and one consequence that flows from each factor.
Typical
Analyse a major financial decision by identifying the key influencing factors, explaining how they shape the decision, and evaluating the likely short- and long-term consequences — including trade-offs between convenience, cost and long-term financial wellbeing.
Wider
  • Analyse a real case study (e.g. a young person taking on a car loan with high interest while receiving youth allowance) using the full factor and consequence framework
  • Evaluate how the ability to 'opt out' if circumstances change should be factored into a major financial decision, and apply this to a real product comparison
  • Design a decision-making guide for a specific major financial decision targeted at a Year 10 audience, demonstrating command of factors and consequences

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe integration demand is the descriptor's full scope — analysing factors *and* consequences together. This node is where students demonstrate that they can connect the analytical moves from nodes 2 and 3 into a coherent account of a specific decision.
