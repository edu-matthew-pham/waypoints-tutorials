← AC9HE10K03 Major financial decisions

Short- and long-term consequences

AC9HE10K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain both the short- and long-term consequences of major financial decisions, distinguishing costs from benefits across different time horizons.

Success criteria

  • Distinguishes at least one short-term and one long-term consequence for a named major financial decision
  • Correctly identifies whether each consequence is a cost or a benefit and provides supporting reasoning

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Factors influencing major decisions.

Width

Minimum
For one major financial decision, identify at least one short-term and one long-term consequence, distinguishing whether each is a cost or a benefit.
Typical
Analyse the short- and long-term costs and benefits of major consumer and financial decisions — including ability to make loan repayments, available savings, depreciation, maintenance costs and insurance, as well as benefits such as independence and convenience (e.g. for purchasing a car or home).
Wider
  • Model the total cost of ownership of a specific major purchase (e.g. a car) over 5 years, accounting for depreciation, insurance, fuel, maintenance and opportunity cost of capital
  • Compare two financing pathways for the same major purchase (e.g. cash vs loan vs lease) and evaluate which produces better long-term outcomes under different assumptions
  • Investigate how a major financial decision taken early in life (e.g. student debt, early home purchase) constrains or enables subsequent financial decisions through compound effects

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary the major decision context — vehicle purchase, home purchase, personal loan — so students can identify which consequences are universal (opportunity cost, ongoing repayment commitment) and which are specific to the decision type.
