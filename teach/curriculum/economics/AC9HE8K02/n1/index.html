Identifying market opportunities

AC9HE8K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how businesses identify needs, niches and gaps in established markets and how these guide the development of new products or services.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a market need, niche or gap that a real business responded to
  • Explains how identifying that opportunity influenced what the business decided to produce or offer

Prior knowledge

Students understand the reasons businesses exist and how different types of businesses provide goods and services (AC9HE7K02). They understand entrepreneur characteristics and how these influence business success (AC9HE7K03). They understand how markets allocate resources through price signals (AC9HE8K01).

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of a business that identified a gap or niche in the market and explain how that opportunity guided what it produced or offered.
Typical
Explain how businesses systematically identify needs, niches and gaps in established markets, and how this analysis shapes the development of specific goods or services. Use two or more examples drawn from different market sectors (e.g. electric vehicles, solar energy, health tech).
Wider
  • Investigate how market research methods (surveys, data analytics, focus groups) help businesses identify opportunities before a product or service exists
  • Examine how disruptive innovations create entirely new market categories rather than filling existing gaps — e.g. how streaming services did not just fill a gap in DVD rental but eliminated the category
  • Research how social enterprises and not-for-profit businesses identify opportunities that traditional profit-seeking businesses overlook, particularly in underserved communities

Hinge

Understanding how businesses identify opportunities is the foundation for explaining both technology-driven adaptation and workforce change — students who cannot explain this link will treat later nodes as disconnected examples rather than a coherent picture of business adaptation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a range of concrete business examples — across industries and scales — to see that opportunity identification is a generalised business behaviour, not a quirk of specific companies. Build the repertoire before asking for analysis.
