Changing nature of work

AC9HE8K02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain current and historical influences on how people work, including technological change, globalisation, communication changes and workforce casualisation.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two distinct influences on the changing nature of work
  • Explains, using a specific example, how one of these influences has changed the way a type of work is performed or organised in Australia

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Technology-driven business adaptation.

Width

Minimum
Identify and explain two influences on the changing nature of work in Australia (e.g. technological change and casualisation), giving at least one concrete example for each.
Typical
Explain multiple influences on the ways people work including: technological change, outsourced labour in the global economy, rapid communication changes, and casualisation of the workforce. Use specific Australian examples and explain how each influence changes employment arrangements or job types.
Wider
  • Investigate changes to the Australian workforce over time using data: jobs growth and decline across industries, changing career length patterns, and shifting demographic composition of the workforce
  • Examine the gig economy in Australia — the rights and responsibilities of workers in platform-mediated casual work (ride share, food delivery, freelance platforms) and the policy debates around classification as employees vs. contractors
  • Research how corporate social responsibility, sustainability practices and changing consumer values are influencing what kinds of work businesses create and how they manage human resources

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.
