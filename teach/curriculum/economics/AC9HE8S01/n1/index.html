Broad and targeted question types

AC9HE8S01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between broad inquiry questions and targeted questions that use economics and business concepts and terms, and explain why targeted questions produce more useful investigations.

Success criteria

  • Develops at least one question using an economics or business concept or term accurately
  • Explains why a targeted concept-driven question is more useful for investigation than a broad question about the same issue

Prior knowledge

Students can develop questions to investigate a contemporary economic and business issue (AC9HE7S01). At Year 8, the expectation shifts toward questions that are more precisely targeted using economics and business concepts and terms, and toward a wider range of issue types.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of a broad question and one targeted question about the same economic or business issue; explain what makes the targeted question more useful for investigation.
Typical
Develop a range of questions for an investigation — including broad framing questions and targeted questions using economics and business concepts and terms (e.g. 'How is the price of goods and services determined?' and 'Why do changes in market price act as a signal about how scarce resources should be allocated?'). Explain what each type of question contributes to the investigation.
Wider
  • Compare the same economic issue investigated using only broad questions versus targeted concept-driven questions, and evaluate the difference in the depth and usefulness of evidence gathered
  • Practise converting everyday observations ('things are getting more expensive') into precise economic questions using specific concepts ('How does the relationship between supply and demand explain recent changes in housing rental prices in Australia?')
  • Investigate how professional economists and journalists frame questions differently about the same issue, and analyse what the different framings reveal about their purposes

Hinge

The distinction between broad and targeted questions determines the quality of all subsequent inquiry work — students who cannot frame targeted questions will produce unfocused investigations and struggle to locate relevant evidence in subsequent skill nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe shift from 'why does X happen?' to a concept-anchored question requires understanding what economic concepts are available and what question type produces useful evidence. Explicit scaffolding — listing relevant concepts, then asking 'which concept does this issue involve?' — develops the targeting habit.
