Orienting an audience to an economic issue

AC9HE7S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop the opening of a response that orients the audience to an economic issue using relevant economic concepts and terms.

Success criteria

  • Writes an opening that names the economic issue and its relevance to the audience
  • Uses at least two economic or business terms accurately in the opening

Prior knowledge

Students can present descriptions and explanations drawing on findings and viewpoints from sources using relevant terms and conventions (AC9HS6S07). The Year 7 demand adds domain-specific economic vocabulary — market, income, business, goods and services, costs and benefits — and requires students to embed data and information from sources explicitly in their explanations.

Width

Minimum
Write an opening paragraph that names the issue, identifies the audience, and uses at least two economic terms accurately.
Typical
Develop a response opening that orients the audience (peers or community members) to the economic issue — introducing the issue, explaining its relevance, and using economic and business concepts and terms such as market, income, business, goods and services, costs and benefits accurately and purposefully.
Wider
  • Adapt the same economic issue explanation for two different audiences — for example, a Year 7 peer and an adult community member — comparing how vocabulary, detail level and framing shift
  • Evaluate a sample economic explanation for audience orientation: does it establish the issue clearly? Does the vocabulary suit the audience? What would improve it?
  • Develop an orientation for an economic issue affecting a specific community (e.g. a First Nations regional community) where the audience is community members rather than students

Hinge

Students who cannot orient their audience to the issue will produce explanations that assume shared context and vocabulary, making their work inaccessible and communicatively ineffective. The orientation is also the point at which economic vocabulary is established — failures here cascade into imprecise use throughout the explanation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need explicit scaffolding for how to open an economic explanation: name the issue, state why it matters, define any specialist terms the audience may not know, then proceed to the argument. Model this structure before students apply it independently.
