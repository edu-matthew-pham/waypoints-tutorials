Why businesses exist

AC9HE7K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify multiple reasons why businesses exist beyond profit, including employment, community service, and production of goods and services.

Success criteria

  • Lists at least two distinct reasons businesses exist, beyond profit
  • Identifies one goods-providing and one service-providing business with an explanation of what each provides

Prior knowledge

Students have explored consumer choices and how individuals make financial decisions (AC9HS6K08). They are familiar with the idea of goods and services from everyday life but have not formally analysed why businesses exist, the range of business structures, or the distinction between goods-producing and service-providing enterprises.

Width

Minimum
Identify two or more reasons why businesses exist, including profit and at least one non-profit reason; give a concrete local example.
Typical
Explain why businesses exist across a range of purposes — profit, employment, providing goods and services, meeting community needs; distinguish between goods-producing and service-providing businesses using local examples including shopping centres, markets, online platforms and independent stores.
Wider
  • Investigate a First Nations-owned business and examine its founding purposes, exploring how community, cultural and economic motivations intersect
  • Compare why a for-profit corporation, a not-for-profit organisation, and a cooperative exist, analysing how their purposes shape what they provide and how
  • Examine how remote community stores differ from urban retailers in purpose, supply constraints and community role

Hinge

Understanding that businesses exist for multiple reasons — not profit alone — is foundational for correctly analysing different business types in the next node. Students who reduce all business existence to profit-seeking will misclassify cooperatives, not-for-profits and First Nations enterprises, blocking meaningful engagement with business diversity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepLocal community examples — including First Nations businesses, cooperatives and community stores — resolve the profit-only misconception more effectively than abstract definitions, because they make visible the genuine diversity of business purposes.
