← AC9HE7K02 Types of businesses

Business types, goods, services and integration

AC9HE7K02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how different types of businesses provide goods and services, connecting business structure and purpose to what is produced, how it reaches consumers, and why this matters.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least two different business types provide goods or services differently, connecting structure to provision
  • Uses business terms (sole proprietor, corporation, cooperative, goods, services, franchise, not-for-profit) accurately in explanation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Types of business structures.

Width

Minimum
Explain how one business type provides goods or services, connecting its structure to what it produces and how it reaches consumers.
Typical
Explain how a range of business types provide goods and services across different distribution channels (shopping centres, markets, online, independent stores, remote community stores); analyse how business structure and purpose shape what is provided and to whom.
Wider
  • Compare how a First Nations-owned business and a major corporation provide similar goods or services — examining differences in supply chain, ownership, community benefit and cultural purpose
  • Evaluate how the shift to online business has changed the types of goods and services available and which business structures benefit most from digital distribution
  • Investigate a social enterprise: how does its structure enable it to pursue a community mission while still operating commercially?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node coordinates the two prior building blocks — purposes and structures — into a complete explanation of how business type shapes what is provided and how. Students should be able to reason from structure to provision, not just label the type.
