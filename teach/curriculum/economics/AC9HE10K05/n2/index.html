Productivity improvement strategies

AC9HE10K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain strategies that businesses use to improve productivity, including training, capital investment, R&D and technology applications.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two productivity improvement strategies with different mechanisms (e.g. one human capital, one technology)
  • Explains how a named strategy is expected to increase output, reduce costs or improve quality

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Workforce management processes.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two strategies for improving productivity and explain how each is expected to increase output or reduce input.
Typical
Identify and explain productivity improvement strategies — training and upskilling, capital investment, increasing R&D, investment in technology applications, use of just-in-time inventory systems, and collection and analysis of data to inform product development or service delivery; explain the mechanism by which each improves productivity.
Wider
  • Investigate how a specific technology application (e.g. automation, AI-assisted logistics, data analytics) has changed productivity in a named industry and evaluate costs and benefits of adoption
  • Examine the productivity paradox — why significant technology investment has not always produced proportionate productivity gains — and evaluate explanations
  • Compare just-in-time inventory against traditional buffer-stock approaches and evaluate the productivity and risk trade-offs of each

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApply productivity strategies to varied industry contexts — manufacturing, services, healthcare — so students understand which strategies generalise and which are sector-specific.
