Workforce management processes

AC9HE10K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain strategies businesses use to manage their workforce effectively, including organisational structures, decision-making culture and employee conditions.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two distinct workforce management strategies and explains the purpose of each
  • Explains how at least one strategy improves workforce engagement or performance

Prior knowledge

Students understand processes businesses use to create and maintain competitive advantage, including the role of entrepreneurs (AC9HE9K04). They can explain how businesses respond to market conditions, manage risk and use innovation to maintain advantage. They have a working understanding of business structures and strategy at Year 9 level.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two workforce management strategies (e.g. organisational structure choices, flexible work arrangements) and explain how each can improve workforce performance.
Typical
Identify and explain strategies for improving workforce management — horizontal vs vertical organisational structures, transparency in decision-making, encouraging initiative, paid parental leave, flexible work arrangements, employer responsibilities to workers and government; explain how each strategy affects workforce engagement and performance.
Wider
  • Investigate how workplace flexibility (e.g. remote work, compressed weeks) affects productivity outcomes and evaluate the evidence for and against flexible work arrangements
  • Compare horizontal and vertical organisational structures in a real industry context and evaluate which structure suits different business types and scales
  • Examine First Nations Australian workforce participation initiatives (e.g. cross-cultural training, 'Closing the Gap' commitments, corporate diversity commitments) and evaluate their design and impact

Hinge

Workforce management is the first of two distinct areas in this descriptor. Students who cannot distinguish between management strategies (structure, culture, conditions) and productivity strategies (training, technology, R&D) will conflate the two in assessments.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDistinguish workforce *management* (how the business organises, motivates and supports its people) from productivity *improvement* (how the business enables people to produce more). Students frequently run these together.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApply workforce management strategies to varied business types — small business, corporation, government organisation — so students can see which strategies are universal and which depend on scale or sector.
