Economic interdependence

AC9HE9K02 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how economic decision-making involves the interdependence of consumers, businesses, the financial sector and government

Prior knowledge

From AC9HE9K01 (direct predecessor): Australia's financial sector — its structure, functions, products and global role. From Y8: market mechanisms and price signals (AC9HE8K01), business adaptation (AC9HE8K02). Students understand how individual actors operate in markets but have not yet analysed how their decisions are mutually dependent within local, national and global economic systems.

By the end

Students explain how economic decision-making involves the interdependence of consumers, businesses, the financial sector and government.

Waypoints

  • 1. The circular flow of economic decision-makingHingeStudents can explain how consumers, businesses, the financial sector and government play interdependent roles in economic decision-making within local, national and global contexts.
  • 2. Globalisation and supply chainsStudents can explain how globalisation supports transnational corporations using supply chains, and can describe advantages and disadvantages for businesses, workers and consumers.
  • 3. Implications of global economic interdependence for AustraliaStudents can analyse implications of interdependence within the global economy for Australian consumers, workers and businesses, including costs and disruption risks.
  • 4. How national economic conditions affect other nationsStudents can explain, using examples, how changes to one nation's economic conditions affect other nations through interdependent relationships.
