Implications of global economic interdependence for Australia

AC9HE9K02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Economics and Business · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse implications of interdependence within the global economy for Australian consumers, workers and businesses, including costs and disruption risks.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two implications of global economic interdependence for Australian actors
  • Explains the mechanism by which a change in another country's conditions affects Australian consumers, workers or businesses
  • Distinguishes implications for different stakeholder groups (consumers vs workers vs businesses)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Globalisation and supply chains.

Width

Minimum
Identify one implication of global economic interdependence for Australian consumers or businesses (e.g. cost effects from overseas regulation changes or supply disruptions).
Typical
Analyse the implications of global economic interdependence for Australian consumers (product costs, price fluctuations, access to variety), workers (employment in trade-exposed industries, skill demands) and businesses (cost structures, supply reliability, regulatory exposure). Include examples of how changes to regulations, economic conditions or disruptions in another country affect Australian actors.
Wider
  • Investigate how changes to Chinese economic policy or consumer preferences have affected Australian export industries (e.g. iron ore, education, agricultural exports) and trace implications for workers and businesses
  • Examine how trade sanctions or geopolitical tensions (e.g. trade disputes between major economies) create uncertainty for Australian businesses operating in global markets
  • Analyse how digital trade and platform economy companies (e.g. streaming services, ride-sharing) have created new forms of global economic interdependence affecting Australian consumers and workers

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe implications vary significantly by stakeholder group and industry. Multiple Australian case contexts (manufacturing, agriculture, services, digital economy) prevent students from generalising from one sector to all.
