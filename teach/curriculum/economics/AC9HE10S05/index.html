Create referenced economic arguments

AC9HE10S05 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

create descriptions, explanations and arguments, using economic and business knowledge, concepts and terms that incorporate and acknowledge and research findings

Prior knowledge

Students can create descriptions, explanations and arguments using economic and business knowledge, concepts and terms that incorporate and acknowledge research findings at Year 9 level (AC9HE9S05). They have practised using discipline-specific vocabulary, presenting structured arguments and acknowledging sources. Y10 builds on this with more complex arguments, authoritative tone, and use of a broader vocabulary set across the full Year 10 topic scope.

By the end

Students create descriptions, explanations and arguments using economic and business knowledge, concepts and terms, incorporating and acknowledging research findings for varied purposes and audiences.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • presenting a description or explanation that orients the audience; for example, peers or representatives of businesses, to the issue, using relevant economic and business concepts and terms such as "economic indicators", "government intervention", "fiscal policy", "standard of living", "economic performance", "federal Budget", "productivity", "organisational structures", "workforce management", "superannuation", "interdependence", "trade", "comparative advantage", "globalisation", "supply chain" and "insurance"
  • explaining decisions and conclusions about a business issue, supported by reasons and representations of data in a range of formats; for example, financial statements or information sheet and research in appropriate formats, such as reports and webpages
  • presenting an argument for a proposal for action in response to an economic or business issue, applying tone appropriate to purpose; for example, using an authoritative tone when presenting an argument and making predictions about impacts, and audience; for example, a business manager or entrepreneur

Waypoints

  • 1. Using economic vocabulary accuratelyHingeStudents can use economic and business terms accurately and contextually — selecting the right term for the right idea and using it in a way that demonstrates understanding.
  • 2. Creating descriptions and explanationsStudents can create structured descriptions and explanations of economic issues that orient the audience to the issue and incorporate research findings with acknowledgement.
  • 3. Constructing economic argumentsStudents can construct an argument for a course of action in response to an economic or business issue, applying an authoritative tone appropriate to purpose and audience.
