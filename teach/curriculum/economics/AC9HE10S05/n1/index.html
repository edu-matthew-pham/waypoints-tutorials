Using economic vocabulary accurately

AC9HE10S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Economics and Business · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Economics and Business: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use economic and business terms accurately and contextually — selecting the right term for the right idea and using it in a way that demonstrates understanding.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least five economic terms accurately, in context, without significant definitional errors
  • Selects terms appropriate to the specific topic (i.e. does not apply terms generically across all topics)

Prior knowledge

Students can create descriptions, explanations and arguments using economic and business knowledge, concepts and terms that incorporate and acknowledge research findings at Year 9 level (AC9HE9S05). They have practised using discipline-specific vocabulary, presenting structured arguments and acknowledging sources. Y10 builds on this with more complex arguments, authoritative tone, and use of a broader vocabulary set across the full Year 10 topic scope.

Width

Minimum
Use at least five economic or business terms accurately in an explanation of an economic issue, selecting terms that are appropriate to the specific topic.
Typical
Use economic and business terms accurately in written and oral communication, including terms such as 'economic indicators', 'government intervention', 'fiscal policy', 'standard of living', 'economic performance', 'federal Budget', 'productivity', 'organisational structures', 'workforce management', 'superannuation', 'interdependence', 'trade', 'comparative advantage', 'globalisation', 'supply chain' and 'insurance'.
Wider
  • Analyse how expert economic commentators (RBA Governor, Treasury Secretary) use technical vocabulary strategically to shape audience understanding and evaluate the communicative choices made
  • Compare how the same economic concept is described for specialist audiences (academic, policy) vs general audiences (news, social media) and evaluate what is gained and lost
  • Investigate how economic terminology has evolved — identifying terms that have changed meaning, been replaced or become contested — and evaluate implications for communication

Hinge

Accurate vocabulary use is the baseline for credible economic communication. Students who use terms loosely or interchangeably (e.g. confusing 'fiscal' and 'monetary', or 'income' and 'wealth') undermine the authority of their arguments.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPractise accurate term use across multiple topics — not just in one explanation. Repeated use in varied contexts builds precision. Peer feedback on vocabulary accuracy is particularly effective here.
