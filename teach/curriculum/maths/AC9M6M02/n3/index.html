← AC9M6M02 Area of rectangles

Area in practical problem contexts

AC9M6M02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can formulate and solve practical problems requiring the area of a rectangle, including problems requiring decomposition of irregular shapes into rectangles.

Success criteria

  • Student solves a practical area problem showing dimensions, formula, calculation, and units.
  • Student correctly finds a missing dimension when area and one side are given.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Area with decimal and fractional dimensions.

Width

Minimum
Solve at least two practical problems requiring area of a rectangle; show working including dimensions, formula application, and units in the answer.
Typical
Solve practical area problems including: finding area from given dimensions, finding a missing dimension given area, decomposing an L-shaped or compound region into rectangles; work with contexts including flooring, gardening, and tiling.
Wider
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' use of area and spatial reasoning in design — e.g. traditional patterns, weaving, or land management — and connect geometric principles to these contexts
  • Design a room or garden layout with specific area requirements and multiple constraints, calculating areas and comparing options

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPractical area problems require students to identify the shape type, select dimensions, apply the formula correctly with units, and interpret the result in context. Decomposing compound shapes adds another layer of coordination. The integration of these moves is the practical problem-solving goal.
