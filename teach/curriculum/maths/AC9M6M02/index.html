Area of rectangles

AC9M6M02 · Year 6 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Establish the formula for the area of a rectangle and use it to solve practical problems

Prior knowledge

Students can solve practical problems involving the perimeter and area of regular and irregular shapes (AC9M5M02). They can measure area using square units. They have not yet formally established the area formula for a rectangle through reasoning, nor applied it systematically to practical problems with decimal dimensions.

By the end

Students can establish the formula for the area of a rectangle through reasoning and use it to solve practical problems.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using one centimetre grid paper to construct rectangles, recording dimensions and areas in a table
  • using the relationship between square units and the array structure to derive a formula for area of a rectangle
  • solving problems involving comparison of lengths and areas using appropriate units
  • investigating the connection between perimeters of different rectangles with the same area

Waypoints

  • 1. Establishing the area formulaHingeStudents can explain why the area of a rectangle is length × width by connecting the formula to the array structure of square unit counting.
  • 2. Area with decimal and fractional dimensionsHingeStudents can apply the area formula to rectangles with decimal dimensions and interpret the result in appropriate square units.
  • 3. Area in practical problem contextsStudents can formulate and solve practical problems requiring the area of a rectangle, including problems requiring decomposition of irregular shapes into rectangles.
