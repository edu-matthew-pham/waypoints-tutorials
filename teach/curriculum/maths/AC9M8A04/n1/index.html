Effect of gradient parameter on linear graphs

AC9M8A04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students investigate the effect of varying the gradient m in y = mx + b on the graph and generalise the relationship between m and steepness/direction.

Lessons on this site

  • What m does to a line

Success criteria

  • Correctly describes the effect of varying m on the graph.
  • States a general rule for positive, negative and zero gradient.
  • Tests the conjecture with at least one new value.

Prior knowledge

Students can graph linear relations and describe relationships (from AC9M8A02, AC9M7A04, AC9M7A06). Students have used digital tools to vary parameters.

Width

Minimum
Using graphing software, vary m across positive, negative and zero values; describe the effect on the graph.
Typical
Systematically vary m; compare y = 2x and y = −2x; generalise the connection between the sign and magnitude of m and the graph's steepness and direction.
Wider
  • Investigate what value of m produces a line perpendicular to a given line — discover the relationship m₁ × m₂ = −1 informally.
  • Explore the case m is undefined (vertical lines x = a) — why can't this be written as y = mx + b?

Hinge

Node 2 requires students to vary both m and b systematically and generalise about parallel lines — students who have not acquired the conjecture-testing scaffold in Node 1 produce unfounded generalisations in Node 2 or confuse the effects of the two parameters.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMaking and testing conjectures requires an explicit investigation scaffold: vary one parameter, hold others constant, record observations, propose a general rule, test with a new case. Students who explore without structure draw weak or incorrect generalisations.
