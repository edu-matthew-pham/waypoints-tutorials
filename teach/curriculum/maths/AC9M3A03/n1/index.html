Building the 3, 5 and 10 times tables

AC9M3A03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can demonstrate and explain the pattern in the 3, 5 and 10 multiplication facts using skip-counting, grouping or repeated addition.

Success criteria

  • Students can state any multiplication fact for 3, 5 or 10 within the expected range.
  • Students can describe a pattern in the 5 or 10 times table using mathematical language.

Prior knowledge

Students can recall and demonstrate proficiency with multiplication facts for twos; extend and apply facts to develop related division facts (AC9M2A03). They have not yet developed automatic recall of 3, 4, 5 and 10 times tables.

Width

Minimum
Use skip-counting or repeated addition to generate the complete 3 and 5 times tables; identify and describe one pattern in each.
Typical
Generate the 3, 5 and 10 times tables using multiple representations (skip-counting, arrays, repeated addition); identify and describe the patterns (e.g. multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5).
Wider
  • Explore how the 10 times table connects to place value and the decimal system — explaining why multiplying by 10 shifts digits left
  • Investigate multiplication patterns using a calculator or spreadsheet, generating lists of multiples and describing emerging patterns

Hinge

Pattern-grounded understanding of the 3, 5 and 10 tables is required for Node 2 (building the 4 times table via doubling) and Node 3 (fluent recall and division derivation) — students with only fragile rote recall will be unable to derive unknown facts under pressure or use the doubling strategy for the 4s, creating gaps that propagate into AC9M3N04 calculation tasks.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who learn times tables purely through rote recall without pattern understanding often have fragile recall under pressure. Seeing the structural pattern (multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5; 3s have a digit sum pattern) builds durable recall.
