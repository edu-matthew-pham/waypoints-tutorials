← AC9M3A03 Multiplication facts 3, 4, 5 and 10

Building the 4 times table and deriving related facts

AC9M3A03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recall the 4 times table and explain its relationship to the 2 times table (doubling).

Success criteria

  • Students can state any multiplication fact for 4 within the expected range.
  • Students can derive a division fact from a known multiplication fact for any of 3, 4, 5 or 10.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Building the 3, 5 and 10 times tables.

Width

Minimum
Generate the 4 times table using doubling from the 2 times table; state three related division facts.
Typical
Build the 4 times table using doubling; practise recall across all facts; derive the related division facts for 3, 4, 5 and 10.
Wider
  • Explore how the 4 times table connects to the 8 times table through another doubling step
  • Apply 4 times table facts to solve real-world problems involving groups of 4 (seasons, legs of animals, corners of squares)

Hinge

The 4 times table and the doubling derivation strategy are both required for Node 3 fluency — students who have not built the 4s cannot complete the full set of recall targets. Additionally, the doubling reasoning scaffold is the key mechanism preventing rote overload at Node 3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe 4=2×2 doubling derivation is a reasoning scaffold that connects the new table to already-known facts. Making this reasoning explicit prevents students from treating the 4 times table as an entirely new memorisation task.
