Addition and subtraction of integers

AC9M7N07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 7 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students add and subtract integers using a number line and understand the effect of adding or subtracting a negative number.

  • Adding and subtracting integers

Success criteria

  • Correctly adds and subtracts integers including cases with negative operands.
  • Interprets adding a negative number as movement in the negative direction.
  • Does not treat subtraction of a negative as subtraction (e.g. 5 − (−3) = 8, not 2).

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Comparing and ordering integers.

Width

Minimum
Calculate simple integer additions and subtractions using a number line (e.g. −3 + 5, 4 − 7, −2 − 3).
Typical
Calculate integer sums and differences without a number line; interpret addition of a negative and subtraction of a negative; apply in context.
Wider
  • Investigate the pattern: subtracting a negative equals adding a positive — explain using number line movement.
  • Explore temperature change problems requiring chaining multiple integer additions and subtractions.

Hinge

The operation-sign/number-sign confusion produces persistent errors in AC9M7N06 rational number operations and AC9M7N09 modelling contexts — students who cannot confidently add and subtract integers cannot handle any signed number computation downstream.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepInteger addition and subtraction is a major misconception site. Students confuse the sign of the number with the operation sign. Number line contexts, thermometer contexts, and financial contexts each address different facets of the misconception.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe rule 'subtract a negative = add a positive' requires explicit reasoning: movement on a number line combined with meaning of the operation. Without scaffolding, students apply rules inconsistently.
