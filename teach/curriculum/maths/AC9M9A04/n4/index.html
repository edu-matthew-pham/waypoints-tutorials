← AC9M9A04 Quadratic functions and equations

Connecting algebraic, graphical and numerical solutions

AC9M9A04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can solve quadratic equations using multiple methods and explain the connections between graphical x-intercepts, factorised form roots, and numerical solutions.

Lessons on this site

  • Three ways to solve

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies x-intercepts of a quadratic graph and states these as roots of the equation
  • Explains why a graph with no x-intercepts means the equation has no real solutions
  • Solves the same quadratic equation using at least two different methods and verifies agreement

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Solving quadratic equations algebraically.

Width

Minimum
Solve one quadratic equation algebraically; sketch the corresponding graph and identify the x-intercepts; explain how the algebraic roots and graphical intercepts are related.
Typical
Solve quadratic equations using algebraic (factorisation), graphical (reading x-intercepts) and numerical (trial and improvement, digital tools) methods; recognise that a graph with no x-intercepts corresponds to an equation with no real solutions; relate x²=a to two symmetric solutions on the graph.
Wider
  • Investigate quadratic equations with no real solutions graphically — connect to the concept of complex roots at a purely conceptual level (the parabola misses the x-axis)
  • Use digital tools to solve quadratic equations numerically and compare precision with algebraic solutions
  • Investigate how quadratic equations model the path of a thrown object — set up and solve an equation to find when the object reaches a given height or hits the ground

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node connects three previously separate methods (algebraic, graphical, numerical) into a unified understanding of quadratic equations. The integration payoff is recognising that each method gives the same answer and that the method choice depends on context — this is the full descriptor demand.
