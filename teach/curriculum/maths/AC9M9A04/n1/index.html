Identifying quadratic relationships

AC9M9A04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify quadratic functions from tables of values (using second differences) and from the form of an equation.

Lessons on this site

  • Recognising a quadratic

Success criteria

  • Correctly calculates first and second differences for a given table of values
  • Correctly identifies a quadratic relationship when second differences are constant
  • Correctly classifies at least three function equations as quadratic or non-quadratic

Prior knowledge

Students can experiment with linear functions and relations using digital tools, making and testing conjectures (AC9M8A04). They have not yet encountered quadratic functions as a distinct family, identified quadratic relationships from tables of values, or solved quadratic equations.

Width

Minimum
Calculate first and second differences for a table of values and use constant second differences to identify a quadratic relationship; identify whether y = ax²+bx+c (a≠0) is quadratic from its equation form.
Typical
Identify quadratic relationships from tables of values (constant second differences), equations and graphs; distinguish quadratics from linear and other functions; recognise the role of the squared term in determining function type.
Wider
  • Investigate how second differences connect to the coefficient of x² (second difference = 2a) and explain the algebraic reason
  • Explore real-world data sets and use second differences to determine whether the underlying relationship might be quadratic
  • Connect the identification of quadratic functions to the classification of polynomial functions by degree
