Quadratic functions and equations

AC9M9A04 · Year 9 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify and graph quadratic functions, solve quadratic equations graphically and numerically, and solve monic quadratic equations with integer roots algebraically, using graphing software and digital tools as appropriate

Prior knowledge

Students can experiment with linear functions and relations using digital tools, making and testing conjectures (AC9M8A04). They have not yet encountered quadratic functions as a distinct family, identified quadratic relationships from tables of values, or solved quadratic equations.

By the end

Students identify and graph quadratic functions, interpret their key features, and solve monic quadratic equations with integer roots algebraically, graphically and numerically.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising that constant second differences in a table of values indicate a quadratic
  • graphing quadratic functions using digital tools; interpreting symmetry, turning point, maximum/minimum values
  • solving quadratic equations algebraically and comparing to graphical solutions
  • using graphs to determine solutions; recognising roots correspond to x-intercepts; if no x-intercepts, no real solutions
  • relating x-intercepts to factorised form using the null factor law; e.g. y = (x−2)(x−3) has x-intercepts at x=2 and x=3
  • recognising x²=a (a>0) has 2 solutions; e.g. x²=39 gives x=±√39 ≈ ±6.245
  • graphing percentages of illumination of moon phases in relation to First Nations Australians' understandings of moon phases

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying quadratic relationshipsStudents can identify quadratic functions from tables of values (using second differences) and from the form of an equation.
  • 2. Graphing quadratic functions and identifying key featuresStudents can graph quadratic functions and identify their key features: turning point, axis of symmetry, x-intercepts, y-intercept, and whether the function has a maximum or minimum.
  • 3. Solving quadratic equations algebraicallyHingeStudents can solve monic quadratic equations with integer roots algebraically using factorisation and the null factor law.
  • 4. Connecting algebraic, graphical and numerical solutionsStudents can solve quadratic equations using multiple methods and explain the connections between graphical x-intercepts, factorised form roots, and numerical solutions.
