Identify the unit of repeat

AC9M1A02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the unit of repeat in a given repeating pattern and explain how they know where the repeat starts and ends.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the unit of repeat in a given pattern.
  • Explains how many elements are in the unit and where the unit begins and ends.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise, copy and continue repeating patterns (AC9MFA01). They have not yet identified the unit of repeat explicitly, represented patterns abstractly using letters or symbols, or recognised repeating structure in the natural number sequence.

Width

Minimum
Identify the unit of repeat in a given repeating pattern; explain how many elements are in the unit.
Typical
Identify the unit of repeat in a variety of repeating patterns (AB, ABC, AABBC types); explain why certain elements are part of the unit; describe the unit and how many times it repeats; distinguish the unit from the full pattern.
Wider
  • Investigate repeating patterns in First Nations Australians' craft practices — such as the making of shell or seed necklaces — identifying the unit of repeat in the colour, size and shape sequence.
  • Explore repeating patterns in everyday environments (textiles, tiling, music) and identify units of repeat of different lengths and structures.

Hinge

Correctly identifying the unit of repeat is a prerequisite for Node 2 — students who cannot isolate the unit cannot abstract it into letter notation. A prototype-based concept of pattern (expecting only AB) causes systematic failure when encountering longer units.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only see AB patterns (two-element units) often fail to recognise longer or more complex units of repeat. Varied pattern structures — ABC, AAB, ABBC — help students focus on the structural property (the unit repeats) rather than pattern appearance.
