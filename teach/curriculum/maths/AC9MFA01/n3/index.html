Continuing a pattern

AC9MFA01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can continue a repeating pattern by identifying what comes next.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly extends a pattern by at least two elements and explains what element comes next
  • Student can identify the repeating unit in the pattern they have continued

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Copying a pattern.

Width

Minimum
Continue a given repeating pattern by adding at least two more elements, explaining what comes next.
Typical
Continue repeating patterns of two or more elements represented in different ways; describe what comes next and why; connect pattern continuation to familiar routines (e.g. setting a table, a repeated song).
Wider
  • Continue and describe repeating patterns from First Nations Australians' cultural contexts (e.g. continuing a pattern from a dance, song or artwork)
  • Create an original repeating pattern and invite a partner to continue it — explaining the rule to check their partner's response
  • Continue patterns involving more than two alternating elements, describing the repeating unit

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepContinuing a pattern requires students to have identified the repeating unit, not just the surface feature. Patterns that look familiar (only two alternating colours) can be continued by guessing without understanding the unit. Broader contexts — sounds, movements, mixed-attribute patterns — require students to isolate and apply the repeating rule rather than pattern-match by surface similarity.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates recognising (node 1) and copying (node 2) into productive use — students must hold the repeating unit in mind and project it forward. The coordination of noticing and reproducing is the key learning here.
