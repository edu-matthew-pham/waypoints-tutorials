← AC9MFA01 Repeating patterns

Noticing repetition

AC9MFA01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify that a pattern repeats and point to where the repetition occurs.

Success criteria

  • Student points to the part of a pattern that repeats and states that it repeats
  • Student identifies at least one pattern represented in two different ways (e.g. colours and shapes)

Prior knowledge

Students have informal experience noticing and creating patterns in play (e.g. arranging blocks in a sequence, singing songs with repetition). They have not yet formally identified or described the repeating unit in a pattern, or continued a pattern beyond what they can see.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of a repeating pattern using materials or shapes, and say 'it repeats'.
Typical
Identify repeating patterns across materials, shapes, sounds and movements; point to the part that repeats and say what comes next.
Wider
  • Identify repeating patterns in images generated by dynamic geometric software or a generative AI tool
  • Identify repeating patterns observed on Country/Place and in First Nations Australians' artwork and material cultures (e.g. shell and seed necklaces)

Hinge

Students who conflate 'pattern' with any arrangement cannot identify the repeating unit — the concept nodes 2 and 3 require. Without unit identification, copying becomes visual mimicry and continuing becomes guessing, not algebraic reasoning.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents at Foundation often confuse a 'pattern' with any arrangement. Encountering repeating patterns across multiple sensory modalities (visual, auditory, movement) helps them isolate the defining feature — repetition of a unit — rather than pattern meaning 'colourful' or 'organised'. Breadth across representation types is what resolves this confusion.
