Assigning probabilities to compound event outcomes

AC9M9P01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can assign probabilities to all outcomes of a compound event and verify that probabilities sum to 1.

Lessons on this site

  • Every outcome, and the total of 1

Success criteria

  • Assigns correct probabilities to all outcomes of a compound event
  • Multiplies probabilities along branches correctly to find the probability of a specific compound outcome
  • Verifies that all outcome probabilities sum to 1

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: With and without replacement.

Width

Minimum
Assign probabilities to all outcomes of one compound event (with or without replacement); verify the probabilities sum to 1.
Typical
Assign probabilities to all outcomes of compound events with and without replacement; calculate the probability of a specific compound outcome by multiplying along branches; verify total probability sums to 1; apply to solve probability problems.
Wider
  • Use repeated trials of First Nations Australian children's instructive games (e.g. Gorri) to calculate probabilities of winning and not winning compound events — compare assigned theoretical probabilities to experimental results
  • Assign probabilities to multi-stage compound events (three draws, selecting 3 cards) and calculate the probability of a specific sequence
  • Investigate symmetry in compound event probability — show that different orderings of the same outcome types (e.g. HT vs TH) have equal probability

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierAssigning correct probabilities integrates the systematic listing of outcomes with the with/without replacement distinction and the multiplication of probabilities along branches. Students who have done each step separately often make errors at the integration point — particularly forgetting to adjust denominator values in without-replacement scenarios.
