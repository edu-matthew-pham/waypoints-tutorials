← AC9M9P01 Compound events

With and without replacement

AC9M9P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Probability · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how replacing or not replacing changes the sample space and the probabilities assigned to outcomes.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Correctly adjusts the sample space and branch probabilities when replacement is removed
  • Explains why without-replacement probabilities change at each stage
  • Correctly assigns probabilities to at least three outcomes in a without-replacement tree diagram

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Systematic listing of compound event outcomes.

Width

Minimum
List outcomes for one compound event first with replacement, then without; compare the two sample spaces and explain how one outcome's probability changes.
Typical
Compare the sample space and probabilities for compound events with and without replacement across a range of contexts; explain the structural reason for the difference (without replacement: each draw reduces the available outcomes); assign correct probabilities to branches of tree diagrams in both cases.
Wider
  • Investigate how card drawing problems (with and without replacement) change probabilities and relate this to real card games
  • Explore how with/without replacement affects the fairness of random selection processes (e.g. selecting team leaders, committee members)
  • Connect the without-replacement structure to combinations — discuss informally how the probability of specific ordered sequences compares to unordered selections

Hinge

Students who apply with-replacement probabilities in without-replacement contexts (or vice versa) assign wrong branch probabilities in N3 tree diagrams. The structural distinction must be secure before probability assignment.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly apply with-replacement probabilities even in without-replacement contexts, or vice versa, because they focus on the type of event rather than the sampling structure. Explicitly contrasting identical scenarios under both conditions — same container, same items, only the replacement rule changes — makes the structural difference salient.
