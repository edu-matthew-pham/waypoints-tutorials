← AC9M2N03 Halves, quarters and eighths

Eighths through repeated halving

AC9M2N03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 2 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create eighths by halving a quarter, explain that an eighth is a half of a quarter, and compare the sizes of halves, quarters and eighths of the same whole.

Success criteria

  • Creates eighths by repeated halving and names each part as one-eighth
  • States that an eighth is smaller than a quarter and a quarter is smaller than a half of the same whole
  • Explains the relationship: a quarter is a half of a half; an eighth is a half of a quarter

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Quarters through repeated halving.

Width

Minimum
Divide a shape or strip into eighths by repeated halving; name each part as one-eighth; state that an eighth is a half of a quarter.
Typical
Create and name halves, quarters and eighths through repeated halving; compare the size of parts (eighths are smaller than quarters, quarters are smaller than halves); relate number of parts to the size of each part.
Wider
  • Use repeated halving to subdivide shapes in different ways; discuss why the more times you halve, the smaller each part becomes
  • Divide a shape into 4 equal parts and 8 equal parts; compare one-quarter and one-eighth; connect to the unit fraction rule (4 equal parts → one-quarter; 8 equal parts → one-eighth)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node brings together halves, quarters and eighths as a connected structure. The key integration is the inverse relationship: more equal parts means each part is smaller. Students need to coordinate the part-count, the name, and the size simultaneously.
