← AC9M4P01 Likelihood and chance events

Reasoning from composition

AC9M4P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use the composition of a set (e.g. contents of a bag) to compare the likelihood of different outcomes.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Students can correctly predict which outcome is more likely given the composition of a set, with reasoning
  • Students can explain why the composition affects likelihood

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Ordering events by likelihood.

Width

Minimum
Predict which outcome is more likely from two scenarios with different set compositions (e.g. two bags with different numbers of red and white balls); explain the reasoning.
Typical
Compare likelihood of selecting different outcomes based on the relative frequencies in a set; explain that more of a certain item means greater likelihood of selecting it; apply to multiple scenarios; connect to the idea that certain outcomes (both at once) are impossible.
Wider
  • Investigate dependent event chains in school contexts: given a high chance of a storm on Friday, what happens to the chance of the outdoor activity? Building multi-step conditional reasoning
  • Explore impossible event pairs (mutually exclusive outcomes — it cannot be hot and cold at the same time; a card cannot be both red and a spade) and connect to logical reasoning

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCommon misconception: the gambler's fallacy — believing that after a long run of heads, a tail is now 'due.' Students who encounter multiple independent event contexts (each coin toss is fresh) rather than only dependent contexts are less likely to develop this misconception.
