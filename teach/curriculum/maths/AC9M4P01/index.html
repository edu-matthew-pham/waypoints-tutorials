Likelihood and chance events

AC9M4P01 · Year 4 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Describe possible everyday events and the possible outcomes of chance experiments and order outcomes or events based on their likelihood of occurring; identify independent or dependent events

Prior knowledge

Students can describe outcomes as likely, unlikely, certain or impossible with reasoning (AC9M3P01). They have not yet ordered events by likelihood on a scale, or identified independent and dependent events.

By the end

Students order events by likelihood, use relative composition to reason about probability, and identify independent and dependent events.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using lists of events familiar to students and ordering them from least likely to most likely
  • predicting the outcome of a coin toss after 5 heads have been flipped, discussing whether a tail is now more likely
  • predicting how likely it is to select a red ball from bags with different compositions of red and white balls
  • identifying school activities where the chance of occurring is affected by the chance of other events
  • listing the outcomes of everyday chance situations and identifying where one cannot happen if the other does
  • identifying different dependent and independent events
  • exploring how ordering outcomes by likelihood is an essential component of early warning systems using AI
  • discussing how likelihood relates to AI predictive text decisions

Waypoints

  • 1. Ordering events by likelihoodStudents can order a set of events from least likely to most likely and justify their reasoning.
  • 2. Reasoning from compositionStudents can use the composition of a set (e.g. contents of a bag) to compare the likelihood of different outcomes.
  • 3. Independent and dependent eventsStudents can identify independent events (where one outcome does not affect another) and dependent events (where it does).
