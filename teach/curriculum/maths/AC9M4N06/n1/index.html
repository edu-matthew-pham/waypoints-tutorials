Efficient addition and subtraction strategies

AC9M4N06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply and explain at least two efficient strategies for multi-digit addition or subtraction.

Success criteria

  • Students can apply at least two different efficient strategies for multi-digit addition or subtraction
  • Students can explain why a chosen strategy is efficient for a given pair of numbers

Prior knowledge

Students can add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers using place value (AC9M3N03) and multiply and divide one- and two-digit numbers (AC9M3N04). They have not yet developed efficient strategies for multi-digit multiplication or applied digital tools strategically.

Width

Minimum
Apply one strategy beyond the standard algorithm (e.g. compensation, near-hundreds) to solve one addition or subtraction problem; explain why this approach is efficient.
Typical
Develop, apply and explain multiple efficient strategies for addition and subtraction (partitioning, compensation, near-numbers, constant difference for subtraction); select strategies based on the numbers involved; use digital tools to verify.
Wider
  • Compare the efficiency of different strategies for the same problem type (e.g. 497 + 348: partition vs compensation) and build a decision rule for when each strategy is more efficient
  • Apply strategies to multi-step problems where an efficient approach for each sub-step reduces overall cognitive load

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyEfficient computation requires a repertoire of strategies, not a single approach. Students who know only one method (e.g. always partition) are less flexible than those who can select from compensation, near-hundreds or constant difference depending on the numbers.
