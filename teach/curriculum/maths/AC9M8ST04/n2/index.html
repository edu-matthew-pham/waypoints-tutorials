← AC9M8ST04 Statistical investigations with samples

Conducting, inferring and reporting with uncertainty

AC9M8ST04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students conduct the investigation, make inferences about the population from the sample, and report findings while explicitly acknowledging the uncertainty introduced by sampling.

Lessons on this site

  • Investigating with samples

Success criteria

  • Makes a valid inference about the population from the sample.
  • Explicitly acknowledges uncertainty in the report.
  • Connects the degree of uncertainty to the sample size and method.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning an ethical investigation with sampling.

Width

Minimum
Conduct a sample investigation; make one inference about the population; state one source of uncertainty in the conclusion.
Typical
Conduct a full investigation cycle; make multiple inferences; report findings including confidence language; connect sample size to the degree of uncertainty acknowledged.
Wider
  • Compare the inferences drawn from two different sample sizes for the same population — discuss how the larger sample changes confidence in the inference.
  • Explore how statistical uncertainty is communicated in public health or environmental reporting — compare with how uncertainty is often omitted in media reports.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierConducting and reporting with acknowledged uncertainty integrates sampling method choice, distribution analysis, inference-making, and communication — the full statistics strand at Year 8.
