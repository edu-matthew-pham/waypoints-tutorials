Planning an ethical investigation with sampling

AC9M8ST04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students plan a statistical investigation including ethical and fair sampling methods, anticipate sources of uncertainty, and identify how findings will be used to make inferences.

Lessons on this site

  • Investigating with samples

Success criteria

  • Specifies the population, sample, and sampling method.
  • Identifies at least one ethical consideration and addresses it in the plan.
  • States what inference will be drawn from the investigation.

Prior knowledge

Students can plan and conduct statistical investigations (from AC9M7ST03). Students understand sampling methods and variation (from AC9M8ST02, AC9M8ST03).

Width

Minimum
Plan a statistical investigation specifying: the population, the sampling method, why it is ethical and fair, and what inference is being sought.
Typical
Design an investigation with explicit ethical consideration; identify potential sources of bias and uncertainty; plan how to communicate uncertainty in the report.
Wider
  • Investigate progress in reconciliation between First Nations Australians and non-Indigenous Australians — evaluate the ethical considerations in sampling and reporting for such sensitive topics.
  • Explore how businesses use electricity consumption data to draw conclusions about event impacts (e.g. pandemic effects) — evaluate the ethical data use.

Hinge

Node 2 requires conducting the investigation and reporting with acknowledged uncertainty — without a valid, ethically-designed plan from Node 1, there is no investigation to conduct and no basis for making or communicating inferences.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPlanning an ethical investigation requires explicit reasoning about who is included, who might be harmed by the data collection, and how to minimise bias — students need a planning scaffold that surfaces these considerations.
