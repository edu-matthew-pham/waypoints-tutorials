Collecting and analysing data

AC9M9ST05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can collect or access data appropriate to the investigation and analyse it using suitable representations and summary statistics.

Lessons on this site

  • Collect and analyse the audit

Success criteria

  • Collects or accesses data that is relevant to the stated statistical question
  • Represents the data using an appropriate display type
  • Calculates at least one relevant summary statistic and states what it means for the investigation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning the investigation.

Width

Minimum
Collect or access data for the investigation; represent it using an appropriate display; calculate at least one summary statistic relevant to the question.
Typical
Collect or access data from appropriate sources; clean and organise data as needed; represent using appropriate displays for the data type; calculate relevant summary statistics (centre, spread); identify patterns or relationships in the data relevant to the investigation question.
Wider
  • Access and analyse secondary data from the ABS or equivalent databases; compare data from multiple sources and evaluate consistency
  • Plan and conduct a consumer research investigation with attention to sampling techniques, data cleaning and the difference between primary and secondary data quality
  • Investigate multi-variable data (e.g. rainfall, land prices, farming costs across multiple regions) and analyse relationships between variables

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often analyse data without maintaining a connection to the original question — reporting statistics and graphs without explaining what they mean for the investigation. Scaffolding the reasoning loop (What did I find? What does this mean for my question?) keeps the analysis purposeful.
