Naming measurement attributes

AC9MFM01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name and distinguish between the four measurement attributes — length, capacity, mass and duration — and connect each to the correct measurement language.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly names at least three of the four measurement attributes when shown an example of each
  • Student uses at least one appropriate comparative word for each attribute named (e.g. longer, heavier, holds more, takes longer)

Prior knowledge

Students have informal experience with size and weight in play and daily life (e.g. 'this is big', 'that's heavy'). They have not yet systematically distinguished between different measurement attributes or used direct comparison with explicit reasoning.

Width

Minimum
Name two of the four measurement attributes and use at least one correct word for each (e.g. 'long/short' for length, 'heavy/light' for mass).
Typical
Name all four measurement attributes and use appropriate language for each (tall/short/long/wide for length; holds more/less for capacity; heavier/lighter for mass; takes longer/shorter for duration); connect measurement words to familiar everyday objects and events.
Wider
  • Investigate objects that have interesting or surprising combinations of attributes (e.g. something tall that is also light) — discussing which attribute you are describing
  • Connect measurement language to familiar contexts on Country/Place (e.g. comparing lengths of natural objects, duration of familiar activities)

Hinge

Students who conflate measurement attributes will compare the wrong attribute in node 2 — measuring length when asked about mass, or using mass language for capacity comparisons. Attribute confusion produces confidently incorrect comparisons and undermines the reasoning communication demanded in node 3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly conflate measurement attributes — particularly confusing length with size in general, or mass with size. Encountering each attribute named correctly across many different familiar objects (not just one canonical example) helps students build distinct categories for each attribute rather than using size as a catch-all term.
