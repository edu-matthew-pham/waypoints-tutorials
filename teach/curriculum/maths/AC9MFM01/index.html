Measurement attributes

AC9MFM01 · Foundation Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify and compare attributes of objects and events, including length, capacity, mass and duration, using direct comparisons and communicating reasoning

Prior knowledge

Students have informal experience with size and weight in play and daily life (e.g. 'this is big', 'that's heavy'). They have not yet systematically distinguished between different measurement attributes or used direct comparison with explicit reasoning.

By the end

Students identify and compare attributes of objects and events across length, capacity, mass and duration using direct comparison, and communicate reasoning about their comparisons.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using language to describe the measurement attributes of length, mass, capacity and duration, and connecting the words with the appropriate attribute; for example, using words like 'tall', 'short', 'wide', 'long', 'high' to describe the attribute of length
  • directly comparing pairs of objects to say which is longer/shorter, and explaining or demonstrating how they know; for example, standing back-to-back to determine who is taller; choosing to line up the bases of a spoon and fork to decide which is longer and explaining why
  • starting 2 events at the same time to decide which takes longer; for example, putting on a pair of sandals with buckles or Velcro, describing the duration using familiar terms and reasoning
  • directly comparing pairs of everyday objects from the kitchen pantry to say which is heavier/lighter; for example, hefting a tin of baked beans and a packet of marshmallows; comparing the same pair of objects to say which is longer/shorter and discussing comparisons

Waypoints

  • 1. Naming measurement attributesHingeStudents can name and distinguish between the four measurement attributes — length, capacity, mass and duration — and connect each to the correct measurement language.
  • 2. Direct comparison of a single attributeHingeStudents can directly compare two objects or events on a single measurement attribute and state which has more or less of that attribute.
  • 3. Communicating reasoning about comparisonsStudents can compare objects or events on a chosen measurement attribute and communicate their reasoning using attribute-specific language.
