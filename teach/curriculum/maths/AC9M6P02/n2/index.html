← AC9M6P02 Simulations with increasing trials

Effect of increasing trials on variation

AC9M6P02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how increasing the number of trials in a probability experiment reduces variation in the experimental probability estimate.

Success criteria

  • Student describes how experimental probability changes as the number of trials increases.
  • Student explains why more trials generally gives a more reliable estimate.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Experimental vs theoretical probability.

Width

Minimum
Compare the experimental probability from 10 trials to that from 50 trials for the same experiment; describe how the result changes; note which gives a more reliable estimate.
Typical
Track cumulative experimental probability across an increasing number of trials; observe convergence toward theoretical probability; describe the effect of increasing trials on variation using appropriate language; explain why a larger sample gives a more reliable estimate.
Wider
  • Graph cumulative experimental probability against number of trials and describe the shape of the resulting curve
  • Investigate how many trials are 'enough' for different precision requirements — connecting to real-world sample size decisions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often accept that 'more trials = more reliable' as a rule without understanding why. Seeing the convergence pattern across different experiment types (coins, dice, spinners, digital simulations) builds a principled rather than rule-based understanding.
