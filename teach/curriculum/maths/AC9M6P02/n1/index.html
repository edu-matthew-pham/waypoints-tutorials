Experimental vs theoretical probability

AC9M6P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Probability · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between theoretical probability (what should happen) and experimental probability (what did happen), and recognise that they may differ in small samples.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student calculates experimental probability from a set of trials.
  • Student compares experimental to theoretical probability and explains why they may differ.

Prior knowledge

Students can conduct repeated chance experiments and use frequency to estimate likelihoods (AC9M5P02). They have observed that results vary. They have not yet used digital simulations, systematically compared experimental with theoretical probability, or investigated the effect of increasing trial numbers.

Width

Minimum
Conduct a chance experiment (e.g. 20 coin flips); calculate experimental probability from results; compare to theoretical probability; note the difference.
Typical
Calculate experimental probability from results of a chance experiment; compare to theoretical probability; explain why experimental results vary from theoretical; recognise that the gap between experimental and theoretical tends to decrease with more trials.
Wider
  • Investigate contexts where theoretical probability is unknown and only experimental probability is available — e.g. what is the probability a thumbtack lands point-up?
  • Compare the gambler's fallacy — 'I've flipped heads 5 times so tails is 'due'' — to the correct understanding of independent events

Hinge

Students who conflate theoretical and experimental probability cannot meaningfully engage with Node 2 or Node 3 — the entire discussion of convergence with more trials requires the distinction to be clear. Students who treat these as interchangeable will misinterpret simulation results.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe distinction between theoretical and experimental probability requires students to reason about two different things that can both be called 'the probability of an event.' Scaffolding 'theoretical is what the maths predicts, experimental is what actually happened' prevents conflation of the two.
