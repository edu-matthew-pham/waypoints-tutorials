← AC9M6P02 Simulations with increasing trials

Digital simulations for large trial counts

AC9M6P02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use digital tools to run simulations with large numbers of trials and use the results to compare experimental and expected outcomes.

Success criteria

  • Student runs a digital simulation and records experimental probabilities.
  • Student compares results from different trial sizes and describes the trend.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Effect of increasing trials on variation.

Width

Minimum
Use a digital tool to run a simulation of at least 100 trials; record results; compare to theoretical probability; describe the comparison.
Typical
Use digital simulations to run 100, 500, and 1000 trial experiments; compare results across trial sizes; identify the trend; compare experimental frequency to expected frequency for the whole simulation; draw conclusions about the effect of trial size on reliability.
Wider
  • Design a simulation for an event where theoretical probability is difficult to calculate analytically (e.g. two dice sum) and use simulation results to estimate probabilities
  • Investigate Monte Carlo methods at a conceptual level — the idea that random simulation can approximate mathematical quantities (e.g. estimating π from random point drops in a square and circle)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierRunning digital simulations and comparing results to expected outcomes integrates experimental/theoretical probability, the effect of trial size, and digital tool use. The interpretation of large-trial results — and connecting back to theoretical expectations — coordinates these into the descriptor's full demand.
