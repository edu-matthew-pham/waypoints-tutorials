Describing coordinate changes when moving a point

AC9M6SP02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 6 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how the coordinates of a point change when it is translated horizontally, vertically, or diagonally in the Cartesian plane.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly identifies which coordinate changes when a point is moved horizontally or vertically.
  • Student predicts the new coordinates of a point after a described movement.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Extending coordinates to all four quadrants.

Width

Minimum
Move a point horizontally and describe the change to x-coordinate; move a point vertically and describe the change to y-coordinate; state which coordinate changes and by how much for each movement.
Typical
Describe coordinate changes for horizontal, vertical, and diagonal movements including movements that cross axes (changing sign); predict the new coordinates of a point after a described movement; connect movements to positive/negative integer addition.
Wider
  • Investigate systematic translations of shapes on the Cartesian plane — if every vertex moves +3 horizontally and −2 vertically, how do coordinates change?
  • Explore reflections across axes: if a point at (3, −4) is reflected across the x-axis, what are the new coordinates? Identify the sign pattern.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often confuse horizontal and vertical movements with x- and y-coordinate changes. The explicit reasoning chain — horizontal movement changes only x, vertical movement changes only y — prevents persistent confusion and provides a tool for checking transformations.
