Expanding and simplifying linear expressions

AC9M8A01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students expand brackets in linear expressions using the distributive property and collect like terms to simplify.

Lessons on this site

  • Expanding brackets

Success criteria

  • Correctly expands brackets including those with negative coefficients.
  • Collects like terms accurately.
  • Does not partially distribute (e.g. only multiply the first term).

Prior knowledge

Students can formulate algebraic expressions and apply the distributive property informally (from AC9M7A02). Students understand variables, constants and operations in expressions.

Width

Minimum
Expand and simplify a linear expression with one set of brackets (e.g. 3(2x + 4) − x).
Typical
Expand and simplify expressions with multiple brackets and negative coefficients; identify and collect like terms; use algebra tiles or area models to verify.
Wider
  • Investigate the distributive property across subtraction: verify that a(b − c) = ab − ac using substitution and area models.
  • Explore common student errors in expansion (e.g. 3(x + 2) = 3x + 2) — explain why the error occurs and how to avoid it.

Hinge

Expansion errors — particularly sign errors with negative coefficients — propagate directly into Node 2 (factorisation requires recognising expanded form) and Node 3 (rearrangement requires reliable expansion to verify). Students who cannot expand fluently cannot check their factorisation or rearrangement by expanding back.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsExpanding and collecting like terms is a procedural skill requiring fluency before factorisation and rearrangement. Errors in sign handling with negative coefficients are the primary failure point.
