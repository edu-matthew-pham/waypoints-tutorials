← AC9M4M02 Perimeter and area

Area — space enclosed by boundary

AC9M4M02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can measure area by covering a shape with unit squares and counting, including partial units at edges.

Success criteria

  • Students can measure the area of a shape by covering with unit squares, including partial units
  • Students can explain what area measures and distinguish it from perimeter

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Perimeter — boundary as length sum.

Width

Minimum
Cover one rectangle with unit squares (no gaps or overlaps) and count to find the area; explain what area measures.
Typical
Measure and compare areas using arrays of paper tiles or mosaic squares; handle partial units at irregular edges by combining halves and parts; demonstrate using one tile repeatedly rather than tiling the whole shape; explain that area is the space enclosed by the boundary.
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Ranger Groups measure areas of land for fire management decisions, contextualising area measurement within caring for Country/Place
  • Measure areas of irregular shapes (leaves, handprints) using square grids and discuss how to handle partial squares systematically — developing an approximation strategy

Hinge

Students who conflate perimeter and area arrive at Node 3 unable to measure or distinguish the two attributes simultaneously — the core task of that node. The confusion must be resolved in Node 2 before meaningful comparison is possible.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPerimeter-area confusion is persistent — students may measure the squares around the edge of a shape (perimeter) when asked for area, or count only internal squares without accounting for boundary ones. Measuring both attributes for the same shape, and discussing what each counts, is the resolution.
