Perimeter and area

AC9M4M02 · Year 4 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Recognise ways of measuring and approximating the perimeter and area of shapes and enclosed spaces, using appropriate formal and informal units

Prior knowledge

Students can measure with metric units and informal units for length and area (AC9M3M02). They have not yet formally distinguished perimeter from area or applied area measurement using arrays of unit squares.

By the end

Students recognise, measure and approximate perimeter and area using appropriate formal and informal units, distinguishing the two attributes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising that perimeter is the sum of the lengths that form the boundary of a shape or enclosed space; using a piece of string or rope to measure the perimeter of irregular shapes
  • creating a range of rectangles representing 'paddocks' on grid paper and establishing different methods of working out the length of the boundary fences
  • recognising that area is the space enclosed by the boundary; measuring and comparing the area of shapes using an array of paper tiles including part units
  • demonstrating how to use one unit repeatedly to measure the area of a shape
  • investigating the ways First Nations Ranger Groups and other groups measure areas of land to make decisions about fire burns

Waypoints

  • 1. Perimeter — boundary as length sumStudents can explain that perimeter is the total length of the boundary and measure it by adding side lengths.
  • 2. Area — space enclosed by boundaryHingeStudents can measure area by covering a shape with unit squares and counting, including partial units at edges.
  • 3. Distinguishing and comparing perimeter and areaStudents can measure and compare both perimeter and area for the same shapes, clearly distinguishing the two attributes.
