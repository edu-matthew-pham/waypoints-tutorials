Distinguishing and comparing perimeter and area

AC9M4M02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can measure and compare both perimeter and area for the same shapes, clearly distinguishing the two attributes.

Success criteria

  • Students can measure both the perimeter and area of a given shape and record each with appropriate units
  • Students can explain the difference between perimeter and area and describe a context where each is the relevant measurement

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Area — space enclosed by boundary.

Width

Minimum
Measure both the perimeter and area of one shape; explain the difference between the two measurements.
Typical
Measure and compare perimeter and area across a range of shapes; explain that two shapes can have the same perimeter but different areas; choose appropriate units for each attribute; recognise that more accurate area measures require attention to partial units.
Wider
  • Explore the paddock problem: create rectangles with the same perimeter on grid paper and compare their areas — discovering that perimeter does not determine area
  • Investigate real-world contexts where perimeter and area are both relevant (fencing vs carpeting a room) and discuss which measurement is needed for each purpose

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe perimeter-area confusion is one of the most robust misconceptions in primary measurement. Contexts where both are measured for the same shape — and where they give different information — are the most effective resolution. The paddock problem (same fence length, different enclosed area) is particularly powerful.
