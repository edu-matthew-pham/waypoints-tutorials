← AC9M4M03 Duration with am and pm

Duration calculations spanning am and pm

AC9M4M03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can calculate the duration between two times, including cases where the start and finish times span the am/pm boundary.

Success criteria

  • Students can calculate the duration between two times that span noon or midnight
  • Students can express duration in appropriate units (hours and minutes, or minutes and seconds)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: am and pm notation.

Width

Minimum
Calculate the duration between two times that are both am or both pm; state the answer in hours and minutes.
Typical
Calculate duration between two times including cases that span the noon or midnight boundary; use number line or bridging strategies (e.g. count to the hour, then add remaining minutes); apply to real contexts (movie duration, bus journey, flight times).
Wider
  • Create a timetable for a real or imagined event (athletics carnival, sports day) involving activities across am and pm, ensuring durations are correctly calculated and times are consistent
  • Investigate flight duration problems including departure and arrival in different time zones — discussing why the quoted duration and the difference in times might not match

Hinge

Students who subtract times directly as whole numbers (e.g. 2:30 pm − 10:45 am = 8:15, missing the carry) produce systematically incorrect duration values. This error propagates directly into Node 3 conversion problems and any real-world timetabling context in this standard.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDuration calculations spanning the noon boundary require students to bridge through 12:00 — a reasoning scaffold (how long to noon? how long after noon?) prevents common errors from subtracting times directly as if they were whole numbers.
