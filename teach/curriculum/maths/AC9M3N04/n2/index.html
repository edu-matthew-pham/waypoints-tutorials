Multiplication strategies for one- and two-digit numbers

AC9M3N04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can multiply a one-digit number by a two-digit number using at least one efficient strategy (e.g. partitioning, doubling).

Success criteria

  • Students can calculate a one-digit by two-digit product using at least one named strategy.
  • Students can explain the strategy they used and why it works.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Multiplication representations.

Width

Minimum
Calculate at least three products involving a two-digit number using a chosen strategy, recording working.
Typical
Apply and compare partitioning, doubling-and-halving and repeated addition strategies to multiply one-digit by two-digit numbers; choose an efficient strategy for a given problem.
Wider
  • Explore the area model as a visual representation of two-digit multiplication (e.g. 4×23 as a 4×20 + 4×3 rectangle)
  • Investigate patterns in multiplication tables, using the area model to explain why the pattern holds

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuilding a repertoire of strategies — partitioning, doubling, using known facts — is the explicit goal of this descriptor. Students need to have encountered enough strategy examples to make genuine choices.
