Volume of right prisms and cylinders

AC9M9M01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can calculate the volume of right prisms and cylinders using V = Ah (cross-sectional area × height).

Lessons on this site

  • Volume of prisms and cylinders

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the cross-sectional area as the base of the V=Ah formula
  • Calculates volume correctly for both right prisms and cylinders
  • Uses appropriate units (cm³, m³, L) and converts between them where required

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Surface area from nets.

Width

Minimum
Calculate the volume of one right prism and one cylinder using the appropriate formula; state units correctly.
Typical
Calculate the volume of a range of right prisms (rectangular, triangular) and cylinders; apply the principle V = Ah to connect cross-sectional area to volume; solve practical problems involving volume (e.g. capacity of a tank).
Wider
  • Investigate the relationship between volume and capacity (1 cm³ = 1 mL) in practical measurement contexts
  • Find different prisms with the same volume but different surface areas; conjecture about which prism shape minimises surface area for a given volume
  • Investigate the volume of objects in First Nations Australian technology and explore how volume relates to capacity and function
