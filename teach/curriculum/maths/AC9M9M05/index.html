Mathematical modelling — proportion, rates and ratio

AC9M9M05 · Year 9 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving direct proportion, rates, ratio and scale, including financial contexts; formulate the problems and interpret solutions in terms of the situation; evaluate the model and report methods and findings

Prior knowledge

Students can calculate and interpret absolute, relative and percentage errors in measurements (AC9M9M04). They have not yet applied the full mathematical modelling cycle to applied problems involving direct proportion, rates, ratio and scale across diverse real-world contexts.

By the end

Students apply the mathematical modelling cycle to solve practical problems involving direct proportion, rates, ratio and scale, interpreting solutions in context and evaluating the model.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • modelling situations involving direct proportion: pro rata pay, exchange rates, multiple quotes, unit conversion, Hooke's law
  • modelling image editing on social media where proportion is not maintained, resulting in distorted images
  • modelling compliance with building and construction standards; e.g. staircase rise and tread, escalator components
  • modelling rates in practical contexts; e.g. density, birth rate, flow, heartbeats
  • exploring fire management techniques used by First Nations Australians using proportion relationships including rate of fire spread vs wind speed, temperature and humidity

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying and formulating proportion and rate relationshipsHingeStudents can identify whether a situation involves direct proportion or a rate, represent it mathematically, and formulate it as a model.
  • 2. Solving and interpreting the modelStudents can solve a proportion or rate model and interpret the solution in the context of the original problem.
  • 3. Evaluating and reporting the modelStudents can evaluate the limitations of a proportion or rate model and report methods and findings.
