← AC9M6A01 Growing patterns with rational numbers

Identifying rules in number patterns

AC9M6A01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the rule governing a number pattern involving rational numbers and use it to extend the pattern.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies the rule for a number pattern involving rational numbers and states it clearly.
  • Student extends the pattern correctly using the identified rule.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and explain the connection between multiplication and division as inverse operations and use this to develop families of number facts (AC9M5A01). They can find unknown values in multiplication and division equations (AC9M5A02). They have not yet described rules for growing patterns or applied pattern rules to rational number sequences.

Width

Minimum
Identify the rule (operation and step size) in at least three number patterns involving decimals or fractions; extend each pattern by at least three terms; verify by checking the rule applies to each step.
Typical
Identify and describe rules for number patterns involving decimals, fractions, and percentages; distinguish additive patterns (constant difference) from multiplicative patterns (constant ratio); extend patterns in both forward and backward directions.
Wider
  • Investigate patterns arising from unit fraction sequences (1/2, 1/3, 1/4...) — what happens as denominators grow?
  • Use a spreadsheet to generate long sequences and look for emerging patterns that are not obvious from the first few terms

Hinge

Correctly identifying a pattern's rule — including distinguishing additive from multiplicative — is the prerequisite for Node 2 and Node 3. Students who default to additive when the rule is multiplicative will generate wrong terms and cannot describe visual growing patterns or generate patterns from rules accurately.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often identify additive patterns readily but miss multiplicative ones. Encountering both types — and patterns where the step involves a rational number (e.g. +0.25, ×½) — is needed to build a general pattern-recognition schema rather than one keyed to whole-number steps.
