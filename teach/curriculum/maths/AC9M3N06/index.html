Mathematical modelling

AC9M3N06 · Year 3 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving additive and multiplicative situations including financial contexts; formulate problems using number sentences and choose calculation strategies, using digital tools where appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation

Prior knowledge

Students can use mathematical modelling to solve practical additive and multiplicative problems, including money (AC9M2N06). They have not yet systematically formulated problems as number sentences for three-digit calculations, or applied modelling across both additive and multiplicative situations with explicit interpretation.

By the end

Students use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving addition, subtraction, multiplication and division in real-world and financial contexts, formulating, calculating and interpreting results.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using mathematical modelling to solve problems involving addition, subtraction, multiplication and division in practical contexts
  • formulating problems using number sentences and selecting appropriate calculation strategies
  • using digital tools where appropriate to solve practical problems
  • interpreting and communicating solutions with reference to the context, including units
  • applying modelling to financial contexts including making purchases and giving change

Waypoints

  • 1. Formulating problems as number sentencesHingeStudents can translate a practical additive or multiplicative situation into a number sentence, identifying the operation needed.
  • 2. Choosing and applying calculation strategiesStudents can select an appropriate calculation strategy for a given problem and apply it, including using digital tools where appropriate.
  • 3. Interpreting and communicating solutionsStudents can interpret a calculated answer in terms of the original situation and communicate the solution with appropriate units and context.
