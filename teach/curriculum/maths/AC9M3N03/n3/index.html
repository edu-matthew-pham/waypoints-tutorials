Subtraction using place value

AC9M3N03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can subtract two- and three-digit numbers using place value partitioning, including cases requiring decomposition.

Success criteria

  • Students can subtract a three-digit number from a three-digit number using at least one place value strategy.
  • Students can correctly apply decomposition where required and produce the correct difference.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Regrouping in addition.

Width

Minimum
Subtract two pairs of three-digit numbers using place value partitioning; at least one problem requires decomposition (borrowing).
Typical
Apply place value strategies — partitioning, rearranging, decomposition — to subtract across a range of two- and three-digit problems; choose the most efficient strategy.
Wider
  • Compare different subtraction strategies (partitioning, counting on/back, compensation) for the same problem, evaluating efficiency
  • Apply subtraction to financial contexts — e.g. calculating change from $100 in real-world purchase scenarios

Hinge

Subtraction with decomposition feeds directly into AC9M3N06 mathematical modelling problems and AC9M3N05 estimation-checking tasks — students who cannot reliably subtract with decomposition cannot complete multi-step problems or check the reasonableness of results in subsequent standards.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSubtraction with decomposition is the highest-error node in this descriptor — the 'borrowing' action is procedurally error-prone. Students who understand the place value rationale through multiple representations (models and written) make fewer errors than those who memorise procedure.
Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsOnce the decomposition concept is understood, students also benefit from practice to build fluency and reduce procedural errors in multi-step problems.
