Prime, composite and square numbers

AC9M6N02 · Year 6 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Identify and describe the properties of prime, composite and square numbers and use these properties to solve problems and simplify calculations

Prior knowledge

Students can express natural numbers as products of their factors and determine if one number is divisible by another (AC9M5N02). They can recognise multiples. They have not yet classified numbers as prime or composite, nor explored square numbers as a distinct number class.

By the end

Students can identify and describe the properties of prime, composite and square numbers and use these properties to simplify calculations and solve problems.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using the definition of a prime number to explain why one is not a prime number
  • testing numbers by using division to distinguish between prime and composite numbers, recording the results on a number chart to identify any patterns
  • representing composite numbers as a product of their factors, including prime factors when necessary and using this form to simplify calculations
  • identifying and describing the product of a number with itself as square
  • using spreadsheets to list all the numbers that have up to 3 factors, recognise any emerging patterns

Waypoints

  • 1. Factors and the prime/composite distinctionHingeStudents can determine the number of factors a whole number has and classify it as prime (exactly two factors: 1 and itself) or composite (more than two factors).
  • 2. Prime factorisationHingeStudents can express composite numbers as products of prime factors using factor trees or repeated division.
  • 3. Square numbers and their propertiesStudents can identify square numbers, recognise them as products of a number multiplied by itself, connect them to square arrays, and identify square roots of familiar square numbers.
  • 4. Using number properties to simplify and solveStudents can use properties of prime, composite and square numbers to solve problems and simplify calculations, including selecting efficient approaches.
