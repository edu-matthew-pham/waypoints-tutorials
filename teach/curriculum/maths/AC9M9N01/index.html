Real number system

AC9M9N01 · Year 9 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise that the real number system includes the rational numbers and the irrational numbers, and solve problems involving real numbers using digital tools

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise terminating and recurring decimals using digital tools (AC9M8N03). They have not yet encountered the formal distinction between rational and irrational numbers as a system, represented the full hierarchy of real number subsets, or used the real number line to solve problems involving irrational values.

By the end

Students recognise that the real number system includes rational and irrational numbers; represent the relationships between subsets of real numbers; and solve problems involving real numbers including substitution into formulas.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating the real number system by representing the relationships between irrationals, rationals, integers and natural numbers and discussing the difference between exact representations and approximate decimal representations of irrational numbers
  • using a real number line to indicate the solution interval for inequalities of the form ax+b<c or ax+b>c
  • using positive and negative rational numbers to solve problems; for example, for financial planning such as budgeting
  • solving problems involving the substitution of real numbers into formulas, understanding that solutions can be represented in exact form or as a decimal approximation, such as calculating the area of a circle using A=πr²
  • investigating the position of rational and irrational numbers on the real number line, using geometric constructions to locate rational numbers and square roots on a number line

Waypoints

  • 1. Subsets of the real number systemHingeStudents can represent the hierarchy of number subsets — natural numbers, integers, rationals, irrationals — and explain what distinguishes each from the others.
  • 2. Exact and approximate representationsStudents can distinguish between exact representations of irrational numbers (e.g. √2, π) and their decimal approximations, and explain why decimal approximations are not equal to the exact value.
  • 3. Real number line and inequalitiesHingeStudents can locate real numbers (including irrationals) on the number line and represent solution intervals for linear inequalities.
  • 4. Solving problems with real numbersStudents can solve problems by substituting real numbers (including irrationals) into formulas, expressing solutions in both exact and approximate decimal form as appropriate.
