Subsets of the real number system

AC9M9N01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can represent the hierarchy of number subsets — natural numbers, integers, rationals, irrationals — and explain what distinguishes each from the others.

Success criteria

  • Correctly classifies at least four different numbers into the appropriate subsets of the real number system
  • Explains the distinguishing property of rational numbers (expressible as p/q for integers p, q, q≠0) in their own words
  • Identifies that integers are rational, not a separate category outside rationals

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise terminating and recurring decimals using digital tools (AC9M8N03). They have not yet encountered the formal distinction between rational and irrational numbers as a system, represented the full hierarchy of real number subsets, or used the real number line to solve problems involving irrational values.

Width

Minimum
Label a diagram showing the nesting of natural numbers, integers, rational and irrational numbers within the reals; give one example of a number belonging to each subset.
Typical
Represent the relationships between all subsets using a diagram; explain what it means for one set to be a subset of another; classify given numbers (e.g. −3, 2/5, √7, π, 0.333…) into the correct subset and justify.
Wider
  • Investigate whether √4 is rational or irrational and explain why not all square roots are irrational
  • Explore how the discovery of irrational numbers (e.g. the diagonal of a unit square) challenged ancient Greek mathematical assumptions about number
  • Investigate the difference between algebraic irrationals (e.g. √2) and transcendental irrationals (e.g. π) at a conceptual level

Hinge

Students who cannot reliably classify numbers into subsets carry category errors into every subsequent node — misidentifying irrationals as rationals corrupts exact-form reasoning, number line placement and formula substitution.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly believe all decimals are rational (identifying 0.333… as 'just a long decimal') or that all square roots are irrational. Classifying a range of examples across all subsets — including edge cases like √4, −0, 0.1̄ — resolves these conflations and builds robust category understanding.
