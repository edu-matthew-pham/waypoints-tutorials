← AC9M9N01 Real number system

Exact and approximate representations

AC9M9N01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between exact representations of irrational numbers (e.g. √2, π) and their decimal approximations, and explain why decimal approximations are not equal to the exact value.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies a given decimal value as an approximation of an irrational and states which irrational it approximates
  • Explains why no finite decimal expansion can exactly equal an irrational number
  • Writes an exact form (e.g. √3) and a correct decimal approximation for the same value, labelling which is which

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Subsets of the real number system.

Width

Minimum
State that √2 and π cannot be written as exact terminating or recurring decimals; write a decimal approximation of √2 to 4 decimal places and explain what 'approximation' means in this context.
Typical
Distinguish between exact forms (e.g. √5, π, 3π/2) and decimal approximations across several examples; explain that no finite or recurring decimal can equal an irrational; recognise when a problem calls for an exact answer vs a decimal approximation.
Wider
  • Investigate what happens when calculators display irrational numbers — discuss what the display shows and what it omits
  • Explore contexts in which exact form is required (e.g. engineering tolerances, symbolic computation) vs contexts where approximation is sufficient
  • Investigate how mathematicians establish that a number is irrational — explore the classic proof that √2 is irrational (proof by contradiction) at a conceptual level
