← AC9M1ST01 Acquire and record categorical data

Identify categories and plan collection

AC9M1ST01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify categories for a data collection question and plan how to record responses before collecting.

Success criteria

  • Names the categories that will be used before data collection begins.
  • Selects a recording format appropriate to the data question.

Prior knowledge

Students can collect, sort and compare data represented by objects and images (AC9MFST01). They have not yet designed data collection processes, selected from multiple recording formats, or reviewed the effectiveness of their collection method.

Width

Minimum
For a given data question, identify at least two categories and choose a recording format before starting to collect data.
Typical
Discuss possible data questions of interest; identify appropriate categories for the variable; select a recording format (list, tally, objects, drawings) that matches the context; predict what the data might show; begin collection with the plan in place.
Wider
  • Explore yes/no question trees as a data collection tool (chatbot role-play), planning the category structure before the questions are asked.
  • Investigate how data categories are structured in First Nations representational systems — examining how different purposes for representing information lead to different categorical frameworks.

Hinge

Students who collect without defined categories produce data that cannot be recorded consistently or compared across responses — this directly undermines Node 2 (recording using consistent formats) and Node 3 (reviewing for completeness and comparability).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents who jump directly to collecting without planning categories often produce data that cannot be compared or interpreted. Scaffolding the pre-collection step — naming categories, choosing format — is the key teaching move for building statistical thinking.
