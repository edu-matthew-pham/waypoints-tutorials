Acquire and record categorical data

AC9M1ST01 · Year 1 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Acquire and record data for categorical variables in various ways including using digital tools, objects, images, drawings, lists, tally marks and symbols

Prior knowledge

Students can collect, sort and compare data represented by objects and images (AC9MFST01). They have not yet designed data collection processes, selected from multiple recording formats, or reviewed the effectiveness of their collection method.

By the end

Students acquire and record data for categorical variables in various ways including using digital tools, objects, images, drawings, lists, tally marks and symbols.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • discussing methods of collecting data to answer a question, sharing ideas and trying out some of the suggested methods; reviewing the data collected
  • collecting and recording information on a topic of interest using lists; examining the data to generate some questions that it could answer
  • creating a tally to record data while observing events such as the year level of students using the bike shed
  • using star charts with stickers or emojis to represent class data
  • role-playing being a chatbot asking simple yes/no questions and collecting data, representing using virtual manipulatives
  • exploring ways of representing, sharing and communicating data through stories and symbols used by First Nations Australians

Waypoints

  • 1. Identify categories and plan collectionHingeStudents can identify categories for a data collection question and plan how to record responses before collecting.
  • 2. Record using multiple formatsStudents can record data for a categorical variable using at least two different recording formats, including at least one involving a mark or symbol system (tally, emoji, sticker).
  • 3. Review and reflect on collectionStudents can review collected data, explain what it shows and suggest how the collection method could be improved next time.
