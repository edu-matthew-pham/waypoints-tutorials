Model additive and multiplicative problems

AC9M2N06 · Year 2 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving additive and multiplicative situations, including money transactions; represent situations and choose calculation strategies; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation

Prior knowledge

Students use mathematical modelling to solve practical additive problems including simple money (AC9M1N05) and equal sharing/grouping problems (AC9M1N06). They have not yet modelled situations requiring a deliberate choice between additive and multiplicative strategies, or interpreted and communicated solutions in terms of context.

By the end

Students use mathematical modelling to solve practical additive and multiplicative problems including money transactions — representing situations, choosing appropriate calculation strategies, and interpreting and communicating solutions in terms of the situation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • modelling an everyday additive or multiplicative situation; deciding the operation and justifying the choice
  • modelling simple money problems with whole dollar amounts
  • modelling practical problems: team sizes, sharing food or money; deciding what to do with remainders
  • modelling 'How many days are left in this year?' using a calendar
  • modelling problems from First Nations Australian children's instructive games; e.g. Yangamini

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying the operationHingeStudents can interpret a practical problem situation and identify whether it requires addition, subtraction, multiplication or division, justifying their choice.
  • 2. Representing and solvingStudents can represent a practical problem using a diagram, number sentence or materials, choose a calculation strategy, and find the answer.
  • 3. Interpreting and communicating solutionsStudents can interpret their calculated answer in terms of the original situation and communicate the solution clearly, including the units or context it refers to.
