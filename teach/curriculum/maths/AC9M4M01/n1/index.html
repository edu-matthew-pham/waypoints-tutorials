Reading between the marks

AC9M4M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read a measurement from a scaled instrument where the value falls between two marked units, identifying the unit each mark represents.

Success criteria

  • Students can read a measurement from a scaled instrument where the value falls between two marked units
  • Students can explain what the unmarked subdivisions on a given instrument represent

Prior knowledge

Students can measure with familiar metric units using instruments with labelled markings (AC9M3M02) and read analog clocks to the nearest minute (AC9M3M04). They have not yet interpreted readings that fall between labelled markings on scaled instruments, or measured duration on clocks without marked minutes.

Width

Minimum
Read one measurement from a scaled instrument (e.g. kitchen scale) where the value falls between two marked values; explain what each marking on the scale represents.
Typical
Interpret partial and unmarked units on analog scales across at least two different attributes (e.g. mass on a kitchen scale, volume in a measuring jug); explain what unit the subdivisions represent; record values using appropriate units.
Wider
  • Make a scaled measuring instrument from scratch (e.g. a measuring cup using a known container and subdivisions), explaining how they determined where to place each marking
  • Compare analog and digital instruments measuring the same attribute — discussing what information each provides and how to handle a digital reading between whole-unit increments

Hinge

Students who cannot identify what each subdivision represents on an unfamiliar instrument cannot meaningfully select or use instruments in Node 2's multi-attribute measurement tasks — they will record incorrect values or avoid instruments where the reading falls between marks.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only practise with fully marked instruments may not recognise what unmarked subdivisions represent. Reading across different instruments (scales, measuring jugs, thermometers) where subdivision sizes differ builds the generalised skill of 'determine the scale unit before reading'.
