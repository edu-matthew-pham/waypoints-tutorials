Measure with scaled instruments

AC9M4M01 · Year 4 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Interpret unmarked and partial units when measuring and comparing attributes of length, mass, capacity, duration and temperature, using scaled and digital instruments and appropriate units

Prior knowledge

Students can measure with familiar metric units using instruments with labelled markings (AC9M3M02) and read analog clocks to the nearest minute (AC9M3M04). They have not yet interpreted readings that fall between labelled markings on scaled instruments, or measured duration on clocks without marked minutes.

By the end

Students interpret partial and unmarked units on scaled instruments across five attributes, recording measures in appropriate units including decimal notation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • reading the mass of objects measured with digital and analog kitchen scales and explaining what unit of mass the lines on the analog scales refer to
  • deciding on which attribute, unit and measuring instrument to use to compare the length and mass of various things
  • using scaled instruments such as tape measures, measuring jugs, kitchen scales and thermometers
  • reading and interpreting the scale of an analog clock without marked minutes to estimate the time to the nearest minute
  • using the timer or alarm function of a clock to alert when a specified duration has elapsed
  • making a scaled measuring instrument such as a tape measure, ruler, sand timer, sun dial or measuring cup
  • exploring the different types of scaled instruments used by First Nations Ranger Groups and other groups to make decisions about caring for Country/Place

Waypoints

  • 1. Reading between the marksHingeStudents can read a measurement from a scaled instrument where the value falls between two marked units, identifying the unit each mark represents.
  • 2. Measurement across multiple attributesStudents can measure and compare across all five attributes using appropriate instruments and units, recording measures with decimal notation where appropriate.
