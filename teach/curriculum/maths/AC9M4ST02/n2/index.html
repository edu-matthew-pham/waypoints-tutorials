Distribution shape and variation

AC9M4ST02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 4 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can discuss the shape of a data distribution and describe the variation in the data.

Success criteria

  • Students can describe the overall shape of a distribution using informal language
  • Students can identify and describe the variation in a data set (whether values are spread out or clustered)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Comparing display types.

Width

Minimum
Describe one feature of a distribution's shape (e.g. which category has the most data, whether values are spread out or clustered).
Typical
Discuss the shape of distributions using informal language (spread out, clustered, even); identify the highest and lowest frequency categories; describe variation — how much the values differ; comment on what the distribution suggests about the original question.
Wider
  • Compare distributions from two different data sets on the same question (e.g. survey results from two different classes) and describe the differences in shape and variation
  • Investigate what happens to a distribution when the sample size changes — collecting data in two rounds and comparing the distributions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDiscussing distribution shape requires students to move beyond individual data points to see the aggregate pattern. The reasoning scaffold — 'look at the whole shape first, then describe where the data clusters' — must be made explicit to shift from point-by-point reading to pattern recognition.
