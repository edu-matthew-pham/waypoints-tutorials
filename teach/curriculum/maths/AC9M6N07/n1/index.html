Finding a fraction of a quantity

AC9M6N07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can find a unit fraction and non-unit fraction of a whole number or measurement quantity using multiplication and division.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly finds a unit fraction of a quantity using division.
  • Student extends to non-unit fractions using the divide-then-multiply method.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that 100% represents the whole and connect familiar percentages to decimal and fraction equivalents (AC9M5N04). They can use mathematical modelling to solve additive and multiplicative problems including financial contexts (AC9M5N09). They have not yet found fractions, decimals or percentages of quantities systematically or applied percentage discount calculations.

Width

Minimum
Find a unit fraction (one-half, one-quarter, one-third, one-fifth) of a given whole number quantity; extend to non-unit fractions (three-quarters, two-thirds); show the division-then-multiplication method.
Typical
Find any familiar fraction of a quantity using efficient strategies; connect to multiplication (finding ¾ of 24 = ¾ × 24 = 18); apply to measurement, money, and data contexts; verify results using estimation.
Wider
  • Apply fraction-of-quantity to real contexts such as recipes, sharing scenarios, and map scales
  • Investigate: if you know ¾ of a quantity is 18, what is the whole? — work backwards from fractions of quantities

Hinge

The divide-then-multiply procedure for finding a fraction of a quantity is the conceptual model that underpins Node 2 (percentage of a quantity) and Node 3 (discount calculations). Students who cannot reliably execute this two-step procedure will fail to understand percentage methods as a connected extension.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFinding a non-unit fraction of a quantity (divide by denominator, multiply by numerator) is a two-step procedure where errors in either step propagate. Fluency with the procedure is needed before percentage and discount applications.
