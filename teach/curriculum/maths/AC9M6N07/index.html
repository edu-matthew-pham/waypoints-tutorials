Fractions, decimals and percentages

AC9M6N07 · Year 6 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Solve problems that require finding a familiar fraction, decimal or percentage of a quantity, including percentage discounts, choosing efficient calculation strategies and using digital tools where appropriate

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that 100% represents the whole and connect familiar percentages to decimal and fraction equivalents (AC9M5N04). They can use mathematical modelling to solve additive and multiplicative problems including financial contexts (AC9M5N09). They have not yet found fractions, decimals or percentages of quantities systematically or applied percentage discount calculations.

By the end

Students can solve problems that require finding a familiar fraction, decimal or percentage of a quantity, including percentage discounts, selecting efficient strategies.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining how 1/3 of a quantity can be achieved by dividing by 3, and how 1/3 can be used to find other fractions of the same quantity
  • investigating percentage discounts of 15%, 30% and 45% using their decimal representations
  • linking percentages to their decimal equivalent of tenths and hundredths and using these to determine percentage discounts
  • explaining the equivalence between percentages and fractions including 33⅓% and 1/3
  • representing a situation with a mathematical expression involving finding a familiar fraction, decimal or percentage of a quantity

Waypoints

  • 1. Finding a fraction of a quantityHingeStudents can find a unit fraction and non-unit fraction of a whole number or measurement quantity using multiplication and division.
  • 2. Finding a percentage of a quantityStudents can find common percentages (10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 20%) of a quantity using efficient strategies connecting percentages to fraction and decimal equivalents.
  • 3. Percentage discounts and financial applicationsHingeStudents can calculate a percentage discount on a price and determine the discounted price.
