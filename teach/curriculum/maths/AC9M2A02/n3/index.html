← AC9M2A02 Addition facts to 20

Related subtraction facts

AC9M2A02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 2 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can derive related subtraction facts from known addition facts (e.g. 8+7=15, so 15-7=8 and 15-8=7) and explain the connection.

Success criteria

  • States both related subtraction facts given an addition fact (e.g. from 8+7=15, gives 15-7=8 and 15-8=7)
  • Explains why the addition and subtraction facts are related using part-part-whole language

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Recall of addition facts to 20.

Width

Minimum
Given an addition fact, state the two related subtraction facts and explain why they are related.
Typical
Derive related subtraction facts systematically from addition facts to 20; use part-part-whole diagrams to show the relationship; apply to solve subtraction problems by recalling the related addition fact.
Wider
  • Partition collections of up to 20 in all possible ways; record all related addition and subtraction facts; explain patterns noticed
  • Apply related facts to check subtraction answers: 'I can check 15-7=8 by adding 7+8'

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe inverse relationship between addition and subtraction is not intuitively obvious to Year 2 students. The reasoning scaffold — 'if the whole is 15 and one part is 7, the other part must be 8' — needs to be made explicit and connected to the part-part-whole model before students can apply it independently.
