← AC9M6SP03 Tessellations and transformations

What makes a tessellation

AC9M6SP03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify what properties of a shape and transformation allow it to tessellate (tile a plane without gaps or overlaps).

Success criteria

  • Student identifies whether a polygon tessellates and explains the reason using interior angles.
  • Student identifies the transformation(s) generating a given tessellation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Combining transformations.

Width

Minimum
Identify whether a given regular polygon tessellates by testing with the interior angle; explain why equilateral triangles, squares, and regular hexagons tessellate and pentagons do not.
Typical
Test whether regular and irregular polygons tessellate; connect tessellation to the interior angle sum at each vertex (must be 360°); identify the transformation rule that generates a given tessellation.
Wider
  • Investigate which regular polygons tessellate and explain the mathematical reason using angle sums
  • Explore semi-regular tessellations (combinations of two or more regular polygons) and identify the transformation rules generating them

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who test only familiar shapes may form an incomplete rule for tessellation. Testing diverse shapes — irregular polygons, shapes with curved edges, combinations — and confronting the 360° angle-sum explanation builds a principled understanding.
