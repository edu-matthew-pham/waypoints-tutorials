← AC9M3A01 Addition and subtraction as inverse operations

Inverse relationship

AC9M3A01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain, using a part-part-whole model or bar model, why addition and subtraction are inverse operations.

Success criteria

  • Students can write all four related facts in an addition/subtraction fact family for a given set of three numbers.
  • Students can explain why addition and subtraction are inverse operations using a diagram or model.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise, describe and create additive patterns using numbers, shapes and objects; identify missing elements in sequences (AC9M2A01). They have not yet formally articulated the inverse relationship between addition and subtraction or applied it to find unknowns in number sentences.

Width

Minimum
Use a part-part-whole diagram for one set of related addition and subtraction facts (e.g. 16+8=24, 24-8=16, 24-16=8), explaining the connection.
Typical
Represent multiple sets of related addition and subtraction facts using part-part-whole diagrams, bar models and number lines; explain the inverse relationship for each.
Wider
  • Explore First Nations Australian stories and dances that represent balance and transformation, connecting to the concept of inverse operations
  • Investigate how the inverse relationship extends to multiplication and division — introducing the structural pattern across operation families

Hinge

Conceptual understanding of the inverse relationship is the foundation for Nodes 2 and 3 — systematic partitioning and finding unknowns both require students to understand why an inverse operation works, not merely remember which operation to apply. Students who only have mechanical fact-family recall cannot flexibly solve unknowns in varied positions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often accept the fact families mechanically without understanding why they work. Multiple representations (part-part-whole, bar model, number line, story context) help students see the structural reason rather than memorise pairs.
