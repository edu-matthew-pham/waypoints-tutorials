← AC9M3A01 Addition and subtraction as inverse operations

Partitioning using inverse operations

AC9M3A01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use the inverse relationship to partition numbers in multiple ways and record all related facts.

Success criteria

  • Students can generate multiple partitions of a given number and write the related addition and subtraction facts.
  • Students can explain the relationship between the partitions and the fact family.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Inverse relationship.

Width

Minimum
Given a total of 24, find at least three different part-part-whole partitions and write all related addition and subtraction facts for each.
Typical
Systematically partition numbers using part-part-whole models; record complete fact families; explain how partitioning relates to the inverse operation.
Wider
  • Explore how many ways a given number can be partitioned, connecting to combinatorics thinking (e.g. 'How many ways can 12 be split?')
  • Apply systematic partitioning to problem-solving: 'The answer is 30 — what could the question be?' using both addition and subtraction

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifySystematic exploration of all partitions for a number builds fluency with fact families and prepares students for algebraic thinking by demonstrating that a number has multiple equivalent decompositions.
