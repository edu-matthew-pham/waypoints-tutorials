Finding unknowns in number sentences

AC9M3A01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can find the unknown value in addition and subtraction number sentences using the inverse operation.

Success criteria

  • Students can find the unknown in a number sentence where the unknown is in any position.
  • Students can explain which inverse operation they used and why.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Partitioning using inverse operations.

Width

Minimum
Solve three number sentences with unknowns in different positions (e.g. □+8=24, 24-□=8, □-8=16) using the inverse operation, showing reasoning.
Typical
Solve a range of number sentences with unknowns in any position, using inverse operations; represent the strategy clearly including with a calculator check.
Wider
  • Explore unknowns in word problems where the unknown position varies — 'Peter had some money, spent $375, now has $158; how much did he start with?'
  • Connect number sentences with unknowns to algebraic equations — introducing box/variable notation as a preview of formal algebra

Hinge

Finding unknowns in number sentences is directly applied in AC9M3N06 mathematical modelling — practical problems frequently require students to find a missing value from a number sentence. Students who cannot apply the inverse operation to unknowns in varied positions will be unable to solve formulated problems where the unknown is not in the most familiar position.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUnknown-in-different-positions problems require students to reason about the structure of the equation, not just apply a procedure. Explicit scaffolding of 'which operation undoes the given one?' prevents random guessing.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierFinding unknowns integrates the inverse relationship and partitioning ideas — students who have understood nodes 1 and 2 can approach unknowns flexibly; those who haven't will guess.
