Compounding errors in repeated calculations

AC9M10M04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how measurement errors compound across repeated or multi-step calculations and describe the effect on accuracy.

Lessons on this site

  • Errors that build up

Success criteria

  • Student explains, with a worked example, how a small error in a single measurement produces a larger error after repeated calculations
  • Student identifies at least one practical context where accumulated measurement error has meaningful consequences

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Impact of individual measurement errors.

Width

Minimum
Given a calculation repeated multiple times (or a multi-step formula), describe how errors accumulate — that repeated rounding or truncation can produce a substantially different final result.
Typical
Analyse how compounding errors affect accuracy in at least two contexts (repeated interest calculations, multi-step geometric calculations, sequential measurements); estimate the accumulated error after n repetitions; discuss when accumulated error becomes practically significant.
Wider
  • Investigate the social impact of measurement errors in First Peoples of Australia contexts — including how errors in population counts or dating results have influenced policy or historical narratives
  • Explore how measurement errors in perception and control systems of autonomous vehicles compound across multiple sensor readings and what safeguards are built in to manage accumulated error

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCompounding errors appear very differently across contexts — financial truncation produces systematic underpayment, geometric errors grow multiplicatively, sequential sensor errors accumulate directionally. Multiple contexts are needed to generalise the concept.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierUnderstanding compounding errors requires integrating knowledge of individual error impact (node 2) with iterative reasoning about how small errors grow across repetitions. This is the node where error analysis becomes applicable to real multi-step situations.
