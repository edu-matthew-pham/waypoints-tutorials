← AC9M8M03 Circumference and area of circles

Applying circle area and circumference formulas

AC9M8M03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students apply A = πr² and C = 2πr to calculate area and circumference, find radius/diameter given area or circumference, and solve composite shape problems.

Lessons on this site

  • Circle problems

Success criteria

  • Correctly calculates area given radius or radius given area.
  • Solves a composite shape problem involving circular components.
  • Uses an appropriate approximation for π and states it.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Deriving the area of a circle formula.

Width

Minimum
Calculate area given radius; find radius given area; solve one practical problem.
Typical
Apply both formulas in practical contexts; find any unknown (r, d, C, or A) given another; solve composite shape problems including semicircles.
Wider
  • Explore traditional weaving designs by First Nations Australians and investigate the significance and use of circles — calculate areas and circumferences in context.
  • Investigate composite shapes combining circles with other polygons — e.g. area of a stadium or athletic track.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCircle measurement problems require recognising which formula applies and which quantity is unknown. Varied practical contexts build the judgment to select the right formula and direction of calculation.
