Solving linear equations algebraically

AC9M8A02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students solve linear equations of the form ax + b = cx + d algebraically, including equations requiring expansion, and verify by substitution.

Success criteria

  • Solves a linear equation with unknowns on both sides correctly.
  • Verifies the solution by substitution.
  • Does not leave the unknown on both sides after the first step.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Graphing linear relations including inequalities.

Width

Minimum
Solve a two-step linear equation with the unknown on both sides (e.g. 3x + 7 = 6x − 9).
Typical
Solve linear equations requiring expansion of brackets; solve equations involving fractions; always verify by substitution.
Wider
  • Solve linear equations arising from geometry problems: find the value of x given angle relationships or perimeter conditions.
  • Investigate equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions — what do these look like graphically?

Hinge

Equation-solving fluency and sign handling are directly required in Node 3 inequality solving — students who make systematic sign errors in equations will apply the same errors in inequalities, including failure to reverse the inequality sign when required.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSolving equations with unknowns on both sides requires procedural fluency and reliable sign handling. This is a high-error-rate skill that needs sustained practice before graphical methods are layered on.
