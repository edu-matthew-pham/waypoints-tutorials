Graphing linear relations including inequalities

AC9M8A02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students graph linear relations of the form y = mx + b and one-variable inequalities on the Cartesian plane, identifying key features.

Success criteria

  • Correctly graphs a linear relation from a rule or table.
  • Identifies gradient and y-intercept from graph and equation.
  • Correctly represents an inequality including the boundary line convention (solid vs dashed).

Prior knowledge

Students can plot ordered pairs and describe linear relationships on the Cartesian plane (from AC9M7A05). Students can solve one-variable linear equations (from AC9M7A03).

Width

Minimum
Graph a linear relation y = mx + b from a table of values and identify gradient and y-intercept.
Typical
Graph linear relations and inequalities (x = a, y = a, y ≤ mx + b); identify gradient and intercepts; describe the effect of changing m and b.
Wider
  • Investigate what happens to the graph when m is negative, zero, or undefined — connect each case to a physical or real-world interpretation.
  • Explore graphing two linear relations on the same axes and identifying the point of intersection — preview of simultaneous equations.

Hinge

Node 3 requires graphical verification of inequality solutions — students who have not worked with inequality graph forms (including boundary line conventions) cannot use graphical methods to verify their algebraic solutions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only graph y = mx + b forms often fail with x = a or inequality forms. Exposure to the full range of linear relation types prevents incomplete understanding.
