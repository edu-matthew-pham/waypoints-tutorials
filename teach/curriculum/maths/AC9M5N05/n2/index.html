Adding and subtracting with related denominators

AC9M5N05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can add and subtract fractions with related denominators by converting to a common denominator.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly converts to a common denominator and completes the operation
  • Student expresses the result in simplest form

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Adding and subtracting with same denominators.

Width

Minimum
Convert one fraction to match the other's denominator and then add or subtract; verify the result.
Typical
Solve problems involving fractions with related denominators; choose the most efficient equivalent form; express answers in simplest form.
Wider
  • Use cooking or construction problem contexts requiring fraction addition
  • Solve multi-step problems combining same and related denominator fractions

Hinge

The conversion-then-operate sequence is the prerequisite for Node 3 — students who skip or conflate steps cannot reliably tackle multi-step problems where both same and related denominator operations occur.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe conversion step requires an explicit sequence: identify related denominators → find conversion factor → convert → operate → simplify. Without scaffolding, students conflate or skip steps.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepRelated denominators vary — some pairs are obvious (halves/quarters), some less so (thirds/sixths, fifths/tenths). Varied pairs prevent students from learning a single pattern.
