Designing algorithms for spatial problems

AC9M10SP03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can design an algorithm (using pseudocode or flowchart) that addresses a defined spatial problem.

Success criteria

  • Student produces pseudocode or a flowchart that is unambiguous and complete for the defined spatial problem
  • Student identifies decision points and loops in the algorithm and explains their role

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Defining and decomposing spatial problems.

Width

Minimum
Write pseudocode or a flowchart for an algorithm that solves a defined spatial problem; the algorithm must specify all steps clearly enough that another person could follow it.
Typical
Design algorithms for spatial problems across at least two different types (e.g. scale model generation and network routing); express algorithms in pseudocode or flowchart; identify decision points and loops; evaluate the algorithm's completeness before testing.
Wider
  • Design algorithms for producing self-similar (fractal) patterns using recursive pseudocode; trace the algorithm for at least two iterations to verify the pattern
  • Investigate how computational thinking approaches to weighted network problems (shortest path, minimum spanning tree) can be expressed as algorithms and test them on small networks

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need experience designing algorithms for multiple spatial problem types — the algorithm structures for scale model generation, floor plan optimisation and network routing are different. A repertoire of problem-type-to-algorithm-structure mappings is the goal.
