Sides of a right-angled triangle relative to an angle

AC9M9SP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can correctly identify the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides for a specified angle in a right-angled triangle.

Lessons on this site

  • Name the sides

Success criteria

  • Correctly labels hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent for a specified angle in at least three different triangle orientations
  • Explains that opposite and adjacent are defined relative to the angle, not absolute positions
  • Correctly re-labels when the specified angle changes within the same triangle

Prior knowledge

Students can identify conditions for congruence and similarity of triangles (AC9M8SP01). They have not yet used similarity to establish that side-length ratios in right-angled triangles are constant for a given angle, or named and used sine, cosine and tangent.

Width

Minimum
Label the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides relative to a specified angle in two different right-angled triangles (different orientations).
Typical
Identify opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse correctly across a range of right-angled triangle orientations and for different specified angles; explain that 'opposite' and 'adjacent' are relative to the angle, not fixed sides of the triangle.
Wider
  • Investigate what happens to the labels when the specified angle changes — show that opposite and adjacent swap when the complementary angle is used
  • Connect the labelling convention to the definitions of sin, cos and tan before formal calculation
  • Draw right-angled triangles in multiple orientations (not just with the right angle at bottom-right) and practise labelling for different angles

Hinge

Correct side labelling relative to the specified angle (not absolute position) is prerequisite for every trig ratio calculation in N2 and N3 and all applied work in AC9M9M03. Every wrong label produces a wrong ratio and a wrong answer.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsCorrect labelling of sides relative to the angle — particularly in non-standard orientations — is a prerequisite for correct ratio application. This is a precision-required identification skill; errors here propagate to every trig calculation.
