← AC9M9SP01 Trigonometric ratios

Constancy of ratios — similarity argument

AC9M9SP01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain using properties of similarity why the ratio of two specified sides is constant for a given angle, regardless of triangle size.

Success criteria

  • Calculates the same ratio (e.g. opposite/hypotenuse) for at least two similar triangles with the same angle and shows the values are equal
  • Explains why the ratio is constant using the property of similar triangles
  • Confirms that the ratio changes when a different angle is used

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sides of a right-angled triangle relative to an angle.

Width

Minimum
Draw two similar right-angled triangles with the same acute angle; measure corresponding sides; calculate the ratio opposite/hypotenuse for both triangles and show it is the same.
Typical
Investigate nested similar triangles (or multiple similar triangles) to verify that the ratio of corresponding sides is constant for a given angle; explain this using the definition of similar triangles (corresponding sides in proportion); do this for at least two different ratios (e.g. opp/hyp and adj/hyp).
Wider
  • Connect the constancy argument to the formal definition of similarity — corresponding sides in similar triangles are proportional by the scale factor
  • Investigate how the constancy property breaks down for non-similar triangles (i.e. different angles) — confirm that the ratio changes when the angle changes
  • Connect the sine ratio to the altitude of a unit-hypotenuse triangle and the cosine to the base, developing geometric intuition for the ratios

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe similarity argument is the conceptual core of this descriptor and the most common point where understanding breaks down — students accept the constancy empirically without understanding why. Scaffolding the argument (similar triangles → corresponding sides proportional → ratio is constant) builds the 'why' alongside the 'what'.
