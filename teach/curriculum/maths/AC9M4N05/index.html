Multiply and divide by powers of 10

AC9M4N05 · Year 4 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Solve problems involving multiplying or dividing natural numbers by multiples and powers of 10 without a calculator, using the multiplicative relationship between the place value of digits

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and represent numbers beyond 10 000 and understand place value conventions (AC9M3N01); they can multiply and divide one- and two-digit numbers (AC9M3N04). They have not yet applied the multiplicative structure of place value to multiply or divide by 10, 100 or 1000.

By the end

Students solve problems involving multiplying and dividing natural numbers by multiples and powers of 10 by applying the multiplicative relationship between place value positions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using physical or virtual materials to demonstrate the multiplicative relationship between the places
  • using materials such as place value charts, numeral expanders or sliders to recognise and explain why multiplying by 10 moves the digits one place to the left
  • using a calculator or other digital tools to recognise and develop an understanding of the effect of multiplying or dividing numbers by 10s, 100s and 1000s

Waypoints

  • 1. Multiplying by 10 — digit movementHingeStudents can explain and demonstrate that multiplying by 10 moves each digit one place to the left.
  • 2. Dividing by 10 and extending to 100 and 1000Students can multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000 using the place value relationship, and solve related problems.
