← AC9M4N05 Multiply and divide by powers of 10

Dividing by 10 and extending to 100 and 1000

AC9M4N05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 4 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000 using the place value relationship, and solve related problems.

Lessons on this site

  • Multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000

Success criteria

  • Students can multiply and divide a number by 10, 100 and 1000 without a calculator
  • Students can solve a practical problem by identifying that ×10 or ÷10 is needed and applying it correctly

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Multiplying by 10 — digit movement.

Width

Minimum
Divide one number by 10 using a place value chart and explain the digit movement; solve one multiplication problem involving 100.
Typical
Use place value charts to demonstrate multiplication and division by 10, 100 and 1000; generalise the pattern (number of places = number of zeros); apply to solve computation problems without a calculator; use digital tools to check and extend the pattern.
Wider
  • Investigate the pattern using a spreadsheet: enter numbers and apply ×10, ×100, ×1000 formulas, record results in a place value table and generalise the pattern from 100 terms
  • Apply the place value pattern to multi-step problems involving metric conversions (e.g. how many millimetres in 4.5 metres?)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to build a repertoire of related problems they can solve using this rule — not just ×10 but ×100, ÷10, ÷100, and related combinations. A narrow exposure to ×10 alone leaves gaps when ×100 and ×1000 appear.
