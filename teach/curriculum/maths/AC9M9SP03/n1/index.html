Representing a geometric process as an algorithm

AC9M9SP03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can represent a known geometric construction or theorem application as a step-by-step algorithm using pseudocode or a flowchart.

Success criteria

  • Writes a step-by-step algorithm with clear, unambiguous steps
  • Correctly identifies at least one decision step (with two branches) and at least one computation step
  • Uses pseudocode or flowchart notation consistently

Prior knowledge

Students can apply the enlargement transformation and identify invariant properties of similar shapes (AC9M9SP02). They have not yet designed and iteratively refined algorithms grounded in geometric constructions and theorems.

Width

Minimum
Write a pseudocode or flowchart algorithm for one geometric process (e.g. applying the triangle inequality to test whether three lengths can form a triangle); identify each step as either a computation or a decision.
Typical
Represent at least two different geometric processes as algorithms (e.g. triangle inequality check, Pythagorean triple generation, angle bisection); use pseudocode or flowchart notation correctly; distinguish sequential steps from decision branches.
Wider
  • Represent a geometric construction (e.g. bisecting an angle using compass and straightedge) as a precise step-by-step algorithm and discuss what makes each step unambiguous
  • Investigate how geometric algorithms underpin computer graphics — describe conceptually how a rotation or reflection of a polygon could be implemented algorithmically
  • Compare pseudocode and flowchart representations of the same algorithm — discuss the trade-offs in clarity and detail
