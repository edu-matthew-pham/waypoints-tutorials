Estimation as a check

AC9M4N07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use estimation to determine what range a calculation answer should fall within, and identify unreasonable answers.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Students can estimate the result of a calculation and state a bounding range
  • Students can identify an unreasonable answer using estimation and explain why it is unreasonable

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Rounding to convenient numbers.

Width

Minimum
Estimate the result of one calculation by rounding and determine a bounding range (the answer will be between ___ and ___); identify whether a given answer is reasonable.
Typical
Use rounded estimates to determine a range for calculation results; identify whether computed answers are reasonable; explain the effect of rounding both numbers up, both down, or one each way on the estimate; apply to multi-step calculations.
Wider
  • Investigate the accumulated effect of rounding in multi-step calculations — when does rounding at each step compound into a significant error?
  • Compare estimation strategies for the same problem: front-end estimation vs rounding vs compatible numbers, and discuss which gives a closer estimate

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need the reasoning scaffold 'what am I over-counting and what am I under-counting when I round?' to develop the habit of predicting estimate direction. Without this, estimation remains a ritual rather than a genuine check on reasonableness.
