← AC9M7A02 Formulating algebraic expressions

Expressions with brackets and multiple operations

AC9M7A02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students formulate algebraic expressions involving brackets, including those where brackets are required to preserve intended meaning.

Lessons on this site

  • When brackets are needed

Success criteria

  • Correctly writes expressions requiring brackets to preserve intended meaning.
  • Can explain why brackets are necessary in a given expression.
  • Evaluates expressions with brackets correctly applying order of operations.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Translating words to algebraic expressions.

Width

Minimum
Write an algebraic expression that requires brackets (e.g. 'the sum of x and 3, multiplied by 4').
Typical
Formulate expressions from multi-step descriptions; explain when brackets are necessary; evaluate expressions for given values.
Wider
  • Compare equivalent expressions with and without brackets: 2(x + 3) vs 2x + 6. Verify equivalence by substitution.
  • Apply distributive property to expand and factorise simple expressions — preview of Y8 algebra.

Hinge

Students who omit necessary brackets formulate expressions with different meanings than intended — this error cascades into AC9M7A03 equation solving where setting up the equation incorrectly means the solution is always wrong regardless of solving skill.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDeciding when brackets are required demands explicit reasoning about order of operations and meaning. Students routinely omit necessary brackets or add unnecessary ones without a clear reasoning process.
