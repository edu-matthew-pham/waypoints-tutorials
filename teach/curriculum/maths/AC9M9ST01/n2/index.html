Evaluating statistical claims — means and medians

AC9M9ST01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate a claim about a population mean or median by linking it back to how the data was collected and what the data displays show.

Success criteria

  • Links a specific statistical claim in a report to the sampling method and data collection process
  • Assesses whether the claim is adequately supported by the evidence described
  • Identifies at least one reason why the reported mean or median might not accurately represent the full population

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How data is obtained — sampling methods in survey reports.

Width

Minimum
Identify one claim about a population mean or median in a survey report; explain whether the sampling method and sample size support or undermine that claim.
Typical
Evaluate statistical claims in survey reports by linking claims to displays, reported statistics and sampling information; assess whether the reported mean or median is a reasonable estimate of the population parameter given how the data was obtained.
Wider
  • Analyse reports from the ABS or other government sources on Australian population data and compare to similar data from other countries (e.g. age distributions in Australia, Cambodia, Tonga) — evaluate whether comparisons are valid given how the data was collected
  • Investigate statistical reports on Australia's trade with Asia-region countries — evaluate whether the reported statistics support the claims made
  • Explore how different ways of computing an average (mean vs median) can produce different impressions from the same data set

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only evaluate reports from one domain (e.g. political polls) apply a narrow template and fail to transfer evaluation skills to scientific, environmental or health reports. Working across diverse report types — different fields, different claim styles, different audiences — builds flexible critical reading that generalises.
