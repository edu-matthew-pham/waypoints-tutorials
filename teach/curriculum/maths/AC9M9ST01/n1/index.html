How data is obtained — sampling methods in survey reports

AC9M9ST01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify from a survey report how data was obtained and explain how the sampling method could affect the representativeness of the sample.

Lessons on this site

  • Who was asked, and how

Success criteria

  • Identifies the sampling method from the report description
  • States at least one way the sampling method could limit representativeness or introduce bias
  • Connects the sampling method to the reliability of the reported statistic

Prior knowledge

Students can compare distributions from random samples and recognise the effect of sample size on variation (AC9M8ST03). They have not yet critically analysed survey reports for methodology, or evaluated whether reported means and medians are reliable estimates of population parameters.

Width

Minimum
Read one survey report and identify the sampling method used; explain one way the method could introduce bias or limit representativeness.
Typical
Analyse survey reports from media sources; identify sampling methods (random, convenience, voluntary response); explain how each method affects representativeness; link sampling method to the reliability of any statistical claims about the population.
Wider
  • Analyse reports on public opinion surveys about environmental issues (e.g. land clearing, wind farms) — evaluate the sampling method and discuss the reasonableness of inferences
  • Investigate how technological and social changes (e.g. decreased landline usage, caller ID avoidance) create new sampling biases in phone surveys
  • Explore how NLP tools are used to analyse large volumes of survey data and discuss limitations of automated analysis

Hinge

Students who only evaluate sample size (not sampling method) cannot assess representativeness in N2 and cannot critically analyse reports. The method-vs-size distinction is the conceptual foundation for all statistical evaluation in this descriptor.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly evaluate surveys only on surface features (large sample = trustworthy) without examining how the sample was obtained. Working across multiple report types — polls, media surveys, research reports — builds the discrimination between sample size and sampling method quality.
