← AC9M7M01 Area of triangles and parallelograms

Deriving the triangle area formula

AC9M7M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students understand why the area of a triangle is half the area of a related rectangle or parallelogram, and can state the formula A = ½bh.

Success criteria

  • States the area formula for a triangle and explains its derivation.
  • Correctly identifies the base and corresponding perpendicular height.
  • Does not confuse the slant side with the perpendicular height.

Prior knowledge

Students can establish and apply the area formula for a rectangle (from AC9M6M02). Students understand area as the measure of a 2D region and can use appropriate units.

Width

Minimum
Explain using a diagram why A = ½bh for a triangle, connecting to the rectangle formula.
Typical
Derive the formula using multiple triangle types (right-angled, acute, obtuse) and verify the connection to the enclosing rectangle in each case.
Wider
  • Investigate using dynamic geometry software: slide the apex of a triangle at a fixed altitude and observe that the area is constant — explain why.
  • Explore how the formula applies when the height is outside the triangle (obtuse case).

Hinge

Students who believe the formula only applies to right-angled triangles misidentify the height in Node 2 problems — applying A = ½bh with the slant side instead of the perpendicular height produces systematically wrong answers for all non-right-angled triangles.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who derive the formula only for right-angled triangles often believe the formula changes for acute or obtuse triangles. Multiple triangle types resolve this.
