Factorising monic quadratics

AC9M9A02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can factorise monic quadratic expressions x²+bx+c with integer roots by finding two integers whose product is c and sum is b.

Lessons on this site

  • Factorising quadratics

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the factor pair (two integers with the correct product and sum) for a monic quadratic
  • Writes the factorised form correctly as (x+p)(x+q)
  • Verifies the factorisation by expanding and recovering the original expression

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Generalising the expansion pattern.

Width

Minimum
Factorise one monic quadratic x²+bx+c with integer roots; show the factor pair search (find integers with product c and sum b); verify by expanding.
Typical
Factorise a range of monic quadratics with integer roots including positive, negative and zero constant terms; use systematic factor pair testing; verify each factorisation by expanding back; recognise when a quadratic does not factorise over integers.
Wider
  • Use digital tools to explore the factorisation of x²+mx+n across a systematic range of integer values of m and n — identify which combinations produce integer factor pairs and which do not
  • Connect factorisation to the null factor law and solving quadratic equations — show how the factorised form locates the x-intercepts
  • Investigate perfect square trinomials and difference of squares as special cases of factorisation

Hinge

Sign-restricted factor-pair search (testing only positive pairs) causes the majority of factorisation failures in AC9M9A04 equation solving. Students cannot solve quadratics algebraically without reliable factorisation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe factor-pair search is the central hinge: students often test only positive factor pairs even when c is positive (missing negative-negative pairs that sum to a negative b). Working across all sign combinations of b and c systematically — positive b/positive c, negative b/positive c, positive b/negative c, negative b/negative c — resolves this.
Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFactor pair search becomes fluent only through practice. Students need enough repetitions with varied b and c values to automatise the search process before the cognitive load of connecting to equation solving is added.
