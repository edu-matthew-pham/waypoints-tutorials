← AC9M8N03 Terminating and recurring decimals

Predicting termination from prime factorisation of denominator

AC9M8N03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students identify whether a fraction terminates by examining whether the prime factorisation of the denominator contains only factors of 2 and/or 5.

Lessons on this site

  • Which decimals stop?

Success criteria

  • Correctly predicts whether a fraction terminates from its denominator's prime factorisation.
  • Explains why the rule works (connection to base 10).
  • Simplifies fractions first where necessary before applying the rule.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying terminating and recurring decimals.

Width

Minimum
State the rule and apply it to determine whether a given fraction terminates, without performing the division.
Typical
Apply the rule across a range of fractions including those in non-simplest form; explain why denominators with only 2s and 5s produce terminating decimals.
Wider
  • Investigate why only factors of 2 and 5 produce terminating decimals in base 10 — connect to the structure of our number system and how this would differ in base 12.
  • Explore mixed examples where simplification first is required before applying the rule.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe connection between prime factorisation and decimal behaviour requires an explicit logical chain that students rarely construct spontaneously. A scaffold linking the denominator structure to the decimal base is needed.
