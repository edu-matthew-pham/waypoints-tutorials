← AC9M6N03 Compare and order fractions

Equivalence as the comparison tool

AC9M6N03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why equivalent fractions are needed to compare fractions with different denominators, and generate equivalent fractions for common fractions by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same factor.

Success criteria

  • Student generates three equivalent fractions for a given common fraction and explains why they are equal.
  • Student verifies equivalence using a diagram or number line representation.

Prior knowledge

Students can compare and order fractions with the same and related denominators including mixed numerals using knowledge of factors and multiples, and represent these on a number line (AC9M5N03). They have not yet compared fractions with unrelated denominators or systematically justified ordering using equivalence across a range of common fractions on the same number line.

Width

Minimum
Generate at least three equivalent fractions for each of: one-half, one-third, one-quarter; explain why multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number preserves value.
Typical
Generate equivalent fractions for a range of common fractions; use diagrams and number lines to verify equivalence; explain the equivalence principle (multiplying by 1 in fraction form); identify when two fractions are equivalent by comparing their simplest forms.
Wider
  • Investigate why different fraction names can describe the same quantity using area models, set models, and number lines simultaneously
  • Explore which fractions are equivalent to familiar decimals (0.5, 0.25, 0.75) and connect the decimal and fraction representations

Hinge

Understanding equivalence as a value-preserving operation is the conceptual prerequisite for Node 2 and Node 3. Students who apply equivalence only procedurally (without understanding why) produce incorrect numerator conversions when finding common denominators, breaking all subsequent comparison and ordering work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often generate equivalent fractions procedurally without understanding why the value is preserved. Multiple representations — diagrams, number lines, area models — help resolve the conceptual gap between the procedure and its meaning.
