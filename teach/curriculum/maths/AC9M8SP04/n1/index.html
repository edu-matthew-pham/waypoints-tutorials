Designing a congruence/similarity classification algorithm

AC9M8SP04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students design a step-by-step algorithm that correctly classifies two triangles as congruent, similar, or neither, using the appropriate conditions.

Success criteria

  • Designs an algorithm that correctly classifies triangles using at least two conditions.
  • Includes clear decision points with yes/no branches.
  • Tests the algorithm with at least two examples and corrects any errors.

Prior knowledge

Students have designed classification algorithms for shapes (from AC9M7SP04). Students know the conditions for congruence and similarity (from AC9M8SP01).

Width

Minimum
Design a flowchart that correctly classifies two triangles as congruent or not using at least two conditions (SSS and SAS).
Typical
Design and test an algorithm covering all congruence and similarity conditions; handle edge cases (e.g. insufficient information); describe how the algorithm works.
Wider
  • Evaluate two different algorithms for the same classification task — compare their efficiency and edge case handling.
  • Extend the algorithm to classify quadrilaterals as congruent or similar — identify what additional conditions are needed.

Hinge

Node 2 requires tracing and evaluating an algorithm — students who have not designed one themselves lack the understanding of decision branching and condition ordering needed to identify errors or describe logic in Node 2.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkAlgorithm design requires systematic reasoning about decision order — which condition to test first, what information is needed, and how to handle each branch. Without explicit structure students produce incomplete or ambiguous algorithms.
