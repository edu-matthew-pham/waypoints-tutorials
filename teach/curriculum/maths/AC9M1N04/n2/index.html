Bridging through 10

AC9M1N04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can add numbers within 20 by partitioning one addend to bridge through 10, using part-part-whole knowledge.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Partitions one addend correctly to make 10 with the other when adding numbers that cross 10.
  • Records the bridging chain with correct number sentences showing each step.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Counting-on and counting-back strategies.

Width

Minimum
Partition one addend to make 10 with the other, then add the remainder; record the bridging strategy as a number sentence (e.g. 7 + 5 = 7 + 3 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12).
Typical
Apply bridging through 10 to a range of additions within 20; use physical materials (ten-frames, counters) to model the partition; record the full bridging chain as a number sentence; connect the strategy to part-part-whole knowledge of combinations to 10.
Wider
  • Investigate which additions within 20 can be most efficiently solved by bridging (those that cross 10) versus those best solved by counting-on or using doubles, building a strategic repertoire.
  • Create story problems that require bridging through 10 and explain why bridging is the most efficient strategy for each.

Hinge

Bridging through 10 is a prerequisite strategy for two-digit addition in Year 2 — students who cannot apply it within 20 will lack the foundational move required when adding across decade boundaries. It is also required for Node 3 subtraction strategies that use the think-addition approach.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepBridging through 10 relies on automatic knowledge of number combinations to 10. Students who don't yet know, for example, that 7 + 3 = 10 cannot apply the bridge. Using ten-frames and part-part-whole materials in varied contexts helps students see the bridging move as a general strategy rather than a procedure to memorise.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe bridging chain (7 + 5 = 7 + 3 + 2 = 10 + 2 = 12) is a multi-step reasoning sequence. Students need the full chain made visible before they can apply it independently. Recording each step explicitly is the scaffold.
