Measure angles with protractor

AC9M5M04 · Year 5 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Estimate, construct and measure angles in degrees, using appropriate tools including a protractor, and relate these measures to angle names

Prior knowledge

Students can identify and name angle types (acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex) (AC9M4M04). They have not yet measured angles in degrees or used a protractor to construct angles.

By the end

Students estimate, construct and measure angles in degrees using a protractor, and relate measures to angle names.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using a protractor to measure angles in degrees and classifying these angles using angle names; for example, an acute angle is less than 90 90°, an obtuse angle is more than 90 90° and less than 180 180°, a right angle is equal to 90 90° and a reflex angle is more than 180 180° and less than 360 360°
  • estimating the size of angles in the environment using a clinometer and describing the angles using angle names
  • using a ruler and protractor to construct triangles, given the angle measures and side lengths
  • using a protractor to measure angles when creating a pattern or string design within a circle
  • recognising the size of angles within shapes that do and do not tesselate, measuring the angles and using the sum of angles to explain why some shapes will tesselate and other shapes do not

Waypoints

  • 1. Degrees as a unitStudents can explain that angles are measured in degrees and relate benchmark angles (90°, 180°, 360°) to angle names.
  • 2. Estimating anglesStudents can estimate angles in degrees using benchmark angles as reference points.
  • 3. Measuring with a protractorHingeStudents can correctly position and read a protractor to measure angles, including obtuse angles.
  • 4. Constructing anglesStudents can construct a given angle in degrees using a protractor.
