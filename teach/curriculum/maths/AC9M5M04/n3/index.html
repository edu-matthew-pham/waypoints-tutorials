Measuring with a protractor

AC9M5M04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can correctly position and read a protractor to measure angles, including obtuse angles.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student positions the protractor correctly on an angle
  • Student reads the correct scale and records the angle in degrees

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Estimating angles.

Width

Minimum
Measure an acute angle using a protractor, selecting the correct scale; record the measurement in degrees.
Typical
Measure acute and obtuse angles using a protractor; identify and use the correct inner/outer scale; verify measurements against estimate.
Wider
  • Measure angles in polygons and verify angle sum properties
  • Investigate digital tools (dynamic geometry software) as alternatives to physical protractors

Hinge

Correct protractor technique — specifically scale selection — is the prerequisite for Node 4 (construction). Students who read the wrong scale construct angles that are consistently wrong by the supplement amount.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsProtractor use has specific procedural requirements (correct scale, vertex alignment, zero-line placement) where errors cluster. Targeted practice with deliberate attention to each step reduces systematic error.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly use the wrong scale (read 60° as 120°). Angles in varied orientations and obtuse angles require actively choosing the correct scale — multiple examples with differing angle sizes resolve this.
