Multiply larger numbers

AC9M5N06 · Year 5 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Solve problems involving multiplication of larger numbers by one- or two-digit numbers, choosing efficient calculation strategies and using digital tools where appropriate; check the reasonableness of answers

Prior knowledge

Students can multiply and divide by powers of 10 and apply multiplication to practical problems (AC9M4N05). They have not yet used efficient written strategies for multiplying larger numbers by two-digit numbers.

By the end

Students solve problems involving multiplication of larger numbers by one- or two-digit numbers, choosing efficient strategies and checking reasonableness.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • solving multiplication problems such as 253 × 4 253×4 using a doubling strategy; for example, 2 × 253 = 506 2×253=506 and 2 × 506 = 1012 2×506=1012
  • solving multiplication problems like 15 × 16 15×16 by thinking of factors of both numbers, 15 = 3 × 5 , 16 = 2 × 8 15=3×5,16=2×8; rearranging the factors to make the calculation easier, 5 × 2 = 10 , 3 × 8 = 24 5×2=10,3×8=24 and 10 × 24 = 240 10×24=240
  • using an array to show place value partitioning to solve multiplication, such as 324 × 8 324×8, thinking 300 × 8 = 2400 , 20 × 8 = 160 , 4 × 8 = 32 300×8=2400,20×8=160,4×8=32 then adding the parts, 2400 + 160 + 32 = 2592 2400+160+32=2592 ; connecting the parts of the array to a standard written algorithm
  • using different strategies used to multiply numbers, explaining how they work and if they have any limitations; for example, discussing how the Japanese visual method for multiplication is not effective for multiplying larger numbers

Waypoints

  • 1. Mental and informal multiplication strategiesStudents can apply and explain mental strategies for multiplication including doubling, halving and place value partitioning.
  • 2. Standard written algorithm for larger numbersHingeStudents can apply a written algorithm to multiply a multi-digit number by a one-digit number, showing working.
  • 3. Two-digit multiplicationStudents can multiply a larger number by a two-digit number using a chosen strategy, explaining the process.
  • 4. Checking reasonablenessStudents can estimate the result of a multiplication and use estimation to check the reasonableness of a calculated answer.
