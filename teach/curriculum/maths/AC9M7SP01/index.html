2D representations of 3D objects

AC9M7SP01 · Year 7 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

represent objects in 2 dimensions; discuss and reason about the advantages and disadvantages of different representations

Prior knowledge

Students can compare parallel cross-sections and recognise their relationship to right prisms (from AC9M6SP01). Students have informal experience with drawings of 3D objects.

By the end

Students represent 3D objects using nets, isometric drawings, and plan/elevation views, and reason about the advantages and disadvantages of each representation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • deconstructing packaging to identify shapes and nets
  • using different nets to construct prisms and determining which nets will make a cube, rectangular prism, triangular prism or pyramid
  • using aerial views of buildings and other three-dimensional structures to visualise the footprint made by the building or structure
  • building objects by interpreting isometric and perspective drawings
  • using isometric and square grid paper to draw views of front, back, side, top and bottom of objects
  • exploring different two-dimensional representations of objects in First Nations Australians' artworks or cultural maps of Country/Place

Waypoints

  • 1. Nets of prisms and other 3D objectsStudents identify, draw and construct nets of prisms and other common 3D objects.
  • 2. Isometric and plan/elevation drawingsHingeStudents draw and interpret isometric drawings and plan/elevation views (front, back, side, top) of 3D objects.
  • 3. Advantages and disadvantages of different representationsStudents reason about when each type of 2D representation is most useful and what information each preserves or loses.
