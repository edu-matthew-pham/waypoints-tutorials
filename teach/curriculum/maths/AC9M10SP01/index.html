Deductive reasoning and geometric proof

AC9M10SP01 · Year 10 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply deductive reasoning to proofs involving shapes in the plane and use theorems to solve spatial problems

Prior knowledge

Students can establish properties of quadrilaterals using congruent triangles and solve related problems (AC9M8SP02). They have not yet applied formal deductive reasoning to construct proofs — writing logically connected sequences of statements with explicit justification — or used theorems as starting points for solving new spatial problems.

By the end

Students apply deductive reasoning to construct proofs involving shapes in the plane and use geometric theorems to solve spatial problems.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • distinguishing between a practical demonstration and a proof
  • developing proofs involving congruent triangles and angle properties using logically connected statements
  • applying relationships to deduce properties of geometric figures (e.g. base angles of isosceles triangle)
  • investigating proofs of geometric theorems and applying logical reasoning and similarity to numerical exercises
  • using dynamic geometric software to investigate shortest paths and proving path properties
  • investigating how automated theorem provers (ATP) and interactive proof assistants (IPA) allow collaborative proof construction

Waypoints

  • 1. Proof vs demonstrationHingeStudents can distinguish between a practical demonstration (showing a result is true in specific cases) and a mathematical proof (showing it must be true in all cases), and explain why the distinction matters.
  • 2. Constructing deductive proofsHingeStudents can construct a deductive proof for a geometric result, writing a sequence of logically connected statements with explicit reasons at each step.
  • 3. Using theorems to solve spatial problemsStudents can identify which geometric theorem applies to a given spatial problem and use it as a basis for solving or proving a result.
