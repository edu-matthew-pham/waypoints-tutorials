← AC9M10A03 Exponential functions

Graphical features of exponential functions

AC9M10A03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can sketch or interpret the graph of an exponential function, identifying key features including the y-intercept, asymptote, and whether it represents growth or decay.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly identifies the y-intercept and horizontal asymptote from an exponential graph or equation
  • Student states whether an exponential function represents growth or decay and connects this to the base value

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Recognising exponential relations.

Width

Minimum
Identify the y-intercept and horizontal asymptote of an exponential graph; state whether the function shows growth or decay; connect the base value to the graph's behaviour.
Typical
Describe and sketch the graphs of exponential functions y = aˣ for a > 1 (growth) and 0 < a < 1 (decay); identify y-intercept, asymptote, domain and range; use digital graphing tools to investigate the effect of varying the base and coefficient.
Wider
  • Investigate the effect of transformations (vertical stretch, vertical translation) on exponential graphs using digital tools; conjecture and test rules for how parameters affect the graph
  • Compare exponential growth curves for different bases (e.g. 2ˣ vs 3ˣ vs 10ˣ) and relate the visual rate of increase to the constant ratio in the corresponding tables

Hinge

Asymptote misconceptions block N3 — students who cannot correctly interpret the horizontal asymptote cannot move fluently between algebraic and graphical representations, because the asymptote is the key feature distinguishing the graphical form.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe horizontal asymptote concept is frequently misunderstood — students need explicit reasoning about why y → 0 but never reaches 0, and how this connects to the algebraic form. Scaffolding this reasoning prevents persistent errors in interpretation.
