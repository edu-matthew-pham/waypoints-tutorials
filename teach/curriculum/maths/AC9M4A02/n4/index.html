← AC9M4A02 Multiplication facts to 10×10

Apply to larger numbers

AC9M4A02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 4 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can extend and apply multiplication and division facts to compute with larger numbers mentally.

Success criteria

  • Students can use a known multiplication fact to solve a related computation with multiples of 10
  • Students can explain the place-value reasoning connecting the known fact to the larger problem

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Recall fluency.

Width

Minimum
Use one known fact (e.g. 6×7=42) to solve one related larger problem mentally (e.g. 6×70, 60×7); explain the place-value reasoning used.
Typical
Apply multiplication facts and place value to solve a range of mental computation problems with larger numbers (e.g. 4×30, 7×80, 560÷7); explain the strategy; select efficient strategies based on the numbers involved.
Wider
  • Investigate how knowledge of factors supports estimation for larger products (e.g. knowing 8×7=56 means 8×70≈560 and 80×70≈5600)
  • Apply fact-based strategies to multi-step problems, identifying where each step uses a known fact as a building block

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe extension from known facts to larger numbers requires explicit place-value reasoning — students need to articulate 'I know 6×7=42, and 6×70 is 10 times larger, so 420.' Without this scaffolding, students resort to repeated addition for larger multiples.
