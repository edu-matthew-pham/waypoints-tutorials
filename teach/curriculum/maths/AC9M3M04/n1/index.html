Minute hand and its relationship to hours

AC9M3M04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the minute hand relates to hours on an analog clock — one full revolution = one hour = 60 minutes.

Success criteria

  • Students can explain that one full revolution of the minute hand equals 60 minutes (one hour).
  • Students can describe where the hour hand points for a given time to the half-hour.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and use the relationship between formal units of time (AC9M3M03) and can read analog time to the quarter-hour from Y2. They have not yet read analog or digital clocks to the nearest minute, or described the relationship between the hour and minute hands at all positions.

Width

Minimum
Explain the minute hand's full circuit using a clock face: state how many minutes elapse per full revolution and connect to the hour hand's movement.
Typical
Describe the relationship between the hour and minute hands across several different times; explain how the hour hand moves as the minute hand completes revolutions.
Wider
  • Explore how the 12-hour clock requires am/pm distinction — investigating how digital clocks encode this and why it matters
  • Compare 12-hour and 24-hour clock formats, connecting to contexts like train timetables and digital devices

Hinge

Understanding that the hour hand moves continuously (not in jumps) is required for accurate time reading in Node 2 — students with the 'static hour hand' misconception will misread times near the hour boundary and produce systematic errors across all to-the-minute readings.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe inter-relationship between both hands is not intuitively obvious — the hour hand continuously moves, not in jumps. Explicit scaffolding of 'where is the hour hand when the minute hand is at 30 minutes?' prevents the common misconception that the hour hand is static.
