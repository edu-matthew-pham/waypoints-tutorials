← AC9M4A01 Unknown values in equations

Inverse relationship

AC9M4A01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 4 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use the inverse relationship between addition and subtraction to find an unknown in a number sentence.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Students can write the related subtraction sentence to solve an unknown-addend equation
  • Students can use a part-part-whole diagram to represent an unknown-addend problem and identify which part is missing

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Equation as balance.

Width

Minimum
Solve one unknown-addend problem (e.g. 27 + □ = 63) by applying subtraction as the inverse operation; explain the reasoning.
Typical
Use part-part-whole diagrams and bar models to represent and solve unknown-addend and unknown-total problems; explicitly articulate the inverse relationship (e.g. □ = 63 − 27 because subtraction undoes addition); apply to two-digit and three-digit problems.
Wider
  • Investigate how the same bar model can generate multiple related equations (a+b=c, c−a=b, c−b=a), building fluency with the full fact family
  • Solve multi-step problems where two unknowns are constrained by the same equation structure

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStudents benefit from repeated practice deriving related subtraction facts from addition facts and vice versa. The connection between the inverse operation and the unknown position needs to become automatic before applying it to larger numbers.
