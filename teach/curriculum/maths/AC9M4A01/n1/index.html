Equation as balance

AC9M4A01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 4 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that an equation means both sides have the same value, using a balance model.

Success criteria

  • Students can explain that both sides of an equation must have the same value
  • Students can identify whether a given equation is true or false and justify using a balance model

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise addition and subtraction as inverse operations and find unknown values in basic number sentences (AC9M3A01). They have not yet applied equivalence reasoning to equations with unknowns on either side, or used properties such as commutativity across multi-addend equations.

Width

Minimum
Use a balance scale or diagram to represent one equation; explain what the equals sign means in terms of balance.
Typical
Represent a range of true and false equations using balance scales and informal units; explain why 7 + 8 = 6 + 9 is true and 5 + 4 = 10 is false using balance reasoning.
Wider
  • Create families of equivalent addition sentences that have the same total, using balance reasoning to verify
  • Investigate whether equations with three addends can be balanced in different ways (e.g. 2+2+3 = 2+3+2 = 3+2+2)

Hinge

Students who read the equals sign as 'write the answer here' cannot engage with Node 4's relational thinking tasks — finding unknowns in equations of the form a + b = □ + c requires understanding equality as balance, not as a command to compute.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe equals sign is commonly misread as 'the answer comes next' rather than as a relational symbol meaning balance. Exposure to equations with expressions on both sides (e.g. 6+8 = □+10) is the resolution — single-answer-format equations reinforce the misconception.
