Counting into the hundreds

AC9M2N01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 2 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can count forward and backward through hundreds boundaries and recognise that the number sequence continues beyond 120 using the same patterns.

Success criteria

  • Counts forward and backward through a hundreds boundary without error
  • Identifies the repeating pattern in the number sequence across hundreds

Prior knowledge

Students recognise, represent and order numbers to at least 120 using physical and virtual materials, numerals and number lines (AC9M1N01). They have not yet encountered three-digit numbers or the place value structure of hundreds.

Width

Minimum
Count forward and backward through at least one hundreds boundary (e.g. 95–105, 195–205) and identify the pattern.
Typical
Count forward and backward through multiple hundreds boundaries; skip count in tens and hundreds from various starting points; locate approximate positions on a 0–1000 number line.
Wider
  • Skip count in 5s, 10s and 100s from non-zero starting points; record on an open number line and describe the pattern
  • Investigate how many tens make 100, how many hundreds make 1000; connect to skip counting patterns

Hinge

Node 2 place value work requires students to treat the hundreds position as a natural extension of the counting sequence. Students who stall or produce errors crossing boundaries (e.g. 199 → 200) cannot form correct three-digit groupings — the counting structure underlies the place value structure.
