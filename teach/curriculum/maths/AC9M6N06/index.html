Multiply and divide decimals

AC9M6N06 · Year 6 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Multiply and divide decimals by multiples of powers of 10 without a calculator, applying knowledge of place value and proficiency with multiplication facts; using estimation and rounding to check the reasonableness of answers

Prior knowledge

Students can solve multiplication of larger numbers by one- or two-digit numbers (AC9M5N06) and division with remainders (AC9M5N07). They can interpret and compare numbers with more than 2 decimal places (AC9M5N01). They have not yet systematically multiplied or divided decimals by powers of 10 or used these as place value operations.

By the end

Students can multiply and divide decimals by multiples of powers of 10 without a calculator using place value knowledge, and use estimation and rounding to check reasonableness.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying place value knowledge including that the value of the digit is 10 times smaller each time a place is moved to the right
  • applying and explaining estimation strategies in multiplicative situations involving a decimal multiplied by a two- or three-digit number
  • explaining the effect of multiplying or dividing a decimal by 10, 100, 1000 in terms of place value — not the decimal point shifting

Waypoints

  • 1. Multiplying decimals by powers of 10HingeStudents can multiply decimal numbers by 10, 100 and 1000 by shifting digits to the left, and explain the effect in terms of place value.
  • 2. Dividing decimals by powers of 10HingeStudents can divide decimal numbers by 10, 100 and 1000 by shifting digits to the right, and explain the effect in terms of place value.
  • 3. Multiplying by multiples of powers of 10Students can multiply and divide decimals by multiples of powers of 10 (e.g. 200, 0.5, 3000) by decomposing the multiplier and applying place value knowledge.
