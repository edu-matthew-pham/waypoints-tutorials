← AC9M3N01 Numbers beyond 10 000

Naming conventions for large numerals

AC9M3N01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 3 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read aloud and write in words any natural number in the ten-thousands range, applying Australian naming conventions correctly.

Success criteria

  • Students can correctly write in words any numeral in the ten-thousands range, including those with zero digits.
  • Students can correctly write the numeral when given a number in words.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Place value to ten-thousands.

Width

Minimum
Read and write in words at least five different numerals in the ten-thousands range, including at least one with a zero digit.
Typical
Read, write and say numbers in the ten-thousands fluently across varied examples; correctly handle zero as a placeholder in naming (e.g. 40 305 = 'forty thousand, three hundred and five').
Wider
  • Research how large numbers are named differently across languages and cultures (e.g. lakh system in South Asian languages vs. thousands system)
  • Explore naming conventions for numbers into the hundred-thousands, generalising the pattern

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsNaming conventions require fluency through repeated practice. Zero-placeholder cases cluster errors; targeted practice with a range of examples is more effective than broadening context.
