Numbers beyond 10 000

AC9M3N01 · Year 3 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise, represent and order natural numbers using naming and writing conventions for numerals beyond 10 000

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise, represent and order numbers to at least 1000 using physical and virtual materials, numerals and number lines (AC9M2N01). They have not yet worked with numbers beyond the thousands range, or applied naming and writing conventions to numbers in the ten-thousands and beyond.

By the end

Students recognise, represent and order natural numbers in the ten-thousands and beyond, applying correct naming and writing conventions for multi-digit numerals.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising the role of zero as a placeholder in five- and six-digit numbers
  • representing and ordering numbers to at least 10 000 on a number line
  • reading, writing and saying numbers beyond 10 000 using Australian naming conventions
  • comparing numbers up to and beyond 10 000 using the symbols <, > and =

Waypoints

  • 1. Place value to ten-thousandsHingeStudents can identify the value of each digit in a five-digit number, naming the ten-thousands place.
  • 2. Naming conventions for large numeralsStudents can read aloud and write in words any natural number in the ten-thousands range, applying Australian naming conventions correctly.
  • 3. Representing large numbers on number linesStudents can place and read numbers beyond 10 000 on a number line, making reasonable estimates of unmarked positions.
  • 4. Ordering and comparing large numbersStudents can order a set of natural numbers beyond 10 000 from smallest to largest and compare any two using < > = symbols.
