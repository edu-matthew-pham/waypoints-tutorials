← AC9M10ST02 Boxplots and continuous distributions

Constructing and interpreting boxplots

AC9M10ST02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct a boxplot from a five-number summary and interpret the key features in the context of the data.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly constructs a boxplot from a five-number summary with accurate scale and labelling
  • Student correctly identifies the median, IQR and any outliers from a given boxplot

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Five-number summary.

Width

Minimum
Draw a boxplot from a given five-number summary; identify and label the five key positions; describe what the box and whiskers represent.
Typical
Construct boxplots from raw data sets; interpret box width (IQR as spread measure), whisker length, and outlier positions; describe distributions in terms of centre and spread using boxplot features; identify outliers using the 1.5 × IQR rule; connect the visual representation to the five-number summary.
Wider
  • Investigate how the identification and handling of outliers is a critical step in machine learning — comparing statistical outlier detection criteria to how data scientists decide whether to include or exclude outliers from model training
  • Explore how boxplots are used in scientific research to compare experimental vs control group distributions and identify practically significant differences

Hinge

Students who misread boxplot features (interpreting box width as frequency, misidentifying the median position) cannot make valid distribution comparisons in N3 — the comparison task depends on correctly reading the features being compared.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents frequently misread boxplots — interpreting box width as frequency rather than spread, or misidentifying the median position. Explicit reasoning about what each feature encodes is essential before comparison work.
