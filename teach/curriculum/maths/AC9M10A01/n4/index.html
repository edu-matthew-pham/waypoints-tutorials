Factorisation of quadratic expressions

AC9M10A01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 10 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can factorise monic and non-monic quadratic expressions, including using the completed square form.

Lessons on this site

  • Factorising when x² has a number
  • Completing the square

Success criteria

  • Student correctly factorises a non-monic quadratic expression by an appropriate method
  • Student converts a monic quadratic to completed square form and explains what each part represents
  • Student explains why factorisation and expansion are inverse operations

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Expansion using the distributive property.

Width

Minimum
Factorise monic quadratic expressions (ax² + bx + c where a=1) by finding factor pairs; explain the relationship between factorisation and expansion.
Typical
Factorise monic and non-monic quadratic expressions using systematic methods (factor pairs, grouping, or completing the square); complete the square for a monic quadratic; explain the inverse relationship between factorisation and expansion.
Wider
  • Investigate the completed square form y = a(x-h)² + k as a lens for understanding the vertex form of a quadratic — connecting algebraic manipulation to graphical features
  • Explore cases where quadratic expressions cannot be factorised over the integers (discriminant negative) and discuss what this means algebraically and graphically

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents frequently treat factorisation methods as disconnected procedures. Wider contexts — including completed square form, connections to graphing, and non-factorisable cases — help students see factorisation as a coherent algebraic transformation rather than a set of tricks.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCompleting the square requires coordinating expansion, factorisation and the relationship between algebraic form and geometric meaning. This is the node where prior procedural skills integrate into a unified understanding.
