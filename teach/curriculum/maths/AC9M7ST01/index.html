Data acquisition and measures of centre

AC9M7ST01 · Year 7 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

acquire data sets for discrete and continuous numerical variables and calculate the range, median, mean and mode; make and justify decisions about which measures of central tendency provide useful insights into the nature of the distribution of data

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret and compare distributions using mode and range (from AC9M6ST01). Students have planned and conducted statistical investigations (from AC9M6ST03).

By the end

Students acquire data sets, calculate range, median, mean and mode, and justify which measure best describes a given distribution.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • understanding that summarising data by calculating measures of centre can help make sense of the data, commenting on skewness or symmetry of data
  • comparing the mean, median, mode and range of displays of data from a given context, and explaining how outliers may affect summary statistics
  • recognising how different data sets can have the same measures of central tendency and experimenting with how varying data affects these measures
  • acquire continuous numerical data by taking measurement samples during a science experiment, observation or field study
  • exploring how descriptive statistics are used by artificial intelligence developers to summarise and analyse data

Waypoints

  • 1. Distinguishing discrete and continuous numerical variablesStudents distinguish discrete from continuous numerical variables and understand how this affects data acquisition.
  • 2. Calculating range, median, mean and modeHingeStudents calculate all four summary statistics for a given data set.
  • 3. Justifying which measure is most usefulStudents reason about which measure of central tendency best describes a given distribution, considering outliers, skewness, and the purpose of the summary.
