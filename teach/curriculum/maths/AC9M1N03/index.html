Quantify sets by skip counting

AC9M1N03 · Year 1 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Quantify sets of objects, to at least 120, by partitioning collections into equal groups using number knowledge and skip counting

Prior knowledge

Students can quantify and compare collections to at least 20 using counting (AC9MFN03). They can recognise and continue skip counting sequences by twos, fives and tens (AC9M1A01). They have not yet applied skip counting systematically to count large collections beyond 20 by grouping into equal groups.

By the end

Students quantify sets of objects, to at least 120, by partitioning collections into equal groups using number knowledge and skip counting.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • counting a large collection of items using groups of fives or tens and skip counting to work out how many there are; recording the amount and connecting the digits in the number to the grouped materials when using groups of 10
  • counting collections of objects such as pencils or images of birds in a tree, by grouping them in tens to enable efficient counting and connecting the digits in the number to the groups of tens and ones
  • counting a large collection of Australian $1 coins by stacking them into piles of 10, skip counting in tens and including any leftover coins to determine the total value

Waypoints

  • 1. Group into equal setsStudents can partition a collection of objects into equal groups of a chosen size, explaining why grouping makes counting more efficient.
  • 2. Skip count the groupsHingeStudents can skip count a grouped collection to find the total and connect the count to the digit representation of the total.
  • 3. Connect groups to digitsStudents can connect the number of tens groups and ones remainder in a counted collection to the digits in the written numeral.
