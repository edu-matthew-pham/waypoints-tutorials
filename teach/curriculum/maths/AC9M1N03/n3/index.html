← AC9M1N03 Quantify sets by skip counting

Connect groups to digits

AC9M1N03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can connect the number of tens groups and ones remainder in a counted collection to the digits in the written numeral.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • After counting a collection by grouping in tens, states the number of tens and ones correctly.
  • Writes the numeral for the total and explains how the tens digit and ones digit relate to the physical groups.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Skip count the groups.

Width

Minimum
After counting a collection by tens, state the number of tens groups and any remaining ones; connect these to the digits in the written numeral.
Typical
Count collections of at least 120 objects by grouping in tens and ones; record the total; explicitly connect the number of tens to the tens digit and the number of ones to the ones digit in the numeral; explain the connection in words.
Wider
  • Count a very large collection (200+ objects) by grouping into hundreds, tens and ones, introducing the idea of three-digit numbers as a natural extension of the grouping strategy.
  • Record the counting process using a simple place-value chart, reinforcing the connection between the physical groups and the numeral structure.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only count by tens with pre-grouped materials (e.g. MAB blocks) sometimes fail to generalise to ungrouped real-world collections. Counting genuinely ungrouped collections (pencils, seeds, tiles) across different contexts strengthens the connection between the grouping act and the numeral produced.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node is where grouping, skip counting, and place-value numeral reading come together as a coordinated skill. The three components all need to operate simultaneously — this is the integration point for the descriptor.
