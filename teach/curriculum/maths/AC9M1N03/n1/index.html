Group into equal sets

AC9M1N03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can partition a collection of objects into equal groups of a chosen size, explaining why grouping makes counting more efficient.

Success criteria

  • Partitions a collection into equal groups of a stated size with no objects left over from the grouping phase.
  • Explains why grouping makes counting more efficient than counting one by one.

Prior knowledge

Students can quantify and compare collections to at least 20 using counting (AC9MFN03). They can recognise and continue skip counting sequences by twos, fives and tens (AC9M1A01). They have not yet applied skip counting systematically to count large collections beyond 20 by grouping into equal groups.

Width

Minimum
Group a collection of 20–40 objects into equal groups of 5 or 10; explain why grouping is helpful.
Typical
Group collections of various sizes into equal groups of 2, 5 or 10; compare different grouping sizes for the same collection; explain the efficiency gained by grouping rather than counting one by one.
Wider
  • Investigate how grouping strategies are used in practical contexts — packaging, stacking, bundling — and discuss what group size is most efficient for different purposes.
  • Explore grouping practices used historically and in First Nations contexts, comparing the choice of group size to the decimal (base-10) grouping used in the standard system.
