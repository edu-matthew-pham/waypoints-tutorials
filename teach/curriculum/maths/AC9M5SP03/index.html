Transformations of shapes

AC9M5SP03 · Year 5 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Describe and perform translations, reflections and rotations of shapes, using dynamic geometric software where appropriate; recognise what changes and what remains the same, and identify any symmetries

Prior knowledge

Students can identify and describe line and rotational symmetry (AC9M4SP03). They have not yet performed and described all three transformation types or systematically analysed what is preserved under each.

By the end

Students describe and perform translations, reflections and rotations; recognise what changes and what remains the same; identify any symmetries.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • understanding and explaining that translations, rotations and reflections can change the position and orientation of a shape but not the shape or size
  • using pattern blocks and paper tracing around a shape and conducting a series of a one-step transformations; continuing to trace each resulting image, then copying the original position and end position on a new sheet of paper
  • demonstrating how different combinations of transformations can produce the same resulting image
  • challenging classmates to select a combination of transformations to move from an original image to the final image, noting the different combinations by using different colours to trace images
  • investigating how animal tracks can be interpreted by First Nations Australians using the transformation of their shapes to help determine and understand animal behaviour

Waypoints

  • 1. TranslationStudents can describe and perform a translation and explain what properties are preserved.
  • 2. ReflectionStudents can describe and perform a reflection across a given mirror line and identify the line of symmetry.
  • 3. RotationStudents can describe and perform a rotation about a point, specifying the centre, angle and direction.
  • 4. What changes and what stays the sameStudents can compare the three transformations and identify which properties are preserved and which change under each.
