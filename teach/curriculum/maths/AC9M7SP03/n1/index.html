Translations using coordinates

AC9M7SP03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Maths · Space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students translate a set of points on the Cartesian plane and describe the translation using coordinate changes.

Success criteria

  • Correctly translates a set of points and records the new coordinates.
  • Describes the translation in terms of horizontal and vertical movement.
  • Verifies that the shape is congruent to the original after translation.

Prior knowledge

Students can plot and read coordinates in all four quadrants of the Cartesian plane (from AC9M6SP02). Students have experienced combinations of transformations to create patterns (from AC9M6SP03).

Width

Minimum
Translate a simple shape on the Cartesian plane and describe the translation as 'x units right/left, y units up/down'.
Typical
Perform and describe translations of sets of points in all directions; verify that shape and size are preserved.
Wider
  • Investigate how translations can be described as vector addition: (x, y) → (x+a, y+b).
  • Explore combinations of two translations — is the order of translation important?

Hinge

Fluency with coordinate reading and recording is prerequisite to Nodes 2 and 3 — students who make coordinate errors in translation produce wrong starting points for reflection and rotation, and cannot verify that transformations preserve shape and size.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTranslation using coordinate notation is foundational to reflections and rotations. Students need fluency with coordinate changes before layer on more complex transformations.
