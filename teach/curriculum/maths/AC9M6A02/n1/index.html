Order of operations including brackets

AC9M6A02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate numerical expressions involving brackets and combinations of operations, applying order-of-operations conventions correctly.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly evaluates expressions involving brackets and mixed operations.
  • Student explains why bracket placement changes the result of an expression.

Prior knowledge

Students can find unknown values in numerical equations involving multiplication and division using properties of numbers and operations (AC9M5A02). They understand inverse operations. They have not yet worked with equations involving brackets or combined operations requiring order-of-operations reasoning.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least five expressions involving brackets and at least two different operations; explain why brackets take priority over other operations.
Typical
Evaluate expressions with brackets, powers (where introduced), and combinations of the four operations using the correct order; verify that the same expression gives different results if bracket placement changes; identify errors in given worked examples.
Wider
  • Investigate the BODMAS/BIDMAS convention history — why was a convention needed and what problems arise without it?
  • Explore calculator behaviour with and without brackets and explain any discrepancies

Hinge

Correct order-of-operations evaluation is the prerequisite for Node 2. Students who misapply bracket priority will apply inverse operations in the wrong sequence when solving for unknowns, producing systematic errors that cannot be self-corrected.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly over-apply left-to-right evaluation or misplace the priority of brackets. Varied expression types — brackets nested, brackets with subtraction, expressions with both addition and multiplication — expose different manifestations of this misconception.
Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsOrder of operations requires procedural precision that underpins all equation work with combined operations. Errors here cascade into every subsequent equation-solving task.
