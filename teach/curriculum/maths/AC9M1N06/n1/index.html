Distinguish sharing from grouping

AC9M1N06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify whether a practical situation involves equal sharing (distributing a total among a known number of recipients) or equal grouping (making groups of a known size).

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies whether a described situation involves sharing or grouping.
  • States what is known and what needs to be found in each situation type.

Prior knowledge

Students can represent practical equal sharing and grouping situations using materials (AC9MFN06). They can add and subtract within 20 using calculation strategies (AC9M1N04). They have not yet modelled sharing and grouping using structured representations (diagrams, Think Boards) and calculation strategies, or connected equal grouping to the idea of repeated equal quantities.

Width

Minimum
Describe one sharing situation and one grouping situation; explain what is known and what is unknown in each.
Typical
Identify and distinguish equal sharing and equal grouping situations from a variety of practical contexts; explain the known and unknown quantities in each type; use materials to model the action in each case.
Wider
  • Compare sharing and grouping situations with the same numbers (e.g. 12 objects shared among 3 vs 12 objects in groups of 3) — identifying what stays the same and what is different in each.
  • Explore sharing and grouping contexts from everyday community and school life, including contexts drawn from First Nations community practices.

Hinge

Confusing sharing and grouping structures causes students to misidentify what is known and unknown in Node 2 and Node 3 — they model the wrong situation and apply the wrong solution strategy as a result.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents frequently confuse sharing and grouping because both involve equal quantities. Varied practical contexts help students focus on the distinction — what is fixed (total, number of groups, or group size) — rather than recognising only one type of situation.
