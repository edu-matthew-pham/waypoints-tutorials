Model sharing and grouping

AC9M1N06 · Year 1 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving equal sharing and grouping; represent the situations with diagrams, physical and virtual materials, and use calculation strategies to solve the problem

Prior knowledge

Students can represent practical equal sharing and grouping situations using materials (AC9MFN06). They can add and subtract within 20 using calculation strategies (AC9M1N04). They have not yet modelled sharing and grouping using structured representations (diagrams, Think Boards) and calculation strategies, or connected equal grouping to the idea of repeated equal quantities.

By the end

Students use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving equal sharing and grouping; represent the situations with diagrams, physical and virtual materials, and use calculation strategies to solve the problem.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • modelling problems involving repeated equal group situations, such as, 'How many wheels are needed for 3 cars?', using materials and drawing a picture to show what they did
  • modelling practical problems involving equal sharing situations; for example, sharing a set of dominoes between the 2 players in a game
  • modelling money problems involving equal sharing; for example, sorting coins from a money box according to their denominations, sharing the coins equally between 4 people

Waypoints

  • 1. Distinguish sharing from groupingHingeStudents can identify whether a practical situation involves equal sharing (distributing a total among a known number of recipients) or equal grouping (making groups of a known size).
  • 2. Represent with materials and diagramsHingeStudents can model an equal sharing or grouping situation using physical materials, a drawing and a number recording, explaining the connections between each.
  • 3. Solve and communicate equal-groups problemsStudents can apply a calculation strategy to solve equal sharing and grouping problems and communicate the solution in terms of the problem context.
