Converting to and from scientific notation

AC9M9M02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can convert numbers between decimal form and scientific notation in both directions.

Lessons on this site

  • Scientific notation

Success criteria

  • Correctly converts at least two large numbers and two small numbers to scientific notation
  • Correctly converts at least two scientific notation values back to decimal form
  • Uses a coefficient between 1 and 10 in every scientific notation expression

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and use rates to solve problems involving the comparison of related quantities (AC9M8M05). They have not yet represented very large or very small quantities in scientific notation or performed calculations with values in scientific notation.

Width

Minimum
Convert two numbers to scientific notation (one large, one small) and convert two numbers from scientific notation to decimal form; verify by checking the position of the decimal point relative to the power of 10.
Typical
Convert a range of very large and very small numbers between decimal and scientific notation; explain why scientific notation requires a coefficient between 1 and 10; connect the exponent to place value and decimal point movement.
Wider
  • Investigate examples from science and engineering (geological time scales, molecular masses, astronomical distances) — practise conversion in context
  • Explore how calculators display numbers in scientific notation and what limits cause the switch from standard to scientific display
  • Connect scientific notation to SI unit prefixes (nano-, micro-, milli-, kilo-, mega-, giga-) and convert between prefix notation and scientific notation

Hinge

Exponent sign errors on very small numbers (0.0004 → 4×10⁴ instead of 4×10⁻⁴) corrupt all calculations in N2 and applied contexts in N3. Reliable bidirectional conversion is the mechanical prerequisite for all subsequent work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStudents commonly err on the sign of the exponent for small numbers (writing 0.0004 as 4×10⁴ instead of 4×10⁻⁴). This is a precision-required mechanical skill that needs fluency before applications are introduced — repeated practice with both directions, especially for very small numbers, is the remedy.
