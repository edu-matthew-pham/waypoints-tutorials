Dollar and cent relationship

AC9M3M06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can state that 100 cents = $1 and use this to convert between dollar and cent representations.

Success criteria

  • Students can convert a cents amount to dollars and cents (e.g. 375¢ = $3.75).
  • Students can convert a dollar amount to cents (e.g. $4.20 = 420¢).

Prior knowledge

No direct predecessor identified in the progression map at Y3. Students have used money in additive modelling contexts at Y2 (AC9M2N06) but have not yet formally studied the dollar-cent relationship or represented money values in multiple ways.

Width

Minimum
Convert three money amounts between cents and dollars; explain the relationship (100 cents = $1).
Typical
Convert money amounts between cents and dollars in both directions; explain the place value connection — dollars are units, cents are hundredths.
Wider
  • Investigate how different countries use different currencies and coin systems, comparing structures
  • Connect the dollar-cent relationship to the decimal place value system — setting up understanding of decimal notation for money amounts

Hinge

The dollar-cent conversion with place value grounding is required for Node 2 and for AC9M3N06 financial modelling tasks — students who have only a mechanical rule (not a place value understanding) produce systematic errors in mixed notation and in making-change calculations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe 100-based relationship between dollars and cents requires explicit connection to place value — students who understand this as a conversion rule without place value grounding often make errors in mixed notation (e.g. writing $1.50 as $1.5 or $150).
