← AC9M8M06 Pythagoras' theorem

Finding unknown side lengths using Pythagoras

AC9M8M06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students apply Pythagoras' theorem to find the hypotenuse or a shorter side in right-angled triangles, including in practical and coordinate geometry contexts.

Lessons on this site

  • Finding a side

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the hypotenuse in a given right-angled triangle.
  • Finds an unknown side correctly using the theorem.
  • Applies the theorem to a practical problem context.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding and stating Pythagoras' theorem.

Width

Minimum
Find the hypotenuse and find a shorter side given the other two sides for at least two examples.
Typical
Apply the theorem to varied practical problems; identify right-angled triangles within diagrams; apply to distance between two points on the Cartesian plane.
Wider
  • Apply the theorem to 3D problems: find the diagonal of a rectangular prism using the theorem twice.
  • Investigate how Pythagoras' theorem can determine distance between two points on a Cartesian plane — connect to applications in predictive text and mapping.

Hinge

Node 3 triangle classification requires comparing a² + b² with c² — students who cannot fluently apply the theorem to find unknown sides (and who misidentify c as the longest side) apply the classification test to the wrong quantities.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApplying Pythagoras requires first identifying the right-angled triangle within a diagram. Students who only practise decontextualised calculations fail to identify the relevant triangle in applied problems.
