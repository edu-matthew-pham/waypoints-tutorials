Reading timetables

AC9M6M03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read and extract information from a multi-row timetable, including departure and arrival times, duration, and frequency of services.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly reads departure and arrival times from a timetable.
  • Student calculates the duration of a journey from timetable information.

Prior knowledge

Students can compare 12- and 24-hour time systems and solve conversion problems (AC9M5M03). They can read and interpret time in both 12- and 24-hour formats. They have not yet applied time reading skills to multi-row timetables or planned itineraries requiring calculation of durations across multiple legs.

Width

Minimum
Read departure and arrival times from a provided timetable for at least three services; identify the duration of at least two journeys; identify the next available service after a given time.
Typical
Extract a range of information from timetables in both 12- and 24-hour formats; calculate journey durations including those that cross the hour; read timetables with multiple routes or services; identify connections between services.
Wider
  • Compare timetables across different transport types (bus, train, ferry) and identify how each is structured and what information each prioritises
  • Investigate how timetables are designed to be read quickly — what conventions make them usable, and what would make them confusing?
