Calculating duration across time periods

AC9M6M03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can calculate the duration of events and journeys, including those spanning hours, days, or involving 12-/24-hour format conversions.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly calculates the duration of a time interval, including one that crosses a time boundary (e.g. midday or midnight).
  • Student shows a strategy for the calculation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Reading timetables.

Width

Minimum
Calculate the duration of at least four time intervals, including at least one that crosses midday or midnight; show working for each.
Typical
Calculate durations using counting-on, subtraction, and number line strategies; convert between 12- and 24-hour format as needed for calculation; determine elapsed time in hours and minutes; apply to checking timetable information.
Wider
  • Investigate duration calculations across time zones — if a flight leaves Sydney at 7:30 pm AEDT and lands in Tokyo 9 hours 45 minutes later, what is the Tokyo arrival time?
  • Explore historical calendars and timekeeping challenges — how were durations calculated before standardised time zones?

Hinge

Reliable duration calculation at time boundaries (midday/midnight crossings) is the prerequisite for Node 3 itinerary planning. Students who make errors at boundaries will plan infeasible itineraries and cannot verify whether connection times are adequate.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDuration calculation is error-prone at boundaries — crossing midday/midnight, dealing with non-decimal time (60 minutes in an hour). Fluency requires targeted practice with these boundary cases rather than routine whole-hour calculations.
