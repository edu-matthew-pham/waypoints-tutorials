← AC9M5A01 Multiplication and division as inverses

Inverse relationship

AC9M5A01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that multiplication and division are inverse operations using materials or arrays.

Success criteria

  • Student demonstrates the inverse relationship using a representation
  • Student explains the connection using their own words

Prior knowledge

Students can find unknown values in equations involving addition and subtraction using inverse reasoning (AC9M4A01). They have not yet applied inverse reasoning to multiplication and division or generated families of related facts.

Width

Minimum
Use an array or diagram to show that a multiplication and its corresponding division use the same three numbers; explain the inverse relationship.
Typical
Demonstrate the inverse relationship for multiple examples using arrays, materials and number sentences; explain why the relationship holds.
Wider
  • Connect to fact family structure: show all four related facts for an array
  • Use the inverse relationship to solve missing-number problems

Hinge

Understanding the inverse relationship is the prerequisite for Node 2 — students who see multiplication and division as unrelated operations cannot generate fact families or apply inverse reasoning in calculation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often treat multiplication and division as completely separate operations. Multiple representations (arrays, materials, number sentences) help build the generalised understanding that they are connected.
