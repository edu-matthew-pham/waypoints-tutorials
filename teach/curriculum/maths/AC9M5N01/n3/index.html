← AC9M5N01 Decimals beyond hundredths

Comparing and ordering decimals

AC9M5N01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare and order sets of decimals with more than 2 decimal places, including decimals greater than one, justifying their reasoning using place value.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly orders a set of decimals with different numbers of decimal places
  • Student identifies and explains the error in an incorrect ordering (e.g. 0.35 > 0.9)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Thousandths and beyond.

Width

Minimum
Compare two decimals with up to 3 decimal places and place them in order, justifying using place value column comparison.
Typical
Order sets of 4-6 decimals with mixed decimal places (e.g. 1.3, 0.27, 1.039, 0.3); explain common ordering errors (e.g. treating longer decimals as larger).
Wider
  • Compare decimals arising from measurement contexts with different units requiring conversion
  • Sort sporting or scientific data (e.g. race times, pH values) where ordering decimals is purposeful
  • Explore how different representations (number line, place value chart) highlight different aspects of comparison

Hinge

Accurate decimal comparison is the prerequisite for number line placement in Node 4 — students who cannot correctly order decimals cannot meaningfully position them on a number line.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe 'longer decimal = bigger number' misconception is persistent. Varied contexts expose the misconception from multiple angles — students who see only abstract lists may retain the error even after instruction.
Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsComparison requires consistent application of the place value alignment procedure. Students benefit from practising the alignment step explicitly before it becomes fluent.
