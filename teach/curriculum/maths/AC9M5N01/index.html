Decimals beyond hundredths

AC9M5N01 · Year 5 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Interpret, compare and order numbers with more than 2 decimal places, including numbers greater than one, using place value understanding; represent these on a number line

Prior knowledge

Students can represent and compare decimals to hundredths on a number line (AC9M4N01). They have not yet interpreted thousandths or beyond, or compared decimals greater than one requiring more than 2 decimal places.

By the end

Students interpret, compare and order numbers with more than 2 decimal places, including numbers greater than one, using place value understanding, and represent these on a number line.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • making models of decimals including tenths, hundredths and thousandths by subdividing materials or grids, and explaining the multiplicative relationship between consecutive places; for example, thousandths are 10 10 times smaller than hundredths; writing numbers into a place value chart to compare and order them
  • renaming decimals to assist with mental computation; for example, when asked to solve 0.6 ÷ 10 0.6÷10 they rename 6 6 tenths as 60 60 hundredths and say, “if I divide 60 60 hundredths by 10 10, I get 6 6 hundredths” and write 0.6 ÷ 10 = 0.06 0.6÷10=0.06
  • using a number line or number track to represent and locate decimals with varying numbers of decimal places and numbers greater than one and justifying the placement; for example, 2.335 2.335 is halfway between 2.33 2.33 and 2.34 2.34; that is, 2.33 < 2.335 < 2.34 2.33<2.335<2.34 and 5.283 5.283 is between 5.28 5.28 and 5.29 5.29, but closer to 5.28 5.28
  • interpreting and comparing the digits in decimal measures; for example, the length or mass of animals or plants, such as a baby echidna weighing 1.78 1.78 kilograms and a platypus weighing 1.708 1.708 kilograms
  • interpreting plans or diagrams showing length measures as decimals, placing the numbers into a decimal place value chart to connect the digits to their value

Waypoints

  • 1. Tenths and hundredths reviewStudents can name the place value of each digit in a decimal to hundredths and explain what each digit represents.
  • 2. Thousandths and beyondHingeStudents can read, write and name decimals with more than 2 decimal places, explaining the place value of each digit.
  • 3. Comparing and ordering decimalsHingeStudents can compare and order sets of decimals with more than 2 decimal places, including decimals greater than one, justifying their reasoning using place value.
  • 4. Representing on a number lineStudents can place decimals with more than 2 decimal places, including numbers greater than one, accurately on a number line.
