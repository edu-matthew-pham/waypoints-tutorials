← AC9M8M04 Duration and time zones

Solving multi-zone scheduling and itinerary problems

AC9M8M04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students solve practical problems involving scheduling across multiple time zones, including travel itineraries and virtual meeting planning.

Lessons on this site

  • Time zones

Success criteria

  • Correctly plans a meeting or itinerary accounting for all time zone offsets.
  • Identifies and resolves day-change issues.
  • Communicates the solution clearly showing local times in each zone.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Converting between time zones.

Width

Minimum
Plan a meeting time that works for two people in different time zones; show the time in both zones.
Typical
Plan an international travel itinerary across multiple time zones; calculate local arrival and departure times; identify scheduling challenges for a multi-national team.
Wider
  • Plan an international travel itinerary across at least three time zones in Asia; calculate all local times; identify the best transit options.
  • Investigate the practical challenge of regular virtual meetings for a company with staff in different Australian states and an international office — propose a fair meeting time.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierMulti-zone scheduling requires coordinating UTC offsets, duration calculation, date change reasoning, and 12/24-hour conversion simultaneously — a genuine integration of all time concepts.
