← AC9M3A02 Mental strategies from known facts

Choosing efficient mental strategies

AC9M3A02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select and apply an efficient mental strategy (bridging, partitioning, compensation) for a given computation with larger numbers.

Success criteria

  • Students can calculate a two- or three-digit addition or subtraction mentally using at least one named strategy.
  • Students can name the strategy they used and explain why they chose it.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Extending known facts to larger numbers.

Width

Minimum
Calculate three two-digit additions mentally using a named strategy; explain the strategy chosen for each.
Typical
Apply bridging through tens, partitioning and compensation strategies across a range of additions and subtractions; choose the most efficient strategy for each.
Wider
  • Compare two strategies applied to the same problem to determine which is more efficient and why (e.g. bridging vs compensation for 78+24)
  • Investigate when compensation produces an estimate rather than an exact answer, and when it produces an exact result

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsMental strategy fluency is built through practice. Once students know the strategies, repeated application in varied contexts is what makes them genuinely 'mental' rather than laboured.
Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyThe descriptor calls for a repertoire of strategies. Students need enough practice with each strategy (bridging, partitioning, compensation) to have genuine choice.
