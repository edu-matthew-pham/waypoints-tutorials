← AC9M3A02 Mental strategies from known facts

Extending known facts to larger numbers

AC9M3A02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a known fact (e.g. 6+7=13) to derive related calculations (e.g. 16+7=23, 60+70=130) by recognising the place value extension.

Success criteria

  • Students can state a related larger-number calculation from a given basic fact and explain the connection.
  • Students can use a known addition fact to derive a larger-number subtraction fact.

Prior knowledge

Students can recall and demonstrate proficiency with addition facts to 20 and related subtraction facts (AC9M2A02). They have not yet applied these facts systematically as a foundation for mental computation with larger numbers.

Width

Minimum
Given three known facts, generate two related larger-number calculations for each and explain the connection.
Typical
Apply the extension pattern across a range of addition and subtraction facts to 20; derive mental calculation results for two- and three-digit extensions.
Wider
  • Explore how the extension principle works for subtraction facts as well — connecting 13-7=6 to 130-70=60, 1300-700=600
  • Investigate how doubles facts (6+6, 7+7) extend to particularly efficient mental strategies for near-doubles in large numbers

Hinge

Recognising the place value extension from basic facts is the conceptual engine behind all mental strategies in Node 2 — students who do not see why facts extend cannot select appropriate strategies (bridging, partitioning, compensation all rely on this principle) and will revert to counting-based approaches with larger numbers.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who know 6+7=13 often cannot see why 16+7=23 without re-counting. Seeing the extension pattern across multiple fact families — and across both addition and subtraction — resolves this.
