Approximations of real numbers

AC9M10N01 · Year 10 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise the effect of using approximations of real numbers in repeated calculations and compare the results when using exact representations

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise the real number system including rational and irrational numbers and solve problems (AC9M9N01). They have not yet investigated how approximation errors accumulate and compound across repeated calculations, or compared results from exact versus approximate representations.

By the end

Students recognise the effect of using approximations of real numbers in repeated calculations and compare results with those obtained using exact representations.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing and contrasting the effect of truncation or rounding on the final result of calculations when using approximations rather than exact representations
  • investigating the impact of approximation on multiple calculations in contexts involving compound shapes, surface area/volume of compound objects, and repeated simple interest calculations

Waypoints

  • 1. Exact vs approximate representationsStudents can distinguish between an exact representation of a real number and an approximation, and identify when approximation introduces error.
  • 2. Truncation vs roundingHingeStudents can describe the difference between truncation and rounding and identify which produces a systematic bias.
  • 3. Effect of approximation in repeated calculationsStudents can demonstrate, using a worked example, that repeated use of an approximate value produces a different final result than using the exact representation, and describe the nature of the accumulation.
