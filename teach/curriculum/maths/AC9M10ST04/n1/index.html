Constructing two-way tables

AC9M10ST04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can organise categorical bivariate data into a two-way table with correct row and column structure.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly constructs a two-way table with accurate cell counts and marginal totals
  • Student labels rows and columns to correctly identify which variable is in which dimension

Prior knowledge

Students can construct scatterplots and describe association in terms of strength, direction and linearity (AC9M10ST03). They have not yet used two-way tables specifically to investigate relationships between categorical variables — this extends bivariate analysis from numerical to categorical data.

Width

Minimum
Given a data set with two categorical variables, construct a two-way frequency table with appropriate row and column labels and correct cell counts.
Typical
Construct two-way tables from raw data for multiple context types (survey data, observational data, experimental data); calculate row totals, column totals and grand total; verify accuracy by checking that margins sum correctly.
Wider
  • Investigate a litter survey context — collect data on two categorical variables (e.g. type of litter and day of week) and construct a two-way table, explaining what the row and column structure reveals
  • Explore how two-way frequency tables are used in medical research and public health to organise data from observational studies

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTally and tabulation errors (double-counting, miscounting) are common. Fluency with accurate data organisation needs practice before proportional analysis.
