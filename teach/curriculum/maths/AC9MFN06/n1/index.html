← AC9MFN06 Equal sharing and grouping

The idea of equal

AC9MFN06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify when a sharing situation is equal and when it is not, and explain what needs to happen to make it equal.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student distributes a collection into equal groups and confirms each group has the same amount using counting or subitising
  • Student identifies an unequal distribution and adjusts it to make groups equal

Prior knowledge

Students can quantify collections using counting and have experience with part-part-whole relationships to 10 (AC9MFN04). They have not yet applied the idea of equal groups — sharing so that each person gets the same amount, or forming groups of the same size.

Width

Minimum
Distribute a small collection of objects between two groups and state whether the groups are equal, adjusting if they are not.
Typical
Identify and create equal and unequal distributions; explain what makes sharing equal ('everyone has the same amount'); compare two groups using counting or subitising to check for equality.
Wider
  • Explore equal sharing in familiar everyday contexts (fruit at snack time, cards dealt in a game) and discuss what equality means in each context
  • Investigate situations where objects cannot be shared equally (leftovers) — discussing what happens to the remainder at Foundation level

Hinge

Nodes 2 and 3 both require students to verify equality of groups — which depends on understanding that equal means same-amount-each, not just distributed. Students who conflate shared with equal cannot confirm their distributions are valid or detect when grouping has produced unequal groups.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly conflate 'shared' with 'equal' — just distributing objects satisfies sharing in their view. Encountering varied sharing situations where equality is verified by checking each group helps students develop the precision of equal as same-amount-each.
