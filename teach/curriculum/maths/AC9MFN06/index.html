Equal sharing and grouping

AC9MFN06 · Foundation Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

represent practical situations that involve equal sharing and grouping with physical and virtual materials and use counting or subitising strategies

Prior knowledge

Students can quantify collections using counting and have experience with part-part-whole relationships to 10 (AC9MFN04). They have not yet applied the idea of equal groups — sharing so that each person gets the same amount, or forming groups of the same size.

By the end

Students represent practical situations involving equal sharing and grouping using materials, and use counting or subitising to determine the size of equal groups.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using materials to role-play equal sharing; for example, sharing pieces of fruit or a bunch of grapes between 4 people
  • representing situations that involve counting several items and sharing them equally between 3 people by subitising or counting each group by ones
  • exploring instructive games of First Nations Australians that involve sharing; for example, playing Yangamini of the Tiwi Peoples of Bathurst Island

Waypoints

  • 1. The idea of equalHingeStudents can identify when a sharing situation is equal and when it is not, and explain what needs to happen to make it equal.
  • 2. Representing equal sharing situationsHingeStudents can represent a practical equal sharing situation using materials and find how many each person receives using counting or subitising.
  • 3. Grouping contextsStudents can represent a practical grouping situation using materials, forming equal groups and counting or subitising to find the number in each group.
