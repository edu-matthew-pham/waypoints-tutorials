← AC9M8M02 Volume and capacity of right prisms

Unit relationships between volume and capacity

AC9M8M02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students understand and apply the relationships 1 cm³ = 1 mL, 1000 cm³ = 1 L, 1 m³ = 1000 L, and use these to convert between volume and capacity units.

Lessons on this site

  • Volume and capacity

Success criteria

  • Correctly converts between cm³, mL, L and m³.
  • Selects appropriate units for a given practical context.
  • Does not confuse cm³ and cm (volume vs length).

Prior knowledge

Students can calculate volume of rectangular and triangular prisms using V = Ah (from AC9M7M02). Students understand unit conversion between cubic units and litres.

Width

Minimum
Convert between cm³ and mL, and between m³ and L, for given volumes.
Typical
Apply unit conversion in practical problem contexts; choose appropriate units for the context; explain the relationship between cubic centimetres and millilitres.
Wider
  • Investigate how unit choice affects the numerical value of a measurement — when is it more practical to use litres vs cubic metres in real contexts?
  • Explore the volume of a cubic metre using 1000 × 1000 mL = 1000 L as a concrete model — connect to large-scale water storage contexts.

Hinge

Unit conversion errors propagate directly into Node 2 — students who confuse cm³ and mL or m³ and L produce answers in the wrong unit or off by a factor of 1000 in practical capacity problems.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsUnit conversion between volume and capacity is a precision skill that students frequently get wrong under pressure. Fluency here is prerequisite to problem-solving.
