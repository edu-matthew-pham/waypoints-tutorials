Equivalence and simplification of fractions

AC9M7N04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students find equivalent fractions using common multiples and express fractions in simplest form using common divisors.

Lessons on this site

  • Equivalent fractions

Success criteria

  • Generates multiple equivalent fractions for a given fraction.
  • Correctly simplifies a fraction to lowest terms.
  • Can explain why the fractions are equivalent (multiplying/dividing numerator and denominator by the same value).

Prior knowledge

Students can compare and order fractions using equivalence, add and subtract fractions, and convert between simple fractions and decimals (from AC9M6N03, AC9M6N04, AC9M6N07). Students understand percentage as parts per hundred.

Width

Minimum
Find two equivalent fractions for a given fraction and simplify a fraction to its lowest terms.
Typical
Use the fraction wall and number line to verify equivalence; apply simplification in computation contexts.
Wider
  • Investigate what the simplest form of a fraction tells you about the relationship between numerator and denominator (coprime numbers).
  • Connect simplification to GCD from AC9M7N02: simplest form means GCD(numerator, denominator) = 1.

Hinge

Simplification errors propagate into every subsequent conversion and operation with rational numbers — Node 2 conversions, Node 3 number line placement, and all of AC9M7N06 depend on fractions being in comparable forms.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFraction equivalence and simplification underpin every subsequent operation with rational numbers. Precision here matters — small errors in simplification compound.
