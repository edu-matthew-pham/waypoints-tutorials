Part-Part-Whole to 10

AC9MFN04 · Foundation Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

partition and combine collections up to 10 using part-part-whole relationships and subitising to recognise and name the parts

Prior knowledge

Students can count collections to at least 20 (AC9MFN03) and subitise collections to 5 (AC9MFN02). They have not yet systematically split a collection into two named parts and used part-part-whole language to describe the relationship.

By the end

Students partition and combine collections up to 10, using part-part-whole relationships and subitising to recognise and name the parts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising numbers represented in physical or virtual ten-frames, and describing their reasoning
  • partitioning collections of up to 10 objects in different ways and saying the part-part-whole relationship
  • representing part-part-whole relationships in numbers up to 10 using physical or virtual materials; identifying numbers represented by dots in standard number configurations such as dominos and dice
  • exploring number groupings in First Nations Australians' counting systems and the different ways of representing these groupings to form and partition numbers, applying this to quantify collections up to 10

Waypoints

  • 1. Recognising parts within a wholeHingeStudents can look at a structured representation of a number up to 10 and identify and name two parts that make the whole.
  • 2. Partitioning a collection in different waysStudents can partition a collection of up to 10 objects into two parts in more than one way, naming both parts and the whole each time.
  • 3. Combining parts to make a wholeStudents can combine two given parts to name the whole, using subitising or counting to verify.
