← AC9MFN04 Part-Part-Whole to 10

Combining parts to make a whole

AC9MFN04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can combine two given parts to name the whole, using subitising or counting to verify.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly names the whole when given two parts (total up to 10), using subitising or counting to verify
  • Student moves flexibly between partitioning and combining for the same number (e.g. can both split 8 into parts and combine given parts to name 8)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Partitioning a collection in different ways.

Width

Minimum
Given two groups of objects (total up to 10), name the whole by combining the parts — using subitising or counting to verify.
Typical
Combine two parts to name the whole across a range of part-part combinations to 10; represent part-part-whole relationships using physical or virtual materials, dominoes and standard number configurations; use both partitioning (whole to parts) and combining (parts to whole) flexibly.
Wider
  • Represent the same part-part-whole relationship in multiple ways (objects, drawings, numerals) and connect between representations
  • Connect part-part-whole thinking to familiar real-world situations — e.g. sharing items into two groups and recombining them
  • Explore part-part-whole relationships using structured tools (rekenreks, ten-frames) to compare which representations make which combinations easiest to see

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCombining integrates part recognition (node 1) and flexible partitioning (node 2) into bidirectional thinking — students move fluently between 'what are the parts?' and 'what is the whole?'. The coordination of both directions is the endpoint demand and the foundation for additive reasoning in Year 1.
