Connecting a question to data collection

AC9MFST01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify what data needs to be collected to answer a given investigative question and describe how they will collect it.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies what information needs to be collected to answer a given investigative question
  • Student describes at least one way to collect the needed data

Prior knowledge

Students have informal experience sorting objects and answering yes/no questions in everyday life. They have not yet participated in a structured data investigation — collecting data in response to a question, organising it, and using the organised data to answer the question.

Width

Minimum
Given one investigative question, identify what information needs to be collected and describe one way to collect it.
Typical
Connect a range of investigative questions to appropriate data collection strategies; distinguish between questions that can be answered by looking at existing objects and questions that require asking people; describe how collected data will answer the question.
Wider
  • Explore how First Nations Australians collect information from the environment to answer questions (e.g. predicting weather events) — connecting data collection to Country/Place knowledge
  • Investigate how an AI system needs to be 'trained' by collecting yes/no data to recognise objects — connecting data collection to digital contexts

Hinge

Nodes 2 and 3 depend on students having collected data that is connected to the investigative question. Students who collect arbitrary data — not matched to the question — cannot sort it into meaningful categories or use it to answer the question. The question-to-data connection is what makes the whole inquiry cycle coherent.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents at Foundation commonly try to answer investigative questions without systematic collection — they look at a collection and report an impression rather than identifying what data is needed. The reasoning scaffold (what is the question? what information would answer it? how would I collect that information?) is the thinking move that makes data collection purposeful.
