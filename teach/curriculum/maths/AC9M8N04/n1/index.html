Multiplication and division of integers

AC9M8N04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students establish and apply the sign rules for multiplication and division of integers, including negative × negative = positive.

Lessons on this site

  • Multiplying and dividing integers

Success criteria

  • Correctly applies sign rules for all combinations of integer multiplication and division.
  • Explains the sign rule for negative × negative.
  • Does not confuse multiplication sign rules with addition sign rules.

Prior knowledge

Students can add and subtract integers (from AC9M7N07) and apply the four operations to positive rational numbers (from AC9M7N06). Students understand properties of operations.

Width

Minimum
Apply sign rules to calculate products and quotients of integers, including two negative numbers.
Typical
Establish sign rules using patterns; apply to multi-step calculations; explain why (−1)² = 1 and (−1)³ = −1.
Wider
  • Investigate the pattern (−1)ⁿ for increasing n — connect to the sign rules and predict for any integer exponent.
  • Explore why negative × negative = positive using a number line or debt/removal model as a conceptual justification.

Hinge

Integer multiplication sign rules are directly required in Node 2 — students who confuse the rules when multiplying integers make the same errors when multiplying negative fractions and decimals, producing systematic sign errors across all rational number operations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe sign rules for multiplication are counter-intuitive, especially negative × negative = positive. Multiple representations (pattern, number line, debt model) each resolve a different facet of the misconception.
