Squares as area models

AC9M7N01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can connect squaring a number to the area of a square with that side length, and list perfect square numbers from 1 to 144.

Lessons on this site

  • Squares and square roots

Success criteria

  • Lists perfect square numbers from 1² to 12² without error.
  • Represents a given perfect square as an area model and states its side length.
  • Does not confuse the square number with its root (e.g. does not say '9 squared is 3').

Prior knowledge

Students can multiply and divide whole numbers fluently, use arrays to represent multiplication, and understand that area of a square = side × side (from Y6 measurement and number work).

Width

Minimum
List perfect squares 1–144 and represent at least three using area diagrams.
Typical
List perfect squares 1–400, connect each to its area diagram, and identify patterns in the sequence (e.g. differences between consecutive squares).
Wider
  • Investigate the last-digit pattern in perfect squares (last digits cycle: 0,1,4,9,6,5,6,9,4,1,0) and use this to quickly rule out non-perfect-squares.
  • Explore why perfect squares always have an odd number of factors, connecting to divisibility.

Hinge

Students who lack automatic recall of perfect squares to 144 cannot access the inverse relationship in Node 2 — the square root concept is not legible without the forward mapping being secure.

Response modes
