Solving by backtracking

AC9M7A03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students solve one-step and two-step linear equations by backtracking (undoing operations in reverse order).

Lessons on this site

  • Solving by backtracking

Success criteria

  • Correctly solves a two-step linear equation using backtracking.
  • Identifies the inverse operations used.
  • Applies backtracking to multi-operation equations in the correct order.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Equation as a balance statement.

Width

Minimum
Solve equations of the form ax = b and x + a = b by backtracking; show the inverse operations used.
Typical
Solve two-step equations (e.g. 3x + 7 = 19) using backtracking; explain the process at each step.
Wider
  • Compare backtracking and the balance/algebraic method for the same equation — note they produce the same result.
  • Apply to contextual problems: write and solve an equation from a word problem.

Hinge

Students who cannot execute the backtracking scaffold cannot transition to the algebraic balance method in Node 3 — both methods require identifying the order of operations, and the backtracking scaffold makes this explicit before the abstract method is introduced.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkBacktracking requires students to map the order of operations forward then reverse it. An explicit scaffold (draw the 'machine', identify steps, reverse) is needed before students apply this fluently.
