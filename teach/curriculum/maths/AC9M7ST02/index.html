Data displays and distribution

AC9M7ST02 · Year 7 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

create different types of numerical data displays including stem-and-leaf plots using software where appropriate; describe and compare the distribution of data, commenting on the shape, centre and spread including outliers and determining the range, median, mean and mode

Prior knowledge

Students can calculate range, median, mean and mode (from AC9M7ST01). Students can interpret distributions using mode and range (from AC9M6ST01).

By the end

Students create and interpret stem-and-leaf plots and other numerical data displays, describing and comparing distributions in terms of shape, centre, spread and outliers.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using ordered stem-and-leaf plots to record and display numerical data collected in a class investigation
  • comparing variation in attributes by category using split stem-and-leaf plots or dot plots
  • connecting features of the data display — highest frequency, clusters, gaps, symmetry or skewness — to the mode, range and median
  • using mean and median to compare data sets, identifying possible outliers and explaining how these may affect the comparison
  • exploring how artificial intelligence systems can use descriptive statistics to identify outliers or anomalies in data; for example, fraud detection

Waypoints

  • 1. Creating stem-and-leaf plotsHingeStudents construct ordered stem-and-leaf plots from a data set and read values from them accurately.
  • 2. Describing distribution shape, centre, spread and outliersStudents describe the distribution of data in terms of its shape (symmetrical, skewed), centre, spread and the presence of outliers.
  • 3. Comparing distributionsStudents compare two distributions using displays and summary statistics, making statements about differences in shape, centre and spread.
