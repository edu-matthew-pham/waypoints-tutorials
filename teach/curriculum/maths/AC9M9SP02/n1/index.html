← AC9M9SP02 Enlargement transformation

Invariant and changing properties under enlargement

AC9M9SP02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify which properties of a shape are preserved under enlargement (angles, shape) and which change (side lengths, perimeter, area).

  • What enlargement keeps

Success criteria

  • Correctly applies enlargement transformation and identifies the image dimensions
  • Lists at least two properties that are invariant (angles, shape) and two that change (lengths, area)
  • Uses the scale factor correctly to calculate new dimensions

Prior knowledge

Students can design, create and test algorithms that identify congruency or similarity of shapes (AC9M8SP04). They have not yet applied the enlargement transformation systematically, or generalised the relationships between scale factor and changes to length, area and volume.

Width

Minimum
Apply one enlargement transformation to a polygon using a given scale factor; list two properties that remain the same and two that change.
Typical
Apply enlargement transformations to a range of 2D shapes with various scale factors (including <1 for reduction); identify invariant properties (angles, shape, orientation) and changing properties (lengths, perimeter, area); verify using measurement and ratio.
Wider
  • Investigate how scale drawings and map scales use enlargement transformations — relate scale factor to real-world dimensions
  • Explore digital zoom and image resizing — discuss how uniform and non-uniform scaling relate to enlargement and distortion
  • Connect enlargement to the conditions for similarity established in AC9M8SP01 — confirm that enlargement always produces similar shapes

Related standards
