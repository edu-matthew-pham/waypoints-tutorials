Enlargement transformation

AC9M9SP02 · Year 9 Maths · Space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply the enlargement transformation to shapes and objects using dynamic geometry software as appropriate; identify and explain aspects that remain the same and those that change

Prior knowledge

Students can design, create and test algorithms that identify congruency or similarity of shapes (AC9M8SP04). They have not yet applied the enlargement transformation systematically, or generalised the relationships between scale factor and changes to length, area and volume.

By the end

Students apply the enlargement transformation to shapes and objects and explain which properties are invariant and which change, including the relationships between scale factor, length, area and volume.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing the ratio of lengths of corresponding sides of similar triangles and angles
  • using the properties of similarity to solve problems involving enlargement
  • investigating and generalising patterns in length, angle, area and volume when side lengths are enlarged by whole and rational numbers; e.g. factor of 3 multiplies area by 9 and volume by 27

Waypoints

  • 1. Invariant and changing properties under enlargementStudents can identify which properties of a shape are preserved under enlargement (angles, shape) and which change (side lengths, perimeter, area).
  • 2. Scale factor relationships for length, area and volumeHingeStudents can generalise the relationships between scale factor k and the scaling of length (×k), area (×k²) and volume (×k³).
