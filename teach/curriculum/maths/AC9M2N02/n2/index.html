← AC9M2N02 Partition three-digit numbers

Non-standard regrouping

AC9M2N02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 2 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can rename a three-digit number using non-standard groupings (e.g. 245 as 24 tens and 5 ones) and explain why both representations are equivalent.

Success criteria

  • Renames a three-digit number in at least one non-standard way using materials
  • Explains why the renamed form represents the same quantity as the standard form

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Standard partitioning.

Width

Minimum
Rename one three-digit number in at least one non-standard way using materials; explain why the value is unchanged.
Typical
Rename two- and three-digit numbers in multiple ways using standard and non-standard groupings; use knowledge of place value to explain equivalence; e.g. 245 as 2 hundreds 45 ones or 24 tens 5 ones.
Wider
  • Find all possible ways to rename a given three-digit number using different groupings; record systematically and look for patterns
  • Use non-standard partitioning to assist mental addition: e.g. rename 138 as 13 tens and 8 ones to add 40 more

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkNon-standard regrouping requires students to reason through equivalence explicitly — it is not immediately obvious that 24 tens and 5 ones equals 245. The reasoning scaffold of 'how many tens are in 2 hundreds?' needs to be made visible before students can apply it independently.
