← AC9M2N02 Partition three-digit numbers

Standard partitioning

AC9M2N02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can partition a three-digit number into hundreds, tens and ones using materials and explain the value of each part.

Success criteria

  • Partitions a three-digit number into hundreds, tens and ones using materials
  • Writes the expanded form of a three-digit number including one with a zero digit

Prior knowledge

Students partition one- and two-digit numbers in different ways including into tens and ones (AC9M1N02). They have not yet partitioned three-digit numbers, worked with non-standard groupings, or reasoned about zero as a placeholder.

Width

Minimum
Partition at least two three-digit numbers into hundreds, tens and ones using base-ten materials; match to expanded form (e.g. 300 + 40 + 5).
Typical
Partition a range of three-digit numbers including those with a zero digit; represent as expanded notation; compare the digits of a numeral with grouped materials.
Wider
  • Partition three-digit numbers with zero in different positions (tens, ones, hundreds) and explain why the numeral still has three digits
  • Use a range of materials (base-ten blocks, bundling sticks, collections of objects) to represent the same number and compare the representations

Hinge

Node 2 non-standard regrouping requires students to reason about how many tens are contained in a hundreds group. Students who omit zeros or misread place value cannot perform this reasoning — they cannot regroup what they have not correctly partitioned.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepZero as a placeholder is a persistent misconception — students may omit zeros or misread 305 as 35. Multiple representations across different contexts (materials, expanded form, numerals) help consolidate the concept that zero holds a position even when no quantity occupies it.
