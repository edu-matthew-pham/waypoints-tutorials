Partition two-digit numbers

AC9M1N02 · Year 1 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Partition one- and two-digit numbers in different ways using physical and virtual materials, including partitioning two-digit numbers into tens and ones

Prior knowledge

Students can partition and combine collections up to 10 using part-part-whole relationships (AC9MFN04). They can represent numbers to at least 20 (AC9MFN01). They have not yet partitioned two-digit numbers systematically into tens and ones, or explored non-standard partitions of two-digit numbers.

By the end

Students partition one- and two-digit numbers in different ways using physical and virtual materials, including partitioning two-digit numbers into tens and ones.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • building knowledge and understanding of the part-part-whole facts to 10, using physical and virtual materials; for example, using virtual ten-frames through a digital app or website to identify pairs of numbers that combine to make 10
  • using physical and virtual materials to partition numbers into counts of tens and ones; for example, recognise 35 as 3 tens and 5 ones or as 2 tens and 15 ones
  • using part-part-whole reasoning and physical or virtual materials to represent 24, then partitioning 24 in different ways and recording the partitions using numbers; for example, 10, 10 and 4 combine to make 24 or 10 and 14 combine to make 24

Waypoints

  • 1. Part-part-whole to 10HingeStudents can identify and represent all pairs of numbers that combine to make 10, using physical or virtual materials.
  • 2. Standard partition: tens and onesStudents can partition a two-digit number into tens and ones using physical materials, and record the partition using numbers.
  • 3. Non-standard partitionsStudents can partition a two-digit number in multiple ways, including non-standard partitions, and record each partition.
