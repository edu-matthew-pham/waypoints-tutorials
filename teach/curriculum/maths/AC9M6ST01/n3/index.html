← AC9M6ST01 Compare distributions

Comparative displays and interpretation

AC9M6ST01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct or read comparative displays (side-by-side bar charts, parallel dot plots) and use them to compare distributions, stating conclusions in the context of the data.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student reads a comparative display and states two specific distributional differences between the datasets.
  • Student relates the statistical comparison to the context of the data (not just the numbers).

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Mode, range and shape as comparative tools.

Width

Minimum
Read a provided comparative display; identify differences in mode, range, and shape between the two distributions; state at least two conclusions in context.
Typical
Construct comparative displays from provided data; identify and discuss distribution differences using mode, range, and shape; consider what the comparison reveals about the context; acknowledge limitations of the comparison.
Wider
  • Investigate comparative displays from media sources (sports statistics, survey results, health data) and evaluate the claims made from the comparison
  • Compare distributions from First Nations Australian community data contexts — e.g. comparing outcomes across contexts — and discuss what fair and respectful data comparison looks like

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierComparative interpretation requires students to read the display, apply mode/range/shape reasoning, and construct contextual statements. This coordinates statistical literacy, mathematical reasoning, and contextual judgment simultaneously.
