Skip counting patterns

AC9M1A01 · Year 1 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Recognise, continue and create pattern sequences, with numbers, symbols, shapes and objects, formed by skip counting, initially by twos, fives and tens

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise, copy and continue repeating patterns (AC9MFA01). They can count to at least 20 (AC9MFN01). They have not yet connected skip counting to pattern sequences, or recognised the structural properties of skip-counting sequences (e.g. digit patterns, growing totals).

By the end

Students recognise, continue and create pattern sequences formed by skip counting by twos, fives and tens, using numbers, symbols, shapes and objects.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using number charts, songs, rhymes and stories to establish skip counting sequences of twos, fives and tens
  • using shapes and objects to represent a growing pattern formed by skip counting
  • recognising the patterns in sequences formed by skip counting; for example, that skip counting in fives starting from zero always results in either a 5 or zero as the final digit
  • counting by twos, fives, or tens to determine how much money is in a collection of coins or notes of the same denomination
  • role-playing being an industrial robot on an assembly line that packs various items into boxes or packets in groups of five or ten
  • using different variations of the popular Korean counting game Sam-yuk-gu for generating skip counting pattern sequences

Waypoints

  • 1. Recognise and continue skip counting sequencesHingeStudents can recognise the rule in a given skip counting sequence (by 2s, 5s or 10s) and continue it correctly.
  • 2. Digit and structural patternsStudents can identify digit patterns in skip counting sequences and explain why they occur (e.g. fives always end in 0 or 5).
  • 3. Create and represent skip counting patternsStudents can create their own skip counting pattern sequence and represent it using numbers, objects or shapes.
