← AC9M1A01 Skip counting patterns

Recognise and continue skip counting sequences

AC9M1A01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise the rule in a given skip counting sequence (by 2s, 5s or 10s) and continue it correctly.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies the skip-counting rule in a given sequence (2s, 5s or 10s).
  • Continues the sequence for at least five additional terms without error.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise, copy and continue repeating patterns (AC9MFA01). They can count to at least 20 (AC9MFN01). They have not yet connected skip counting to pattern sequences, or recognised the structural properties of skip-counting sequences (e.g. digit patterns, growing totals).

Width

Minimum
Identify the rule in a given sequence (count by 2s, 5s or 10s); continue the sequence for at least five more terms.
Typical
Recognise and continue skip counting sequences by 2s, 5s and 10s using number charts, songs and rhymes; identify the rule; extend sequences starting from non-zero values; use patterns to check predictions about next terms.
Wider
  • Explore skip counting sequences from other cultural contexts — including the Korean counting game Sam-yuk-gu — identifying how the pattern rule operates in a different representational form.
  • Investigate skip counting sequences on a hundreds chart, colouring the multiples and describing the visual pattern produced.

Hinge

Skip counting fluency is a prerequisite for Node 2 (digit and structural pattern identification) and Node 3 (creating sequences) — students who cannot reliably continue a sequence cannot identify digit patterns or generate their own sequences.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSkip counting fluency by 2s, 5s and 10s requires practice — the sequences must be automatic before students can use them reliably for quantification and pattern work. Songs and repeated oral practice are appropriate consolidation tools here.
