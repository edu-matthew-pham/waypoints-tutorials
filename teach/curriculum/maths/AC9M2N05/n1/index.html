← AC9M2N05 Multiply and divide with strategies

Equal groups and repeated addition

AC9M2N05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 2 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can represent a multiplication situation as equal groups and as a repeated addition number sentence.

Success criteria

  • Makes equal groups for a given multiplication situation and counts the total
  • Writes a repeated addition sentence that matches the situation

Prior knowledge

Students use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving equal sharing and grouping (AC9M1N06). They have not yet formalised arrays or used repeated addition, skip counting and partitioning as multiplication and division strategies.

Width

Minimum
Make equal groups for a multiplication situation (e.g. 4 trays of 6); count by repeated addition; write the addition sentence.
Typical
Represent multiplication situations as equal groups, skip counting and repeated addition; write number sentences; use materials or diagrams to support calculation.
Wider
  • Connect skip counting to repeated addition: e.g. 3, 6, 9, 12 is the same as 3+3+3+3; record both and explain the relationship
  • Find the total in equal-group problems from everyday contexts (wheels on cars, windows in carriages) by skip counting and repeated addition

Hinge

Node 2 arrays require students to interpret rows as equal groups and columns as equal groups. Students who cannot form or count equal groups cannot name arrays or use them to find totals — the array concept is built directly on the equal-groups foundation.
