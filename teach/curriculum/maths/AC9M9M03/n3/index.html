Similarity, scale and proportion in triangles

AC9M9M03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply similarity and scale factor to solve problems involving corresponding sides and areas of similar triangles.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies corresponding sides in similar triangles and the scale factor
  • Correctly applies the scale factor to find an unknown side
  • Correctly applies k² (not k) when finding the ratio of areas of similar figures

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Trigonometric ratios — finding unknown angles.

Width

Minimum
Identify corresponding sides in two similar right-angled triangles; use the scale factor to find one unknown side length.
Typical
Apply similarity and scale factor to find unknown sides in similar triangles; establish the relationship between scale factor and the ratio of areas (area scales as k²); use proportional reasoning to solve practical scale problems (e.g. estimating heights from photographs).
Wider
  • Design and use a Biltmore stick (based on similar triangle principles) to estimate the height of a tree; connect to rates and proportional reasoning
  • Investigate proportional estimation using photographs — take a photograph with a known reference object and use scale to estimate an unknown measurement
  • Explore the triangle inequality and generalise the relationship between Pythagoras' theorem and inequalities for acute (c²<a²+b²) and obtuse (c²>a²+b²) triangles

Hinge

Applying k rather than k² for area scaling is a persistent error that undermines N4 multi-concept problems whenever area scaling is part of the solution.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly confuse scale factor for length with scale factor for area — applying the same multiplier to both. Working across multiple examples where length and area scale differently (k and k²) resolves this conflation.
