Trigonometric ratios — finding unknown sides

AC9M9M03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply sine, cosine and tangent ratios to find unknown sides in right-angled triangles.

Success criteria

  • Correctly labels opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse relative to a specified angle
  • Selects the correct trigonometric ratio for the given information
  • Calculates the unknown side correctly to an appropriate number of decimal places

Prior knowledge

Students can use Pythagoras' theorem to find unknown side lengths in right-angled triangles (AC9M8M06). They have not yet applied trigonometric ratios in right-angled triangles, or combined similarity, scale, and Pythagoras in multi-concept spatial problems.

Width

Minimum
Identify the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse relative to a given angle; apply one trigonometric ratio (sin, cos or tan) to find one unknown side; verify using a calculator.
Typical
Select and apply the appropriate trigonometric ratio (sin, cos, tan) to find unknown sides in right-angled triangles across a variety of configurations; correctly identify which sides are opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse relative to the given angle.
Wider
  • Apply trigonometric ratios to practical contexts (ramps, angles of elevation, surveying) — draw the right-angled triangle and label before calculating
  • Investigate the relationship between trigonometric ratios for complementary angles (sin θ = cos(90°−θ))
  • Connect trigonometric ratios to the unit circle (sine = y-coordinate, cosine = x-coordinate for a unit-radius point) as a conceptual bridge to Year 10 work

Hinge

Correct side labelling (opposite/adjacent relative to the angle, not fixed positions) is prerequisite for every trig ratio application. Students who mislabel sides select the wrong ratio and produce wrong answers in N2 and all applied work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsCorrectly identifying opposite and adjacent sides relative to the angle (not relative to the triangle) is a persistent source of error. Fluency in labelling sides — particularly for non-standard triangle orientations — requires varied practice before applying ratios in problem contexts.
