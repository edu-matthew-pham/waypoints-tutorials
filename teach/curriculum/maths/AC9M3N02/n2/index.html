← AC9M3N02 Unit fractions

Multiples of unit fractions

AC9M3N02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name and represent multiples of unit fractions (e.g. 2/3, 3/4, 4/5) and locate them on a number line.

Success criteria

  • Students can correctly name and write a fraction from a shaded area model or marked number line.
  • Students can represent a given fraction (e.g. 3/5) on a number line or area model.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What a unit fraction means.

Width

Minimum
Shade or mark three different multiples of a unit fraction on area models and a number line (e.g. 2/4, 3/5).
Typical
Represent multiples of halves, thirds, quarters, fifths and tenths using area models, number lines and sets; name fractions correctly using numerator and denominator.
Wider
  • Explore fractions beyond one whole (e.g. 5/4, 7/3) as natural extensions of the multiple pattern on a number line
  • Investigate how tenths connect to decimal notation, using number lines to show both representations

Hinge

Understanding the numerator as a count is directly required for Node 3 — combining fractions to complete the whole requires students to count parts toward the total. Students who conflate numerator and denominator roles will be unable to reason about what is missing from the whole.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents benefit from explicit scaffolding of the numerator as a count — 'we are counting how many parts of size 1/4'. This framing prevents confusion between numerator and denominator roles and makes the multiple structure legible.
