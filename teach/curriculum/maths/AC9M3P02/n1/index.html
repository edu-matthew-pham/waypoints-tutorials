← AC9M3P02 Conducting chance experiments

Identifying and recording possible outcomes

AC9M3P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 3 Maths · Probability · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can list all possible outcomes for a simple chance experiment before conducting it, and set up a recording method.

Success criteria

  • Students can list all possible outcomes for a given chance experiment.
  • Students can construct a recording table for results before beginning the experiment.

Prior knowledge

Students can identify chance events and describe outcomes as likely, unlikely, certain or impossible (AC9M3P01). They have not yet conducted repeated experiments, recorded results systematically, or observed and discussed variation in outcomes.

Width

Minimum
Before an experiment (e.g. flipping a coin, spinning a spinner), list all possible outcomes and design a tally or frequency table to record results.
Typical
List all possible outcomes for varied experiments (coins, spinners, dice, coloured counters); create appropriate recording tables; explain why listing outcomes before experimenting is useful.
Wider
  • Explore experiments with more than two outcomes (e.g. a four-section spinner) and identify how the number of possible outcomes affects likelihood
  • Compare the number of possible outcomes across different experiments and predict which would show more variation

Hinge

A complete pre-experiment outcomes list and recording table are prerequisites for Node 2 (systematic data collection) and Node 3 (recognising variation) — students who skip this step produce incomplete or unstructured data that cannot support the variation discussion the descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkListing outcomes before experimenting is a reasoning discipline that doesn't come naturally — students typically want to start and record as they go. Explicit scaffolding of 'predict, then test' establishes the experimental structure the descriptor requires.
