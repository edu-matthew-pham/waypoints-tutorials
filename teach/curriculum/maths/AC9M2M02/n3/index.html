Halves and quarters in time and events

AC9M2M02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 2 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify halves and quarters of an hour and connect fraction language to real-world time and event contexts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies half an hour and a quarter of an hour in a given context
  • Connects 'half past' and 'quarter past' to the position of the big hand on a clock face

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Halves and quarters in length and position.

Width

Minimum
Identify half an hour and quarter of an hour; connect to clock positions (half past, quarter past).
Typical
Recognise and use halves and quarters in time contexts: half an hour, quarter of an hour, half time and quarters in sporting events; connect to clock language and to the clock face as a circle divided into halves and quarters.
Wider
  • Discuss how sirens in Australian Rules Football represent quarters and half-time; connect to the clock face as a circle divided by quarter-turns
  • Investigate other events or activities that use halves and quarters of time (e.g. cooking timers, race laps); explain what each fraction refers to

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node brings together the abstract fraction concept (equal parts of a whole) and time as a measurement context. The integration challenge is connecting 'quarter of an hour' to both the numerical fraction (15 out of 60 minutes) and the physical clock face (one quarter of the circle).
