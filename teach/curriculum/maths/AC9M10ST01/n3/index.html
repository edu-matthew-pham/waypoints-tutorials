Ethical considerations in statistical reporting

AC9M10ST01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify ethical considerations in statistical reporting and explain how ethical or political motivations can influence how data is collected, framed, or presented.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: ethics in reporting data

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least one ethical consideration in a statistical report that goes beyond technical accuracy
  • Student explains how an ethical or political framing has shaped a statistical claim or its presentation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Evaluating samples and representativeness.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one ethical consideration in a described statistical report (e.g. who collected the data, who benefits from the conclusion, whose perspective is absent).
Typical
Identify multiple ethical considerations across different statistical reports; explain how ethical or political framing shapes data collection, display choice, and conclusion drawing; distinguish between statistical error and deliberate misrepresentation; evaluate statistical claims in terms of both technical accuracy and ethical transparency.
Wider
  • Investigate how the concept of Indigenous data sovereignty challenges conventional approaches to government-collected data about First Nations Australians — examining who has the right to collect, use and interpret data about a community
  • Analyse a specific case of statistical reporting in public health or environmental policy and evaluate the ethical responsibilities of researchers, media and government in communicating uncertainty and risk

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEthical analysis of statistical reporting is a genuine hinge — students need to move beyond 'this graph is misleading' to understanding how statistical choices encode values and power. Multiple context types (health, government, AI) build this more complex critical lens.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierEthical critique integrates technical accuracy analysis (node 1), sample evaluation (node 2), and value judgements about who is served by a statistical claim. It is where statistical literacy becomes critical literacy.
