Posing and refining the investigation question

AC9M6ST03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can pose a statistical question for investigation, identify what data is needed, and refine the question based on feasibility or initial findings.

Success criteria

  • Student poses a statistical question and explains what data is needed to answer it.
  • Student identifies at least one way the question could be refined for better investigation.

Prior knowledge

Students can plan and conduct statistical investigations, choose appropriate displays and communicate findings (AC9M5ST03). They have experience with the full investigation cycle. They have not yet practised refining questions based on initial findings or working with the full range of variable types from AC9M6ST01.

Width

Minimum
Pose a statistical question; identify what data would answer it; consider whether the data is collectible; refine the question if needed to make it answerable.
Typical
Pose questions that lead to interesting statistical investigations; distinguish statistical questions (expecting variability) from mathematical questions (single answer); anticipate potential issues with data collection before starting; refine based on a pilot sample.
Wider
  • Investigate question design — how does the wording of a question affect what data is collected? Compare two different phrasings of the same question.
  • Pose a question from a real community context and discuss how the question framing affects who would want to investigate it

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who generate only one investigation question type (e.g. 'how many...') develop a narrow concept of statistical investigation. Exposure to comparison questions, association questions, and change-over-time questions broadens the concept of what statistical investigation can address.
