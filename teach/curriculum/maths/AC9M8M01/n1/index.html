Decomposing composite shapes for area

AC9M8M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students decompose a composite shape into known shapes and calculate the total area by summing component areas.

Lessons on this site

  • Composite shapes

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies a valid decomposition of the composite shape.
  • Calculates each component area accurately.
  • Combines areas correctly (adding or subtracting as required).

Prior knowledge

Students can apply area formulas for triangles, rectangles and parallelograms (from AC9M7M01). Students can calculate circumference of circles (from AC9M7M03).

Width

Minimum
Decompose a composite shape made of two rectangles or a rectangle and triangle; calculate total area.
Typical
Decompose and calculate area for a range of composite shapes including those with subtracted regions (e.g. shape with a hole); select appropriate decomposition strategy.
Wider
  • Investigate floor plan problems requiring area calculation for irregular rooms — apply to real building design contexts.
  • Explore shapes requiring both addition and subtraction of areas (e.g. a frame) and generalise the approach.

Hinge

Node 2 requires the same dimension-identification discipline for irregular shapes — students who have not internalised the decomposition strategy in Node 1 apply it inconsistently to irregular shapes where the component boundaries are less obvious.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDecomposition requires an explicit strategy: identify the component shapes, identify dimensions from the diagram, calculate each area, combine. Students who proceed without this strategy make errors in dimension identification.
