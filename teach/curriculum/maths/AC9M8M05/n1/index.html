Rates as comparisons of different units

AC9M8M05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students recognise that a rate compares two quantities with different units and express rates in standard form (quantity per unit).

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies and writes a rate from a practical context.
  • Converts between related rate units.
  • Distinguishes a rate (different units) from a ratio (same units).

Prior knowledge

Students can solve ratio problems (from AC9M7N08). Students understand proportional reasoning and unit conversion.

Width

Minimum
Identify and write rates from practical contexts (e.g. km/h, $/kg); convert between related rate units.
Typical
Identify constant and average rates in real contexts; apply rates to unit conversion problems; distinguish rates from ratios.
Wider
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' land management practices involving rates — e.g. rate of fire spread under different environmental conditions.
  • Compare multiple rate units for the same phenomenon (e.g. km/h, m/s, miles/hour) and practise conversion between them.

Hinge

Students who only recognise speed as a rate cannot identify the rate structure in Node 2 problems involving pay rates, tax rates, or dilution — they cannot set up the calculation because they don't recognise these as rate problems.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepRates appear in an enormous variety of contexts. Students who only practise speed calculations fail to recognise pay rates, dilution rates, or interest rates as the same mathematical structure.
