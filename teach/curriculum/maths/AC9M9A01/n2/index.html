← AC9M9A01 Exponent laws

Exponent laws with numerical expressions

AC9M9A01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply all exponent laws (product, quotient, power of a power, power of a product, zero exponent) to simplify numerical expressions with integer exponents and explain why each law works.

Success criteria

  • Correctly names and applies at least three distinct exponent laws to simplify a multi-step numerical expression
  • Justifies at least one step using first-principles counting of factors
  • Correctly handles the zero exponent (any non-zero base to the power 0 equals 1)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Negative integer exponents.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least three exponent laws to simplify numerical expressions involving integer exponents; justify each step using the definition of an exponent (repeated multiplication/division).
Typical
Apply all five exponent laws to numerical expressions; relate each law to first-principles reasoning (e.g. b²×b³ = b⁵ because counting factors gives 2+3=5 factors); simplify multi-law expressions; identify which law applies in each step.
Wider
  • Investigate why (ab)² = a²b² but (a+b)² ≠ a²+b² — connect to the distributive property and preview of binomial expansion
  • Apply exponent laws to expressions involving SI prefixes in combined calculations (e.g. 3×10⁴ × 2×10⁻⁶)
  • Explore how the exponent laws underpin logarithm rules (e.g. log(ab) = log a + log b corresponds to the product law) — conceptual connection only

Hinge

Fluency selecting the correct law for the correct operation is the direct prerequisite for N3. Students who conflate product and power-of-a-power laws carry the error into algebraic simplification where it is harder to detect.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSelecting the correct law and applying it without error requires fluency built through varied practice. Students who can explain the laws conceptually still commonly make errors on multi-step simplifications — particularly confusing product law (add exponents) with power of a power law (multiply exponents).
