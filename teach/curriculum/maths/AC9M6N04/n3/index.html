← AC9M6N04 Add and subtract decimals

Estimation and rounding as a reasonableness check

AC9M6N04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can round decimal numbers to check the reasonableness of addition and subtraction results, and identify when a calculated answer is unreasonable.

Success criteria

  • Student uses rounding to estimate a decimal sum or difference and compares the estimate to their calculated answer.
  • Student identifies whether a given calculated answer is reasonable using estimation and explains their reasoning.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Adding and subtracting decimals.

Width

Minimum
Round two decimal numbers to the nearest whole number or one decimal place; use rounded values to estimate a sum or difference; compare estimate to calculated answer and judge reasonableness.
Typical
Apply rounding and estimation before or after calculations to judge reasonableness; identify and explain errors in given decimal calculations using estimation; select appropriate rounding precision for the context (e.g. money to the nearest cent, measurements to one decimal place).
Wider
  • Investigate real-world contexts where estimation is more useful than exact calculation (e.g. shopping budgets, approximate measurements) and justify when each is appropriate
  • Compare different estimation strategies (front-end estimation, rounding to different precisions) and evaluate their accuracy across different calculation types

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often use estimation as a post-hoc check but do not have a concept of what 'unreasonable' looks like until they encounter varied contexts. Using estimation across money, measurement, and data contexts builds the judgment needed to catch errors automatically.
