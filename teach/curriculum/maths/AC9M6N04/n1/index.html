← AC9M6N04 Add and subtract decimals

Place value alignment for decimal operations

AC9M6N04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can align decimal numbers by place value to set up addition and subtraction calculations, including numbers with different numbers of decimal places.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly aligns decimal numbers for calculation, including adding placeholder zeros where needed.
  • Student explains why alignment ensures correct place value combinations.

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret, compare and order numbers with more than 2 decimal places using place value understanding (AC9M5N01). They can solve problems involving multiplication of larger numbers and use estimation to check reasonableness (AC9M5N06). They have not yet systematically applied place value to addition and subtraction of decimals with different numbers of decimal places.

Width

Minimum
Set up addition and subtraction of two decimal numbers by aligning decimal points; explain why alignment ensures like values are combined; add zeros as placeholders where needed.
Typical
Align decimal numbers with varying numbers of decimal places for both addition and subtraction; use placeholder zeros to maintain alignment; apply alignment strategy to both vertical algorithm and mental calculation setups.
Wider
  • Investigate what happens when decimals are added without alignment (common error) and diagnose the resulting errors
  • Connect decimal place value alignment to the same principle in whole number algorithms — why do we align ones with ones?

Hinge

Misalignment in place value setup produces systematic errors in Node 2 calculations. Students who do not reliably align decimals before calculating will produce confidently wrong answers in every subsequent decimal addition and subtraction task.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMisalignment is the primary error in decimal addition and subtraction. Students benefit from seeing the error pattern in varied examples — not just being shown the correct method — to understand why alignment is necessary, not merely procedural.
Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAlignment must become automatic before multi-digit decimal calculations are reliable. This is a precision prerequisite for the calculation nodes below.
