Adding and subtracting decimals

AC9M6N04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can accurately add and subtract decimal numbers with up to three decimal places using written and mental strategies.

Success criteria

  • Student accurately adds and subtracts decimal numbers with up to two decimal places using a written strategy.
  • Student identifies and corrects a calculation error in a provided worked example.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Place value alignment for decimal operations.

Width

Minimum
Add and subtract pairs of decimal numbers with up to two decimal places using a written algorithm; check result is reasonable by inspection.
Typical
Add and subtract decimal numbers with up to three decimal places using written algorithms, mental strategies (e.g. partitioning, compensation), and digital tools; select the most appropriate strategy for a given calculation.
Wider
  • Investigate multi-step decimal calculations in realistic contexts (e.g. calculating change, comparing totals across multiple items)
  • Explore when mental strategies for decimal addition/subtraction are more efficient than written algorithms and articulate the conditions

Hinge

Fluent, accurate decimal addition and subtraction is a prerequisite for AC9M6N07 (fractions, decimals and percentages of quantities) and AC9M6N09 (modelling with rational numbers). Students with persistent calculation errors cannot execute multi-step rational number problems reliably.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate decimal calculation requires fluency with the written algorithm and reliable place value alignment. Errors compound across multi-step problems. Targeted practice builds the precision needed for application contexts.
