← AC9M4N01 Decimals — tenths and hundredths

Decimal notation conventions and contexts

AC9M4N01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 4 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name, write and read decimals to hundredths in money and measurement contexts, explaining how notation conventions differ.

Success criteria

  • Students can correctly read a decimal in money notation and in measurement notation, noting the different conventions
  • Students can explain that the digits after the decimal point represent tenths and hundredths in both money and measurement

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Hundredths and multiplicative structure.

Width

Minimum
Write at least one decimal value in both money notation and measurement notation; read each aloud correctly (e.g. $2.75 as 'two dollars seventy-five', 2.75 m as 'two point seven five metres').
Typical
Connect decimal notation to both money and metric measurement contexts; explain the similarities and differences in how each is read (money vs measurement conventions); recognise that in both, the digits after the decimal point represent tenths and hundredths of the unit.
Wider
  • Compare how decimals are read across different real-world contexts (sporting scores, scientific measures, GPS coordinates) and discuss why different conventions exist
  • Investigate how currency decimals connect to hundredths: 75 cents is 75 hundredths of a dollar — verify this by counting coins and connecting to decimal notation
