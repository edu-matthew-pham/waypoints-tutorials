Decimals — tenths and hundredths

AC9M4N01 · Year 4 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Recognise and extend the application of place value to tenths and hundredths and use the conventions of decimal notation to name and represent decimals

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise, represent and order natural numbers and understand place value for hundreds, tens and ones (AC9M3N01, AC9M3N02). They have not yet extended place value beyond the ones place to fractional parts of a whole.

By the end

Students recognise tenths and hundredths as extensions of the place value system, name and represent decimals in multiple ways, and connect decimal notation to money and measurement.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using a bar to represent the whole, dividing it into 10 equal pieces with each piece representing 0.1 or a tenth of the whole length
  • using materials to show the multiplicative relationship between the whole, tenths and hundredths; for example, using a bundle of 10 straws
  • recognising that one is the same as ten-tenths and one-tenth is the same as 10 hundredths and using this relationship to rename decimals
  • making models of measurement attributes to show the relationship between the base unit and parts of the unit; for example, 1.5 metres is one metre and five-tenths of the next metre
  • counting large quantities of mixed notes and coins, writing the total using dollars and cents
  • comparing the way money and measures are read and said, and explaining how they are the same and different

Waypoints

  • 1. Tenths as place valueHingeStudents can partition a whole into ten equal parts and represent one or more tenths using decimal notation.
  • 2. Hundredths and multiplicative structureHingeStudents can represent hundredths and explain the multiplicative relationship between ones, tenths and hundredths.
  • 3. Decimal notation conventions and contextsStudents can name, write and read decimals to hundredths in money and measurement contexts, explaining how notation conventions differ.
