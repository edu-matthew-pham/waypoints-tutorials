Pi and circle features

AC9M7M03 · Year 7 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

describe the relationship between π and the features of circles including the circumference, radius and diameter

Prior knowledge

Students are familiar with circle vocabulary (centre, radius, diameter) from informal prior experience. Students can measure length and calculate with decimals (from AC9M6M01, AC9M7N06).

By the end

Students describe π as the constant ratio of circumference to diameter and use this relationship to calculate circumference and diameter.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising the features of circles and their relationships to one another; for example, labelling the parts of a circle including centre, radius, diameter, chord, arc
  • comparing the circumference of circles in relation to their radius and diameter, to establish measurement formulas
  • investigating π as the constant in the proportional relationship between the circumference of a circle and its diameter, and historical approximations
  • investigating the applications and significance of circles in everyday life of First Nations Australians such as in basketry, symbols and architecture

Waypoints

  • 1. Features of circles and their relationshipsHingeStudents can identify and name the features of a circle (radius, diameter, circumference, chord) and state that diameter = 2 × radius.
  • 2. Discovering π as a constant ratioStudents investigate the ratio C/d for multiple circles and recognise that it is always approximately 3.14 — this constant is π.
  • 3. Using π to calculate circumference and diameterStudents use C = πd (or C = 2πr) to calculate the circumference given the diameter or radius, and rearrange to find diameter given circumference.
