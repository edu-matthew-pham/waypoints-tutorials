Discovering π as a constant ratio

AC9M7M03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students investigate the ratio C/d for multiple circles and recognise that it is always approximately 3.14 — this constant is π.

Lessons on this site

  • Discovering π

Success criteria

  • Measures and calculates C/d for at least two circles and observes the result is approximately 3.14.
  • States that π ≈ 3.14 and explains what it represents.
  • Does not treat π as just a button on a calculator without understanding its meaning.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Features of circles and their relationships.

Width

Minimum
Measure the circumference and diameter of at least two circles; calculate C/d and observe the approximate constancy.
Typical
Calculate C/d for several circles with different sizes; record results; discuss why the ratio is constant; state that this constant is named π.
Wider
  • Investigate the history of π and how mathematicians approximated it using polygons.
  • Explore why π is irrational — it cannot be written as an exact fraction, only approximated.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe constancy of C/d is not obvious from a single measurement. Multiple circles of very different sizes make the invariance of π compelling rather than arbitrary.
