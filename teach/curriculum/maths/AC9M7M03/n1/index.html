Features of circles and their relationships

AC9M7M03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and name the features of a circle (radius, diameter, circumference, chord) and state that diameter = 2 × radius.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies and labels all key features of a circle.
  • States that diameter = 2 × radius and applies it correctly.
  • Does not confuse radius with diameter.

Prior knowledge

Students are familiar with circle vocabulary (centre, radius, diameter) from informal prior experience. Students can measure length and calculate with decimals (from AC9M6M01, AC9M7N06).

Width

Minimum
Label all features on a circle diagram; state the relationship between radius and diameter.
Typical
Measure radius and diameter of several circles; verify d = 2r consistently; identify chord, arc, sector informally.
Wider
  • Investigate circles in First Nations Australian contexts: circular designs in weaving, significance of circles in cultural practices.
  • Explore how changing the radius changes both the diameter and circumference proportionally.

Hinge

Students who confuse radius and diameter use the wrong measurement when calculating C/d in Node 2, making π appear to be approximately 6.28 or 1.57 — this completely undermines the discovery of π's constancy and all subsequent circumference calculations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsCircle vocabulary and the d = 2r relationship must be automatic before investigating π. Students who confuse radius and diameter make systematic errors in all circle calculations.
