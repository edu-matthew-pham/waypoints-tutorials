Identifying statistical claims in media

AC9M6ST02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify when a media argument is supported by statistical evidence and locate the data, representation, and conclusion in the argument.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies a statistical claim, the supporting data, and the conclusion in a media example.
  • Student notes whether the data source is stated.

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret line graphs and discuss relationships (AC9M5ST02). They have not yet critically evaluated statistical claims in media sources or examined the connection between data collection methods and conclusions.

Width

Minimum
Find two examples of statistical arguments in media (newspaper, website, advertisement); identify the claim, the data used, and the display; note what context is given.
Typical
Identify statistical arguments across traditional and digital media formats; distinguish claims that are supported by data from those that are not; locate the data source, representation type, and conclusion in each argument.
Wider
  • Compare how the same dataset is presented differently across multiple media outlets and discuss what each presentation emphasises or omits
  • Examine historical examples of misleading statistical reporting and identify the techniques used

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStatistical literacy requires recognising statistical arguments in varied media contexts — advertising, news reporting, social media, scientific communication. Students need broad exposure to develop recognition across contexts rather than just in school-constructed examples.
