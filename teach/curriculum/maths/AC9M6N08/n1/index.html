Rounding rational numbers for estimation

AC9M6N08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can round fractions, decimals and percentages to convenient values to produce estimates, selecting the rounding precision appropriate to the context.

Success criteria

  • Student rounds decimals and fractions to convenient values and uses them to produce a reasonable estimate.
  • Student explains why their chosen rounding precision is appropriate for the context.

Prior knowledge

Students can check and explain the reasonableness of solutions using estimation strategies appropriate to the context including financial contexts (AC9M5N08). They can use mathematical modelling with additive and multiplicative situations. They have not yet applied estimation strategies specifically to rational number (fraction, decimal, percentage) problems.

Width

Minimum
Round a decimal to the nearest whole number and a fraction to the nearest half or whole number; use rounded values to estimate a sum, difference or product.
Typical
Round rational numbers (fractions, decimals, percentages) to convenient nearby values for estimation; select rounding precision based on the context and desired accuracy; explain the trade-off between rounding precision and estimate accuracy.
Wider
  • Investigate over-estimation vs under-estimation — when does rounding up every number in an addition produce a useful bound? When does it over-estimate?
  • Apply rounding estimation to budget planning — estimating total cost before calculating exactly
