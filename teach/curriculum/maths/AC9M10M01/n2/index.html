Surface area of composite objects

AC9M10M01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can calculate the surface area of a composite object by summing exposed faces of component solids.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly calculates the surface area of a composite object with no missing or double-counted faces
  • Student states the answer in appropriate units

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Decomposing composite objects.

Width

Minimum
Calculate the surface area of a composite solid formed from two right prisms or cylinders, correctly excluding internal faces.
Typical
Calculate surface areas of composite objects formed from multiple components including prisms, cylinders and combinations; estimate surface areas for practical purposes; apply appropriate units and check reasonableness of answers.
Wider
  • Estimate the surface area of composite objects in practical contexts where exact dimensions are unavailable, discussing the accuracy needed for the application
  • Investigate how surface area to volume ratio changes as objects become more complex, with applications to packaging design or heat transfer

Hinge

Surface area calculation errors cascade into N4 — modelling problems requiring surface area (e.g. material cost estimation) produce meaningless results if the calculation step is unreliable.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSurface area calculation errors are systematic — missing faces, double-counting, unit errors. Fluency requires deliberate practice across multiple composite configurations before modelling applications.
