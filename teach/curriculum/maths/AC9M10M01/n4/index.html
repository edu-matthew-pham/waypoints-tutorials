← AC9M10M01 Surface area and volume of composite objects

Modelling with composite objects

AC9M10M01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use surface area and volume calculations for composite objects to solve a practical problem, stating assumptions and interpreting results in context.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies the composite object structure relevant to the practical problem and performs the correct calculation
  • Student states the result in context-appropriate terms and identifies at least one assumption made
  • Student checks whether the result is reasonable given the context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Volume of composite objects.

Width

Minimum
Apply surface area or volume calculation to solve a practical problem involving a composite object; state the answer in context-relevant terms.
Typical
Model practical problems involving composite objects (e.g. rainwater collection from a composite roof, optimal storage container design, supply estimation for an event); identify and state assumptions; interpret and evaluate results; choose between competing designs using calculated values.
Wider
  • Design and evaluate optimal composite container configurations for a given volume constraint (e.g. maximum rainwater storage fitting a given space), modelling multiple configurations and comparing
  • Investigate how surface area to volume ratio of composite biological structures (e.g. lung alveoli, tree branching) is optimised for function — connecting measurement to real-world design principles

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepTranslating practical problems into composite object decompositions varies substantially across context types. Students who only model one context type (e.g. construction) may not apply the same decomposition thinking to biological or design contexts. Multiple context types build flexible application.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates decomposition, surface area and volume calculation with the full modelling cycle — formulation, calculation, interpretation and evaluation. It is the integration point for all prior nodes in this descriptor.
