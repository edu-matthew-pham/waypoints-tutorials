Posing an investigative question

AC9M5ST03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can pose a statistical question that can be answered with data, distinguishing it from a question with a single certain answer.

Success criteria

  • Student poses a question that requires data to answer
  • Student explains why the question is statistical (variability in answers expected)

Prior knowledge

Students can conduct statistical investigations with guidance (AC9M4ST03). They have not yet planned the full investigation cycle independently, including posing their own question and selecting displays.

Width

Minimum
Pose a statistical question about a given topic; explain why it requires data to answer.
Typical
Pose and refine statistical questions; distinguish statistical questions from fixed-answer questions; connect the question to the data that would answer it.
Wider
  • Compare statistical and causal questions: what can statistics answer vs what requires experiment?
  • Investigate how survey questions are designed to be statistical

Hinge

A poorly posed question cannot be answered by data — students who cannot distinguish statistical from fixed-answer questions produce investigations with no collectible data, collapsing every subsequent phase (Mode B phase collapse).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe distinction between a statistical question and a fixed-answer question requires an explicit reasoning scaffold: 'what data would I need?' and 'would different samples give different answers?' Students who skip this step design uncollectable investigations.
