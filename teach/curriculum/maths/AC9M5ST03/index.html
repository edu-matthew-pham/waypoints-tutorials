Plan statistical investigations

AC9M5ST03 · Year 5 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Plan and conduct statistical investigations by posing questions or identifying a problem and collecting relevant data; choose appropriate displays and interpret the data; communicate findings within the context of the investigation

Prior knowledge

Students can conduct statistical investigations with guidance (AC9M4ST03). They have not yet planned the full investigation cycle independently, including posing their own question and selecting displays.

By the end

Students plan and conduct statistical investigations, posing questions, collecting and displaying data, interpreting findings and communicating conclusions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • posing questions about insect diversity in the playground, collecting data by taping a one-metre-square piece of paper to the playground, and observing the type and number of insects on it over time
  • posing a question or identifying a problem of interest, collecting, interpreting and analysing the data and discussing if the data generated provides the information necessary to answer the questions
  • developing survey questions that are objective, without opinion and have a balanced set of answer choices without bias
  • exploring First Nations Ranger Groups’ and other groups’ biodiversity detection techniques to care for Country/Place, posing investigative questions, collecting and interpreting related data to represent and communicate findings

Waypoints

  • 1. Posing an investigative questionHingeStudents can pose a statistical question that can be answered with data, distinguishing it from a question with a single certain answer.
  • 2. Collecting and displaying dataStudents can collect data relevant to their question and choose and construct an appropriate display.
  • 3. Interpreting and communicating findingsStudents can interpret their data display and communicate findings within the context of their investigation.
