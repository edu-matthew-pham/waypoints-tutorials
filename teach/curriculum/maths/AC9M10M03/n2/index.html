Bearings and direction problems

AC9M10M03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret compass bearings and use trigonometry to solve navigation problems involving direction and distance.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly translates a described bearing problem into a labelled right-angled triangle diagram
  • Student solves for the required distance or angle using appropriate trigonometric ratios

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Angles of elevation and depression.

Width

Minimum
Interpret a three-figure compass bearing; draw a right-angled triangle from a described bearing problem; solve for a distance or angle.
Typical
Solve navigation problems using bearings including multi-leg journeys; decompose bearings problems into right-angled triangle components; calculate final bearing and distance from a multi-step trip; apply both Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometric ratios as needed.
Wider
  • Explore navigation techniques used by First Nations Australians — including wayfinding using stars, landforms and ecological markers — and investigate connections between these spatial reasoning systems and trigonometric principles
  • Investigate how bearings problems arise in orienteering course design and plan a multi-leg course specifying bearing and distance for each leg

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkConverting bearing descriptions to labelled right-angled triangles requires a systematic process that students find non-obvious. The translation from compass direction to triangle orientation needs explicit scaffolding.
