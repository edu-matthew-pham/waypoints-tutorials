Angles of elevation and depression

AC9M10M03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify angles of elevation and depression in a diagram or described situation and set up the correct right-angled triangle for calculation.

Lessons on this site

  • Looking up, looking down

Success criteria

  • Student correctly identifies and labels the angle of elevation or depression and the relevant sides in a diagram
  • Student selects and applies the correct trigonometric ratio to solve for an unknown side or angle

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise sine, cosine and tangent as constant ratios for a given angle using similarity (AC9M9SP01). They have not yet applied Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry to multi-step practical problems involving bearings, elevation and depression, or decomposition of 3D problems into 2D.

Width

Minimum
Identify the angle of elevation or depression in a diagram; label the relevant sides (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse) relative to the angle; select and apply the correct trigonometric ratio.
Typical
Solve problems involving angles of elevation and depression across multiple contexts (building heights, cliff distances, aircraft angles); draw and label diagrams from verbal descriptions; apply both sin, cos and tan as appropriate; use a clinometer context to connect measurement to calculation.
Wider
  • Investigate how clinometers are used in practical surveying and apply the technique to measure a building or tree height, comparing the measured angle to the calculated result
  • Explore how elevation and depression problems arise in navigation contexts and connect to angle of inclination in road design or ramp accessibility standards

Hinge

Students who cannot correctly set up diagrams for elevation/depression problems cannot decompose 3D problems in N3 — the 2D triangle extraction step in N3 requires confident diagram-drawing from N1.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents frequently confuse elevation and depression angles, especially when the observer is at height rather than ground level. Multiple problem setups — observer below, observer above, horizontal reference varying — stabilise the concept.
