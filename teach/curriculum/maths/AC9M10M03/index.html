Pythagoras and trigonometry in practical contexts

AC9M10M03 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

solve practical problems applying Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry of right-angled triangles, including problems involving direction and angles of elevation and depression

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise sine, cosine and tangent as constant ratios for a given angle using similarity (AC9M9SP01). They have not yet applied Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry to multi-step practical problems involving bearings, elevation and depression, or decomposition of 3D problems into 2D.

By the end

Students solve practical multi-step problems applying Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry of right-angled triangles, including problems involving direction, bearings, and angles of elevation and depression.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying right-angled trigonometry to solve navigation problems involving bearings
  • applying Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry to surveying and design, decomposing 3D problems into 2D
  • using a clinometer to measure angles of inclination and applying trigonometry to determine building heights
  • applying Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry with dynamic geometric software to model elevation and depression situations
  • investigating how autonomous vehicles use Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry to calculate distance and navigate
  • exploring navigation and surveying by First Nations Australians and connections to trigonometry

Waypoints

  • 1. Angles of elevation and depressionHingeStudents can identify angles of elevation and depression in a diagram or described situation and set up the correct right-angled triangle for calculation.
  • 2. Bearings and direction problemsStudents can interpret compass bearings and use trigonometry to solve navigation problems involving direction and distance.
  • 3. Decomposing 3D problems into 2DStudents can identify a right-angled triangle embedded in a 3D situation, extract the relevant 2D problem, and solve it using Pythagoras' theorem or trigonometry.
