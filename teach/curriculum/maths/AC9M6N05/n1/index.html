← AC9M6N05 Add and subtract fractions

Finding a common denominator for calculation

AC9M6N05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can find a common denominator for fractions with unrelated denominators and convert both fractions before adding or subtracting.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly finds a common denominator for two fractions with different denominators.
  • Student converts both fractions correctly and calculates the sum or difference.

Prior knowledge

Students can solve problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions with the same or related denominators using different strategies (AC9M5N05). They can compare and order fractions using equivalence (AC9M6N03). They have not yet added or subtracted fractions with unrelated denominators requiring a common denominator to be found.

Width

Minimum
Find a common denominator for two fractions with different denominators; convert both to equivalent fractions with the common denominator; perform the addition or subtraction and simplify the result.
Typical
Find common denominators for pairs of fractions across a range of denominator combinations; convert, calculate, and simplify results; select the lowest common denominator where possible for simpler calculations.
Wider
  • Explore why using the lowest common denominator reduces simplification effort — compare calculations using different common denominators for the same pair of fractions
  • Investigate adding three fractions with different denominators and develop an efficient multi-step approach

Hinge

Accurate common denominator finding and numerator conversion is the procedural foundation for Node 2 (mixed numerals) and Node 3 (problem contexts). Students who cannot reliably convert will produce wrong results in every subsequent fraction addition and subtraction task.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFinding common denominators for unrelated fractions is procedurally demanding and error-prone at this stage. Errors in denominator selection or numerator conversion cascade directly into incorrect sums. Fluency with this procedure is the prerequisite for problem-solving application.
