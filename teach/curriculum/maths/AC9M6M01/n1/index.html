← AC9M6M01 Convert metric units

Metric conversion as powers-of-10 operation

AC9M6M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that metric conversions involve multiplying or dividing by powers of 10 and apply this to convert between adjacent metric units.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly converts between adjacent metric units across at least two measurement types.
  • Student states the conversion factor and whether to multiply or divide for a given conversion.

Prior knowledge

Students can choose appropriate metric units when measuring length, mass and capacity (AC9M5M01). They can multiply and divide by powers of 10 (AC9M6N06). They have not yet systematically applied decimal representations in metric conversions or selected appropriate units for problem contexts.

Width

Minimum
Convert between adjacent metric units for length (km↔m, m↔cm, cm↔mm), mass (kg↔g), and capacity (L↔mL) by multiplying or dividing by 10, 100, or 1000; identify the conversion factor for each pair.
Typical
Convert between adjacent and non-adjacent metric units across length, mass and capacity; connect the conversion factor to the prefix system (kilo = ×1000, centi = ÷100, milli = ÷1000); apply powers-of-10 reasoning to conversion without memorising separate rules.
Wider
  • Investigate the SI metric prefix system beyond kilo and milli — mega, giga, micro, nano — and connect to scientific and technology contexts (gigabytes, nanometres)
  • Explore how metric units are defined in terms of each other and why the prefix system makes conversion simple compared to imperial units

Hinge

Reliable conversion across measurement types (not just length) is the prerequisite for Node 3 problem solving, which embeds conversion decisions in multi-attribute problems. Students who have only surface familiarity with length conversions will fail to identify or execute conversions for mass and capacity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often convert correctly within a familiar domain (length) but fail to transfer the same powers-of-10 logic to mass or capacity. Encountering all three measurement types with the same underlying reasoning builds a general conversion schema.
