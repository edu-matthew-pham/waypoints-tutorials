Intersections of functions

AC9M10A05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 10 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use digital tools to locate intersection points of two functions and identify coordinate values, including using zoom or bisection to refine approximations.

Success criteria

  • Student locates the intersection of two given functions graphically and states the coordinates
  • Student can refine an approximate intersection point using a zoom or bisection approach

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Making and testing conjectures with digital tools.

Width

Minimum
Locate the approximate intersection of two graphed functions using a digital tool; read off the coordinates.
Typical
Locate intersections of linear, quadratic and exponential function pairs; apply zoom or bisection methods to refine approximations to specified accuracy; identify intersections of a linear function with a quadratic or a circle; connect intersection points to the algebraic meaning (simultaneous solution).
Wider
  • Apply a bisection algorithm to systematically narrow an interval containing a root of a quadratic, connecting the numerical method to graphical behaviour and developing a general procedure
  • Investigate how intersections of functions are used to solve equations that cannot be solved by standard algebraic methods — appreciating digital tools as extending algebraic capability

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only find intersections of linear function pairs may not recognise the same process applies to quadratic-linear, exponential-linear, or circle-linear intersections. Multiple function pairings are needed to generalise the concept.
