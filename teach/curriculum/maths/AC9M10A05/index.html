Experiment with functions and relations

AC9M10A05 · Year 10 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

experiment with functions and relations using digital tools, making and testing conjectures and generalising emerging patterns

Prior knowledge

Students can use mathematical modelling with linear, quadratic or exponential models to solve growth and decay problems (AC9M10A04). They have not yet experimented systematically across the range of function and relation types at this year band — including circles, intersections of different function types, and bisection methods — using conjecture and generalisation as the primary inquiry mode.

By the end

Students experiment with functions and relations using digital tools, making and testing conjectures, and generalising emerging patterns across linear, quadratic, exponential and circular representations.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying the graphing zoom functionality and systematically refining intervals to identify approximate location of points of intersection
  • applying a bisection algorithm to determine the approximate location of horizontal axis intercepts of a quadratic
  • applying transformations to the graph of x² + y² = 1
  • identifying coordinates of intersection points of linear with quadratic or circular functions
  • identifying intervals where a given quadratic function is positive or negative
  • using a table of values to determine when an exponential growth or decay function exceeds or falls below a given value
  • investigating how functions and relations serve as the mathematical underpinnings of machine learning

Waypoints

  • 1. Making and testing conjectures with digital toolsHingeStudents can use a digital graphing tool to explore a function family, formulate a conjecture about an observed pattern, and test it by varying parameters.
  • 2. Intersections of functionsStudents can use digital tools to locate intersection points of two functions and identify coordinate values, including using zoom or bisection to refine approximations.
  • 3. Circles and transformations as relationsStudents can work with the unit circle x² + y² = 1 as a relation, apply transformations, and identify key features of the transformed relation.
  • 4. Generalising emerging patternsStudents can generalise a pattern observed across multiple function experiments into a stated rule or conjecture that applies across a family of cases.
