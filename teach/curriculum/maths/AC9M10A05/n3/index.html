Circles and transformations as relations

AC9M10A05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can work with the unit circle x² + y² = 1 as a relation, apply transformations, and identify key features of the transformed relation.

Lessons on this site

  • Circles as relations

Success criteria

  • Student correctly graphs the unit circle using a digital tool and explains why it is not a function
  • Student identifies the effect of a given transformation on the circle's centre and radius

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Intersections of functions.

Width

Minimum
Recognise x² + y² = 1 as a circle centred at the origin with radius 1; graph it using a digital tool; identify that it is a relation, not a function.
Typical
Apply transformations (translation, scaling) to x² + y² = 1; identify the centre and radius of the resulting circle; find intersection points of a circle with a linear function graphically; investigate intervals where a quadratic function is positive or negative.
Wider
  • Explore transformations of the unit circle algebraically, connecting the transformed equation to the geometric centre and radius, and generalising the standard form (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r²
  • Investigate the difference between functions and relations more broadly — why a circle is a relation but not a function, and how this distinction matters for analysis

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe function/relation distinction is a genuine conceptual hinge — students who have only worked with functions may not recognise that y = f(x) is not the only valid form of a mathematical relationship. The circle provides the canonical context for making this distinction clear.
