← AC9M3M01 Metric units and estimation

Naming metric units for everyday attributes

AC9M3M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can match metric units (mm, cm, m, km; mL, L; g, kg) to appropriate everyday measurement contexts.

Success criteria

  • Students can identify the appropriate metric unit for a given everyday measurement context.
  • Students can explain why a particular unit is appropriate for a given attribute.

Prior knowledge

Students can measure and compare objects based on length, capacity and mass using appropriate uniform informal units and smaller units for greater accuracy (AC9M2M01). They have not yet named or used metric units, or connected informal measurement to the metric system.

Width

Minimum
Sort six everyday items into groups by measurement attribute and name the appropriate metric unit for each attribute.
Typical
Examine packaging and real-world items; match measurement attributes (length, capacity, mass) to appropriate metric units; explain why a given unit is appropriate or inappropriate.
Wider
  • Investigate how metric units on product packaging represent different scales — e.g. comparing 500mL and 2L containers, 50g and 1kg packets
  • Explore the structure of the metric system — prefixes milli-, centi-, kilo- — as a pattern that applies across all measurement attributes

Hinge

Correct unit identification is the prerequisite for AC9M3M02 (measuring with instruments) — students who cannot match the attribute to the appropriate unit cannot select the right instrument or interpret the measurement result correctly.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly confuse units across attributes (e.g. using kilograms for length) or fail to distinguish between units within an attribute (cm vs m). Exposure across varied real-world items resolves this more effectively than explicit rules.
