← AC9M10M02 Logarithmic scales

Interpreting logarithmic scales in context

AC9M10M02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read and interpret data presented on a logarithmic scale in at least two real-world contexts, explaining what the scale reveals about the data.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly reads a value from a logarithmic scale graph and expresses it in appropriate units
  • Student explains why a logarithmic scale is more appropriate than a linear scale for a given data set

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Order of magnitude and the logarithmic idea.

Width

Minimum
Read and interpret a graph using a logarithmic scale (e.g. Richter scale, decibel scale); explain what a one-unit increase on the scale means in terms of the quantity measured.
Typical
Interpret logarithmic scales across multiple contexts (Richter, decibel, pH, exponential growth graphs); explain when a log scale is more appropriate than a linear scale (wide range of values, exponential growth, percentage change); compare the same data displayed on linear and log axes.
Wider
  • Investigate dating methods at geological sites significant to First Peoples of Australia, including the Madjedbebe dig — examining how logarithmic scales and scientific notation are used in radiometric dating and discussing the measurement accuracy and social significance of the findings
  • Investigate how logarithmic scaling is used in machine learning algorithms to compress large value ranges — connecting the mathematical concept to a contemporary data science application

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepLog scale interpretation is a genuine hinge — students who can read a Richter scale often cannot interpret a pH scale or a log-transformed growth graph because they treat each as a separate skill rather than the same mathematical structure. Multiple contexts stabilise the underlying concept.
