Logarithmic scales

AC9M10M02 · Year 10 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret and use logarithmic scales in applied contexts involving small and large quantities and change

Prior knowledge

Students can use mathematical modelling to solve proportion, rate, ratio and scale problems (AC9M9M05). They have not yet encountered logarithmic scales or the concept of order-of-magnitude calibration.

By the end

Students interpret and use logarithmic scales across applied contexts, explaining why a logarithmic scale is appropriate for certain data and reading values correctly from logarithmic representations.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • understanding that the logarithmic scale is calibrated in terms of order of magnitude
  • identifying and interpreting data representations that use logarithmic scales and discussing when appropriate vs not appropriate
  • investigating and interpreting logarithmic scales in real-world contexts (Richter, decibel, sensitivity scales, growth in investments, spread of micro-organisms)
  • investigating how logarithmic scaling can be used in machine learning algorithms to compress large values
  • investigating dating methods of geological sites including the Madjedbebe dig using logarithmic scales and measurement accuracy

Waypoints

  • 1. Order of magnitude and the logarithmic ideaHingeStudents can explain that a logarithmic scale is calibrated in orders of magnitude and describe how equal intervals on a log scale represent multiplicative (not additive) steps.
  • 2. Interpreting logarithmic scales in contextStudents can read and interpret data presented on a logarithmic scale in at least two real-world contexts, explaining what the scale reveals about the data.
  • 3. Using logarithmic scales for change and comparisonStudents can use logarithmic scales to compare quantities that span multiple orders of magnitude and interpret what differences between scale values mean.
