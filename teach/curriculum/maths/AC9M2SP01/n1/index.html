← AC9M2SP01 Classify shapes by sides

Spatial terms for shape features

AC9M2SP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 2 Maths · Space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use the terms straight, curved, parallel and opposite correctly to describe the sides and features of shapes.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies straight and curved sides in given shapes
  • Identifies a pair of parallel sides in a polygon and explains what makes them parallel

Prior knowledge

Students sort, compare and classify shapes by features using informal language (AC9M1SP01). They have not yet used specific spatial terms (parallel, opposite, curved, straight) or classified shapes by number of sides systematically.

Width

Minimum
Identify straight and curved sides on a given shape; identify one pair of parallel sides in a shape where they exist.
Typical
Use all four terms (straight, curved, parallel, opposite) to describe shape features; identify opposite sides and parallel sides in polygons; distinguish between shapes with and without parallel sides.
Wider
  • Investigate shapes of different sporting fields and label their features using spatial terms: sidelines, parallel lines, curved boundaries
  • Use digital tools to create and manipulate shapes; drag vertices and describe the effect on side properties using spatial language

Hinge

Nodes 2 and 3 both rely on students using spatial terms to describe classification properties and orientation-invariant features. Students who conflate 'parallel' with 'same length' or cannot identify curved vs straight sides cannot accurately classify shapes or explain why orientation doesn't change type.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify step'Parallel' is a new term at Year 2 and students commonly confuse it with 'the same length' or apply it only to horizontal lines. Multiple contexts — different shape orientations, real-world examples, digital tools — help establish the correct concept (same direction, never meeting) across varied representations.
