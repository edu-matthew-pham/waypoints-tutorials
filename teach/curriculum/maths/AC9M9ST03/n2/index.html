← AC9M9ST03 Comparative data distributions

Comparative representations

AC9M9ST03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can represent two or more data sets using the same comparative display type to allow direct comparison.

Lessons on this site

  • Two data sets, one scale

Success criteria

  • Constructs a correctly labelled comparative representation with aligned scales
  • Reads at least two summary statistics (e.g. median, range) directly from the representation
  • Identifies which data set has greater centre and greater spread from the display

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Describing distribution shape.

Width

Minimum
Construct a back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot or parallel dot plot for two data sets; ensure the scales are aligned for direct comparison.
Typical
Construct and interpret back-to-back stem-and-leaf plots, parallel box plots and comparative histograms; align scales correctly; read summary statistics directly from comparative representations.
Wider
  • Construct and compare two or more representations of the same data pair (e.g. back-to-back stem-and-leaf and parallel box plots) — discuss what each representation reveals and obscures
  • Explore comparative data from NIAA 'Closing the Gap' reports — construct appropriate comparative representations and discuss what they show
  • Investigate health trend data across demographic groups using grouped histograms and evaluate what the comparison reveals
