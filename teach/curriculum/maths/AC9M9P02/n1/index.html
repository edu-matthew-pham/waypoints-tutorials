Relative frequency as a probability estimate

AC9M9P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can calculate relative frequencies from data and explain how they estimate theoretical probability for compound events.

Lessons on this site

  • Records as probabilities

Success criteria

  • Correctly calculates relative frequency as (number of times event occurred) ÷ (total number of trials)
  • States that larger samples give more reliable relative frequency estimates
  • Uses a relative frequency to make a probability estimate for a future event

Prior knowledge

Students can list all outcomes for compound events with and without replacement and assign probabilities (AC9M9P01). They have not yet used relative frequencies from data to estimate probabilities involving compound logical connectives ('and', inclusive 'or', exclusive 'or'), or used Venn diagrams and two-way tables for this purpose.

Width

Minimum
Calculate relative frequencies for two events from a given data set; explain why a larger data set gives a more reliable probability estimate.
Typical
Calculate relative frequencies for a range of events from given or collected data; connect long-run relative frequency to theoretical probability; use relative frequencies to make probability estimates and predictions.
Wider
  • Investigate how large population data sets (e.g. census data, health surveys) are used to estimate population-level probabilities — discuss the role of sample size in reliability
  • Design and test an algorithm to calculate relative frequencies from a generated data set — evaluate reliability as the data set grows
  • Connect relative frequency to the frequentist interpretation of probability — contrast with classical (equally likely outcomes) probability
