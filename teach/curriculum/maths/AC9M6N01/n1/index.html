Meaning of integers

AC9M6N01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 6 Maths · Number

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what integers are, distinguish positive and negative integers from whole numbers, and give real-world examples of situations where negative integers are necessary.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least two real-world situations where negative integers are needed and explains what zero represents in each.
  • Student distinguishes between situations requiring only whole numbers and those requiring integers.

Prior knowledge

Students can express natural numbers as products of factors, recognise multiples and determine divisibility (AC9M5N02). They can use mathematical modelling to solve additive and multiplicative problems including financial contexts (AC9M5N09). They have not yet encountered negative integers or coordinate systems extending beyond positive values.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two real-world contexts where negative integers are used (e.g. temperature below zero, financial debt) and explain why negative numbers are needed in each.
Typical
Recognise integers across a range of contexts including temperature, altitude, financial situations, and time (before/after an event); explain what zero represents in each context and why positive/negative directions are meaningful.
Wider
  • Investigate how integers are used in scientific contexts such as sea level, electrical charge, or geologic time
  • Explore how different cultures and historical periods developed the concept of negative numbers and when these were formally accepted in mathematics

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often learn integers only in temperature contexts and then fail to recognise the integer structure in financial or directional settings. Broader real-world contexts early on prevent exemplar convergence on temperature as the only 'negative number' situation.
