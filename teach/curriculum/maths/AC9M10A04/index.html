Model growth and decay

AC9M10A04 · Year 10 Maths · Algebra

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving growth and decay, including financial contexts; formulate problems, choosing to apply linear, quadratic or exponential models; interpret solutions in terms of the situation; evaluate and modify models as necessary and report assumptions, methods and findings

Prior knowledge

Students can connect algebraic and graphical representations of exponential relations and solve exponential equations (AC9M10A03). They have not yet chosen between linear, quadratic and exponential models for a given applied context, nor evaluated and modified models, nor reported assumptions and findings in a full modelling cycle.

By the end

Students use mathematical modelling to solve applied growth and decay problems by selecting, applying, evaluating and reporting linear, quadratic or exponential models; interpret solutions in terms of the situation; and report assumptions, methods and findings.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • modelling situations and choosing between linear, quadratic and exponential models by representing relationships in a table of values and recognising constant first differences, constant second differences, and constant ratio
  • modelling situations involving exponential growth and decay; determining doubling time and half-life
  • modelling situations involving compound interest using authentic data and interest rates
  • modelling and investigating how exponential equations are used in carbon dating to estimate the age of First Nations Australians' artefacts or material culture
  • modelling and formulating situations involving population growth of native animals on Country/Place with varying reproductive behaviour, using exponential equations and critiquing their applicability

Waypoints

  • 1. Selecting the appropriate modelHingeStudents can determine whether a given growth or decay situation is best modelled as linear, quadratic or exponential by analysing the pattern of change.
  • 2. Formulating and applying the modelStudents can write an equation for a growth or decay model from a described context and use it to answer questions about the situation.
  • 3. Interpreting and evaluating the modelStudents can interpret modelled results in terms of the original situation and evaluate whether the model is appropriate, identifying key assumptions and limitations.
