Formulating linear models

AC9M8A03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Algebra · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students identify a linear relationship in a practical context, formulate it as a function y = mx + b, and identify the meaning of gradient and intercept in context.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies gradient and intercept from a practical context.
  • Formulates the linear function in equation form.
  • Interprets m and b in terms of the problem context.

Prior knowledge

Students can graph linear relations and solve linear equations (from AC9M8A02). Students have experience with mathematical modelling (from AC9M7N09).

Width

Minimum
Given a practical context with a constant rate and fixed component, formulate the linear function and identify m and b in context.
Typical
Formulate linear models for varied contexts (taxi fares, pay rates, motion); identify gradient as rate of change and intercept as fixed value; choose table, graph or equation as appropriate.
Wider
  • Model patterns on Country/Place with linear functions — investigate Indigenous knowledge connections to linear relationships and use the model as a predictive tool.
  • Compare two linear models on the same axes (e.g. two taxi companies) and find the break-even point algebraically.

Hinge

Node 2 requires solving and interpreting the model — students who cannot formulate the linear function from context have no model to solve, interpret, or review for appropriateness.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFormulating a linear model requires recognising the constant rate structure across varied contexts. Students who only practise with one context type fail to identify the linear structure in novel situations.
