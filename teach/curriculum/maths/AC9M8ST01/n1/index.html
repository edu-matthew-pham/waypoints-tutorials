← AC9M8ST01 Data collection techniques

Census vs sampling and collection technique selection

AC9M8ST01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 8 Maths · Statistics · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students distinguish census from sampling and identify when each is appropriate; describe the main data collection techniques.

Lessons on this site

  • Census, sample, experiment or observation

Success criteria

  • Correctly distinguishes census from sampling.
  • Identifies an appropriate collection technique for a given context and justifies the choice.
  • Explains at least one practical limitation of sampling.

Prior knowledge

Students can plan and conduct statistical investigations and identify variable types (from AC9M7ST03, AC9M7ST01). Students understand sampling informally.

Width

Minimum
State the difference between census and sampling; identify which is appropriate for a given situation and explain why.
Typical
Compare census, sampling, experiment and observation as data collection techniques; evaluate each for practicality, cost, and appropriateness in varied contexts.
Wider
  • Investigate how decisions concerning sampling relate to training AI systems — explain why biased sampling in training data leads to biased AI outputs.
  • Explore how digital measuring devices and simulations change the practicalities of data collection compared to manual methods.

Hinge

Students who cannot evaluate collection techniques for appropriateness cannot identify sources of bias in Node 2 — the bias evaluation depends on first understanding what the technique does and who it includes or excludes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepJudgment about appropriate data collection technique requires encountering a wide range of contexts — scientific experiments, surveys, social studies, AI training. Students who only consider surveys miss the full range.
