Angle relationships

AC9M6M04 · Year 6 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Identify the relationships between angles on a straight line, angles at a point and vertically opposite angles; use these to determine unknown angles, communicating reasoning

Prior knowledge

Students can estimate, construct and measure angles in degrees using a protractor (AC9M5M04). They understand acute, obtuse, straight, and reflex angles and can measure them. They have not yet identified geometric relationships between angles at a point or on a line, or used these to determine unknown angles.

By the end

Students can identify the relationships between angles on a straight line, at a point, and vertically opposite; use these to determine unknown angles; communicate their reasoning.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using protractors or dynamic geometry software to measure and generalise about angles formed when lines are crossed
  • demonstrating the meaning of language associated with angle properties including complementary, supplementary, vertically opposite
  • using the properties of supplementary and complementary angles to represent spatial situations with number sentences and solving for unknown angles

Waypoints

  • 1. Angles on a straight lineHingeStudents can identify that angles on a straight line sum to 180° and use this to find an unknown angle.
  • 2. Angles at a pointStudents can identify that angles at a point sum to 360° and use this to find unknown angles.
  • 3. Vertically opposite anglesHingeStudents can identify vertically opposite angles in a diagram and use the property that they are equal to find unknown angles.
  • 4. Combining angle relationships to solve problemsStudents can identify which angle relationships apply in a multi-step diagram and use a combination of relationships to find multiple unknown angles, communicating their reasoning.
