Relationships between time units

AC9M3M03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can state the relationships between days, hours, minutes and seconds and use these to convert between adjacent units.

Success criteria

  • Students can state the number of seconds in a minute, minutes in an hour, and hours in a day.
  • Students can convert a given duration between adjacent time units.

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and read time on an analog clock to the hour, half-hour and quarter-hour (AC9M2M04). They have not yet worked with the full hierarchy of time units (days, hours, minutes, seconds) or used these relationships to estimate and compare durations.

Width

Minimum
State the number of hours in a day, minutes in an hour, and seconds in a minute; convert two given time quantities (e.g. '3 hours = ? minutes').
Typical
Apply the full hierarchy of time unit relationships; convert across adjacent units in both directions; explain why the relationships have the values they do (history of 60-based time).
Wider
  • Explore why different cultures have used different time unit systems — e.g. ancient Egyptian hours, Indigenous seasonal calendars
  • Investigate how digital devices represent time and compare digital time displays with the unit relationships

Hinge

Time unit relationships are the prerequisite for Node 2 duration estimation and comparison — students who do not know that 1 hour = 60 minutes cannot estimate whether an event will take 'about 45 minutes' or convert between units when comparing durations. The non-decimal relationships are error-prone and must be secure before application.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTime unit relationships (60 seconds, 60 minutes, 24 hours) are non-decimal and must be memorised. Practice with varied conversion problems is more effective than broadening context here.
