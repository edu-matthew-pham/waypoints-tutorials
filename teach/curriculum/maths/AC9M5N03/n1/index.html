Equivalent fractions

AC9M5N03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 Maths · Number · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can generate equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same factor.

Success criteria

  • Student generates equivalent fractions correctly using multiplication and division
  • Student explains why two fractions are equivalent using numerator-denominator reasoning

Prior knowledge

Students can represent fractions as parts of a whole and on a number line, and identify equivalences (AC9M4N03, AC9M4N04). They have not yet compared fractions with related (not same) denominators, or worked with mixed numerals systematically.

Width

Minimum
Generate at least 3 equivalent fractions for a given fraction using multiplication or division; explain why they are equivalent.
Typical
Use factor knowledge to find equivalent fractions systematically; represent equivalences on fraction walls and number lines; simplify fractions using common factors.
Wider
  • Connect equivalent fractions to percentage and decimal representations
  • Use area models to justify equivalence visually

Hinge

Equivalent fraction generation is directly required by Node 2 — students who cannot convert between equivalent forms cannot identify related denominators or make the conversion needed to compare fractions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFluency with equivalence generation is the prerequisite skill for all comparison and addition of fractions. Students need it to be automatic before comparison tasks become tractable.
