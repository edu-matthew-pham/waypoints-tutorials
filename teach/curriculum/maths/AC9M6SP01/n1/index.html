What a cross-section is

AC9M6SP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Maths · Space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe a cross-section as a 2D slice through a 3D object and identify the shape of a cross-section from a given object.

Success criteria

  • Student describes a cross-section as a 2D slice through a 3D object.
  • Student correctly identifies the cross-sectional shape of at least two common objects.

Prior knowledge

Students can connect objects to their nets and build objects from nets using spatial and geometric reasoning (AC9M5SP01). They understand faces, edges, and vertices of 3D objects. They have not yet explored cross-sections as a tool for understanding 3D shapes, or identified the defining property of a right prism.

Width

Minimum
Describe what a cross-section is; identify the cross-sectional shape of at least four 3D objects (cube, rectangular prism, cylinder, triangular prism); sketch the cross-section.
Typical
Identify horizontal, vertical, and diagonal cross-sections of common 3D objects; draw cross-sections; describe what changes about the cross-section shape when the cutting angle changes; connect cross-sections to the faces of prisms.
Wider
  • Investigate cross-sections of food — cutting a capsicum, carrot, or orange at different angles — and sketch the resulting shapes
  • Explore CT scans and MRI imaging as real-world applications of cross-sectional thinking in medicine

Hinge

A robust concept of cross-section as a 2D slice is the prerequisite for Node 2, where constancy of cross-section is the defining property of a prism. Students with a fragile or incomplete cross-section concept cannot evaluate whether cross-sections are constant.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCross-section is a genuinely unfamiliar concept for most Year 6 students. Multiple physical contexts — food, construction materials, everyday objects — help students build a robust concept of 'slice' before the geometric formalisation.
