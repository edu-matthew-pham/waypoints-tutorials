Conditional probability

AC9M10P01 · Year 10 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use the language of "if ... then", "given", "of", "knowing that" to describe and interpret situations involving conditional probability

Prior knowledge

Students can design simulations comparing simple and compound event probabilities (AC9M9P03). They have not yet used the specific language of conditional probability ('given', 'if...then', 'knowing that') to describe or interpret situations, nor distinguished dependent from independent events using this language.

By the end

Students use the language of conditional probability correctly to describe and interpret situations involving dependent and independent events, using two-way tables, Venn diagrams, arrays and tree diagrams.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using two-way tables and Venn diagrams to understand conditional statements and identify common mistakes in interpreting conditional language
  • using arrays and tree diagrams to represent, interpret and compare probabilities of dependent and independent events
  • investigating how conditional probability is used in natural language processing tasks (text/image generation, translation, recommendation systems)

Waypoints

  • 1. The language of conditional probabilityHingeStudents can interpret statements using conditional probability language ('given', 'if...then', 'knowing that') and identify what information is being conditioned on.
  • 2. Representing conditional probabilityHingeStudents can use two-way tables, Venn diagrams and tree diagrams to represent situations involving conditional probability and read conditional probabilities from these representations.
  • 3. Dependent and independent eventsStudents can use conditional probability language and representations to determine whether two events are dependent or independent.
