Dependent and independent events

AC9M10P01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use conditional probability language and representations to determine whether two events are dependent or independent.

Lessons on this site

  • Independent or not?

Success criteria

  • Student correctly determines whether two events are dependent or independent by comparing P(A|B) and P(A)
  • Student explains in plain language what it means for two events to be independent in terms of conditional probability

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Representing conditional probability.

Width

Minimum
Calculate P(A|B) and P(A) for a given situation; determine whether the events are independent by checking whether conditioning on B changes the probability of A.
Typical
Use arrays and tree diagrams to represent dependent and independent event probabilities; compare probabilities of dependent and independent events; identify whether conditioning changes the probability (dependent) or not (independent); connect independence to the language 'knowing that B has occurred does not change the probability of A'.
Wider
  • Investigate how conditional probability is used in recommendation systems: 'given that a user watched X, what is the probability they will watch Y?' — and discuss whether the events are truly independent or dependent
  • Explore real-world cases where events that appear independent are actually dependent (e.g. disease co-occurrence, correlated financial risks) and discuss the consequences of incorrectly assuming independence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe distinction between dependent and independent events requires checking in multiple contexts — students who only practise with card draws may assume independence is rare. Varied contexts (medical, technological, financial) build reliable dependence-testing.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates conditional language (node 1) and representations (node 2) into the full concept of conditional probability: what it means for one event's probability to depend on another. It connects the linguistic, graphical and numerical aspects into coherent understanding.
