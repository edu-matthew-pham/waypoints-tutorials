← AC9MFM02 Days and times of day

Connecting events to days and times

AC9MFM02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can connect familiar events and actions to specific days of the week and times of day, using temporal language to describe and justify sequences.

Success criteria

  • Student connects at least three familiar events to the correct day or time of day and justifies at least one placement using temporal language
  • Student correctly uses at least two temporal words (before, after, yesterday, today, tomorrow, next) in context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Sequencing days of the week.

Width

Minimum
Connect at least three familiar events to the correct day and time of day, and use at least one temporal word (before, after, yesterday, tomorrow) to describe the sequence.
Typical
Connect familiar school and home events to specific days and times of day; use temporal language (before, after, next, yesterday, today, tomorrow) to describe and justify sequences; create or interpret a pictorial weekly diary or roster.
Wider
  • Create and interpret a classroom roster connecting tasks to specific days, and answer questions like 'Who watered the garden yesterday?' or 'Whose turn is it today?'
  • Explore how different families and communities organise their week — including cultural or community events on specific days — and connect these to the days-and-times framework
  • Investigate events that happen at the same time each day (routines) and events that happen on the same day each week, distinguishing daily from weekly patterns

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkConnecting events to days and times requires students to use temporal language as a reasoning tool — not just label an event, but justify why it goes where it does ('It happens in the morning because we do it when we arrive at school'). The reasoning scaffold distinguishes this node from simple recall.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates times of day (node 1) and day sequence (node 2) into a unified temporal framework. Students must coordinate knowledge of the daily cycle and the weekly cycle to place events correctly — this coordination is the endpoint demand.
