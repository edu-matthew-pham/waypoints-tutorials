← AC9M7M02 Volume of right prisms

Volume as cross-section area times height

AC9M7M02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 7 Maths · Measurement · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students understand that the volume of any right prism equals the area of its cross-section multiplied by its height.

Lessons on this site

  • Volume in layers

Success criteria

  • States that V = Ah for right prisms and explains what A and h represent.
  • Correctly identifies the cross-section and its area for a given prism.
  • Does not confuse the total surface area with the cross-sectional area.

Prior knowledge

Students can calculate area of triangles, rectangles and parallelograms (from AC9M7M01, AC9M6M02). Students understand volume as the measure of 3D space and have worked with rectangular prisms using unit cubes.

Width

Minimum
Explain and demonstrate why V = Ah for a rectangular prism using unit cubes; state that the same principle applies for triangular prisms.
Typical
Connect the area of the base (cross-section) to the volume; explain why the cross-section must be consistent (uniform) throughout the height.
Wider
  • Build rectangular prisms from unit cubes and verify V = l × w × h = Ah for various dimensions.
  • Explore why V = Ah only holds for prisms with uniform cross-section — investigate what changes for a pyramid.

Hinge

Students who don't generalise beyond V = lwh cannot calculate the volume of triangular prisms in Node 2 — they lack the cross-section principle that makes V = Ah work for any right prism.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who learn V = lwh as a formula for rectangular prisms only often fail to generalise to triangular prisms. The cross-section principle requires reinforcement across multiple prism types.
