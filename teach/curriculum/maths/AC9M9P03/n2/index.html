← AC9M9P03 Chance experiments and simulations

Conducting the experiment and comparing probabilities

AC9M9P03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can conduct a repeated chance experiment or simulation using digital tools, compare results for simple and compound events, and describe results.

Success criteria

  • Conducts the simulation with a sufficient number of trials (at least 50) using a digital tool
  • Correctly calculates relative frequencies for both the simple and compound events
  • Compares the two results and describes what the comparison reveals

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Designing a chance experiment or simulation.

Width

Minimum
Conduct a simulation with at least 50 trials using a digital tool; calculate the relative frequency of a simple event and one related compound event; compare the two results.
Typical
Conduct repeated simulations with sufficient trials to see convergence; compare probabilities of simple and compound events (with and without replacement); describe results including what the simulation reveals about the relationship between simple and compound probabilities; compare experimental and theoretical probabilities.
Wider
  • Use repeated trials of First Nations Australian children's instructive games (e.g. Gorri) to calculate probabilities of winning and not winning — compare experimental results from simulation to theoretical predictions derived from systematic listing
  • Conduct two versions of the same experiment (with and without replacement) using digital tools and compare the effect on compound event probabilities
  • Investigate how increasing the number of trials affects the closeness of experimental to theoretical probability — connect to the law of large numbers conceptually

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly interpret small-sample simulation results as definitive (confusing experimental variability with theoretical probability). Running simulations under different conditions — varying trial counts, with/without replacement — builds understanding of when and why experimental results diverge from theoretical expectations.
