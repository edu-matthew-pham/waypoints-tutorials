Designing a chance experiment or simulation

AC9M9P03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can design a chance experiment or simulation that will generate data to compare the probability of a simple event to a related compound event.

Lessons on this site

  • Designing a simulation

Success criteria

  • Specifies the outcome space, what is recorded, and the number of trials
  • Correctly models the probability structure (with/without replacement, equal/unequal probabilities)
  • Explains how the simulation replicates the real event

Prior knowledge

Students can calculate relative frequencies from data to estimate probabilities of compound events involving 'and', inclusive 'or' and exclusive 'or' (AC9M9P02). They have not yet designed and conducted their own simulations using digital tools to compare simple and compound event probabilities.

Width

Minimum
Design one simulation (manual or digital) to investigate a compound probability question; state what data will be recorded and how the simulation replicates the real event.
Typical
Design experiments and simulations for a range of compound probability questions; specify the number of trials; identify what data to collect; ensure the simulation correctly models the probability structure (correct outcome space, with/without replacement as appropriate).
Wider
  • Design a simulation to investigate a counterintuitive probability result (e.g. probability that at least two people in a group share a birthday for small group sizes) and explain the design rationale
  • Investigate how real-world simulations are used in risk modelling and forecasting — discuss what makes a simulation a reliable model
  • Compare manual (physical) and digital simulation approaches — discuss the advantages of digital tools for large numbers of trials

Related standards
