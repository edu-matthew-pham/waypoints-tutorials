← AC9M9P03 Chance experiments and simulations

Describing and evaluating results

AC9M9P03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 Maths · Probability

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe simulation results, compare experimental to theoretical probabilities, and explain any discrepancies.

Success criteria

  • States the experimental probability and the corresponding theoretical probability
  • Explains why the two values are unlikely to be identical for a finite number of trials
  • Identifies what aspect of the design or trial count could be changed to reduce the discrepancy

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Conducting the experiment and comparing probabilities.

Width

Minimum
Compare one experimental (simulation) probability to the corresponding theoretical probability; explain one reason why the results might differ.
Typical
Compare experimental probabilities from simulation to theoretical probabilities; describe the relationship between simple and compound event probabilities revealed by the simulation; explain why experimental and theoretical results differ, and what would reduce the discrepancy.
Wider
  • Evaluate the reliability of simulation results across different trial counts — present results from 10, 50, 200 and 1000 trials and discuss convergence
  • Discuss and evaluate the use of simulation in real-world contexts (e.g. insurance risk modelling, weather forecasting) — assess what makes a simulation trustworthy
  • Connect simulation results to the design of fair games and discuss what probability results reveal about whether a game is fair

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDescribing and evaluating results integrates the full investigation cycle: design, conduct, compare and explain. The integration challenge is connecting what the simulation shows to what it means probabilistically — moving from 'I got 0.48' to 'this is consistent with a theoretical probability of 0.5 because...' This is the descriptor's full demand.
