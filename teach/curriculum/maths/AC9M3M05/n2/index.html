Comparing angles with right angles

AC9M3M05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 Maths · Measurement

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can classify everyday angles as equal to, greater than, or less than a right angle.

Success criteria

  • Students can classify an angle as less than, equal to, or greater than a right angle.
  • Students can identify right angles in everyday environments using a right angle tester.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Angle as a measure of turn.

Width

Minimum
Identify three everyday angles (doors, corners, open books) as greater than, less than, or equal to a right angle; justify each classification.
Typical
Compare a range of everyday angles with a right angle benchmark using a right angle tester; classify as acute (less than), right (equal to) or obtuse (greater than).
Wider
  • Explore how right angles are used in construction and design — identifying right angles in building structures and why they are important
  • Introduce informal angle language (acute, obtuse) as natural extensions of the 'less than right angle / greater than right angle' description
