Interpret and follow directions

AC9M1SP02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 Maths · Space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret and follow a sequence of directional instructions using forwards, backwards, left, right and straight ahead to reach a stated location.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Follows a multi-step direction sequence correctly, reaching the target location.
  • Demonstrates correct understanding of left/right relative to own orientation.

Prior knowledge

Students can describe position and location of themselves and objects within a familiar space (AC9MFSP02). They have not yet given or followed multi-step directional instructions, created algorithms for movement, or used ordinal and positional language to direct others.

Width

Minimum
Follow a three-step sequence of directions using at least two directional terms to reach a target location.
Typical
Interpret and follow multi-step directions using forwards, backwards, straight ahead, left, right; understand that left/right are relative to the mover's orientation; follow directions given by a peer or teacher; reach target locations reliably.
Wider
  • Follow directions that describe a familiar journey using landmark-based language from a First Nations perspective, recognising how directional language is shaped by relationship to Country/Place.
  • Program a floor robot (Bee-Bot or equivalent) by entering a direction sequence, testing it and debugging when the robot doesn't reach the target.

Hinge

Reliable left/right orientation is a prerequisite for Node 2 (giving directions) and Node 3 (creating algorithms) — students who confuse their own left/right give systematically incorrect directions and produce algorithms that fail when tested.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsLeft/right confusion is extremely common at Year 1 and requires targeted practice. Students need repeated experience with left/right from their own perspective — and from another person facing them — before they can reliably follow peer-given directions.
