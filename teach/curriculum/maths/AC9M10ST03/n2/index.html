Describing association

AC9M10ST03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the association between two variables in terms of strength (strong, moderate, weak), direction (positive, negative, none) and linearity (linear, non-linear).

Lessons on this site

  • Describing association

Success criteria

  • Student correctly describes a scatterplot's association using strength, direction and linearity with appropriate vocabulary
  • Student explains what the direction of association means in the specific context of the data

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Constructing and reading scatterplots.

Width

Minimum
Describe the association shown in a given scatterplot using strength, direction and linearity vocabulary.
Typical
Describe association across multiple scatterplots in varied contexts; use precise vocabulary (strong positive linear, weak negative, no association, non-linear); explain what positive and negative association means in context; use a line of good fit by eye to assess linearity informally.
Wider
  • Investigate AI systems that analyse bivariate data to forecast or make predictions using correlation analysis — discussing limitations including inability to capture causality or account for contextual factors
  • Explore how the same data can appear to show different associations depending on scale choices, and discuss how this connects to the misleading representations work of AC9M10ST01

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAssociation description vocabulary (strength, direction, linearity) needs to be applied across varied contexts for it to become reliable. Students who only describe association in one domain (e.g. physical science data) may lack the language flexibility for social, biological or economic scatterplots.
