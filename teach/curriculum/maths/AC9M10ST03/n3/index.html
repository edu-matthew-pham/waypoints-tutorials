Association vs causation

AC9M10ST03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 10 Maths · Statistics

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Maths: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between association and causation and give examples of associated variables that are not causally related.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly explains the distinction between association and causation
  • Student gives a plausible alternative explanation (e.g. confounding variable or common cause) for a described association

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Describing association.

Width

Minimum
State the distinction between association and causation; give one example of two variables that are associated but where causation is not established.
Typical
Explain why association does not imply causation; identify confounding variables that might explain an association; discuss multiple plausible explanations for an observed association (direct causation, reverse causation, common cause, coincidence); apply this reasoning to contexts such as health, climate and social media.
Wider
  • Investigate the relationship between two variables of spear throwers used by First Peoples of Australia — construct and describe the scatterplot, make comparisons and draw conclusions within the limits of the data
  • Analyse a specific claimed association in the media (e.g. social media use and wellbeing, climate variables) using statistical evidence — making, justifying and critiquing claims about association vs causation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAssociation vs causation is a genuine conceptual hinge — students who understand it in one context frequently revert to causal language in new contexts. Multiple examples from health, social science, climate and technology are needed to stabilise the distinction.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe causation discussion integrates scatterplot construction (node 1), association description (node 2), and statistical reasoning about evidence into the full critical analysis of bivariate relationships. It is the endpoint of this descriptor's learning progression.
