Construct evidence-based arguments

AC9S7I07 · Years 7–8 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

construct evidence-based arguments to support conclusions or evaluate claims and consider any ethical issues and cultural protocols associated with using or citing secondary data or information

Prior knowledge

Students can compare methods, recognise sources of error and select evidence to draw reasoned conclusions (AC9S5I05). They have not yet constructed arguments with explicit evidence-reasoning structure or considered ethical issues around using secondary data.

By the end

Students construct evidence-based arguments to support conclusions or evaluate claims, considering ethical issues and cultural protocols associated with using or citing secondary data or information.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • constructing an argument supported by evidence and reasoning to support or reject a hypothesis
  • drawing a logical conclusion in consideration of the method of data collection, quality of evidence and limitations or significance of a claim
  • exploring how to determine credibility of a source
  • investigating the cultural, historical and archaeological evidence used in the scientific debate about the role of early First Nations Australians in the extinction of Australian megafauna
  • researching the development of commercial products that are founded on the traditional knowledges and practices of First Nations Australians and discussing related ethical considerations associated with biopiracy and intellectual property rights

Waypoints

  • 1. Drawing conclusions from evidenceStudents can draw a conclusion from evidence that supports or rejects a hypothesis and identify the evidence used
  • 2. Constructing evidence-based argumentsStudents can construct an argument supported by evidence and reasoning to support a conclusion or evaluate a claim
  • 3. Ethics of using secondary dataStudents can consider ethical issues and cultural protocols associated with using or citing secondary data or information
