Earth's internal structure — layers, states and the lithosphere

AC9S8U03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe Earth's layered internal structure, identify the state and composition of each layer, and explain that the lithosphere is the rigid outer layer.

Lessons on this site

  • What's inside the Earth?

Success criteria

  • Names Earth's main internal layers and describes the state (solid/liquid) of each
  • Explains that the lithosphere is the rigid outer layer (crust + upper mantle)
  • States that temperature and pressure increase with depth

Prior knowledge

Students can describe observable properties of rocks and minerals (AC9S3U02) and explain how weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition change Earth's surface (AC9S5U02). Prior learning is entirely surface-level — there is no curriculum treatment of Earth's interior at any prior year level. Earth-Sun-Moon cycles (AC9S7U03) address celestial geometry, not geological interior. Students have no prior exposure to Earth's internal structure, plates, or tectonic processes.

Width

Minimum
Students name Earth's main layers, state whether each is solid or liquid, and identify the lithosphere as the rigid outer layer.
Typical
Students describe crust (thin, solid, oceanic vs continental), mantle (mostly solid but flows slowly over geological time, convection occurs in upper mantle), outer core (liquid iron/nickel), inner core (solid); explain that temperature and pressure increase with depth; describe the lithosphere as crust plus rigid upper mantle; explain that Australia's ancient craton reflects a long history of tectonic stability.
Wider
  • Investigate how we know about Earth's interior without direct observation — what does seismic wave behaviour reveal about layer boundaries and states?
  • Compare the relative thickness of the crust to Earth's radius using a scale model — why is the lithosphere described as like the skin of an apple?

Hinge

The lithosphere concept is the prerequisite for the plate model in N2. Students who do not understand that there is a rigid outer layer sitting on a semi-fluid asthenosphere cannot grasp why plates can move independently, or why they behave as they do at boundaries in N3–N5.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents have no prior mental model of Earth's interior. The layered structure with varying states — particularly the mantle as solid yet able to flow over geological time — is counterintuitive. This node must build the interior model explicitly before any tectonic mechanism is introduced.
