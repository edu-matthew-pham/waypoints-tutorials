Tectonic plates and the driving mechanism

AC9S8U03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that the lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates and describe mantle convection as the mechanism driving plate movement.

Lessons on this site

  • What moves the plates?

Success criteria

  • Explains that the lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates that move
  • Describes mantle convection as the mechanism driving plate movement
  • States that plates move at rates of centimetres per year

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Earth's internal structure — layers, states and the lithosphere.

Width

Minimum
Students state that the lithosphere is divided into moving plates and identify convection in the mantle as the driving mechanism.
Typical
Students describe plates as large rigid sections of lithosphere; explain that heat from Earth's core drives convection currents in the mantle; describe how convection creates slab pull and ridge push forces; explain that plates move at rates of centimetres per year (fingernail growth rate); describe the major plates; connect the extreme age and stability of the Australian craton to its position away from active plate boundaries.
Wider
  • Investigate the relative significance of slab pull, ridge push and mantle convection — which force dominates and what evidence supports this?
  • Research First Nations Australians' cultural accounts of earthquakes and volcanic events — what geological record do these accounts preserve?

Hinge

The plate-as-moving-unit concept is the structural prerequisite for all three boundary types. Students who do not understand that plates are distinct rigid units that move relative to each other cannot explain why different boundary types produce different features — the feature depends entirely on the direction of relative plate motion.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe plate model requires students to reconceptualise Earth's surface from a fixed landscape to a dynamic system of moving pieces. The convection mechanism must be established before boundary types — without a driving mechanism, plate movement appears arbitrary and boundary feature formation cannot be explained causally.
