Science collaboration and building on others' work

AC9S5H01 · Years 5–6 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

examine why advances in science are often the result of collaboration or build on the work of others

Prior knowledge

Students can examine how data is used to develop scientific explanations (AC9S3H01, 3-4 band). They have not yet examined the collaborative and cumulative nature of science — the idea that advances typically require multiple people, disciplines and prior discoveries.

By the end

Students examine real examples of scientific advances that required collaboration across disciplines, institutions or cultures, or that built on the prior work of others — explaining why scientific knowledge advances more effectively through collective effort than individual work alone.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • researching how the recent discovery of a biofluorescent flying squirrel led to discoveries of more fluorescent mammals, such as wombats, bilbies, echidna and bandicoots as scientists collaborated with other scientists across fields of science and internationally
  • researching why European naturalists and scientists first thought the platypus was a faked animal, and how scientists such as those in the Platypus Conservation Initiative are collaborating in ongoing research to understand the features and behaviours of platypuses
  • investigating how contemporary soil erosion management practices adapt and build on First Nations Australians' fire management and agricultural practices
  • exploring why developing new erosion mitigation techniques such as contour banks and strip cropping requires geologists, hydrologists and farmers to collaborate
  • exploring how understanding of light and optics has developed by comparing the ideas of Plato, Euclid, Ptolemy, Ibn al-Haytham and Roger Bacon

Waypoints

  • 1. Scientific advances build on prior workHingeStudents can describe at least one example where a scientific advance built on the prior work of others, explaining what earlier knowledge was required and how the new discovery extended or depended on it.
  • 2. Collaboration across disciplines and institutions advances scienceStudents can explain why complex scientific questions often require collaboration across disciplines, institutions or countries, and describe at least one example where such collaboration produced an advance.
