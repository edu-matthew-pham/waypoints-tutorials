← AC9S3U03 Heat energy and transfer

Materials differ in how well they conduct or insulate heat

AC9S3U03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 3 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that some materials (conductors) transfer heat quickly and others (insulators) transfer heat slowly, give examples of each, and connect material selection to everyday uses for keeping things hot or cold.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two conductors and two insulators with examples
  • Explains the difference between a conductor and an insulator in terms of how quickly heat transfers
  • Gives at least one everyday example where conductor or insulator properties determine material selection

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Heat transfers from warmer to cooler objects.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least two conductors and two insulators, explain the difference between them, and give one everyday example of each being used for its heat transfer properties.
Typical
Students investigate heat transfer through different materials (metals, plastics, ceramics, fabrics), classify them as better or worse conductors using evidence, explain why conductors and insulators are selected for specific uses, and connect material properties to everyday objects (saucepan handles, oven mitts, thermos flasks, building insulation).
Wider
  • Explore how First Nations Australians developed clothing from animal skins (possum fur cloaks, kangaroo skin) that trap heat close to the body — examining how insulation properties guided material selection
  • Investigate how engineers test insulation properties of materials and how this information is used to design food packaging, building insulation or cold-weather clothing (AC9S3H02)
  • Plan and conduct a fair test to determine which material is the best insulator for keeping a substance cold — applying conductor/insulator knowledge to an investigation (AC9S3I02)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly know metals are conductors and fabrics are insulators without understanding why different materials are selected in different contexts. Encountering conductor/insulator properties across varied everyday contexts — cooking, building, clothing, food storage — helps students develop a more flexible understanding of how material properties determine use.
