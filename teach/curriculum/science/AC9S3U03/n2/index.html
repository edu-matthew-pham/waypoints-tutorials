← AC9S3U03 Heat energy and transfer

Heat transfers from warmer to cooler objects

AC9S3U03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 3 Science · Physical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that heat energy transfers from a warmer object to a cooler object when they are in contact, describe what happens to the temperature of each object during the transfer, and use temperature measurements to support their explanation.

Success criteria

  • Explains that heat transfers from warmer to cooler objects, not from cold to warm
  • Describes what happens to the temperature of both objects during heat transfer (warm cools, cool warms) until they reach the same temperature
  • Uses thermometer observations to support the explanation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Heat energy comes from identifiable sources.

Width

Minimum
Students describe what happens to the temperature of a warm and a cold object when they are placed in contact, and explain the direction of heat transfer using observations.
Typical
Students observe and measure temperature change when objects of different temperatures are placed in contact, explain that heat flows from warmer to cooler until temperatures equalise, and use thermometer readings to support their explanation.
Wider
  • Investigate how 18th-century physicists Jean Ingenhousz and Benjamin Thompson collected temperature data to determine best conductors and insulators — connecting systematic observation to scientific explanation (AC9S3H01)
  • Model heat transfer using drawings, role-play or arrows to show the direction of energy movement — applying a representation to make the invisible visible
  • Explore how heat transfer from warm ocean water to cooler air drives weather patterns — extending the object-to-object transfer concept to environmental scale

Hinge

The direction of heat transfer — warm to cool, never the reverse — is the conceptual prerequisite for understanding conduction and insulation in Node 3. Students holding the 'cold flows in' misconception will systematically misexplain why insulators work and produce inverted reasoning about conductor/insulator selection.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly believe cold 'flows into' a warm object (a cold drink makes your hand cold) rather than heat flowing from the warm hand to the cold drink. Making the direction of transfer explicit — heat always moves from warmer to cooler, never the reverse — and supporting this with thermometer evidence is the key conceptual scaffold before students can understand insulation.
