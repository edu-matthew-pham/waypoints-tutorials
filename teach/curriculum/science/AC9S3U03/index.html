Heat energy and transfer

AC9S3U03 · Year 3 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify sources of heat energy and examine how temperature changes when heat energy is transferred from one object to another

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how objects move and respond to pushes and pulls (1-2 band). They have informally experienced heat and cold but have not yet identified heat as a form of energy, distinguished temperature from heat, or examined heat transfer between objects systematically.

By the end

Students identify sources of heat energy in everyday and natural contexts, explain that heat energy transfers from warmer to cooler objects, use thermometers to measure temperature change, and identify how materials vary in their ability to conduct or insulate heat.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring how we sense heat and identifying sources of heat such as the sun, fire, electrical devices and geothermal springs
  • recognising that changes in heat energy can be measured using a thermometer
  • observing and, with assistance, measuring, what happens when a cold object is placed in direct contact with a warm object and proposing explanations
  • modelling the movement of heat from one object to another using drawing or role-play
  • investigating how well heat is transferred by different types of materials such as metals, plastics and ceramics and identifying how materials are used to keep things hot and cold
  • exploring how First Nations Australians developed clothing from animal skins such as possum furs and kangaroo skin cloaks that trap heat close to the body to stay warm

Waypoints

  • 1. Heat energy comes from identifiable sourcesStudents can identify and classify sources of heat energy in everyday and natural contexts (sun, fire, electrical devices, geothermal, friction, body heat) and explain that heat is a form of energy.
  • 2. Heat transfers from warmer to cooler objectsHingeStudents can explain that heat energy transfers from a warmer object to a cooler object when they are in contact, describe what happens to the temperature of each object during the transfer, and use temperature measurements to support their explanation.
  • 3. Materials differ in how well they conduct or insulate heatStudents can explain that some materials (conductors) transfer heat quickly and others (insulators) transfer heat slowly, give examples of each, and connect material selection to everyday uses for keeping things hot or cold.
