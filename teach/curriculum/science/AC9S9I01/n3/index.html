← AC9S9I01 Investigable questions, predictions and hypotheses

Hypotheses: testable, refutable, relationship-specifying

AC9S9I01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can write a formal hypothesis that specifies the predicted relationship between variables in a form that can be supported or refuted through evidence, and explain why scientific hypotheses must be refutable.

Success criteria

  • Can write a hypothesis that specifies the relationship between named variables.
  • Can identify what result would refute their hypothesis.
  • Does not write vague or circular hypotheses — the prediction must be specific enough to test.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Reasoned predictions.

Width

Minimum
Write a hypothesis for one investigation using an if-then-because structure (or equivalent), specifying the independent variable, predicted effect on dependent variable, and a reason. Explain why the hypothesis must be refutable — what result would refute it.
Typical
Write hypotheses for two or three investigations and evaluate each for refutability — identifying what evidence would support it and what evidence would refute it. Explain why a hypothesis that cannot be refuted is not scientifically useful.
Wider
  • Examine the concept of a null hypothesis — the prediction that there is no effect — and explain how it is used alongside the experimental hypothesis in formal scientific investigations.
  • Develop hypotheses to explore an explanatory model (e.g. 'If heat energy transfers from higher to lower temperature until equilibrium is reached, then placing objects of different temperatures in contact will result in...') — connecting hypothesis writing to model testing.

Hinge

Students who write unfalsifiable hypotheses (e.g. 'plants will grow differently') cannot use investigation results to either support or refute their hypothesis — the conclusion stage of the investigation collapses. Refutability is the hinge: a hypothesis that cannot be tested cannot be advanced or rejected, and the entire investigation loses its logical structure.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of refutability is philosophically non-trivial. Scaffold the argument: 'If no possible result could prove your hypothesis wrong, then your hypothesis doesn't actually predict anything testable — it's not a scientific hypothesis.' Concrete counter-examples (e.g. 'plants respond to their environment' — true but untestable) make this clearer than abstract explanation.
