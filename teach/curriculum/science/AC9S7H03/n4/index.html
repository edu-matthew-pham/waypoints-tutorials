← AC9S7H03 Science responses impact society

Evaluating scientific responses with multiple considerations

AC9S7H03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 7–8 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine a proposed scientific response by weighing multiple considerations and explaining why stakeholders may reach different conclusions

Success criteria

  • Examines a scientific response using at least two consideration types
  • Explains why different stakeholders evaluate the response differently
  • Connects stakeholder conclusions to their priorities rather than framing disagreement as ignorance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Ethical, environmental, social and economic considerations.

Width

Minimum
Examine one proposed scientific response using at least two consideration types; explain why different stakeholders may evaluate it differently
Typical
Compare how two different stakeholder groups evaluate the same scientific response; explain how their different priorities lead to different conclusions
Wider
  • Evaluate a proposed ecological reserve by analysing competing interests — conservation scientists, landowners, First Nations communities, local government — and explain what each prioritises
  • Analyse the development of laboratory-grown meat from the perspective of farmers, environmentalists, consumers and ethicists
  • Construct an evidence-based argument for or against a proposed scientific response, explicitly addressing counter-arguments from stakeholders with different priorities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the response identification (Node 1), trade-off analysis (Node 2), and four-lens framework (Node 3) into a coordinated stakeholder evaluation. Students who can apply each element separately but cannot coordinate them into a multi-stakeholder analysis have not met the descriptor's full evaluative demand.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe argument-construction direction — arguing for or against a response while addressing counter-arguments — requires explicit scaffolding of the claim-evidence-counterargument structure. Students need support both in selecting and weighting considerations and in representing opposing stakeholder positions fairly rather than as straw men.
