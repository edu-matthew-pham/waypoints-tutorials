Scientific responses to contemporary issues

AC9S7H03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 7–8 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify proposed scientific responses to contemporary issues and describe the intended impact

Success criteria

  • Identifies a proposed scientific response and the contemporary issue it addresses
  • Describes the intended impact of the response
  • Distinguishes the scientific response from a political or social response

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate how individuals and communities use scientific knowledge to identify problems and make decisions (AC9S5H02). They have not yet examined how proposed scientific responses to issues carry ethical, environmental, social and economic trade-offs.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of a proposed scientific response to a contemporary issue; describe the problem it addresses and the intended impact
Typical
Compare two proposed scientific responses to the same or related issues; explain how each addresses the problem differently
Wider
  • Investigate how laboratory-grown meat is proposed as a response to ecosystem impacts of livestock farming
  • Examine how desalination plants are proposed as a response to freshwater scarcity
  • Research how ecological reserves are proposed as a response to biodiversity loss and competing land use interests

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a repertoire of scientific response examples across different issue types — food production, water scarcity, biodiversity — to be able to reason about impacts across contexts in later nodes. A single example limits the range of consideration types students can encounter; multiple examples build the conceptual library needed for the four-lens analysis in Node 3.
