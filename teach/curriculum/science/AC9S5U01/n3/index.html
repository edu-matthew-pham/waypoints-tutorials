← AC9S5U01 Structural features and survival

Features and behaviours match the challenges of specific habitats

AC9S5U01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 5 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can connect the structural features and behaviours of an organism to the specific physical conditions and challenges of its habitat — explaining why this organism is well-suited to this habitat and less suited to a different one.

Success criteria

  • Connects at least two features or behaviours to specific physical conditions of a named habitat
  • Predicts at least one challenge an organism would face if placed in a different habitat, with reasoning

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Behaviours also enable survival in specific habitats.

Width

Minimum
Students explain why a named organism is well-suited to its specific habitat by connecting at least two features or behaviours to the physical conditions of that habitat.
Typical
Students compare two organisms from different habitats, explain how each is suited to its own habitat's conditions, and predict what challenges each would face if placed in the other's habitat — using this to demonstrate that adaptation is habitat-specific, not universally 'better'.
Wider
  • Pose and investigate questions about survival: 'Do all animals living in desert habitats have ways to survive without water?' — examining whether habitat challenges predict adaptation patterns
  • Examine how decisions are made to farm particular crops or animals based on local habitat conditions — connecting biological adaptation knowledge to agricultural decision-making (AC9S5H02)
  • Research how introduced species that lack local adaptations affect native species — examining what happens when organism-habitat matching breaks down

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to integrate structural feature knowledge, behavioural adaptation knowledge and habitat condition knowledge in a single explanatory account. Students must coordinate all three to explain organism-habitat fit and predict mismatch consequences — this is the synthesis challenge of the descriptor.
