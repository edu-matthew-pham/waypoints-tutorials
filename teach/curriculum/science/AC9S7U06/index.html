Pure substances and mixtures

AC9S7U06 · Year 7 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use a particle model to describe differences between pure substances and mixtures and apply understanding of properties of substances to separate mixtures

Prior knowledge

Students know how the properties of materials influence their use (AC9S4U04), have compared reversible changes including dissolving and changes of state (AC9S6U04), and have an introductory particle model from AC9S7U05. They have not formally distinguished pure substances from mixtures at the particle level or applied separation techniques systematically.

By the end

Students use a particle model to describe the difference between pure substances and mixtures, and select and apply appropriate separation techniques based on properties of substances.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using representations of particles to show the difference between samples of pure substances and mixtures, and identifying examples of each
  • examining different solutions and identifying the solvent and solute
  • investigating and using a range of physical separation techniques such as filtration, decantation, evaporation, crystallisation, chromatography and distillation
  • exploring and comparing separation methods used in a variety of situations such as in the home, recycling industries and purifying water
  • analysing how the physical properties of substances in mixtures, such as particle size, density or volatility, determine the separation technique used
  • investigating separation techniques used by First Nations Australians, such as hand-picking, sieving, winnowing, yandying, filtering, cold-pressing and steam distilling

Waypoints

  • 1. Pure substances vs mixtures at the particle levelHingeStudents can distinguish pure substances from mixtures using particle diagrams and classify familiar materials correctly
  • 2. Properties as the basis for separationHingeStudents can explain that separation techniques exploit differences in physical properties and match property differences to techniques
  • 3. Filtration and evaporationStudents can perform or describe filtration and evaporation and justify their use for appropriate mixtures
  • 4. Distillation and chromatographyStudents can describe the principles of distillation and chromatography and identify mixtures each technique is appropriate for
  • 5. Selecting and justifying separation techniquesStudents can select and justify an appropriate separation technique for an unfamiliar mixture using property differences
