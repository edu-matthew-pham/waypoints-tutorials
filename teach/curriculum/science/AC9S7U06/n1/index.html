Pure substances vs mixtures at the particle level

AC9S7U06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish pure substances from mixtures using particle diagrams and classify familiar materials correctly

Lessons on this site

  • Pure substance or mixture?

Success criteria

  • Correctly distinguishes pure substance from mixture using particle diagrams
  • Classifies 5 familiar materials correctly with justification
  • Does not confuse homogeneous mixtures (e.g. solutions) with pure substances

Prior knowledge

Students can classify materials as solids, liquids or gases and have an introductory particle model from AC9S7U05. They have not formally distinguished pure substances from mixtures at the particle level or applied separation techniques systematically.

Width

Minimum
Distinguish pure substance from mixture using particle diagrams; classify 5 familiar materials correctly with justification
Typical
Draw particle diagrams for a pure substance, a homogeneous mixture and a heterogeneous mixture; explain the key differences
Wider
  • Use representations of particles to show the difference between samples of pure substances and mixtures; identify examples of each in everyday contexts
  • Examine different solutions and identify the solvent and solute; represent at the particle level
  • Investigate a borderline case (e.g. alloy, saltwater at different concentrations) — is it pure or a mixture? What does this reveal about the definition?

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish pure substances from mixtures at the particle level cannot select appropriate separation techniques in Nodes 3-5 — technique selection requires knowing what type of mixture is present.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe pure/mixture distinction at the particle level is prerequisite for all separation technique selection. Students who cannot make this distinction select inappropriate techniques and cannot explain their choices. Seeing the distinction across multiple material types — solutions, alloys, everyday materials — forces students to apply the particle-level definition rather than relying on visual appearance.
