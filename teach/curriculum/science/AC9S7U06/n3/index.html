Filtration and evaporation

AC9S7U06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform or describe filtration and evaporation and justify their use for appropriate mixtures

Lessons on this site

  • Filtration and evaporation

Success criteria

  • Correctly describes or performs filtration and evaporation
  • Identifies a mixture appropriate for each technique with property reasoning
  • Explains why the chosen technique works using property differences

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Properties as the basis for separation.

Width

Minimum
Correctly perform or describe filtration and evaporation; identify a mixture each technique is appropriate for and explain why using property differences
Typical
Design a procedure to separate a given mixture using filtration and/or evaporation; predict and explain the result at the particle level
Wider
  • Investigate and use filtration and evaporation in a range of practical contexts; evaluate effectiveness
  • Investigate separation techniques used by First Nations Australians such as hand-picking, sieving, winnowing and filtering — explain the property differences each exploits
  • Investigate the effect of a variable (e.g. filter paper grade, temperature) on separation efficiency; analyse results using particle theory

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPerforming or describing filtration and evaporation correctly requires procedural precision — students commonly describe steps in the wrong order, omit key steps, or confuse the two techniques. Practice with feedback on procedural accuracy is what helps, not more separation technique contexts.
