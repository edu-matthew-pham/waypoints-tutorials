Distillation and chromatography

Students can describe the principles of distillation and chromatography and identify mixtures each technique is appropriate for

Success criteria

  • Describes the principle of distillation and chromatography
  • Identifies a mixture appropriate for each technique
  • Distinguishes when distillation is preferred over evaporation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Filtration and evaporation.

Width

Minimum
Describe the principle of distillation and chromatography; identify a mixture each is appropriate for and explain why
Typical
Compare distillation and evaporation — explain when each is appropriate and why, using particle theory and property differences
Wider
  • Investigate crystallisation, chromatography and distillation; explain the property differences each exploits
  • Investigate separation techniques used by First Nations Australians such as yandying, cold-pressing and steam distilling — connect to property principles
  • Analyse a chromatography result — calculate Rf values, identify components, evaluate what the result reveals about mixture composition

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly confuse distillation with evaporation — both involve heat and phase change — without reasoning through the key difference: distillation collects the vapour, evaporation does not. Scaffolding the comparison explicitly (same property exploited, different outcome sought, different equipment needed) is what resolves the confusion.
