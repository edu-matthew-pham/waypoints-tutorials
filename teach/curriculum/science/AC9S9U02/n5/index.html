← AC9S9U02 Reproduction and species survival

Comparing reproductive strategies — analysing survival trade-offs

AC9S9U02 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare sexual and asexual reproduction and analyse how each strategy enables species survival in different environmental contexts.

Success criteria

  • Compares sexual and asexual reproduction across variation, speed and resource cost
  • Analyses the survival advantage of each strategy in a specific named environmental context
  • Explains why some species use both strategies depending on conditions

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Asexual reproduction — strategies, speed and survival contexts.

Width

Minimum
Students compare sexual and asexual reproduction across at least two dimensions (variation, speed, cost) and explain which strategy is advantageous in a named context.
Typical
Students construct a comparative analysis of both strategies; explain that many organisms use both strategies depending on conditions; analyse specific examples where strategy choice reflects environmental pressure; connect reproductive strategy to organism complexity and parental investment; explain how reproductive strategy relates to species survival over evolutionary time.
Wider
  • Analyse a species that uses both sexual and asexual reproduction (e.g. strawberry plants, coral, Daphnia) — predict which strategy dominates under what conditions and why
  • Evaluate how human agricultural practices exploit asexual reproduction (cloning, cuttings) and discuss the genetic diversity implications

Response modes

It is the descriptor's 'analyse how the processes enable survival' demand applied to both reproduction types simultaneously.
