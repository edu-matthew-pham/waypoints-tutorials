Redshift and the expanding universe

AC9S10U03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 10 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the redshift of light from distant galaxies provides evidence that the universe is expanding, and connect this to the big bang model.

Lessons on this site

  • How do we know the universe is expanding?

Success criteria

  • Explains that redshift of light from distant galaxies indicates they are moving away from us
  • Connects the expanding universe (evidenced by redshift) to the big bang model
  • Describes Hubble's observations as a key evidential basis for the expanding universe

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: The big bang model — origin and timeline of the universe.

Width

Minimum
Students describe that light from distant galaxies is redshifted (shifted toward longer wavelengths) and explain that this indicates galaxies are moving away from us.
Typical
Students explain the Doppler effect as the mechanism behind redshift; describe Hubble's observations that more distant galaxies have greater redshift; explain that an expanding universe is consistent with — and predicted by — the big bang model; explain that looking at distant objects is looking back in time.
Wider
  • Examine real redshift data from galaxy spectra and calculate recession velocity — what does the relationship between distance and velocity (Hubble's Law) show?
  • Investigate blue shift as a contrasting case — which galaxies are blue-shifted and what does this indicate about local vs cosmological motion?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly confuse redshift with the object emitting red light, rather than understanding it as a wavelength shift indicating motion. The Doppler analogy (sound pitch from a moving source) is the standard bridge, but students need explicit connection to the big bang model — redshift is evidence of expansion, not just motion.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepHubble's observations, modern redshift surveys, and the distinction between local and cosmological recession are distinct investigative contexts that build the 'analyse evidence' capacity the descriptor demands.
