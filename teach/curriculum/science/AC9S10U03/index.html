Big Bang theory

AC9S10U03 · Year 10 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

describe how the big bang theory models the origin and evolution of the universe and analyse the supporting evidence for the theory

Prior knowledge

Students can model and explain how Earth-sun-moon positions cause eclipses, seasons and tides (AC9S7U03) and describe daily and seasonal environmental changes (AC9S1U02, AC9S2U01). They have scale and cyclic-phenomenon understanding at the solar system level. They have not yet encountered cosmological scale (galaxies, light-years, astronomical units), the origin and timeline of the universe, or the evidence base for the big bang — cosmic microwave background radiation, redshift of galaxies, or Hubble's observations.

By the end

Students describe how the big bang theory models the origin and evolution of the universe — including its major stages and the formation of stars and galaxies — and analyse the supporting evidence including redshift, cosmic microwave background radiation and the observed proportions of matter.

Waypoints

  • 1. Scale and structure of the universeStudents can describe the major structural components of the universe using appropriate scale and units, and place Earth within the cosmic hierarchy.
  • 2. The big bang model — origin and timeline of the universeHingeStudents can describe the big bang theory as a model of the origin and evolution of the universe and outline the major stages from the initial singularity to the formation of stars and galaxies.
  • 3. Redshift and the expanding universeStudents can explain how the redshift of light from distant galaxies provides evidence that the universe is expanding, and connect this to the big bang model.
  • 4. Cosmic microwave background radiation and matter proportionsStudents can describe how cosmic microwave background radiation and the observed proportions of matter in the universe provide independent lines of evidence supporting the big bang theory.
  • 5. Analysing the evidence base — how multiple lines support the theoryStudents can analyse how redshift, CMB and matter proportions together constitute a mutually supporting evidence base for the big bang theory, and explain what each line independently demonstrates.
