← AC9S10U05 Newton's laws of motion

Multi-force analysis and free body diagrams

AC9S10U05 n6 · waypoint 6 of 8 · Year 10 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw a free body diagram for an object with multiple forces and calculate net force, then apply F = ma to determine acceleration.

Lessons on this site

  • What forces act on it?

Success criteria

  • Draws a correctly labelled free body diagram for an object with at least three forces
  • Calculates net force and resulting acceleration from a free body diagram
  • Applies the result to explain a real-world scenario (e.g. vehicle safety, sport)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 5: Newton's Third Law — action-reaction pairs.

Width

Minimum
Students draw a free body diagram for a scenario with two or three forces and calculate net force and resulting acceleration.
Typical
Students apply free body diagrams to objects at rest, constant velocity, and accelerating; include normal force, friction, gravity, and applied force; calculate net force and acceleration for multi-step scenarios; explain how airbags, seatbelts and crumple zones extend stopping time to reduce force.
Wider
  • Analyse a vehicle collision scenario: given stopping distance and mass, calculate impact force with and without crumple zone — evaluate the safety benefit quantitatively
  • Design a free body diagram for a multi-phase motion (e.g. a rocket launching then coasting) — identify when each law applies and when net force changes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFree body diagrams become meaningful across varied force configurations — not just 'a box on a table' but objects on inclines, in vehicles, in sport contexts.
