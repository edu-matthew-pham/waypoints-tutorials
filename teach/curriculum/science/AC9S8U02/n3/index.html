← AC9S8U02 Structure, function and organ systems

Organs — tissues working together for an integrated function

AC9S8U02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 8 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe an organ as a structure composed of multiple tissue types, each contributing to the organ's overall function.

Lessons on this site

  • What is an organ made of?

Success criteria

  • Names at least two tissue types in a named organ and explains the contribution of each
  • Explains why organs require multiple tissue types
  • Analyses how a tissue-level change affects organ function

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Tissues — groups of specialised cells performing a shared function.

Width

Minimum
Students name two tissue types in one organ and explain how each contributes to the organ's function.
Typical
Students analyse: the heart (cardiac muscle tissue, epithelial lining, connective tissue — contraction, smooth flow, structural integrity); a leaf (epidermis, mesophyll, vascular tissue — gas exchange, photosynthesis, water/sugar transport); explain that organs require multiple tissue types because no single tissue can perform all the functions an organ needs; compare 2D and 3D representations of organs to understand spatial relationships.
Wider
  • Compare 2D and 3D representations of the same organ — what spatial relationships are visible in 3D that flat diagrams cannot show?
  • Investigate how a disorder at the tissue level affects organ function — e.g. how atherosclerosis (connective tissue thickening in arteries) impairs the circulatory organ's function

Hinge

The organ-as-multi-tissue-structure concept is the prerequisite for organ system analysis. Students who think of organs as single-tissue structures cannot explain why organ function depends on the coordination of component tissues, and cannot analyse how organ failure propagates from tissue disorder.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly think of organs as single things rather than integrated multi-tissue structures. Making the tissue composition of organs explicit — and explaining why multiple tissue types are required — is the conceptual move that connects tissue-level analysis to organ-level function.
