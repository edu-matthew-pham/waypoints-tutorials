← AC9S8U02 Structure, function and organ systems

Tissues — groups of specialised cells performing a shared function

AC9S8U02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 8 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can define a tissue as a group of similar specialised cells performing a shared function, and give examples from plant and animal systems.

Lessons on this site

  • What is a tissue?

Success criteria

  • Defines a tissue as a group of similar specialised cells performing a shared function
  • Names at least one animal and one plant tissue with the function of each
  • Explains how tissue function emerges from the collective action of its component cells

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Cell specialisation — same DNA, different structure and function.

Width

Minimum
Students define tissue and give one plant and one animal tissue example with the shared function of each.
Typical
Students describe: muscle tissue (muscle cells — contraction); epithelial tissue (epithelial cells — lining/protection); xylem tissue (xylem cells — water transport); phloem tissue (sieve tube cells — sugar transport); explain that tissues perform functions individual cells cannot perform at scale; explain that tissue identity is defined by cell type and function, not just location.
Wider
  • Examine prepared slides of muscle, epithelial and plant vascular tissue — describe how the arrangement of cells in each tissue reflects its function
  • Research how a tissue disorder (e.g. fibrosis — abnormal connective tissue) affects the function of the organ it composes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe tissue concept extends specialisation to the collective level — similar cells acting together. Students need to understand that tissue function emerges from cell arrangement and cooperation, not just from individual cell properties. This is the building block for organ composition in N3.
