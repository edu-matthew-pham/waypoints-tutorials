Cell specialisation — same DNA, different structure and function

AC9S8U02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how cells with the same genetic information become specialised for different functions, and describe structural features of two or more specialised cell types.

Lessons on this site

  • Why are cells different shapes?

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two structural features of a specialised cell and explains how each serves the cell's function
  • Explains that cells with the same DNA can be specialised for different functions
  • Connects organelle complement to cell specialisation for at least one cell type

Prior knowledge

Students can describe organelle functions and cell specialisation (AC9S8U01) and explain how structural features enable survival at the organism level (AC9S5U01). They have not yet encountered the tissue or organ as organisational levels, or analysed how structure at sub-organism levels enables system-level survival functions.

Width

Minimum
Students describe two structural features of one specialised cell type and explain how each feature serves the cell's function.
Typical
Students analyse: red blood cells (biconcave, no nucleus — maximises haemoglobin, flexible for capillaries); xylem cells (hollow, thick walls — water transport); nerve cells (long axon, many dendrites — signal transmission over distance); muscle cells (elongated, many mitochondria — contraction); explain that specialisation means organelle complement and shape are matched to function; connect to N5 of AC9S8U01.
Wider
  • Compare the organelle complement of a secretory cell (many ribosomes, large Golgi) to a muscle cell (many mitochondria) — explain each difference using function
  • Investigate how an artificial organ mimics real cell specialisation — what cell-level properties does the artificial version replicate or approximate?

Hinge

Cell specialisation is the conceptual mechanism that explains tissue formation. Students who cannot explain why cells differ structurally cannot understand why similar cells group into tissues in N2, and cannot analyse organ structure in N3 in terms of the specialised cells that compose it.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe structure-function reasoning method must be practised at the cell level before it is applied at the tissue and organ level. Students who learn specialised cell features as a list without the reasoning framework ('this feature enables this function') cannot transfer the analysis to novel cell types or to tissues.
