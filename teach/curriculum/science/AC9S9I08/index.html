Communicate ideas and findings effectively

AC9S9I08 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

write and create texts to communicate ideas, findings and arguments effectively for identified purposes and audiences, including selection of appropriate content, language and text features, using digital tools as appropriate

Prior knowledge

Students can write and communicate scientific ideas and findings (AC9S7I08). They have not yet explicitly analysed communication choices for different purposes and audiences, or written scientific reports that include validity and reproducibility discussion as a required structural component.

By the end

Students write and create texts that communicate ideas, findings and arguments effectively for specific purposes and audiences — selecting appropriate content, language and text features for each context. Students can write a scientific investigation report (including background, results explanation and validity/reproducibility discussion), and produce texts for non-specialist audiences (e.g. presentations, social media campaigns, community reports) — selecting appropriate language and cultural responsiveness in all contexts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing and contrasting scientific texts for different purposes and audiences
  • selecting culturally responsive content maintaining cultural protocols
  • writing investigation reports including theory, results and validity discussion
  • developing interactive presentations showing body system feedback loops
  • planning a social media campaign to reduce carbon footprint
  • collaborating to prepare a local government report on carbon storage

Waypoints

  • 1. Purpose, audience and communication choicesHingeStudents can analyse scientific texts for different purposes and audiences and explain how the author's choices — of content, language and text features — reflect the purpose and audience.
  • 2. Writing a scientific investigation reportStudents can write a scientific investigation report that includes: a background paragraph referencing relevant scientific knowledge; an explanation of results using scientific knowledge; and a discussion that considers validity and reproducibility.
  • 3. Communicating for non-specialist audiencesStudents can create texts for non-specialist purposes and audiences — including selecting appropriate content, language and cultural sensitivity — such as social media campaigns, community reports or public presentations.
