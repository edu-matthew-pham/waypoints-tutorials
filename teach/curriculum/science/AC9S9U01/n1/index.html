← AC9S9U01 Body systems and negative feedback

Stimuli and receptors — detecting change

AC9S9U01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 7 · Year 9 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish internal from external stimuli and identify the receptor type involved in detecting a named stimulus.

Lessons on this site

  • What counts as a stimulus?

Success criteria

  • Distinguishes internal from external stimuli with examples
  • Identifies an appropriate receptor for a named stimulus and describes its role

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse structure-function relationships across cells, tissues and organs in body systems (AC9S8U02). They have not yet examined how multiple systems coordinate responses to stimuli, or modelled negative feedback as a regulatory mechanism.

Width

Minimum
Students identify the stimulus type (internal/external) and name an appropriate receptor for one stimulus example.
Typical
Students sort multiple stimuli into internal/external categories; match stimuli to receptor types; explain why receptor specificity matters; distinguish sensory modalities.
Wider
  • Investigate what happens when receptor function is impaired — e.g. sensory neuropathy, colour blindness — and predict consequences for the response pathway
  • Compare receptor sensitivity thresholds across modalities — why can we detect very faint sounds but not very faint tastes?

Hinge

Receptors are the entry point of the pathway model all subsequent nodes build on. Students who conflate stimulus with receptor, or cannot place the receptor correctly in the pathway, cannot construct the receptor → command centre → effector sequence in N2 or locate where deviation is detected in the feedback loop in N4.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat stimulus-response as undifferentiated — 'the body just reacts.' Making the receptor explicit as a distinct, specific detection component is the scaffold that allows the pathway model to be built in N2.
