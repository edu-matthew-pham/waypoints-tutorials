Comparing nervous and endocrine coordination

AC9S9U01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 7 · Year 9 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare the nervous and endocrine systems on signal type, speed, duration, and specificity, and predict which system coordinates a given stimulus.

Success criteria

  • Compares the nervous and endocrine systems on signal type, speed, and duration
  • Predicts which system coordinates a given novel stimulus and justifies the prediction

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Nervous and endocrine systems as coordination mechanisms — signal types and timescales.

Width

Minimum
Students produce a comparison of the two systems on at least three dimensions: signal type, response speed, and response duration.
Typical
Students compare across signal type, speed, duration, specificity, and reversibility; explain why different stimuli are coordinated by different systems or both; predict which system coordinates a novel stimulus and justify; use annotated diagrams or comparison tables.
Wider
  • Analyse the stress response as a case where both systems coordinate simultaneously — explaining the advantage of dual coordination
  • Examine how imaging technologies (MRI, PET scans) have enabled real-time observation of nervous and endocrine activity — connecting technology advances to scientific knowledge (AC9S9H02)

Hinge

Comparison of the two systems is the explicit demand of the descriptor's 'compare' verb and the progression endpoint. Students who cannot compare beyond surface-level listing cannot apply the model predictively or evaluate why disorders of one system produce different consequences than disorders of the other.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly list features of each system separately rather than constructing a genuine comparison. Structured comparison tasks — same stimulus, both systems, same dimensions — build the analytical habit the descriptor requires.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEncountering the comparison across multiple stimulus types (temperature, blood glucose, stress, exercise) builds a more generalisable model than a single comparison task.
