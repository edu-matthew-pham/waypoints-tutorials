Receptor → command centre → effector pathway

AC9S9U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 7 · Year 9 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can trace the receptor → command centre → effector pathway for a named stimulus, describing the role of each component.

Success criteria

  • Traces the receptor → command centre → effector pathway for a named stimulus
  • Describes the distinct role of each component in the response

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Stimuli and receptors — detecting change.

Width

Minimum
Students trace the three-component pathway for one named stimulus and describe what each component does.
Typical
Students apply the pathway to multiple stimuli; represent pathways as correctly sequenced flow diagrams; explain why all three components are necessary; identify where signals travel between components.
Wider
  • Compare the receptor → command centre → effector model to a technological control system (thermostat, cruise control) — identifying structural parallels and limits of the analogy
  • Investigate how pathway disruption at each component produces different failure modes — receptor damage vs command centre damage vs effector damage

Hinge

The three-component pathway is the structural scaffold N3 and N4 apply directly. Students who cannot place command centre and effector correctly cannot distinguish where nervous vs endocrine signalling differs, and cannot locate the corrective response within the feedback loop.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly skip the command centre and treat stimulus-response as a direct receptor-to-effector connection. Making the command centre explicit — and its role in processing and directing — is the scaffold for comparing neural and endocrine coordination in N3.
