Body systems and negative feedback

AC9S9U01 · Year 9 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compare the role of body systems in regulating and coordinating the body's response to a stimulus, and describe the operation of a negative feedback mechanism

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse structure-function relationships across cells, tissues and organs in body systems (AC9S8U02). They have not yet examined how multiple systems coordinate responses to stimuli, or modelled negative feedback as a regulatory mechanism.

By the end

Students compare how the nervous and endocrine systems coordinate responses to stimuli — using models of receptor-command centre-effector pathways — and describe how negative feedback maintains homeostasis, including the effects of feedback system disorders.

Waypoints

  • 1. Stimuli and receptors — detecting changeHingeStudents can distinguish internal from external stimuli and identify the receptor type involved in detecting a named stimulus.
  • 2. Receptor → command centre → effector pathwayHingeStudents can trace the receptor → command centre → effector pathway for a named stimulus, describing the role of each component.
  • 3. Nervous and endocrine systems as coordination mechanisms — signal types and timescalesStudents can describe how the nervous and endocrine systems each implement the receptor → command centre → effector pathway, distinguishing signal type, speed, and duration.
  • 4. Comparing nervous and endocrine coordinationHingeStudents can compare the nervous and endocrine systems on signal type, speed, duration, and specificity, and predict which system coordinates a given stimulus.
  • 5. Negative feedback architecture — set point, deviation, corrective response, return detectionHingeStudents can describe the four components of a negative feedback mechanism and explain why it maintains stability rather than amplifying change.
  • 6. Negative feedback in canonical biological cases — tracing complete loopsStudents can trace a complete negative feedback loop for at least two named biological examples, correctly identifying all four components in each.
  • 7. Feedback system disorders — locating failure in the loopStudents can explain how a disorder of a feedback system disrupts homeostasis by identifying which component of the loop has failed and describing the consequence.
