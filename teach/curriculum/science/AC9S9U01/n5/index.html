← AC9S9U01 Body systems and negative feedback

Negative feedback architecture — set point, deviation, corrective response, return detection

AC9S9U01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 7 · Year 9 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the four components of a negative feedback mechanism and explain why it maintains stability rather than amplifying change.

Lessons on this site

  • How does your body stay at 37 °C?

Success criteria

  • Labels and describes the role of each of the four components of a negative feedback loop
  • Explains why the mechanism is described as 'negative' — it opposes rather than amplifies deviation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Comparing nervous and endocrine coordination.

Width

Minimum
Students label and describe the role of each of the four components in a negative feedback loop using a generic or diagrammatic model.
Typical
Students represent negative feedback as a complete flow diagram; explain why the mechanism is called 'negative'; distinguish negative from positive feedback; explain why homeostasis is essential for cell function.
Wider
  • Compare negative and positive feedback — identifying where positive feedback is used in biological systems (childbirth, blood clotting) and why amplification is appropriate in those contexts
  • Model the feedback architecture using a non-biological analogy and evaluate the strengths and limits of the analogy

Hinge

The four-component abstract model is the scaffold N6 applies to biological cases and N7 applies to disorder analysis. Students who cannot identify set point, deviation, corrective response, and return detection in a generic model cannot locate these elements in a specific biological system or identify which component fails in a disorder.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly understand feedback from examples without being able to articulate the abstract architecture. Making the four components explicit — and distinguishing 'negative' as opposing rather than bad — is the scaffold that allows transfer to novel biological cases in N6 and disorder analysis in N7.
