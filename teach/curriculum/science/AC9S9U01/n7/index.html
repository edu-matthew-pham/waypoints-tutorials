Feedback system disorders — locating failure in the loop

AC9S9U01 n7 · waypoint 7 of 7 · Year 9 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how a disorder of a feedback system disrupts homeostasis by identifying which component of the loop has failed and describing the consequence.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: feedback loops and what goes wrong

Success criteria

  • Identifies which component of the feedback loop fails in a named disorder
  • Explains the homeostatic consequence of that failure

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 6: Negative feedback in canonical biological cases — tracing complete loops.

Width

Minimum
Students explain one feedback system disorder, identifying the affected component (receptor, command centre, effector, or return detection) and describing the homeostatic consequence.
Typical
Students analyse multiple disorders (type 1 diabetes, hypothyroidism, hypertension) by locating the failure point in the loop; explain why the same disorder produces different consequences depending on which component fails; connect disorder analysis to treatment approaches.
Wider
  • Investigate how medical devices (insulin pumps, pacemakers, CPAP machines) artificially replace or supplement the failed component — mapping device function onto the four-component architecture
  • Evaluate claims about a health product using the feedback model as a diagnostic lens — identifying what feedback mechanism the product claims to support and whether the claim is coherent

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe disorders as 'the body not working' without locating the failure within the loop architecture. Explicitly asking 'which component has failed?' applies the four-component model diagnostically and consolidates N5 understanding.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAnalysing multiple disorders across different systems reinforces that the same feedback architecture underlies all homeostatic regulation, and that failure at different components produces predictably different consequences.
