Classification and biodiversity

AC9S7U01 · Year 7 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate the role of classification in ordering and organising the diversity of life on Earth and use and develop classification tools including dichotomous keys

Prior knowledge

Students can group living things by their external features (AC9SFU01), compare living and non-living things and the life cycles of plants and animals (AC9S3U01), and explain how structural features and behaviours help living things survive in their habitats (AC9S5U01). They have not studied formal taxonomy or constructed classification tools.

By the end

Students investigate why classification systems are used, apply and construct dichotomous keys, and evaluate how classification tools order and organise biodiversity.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • observing and identifying the similarities and differences of particular features within and between groups of organisms
  • creating and modifying a dichotomous key to classify organisms into groups and groups within groups
  • naming and classifying species using scientific conventions from the Linnaean hierarchical classification system, such as kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species
  • considering the reasons for classifying living things, such as identification and communication
  • examining how biological classification has changed over time through improvements in microscopy
  • using provided dichotomous keys to identify organisms surveyed on a field trip
  • investigating First Nations Australians' systems of classifying living things and how these systems differ from those used by contemporary science

Waypoints

  • 1. Purpose of classificationStudents can explain why classification is useful for organising and communicating about living things
  • 2. Taxonomy: major groups and kingdomsHingeStudents can place organisms into major taxonomic groups using observable features and justify their classification
  • 3. Using dichotomous keysHingeStudents can use a dichotomous key to identify organisms by applying binary decisions based on observable features
  • 4. Constructing dichotomous keysStudents can construct a dichotomous key that correctly classifies a set of organisms using observable features
  • 5. Classification systems change over timeStudents can explain how classification systems change in response to new evidence and evaluate what this reveals about scientific knowledge
