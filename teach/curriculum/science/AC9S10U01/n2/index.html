Mitosis — cell division for growth and repair

AC9S10U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the outcome of mitosis and describe its role in growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Success criteria

  • States that mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells
  • Explains that chromosome number is maintained through mitosis
  • Identifies growth and repair as the biological purposes of mitosis

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Chromosomes, DNA and genes — the structural hierarchy.

Width

Minimum
Students state that mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells from one parent cell and explain its role in growth or repair.
Typical
Students describe that mitosis copies all chromosomes before division to maintain chromosome number; explain why genetic identity is preserved; connect to asexual reproduction from AC9S9U02; distinguish mitosis from meiosis in terms of purpose and outcome.
Wider
  • Investigate how uncontrolled mitosis is the basis of cancer — what goes wrong, and how do treatments target dividing cells?
  • Compare mitosis-based asexual reproduction (identical offspring) to sexual reproduction — what are the evolutionary trade-offs?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMitosis is primarily a contrast node — its value is in establishing what meiosis is NOT. Students need a clear outcome model (two identical cells, chromosome number preserved) before the reduction and recombination logic of meiosis in N3 is meaningful.
