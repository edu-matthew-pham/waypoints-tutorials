Alleles and Mendelian inheritance — dominant and recessive

AC9S10U01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain dominant and recessive allele relationships and use Punnett squares to predict genotype and phenotype ratios in monohybrid crosses.

Lessons on this site

  • Can you predict the offspring?

Success criteria

  • Constructs a Punnett square for a monohybrid cross and states correct genotype and phenotype ratios
  • Uses correct allele notation and correctly forms gametes from each parental genotype
  • Interprets a pedigree diagram to identify phenotypes and infer genotypes for dominant/recessive traits

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Meiosis — halving chromosome number and generating variation.

Width

Minimum
Students correctly construct a Punnett square for a monohybrid cross involving dominant and recessive alleles and state the expected phenotype ratio.
Typical
Students apply Punnett squares to multiple cross types (PP × pp, Pp × Pp, Pp × pp); predict and explain genotype and phenotype ratios; use correct notation (capital/lowercase); connect allele pairs to the chromosome pairs separated by meiosis; interpret pedigree diagrams for dominant and recessive traits.
Wider
  • Investigate a real genetic condition (e.g. cystic fibrosis or haemochromatosis) using pedigree analysis — identify carriers and predict risk for offspring
  • Explore First Nations Australians' kinship systems as evidence of observed heredity patterns — what inheritance regularities do marriage laws reflect?

Hinge

Mendelian prediction is the core quantitative demand of the descriptor. Students who cannot correctly assign alleles to gametes via Punnett squares — the most common failure point — produce incorrect ratios throughout and cannot extend to sex-linked patterns in N6 or pedigree interpretation. Punnett square errors typically stem from unresolved meiosis logic from N3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPunnett square construction requires procedural precision — correct allele assignment, correct gamete formation from each parent, correct offspring reading. Errors are systematic and students need structured practice with self-correction before moving to sex-linked patterns.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPedigree diagrams, real genetic conditions, and kinship structure examples each apply the Mendelian model to a different representational and cultural context. This is genuine width — the model is the same, the context and representation vary.
