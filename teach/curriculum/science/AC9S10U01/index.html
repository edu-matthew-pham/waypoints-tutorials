Genetics and inheritance

AC9S10U01 · Year 10 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explain the role of meiosis and mitosis and the function of chromosomes, DNA and genes in heredity and predict patterns of Mendelian inheritance

Prior knowledge

Students can describe reproductive structures and processes in animals and plants and analyse how sexual and asexual reproduction enable species survival (AC9S9U02). They can recognise cells as the basic units of living things and describe specialised cell structures (AC9S8U01). They have not yet examined the molecular basis of heredity — chromosomes, DNA and genes as functional units — or the mechanics of meiosis and mitosis as cell division processes, or Mendelian inheritance as a predictive model.

By the end

Students explain the roles of meiosis and mitosis in heredity, describe the structural relationship between chromosomes, DNA and genes, and predict genotype and phenotype ratios in monohybrid crosses including dominant/recessive and sex-linked patterns.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using models and diagrams to represent the relationship between genes, chromosomes, and DNA
  • explaining how genetic information passed on to offspring from both parents by meiosis and fertilisation increases variation
  • using Mendelian inheritance to predict offspring genotypes and phenotypes in monohybrid crosses
  • using pedigree diagrams to show patterns of inheritance through multigenerational families
  • investigating First Nations Australians' knowledges of heredity as evidenced by kinship and family structures
  • exploring environmental and other factors that cause mutations

Waypoints

  • 1. Chromosomes, DNA and genes — the structural hierarchyHingeStudents can describe the structural relationship between chromosomes, DNA and genes, and explain how genetic information is organised and stored.
  • 2. Mitosis — cell division for growth and repairStudents can explain the outcome of mitosis and describe its role in growth, repair and asexual reproduction.
  • 3. Meiosis — halving chromosome number and generating variationHingeStudents can explain how meiosis halves the chromosome number to produce gametes and describe how meiosis generates genetic variation.
  • 4. Alleles and Mendelian inheritance — dominant and recessiveHingeStudents can explain dominant and recessive allele relationships and use Punnett squares to predict genotype and phenotype ratios in monohybrid crosses.
  • 5. Fertilisation and heredity — connecting meiosis to offspring variationStudents can explain how meiosis and fertilisation together determine heredity and account for variation among siblings from the same parents.
  • 6. Sex-linked inheritanceStudents can explain sex determination by sex chromosomes and predict genotype and phenotype ratios for sex-linked traits using Punnett squares.
