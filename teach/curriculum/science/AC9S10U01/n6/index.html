Sex-linked inheritance

AC9S10U01 n6 · waypoint 6 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain sex determination by sex chromosomes and predict genotype and phenotype ratios for sex-linked traits using Punnett squares.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: mitosis, fertilisation and sex-linked traits

Success criteria

  • Explains that sex is determined by the X and Y chromosome pair
  • Uses correct X-linked allele notation in a Punnett square and predicts phenotype ratios for males and females
  • Identifies why X-linked recessive conditions are more frequently expressed in males than females

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 5: Fertilisation and heredity — connecting meiosis to offspring variation.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that sex is determined by X and Y chromosomes and correctly apply a Punnett square to predict phenotype ratios for one sex-linked trait.
Typical
Students explain why sex-linked conditions (e.g. colour blindness, haemophilia) affect males and females differently; apply X-linked notation correctly; predict carrier status in females; interpret pedigrees for X-linked traits; explain why Y-linked traits pass only father to son.
Wider
  • Analyse a pedigree for an X-linked condition across three generations — identify carriers, predict risk for specific family members
  • Compare rates of sex-linked condition expression in males vs females using probability analysis — why are males more frequently affected by X-linked recessive conditions?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSex-linked inheritance requires students to adapt the Punnett square notation they established in N4 — alleles are now carried on chromosomes of different sizes, and males have only one copy of X-linked genes. The logic extension from N4 must be made explicit, not assumed.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMulti-generational pedigrees for X-linked conditions (haemophilia, colour blindness) provide genuine width — students apply the sex-linked model to varied family structures and predict carrier and affected status. This is the elaboration's explicit expectation.
