Habitat disruption affects food chains

AC9S4U01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 4 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how removing or adding an organism to a habitat affects the feeding relationships in a food chain, using examples of introduced species or habitat change.

Success criteria

  • Predicts the effect on at least one other organism when a named organism is removed from a food chain and explains the reasoning
  • Identifies at least one real-world example where a food chain was disrupted and describes the cause

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Food chains show feeding sequences.

Width

Minimum
Students predict and explain what would happen to one organism in a food chain if another organism were removed, using a given habitat example.
Typical
Students explain how the removal of a producer or key consumer cascades through a food chain, and describe at least one real-world example of food chain disruption in Australia.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific case of introduced predator impact on Australian native species (e.g. foxes and small mammals) and explain the cascading effects through the food chain
  • Research how First Nations Australians use food chain knowledge to manage Country, including controlled burning and its effects on food chains
  • Explore the ecological role of decomposers in recovery after disruption — what happens to dead matter when predator populations crash

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents struggle to extend their reasoning beyond the immediately removed organism — they see one step of the chain affected rather than cascading effects. Varied disruption scenarios (producer loss, consumer loss, introduced predator) and Australian contexts help students develop a more flexible and accurate model of chain interdependence.
