Food chains and habitats

AC9S4U01 · Year 4 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explain the roles and interactions of consumers, producers and decomposers within a habitat and how food chains represent feeding relationships

Prior knowledge

Students can compare characteristics of living and non-living things and explain differences between plant and animal life cycles (AC9S3U01). They have not yet examined feeding relationships between organisms or the role of decomposers in a habitat.

By the end

Students explain the roles of producers, consumers and decomposers within a habitat — including how each group obtains energy and contributes to the habitat — and construct food chains to represent feeding relationships between named organisms across different habitats.

Waypoints

  • 1. Producers make their own foodHingeStudents can identify producers as living things that make their own food using sunlight and explain why this makes them the starting point of a food chain.
  • 2. Consumers eat other organismsStudents can classify animals as consumers, distinguish between herbivores, carnivores and omnivores, and explain that consumers obtain energy by eating producers or other consumers.
  • 3. Decomposers break down waste and remainsStudents can identify decomposers as living things that break down dead organisms and waste, and explain why decomposers are important to a habitat.
  • 4. Food chains show feeding sequencesHingeStudents can construct a food chain for a named habitat using correct notation (arrows showing energy flow) and label each organism as producer, consumer or decomposer.
  • 5. Habitat disruption affects food chainsStudents can explain how removing or adding an organism to a habitat affects the feeding relationships in a food chain, using examples of introduced species or habitat change.
