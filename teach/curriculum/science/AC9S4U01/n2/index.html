Consumers eat other organisms

AC9S4U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 4 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can classify animals as consumers, distinguish between herbivores, carnivores and omnivores, and explain that consumers obtain energy by eating producers or other consumers.

Success criteria

  • Classifies at least three animals from a named habitat as herbivore, carnivore or omnivore with reasoning
  • Explains that consumers get their energy from eating other living things, not from sunlight directly

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Producers make their own food.

Width

Minimum
Students classify animals from a named habitat as consumers and sort examples into herbivore, carnivore or omnivore categories with justification.
Typical
Students explain how energy moves from producers to consumers and identify multiple levels of consumers (primary, secondary) in a habitat without using that terminology explicitly.
Wider
  • Examine consumers that are difficult to classify — omnivores that shift diet by season, scavengers, parasites — and discuss where they fit
  • Explore how First Nations Australians classify animals and their feeding relationships, and compare with the producer/consumer framework
  • Investigate how the removal of one consumer from a habitat affects others — connects to food chain disruption in the next node

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly conflate 'consumer' with 'animal that eats meat' and struggle with omnivores and scavengers. Varied examples across habitats and feeding types help students build a working classification that holds up under edge cases and prepares them for accurate food chain construction.
