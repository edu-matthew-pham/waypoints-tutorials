Food chains show feeding sequences

AC9S4U01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 4 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct a food chain for a named habitat using correct notation (arrows showing energy flow) and label each organism as producer, consumer or decomposer.

  • Who eats whom? A first look

Success criteria

  • Constructs a food chain with arrows pointing in the direction of energy flow (not toward predator)
  • Correctly labels each organism in the chain as producer, consumer or decomposer
  • Explains what the arrows in a food chain represent

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Decomposers break down waste and remains.

Width

Minimum
Students construct a simple food chain (3–4 links) for a given habitat using arrow notation and label each organism correctly.
Typical
Students construct food chains for two different habitats, explain what the arrows represent (energy flow, not just 'eats'), and compare the chains to identify structural similarities.
Wider
  • Compare food chains across markedly different habitats — arid desert, tropical rainforest, reef — and explain what determines chain length
  • Represent the same habitat's feeding relationships as both a food chain and an informal food web, and discuss what a food chain can and cannot show
  • Construct food chains from First Nations ecological knowledge of a local habitat and compare to textbook examples

Hinge

Arrow direction errors (pointing toward predator rather than in the direction of energy flow) are prerequisite errors that propagate into Node 5 — students who cannot construct accurate chains cannot reason about what happens when an organism is removed, because they lack a correct model of energy flow sequence.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsArrow direction errors are common and persistent — students draw arrows toward the predator ('what eats what') rather than in the direction of energy flow. Accurate notation requires deliberate practice with feedback, not more examples of different habitats.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierFood chain construction requires coordinating the three roles (producer, consumer, decomposer) into a single representation. Students who have each role as a separate idea must now apply all three simultaneously while observing notation conventions.
