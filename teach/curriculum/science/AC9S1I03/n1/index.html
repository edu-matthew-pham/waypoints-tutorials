← AC9S1I03 Observing and measuring

Observations are made and recorded systematically

AC9S1I03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Years 1–2 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make observations using their senses and appropriate tools, record observations using at least two formats (drawing, count, informal measurement, digital photo), and explain what an observation is.

Success criteria

  • Makes and records observations in at least two different formats (e.g. drawing and informal measurement)
  • Explains what an observation is in their own words

Prior knowledge

Students can make observations using their senses (F band). They now extend to making observations more systematically and recording them using informal measurements and digital tools.

Width

Minimum
Students make and record observations in two different formats for a given investigation.
Typical
Students explore what an observation is through guided discussion; make observations using senses and tools; use informal measurements (cups, handspans, paces, blocks, string) to record quantities; photograph or video observations; and organise recorded observations in tables or drawings.
Wider
  • Use informal measurements such as blocks to measure plant growth or paces to measure how far an object has moved — applying informal measurement to quantify observations
  • Record observations through text, drawing, counts, informal measurements and digital photography — building a repertoire of recording formats
  • Explore how digital photography can capture observations that are hard to record by hand — comparing recording tools
