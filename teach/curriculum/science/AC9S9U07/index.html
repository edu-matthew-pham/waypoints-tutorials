Chemical equations and conservation of mass

AC9S9U07 · Year 9 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

model the rearrangement of atoms in chemical reactions using a range of representations, including word and simple balanced chemical equations, and use these to demonstrate the law of conservation of mass

Prior knowledge

Students can compare physical and chemical changes and identify indicators of energy change in chemical reactions (AC9S8U07). They can classify matter as elements, compounds and mixtures and use chemical symbols and formulas (AC9S8U06). They can compare reversible and irreversible changes (AC9S6U04). They have not yet modelled atom rearrangement as the mechanism of chemical change, written balanced symbolic equations, or applied conservation of mass as a quantitative principle to chemical reactions.

By the end

Students model chemical reactions as atom rearrangement using word equations, molecular models and balanced symbolic equations, and use conservation of mass — demonstrated through atom counting — to explain why equations must be balanced.

Waypoints

  • 1. Reactants and products — identifying what changes in a chemical reactionStudents can identify reactants and products in a chemical reaction and describe the change in terms of new substances being formed.
  • 2. Atom rearrangement — chemical reactions as atomic-level redistributionHingeStudents can model a chemical reaction as the rearrangement of atoms using molecular models or diagrams, showing that atoms are neither created nor destroyed.
  • 3. Word equations — naming reactants and productsStudents can write correct word equations for named chemical reactions, correctly identifying all reactants and products.
  • 4. Balanced symbolic equations — demonstrating conservation of massHingeStudents can write and balance simple symbolic chemical equations and explain why balancing demonstrates the law of conservation of mass.
  • 5. Conservation of mass — connecting representation to empirical evidenceStudents can use balanced equations to explain the law of conservation of mass, and connect this to experimental evidence from reactions in closed and open systems.
