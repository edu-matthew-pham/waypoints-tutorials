Reactants and products — identifying what changes in a chemical reaction

AC9S9U07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify reactants and products in a chemical reaction and describe the change in terms of new substances being formed.

Lessons on this site

  • What goes in, what comes out?

Success criteria

  • Identifies reactants and products in a named chemical reaction
  • Explains that chemical reactions produce new substances with different properties
  • Connects reactant-product change to prior indicators of chemical change (AC9S8U07)

Prior knowledge

Students can compare physical and chemical changes and identify indicators of energy change in chemical reactions (AC9S8U07). They can classify matter as elements, compounds and mixtures and use chemical symbols and formulas (AC9S8U06). They can compare reversible and irreversible changes (AC9S6U04). They have not yet modelled atom rearrangement as the mechanism of chemical change, written balanced symbolic equations, or applied conservation of mass as a quantitative principle to chemical reactions.

Width

Minimum
Students correctly identify reactants and products in a named reaction and state that new substances with different properties are formed.
Typical
Students identify reactants and products in multiple reaction types; connect to prior physical vs chemical change knowledge from AC9S8U07 — new substances with different properties are the defining feature; explain that chemical change involves bond breaking and forming; describe observable evidence (colour change, gas production, precipitate, energy change) as indicators of product formation.
Wider
  • Investigate why most elements are not found in their elemental state — what does their chemical reactivity tell us about the products formed in natural reactions?
  • Examine a combustion reaction in terms of reactants and products — identify all products, including water vapour, not just CO₂

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents have prior knowledge of chemical change indicators but not the reactant-product framing as a formal input-output model. Establishing this framing — reactants consumed, products formed — is the conceptual orientation for the atom rearrangement model in N2.
