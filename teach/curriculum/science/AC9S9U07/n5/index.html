← AC9S9U07 Chemical equations and conservation of mass

Conservation of mass — connecting representation to empirical evidence

AC9S9U07 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use balanced equations to explain the law of conservation of mass, and connect this to experimental evidence from reactions in closed and open systems.

Lessons on this site

  • Where does the mass go?

Success criteria

  • Explains why total mass is conserved in a closed system chemical reaction
  • Explains apparent mass changes in open system reactions using conservation of mass
  • Uses a balanced equation to connect the symbolic representation to the empirical mass conservation result

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Balanced symbolic equations — demonstrating conservation of mass.

Width

Minimum
Students explain why a reaction in a closed system shows no change in total mass, and why an open system may appear to lose or gain mass.
Typical
Students investigate or analyse data from closed system reactions to confirm mass conservation; explain apparent mass changes in open systems (gas escaping or being absorbed from air); use balanced equations to predict mass of products from known reactant masses; evaluate the claim that 'mass is conserved in chemical reactions' against experimental data.
Wider
  • Investigate green chemistry principles — if mass is always conserved, why does green chemistry focus on waste? Reconcile atom economy with conservation of mass
  • Examine a reaction where mass appears to change in an open system and explain the discrepancy using conservation of mass — what left or entered the system?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates atom rearrangement (N2), word equations (N3) and balanced symbolic equations (N4) into an empirically grounded account of conservation of mass. Connecting the symbolic model to experimental data — particularly explaining open vs closed system discrepancies — is the descriptor's 'demonstrate the law of conservation of mass' demand.
