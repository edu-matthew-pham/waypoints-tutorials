Atom rearrangement — chemical reactions as atomic-level redistribution

AC9S9U07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can model a chemical reaction as the rearrangement of atoms using molecular models or diagrams, showing that atoms are neither created nor destroyed.

Lessons on this site

  • Where do the atoms go?

Success criteria

  • Uses a molecular model or diagram to represent a simple reaction showing atoms before and after
  • Counts atoms on each side of a representation and confirms they match
  • Explains that atoms are rearranged — not created or destroyed — in chemical reactions

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Reactants and products — identifying what changes in a chemical reaction.

Width

Minimum
Students use a molecular model or diagram to represent a simple reaction (e.g. hydrogen + oxygen → water) showing the same atoms before and after.
Typical
Students model multiple reactions using molecular models, structural diagrams, or ball-and-stick representations; count atoms on each side to verify they match; explain that bonds between atoms break and reform to produce new arrangements; explain that the type and number of atoms is unchanged — only their arrangement changes; connect this to why mass is conserved.
Wider
  • Investigate a reaction in a closed system (sealed container) and demonstrate conservation of mass by weighing before and after — relate the result to the atom rearrangement model
  • Use molecular modelling software to build reactant and product structures for a named reaction and confirm atom count matches

Hinge

Atom rearrangement is the mechanistic foundation of the conservation of mass principle and the conceptual basis for why equations must be balanced. Students who understand chemical change only at the observable level — new substance formed — cannot understand why balancing an equation is a meaningful scientific constraint rather than an arbitrary rule.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat balancing equations as a procedural task without understanding why it is required. Making atom rearrangement explicit — same atoms, new arrangement — is the conceptual move that makes conservation of mass a necessary consequence rather than an imposed rule.
