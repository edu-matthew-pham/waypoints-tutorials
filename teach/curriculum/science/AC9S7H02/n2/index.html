Cultural knowledge systems contribute to science

AC9S7H02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 7–8 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate how knowledge systems from different cultures contribute to scientific understanding

Success criteria

  • Names a specific cultural knowledge contribution to scientific understanding
  • Explains what the contribution is rather than just naming the culture
  • Does not frame cultural knowledge as inferior to or separate from science

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Scientists bring perspectives to their work.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example where cultural knowledge has contributed to scientific understanding; explain the contribution
Typical
Investigate First Nations Australians' classification systems and explain how they differ in context and purpose from Linnaean taxonomy; identify what each contributes
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Australians have developed sustainable harvesting practices and cultural protocols based on deep ecological understandings
  • Explore the work of Wang Zhenyi, including her experiments on lunar eclipses, as an example of non-Western scientific contribution
  • Compare how different cultural knowledge systems approach the same natural phenomenon and what each reveals

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly hold a deficit view — that cultural knowledge is either 'real science' or 'not science' — rather than understanding that different knowledge systems ask different questions and contribute different insights. Investigating multiple cultural contexts (First Nations classification, Wang Zhenyi, sustainable harvesting) helps students see that contributions are genuine and varied, not tokenistic. Single-context teaching reinforces the deficit view; breadth resolves it.
