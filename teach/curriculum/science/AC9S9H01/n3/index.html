← AC9S9H01 Peer review validates scientific knowledge

Knowledge refined: rejection and revision cases

AC9S9H01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine cases where scientific knowledge was initially rejected, revised or debated before being accepted, and explain what role evidence played in the eventual outcome.

Success criteria

  • Can describe a case where scientific knowledge was refined and explain what evidence drove the change.
  • Can explain that validation is not a single event — knowledge builds through accumulation of consistent evidence.
  • Does not describe initial rejection as simply 'scientists being wrong' — explains the epistemic reasons for caution.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: The publication and peer review process.

Width

Minimum
Describe one case where a scientific claim was initially rejected and later validated (e.g. Marshall and Warren's discovery that bacteria cause peptic ulcers), explaining what evidence led to eventual acceptance.
Typical
Examine two cases of knowledge refinement — one where initial rejection was eventually overturned by accumulating evidence, and one where scientific consensus has built over time (e.g. global warming, or consistency of evidence for the particle model of electricity from Thomson, Millikan and Rutherford) — explaining what the cases reveal about how the process works.
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Australians' traditional fire management knowledge was initially dismissed by Western scientific institutions and later validated — examining what this case reveals about whose knowledge counts and how validation processes can be culturally biased.
  • Examine how Marie and Pierre Curie's discovery of new elements was validated — including the role of reproducibility and measurement precision in overcoming initial scepticism.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who understand peer review from a single case (commonly the bacteria/ulcers example) may develop a simplified picture of how rejection and validation work. Varying the cases — across time periods, knowledge types, cultural contexts — builds a more accurate understanding of validation as an ongoing, sometimes contested process.
