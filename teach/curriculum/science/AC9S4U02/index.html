Water cycle

AC9S4U02 · Year 4 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify sources of water and describe key processes in the water cycle, including movement of water through the sky, landscape and ocean; precipitation; evaporation; and condensation

Prior knowledge

Students can describe observable properties of solids and liquids and explain how adding or removing heat energy causes changes of state (AC9S3U04). They can compare properties of soils, rocks and minerals as Earth resources (AC9S3U02). They have not yet connected state changes to the movement of water through the environment as a continuous cycle.

By the end

Students describe the key processes of the water cycle — evaporation, condensation and precipitation — trace the movement of water through sky, landscape and ocean, and identify sources of water including how First Nations Australians and others understand and manage water resources.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying everyday examples of precipitation (rain or snow), evaporation (wet washing or paint drying) and condensation of water (water droplets on a cold water bottle)
  • identifying local water sources and exploring how they change over time, such as rain puddles that evaporate or a local creek that flows faster after rain
  • exploring where tap water comes from and predicting what happens to water that goes down the drain
  • exploring a game or simulation of the water cycle, identifying key processes and creating their own representation of the water cycle
  • recognising that clouds are tiny water droplets suspended in air, observing a 'cloud in a bottle' demonstrated by a teacher and discussing what conditions are needed for clouds to form and for rain or snow to fall
  • exploring First Nations Australians' connections with and valuing of water and water resource management
  • recognising First Nations Australians' knowledges and understandings of evaporation and how the effect of evaporation can be reduced to conserve water, such as by covering surfaces
  • considering why we are encouraged to save and recycle water, and actions people can take to reduce water consumption and waste

Waypoints

  • 1. Water exists in different forms and locationsStudents can identify sources of water in the environment — sky, landscape, ocean, underground — and recognise that water exists as liquid, vapour and solid depending on conditions.
  • 2. Evaporation — liquid water becomes vapourHingeStudents can explain evaporation as the process by which liquid water becomes water vapour and identify everyday examples of evaporation occurring.
  • 3. Condensation — vapour becomes liquid waterHingeStudents can explain condensation as the process by which water vapour becomes liquid water and identify everyday examples where condensation is visible.
  • 4. Precipitation — water falls from cloudsStudents can explain precipitation as water falling from clouds in different forms (rain, snow, hail) and describe the conditions that determine which form precipitation takes.
  • 5. The water cycle — continuous movement through Earth's systemsStudents can trace the movement of water through the complete cycle — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff and collection — and explain that the water cycle is continuous.
