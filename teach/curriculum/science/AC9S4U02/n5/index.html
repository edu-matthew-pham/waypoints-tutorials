← AC9S4U02 Water cycle

The water cycle — continuous movement through Earth's systems

AC9S4U02 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 4 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can trace the movement of water through the complete cycle — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff and collection — and explain that the water cycle is continuous.

Success criteria

  • Traces the path of water through at least three named stages of the water cycle, correctly labelling the process at each transition
  • Explains that the water cycle is continuous and driven by solar energy
  • Identifies at least two locations where water is stored (e.g. ocean, atmosphere, groundwater, ice)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Precipitation — water falls from clouds.

Width

Minimum
Students trace a water molecule through at least three stages of the water cycle, naming the processes at each transition and identifying where the water is at each stage.
Typical
Students describe the complete water cycle including the movement of water through sky, landscape and ocean, explain that solar energy drives the cycle, and represent it in a labelled diagram or model.
Wider
  • Explore how hydrologists use rainfall and water use data to explain river flow and seasonal variation (AC9S4H01) — connecting the abstract cycle to real data
  • Examine how land use changes (deforestation, urbanisation) affect the water cycle at local and regional scales
  • Consider how First Nations Australians' water resource management practices are grounded in understanding the water cycle, including seasonal variation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe water cycle integrates evaporation, condensation and precipitation — three processes students have encountered separately — into a single continuous system. Students must now coordinate these components and understand that the same water moves repeatedly through the cycle, driven by solar energy.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to model the water cycle as a simple loop (rain falls, evaporates, becomes rain again) without accounting for groundwater, ocean reservoirs, or long-term storage in ice. Varied representations — local creek, ocean, underground — help students build a more complete model of the cycle.
