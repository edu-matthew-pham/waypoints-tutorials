← AC9S9U06 Atomic model and radioactive decay

Decay sequences — how unstable isotopes reach stability

AC9S9U06 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how a series of decay events transforms an unstable isotope into a stable one, and explain the relationship between the descriptor's descriptor claim that 'natural radioactive decay results in stable atoms'.

Lessons on this site

  • From uranium to lead

Success criteria

  • Traces a named decay sequence and identifies the stable end product
  • Tracks changes to atomic number and mass number at each decay step
  • Explains how the sequence of decay events moves the nucleus toward stability

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Half-life — rate of radioactive decay.

Width

Minimum
Students trace a simple one or two-step decay sequence (e.g. radon-222 → polonium-218 via alpha decay) and identify the stable end product.
Typical
Students trace multi-step decay chains (e.g. uranium-238 → lead-206); track changes to atomic number and mass number at each step; explain that each decay step moves the nucleus toward a more stable neutron-to-proton ratio; connect to the historical development of atomic theory — decay provided early evidence for nuclear structure.
Wider
  • Investigate how mass and energy are connected in nuclear reactions — what happens to the 'missing mass' in a decay event? (binding energy / E=mc² at a qualitative level)
  • Examine how the discovery of radioactive decay changed the understanding of the atom's internal structure — what did natural transmutation reveal that the earlier model could not explain?
