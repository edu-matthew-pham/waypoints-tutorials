← AC9S5I01 Questioning and predicting

Investigable questions test relationships between variables

AC9S5I01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Science · Inquiry · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can pose investigable questions that specifically target a relationship between two variables — framed as 'Does X affect Y?' or 'How does X change when Y changes?' — and explain what makes a question investigable.

Success criteria

  • Poses a question that targets a relationship between two named variables
  • Explains why the question is investigable — what would be changed and what would be measured

Prior knowledge

Students can pose questions targeting patterns and relationships and make observation-grounded predictions (AC9S3I01, 3-4 band). They now extend to posing questions that test causal relationships and making reasoned predictions — predictions that are explicitly justified with prior knowledge, not just prior observation.

Width

Minimum
Students pose one investigable question targeting a relationship between two variables and explain why it is investigable.
Typical
Students pose investigable relationship questions across two or more Y5-6 science contexts (biological, geological, physical), explain what makes each question investigable (variables identifiable, testable by observation or measurement), and distinguish from questions that cannot be scientifically investigated.
Wider
  • Pose questions about landscape features and how they were changed by weathering, erosion or deposition — examining what observation or measurement would test the relationship
  • Use information from First Nations Australians to formulate investigable questions about structural features and behaviours of living things — examining how traditional knowledge can guide scientific question formulation
  • Compare the structure of relationship questions in different science domains (biological: survival features; geological: erosion rates; physical: light behaviour) and identify what makes each type investigable

Hinge

Posing a well-formed relationship question is the entry point for I02 planning — students who cannot frame a question around two identifiable variables cannot independently decide what to change and measure. Thin treatment here means I02 planning defaults to teacher-supplied questions rather than student-generated ones.
