← AC9S5I01 Questioning and predicting

Reasoned predictions are justified with prior scientific knowledge

AC9S5I01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make reasoned predictions that are explicitly justified using prior scientific knowledge — distinguishing a reasoned prediction from an observation-based prediction by the nature of the justification.

Success criteria

  • Makes a prediction and explicitly states the prior scientific knowledge (not just prior observation) that justifies it
  • Identifies what outcome would confirm or challenge the prediction

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Investigable questions test relationships between variables.

Width

Minimum
Students make a reasoned prediction for a named investigation scenario and explicitly state the prior scientific knowledge that justifies it.
Typical
Students make reasoned predictions across two or more science contexts, explicitly linking each prediction to a scientific principle or prior knowledge (not just a prior observation), and explain what outcome would confirm or challenge the prediction.
Wider
  • Make reasoned predictions about habitat survival: 'Will animals in desert habitats all have water conservation adaptations?' — justifying with knowledge of natural selection and habitat demands
  • Make reasoned predictions about erosion: 'Will steeper slopes show more erosion?' — justifying with knowledge of water velocity and transport capacity
  • Make reasoned predictions about light: 'Will light reflecting off a curved mirror behave differently from a flat mirror?' — justifying with knowledge of straight-line travel and angle of reflection
