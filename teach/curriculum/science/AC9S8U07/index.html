Physical and chemical changes

AC9S8U07 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compare physical and chemical changes and identify indicators of energy change in chemical reactions

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that physical changes preserve material composition (AC9S2U03), compare reversible and irreversible changes at an observable level (AC9S6U04), use particle theory for substance properties (AC9S7U05), and classify matter as elements, compounds or mixtures (AC9S8U06). AC9S6U04 provides a reversibility heuristic that mostly works but fails for dissolving. The new substance criterion — grounded in compound formation from AC9S8U06 — is new. Exothermic and endothermic as named categories of energy change in reactions are new.

By the end

Students compare physical and chemical changes using the new substance criterion, identify observable indicators of chemical change, and explain exothermic and endothermic reactions as categories of energy change — applying all three criteria to classify and analyse unfamiliar changes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • performing simple chemical reactions to identify the indicators of chemical change such as gas production, solid production, colour change and temperature change
  • analysing and interpreting data on the properties of substances before and after the substances interact to determine if a chemical or physical change has occurred
  • investigating and identifying energy changes in different chemical reactions such as differences in temperature
  • examining how the physical and chemical properties of a substance will affect its production or use
  • discussing where indicators of chemical change are used for identifying the presence of particular substances, such as in soil, water and medical testing kits

Waypoints

  • 1. Physical change at the particle level — rearrangement without new substanceStudents can explain physical change as rearrangement of particles without formation of new substances, and represent this using the particle model.
  • 2. Chemical change — new substance formation at the particle levelHingeStudents can explain chemical change as the formation of new substances through bond breaking and forming, and apply the new substance criterion to classify changes.
  • 3. Observable indicators of chemical changeStudents can identify and explain four observable indicators of chemical change and explain why each suggests a new substance has formed.
  • 4. Exothermic reactions — energy released to surroundingsStudents can identify exothermic reactions by temperature rise, explain that energy is released as bonds form in the products, and give examples.
  • 5. Endothermic reactions — energy absorbed from surroundingsStudents can identify endothermic reactions by temperature drop, explain that energy is absorbed to break reactant bonds, and give examples.
  • 6. Comparing physical and chemical changes — integrating all criteriaStudents can apply the new substance criterion, observable indicators and energy change together to classify and analyse unfamiliar changes.
