Physical change at the particle level — rearrangement without new substance

AC9S8U07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain physical change as rearrangement of particles without formation of new substances, and represent this using the particle model.

Success criteria

  • Explains physical change as particle rearrangement without formation of new substances
  • Represents a physical change using a particle diagram showing no new substance
  • Explains why reversibility is a consequence of physical change, not the definition

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that physical changes preserve material composition (AC9S2U03), compare reversible and irreversible changes at an observable level (AC9S6U04), use particle theory for substance properties (AC9S7U05), and classify matter as elements, compounds or mixtures (AC9S8U06). AC9S6U04 provides a reversibility heuristic that mostly works but fails for dissolving. The new substance criterion — grounded in compound formation from AC9S8U06 — is new. Exothermic and endothermic as named categories of energy change in reactions are new.

Width

Minimum
Students explain physical change using the particle model — particles rearrange but atom types and bonding remain the same.
Typical
Students describe physical changes (melting, dissolving, tearing, bending) using particle diagrams — particles change position or arrangement but no new compound is formed; connect to AC9S2U03 (physical change preserves material composition) and AC9S3U04 (state changes); explain that physical changes are typically reversible because no new substance has formed; note that reversibility is a consequence, not the definition.
Wider
  • Represent dissolving at the particle level — show NaCl ions separating and dispersing in water — and explain why this is a physical change despite appearing to 'disappear'
  • Investigate which physical changes are reversible and which are difficult to reverse in practice (e.g. tearing paper) — explain using particle rearrangement

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe particle-level representation of physical change establishes the baseline against which chemical change will be contrasted in N2. Dissolving as a physical change is important to address here — it is the case most likely to be misclassified as chemical change, and the particle diagram makes the 'no new substance' criterion visible.
