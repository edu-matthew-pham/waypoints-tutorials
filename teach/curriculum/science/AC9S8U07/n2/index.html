← AC9S8U07 Physical and chemical changes

Chemical change — new substance formation at the particle level

AC9S8U07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain chemical change as the formation of new substances through bond breaking and forming, and apply the new substance criterion to classify changes.

Lessons on this site

  • Is it a new substance?

Success criteria

  • States that new substance formation is the criterion defining chemical change
  • Correctly classifies at least three changes using the new substance criterion
  • Explains why reversibility alone is not a reliable criterion using a named counterexample

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Physical change at the particle level — rearrangement without new substance.

Width

Minimum
Students state the new substance criterion and correctly classify three unfamiliar changes as physical or chemical.
Typical
Students explain that chemical change involves bonds breaking between atoms and new bonds forming — producing compounds with different atom arrangements and different properties; connect to AC9S8U06's compound concept — a new compound is a new substance; explain that chemical changes are typically not easily reversed because new substances have different properties from reactants; apply the new substance criterion to classify: burning (new compounds formed), rusting (iron oxide ≠ iron), cooking (proteins/starches structurally altered).
Wider
  • Investigate an ambiguous case — is dissolving chemical or physical? Predict using the new substance criterion, test by evaporation, and evaluate the result
  • Analyse before-and-after property data for three unfamiliar changes and classify each using the new substance criterion — explain your reasoning

Hinge

The new substance criterion replaces the unreliable reversibility heuristic from AC9S6U04. Students who retain reversibility as the primary test will misclassify dissolving (reversible but not chemical) and some reactions. The new substance criterion must be explicitly established as the correct test before indicators and energy change are introduced.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExplicitly confronting the reversibility heuristic failure — dissolving is reversible but not chemical; some chemical reactions can be reversed — is necessary. Students need the new substance criterion presented as a replacement, not an addition, to their prior classification tool. Particle-level diagrams showing bond-breaking and bond-forming make the criterion visible.
