Comparing physical and chemical changes — integrating all criteria

AC9S8U07 n6 · waypoint 6 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply the new substance criterion, observable indicators and energy change together to classify and analyse unfamiliar changes.

Lessons on this site

  • Physical or chemical? Use all the clues

Success criteria

  • Applies the new substance criterion, observable indicators and energy change to classify an unfamiliar change
  • Explains why multiple converging criteria increase classification confidence
  • Identifies a case where a single indicator is misleading and explains using the new substance criterion

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 5: Endothermic reactions — energy absorbed from surroundings.

Width

Minimum
Students apply all three criteria to classify two unfamiliar changes and justify each classification.
Typical
Students analyse before-and-after data (appearance, temperature, mass, properties) to classify changes; explain that multiple converging indicators increase confidence; examine cases where a single indicator is misleading — temperature change also occurs in physical processes (state change) without chemical change; connect to AC9S8U06 — chemical change produces a new compound; evaluate the claim that 'if it glows, it must be chemical' against counterexamples.
Wider
  • Analyse a multi-step process (cooking a meal, or a First Nations food preparation technique) — identify all physical and chemical changes involved and classify each using the full criterion set
  • Investigate how chemical change indicators are used in environmental or medical testing — what does each indicator detect, and how is false-positive risk minimised?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates particle-level physical change (N1), new substance criterion (N2), indicators (N3) and energy change (N4–N5) into a complete analytical framework. Applying all criteria to novel cases — particularly cases where a single indicator could mislead — is the descriptor's full 'compare physical and chemical changes' demand.
