Exothermic reactions — energy released to surroundings

AC9S8U07 n4 · waypoint 4 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify exothermic reactions by temperature rise, explain that energy is released as bonds form in the products, and give examples.

Lessons on this site

  • Hotter or colder?

Success criteria

  • Identifies exothermic reactions by a temperature rise in the surroundings
  • Explains that exothermic reactions release energy stored in chemical bonds
  • Gives at least two examples of exothermic reactions

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Observable indicators of chemical change.

Width

Minimum
Students identify exothermic reactions by observable temperature rise and give two examples.
Typical
Students describe exothermic reactions (combustion, neutralisation, respiration, hand warmers); explain that when products form, the energy stored in new bonds is less than was stored in reactant bonds — the difference is released as heat; explain why temperature rises in the surroundings; connect to AC9S8U05's energy classification — chemical potential energy → thermal energy; describe applications: hand warmers, self-heating cans, fire-starting.
Wider
  • Investigate the temperature change in a neutralisation reaction — measure the heat released and compare different acid/base combinations
  • Examine how exothermic reactions are exploited in hand warmers and self-heating food cans — what chemical system is used and how is the release triggered?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExothermic and endothermic are most clearly understood in parallel contrast. Treating exothermic first — where the temperature rise is observable and intuitive — provides the reference point for the less intuitive endothermic in N5.
