Endothermic reactions — energy absorbed from surroundings

AC9S8U07 n5 · waypoint 5 of 6 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify endothermic reactions by temperature drop, explain that energy is absorbed to break reactant bonds, and give examples.

  • Hotter or colder?

Success criteria

  • Identifies endothermic reactions by a temperature fall in the surroundings
  • Explains that endothermic reactions absorb energy from surroundings
  • Gives at least two examples of endothermic reactions and distinguishes them from exothermic

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Exothermic reactions — energy released to surroundings.

Width

Minimum
Students identify endothermic reactions by observable temperature fall and give two examples.
Typical
Students describe endothermic reactions (photosynthesis, thermal decomposition, dissolving ammonium nitrate); explain that the energy required to break reactant bonds exceeds the energy released in product bond formation — the difference is absorbed from surroundings as heat; explain why temperature falls in the surroundings; describe applications: cold packs; contrast with exothermic in terms of energy direction.
Wider
  • Investigate the temperature change when ammonium nitrate dissolves in water — explain the observation using bond energy reasoning
  • Compare an exothermic and endothermic reaction using the same experimental setup — identify all variables that need controlling to make the comparison fair

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMultiple endothermic reaction contexts — dissolving, photosynthesis, thermal decomposition, cold packs — each apply the same energy-absorption concept in a different domain. Breadth here is more productive than depth on a single example.
