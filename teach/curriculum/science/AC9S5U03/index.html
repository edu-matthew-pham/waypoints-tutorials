Light

AC9S5U03 · Year 5 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify sources of light, recognise that light travels in a straight path and describe how shadows are formed and light can be reflected and refracted

Prior knowledge

Students have informally encountered light and shadows in earlier years. They have not yet examined light as travelling in straight paths, distinguished natural from artificial sources, or described reflection and refraction as distinct interactions between light and matter.

By the end

Students identify natural and artificial light sources, explain that light travels in straight paths from a source, describe shadow formation and the conditions that affect shadow size and shape, explain reflection and refraction as different interactions of light with surfaces and media, and represent these using ray diagrams.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • distinguishing between natural (such as glow worms, the sun and stars) and artificial (such as light bulbs or candles) sources of light
  • investigating the shadows that are formed when light is completely or partially blocked by an object, such as when using a sundial or shadow puppets
  • drawing ray diagrams to show how the path of light from a source reflects off surfaces into the eye
  • observing refraction of light using prisms or water droplets and examining the rainbow effect produced
  • exploring how 'holograph' videos use the refractive properties of light to create an image that appears to be 3-dimensional
  • exploring the use of reflection of light by mirrors such as in periscopes and mirror mazes
  • recognising First Nations Australians' understanding of refraction as experienced in spearfishing and in shimmering body paint, and reflection as evidenced by materials selected for construction of housing

Waypoints

  • 1. Light travels in straight paths from sourcesHingeStudents can identify natural and artificial light sources, explain that light travels in straight paths (rays) from a source, and use this to explain why objects cast shadows.
  • 2. Shadows form when light is blockedStudents can explain how shadows form when an opaque object blocks light, describe how shadow size and shape change with the position and distance of the light source, and represent shadow formation using ray diagrams.
  • 3. Light reflects off surfaces and refracts when changing mediumStudents can explain reflection as light bouncing off a surface in a predictable direction, describe refraction as the bending of light when it passes between different media, give everyday examples of each, and draw ray diagrams for reflection.
