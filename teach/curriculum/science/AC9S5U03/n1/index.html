Light travels in straight paths from sources

AC9S5U03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 5 Science · Physical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify natural and artificial light sources, explain that light travels in straight paths (rays) from a source, and use this to explain why objects cast shadows.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two natural and two artificial light sources
  • Explains that light travels in straight paths (rays) from a source
  • Uses straight-line travel to explain why an object casts a shadow

Prior knowledge

Students have informally encountered light and shadows in earlier years. They have not yet examined light as travelling in straight paths, distinguished natural from artificial sources, or described reflection and refraction as distinct interactions between light and matter.

Width

Minimum
Students distinguish natural from artificial light sources with examples, and explain that light travels in straight paths from a source.
Typical
Students classify light sources as natural (sun, stars, fire, bioluminescence) and artificial (light globes, LEDs, candles), explain that light travels in straight lines from a source in all directions, and use straight-line travel to explain why we can see objects and why shadows form where light is blocked.
Wider
  • Investigate bioluminescent organisms (glow worms, deep sea creatures, fireflies) as natural light sources — examining how living things produce light and why
  • Explore how understanding of light and optics has developed historically — comparing ideas of Plato, Euclid, Ibn al-Haytham and Roger Bacon about the nature of light and how we see (AC9S5H01)
  • Research how First Nations Australians use shimmering body paint as an application of light reflection properties — connecting cultural practice to light behaviour

Hinge

Straight-line travel of light is the foundational model for all subsequent nodes — Node 2 shadow formation and Node 3 reflection/refraction both depend on the ray model. Students who do not hold this model cannot construct valid ray diagrams or predict shadow and reflection behaviour.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe historical development of optics (AC9S5H01) provides a rich cross-strand direction that connects the science content to how scientific understanding is built over time. Bioluminescence offers a genuinely interesting natural light source that broadens students' concept beyond sun and lamps — and breadth of light source examples reinforces the straight-line travel model across varied phenomena, addressing the hinge risk.
