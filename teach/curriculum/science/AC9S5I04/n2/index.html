← AC9S5I04 Processing and representing data

Representations are used to identify and describe patterns, trends and relationships

AC9S5I04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Science · Inquiry · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a completed representation to identify a pattern, trend or relationship in the data and describe what it means in the context of the investigation question.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one pattern, trend or relationship in a completed representation
  • Describes what the pattern or relationship means in the context of the investigation question — not just what the graph shows, but what it tells us

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Representations are selected and constructed for a given data type.

Width

Minimum
Students use one completed representation to identify and describe one pattern or relationship, connecting it to the investigation question.
Typical
Students use representations across Y5-6 science contexts to describe patterns and relationships — predation and camouflage outcomes from column graphs, erosion patterns from maps, force interactions from ray diagrams, structural feature distributions from annotated sketches — and explain what each pattern or relationship means for the investigation question.
Wider
  • Construct column graphs from repeated simulation data (predation and camouflage outcomes) and explain why using values across multiple runs gives a more reliable pattern than a single run
  • Use a representation to distinguish a clear pattern from a noisy or ambiguous result — examining what makes a pattern reliable enough to support a conclusion
  • Connect an identified pattern directly to a prediction made before the investigation — evaluating whether the pattern confirms, partially confirms or contradicts the prediction

Hinge

Pattern identification from representations is directly required by I05 evaluating — students must select evidence from data to draw conclusions. A student who cannot identify what a representation shows cannot distinguish evidence from noise, and cannot draw a reasoned conclusion at I05.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents readily describe what a representation shows at surface level ('the column graph shows group A had more') without identifying a pattern or connecting it to the investigation question. Encountering the interpretation move across varied representation types and science contexts — where 'what does this show?' requires different reasoning for a ray diagram vs a map vs a column graph — builds a more generalisable interpretation capability.
