← AC9S4U04 Properties of materials

Objects often combine materials with different properties

AC9S4U04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 4 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine a multi-material object, identify the different materials present, and explain why each material was selected based on its properties and the specific demands it meets in that object.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two different materials in a named object and names at least one property of each
  • Explains the specific property-to-function connection for each identified material in the object
  • Identifies at least one property that would make a substitute material unsuitable for one component

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Properties determine material selection.

Width

Minimum
Students identify two different materials in a familiar object and explain the property rationale for each.
Typical
Students systematically examine a multi-material object (shoe, lunchbox, tent, backpack), map each material to its location and function, and explain the property rationale for each material choice.
Wider
  • Design and build a test object for a specific purpose (tent, bird feeder, lunchbox) selecting materials based on property analysis — connects to AC9S4I02 (fair testing of designs)
  • Investigate which materials in a chosen object can be recycled and research alternatives for materials with poor sustainability properties
  • Consider how First Nations Australians combine natural materials in traditional objects — e.g. spears combining wood, sinew, stone — and analyse the property rationale for each component

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to coordinate material identification, property vocabulary, and the selection rationale framework — applying all three simultaneously to a complex multi-material object. Students who have each component as a separate skill must now deploy them together in a single analytical task.
