Properties of materials

AC9S4U04 · Year 4 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

examine the properties of natural and made materials including fibres, metals, glass and plastics and consider how these properties influence their use

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that objects are made of materials and describe observable properties of those materials (AC9SFU03). They can recognise that physical actions change the shape of materials without changing their composition (AC9S2U03). They have not yet systematically compared material classes (fibres, metals, glass, plastics) or connected material properties to selection for specific uses.

By the end

Students examine properties of natural and made materials across fibres, metals, glass and plastics, use property vocabulary to describe and compare materials, and explain how specific properties determine why materials are selected for particular uses.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying and naming materials in the classroom, and grouping objects made of similar materials or combinations of materials
  • exploring vocabulary for describing properties; observing different fibres, metals, glass and plastics; and using appropriate terms to describe, compare and contrast their properties
  • investigating familiar objects, such as shoes, drink containers or backpacks, examining the combination of materials from which they are made and suggesting reasons for those combinations based on properties of materials
  • considering how First Nations Australians use materials for different purposes, such as tools, clothing and shelter, based on their properties
  • designing, building and testing an object or structure for a specific purpose, such as a tent, lunchbox or bird feeder
  • investigating which materials can be recycled and researching alternatives for materials such as single use plastics

Waypoints

  • 1. Materials have observable and testable propertiesHingeStudents can use property vocabulary to describe and compare materials, making observations and using appropriate terms (hardness, flexibility, transparency, conductivity, texture, absorbency).
  • 2. Material classes: fibres, metals, glass, plasticsStudents can identify examples of fibres, metals, glass and plastics, and describe characteristic properties of each class that distinguish it from others.
  • 3. Properties determine material selectionStudents can explain why a specific material is used for a particular purpose by connecting one or more of its properties to the demands of that use.
  • 4. Objects often combine materials with different propertiesStudents can examine a multi-material object, identify the different materials present, and explain why each material was selected based on its properties and the specific demands it meets in that object.
