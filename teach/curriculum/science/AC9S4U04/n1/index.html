← AC9S4U04 Properties of materials

Materials have observable and testable properties

AC9S4U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 4 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use property vocabulary to describe and compare materials, making observations and using appropriate terms (hardness, flexibility, transparency, conductivity, texture, absorbency).

Success criteria

  • Uses at least four property terms accurately when describing a given material
  • Distinguishes between properties that can be observed without testing (colour, transparency) and properties that require testing (hardness, absorbency)

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise that objects are made of materials and describe observable properties of those materials (AC9SFU03). They can recognise that physical actions change the shape of materials without changing their composition (AC9S2U03). They have not yet systematically compared material classes (fibres, metals, glass, plastics) or connected material properties to selection for specific uses.

Width

Minimum
Students use at least four property terms to describe and compare two materials, making observations to justify each term.
Typical
Students systematically describe a range of materials using property vocabulary, distinguishing between properties they can observe directly and properties they need to test.
Wider
  • Explore how First Nations Australians use material properties to select appropriate natural materials for tools, clothing, shelter and musical instruments
  • Investigate how property vocabulary is used in materials science and engineering — product datasheets, material specifications
  • Compare how the same material can have different properties in different forms (e.g. glass as flat sheet vs glass fibre vs tempered glass)

Hinge

Property vocabulary is the language for all subsequent nodes. Students who lack accurate property terms produce vague selection rationales ('it's good because it's strong') in Nodes 3 and 4, making the property-to-demand reasoning chain impossible to complete correctly.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a working vocabulary of property terms through applying them to varied materials before they can use these terms reliably in comparative and explanatory tasks. Rushing to selection-for-use before property vocabulary is established produces vague explanations ('it's good because it's strong').
