← AC9S4U04 Properties of materials

Material classes: fibres, metals, glass, plastics

AC9S4U04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 4 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify examples of fibres, metals, glass and plastics, and describe characteristic properties of each class that distinguish it from others.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two examples from each of the four material classes (fibres, metals, glass, plastics)
  • Names at least two characteristic properties of each class that distinguish it from others

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Materials have observable and testable properties.

Width

Minimum
Students identify at least two examples from each of the four material classes and name two characteristic properties of each class.
Typical
Students compare the four material classes using a property framework, identify natural vs made materials within each class, and give examples of where each class is typically used based on its characteristic properties.
Wider
  • Investigate natural vs synthetic versions within a class (natural fibres: wool, cotton; synthetic: nylon, polyester) and compare properties
  • Research how glass is made from natural materials (silica sand) — connecting to the idea that 'made' materials are often processed from natural sources
  • Examine how plastics are classified by resin code and how these different types have different properties and recycling outcomes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to have narrow prototypes for each class — 'metal' means iron/steel, 'fibre' means string. Exposure to the full range within each class (metals including aluminium, copper, gold; fibres including natural and synthetic; glass including safety glass and optical fibre) builds a more robust class concept and prevents selection-for-use errors based on prototype thinking.
