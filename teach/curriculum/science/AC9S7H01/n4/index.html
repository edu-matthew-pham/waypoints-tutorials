← AC9S7H01 Evidence changes knowledge

Evidence and perspectives together refine knowledge

AC9S7H01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 7–8 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the interplay of new evidence and different perspectives leads to ongoing refinement of scientific knowledge

Success criteria

  • Explains how both new evidence and different perspectives contributed to a knowledge change
  • Identifies that scientific knowledge refinement is an ongoing process

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Different perspectives shape knowledge.

Width

Minimum
Explain how both new evidence and different perspectives contributed to a change in scientific knowledge in one case; identify that this process is ongoing
Typical
Construct an explanation tracing how a specific area of scientific knowledge (e.g. classification, astronomy) has been refined through multiple rounds of new evidence and perspective shifts
Wider
  • Evaluate whether a specific change in scientific knowledge was driven primarily by new evidence, new perspectives, or both — justify with examples
  • Investigate a current area where scientific knowledge is being refined by new evidence or perspectives and explain what is changing and why
  • Compare two cases of knowledge change — one driven primarily by technology (new evidence) and one driven primarily by cultural perspective — and explain what each reveals about how science works

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node brings together the evidence strand (Node 2) and the perspectives strand (Node 3) into a coordinated explanation of knowledge refinement. Students who can explain each separately but cannot articulate their interplay have not met the descriptor's full demand — the integration of both into a coherent account of how science develops is the learning.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe evaluative tasks in the wider tier — judging whether a change was driven by evidence or perspective, or both — require explicit reasoning scaffolding. Students need to be supported in constructing the argument structure: claim, evidence strand, perspective strand, and how they interact. Without this scaffold, students produce description rather than evaluation.
