Soils, rocks and minerals

AC9S3U02 · Year 3 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compare the observable properties of soils, rocks and minerals and investigate why they are important Earth resources

Prior knowledge

Students can observe and describe properties of natural and made materials (AC9SFU03, F band). They have not yet systematically compared soil types, distinguished rocks from minerals, or examined Earth materials as resources that living things depend on.

By the end

Students compare observable properties of soils, rocks and minerals using appropriate vocabulary, understand that minerals are components of rocks, and explain why soils, rocks and minerals are important resources for living things including humans.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining different soils from local areas and using magnifying glasses to observe their components, such as pebbles, sand or plant matter as well as living things such as earthworms and insects
  • exploring the school grounds or a local area and observing or collecting different types of rocks and describing similarities or differences such as texture, colour, grain or crystal size
  • recognising that minerals are the building blocks of rocks and that the different characteristics of rocks depend on the minerals they are made up of
  • identifying rocks as key components of the built and natural environment and recognising uses of minerals such as gemstones in jewellery, graphite in pencils, and table salt in food
  • investigating First Nations Australians' knowledges of different rock and mineral types, and how they were used such as for stone blades, grindstones and pigments
  • describing ways in which living things including humans depend on soils, such as for food, growing plants, providing habitat for organisms, and holding and cleaning water
  • examining information on plant tags and exploring the vocabulary used to describe soils and different plant soil requirements
  • investigating which rocks or minerals are quarried or mined locally or regionally and how those resources are used

Waypoints

  • 1. Soils have observable properties and componentsStudents can describe the observable components of soil samples (pebbles, sand, clay, plant matter, living things) and compare the properties of different soils using observable evidence.
  • 2. Rocks and minerals have observable propertiesStudents can describe and compare observable properties of rocks and minerals (colour, texture, grain size, crystal size, hardness, lustre), and explain that minerals are the building blocks of rocks.
  • 3. Soils, rocks and minerals are important Earth resourcesStudents can explain why soils, rocks and minerals are important resources for living things — including how living things depend on soils and how humans use rocks and minerals — and give specific examples of each.
