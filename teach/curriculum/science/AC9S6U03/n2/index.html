← AC9S6U03 Electrical circuits

Conductors allow electricity to flow; insulators do not

AC9S6U03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Science · Physical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish electrical conductors from insulators, classify common materials, explain why conductors and insulators are used in specific components, and connect this to safe electrical design.

Success criteria

  • Classifies at least four materials as electrical conductors or insulators
  • Explains why a wire has a metal core with a plastic coating — connecting conductor and insulator properties to a real design decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: A circuit requires a complete conducting path and energy source.

Width

Minimum
Students classify at least four materials as electrical conductors or insulators with examples, and explain why this distinction matters in electrical devices.
Typical
Students investigate different electrical conductors and insulators; explain that conductors allow electrons to move freely while insulators restrict flow; classify metals as conductors and most plastics, rubber and ceramics as insulators; explain why wires are metal with plastic coating; and connect conductor/insulator selection to safety.
Wider
  • Use an ammeter to compare conductivity of different materials — examining how current measurement reveals conductor quality rather than just a binary conductor/insulator distinction
  • Examine how knowledge of electrical conductors and insulators informs the design of electrical safety guidelines for children (AC9S6H02)
  • Connect electrical insulation to heat insulation studied in Y3 (AC9S3U03) — examining what these two types of insulation have in common and what is different

Hinge

The conductor/insulator distinction is directly required for Node 3 component analysis — students must understand material properties to explain why specific components transform energy as they do, and why circuit design places conductors and insulators in specific roles. Without this, N3 energy transformation explanations lack material-level grounding.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents know metals conduct electricity but often do not understand why the distinction matters in circuit design and safety. Encountering conductor/insulator properties across circuit components, safety equipment, and everyday objects builds a more complete understanding of how material properties are applied in electrical engineering.
