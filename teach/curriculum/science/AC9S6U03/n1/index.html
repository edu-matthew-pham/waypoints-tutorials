← AC9S6U03 Electrical circuits

A circuit requires a complete conducting path and energy source

AC9S6U03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 Science · Physical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what is required for an electrical circuit to work — a complete conducting path from and back to the energy source — and identify what happens to the circuit when the path is broken.

Success criteria

  • Explains that a circuit requires a complete conducting path and an energy source
  • Explains what happens to the circuit when the path is broken and why

Prior knowledge

Students understand that heat transfers from warm to cool objects and can classify materials as conductors or insulators of heat (AC9S3U03). They have classified energy types as kinetic and potential (AC9S8U05 — later band). They have not yet examined electrical circuits, circuit components or electrical conductors and insulators.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that a circuit requires a complete path and an energy source, and describe what happens when the path is broken.
Typical
Students construct real or virtual circuits, identify the necessary components (energy source, conducting material, load), explain that electricity flows only in a complete circuit, explore the role of switches in opening and closing the circuit, and explain why a broken path stops the circuit.
Wider
  • Explore electrical hazards in the home and identify safety measures — fuses, circuit breakers, insulation — that prevent dangerous current flow (AC9S6H02)
  • Investigate why underground power cables were developed and how scientific knowledge about electrical safety informs infrastructure decisions (AC9S6H02)
  • Examine how the development of electrical circuits has required collaboration across science and engineering — connecting circuit science to the broader history of electrical technology

Hinge

The complete-circuit model is prerequisite for Nodes 2 and 3 — students who believe electricity is consumed at the load cannot reason about why conductors and insulators matter (N2) or how energy is transferred through the whole circuit (N3). The 'electricity used up' misconception produces systematic errors in all subsequent circuit reasoning.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly believe electricity 'comes out' of one terminal of a battery and is used up, rather than flowing continuously in a complete loop. Making the complete-circuit logic explicit — electricity must flow from the source, through the circuit, and back to the source — is necessary before students can explain why breaks stop circuits or why components in series all stop when one fails.
