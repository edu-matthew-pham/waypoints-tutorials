Electrical circuits

AC9S6U03 · Year 6 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate the transfer and transformation of energy in electrical circuits, including the role of circuit components, insulators and conductors

Prior knowledge

Students know that heat energy moves from warmer to cooler objects and can sort materials into conductors and insulators of heat (AC9S3U03). They have met sound as energy that makes objects vibrate (AC9S2U02) and explored light from different sources (AC9S5U03). They have not yet examined electrical circuits, circuit components or electrical conductors and insulators.

By the end

Students investigate the requirements for a working electrical circuit, identify the role of each component, distinguish electrical conductors from insulators, construct and represent circuits using standard conventions, and explain how electrical energy is transferred and transformed within a circuit.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying necessary components for an electric circuit such as a source of electrical energy and conducting material such as metal wires
  • constructing a real or virtual circuit to examine requirements to allow the flow of electricity, including exploring the construction and role of switches
  • constructing representations of electrical circuits and their components using accepted conventions
  • examining the purpose of different components such as switches and bulbs and exploring use of ammeters to measure current
  • investigating different electrical conductors and insulators and examining why they may be used
  • exploring how electricity is used in the home and identifying electrical hazards and safety measures used to mitigate these hazards

Waypoints

  • 1. A circuit requires a complete conducting path and energy sourceHingeStudents can explain what is required for an electrical circuit to work — a complete conducting path from and back to the energy source — and identify what happens to the circuit when the path is broken.
  • 2. Conductors allow electricity to flow; insulators do notHingeStudents can distinguish electrical conductors from insulators, classify common materials, explain why conductors and insulators are used in specific components, and connect this to safe electrical design.
  • 3. Electrical energy is transferred and transformed by circuit componentsStudents can explain how electrical energy is transferred through a circuit and transformed by components into light, heat and sound, and represent circuits using standard conventions.
