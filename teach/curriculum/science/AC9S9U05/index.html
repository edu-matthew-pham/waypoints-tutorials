Conservation of energy and efficiency

AC9S9U05 · Year 9 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply the law of conservation of energy to analyse system efficiency in terms of energy inputs, outputs, transfers and transformations

Prior knowledge

Students can classify energy as kinetic or potential and explain energy transfers and transformations in simple systems (AC9S8U05). They can explain energy transfer and transformation in electrical circuits (AC9S6U03). They have qualitative understanding of energy transformation but have not yet applied conservation of energy as a quantitative law, or analysed system efficiency — the proportion of useful output energy — as a calculated quantity.

By the end

Students apply the law of conservation of energy to account for all energy inputs and outputs in a system, calculate efficiency as useful output divided by total input, and use Sankey diagrams to represent energy flow — applying this analysis to real systems including electricity generation and appliances.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining that the law of conservation of energy explains that total energy is maintained in energy transfer and transformation in a system
  • explaining efficiency and recognising that in energy transfer and transformation a variety of processes can occur, so that the amount of usable energy is reduced and the system is not 100% efficient
  • using and critiquing representations such as Sankey diagrams to show energy inputs, changes and outputs in a system
  • investigating the efficiency of ground ovens used by First Nations Australians
  • comparing the efficiency of electricity generation from coal and other sources such as nuclear, hydroelectricity, gas, solar and wind
  • examining the meaning of energy star ratings given to appliances such as refrigerators and washing machines and criteria used to determine these ratings
  • examining how improving efficiency in energy transfer and transformations in sporting activities such as pole vaulting or archery improves athletic performance

Waypoints

  • 1. Conservation of energy — total energy is maintained in any systemHingeStudents can state the law of conservation of energy and apply it to account for all energy in a system — including energy that is transferred as heat or sound rather than as useful output.
  • 2. Efficiency — useful output as a proportion of total inputHingeStudents can define efficiency, calculate it as a ratio of useful output to total input energy, and explain why no real system achieves 100% efficiency.
  • 3. Sankey diagrams — representing energy inputs, outputs and lossesStudents can construct and interpret Sankey diagrams to represent energy flow through a system, reading off efficiency and identifying where energy losses occur.
  • 4. Applying conservation of energy and efficiency to real systemsStudents can apply the law of conservation of energy and efficiency analysis to evaluate real energy systems and identify where improvements could be made.
