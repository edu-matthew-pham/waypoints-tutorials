Conservation of energy — total energy is maintained in any system

AC9S9U05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 9 Science · Physical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can state the law of conservation of energy and apply it to account for all energy in a system — including energy that is transferred as heat or sound rather than as useful output.

Lessons on this site

  • Where does the energy go?

Success criteria

  • States the law of conservation of energy correctly
  • Explains that 'wasted' energy is transferred to the environment, not destroyed
  • Accounts for all energy outputs (useful + wasted) in a simple system

Prior knowledge

Students can classify energy as kinetic or potential and explain energy transfers and transformations in simple systems (AC9S8U05). They can explain energy transfer and transformation in electrical circuits (AC9S6U03). They have qualitative understanding of energy transformation but have not yet applied conservation of energy as a quantitative law, or analysed system efficiency — the proportion of useful output energy — as a calculated quantity.

Width

Minimum
Students state that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed, and apply this to explain why the total energy in a closed system remains constant.
Typical
Students apply conservation of energy to trace all energy outputs in a system (useful + wasted); explain that 'wasted' energy is not lost — it is transferred to the environment, typically as heat or sound; connect to prior kinetic/potential energy knowledge from AC9S8U05; explain that conservation of energy applies even when energy appears to disappear.
Wider
  • Investigate the energy transformations in a bouncing ball — where does the kinetic energy go at each bounce, and can you account for all of it?
  • Apply conservation of energy to a pendulum system — trace energy between kinetic and potential forms over multiple swings and explain why the pendulum eventually stops

Hinge

Conservation of energy is the law the entire standard applies. Students who believe energy is 'used up' or 'disappears' in a system cannot correctly identify all outputs when calculating efficiency in N2, and will misinterpret Sankey diagrams as showing energy loss rather than energy redistribution. This misconception is common and must be resolved before efficiency analysis is introduced.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe misconception that energy is 'used up' or 'lost' in a transformation is persistent and directly blocks efficiency analysis. Students must explicitly encounter and resolve this before N2 — efficiency only makes sense as a ratio if total energy is conserved.
