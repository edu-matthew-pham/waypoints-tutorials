← AC9S5I02 Planning and conducting

Repeatable investigations require a recorded, reproducible method

AC9S5I02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan and record an investigation method in sufficient detail that another person could repeat it, and explain why repeatability is important for reliable findings.

Success criteria

  • Records an investigation method in sufficient detail for another student to repeat it
  • Explains why repeatability is important for the reliability of findings

Prior knowledge

Students can use provided scaffolds to plan fair tests (AC9S3I02, 3-4 band). They now take greater ownership — deciding variables independently, planning for repeatability, and considering field site permissions and risks without a provided scaffold.

Width

Minimum
Students record an investigation method for a given question in enough detail that another student could follow it, and explain why repeatability matters.
Typical
Students plan investigations across different approaches (experimental testing, field observation, research, virtual simulation), record methods in sufficient detail for reproduction, and explain that repeatable findings are more reliable than single-trial results.
Wider
  • Compare different investigation approaches for the same question (experimental vs field observation vs simulation) and evaluate the trade-offs in repeatability, control and ecological validity
  • Plan a field excursion to collect erosion data — using maps or aerial photographs to predict sites, planning observation methods and recording a method that could be repeated by another group
  • Consult with First Nations Australians to identify local areas that require permission before accessing — examining the intersection of scientific investigation and cultural protocols on Country
