Living things have basic needs

AC9S1U01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 1 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name the basic needs of plants and animals — air, water, food and shelter — and explain that all living things must have their needs met to survive.

Success criteria

  • Names the four basic needs of living things (air, water, food, shelter)
  • Gives at least one example of each need being met for a named plant or animal

Prior knowledge

Students can observe and describe features of living things and their environments (AC9SFU01, F band). They have not yet systematically identified needs of living things or described how habitat features meet those needs.

Width

Minimum
Students name the basic needs of plants and animals and give one example of each need being met for a named living thing.
Typical
Students identify and compare the needs of a variety of plants and animals including humans; explain that different living things have the same basic needs even if they meet them differently; and connect their own experiences (caring for pets, watering plants) to need-meeting.
Wider
  • Recognise how First Nations Australians care for living things — examining how traditional ecological knowledge addresses the needs of plants and animals on Country
  • Explore why caring for plants and animals matters beyond the individual — as sources of food, fibre and ecological balance
  • Learning from farmers, bush care volunteers or nursery owners about how they observe plant and animal needs and design habitats to meet them

Hinge

N2 requires students to understand what basic needs are before they can describe how habitat features meet those needs. Without knowing what air, water, food and shelter mean as needs, students cannot reason about which habitat features are relevant or why.
