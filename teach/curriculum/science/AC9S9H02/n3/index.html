← AC9S9H02 Science and technology advance together

The two-way relationship

AC9S9H02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate the two-directional relationship between science and technology — recognising that the relationship is iterative, not sequential, and can examine multiple cases where each has enabled the other in a cycle of development.

Success criteria

  • Can explain what 'two-directional' means in the science-technology relationship — not just state it.
  • Can provide an example where both directions are visible — technology enabling science AND science enabling technology in the same domain.
  • Does not treat the relationship as sequential — understands it as iterative and ongoing.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Science enabling technology.

Width

Minimum
Describe one domain where technology and science have advanced each other iteratively (e.g. medical imaging: understanding of EM radiation → X-ray technology → MRI understanding → MRI technology → new anatomical knowledge) and explain why the relationship is described as two-directional.
Typical
Investigate two or three domains where science and technology have advanced each other across multiple iterations, and explain how scientists and engineers make machines or processes more efficient using scientific understanding as the lever.
Wider
  • Examine how scientists and engineers make machines more energy efficient — connecting AC9S9U05 (conservation of energy, Sankey diagrams) to the applied science of engineering design.
  • Investigate how understanding of the nature of matter and energy has changed over time — from classical mechanics to quantum mechanics — and how each theoretical advance enabled new technologies (transistors, lasers, MRI) that in turn revealed new science.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe two-way relationship is the integrative claim of this descriptor. Students who have described individual cases of technology-enabling-science (Node 1) and science-enabling-technology (Node 2) need to coordinate these into a general claim: the relationship is reciprocal and iterative. The integration is conceptual — seeing the pattern across cases, not just collecting examples.
