Questioning and predicting

AC9S3I01 · Years 3–4 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

pose questions to explore observed patterns and relationships and make predictions based on observations

Prior knowledge

Students can pose simple questions and make predictions based on their experiences (AC9S1I01, 1-2 band). They have not yet posed questions that target patterns or relationships specifically, or made predictions that are explicitly grounded in prior observations rather than intuition.

By the end

Students pose investigable questions that target patterns and relationships in observed phenomena, and make predictions that are explicitly grounded in prior observations, distinguishing between questions that can be tested and those that cannot.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • posing questions about why some materials are used more often than others for particular products
  • predicting the effect on food chains when living things are removed from or die out in an area
  • consulting with First Nations Australians about how to predict the location of water sources from observation of landscape features
  • making predictions about the distances over which magnets will attract or repel each other

Waypoints

  • 1. Questions target patterns and relationshipsHingeStudents can pose investigable questions that go beyond naming or describing — targeting a pattern (what changes when X changes?) or relationship (how does X affect Y?) in an observed phenomenon.
  • 2. Predictions are grounded in prior observationsStudents can make predictions that are explicitly linked to prior observations or patterns they have noticed, and distinguish between a prediction (grounded in evidence) and a guess.
