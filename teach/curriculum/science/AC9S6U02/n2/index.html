← AC9S6U02 Earth, sun and solar system

Earth's rotation causes day and night

AC9S6U02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a model to explain how Earth's rotation on its axis causes day and night, and explain why different places on Earth experience daylight at different times.

Success criteria

  • Explains that day and night are caused by Earth's rotation on its axis
  • Uses a model or diagram to show that the side of Earth facing the sun has day and the side facing away has night

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Earth and planets orbit the sun; gravity keeps them in orbit.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that day and night are caused by Earth rotating on its axis, using a model or diagram to show the mechanism.
Typical
Students use 3D models or role-play to model Earth's rotation causing day and night; explain why it appears that the sun moves across the sky (it is Earth rotating, not the sun moving); explain why different regions on Earth are in daylight or darkness at the same moment; and connect rotation to the 24-hour day length.
Wider
  • Research First Nations Australians' understandings of the night sky and its use for timekeeping — examining how sky observation was used to track time and seasons before mechanical clocks
  • Use real-time views of Earth from space to observe which regions are in daylight and darkness at a given moment — connecting the model to live observation
  • Examine how different cultures have tracked day length for agricultural and ceremonial purposes — connecting observable sky phenomena to human timekeeping systems

Hinge

The geocentric misconception (sun moves around Earth) must be resolved at Node 2 before Node 3 axial tilt reasoning can proceed — students who believe the sun moves cannot model or understand why tilt causes different hemispheres to receive different sunlight amounts at different orbital positions. The model-building at N2 is the conceptual foundation for the more complex N3 spatial reasoning.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly believe the sun moves around Earth rather than that Earth rotates — geocentric intuition is persistent. The model-based reasoning chain (Earth rotates on its axis → the side facing the sun has day → the side facing away has night → one rotation = one day) must be established clearly using physical models before students can explain variable day length.
