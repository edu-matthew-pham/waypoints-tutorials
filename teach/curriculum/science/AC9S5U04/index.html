Particle model of matter

AC9S5U04 · Year 5 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explain observable properties of solids, liquids and gases by modelling the motion and arrangement of particles

Prior knowledge

Students can classify materials as solids or liquids by observable properties and explain changes of state using heat energy (AC9S3U04). They have not yet used a particle model to explain why solids, liquids and gases behave as they do, or examined gases as a third state of matter with distinct properties.

By the end

Students use a particle model — describing the arrangement, spacing and motion of particles — to explain the observable properties of solids, liquids and gases, and connect state changes to changes in particle behaviour when heat energy is added or removed.

Waypoints

  • 1. Gases have observable properties distinct from solids and liquidsHingeStudents can describe observable properties of gases — including that gases have mass, can be compressed, and take the shape and volume of their container — and distinguish these from the properties of solids and liquids.
  • 2. Particle arrangement and motion explain state propertiesHingeStudents can use a particle model to explain the observable properties of each state — describing how particle arrangement (close/far apart) and motion (vibrating/flowing/moving freely) differ across solids, liquids and gases.
  • 3. State changes are explained by changes in particle behaviourStudents can explain state changes (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation) using the particle model — describing how adding or removing heat energy changes particle motion and the transitions between states.
