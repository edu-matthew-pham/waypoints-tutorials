← AC9S5U04 Particle model of matter

State changes are explained by changes in particle behaviour

AC9S5U04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 5 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain state changes (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation) using the particle model — describing how adding or removing heat energy changes particle motion and the transitions between states.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least one state change using particle arrangement and motion — describing what happens to particles when heat is added or removed
  • Explains why mass is conserved during a state change using the particle model

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Particle arrangement and motion explain state properties.

Width

Minimum
Students explain melting using the particle model — describing how adding heat energy increases particle motion until particles can move past each other.
Typical
Students explain all common state changes (melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation) using the particle model, connecting heat energy addition/removal to changes in particle motion; explain why mass is conserved during state change (same particles, different arrangement); and connect this to their prior knowledge of observable state change (AC9S3U04).
Wider
  • Examine First Nations Australians' knowledge of state changes in beeswax and resins — connecting particle-model explanation to culturally significant material use
  • Investigate how changes of state in materials enable recycling — explaining at the particle level why glass and metals can be returned to liquid state and reformed
  • Connect particle model state change to the water cycle (AC9S4U02) — explaining evaporation and condensation as particle-level processes that drive the macroscopic cycle

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierState change explanation using the particle model requires students to integrate observable state change knowledge (AC9S3U04), particle model knowledge, and heat energy concepts in a single explanatory account. This is the synthesis move of the descriptor — applying the model to explain a familiar phenomenon at a new level of explanation.

Related standards
