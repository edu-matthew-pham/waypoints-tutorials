← AC9S5U04 Particle model of matter

Particle arrangement and motion explain state properties

AC9S5U04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a particle model to explain the observable properties of each state — describing how particle arrangement (close/far apart) and motion (vibrating/flowing/moving freely) differ across solids, liquids and gases.

Lessons on this site

Success criteria

  • Describes particle arrangement and motion for each of the three states
  • Uses the particle model to explain at least two observable properties — one for solids and one for gases

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Gases have observable properties distinct from solids and liquids.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the particle arrangement and motion for each of the three states and connect each to at least one observable property of that state.
Typical
Students explain all major observable properties of solids, liquids and gases using particle arrangement and motion — fixed shape (particles tightly packed, vibrating in place), liquid flow (particles close but able to move past each other), gas compression (particles far apart, moving freely) — and represent these models using diagrams or role-play.
Wider
  • Use role-play to model particle arrangement and motion in solids, liquids and gases — examining how collective behaviour of particles produces the observable properties of each state
  • Observe a virtual demonstration of coloured gases being compressed and explain the change in colour intensity using the particle model — applying the model to predict observable outcomes
  • Explore whether there is a 'correct' way to represent particles — examining different representations (diagrams, models, animations) and what each communicates and omits

Hinge

The particle model as an explanatory tool is directly required for Node 3 state change explanation — students must describe particle behaviour changing during melting, evaporation and condensation. Without the model internalised as explanatory (not just diagrammatic), state change explanations collapse to observable descriptions with no particle-level reasoning.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe particle model is the first time students are asked to explain observable properties using an invisible underlying structure. The reasoning chain — particles have arrangement and motion → arrangement and motion determine macroscopic behaviour → observable properties are evidence of particle-level structure — is the scaffold that makes the model genuinely explanatory rather than decorative labelling of diagrams.
