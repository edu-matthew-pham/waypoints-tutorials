← AC9S6U04 Reversible and irreversible changes

Irreversible changes produce new substances

AC9S6U04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that irreversible changes produce new substances with different properties, identify observable evidence of new substance formation, and give examples of common irreversible changes.

Success criteria

  • Explains that irreversible changes produce new substances with different properties
  • Names at least two observable indicators that an irreversible change has occurred
  • Distinguishes an irreversible change from a reversible change by the presence or absence of new substance formation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Reversible changes do not produce new substances.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that cooking and rusting produce new substances with different properties, and identify at least two observable indicators that an irreversible change has occurred.
Typical
Students examine the substances produced in cooking and rusting and compare them with original substances (different colour, texture, composition); explain that irreversible changes cannot be undone because new substances have formed; identify observable indicators of new substance formation; and compare energy requirements for reversible vs irreversible changes.
Wider
  • Examine First Nations Australians' knowledge of irreversible processes such as the use of fuels for torches — exploring how controlled combustion has been used purposefully across generations
  • Compare how the amount of heat energy added determines whether a reversible state change or an irreversible change occurs — examining the threshold between melting and burning for different materials
  • Connect irreversible changes to chemical change indicators studied in Y8 (AC9S8U07) — tracing how this foundational distinction develops into formal chemistry

Hinge

Understanding irreversibility as caused by new substance formation (not just visual change) is required for Node 3 systematic classification — students who identify irreversibility by appearance alone will fail on ambiguous cases, especially those where visual change is subtle. The substance-change criterion must be established here before it can be applied systematically.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often identify cooking as irreversible ('you can't uncook an egg') without understanding why — that new proteins have formed with different properties. Encountering multiple irreversible changes (rusting, burning, cooking, digestion) with evidence of new substance formation builds a more principled understanding of irreversibility grounded in substance change rather than visual appearance.
