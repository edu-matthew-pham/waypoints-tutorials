Developing investigable questions

AC9S7I01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Science · Inquiry

Checkpoint

Students can develop investigable questions to explore scientific models or test relationships between variables

Success criteria

  • Develops an investigable question that identifies variables or a model to explore
  • The question is testable with available methods and resources
  • Explains how the question connects to the model or relationship being investigated

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Investigable vs non-investigable questions.

Width

Minimum
Develop one investigable question to explore a model or test a relationship; identify the variables involved
Typical
Develop investigable questions for two different purposes — one to explore a model and one to test a relationship; explain how the question connects to the model or relationship
Wider
  • Develop investigable questions to explore scientific models such as 'How does particle theory explain the properties of substances?'
  • Develop investigable questions to test relationships such as 'How does the volume of a balloon change as it is heated and cooled?'
  • Develop multi-variable investigable questions where the relationship between variables is not immediately obvious

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly write questions that are too broad ('What affects plant growth?') or too narrow ('Does water help plants?') to be genuinely investigable. Developing questions across different science contexts — models, patterns, relationships — helps students calibrate the right scope and specificity. Each new context forces fresh calibration rather than pattern-matching a single example.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkQuestion development is not intuitive — students need an explicit reasoning scaffold: what is the model or relationship I'm exploring, what would change, and what would I measure? Without this scaffold, students produce questions that sound investigable but don't actually specify the variables or connect to the phenomenon. The scaffold is what makes breadth across contexts productive rather than just generating more poorly-formed questions.
