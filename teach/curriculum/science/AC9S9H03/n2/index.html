Analysing adoption cases

AC9S9H03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse specific cases of scientific knowledge or practice adoption, identifying the key factors that contributed to or inhibited adoption in each case.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least two adoption factors in a named case and explain how each operated.
  • Can explain whether adoption was rapid or slow and connect this to the factor profile.
  • Does not simply describe what was adopted — analyses the conditions that made adoption possible.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Adoption factors: the concept.

Width

Minimum
Analyse one case of adoption (e.g. solar panels, vaccination, recycling, land restoration through Landcare) and identify at least two factors that contributed to adoption — explaining how each factor operated in that case.
Typical
Analyse two adoption cases — selecting from different domains (health, environment, technology, Indigenous practices) — and for each, identify and explain the key factors, including any that inhibited adoption before it occurred.
Wider
  • Examine how government initiatives (e.g. Landcare, renewable energy subsidies) function as adoption-enabling mechanisms — connecting policy to the economic and accessibility factors in the framework.
  • Investigate the adoption trajectory of solar panels and battery storage — examining how manufacturing cost reduction, government incentives and community energy concerns combined to drive adoption, and analysing why earlier periods saw low adoption despite the technology existing.
  • Analyse how First Nations Australians' fire management practices are being adopted in contemporary fire management — examining which factors (evidence quality, economic benefit, government recognition, cultural respect) have been operative and which have posed barriers.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAdoption analysis can calcify around a single case type (typically environmental technology). Varying the cases across health practices, environmental management, technology adoption, and Indigenous knowledge practices ensures students encounter the full range of factor combinations and do not over-generalise from one domain.
