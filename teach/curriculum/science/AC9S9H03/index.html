Factors in science adoption

AC9S9H03 · Years 9–10 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse the key factors that contribute to science knowledge and practices being adopted more broadly by society

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how scientific knowledge and its applications affect people's lives (AC9S7H03) and examine how scientific communication shapes policy (AC9S7H04). They have not yet systematically analysed the factors that determine whether scientific knowledge or practices are widely adopted — moving from describing the relationship between science and society to analysing the conditions for adoption.

By the end

Students analyse key factors that contribute to scientific knowledge and practices being adopted more broadly by society — including evidence quality, accessibility, economic feasibility, government support, community trust, media communication and cultural alignment. Students can examine cases of adoption and non-adoption to identify which factors were decisive, including examples involving First Nations Australians' knowledge practices.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • researching citizen science projects related to public health
  • investigating how Dr Helen Mayo's research led to reduction in infant mortality
  • examining how assisted reproductive technologies became widely used
  • examining how Landcare supports adoption of land restoration practices
  • investigating how First Nations fire management practices are informing contemporary fire management
  • analysing factors leading to adoption of solar panels and battery storage
  • investigating how concern for environment led to widespread recycling

Waypoints

  • 1. Adoption factors: the conceptHingeStudents can explain that scientific knowledge being valid or published is not sufficient for broad societal adoption, and identify the types of factors that affect whether adoption occurs.
  • 2. Analysing adoption casesStudents can analyse specific cases of scientific knowledge or practice adoption, identifying the key factors that contributed to or inhibited adoption in each case.
