Explaining physical properties using particle theory

AC9S7U05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use particle theory to explain physical properties of substances including compressibility, viscosity and density

Success criteria

  • Uses particle theory to explain at least 2 physical properties of a given substance
  • Explanation references arrangement, motion or attraction — not just state labels
  • Correctly links a named property to the relevant particle behaviour

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Particle arrangement, motion and attraction in each state.

Width

Minimum
Use particle theory to explain at least two physical properties (e.g. compressibility, viscosity, density) of a given substance
Typical
Compare the physical properties of three substances using particle theory; explain why properties differ in terms of arrangement, motion and attraction
Wider
  • Investigate properties of materials such as density, melting point and compressibility and explain in terms of particle arrangement
  • Explain the process of diffusion in a liquid and a gas in terms of particle motion
  • Predict the properties of an unfamiliar substance given only its particle diagram; justify predictions and test against published data

Hinge

Using particle theory explanatorily — not just descriptively — is directly prerequisite for AC9S7U06 (Pure Substances and Mixtures), the immediate downstream standard. Selecting and justifying separation techniques requires students to explain property differences at the particle level; students who can only label diagrams cannot access the property-technique reasoning that underpins every node in U06.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThis is the key application node — using the model to explain rather than just describe. Each new property context (compressibility, viscosity, density, diffusion) forces a fresh application of the particle model, resolving the misconception that particle theory is a recall task rather than an explanatory framework. The prediction direction — properties from diagram — is particularly effective at exposing whether students can actually use the model.
