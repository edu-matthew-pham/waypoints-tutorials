Introducing the particle model

AC9S7U05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can match particle diagrams to states of matter and state the key ideas of particle theory

Success criteria

  • Matches particle diagrams to correct states of matter
  • States the 4 key ideas of particle theory
  • Identifies differences in arrangement, motion and attraction across states

Prior knowledge

Students know that matter exists as solids, liquids and gases and can describe observable properties. They have not used particle theory to explain properties or modelled particle behaviour at a submicroscopic level.

Width

Minimum
State the key ideas of particle theory (particles, spacing, motion, attraction); correctly match particle diagrams to solid, liquid and gas
Typical
Explain three observable properties of a substance using particle theory language; draw and annotate particle diagrams
Wider
  • Use and construct models, diagrams or virtual simulations to represent particle arrangement in different states
  • Evaluate the particle model as a model — what does it explain well, what does it not explain, and what evidence supports it?
  • Compare the particle model with how materials properties are described in Design and Technologies contexts

Hinge

Students who cannot reliably reproduce the four-component particle model (particles, spacing, motion, attraction) cannot use it as an explanatory framework in Node 2 onwards — they treat diagrams decoratively rather than as reasoning tools.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsMatching particle diagrams to states and stating the four key ideas is a precision task that students commonly approximate — they confuse spacing and motion, or omit attraction. Practice with feedback on the four-component structure (particles, spacing, motion, attraction) until the model is reliably reproduced is what helps here.
