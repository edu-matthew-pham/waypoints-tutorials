Elements, compounds and mixtures

AC9S8U06 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

classify matter as elements, compounds or mixtures and compare different representations of these, including 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional models, symbols for elements and formulas for molecules and compounds

Prior knowledge

Students can describe observable material properties (AC9SFU03), recognise physical change preserves material composition (AC9S2U03), explain solid/liquid/gas properties using a particle model (AC9S5U04), distinguish pure substances from mixtures and apply separation techniques (AC9S7U06), and describe particle arrangement, motion and attraction forces (AC9S7U05). The particle model treats particles as undifferentiated — particles have no identity (atom type). The concept of atom type, element as one atom type, and compound as chemically combined different atom types are all new. Chemical symbols and molecular formulas are new.

By the end

Students classify matter as elements (one atom type), compounds (two or more atom types chemically combined in fixed ratios) or mixtures (substances not chemically combined), and use chemical symbols, formulas and 2D/3D models to represent each — selecting representations based on what information each conveys.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using virtual and physical models to distinguish between elements and compounds in terms of types of atoms
  • examining how Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the elements in the first version of the periodic table and comparing his arrangement with the current version
  • explaining why elements are represented by symbols, compounds and molecules by formulas and mixtures by percentages
  • using representations to show the classification of matter as elements, compounds and different types of mixtures such as solutions, suspensions and colloids
  • examining the information conveyed by different types of representations of elements and compounds and identifying where and why these different representations are used
  • creating a timeline or models to show how the concept of an element has changed over time from Democritus to John Dalton

Waypoints

  • 1. Atoms and atom types — giving the particle model identityHingeStudents can explain that particles in the particle model are atoms, that atoms have type (element identity), and that different substances are made of different atom types.
  • 2. Elements — one atom type, symbols and the periodic tableStudents can define elements as pure substances consisting of only one type of atom and use chemical symbols to represent them.
  • 3. Compounds — different atom types chemically combinedHingeStudents can define compounds as substances containing two or more atom types chemically combined in fixed ratios, and use formulas to represent them.
  • 4. Mixtures — substances physically combined, properties retainedStudents can define mixtures, explain that components retain their properties and can be separated by physical means, and distinguish mixtures from compounds.
  • 5. Representations — comparing symbols, formulas and 2D/3D modelsStudents can compare representations of elements, compounds and mixtures and explain what information each representation conveys and where each is most useful.
