← AC9S8U06 Elements, compounds and mixtures

Representations — comparing symbols, formulas and 2D/3D models

AC9S8U06 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare representations of elements, compounds and mixtures and explain what information each representation conveys and where each is most useful.

Lessons on this site

  • Symbols, formulas or models?

Success criteria

  • Identifies what information a symbol, formula and 3D model each convey for a named substance
  • Explains one advantage and one limitation of each representation type
  • Selects the most appropriate representation for a given purpose and justifies the choice

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Mixtures — substances physically combined, properties retained.

Width

Minimum
Students identify what information is shown by a symbol, a formula and a 3D model for a named substance, and explain one advantage of each.
Typical
Students compare: symbols (identity, periodic table position — no structural info); molecular formulas (atom types and ratios, compact — no spatial info); structural formulas (bonding connections, 2D arrangement); 3D ball-and-stick/space-filling models (spatial arrangement, bond angles, relative size); particle diagrams for mixtures (relative proportions, unbonded); evaluate which representation is most useful for a given purpose; explain that no single representation captures everything.
Wider
  • Examine multiple representations of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) — symbol, molecular formula, structural formula, 3D model — and identify what each reveals and conceals about the molecule
  • Compare physical and digital 3D molecular models of the same compound and evaluate each for accuracy, utility and accessibility
