Solids, liquids and change of state

AC9S3U04 · Year 3 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate the observable properties of solids and liquids and how adding or removing heat energy leads to a change of state

Prior knowledge

Students can observe and describe properties of materials and sort them by observable features (AC9SFU03, F band). They have not yet formally defined solid and liquid using observable properties, investigated change of state, or connected heat energy addition/removal to state transitions.

By the end

Students describe the observable properties that distinguish solids from liquids, explain that adding or removing heat energy causes changes of state (solid ↔ liquid), and give examples of state change in everyday and cultural contexts — including how state change enables recycling of materials.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • observing the properties of substances and classifying them as solids (that hold their shape) or liquids (that fill the bottom of containers)
  • investigating ice melting or water freezing in a sealed bag and explaining their observations
  • using ice cubes, butter or chocolate to explore how changes of state involve the removal of heat or the addition of heat
  • investigating how changes of state in materials used by First Nations Australians such as beeswax or resins are important for their use
  • exploring how changes from solid to liquid and liquid to solid can help us recycle materials such as glass or plastics

Waypoints

  • 1. Solids and liquids have distinct observable propertiesHingeStudents can distinguish solids from liquids using observable properties — solids hold their shape, liquids take the shape of their container and flow — and correctly classify materials including ambiguous cases.
  • 2. Adding or removing heat causes change of stateStudents can explain that adding heat energy causes a solid to melt (become liquid) and removing heat energy causes a liquid to freeze (become solid), and describe what is observed during each change.
  • 3. State change is reversible and conserves the substanceStudents can explain that state changes are reversible (a melted solid can be refrozen), that the substance itself does not change during a state change (only its form changes), and use evidence from an investigation (mass before and after in a sealed container) to support this.
