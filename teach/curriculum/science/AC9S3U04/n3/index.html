← AC9S3U04 Solids, liquids and change of state

State change is reversible and conserves the substance

AC9S3U04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 3 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that state changes are reversible (a melted solid can be refrozen), that the substance itself does not change during a state change (only its form changes), and use evidence from an investigation (mass before and after in a sealed container) to support this.

Success criteria

  • Explains that state change is reversible and that the substance does not change — only its form
  • Predicts that the mass of a substance will be the same before and after melting in a sealed container
  • Distinguishes between a substance changing state and a substance changing into a different substance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Adding or removing heat causes change of state.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that melting and freezing are reversible and that the substance does not change — only its state — giving at least one example.
Typical
Students investigate ice melting or water freezing in a sealed container, explain that the mass does not change even though the state does, describe how the change can be reversed, and distinguish state change (reversible, substance unchanged) from chemical change (new substance formed).
Wider
  • Explore how the reversibility of state change is used to recycle materials — glass, metals, plastics — examining which materials can be returned to a liquid state and reformed
  • Investigate whether mass changes when a substance changes state by predicting and then measuring mass before and after melting in a sealed container — applying measurement skills to test a prediction
  • Examine a case where state change appears irreversible (burning) and discuss why — introducing the distinction between physical and chemical change informally

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly believe that melting changes what the substance is — ice becomes water, which is different. The reasoning chain — same substance, same particles, just different arrangement → mass unchanged → change is reversible → this is different from a chemical change — is the conceptual foundation for all later chemistry work on physical vs chemical change (AC9S8U07).
