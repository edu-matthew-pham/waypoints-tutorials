← AC9S3H01 Data and scientific explanations

Data is recorded evidence from observations

AC9S3H01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Science · Human endeavour · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish data from explanation — identifying what counts as data (recorded observations, measurements, survey results) and what counts as an explanation (an account of why or how).

Lessons on this site

Success criteria

  • Distinguishes data from explanation given a mixed set of statements from a scenario
  • Explains that data is collected through observation or measurement, and that the scientist uses the data to construct an explanation

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how people use science in daily life and use patterns to make scientific predictions (AC9S1H01, 1-2 band). They have not yet examined the specific role of data in developing scientific explanations or how the scientific community shares and evaluates explanations.

Width

Minimum
Students identify examples of data and examples of explanations from a given scenario and describe the difference between the two.
Typical
Students explain that data is collected through systematic observation or measurement, and that data supports (but does not automatically produce) an explanation — the scientist interprets the data.
Wider
  • Examine a simple scientific journal article or report and identify the data section and explanation/conclusion section — exploring why scientists communicate this way
  • Explore how First Nations Australians develop and test predictions through systematic observation — for example, observing landscape features or seasonal indicators — and discuss what makes this a form of data collection
  • Investigate the difference between reliable and unreliable data (one observation vs many observations, careful vs careless measurement)

Hinge

The data/explanation distinction is the foundational concept for the entire standard. Students who cannot separate what was recorded from what it means cannot examine the data-to-explanation pathway in Node 3, and will conflate evidence and conclusion in all subsequent inquiry work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe data/explanation distinction is non-obvious at Year 4. Students tend to treat observations and explanations as the same kind of thing — 'I saw X therefore X happens'. Making the distinction explicit (data describes what was recorded; explanation accounts for why it happened using the data) is a necessary scaffold before students can examine how scientists move from data to explanation.
