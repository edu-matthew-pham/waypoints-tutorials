Data and scientific explanations

AC9S3H01 · Years 3–4 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

examine how people use data to develop scientific explanations

Prior knowledge

Students can describe how people use science in daily life and use patterns to make scientific predictions (AC9S1H01, 1-2 band). They have not yet examined the specific role of data in developing scientific explanations or how the scientific community shares and evaluates explanations.

By the end

Students examine how scientists and other knowledge-holders use data to develop and support scientific explanations, identifying what data is, how it is collected, and how it supports — rather than simply produces — an explanation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining age-appropriate scientific journal articles, identifying common text features and exploring why the scientific community might have conventions for sharing information about data and explanations
  • viewing or listening to documentaries or news reports that feature researchers and identifying how they talk about their area of research, particularly references to observations, data and evidence
  • investigating how ecologists use food chain data to develop explanations for population decline of native species such as the Richmond birdwing butterfly, and to develop strategies to increase their population
  • explore how hydrologists use rainfall and water use data to explain the amount of water flowing in rivers and why this changes over time
  • investigating how First Nations Australians test predictions and gather data in the development of technologies and processes

Waypoints

  • 1. Data is recorded evidence from observationsHingeStudents can distinguish data from explanation — identifying what counts as data (recorded observations, measurements, survey results) and what counts as an explanation (an account of why or how).
  • 2. Scientists collect data to answer questionsStudents can describe how scientists or knowledge-holders collect data in response to a question, including the type of data collected and why that data is relevant to the question.
  • 3. Data supports the development of explanationsStudents can examine a real-world case where data was used to develop a scientific explanation, tracing the connection between the data collected and the explanation produced.
