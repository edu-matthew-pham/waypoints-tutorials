Connecting and comparing data sources

AC9S9I05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can connect and compare data from multiple sources to identify consistencies, inconsistencies and what they mean for scientific claims — including discussing the validity of extrapolation from data.

Success criteria

  • Can identify consistency and inconsistency across two data sources on the same topic.
  • Can explain what an inconsistency implies for confidence in a conclusion.
  • Can discuss the validity of an extrapolation — identifying the assumption it requires.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identifying and explaining anomalies.

Width

Minimum
Compare data from two sources on the same topic, identify one point of consistency and one inconsistency, and explain what the inconsistency means for the confidence of the conclusion.
Typical
Connect data from multiple sources — primary data from an investigation and secondary published data — to evaluate whether findings are consistent, identifying inconsistencies and discussing their implications for the validity of conclusions. Discuss the validity of extrapolating beyond the data collected.
Wider
  • Analyse representations of data from atmospheric monitoring and ice cores to highlight inconsistencies — examining how scientists deal with data from different sources that don't perfectly align.
  • Examine the validity of extrapolating from experimental data (e.g. extrapolating a heating curve beyond the measured range) — considering what assumptions are required and when extrapolation is or isn't justified.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierMulti-source data connection requires integrating pattern identification (Node 1), anomaly analysis (Node 2) and validity reasoning (AC9S9I06) into a coherent analytical judgment: 'These sources agree on X; they disagree on Y; the disagreement suggests Z about the reliability of one or both.' This integration is the descriptor's analytical demand.
