← AC9S5U02 Weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition

Erosion, transportation and deposition move and redistribute material

AC9S5U02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 5 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that erosion picks up and moves weathered material, transportation carries it, and deposition lays it down in a new location — describing the agents involved (water, wind, ice, gravity) and the landforms each process produces.

Success criteria

  • Explains the three-stage sequence: erosion (picks up) → transportation (carries) → deposition (lays down)
  • Names at least two agents of erosion and identifies a landform feature produced by each

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Weathering breaks down rocks in place.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the sequence of erosion → transportation → deposition with one agent (water or wind), naming the agent and describing what happens at each stage.
Typical
Students describe erosion, transportation and deposition by multiple agents (water, wind, ice, gravity), identify the landforms each produces (river deltas, sand dunes, alluvial fans, cliffs), compare fast and slow timescales of these processes, and give local or regional examples.
Wider
  • Use aerial photographs or maps to identify patterns in erosion site locations at local or regional scale — examining how topography and land cover influence erosion distribution
  • Model landscape erosion using sand, gravel, soil and water to explore factors that affect erosion rates — connecting model behaviour to real landscape processes
  • Examine the effects of significant rainfall (monsoon, flood events) on transportation and deposition of river sediments in the Asia-Pacific region — exploring rapid event-driven change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents typically think of erosion only as water cutting into riverbanks, missing wind erosion, glacial erosion, gravity-driven mass movement and coastal erosion. Encountering erosion agents across varied landform contexts builds a more complete and accurate model of how landscapes change.
