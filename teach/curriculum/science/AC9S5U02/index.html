Weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition

AC9S5U02 · Year 5 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

describe how weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition cause slow or rapid change to Earth's surface

Prior knowledge

Students can compare observable properties of soils and rocks and explain their importance as Earth resources (AC9S3U02). They have not yet distinguished weathering from erosion, examined the agents driving each, or connected these processes to landscape change over time.

By the end

Students describe how weathering breaks down rock material and how erosion, transportation and deposition move and redistribute that material to change Earth's surface — across both slow geological and rapid event-driven timescales — and examine human impacts on these processes.

Waypoints

  • 1. Weathering breaks down rocks in placeHingeStudents can distinguish weathering from erosion — explaining that weathering breaks down rock material in place through mechanical and biological means — and identify agents of weathering with examples.
  • 2. Erosion, transportation and deposition move and redistribute materialStudents can explain that erosion picks up and moves weathered material, transportation carries it, and deposition lays it down in a new location — describing the agents involved (water, wind, ice, gravity) and the landforms each process produces.
  • 3. Human activity changes erosion ratesStudents can explain how human activity alters natural erosion rates and predict the consequences of specific land use changes on local landscapes.
