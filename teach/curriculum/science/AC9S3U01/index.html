Life cycles

AC9S3U01 · Year 3 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compare characteristics of living and non-living things and examine the differences between the life cycles of plants and animals

Prior knowledge

Students can identify and describe features of living things and group them based on observable characteristics (AC9SFU01, F band; AC9S1U01, 1-2 band). They have not yet formally compared living and non-living things using a characteristic framework, or examined the different structural forms life cycles can take (metamorphic vs non-metamorphic).

By the end

Students compare characteristics of living and non-living things using observable evidence, and examine how plant and animal life cycles differ in structure — distinguishing metamorphic from non-metamorphic cycles and connecting the physical characteristics of organisms to their life stage.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • classifying a collection of objects as living, once living or non-living and explaining their reasoning
  • observing and describing differences between metamorphic (such as butterflies, beetles or frogs) and non-metamorphic life cycles of animals, including humans
  • comparing the physical characteristics of an animal such as a frog or moth with its activity at different stages of its life cycle
  • representing stages of a plant or animal's life cycle using drawings, digital photographs, graphic organisers or concrete materials
  • investigating how First Nations Australians understand and utilise the life cycles of certain species

Waypoints

  • 1. Living, once living and non-living things have distinct characteristicsHingeStudents can classify objects as living, once living or non-living and explain their reasoning using observable characteristics (growth, reproduction, response to environment, need for nutrients).
  • 2. Life cycles describe stages of growth and changeStudents can describe the life cycle of a named plant and a named animal, identifying the stages in sequence and explaining what changes occur at each stage.
  • 3. Metamorphic and non-metamorphic cycles differ in structureStudents can distinguish between metamorphic life cycles (dramatic change in body form between stages) and non-metamorphic cycles (gradual growth without structural transformation), and give examples of each.
