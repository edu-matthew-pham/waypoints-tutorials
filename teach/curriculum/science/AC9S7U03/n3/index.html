Eclipses: solar and lunar

AC9S7U03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the conditions required for solar and lunar eclipses and why eclipses do not occur every month

Lessons on this site

  • Why isn't there an eclipse every month?

Success criteria

  • States the conditions required for both solar and lunar eclipses
  • Correctly distinguishes umbra from penumbra
  • Explains why eclipses don't occur every month

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Moon phases and the lunar cycle.

Width

Minimum
Explain the conditions required for a solar and lunar eclipse; correctly distinguish umbra from penumbra; explain why eclipses don't occur every month
Typical
Model both eclipse types using a light source; explain the role of the moon's orbital tilt in eclipse frequency
Wider
  • Use physical models or virtual simulations to explain cyclic patterns of lunar and solar eclipses
  • Research First Nations Australians' oral traditions and cultural recordings of solar and lunar eclipses; compare with contemporary understandings
  • Analyse historical eclipse records — explain why ancient cultures found eclipses unpredictable and what understanding was missing

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEclipse explanation requires 3D spatial reasoning about three bodies simultaneously — the hardest conceptual step in this standard. Students who rely on 2D diagrams alone consistently misexplain why eclipses are rare. Broader contexts — historical records, cultural accounts, simulation — help students build the 3D mental model that 2D diagrams cannot provide.
