Seasons

AC9S7U03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain seasons as caused by axial tilt and correctly relate tilt to solar angle and day length at different latitudes

Lessons on this site

  • Why is summer hot?

Success criteria

  • Explains seasons as caused by axial tilt, not distance from the sun
  • Links tilt to solar angle and day length
  • Correctly identifies opposite seasons in Northern and Southern hemispheres

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Eclipses: solar and lunar.

Width

Minimum
Explain that seasons are caused by Earth's axial tilt, not distance from the sun; correctly link tilt to solar angle and day length
Typical
Use a model to demonstrate why the Southern and Northern hemispheres experience opposite seasons simultaneously
Wider
  • Compare seasonal patterns at the equator, temperate zones, and poles — explain differences using the tilt model
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' seasonal calendars (e.g. six-season calendars) and compare with the four-season European model
  • Analyse real insolation data for two locations at different latitudes across a year; explain the patterns using the tilt model

Hinge

The distance misconception — seasons caused by proximity to the sun — is explicitly noted as assessed and extremely persistent. Students who hold it produce confidently wrong season explanations and cannot correctly account for opposite hemispheric seasons.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe distance misconception — seasons caused by proximity to the sun — is extremely persistent and directly assessed. Without resolving it here, students produce confidently wrong answers. High width investment across multiple latitude contexts (equator, temperate, polar) and cultural calendar comparisons forces students to confront the misconception repeatedly until the tilt explanation displaces it.
