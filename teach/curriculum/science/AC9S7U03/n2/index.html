Moon phases and the lunar cycle

AC9S7U03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain moon phases as a result of the moon's position relative to Earth and sun and sequence them correctly

Lessons on this site

  • Why does the moon change shape?

Success criteria

  • Correctly sequences all 8 moon phases
  • Explains phases as caused by the moon's position, not Earth's shadow
  • Does not confuse moon phases with eclipses

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Earth's rotation, revolution and axial tilt.

Width

Minimum
Correctly sequence all 8 moon phases; explain that phases result from the moon's position relative to Earth and sun, not Earth's shadow
Typical
Model moon phases using a light source and spheres; predict the phase visible from Earth at a given moon position
Wider
  • Analyse a month of real moon phase data — identify the pattern, calculate the period, identify anomalies
  • Research knowledges held by First Nations Australians regarding the phases of the moon and the lunar cycle
  • Investigate how lunar calendars from different cultures use moon phases differently and why

Hinge

The shadow misconception — phases caused by Earth's shadow — persists directly into eclipse explanation in Node 3. Students who hold it will explain lunar eclipses as moon phases and cannot correctly distinguish the two phenomena.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSequencing all 8 moon phases correctly and explaining the position-based cause is a precision task that students commonly get partially right. The common error — confusing phases with Earth's shadow — is persistent and needs targeted correction through repeated modelling practice, not more contexts.
