Communicating

AC9S1I06 · Years 1–2 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

write and create texts to communicate observations, findings and ideas, using everyday and scientific vocabulary

Prior knowledge

Students can share ideas informally (F band). They now begin to create texts — written, drawn, modelled or role-played — that communicate observations and ideas using both everyday and scientific vocabulary.

By the end

Students write and create texts to communicate science observations and ideas, using scientific vocabulary alongside everyday language and selecting from a range of forms (drawing, model, label, role-play, sequential representation).

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring difference between everyday and scientific vocabulary
  • acknowledging First Nations Australians' ways of representing and sharing observations
  • creating models of plant or animal habitats
  • representing seasonal plant changes using sequential drawings or photographs
  • representing push and pull forces using role-play, labels, arrows or drawings
  • role-playing how people use patterns for predictions

Waypoints

  • 1. Texts communicate science observations using scientific vocabularyStudents can create a text (written, drawn, modelled or performed) that communicates a science observation or idea, using at least two science vocabulary terms correctly.
