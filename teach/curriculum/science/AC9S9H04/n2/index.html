Examining cases of society-driven research

AC9S9H04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine specific cases where the values and needs of society have influenced the focus of scientific research — identifying the societal driver and the resulting research direction.

Success criteria

  • Can identify the societal need or value that drove a named research direction.
  • Can explain the connection — not just list the case but trace the pathway from value/need to research priority.
  • Does not treat all research as societally driven in the same way — can distinguish government mandate, market incentive, community need and cultural value as distinct mechanisms.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Who decides what gets researched?.

Width

Minimum
Examine one case where a societal need or value drove a specific research direction (e.g. need to minimise greenhouse gas production leading to renewable energy research; need for food security leading to agricultural biotechnology research) and explain the connection between the societal need and the research focus.
Typical
Examine two or three cases of society-driven research from different domains — health, environment, technology — identifying the societal driver in each and explaining how it shaped what research was done and what wasn't.
Wider
  • Examine how Australia has developed AI systems for IVF viability prediction — connecting community health needs and values (fertility, family formation) to a specific research direction that has produced technological outcomes.
  • Investigate how First Nations Peoples of the Torres Strait are at the forefront of developing measures to prevent transfer of infectious diseases and pests — examining how community health sovereignty shapes the focus of this scientific work.
  • Examine how the need to minimise greenhouse gas production has led to scientific advances in carbon capture, renewable energy, and green chemistry — tracing the societal value to multiple research streams.
  • Investigate why many manufacturers are adopting green chemistry processes — examining the economic, regulatory and environmental values driving this research and development direction.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSingle-case analysis can produce an oversimplified model of the society-research relationship. Varying cases across health, environment and technology shows that the relationship operates through different mechanisms — direct government mandate, market incentive, community advocacy, regulatory pressure — preventing any single mechanism from being treated as universal.
