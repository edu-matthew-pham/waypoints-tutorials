Who decides what gets researched?

AC9S9H04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Science · Human endeavour · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that scientific research requires funding and that decisions about what research is funded reflect societal values and priorities — introducing the concept that research focus is not determined by science alone.

Success criteria

  • Can explain that research requires funding and that funding decisions reflect priorities.
  • Can identify at least one way societal values influence research direction.
  • Does not treat research focus as determined by scientific curiosity alone — understands external shaping.

Prior knowledge

Students can examine how scientific communication shapes policy and societal responses (AC9S7H04), and have analysed adoption factors (AC9S9H03). They have not yet examined the reverse direction — how societal values and needs shape what research is funded and pursued in the first place.

Width

Minimum
Explain that scientific research requires funding, that funding bodies (governments, industries, foundations) make decisions about which research to support, and that these decisions reflect priorities beyond scientific merit alone.
Typical
Describe how governments, industries and research institutions determine research funding priorities, and identify examples of how these priorities reflect current societal values and needs (e.g. health concerns driving biomedical research funding; climate concern driving renewable energy research).
Wider
  • Explore how governments determine which scientific research projects are funded — examining specific funding bodies (ARC, NHMRC in Australia) and the criteria they use.
  • Consider why some societally important questions receive less funding than their importance might warrant — examining how commercial viability, political priorities or cultural biases can shape funding allocation.

Hinge

Students who do not understand that research direction is shaped by funding decisions will treat the cases at Node 2 as coincidences rather than systemic patterns. The funding-priority-values chain is the reasoning scaffold needed for meaningful examination of why particular research exists.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe claim that societal values shape research is not obvious — students typically think scientists choose what to investigate based on curiosity alone. Scaffold the argument: 'A scientist needs equipment, time and a lab. These require money. Money comes from funding bodies with priorities. Therefore the research that gets done reflects those priorities.' Making the causal chain explicit is the learning.
