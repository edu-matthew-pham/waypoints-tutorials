Wave and particle models

AC9S9U04 · Year 9 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use wave and particle models to describe energy transfer through different mediums and examine the usefulness of each model for explaining phenomena

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how light travels in straight paths and describe reflection and refraction (AC9S5U03). They can explain energy transfer and transformation in electrical circuits (AC9S6U03) and classify energy as kinetic or potential (AC9S8U05). They can use particle theory to explain physical properties of substances (AC9S7U05). They have encountered both particle and wave ideas separately but have not yet applied them as competing or complementary models for explaining energy transfer, or evaluated which model is more useful for a given phenomenon.

By the end

Students use wave and particle models to describe how energy is transferred through different mediums — including sound, heat and light — and examine the usefulness and limitations of each model for explaining phenomena including electromagnetic radiation.

Waypoints

  • 1. The particle model of energy transfer — conduction and convectionStudents can explain conduction and convection using the particle model, describing how particle motion and collisions transfer energy through matter.
  • 2. The wave model of energy transfer — soundHingeStudents can describe sound as a wave and explain how sound energy is transferred through a medium using the wave model, including the relationship between wave properties and sound characteristics.
  • 3. The particle model of electricity — static charge and currentStudents can explain static electricity and electrical current using the particle model of charge, and connect particle behaviour to concepts of voltage, conductors and insulators.
  • 4. The wave model of light and electromagnetic radiationStudents can describe light and electromagnetic radiation as waves and explain how the wave model accounts for phenomena including reflection, refraction and the electromagnetic spectrum.
  • 5. Evaluating model usefulness — wave vs particle for different phenomenaStudents can evaluate the usefulness of wave and particle models for explaining different energy transfer phenomena and identify where each model succeeds or fails.
