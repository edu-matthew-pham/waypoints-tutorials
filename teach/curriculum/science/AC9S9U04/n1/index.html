The particle model of energy transfer — conduction and convection

AC9S9U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain conduction and convection using the particle model, describing how particle motion and collisions transfer energy through matter.

Lessons on this site

  • How does heat get through?

Success criteria

  • Explains conduction using particle collisions as the transfer mechanism
  • Explains convection using bulk particle movement driven by density differences
  • Applies the particle model to explain why a named material is a good conductor or insulator

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how light travels in straight paths and describe reflection and refraction (AC9S5U03). They can explain energy transfer and transformation in electrical circuits (AC9S6U03) and classify energy as kinetic or potential (AC9S8U05). They can use particle theory to explain physical properties of substances (AC9S7U05). They have encountered both particle and wave ideas separately but have not yet applied them as competing or complementary models for explaining energy transfer, or evaluated which model is more useful for a given phenomenon.

Width

Minimum
Students explain conduction as energy transfer through particle collisions in a solid, and convection as bulk movement of particles carrying energy in a fluid.
Typical
Students apply the particle model to explain why metals conduct heat well (closely packed particles, efficient collision transfer) while insulators do not; explain convection currents as density-driven particle movement; connect to AC9S7U05's particle theory; describe how First Nations Australians' traditional clothing and bedding exploit or minimise conduction and convection.
Wider
  • Investigate insulation designs from First Nations Australians' clothing and bedding across different climate regions — what particle-level mechanisms do the materials exploit?
  • Design a fair test comparing the insulating properties of two materials and explain the results using the particle model

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents have prior particle theory (AC9S7U05) applied to physical properties but not to energy transfer. Explicitly connecting particle motion to energy transfer — collision as the mechanism, not just 'heat moves through the material' — establishes the particle model as an explanatory tool before the wave model is introduced.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepConduction and convection in varied material and cultural contexts build the model's explanatory range before it is compared with the wave model.
