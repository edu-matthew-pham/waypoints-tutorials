The particle model of electricity — static charge and current

AC9S9U04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain static electricity and electrical current using the particle model of charge, and connect particle behaviour to concepts of voltage, conductors and insulators.

Lessons on this site

  • Where does static charge come from?
  • How does current flow?

Success criteria

  • Explains static electricity as electron transfer between materials producing charge imbalance
  • Describes electrical current as directed electron flow through a conductor
  • Distinguishes conductors from insulators using the particle model (free vs bound electrons)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: The wave model of energy transfer — sound.

Width

Minimum
Students explain static electricity as the accumulation of charge due to electron transfer, and electrical current as the flow of electrons through a conductor.
Typical
Students explain charging by friction, induction and contact using electron transfer; describe current as directed electron flow driven by voltage; explain conductors as materials with free electrons and insulators as materials without; connect to electrical circuit concepts from AC9S6U03; explain electrostatic discharge as rapid electron movement to neutralise charge imbalance.
Wider
  • Investigate an electrostatics phenomenon (lightning, Van de Graaff generator) and explain it fully using the particle model of charge
  • Examine how semiconductor materials — with intermediate conductivity — challenge the simple conductor/insulator binary, and explore applications in electronics

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents have prior knowledge of circuits (AC9S6U03) but at the observable level — components, connections, current as a functional concept. The particle model reframes current as electron flow and extends the model to static electricity, which has no circuit equivalent. This is a model extension, not revision.
