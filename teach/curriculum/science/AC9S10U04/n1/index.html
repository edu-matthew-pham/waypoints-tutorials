Solar radiation and Earth's energy input

AC9S10U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how solar radiation reaches Earth and identify the factors that determine how much energy is absorbed versus reflected by the surface and atmosphere.

Lessons on this site

  • Where does the sunlight go?

Success criteria

  • Identifies the sun as the primary energy input to Earth's climate system
  • Distinguishes absorbed from reflected solar radiation with at least one surface example
  • Explains why different surfaces absorb or reflect different proportions of solar radiation

Prior knowledge

Students can represent the carbon cycle and explain how combustion, photosynthesis and respiration depend on interactions between Earth's spheres (AC9S9U03). They can describe how Earth's tilt and revolution produce seasonal change (AC9S6U02) and model food web energy flow through ecosystems (AC9S7U02). They have not yet examined energy exchange mechanisms between spheres (radiation, conduction, convection), the greenhouse effect as a quantitative balance, or how perturbations to that balance produce measurable global-scale patterns.

Width

Minimum
Students identify the sun as Earth's primary energy source and distinguish between absorbed and reflected radiation using one surface type as an example (e.g. ocean vs ice).
Typical
Students compare absorption and reflection across surface types (ocean, ice, land, vegetation); introduce albedo as a concept; describe why the angle of incidence affects energy input at different latitudes.
Wider
  • Investigate albedo data for different surfaces and predict how land-use change affects the local energy budget
  • Compare solar energy input at the equator vs poles and explain why this drives atmospheric and oceanic circulation

Hinge

All subsequent nodes build on the concept of an energy input that can be absorbed, reflected, or re-emitted. Students who treat solar energy as uniformly heating Earth without understanding the absorbed/reflected distinction cannot grasp why surface changes (melting ice, deforestation) alter the energy balance in N2, or why the greenhouse effect is about re-emitted rather than incoming radiation in N3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly assume the sun simply 'heats the Earth' without differentiating absorbed vs reflected energy. Making the energy input split explicit — and anchoring it to a concrete surface comparison — is the foundation the energy balance model in N2 requires.
