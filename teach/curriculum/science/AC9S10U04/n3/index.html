The greenhouse effect — trapping outgoing radiation

AC9S10U04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how greenhouse gases in the atmosphere trap outgoing infrared radiation and describe the role of this mechanism in maintaining Earth's surface temperature.

Lessons on this site

  • How do greenhouse gases warm the Earth?

Success criteria

  • Explains that Earth emits infrared radiation and that greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit it
  • Distinguishes natural greenhouse effect from enhanced greenhouse effect
  • Locates the greenhouse mechanism correctly within the energy balance model from N2 (atmosphere slows outgoing, not incoming, energy)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Energy balance between Earth's spheres.

Width

Minimum
Students describe that Earth emits infrared radiation and that greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit it, slowing energy loss to space.
Typical
Students name key greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, H₂O vapour, N₂O); distinguish natural vs enhanced greenhouse effect; explain why Earth would be far colder without the natural greenhouse effect; describe how the atmosphere's greenhouse function fits within the N2 energy balance model.
Wider
  • Compare the greenhouse effects of different gases using global warming potential data and explain why CH₄ has greater short-term impact than CO₂ despite lower concentration
  • Trace how a molecule of CO₂ emitted by combustion eventually affects the energy balance model built in N2

Hinge

The greenhouse mechanism is the conceptual link between the energy balance model (N2) and the explanation of climate change (N4). Students who conflate the greenhouse effect with ozone depletion, or who cannot locate the mechanism within the energy flow model, cannot explain how increasing greenhouse gas concentrations alter the balance, which is the core demand of the descriptor.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe greenhouse effect carries two persistent misconceptions: conflation with ozone depletion, and the belief that greenhouse gases trap incoming (rather than outgoing) radiation. These must be addressed explicitly before students can correctly place the mechanism in the energy balance model. Explicit comparison of natural vs enhanced greenhouse effect helps here.
