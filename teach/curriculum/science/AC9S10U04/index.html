Global climate change

AC9S10U04 · Year 10 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use models of energy flow between the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere to explain patterns of global climate change

Prior knowledge

Students can represent the carbon cycle and explain how combustion, photosynthesis and respiration depend on interactions between Earth's spheres (AC9S9U03). They can describe how Earth's tilt and revolution produce seasonal change (AC9S6U02) and model food web energy flow through ecosystems (AC9S7U02). They have not yet examined energy exchange mechanisms between spheres (radiation, conduction, convection), the greenhouse effect as a quantitative balance, or how perturbations to that balance produce measurable global-scale patterns.

By the end

Students use models of energy flow between the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere to explain patterns of global climate change, including analysing indicators of change and evaluating strategies to reduce or mitigate effects.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the role of radiation from the sun and how its interactions with atmosphere, ocean and land are the foundation for the global climate system
  • investigating indicators of climate change such as changes in ocean and atmospheric temperatures, sea levels, biodiversity, species distribution, permafrost and sea ice
  • identifying changes in global climate over time and exploring visualisations and simulations
  • examining the factors that drive deep ocean currents and their role in regulating global climate
  • investigating how quantum computers enhance modelling of complex weather and climate systems
  • predicting changes to the Earth system and identifying strategies to reduce or mitigate climate change

Waypoints

  • 1. Solar radiation and Earth's energy inputHingeStudents can describe how solar radiation reaches Earth and identify the factors that determine how much energy is absorbed versus reflected by the surface and atmosphere.
  • 2. Energy balance between Earth's spheresHingeStudents can describe how energy flows between the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere and explain what it means for Earth to be in energy balance.
  • 3. The greenhouse effect — trapping outgoing radiationHingeStudents can explain how greenhouse gases in the atmosphere trap outgoing infrared radiation and describe the role of this mechanism in maintaining Earth's surface temperature.
  • 4. Human activity, greenhouse gas concentrations and energy imbalanceStudents can explain how human activities increase atmospheric greenhouse gas concentrations and describe how this creates an energy imbalance that drives warming.
  • 5. Indicators of climate change across Earth's spheresStudents can identify and interpret multiple indicators of climate change and explain each as evidence of the energy imbalance described in N4.
  • 6. Using energy flow models to explain and evaluate responses to climate changeStudents can use the energy flow model to explain patterns of global climate change and evaluate strategies designed to reduce or mitigate its effects in terms of the model.
