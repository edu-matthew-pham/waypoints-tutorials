Indicators of climate change across Earth's spheres

AC9S10U04 n5 · waypoint 5 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and interpret multiple indicators of climate change and explain each as evidence of the energy imbalance described in N4.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: causes, evidence and responses

Success criteria

  • Names at least three distinct climate change indicators across different spheres
  • Explains the mechanism behind at least two indicators using the energy flow model
  • Interprets a climate data visualisation as evidence of energy imbalance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Human activity, greenhouse gas concentrations and energy imbalance.

Width

Minimum
Students name at least three indicators of climate change (e.g. rising atmospheric temperature, sea level rise, reduced sea ice) and explain one in terms of the energy imbalance model.
Typical
Students interpret real climate data for multiple indicators; explain the mechanism behind each (ocean warming → thermal expansion → sea level rise; melting ice → albedo feedback; warming → species distribution shifts); recognise that indicators are interdependent within the Earth system.
Wider
  • Analyse a visualisation of multiple climate indicators over time and identify which sphere each primarily reflects; explain why some indicators lag behind the energy imbalance
  • Investigate one indicator in depth — e.g. permafrost thaw — and trace its contribution to a feedback loop within the energy model

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepIndicators span all four spheres and multiple data types. Working across varied contexts — not just atmospheric temperature — builds the 'patterns' reading the descriptor demands and prevents reductive climate change understanding (change = warming only).
