Construct evidence-based arguments ethically

AC9S9I07 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

construct arguments based on analysis of a variety of evidence to support conclusions or evaluate claims, and consider any ethical issues and cultural protocols associated with accessing, using or citing secondary data or information

Prior knowledge

Students can construct evidence-based arguments (AC9S7I07). They have not yet applied ethical considerations and cultural protocols specifically to secondary data use, or constructed arguments based on analysis of multiple sources — including evaluation of source credibility as part of the argument.

By the end

Students construct arguments based on analysis of a variety of evidence — evaluating the credibility and relevance of multiple sources to support a conclusion or evaluate a claim. Students consider ethical issues associated with accessing, using and citing secondary data, and apply cultural protocols when working with First Nations knowledges — including acknowledging contributions and the relationship between Indigenous knowledge and contemporary science.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining secondary data for source credibility and data validity
  • identifying multiple consistent evidence sources for a claim
  • interrogating evidence and reasoning for claims about ancient artefact age
  • researching methods used in media-reported studies to evaluate headline validity
  • examining secondary data for personal information and appropriate citation
  • acknowledging relationship between First Peoples' knowledges and contemporary science
  • constructing an argument for First Nations contributions to medicine

Waypoints

  • 1. Constructing multi-source argumentsHingeStudents can construct an argument that draws on multiple sources of evidence, evaluating the credibility of each source and explaining how the evidence collectively supports the conclusion.
  • 2. Ethical issues in secondary data useStudents can identify ethical issues associated with accessing, using and citing secondary data — including data privacy, correct attribution, and relevance to the investigation question.
  • 3. Cultural protocols with First Nations knowledgesStudents can apply cultural protocols when accessing, using or citing First Nations knowledges — including acknowledging the co-contributions of Indigenous knowledge and contemporary science, and constructing arguments that recognise these relationships appropriately.
