← AC9S9I02 Plan valid, reproducible investigations

Identifying and controlling sources of error

AC9S9I02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify possible sources of error in an investigation method and explain how controlling or accounting for each error source increases validity and reproducibility.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least two sources of error with specific explanations — not generic 'human error'.
  • Can describe a specific control strategy for each identified error source.
  • Can explain how uncontrolled error affects the validity of conclusions.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Validity and reproducibility as method criteria.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two sources of error in a described or planned investigation and explain how each could be controlled or reduced.
Typical
Systematically identify multiple sources of error across measurement, procedure and environmental factors, and describe specific control strategies for each — distinguishing between errors that can be eliminated and those that must be minimised and acknowledged.
Wider
  • Examine the difference between random error (varies unpredictably) and systematic error (consistently biased in one direction), and explain how each affects data quality differently — connecting to AC9S9I06 (assessing validity of methods).
  • Identify possible sources of error in a method and examine whether further testing or extra variable control is needed — applying the principle that error identification should drive method refinement, not just documentation.
