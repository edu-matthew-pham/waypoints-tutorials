← AC9S8U01 Cells as basic units

Additional organelles — mitochondria, ribosomes and cell specialisation

AC9S8U01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the roles of mitochondria and ribosomes as organelles present in both cell types, and recognise that cells are specialised — different cells have different organelle complements.

Lessons on this site

  • What makes a plant cell different?

Success criteria

  • States the function of mitochondria and ribosomes
  • Explains why a named specialised cell has more or fewer of a particular organelle
  • Describes cell specialisation as variation in organelle complement reflecting cell function

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Plant-specific structures — cell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuole.

Width

Minimum
Students state the function of mitochondria (energy release) and describe one example of a specialised cell whose organelle complement reflects its function.
Typical
Students describe mitochondria as the site of cellular respiration (energy release for cell activity); ribosomes as protein synthesis sites; explain that the number and type of organelles varies with cell function — muscle cells have many mitochondria, secretory cells have many ribosomes, root hair cells have no chloroplasts; connect organelle complement to the cell's role in the organism; introduce the concept that specialisation is built on the same DNA base.
Wider
  • Investigate a cell disorder that results from organelle malfunction — e.g. mitochondrial disease — and explain how failure at the organelle level affects cell, tissue and organism function
  • Explore how augmented or virtual reality tools allow 'zooming in' to explore organelle scale within cells

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the structure-function principle established in N3 and N4 into the broader concept of cell specialisation — different organelle complements for different functions. This is the bridge to AC9S8U02's tissue and organ system analysis, where specialisation is the mechanism enabling tissue formation.
