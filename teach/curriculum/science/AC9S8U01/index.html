Cells as basic units

AC9S8U01 · Year 8 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise cells as the basic units of living things, compare plant and animal cells, and describe the functions of specialised cell structures and organelles

Prior knowledge

Students can group living things by external features (AC9SFU01), compare living and non-living things and life cycles (AC9S3U01), explain how structural features enable survival (AC9S5U01), and classify organisms using dichotomous keys (AC9S7U01). All prior learning is at the organism level — external structures, observable features, habitat relationships. There is no prior curriculum treatment of internal cell structure, cellular scale, or sub-organism organisation.

By the end

Students recognise that all living things are made of cells, compare plant and animal cells identifying structures present in each, and describe the functions of key organelles including nucleus, cell membrane, cell wall, cytoplasm, chloroplasts and vacuoles.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring an augmented or virtual reality tour of a plant or animal to 'zoom in' and understand the scale of cells
  • identifying the structure and function of organelles in cells including the nucleus, cell membrane, cell wall, cytoplasm, chloroplasts and vacuoles
  • examining a variety of cells, including single-celled organisms, using a light microscope, a digital microscope, simulations and photomicrographs
  • comparing the similarities and differences of plant cells and animal cells visible with a light microscope and represented in a digital or physical model
  • designing a physical or digital model of a cell and explaining how the representation models the cell
  • considering how the invention of the microscope has contributed to understanding of cell structure

Waypoints

  • 1. Cellular scale — why cells are invisible and require magnificationStudents can describe the scale of cells relative to organisms and explain why microscopes are required to observe them.
  • 2. Cell theory — all living things are made of cellsHingeStudents can state that all living things — including plants, fungi and single-celled organisms — are made of cells, and distinguish single-celled from multicellular organisms.
  • 3. Cell structures shared by plant and animal cells — nucleus, membrane, cytoplasmHingeStudents can identify and describe the function of structures present in both plant and animal cells: nucleus, cell membrane and cytoplasm.
  • 4. Plant-specific structures — cell wall, chloroplasts, large central vacuoleStudents can identify cell wall, chloroplasts and large central vacuole as plant-specific structures and explain the functional significance of each.
  • 5. Additional organelles — mitochondria, ribosomes and cell specialisationStudents can describe the roles of mitochondria and ribosomes as organelles present in both cell types, and recognise that cells are specialised — different cells have different organelle complements.
