← AC9S10U07 Reaction types and rates

Rate factors — temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts

AC9S10U07 n6 · waypoint 6 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how each of the four rate factors affects reaction rate using collision theory, and investigate one factor experimentally.

  • Why does heat speed up reactions?

Success criteria

  • Explains how each of the four rate factors affects reaction rate using collision theory
  • Distinguishes catalysts from reactants and explains why catalysts are not consumed
  • Designs or describes an investigation to test the effect of one rate factor

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 5: Collision theory — the particle basis of reaction rates.

Width

Minimum
Students explain how two of the four rate factors affect rate using collision theory language (collision frequency or energy).
Typical
Students explain all four factors using collision theory: temperature (increases particle kinetic energy → more collisions exceed activation energy); concentration (more particles per volume → more frequent collisions); surface area (more exposed particles → more collision opportunities); catalysts (lower activation energy → greater proportion of collisions succeed); distinguish catalyst from reactant.
Wider
  • Investigate the effect of temperature or concentration on a visible reaction (e.g. sodium thiosulfate + HCl, or hydrogen peroxide + yeast) and quantitatively analyse the rate change
  • Examine reactions employed by First Nations Australians — identify which rate factors are being exploited in a specific food preparation or substance production technique

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEach rate factor is a distinct investigative context and the elaborations explicitly expect investigation. Applying collision theory to each factor in turn — rather than listing them — builds the explanatory fluency the descriptor's 'investigate' demand requires.
