Synthesis reactions — combining reactants to form a single product

AC9S10U07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify synthesis reactions by their structural pattern (A + B → AB) and predict the product from given reactants.

Lessons on this site

  • What kind of reaction is it?

Success criteria

  • Identifies a synthesis reaction by its structural pattern (two or more reactants → one product)
  • Writes a correct word equation for a named synthesis reaction
  • Predicts the product of a synthesis reaction from given reactants

Prior knowledge

Students can model atom rearrangement in chemical reactions using word and balanced equations and demonstrate conservation of mass (AC9S9U07). They can compare physical and chemical changes and identify energy change indicators (AC9S8U07). They understand elements, compounds and mixtures and can use chemical symbols and formulas (AC9S8U06). They have not yet categorised reactions by structural type (synthesis, decomposition, displacement), identified patterns within those categories, or examined what variables control how fast reactions proceed.

Width

Minimum
Students identify the synthesis pattern from a given equation and write a word equation for one synthesis reaction.
Typical
Students identify synthesis reactions across multiple examples (metal + oxygen, hydrogen + oxygen, sodium + chlorine); write word and symbolic equations; predict the product of a named synthesis reaction; explain why synthesis reactions are used in manufacturing useful compounds; connect to balanced equation skills from AC9S9U07.
Wider
  • Investigate industrial synthesis reactions (e.g. Haber process for ammonia) and explain why controlling reaction conditions is economically important
  • Investigate chemical reactions employed by First Nations Australians in production of substances — identify which reaction type(s) are involved

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to recognise reaction type from structural pattern — not just from naming or memorisation. Synthesis is the most structurally transparent type (multiple reactants → one product) and provides the reference point for contrasting decomposition and displacement in subsequent nodes.
