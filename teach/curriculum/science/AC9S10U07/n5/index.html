← AC9S10U07 Reaction types and rates

Collision theory — the particle basis of reaction rates

AC9S10U07 n5 · waypoint 5 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain reaction rate in terms of particle collision frequency and energy, and use collision theory to predict how changing conditions will affect rate.

Lessons on this site

  • Why does heat speed up reactions?

Success criteria

  • Explains that reactions require particle collisions with energy above the activation energy threshold
  • Predicts that increasing collision frequency or energy increases reaction rate
  • Identifies at least two ways to increase collision frequency or energy

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Reaction type patterns — classifying and predicting across all three types.

Width

Minimum
Students state that chemical reactions require particles to collide with sufficient energy, and explain why more frequent or more energetic collisions increase reaction rate.
Typical
Students describe that only collisions with energy above the activation energy threshold result in reaction; explain why concentration, temperature, surface area and catalysts each increase collision frequency or energy; represent the relationship between collision theory and observable reaction rate.
Wider
  • Design an investigation to test the effect of one variable (temperature, concentration or surface area) on reaction rate — predict using collision theory and evaluate against results
  • Examine how catalysts work at the particle level — why do they lower activation energy without being consumed?

Hinge

Collision theory is the explanatory model for all four rate factors. Students who lack the collision model learn rate factors as isolated memorised rules (heat = faster, more acid = faster) without a unifying explanation. They cannot transfer to an unseen factor, cannot explain WHY each variable works, and cannot use the model to design investigations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCollision theory requires students to think about reactions at the particle level — a level below what is directly observable. Students need the mental model established explicitly before the four rate factors are introduced, or the factors remain disconnected facts.
