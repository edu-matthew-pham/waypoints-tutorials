Displacement reactions — patterns of reactivity and product prediction

AC9S10U07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 6 · Year 10 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify single and double displacement reactions and use reactivity patterns to predict whether a displacement reaction will occur and what the products will be.

Lessons on this site

  • What kind of reaction is it?

Success criteria

  • Identifies single and double displacement reactions from given equations
  • Uses an activity series to predict whether a single displacement reaction will occur
  • Predicts the products of a neutralisation or precipitation reaction

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Decomposition reactions — breaking a compound into simpler products.

Width

Minimum
Students identify a single displacement reaction from a given equation and explain why a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one.
Typical
Students identify single displacement reactions (metal + acid, metal + salt solution) and double displacement reactions (precipitation, neutralisation); use an activity series to predict whether single displacement will occur; write word and symbolic equations; explain neutralisation as a double displacement producing water and a salt; identify the precipitate in a double displacement reaction.
Wider
  • Investigate a metal reactivity series experimentally — place metals in acid or salt solutions and rank them by reactivity; compare to the published activity series
  • Examine First Nations Australians' production of acids or ethanol and identify which reaction type(s) are involved, predicting products from the reaction pattern

Hinge

Displacement reactions are structurally more complex than synthesis or decomposition — both single and double displacement require pattern recognition and the activity series adds a predictive layer. Students who conflate displacement with synthesis/decomposition, or who cannot use reactivity to predict whether a reaction proceeds, cannot integrate across all three reaction types in N4 or apply the classification tool to unseen reactions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSingle displacement requires an additional decision — will this reaction occur? — that synthesis and decomposition do not. Students need the activity series as a tool and must understand what it encodes (relative reactivity based on electron-giving tendency) before they can use it predictively. This is a new reasoning move, not just pattern recognition.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMetal + acid, metal + salt, neutralisation and precipitation are structurally distinct displacement contexts. Each exercises the type-identification and prediction skills from a different angle, and each maps to real-world applications named in the elaborations.
