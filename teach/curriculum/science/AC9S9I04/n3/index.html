← AC9S9I04 Select and construct appropriate representations

Constructing and using mathematical relationships

AC9S9I04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply mathematical relationships (formulae, ratios, proportional reasoning) to process data, and construct representations that accurately reflect quantitative relationships.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can apply a given formula correctly to process data.
  • Can construct a representation where numerical values determine proportional layout (e.g. Sankey diagram).
  • Can explain what the processed representation reveals that raw data does not.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Descriptive statistics: selecting the appropriate measure.

Width

Minimum
Apply one given formula or ratio to process a data set (e.g. applying a carbon storage formula to biomass measurements, or applying a ratio to construct a proportional Sankey diagram) and explain what the processed result represents.
Typical
Apply mathematical relationships to process data across two or three investigation contexts, construct representations where numerical proportionality is required (e.g. Sankey diagrams with scaled arrows), and explain how mathematical processing transforms raw data into meaningful scientific information.
Wider
  • Use spreadsheet software to present data in tabular and graphical forms — examining how digital tools manage the construction of accurate proportional representations that would be tedious by hand.
  • Apply ratios to accurately represent usable and waste energy in Sankey diagrams — connecting mathematical relationship application to the AC9S9U05 content.

Related standards

AC9S9I05 Analyse data for patterns and anomalies · AC9S9U05 Conservation of energy and efficiency · AC9S9U07 Chemical equations and conservation of mass
