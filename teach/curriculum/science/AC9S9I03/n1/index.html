Precision in measurement and recording

AC9S9I03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what precision means in measurement, select equipment based on required precision, and record data values to the correct number of significant figures with appropriate units.

Success criteria

  • Can select between two instruments based on required precision and explain the choice.
  • Can record data to the correct number of significant figures for the instrument used.
  • Does not record more significant figures than the instrument can provide.

Prior knowledge

Students can select and use equipment to generate and record data (AC9S7I03). They have not yet focused on precision as a criterion for equipment selection, significant figures and scientific notation as recording conventions, or sample size justification as a design decision.

Width

Minimum
Explain the difference between accuracy and precision in measurement, identify which of two instruments provides greater precision for a given measurement task (e.g. measuring cylinder vs. pipette), and record one data value to the correct number of significant figures.
Typical
Select appropriate equipment based on required measurement precision across multiple scenarios, record all data values to the correct significant figures with correct units, and explain how instrument precision affects the degree of precision achievable in data recording.
Wider
  • Examine the degree of accuracy that different instruments provide and what this means for the conclusions that can be drawn — connecting instrument precision to the limits of claim validity.
  • Use data loggers, examining scale settings and output representation choices — exploring how digital equipment introduces precision considerations beyond those of manual instruments.

Hinge

Students who record data to arbitrary significant figures, or who cannot select equipment appropriate to the required precision, will produce data that cannot support valid conclusions at AC9S9I06. Precision errors cascade into the analysis and evaluation stages — a data set recorded to insufficient precision cannot be improved retrospectively.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSignificant figure recording and unit consistency are procedural conventions requiring practice for reliable execution. Errors in these conventions are systematic and affect every data value. Students need enough practice to execute these reliably without conscious attention before they can focus on the broader investigation design.
