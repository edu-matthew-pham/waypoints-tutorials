Planning reproducible methods

AC9S7I02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a method that another person could reproduce to obtain comparable results

Success criteria

  • Plans a method with sufficient detail for another person to reproduce
  • Method logically tests the stated hypothesis
  • Peer review confirms the method is followable

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Variables and assumptions.

Width

Minimum
Identify the features that make an investigation reproducible; compare a method with its hypothesis and assess whether it reasonably tests the hypothesis
Typical
Plan a reproducible investigation method for a given hypothesis; have another student review it and identify gaps; revise based on feedback
Wider
  • Limited width — writing reproducible methods is a precision skill that does not vary meaningfully across investigation types. All wider directions (multiple trials, peer review, explicit variable statements) are more practice on the same reproducibility criteria, not broader contexts.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsWriting reproducible methods is a precision task — students commonly write methods that are too vague for someone else to follow. The skill does not vary meaningfully across contexts; what helps is practice writing, peer-testing and refining methods until sufficient detail becomes habitual. More investigation contexts don't help; more writing-and-feedback cycles do.
