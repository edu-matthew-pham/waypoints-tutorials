← AC9S9I06 Assess validity and evaluate conclusions

Conflicting evidence and areas of uncertainty

AC9S9I06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify conflicting evidence within or across investigations and explain how areas of uncertainty may support viable alternative conclusions.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify an area of uncertainty or conflicting evidence in a described investigation.
  • Can explain how that uncertainty or evidence could support an alternative conclusion.
  • Does not treat all uncertainty as equally undermining — can evaluate the significance of identified uncertainty.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identifying assumptions in methods and conclusions.

Width

Minimum
Identify one area of uncertainty or one piece of conflicting evidence in a described investigation context and explain how it could lead to an alternative conclusion.
Typical
Identify conflicting evidence and areas of uncertainty across an investigation and evaluate whether they are sufficient to support a viable alternative conclusion — distinguishing between uncertainty that undermines the main conclusion and uncertainty that is present but does not change the conclusion significantly.
Wider
  • Examine cases where conflicting evidence led to prolonged scientific debate before resolution — connecting evaluation skills to the human endeavour of science (AC9S9H01, H02).
  • Examine whether areas of uncertainty in an investigation could lead to a viable alternative conclusion — practising the explicit construction of an alternative conclusion from the same evidence set.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierConclusion evaluation integrates source validity (Node 1), assumption identification (Node 2) and evidence assessment into a structured judgment: 'The conclusion is/is not valid because the method [addresses/does not address] these assumptions, and there is [no/some/significant] conflicting evidence that could support an alternative conclusion.' This argument structure is the integration task.
