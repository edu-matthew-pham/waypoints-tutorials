Physical changes to materials

AC9S2U03 · Year 2 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise that materials can be changed physically without changing their material composition and explore the effect of different actions on materials including bending, twisting, stretching and breaking into smaller pieces

Prior knowledge

Students can observe and describe properties of materials (AC9SFU03, F band). They have not yet distinguished between changing the form of a material and changing its composition, or systematically explored multiple physical change actions.

By the end

Students recognise that physical actions (bending, twisting, stretching, folding, cutting, crushing) change the shape or size of a material without changing what it is made of, and explore how different materials respond differently to the same physical action.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring how materials can be physically changed for particular purposes
  • manipulating paper, fabric and other materials through multiple physical changes
  • crushing chalk and comparing properties of stick vs powder
  • exploring how First Nations Australians make physical changes to natural materials
  • creating an 'odd one out' game using same-material physical changes

Waypoints

  • 1. Physical changes alter shape without changing compositionHingeStudents can explain that physically changing a material (bending, twisting, stretching, breaking) changes its shape or size but not what it is made of.
  • 2. Different actions affect materials differentlyStudents can explore and describe how different physical actions affect different materials and explain how physical changes can be purposeful.
