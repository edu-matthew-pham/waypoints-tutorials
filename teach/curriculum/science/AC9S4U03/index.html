Forces and motion

AC9S4U03 · Year 4 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

identify how forces can be exerted by one object on another and investigate the effect of frictional, gravitational and magnetic forces on the motion of objects

Prior knowledge

Students can describe pushes and pulls in terms of strength and direction and predict their effects on motion and shape (AC9S1U03). They have not yet encountered named force types (friction, gravity, magnetism) or the concept that forces are exerted by one object on another, including at a distance.

By the end

Students identify how forces are exerted between objects — including contact and non-contact forces — and describe the effects of friction, gravity and magnetism on the motion of objects, using force arrows to represent direction and magnitude.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring the effect of magnets on other magnets and how magnetic forces can pull objects from a distance
  • exploring the positive and negative effects of friction on their everyday experiences, such as how friction causes objects to slow down and stop
  • recognising that gravity is the force that pulls all objects towards the centre of Earth and that gravitational force acts on an object regardless of whether it is moving or not moving
  • observing how the pushing force of a liquid enables an object to float
  • investigating the effect of forces on the movement of objects in traditional First Nations Australians' children's instructive toys and games
  • examining shoe sole design and identifying patterns in sole design and use related to friction
  • watching a video of astronauts walking on the moon or dropping objects on its surface, and discussing the force they are observing
  • exploring how force arrows can be used to represent the direction and magnitude of forces acting on an object

Waypoints

  • 1. Forces are exerted by one object on anotherHingeStudents can explain that a force is an interaction between two objects — one exerting, one receiving — and distinguish between contact forces (objects touching) and non-contact forces (acting at a distance).
  • 2. Friction slows motion between surfacesStudents can explain that friction is a force that acts between two surfaces in contact, describe its effect on motion (slowing or stopping), and identify factors that affect friction strength.
  • 3. Gravity pulls objects toward EarthStudents can explain that gravity is a force that pulls all objects toward the centre of Earth, acts on objects regardless of whether they are moving or stationary, and explain why objects fall when dropped.
  • 4. Magnetic forces attract and repelStudents can explain that magnets exert forces on other magnets and on some metals, describe attraction and repulsion, and identify that magnetic force acts at a distance.
  • 5. Force arrows represent forces on objectsStudents can draw and interpret force arrow diagrams showing the direction and relative magnitude of forces acting on an object, and use these to describe the overall effect on motion.
