← AC9S4U03 Forces and motion

Gravity pulls objects toward Earth

AC9S4U03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 4 Science · Physical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that gravity is a force that pulls all objects toward the centre of Earth, acts on objects regardless of whether they are moving or stationary, and explain why objects fall when dropped.

Success criteria

  • Explains that gravity pulls all objects toward the centre of Earth
  • Confirms that gravity acts on objects whether they are moving or stationary (does not restrict gravity to falling objects)
  • Gives a correct example of gravity's effect on a stationary object

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Friction slows motion between surfaces.

Width

Minimum
Students explain that gravity pulls objects toward Earth and that it acts on all objects, giving examples of gravity's effect on moving and stationary objects.
Typical
Students explain that gravitational force acts continuously on objects (a held object, a stationary object, a falling object) and describe what would change if gravity were weaker — connecting to astronauts on the moon.
Wider
  • Watch footage of astronauts on the moon or dropping objects in a vacuum and describe the force they are observing — connecting to the idea that gravity varies with planetary mass
  • Investigate the pushing force of a liquid (buoyancy) and discuss how it can counteract gravity, allowing objects to float
  • Explore how gravity acts on objects in free fall — using slow-motion footage of falling objects to describe the motion

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly believe gravity only acts when objects are falling or moving, not on stationary objects. They also conflate gravity with 'weight' as a fixed property. Varied contexts — a stationary book, a falling apple, an astronaut on the moon, a floating boat — help students see gravity as a continuous force that acts regardless of motion state.
