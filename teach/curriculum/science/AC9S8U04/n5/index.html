Rock properties, formation history and use

AC9S8U04 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the properties of each rock type reflect its formation history and evaluate the suitability of specific rocks for named uses.

Success criteria

  • Explains the properties of at least two rock types in terms of their formation processes
  • Connects named rock properties to suitability for a specific use
  • Identifies how traditional rock selection practices reflect knowledge of formation-linked properties

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: The rock cycle — processes connecting all three types over geological time.

Width

Minimum
Students explain the use of two rocks by connecting their properties to formation and to the demands of the application.
Typical
Students connect: granite (interlocked large crystals, hard, weather-resistant → building, benchtops); marble (recrystallised calcite, polishable → sculpture, tiles); sandstone (layered, porous, relatively soft → building, grinding); slate (fine-grained metamorphic, splits in sheets → roofing); coal (compressed organic material → fuel); connect First Nations rock selection to observable formation-linked properties; use dichotomous keys to identify rock samples.
Wider
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' quarrying practices — how were grindstones, hammerstones and cutting tools selected based on observable properties, and what does this reveal about practical knowledge of formation-linked properties?
  • Evaluate the environmental impact of quarrying a specific rock type and examine rehabilitation initiatives

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the formation-property links established in N1–N3 and the cycle understanding from N4 into practical application — properties as a consequence of formation history determining use. It is the descriptor's 'examine how properties reflect formation and influence use' demand.
