Metamorphic rocks — heat and pressure without melting

AC9S8U04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain metamorphic rock formation through heat and pressure applied to existing rock, and describe observable properties that reflect these conditions.

Success criteria

  • Explains that metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are subjected to heat and pressure without melting
  • Connects banding or foliation to the directional compression of metamorphism
  • Identifies at least one parent rock and its metamorphic product

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Igneous rocks — magma cooling and crystallisation.

Width

Minimum
Students state that metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are subjected to heat and pressure without melting, and identify one observable property reflecting this.
Typical
Students explain that existing rocks (any type) subjected to intense heat and pressure underground undergo recrystallisation without melting; explain banding/foliation as a result of minerals being compressed and reoriented; explain that metamorphic rocks are denser and harder than their precursors; identify parent rock and metamorphic product pairs (shale → slate → phyllite → schist; limestone → marble); connect to plate tectonics — subduction zones and mountain-building provide the heat and pressure.
Wider
  • Examine the metamorphic series from shale → slate → phyllite → schist → gneiss and explain what increasing grade of metamorphism reveals about the conditions of formation
  • Compare a metamorphic rock to its parent rock and identify the structural changes produced by heat and pressure

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMetamorphic formation is the least intuitive of the three types — heat and pressure without melting is a process with no everyday equivalent. Students need the formation conditions explained explicitly (subduction, burial, mountain building) with the observable consequences (banding, density, hardness) derived from those conditions.
