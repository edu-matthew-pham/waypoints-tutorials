Sedimentary rocks — weathering, transport, deposition and lithification

AC9S8U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the formation of sedimentary rocks through the sequence of weathering, erosion, transportation, deposition and lithification, and connect observable properties to formation.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: sedimentary, metamorphic and the rock cycle

Success criteria

  • Describes the formation sequence: weathering → erosion → transport → deposition → lithification
  • Connects at least two observable properties of sedimentary rock to formation steps
  • Identifies fossils as occurring in sedimentary rocks and explains why

Prior knowledge

Students can compare observable properties of rocks and minerals (AC9S3U02) and describe weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition as surface processes (AC9S5U02). Plate tectonics provides heat, pressure and uplift mechanisms (AC9S8U03). Prior learning covers surface rock identification and surface processes only — rock formation processes (igneous crystallisation, metamorphism, lithification) and the rock cycle as a system are entirely new.

Width

Minimum
Students describe the four-step formation sequence and identify one observable property of sedimentary rock that reflects each.
Typical
Students describe: physical and chemical weathering breaks rock into sediment; erosion and transportation carry sediment; deposition layers sediment; lithification (compaction and cementation) produces rock; explain layering as a record of deposition events; explain rounded grains as evidence of transport; explain that fossils occur in sedimentary rocks (organisms buried in sediment); identify examples (sandstone, limestone, shale, coal).
Wider
  • Examine fossil evidence in sedimentary rock samples to predict the environment and approximate timescale of formation
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' selection of sedimentary rocks for specific purposes — what observable properties reflect formation and guided selection?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSedimentary formation is the most connected to prior knowledge — weathering and erosion from AC9S5U02 are the first steps. Building the full formation chain from prior surface processes to completed rock is the appropriate entry point. Formation-property reasoning (rounded grains = transported; layers = sequential deposition) establishes the method used in N2 and N3.
