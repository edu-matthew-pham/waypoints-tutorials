The rock cycle — processes connecting all three types over geological time

AC9S8U04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the rock cycle as a set of interconnected processes by which rocks are continuously transformed between types, and identify the geological timescales involved.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: sedimentary, metamorphic and the rock cycle

Success criteria

  • Traces at least two complete rock cycle pathways, naming the process at each step
  • Compares the timescales of surface and deep rock cycle processes
  • Explains how plate tectonics drives the rock cycle

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Metamorphic rocks — heat and pressure without melting.

Width

Minimum
Students trace at least two complete pathways in the rock cycle, naming the process at each transformation step.
Typical
Students describe all major pathways: weathering/erosion → sediment → sedimentary; burial/heat/pressure → metamorphic; melting → magma → igneous; uplift (any deep rock) → surface → weathering again; compare timescales: surface weathering (thousands to millions of years), burial and metamorphism (tens of millions), subduction and melting (similar); explain that plate tectonics drives the cycle by providing uplift, subduction, and heat; emphasise that no rock is permanent.
Wider
  • Trace the journey of a specific mineral (e.g. quartz) through multiple rock cycle stages and estimate the total time elapsed
  • Investigate how mining of ores and minerals intersects with the rock cycle — what processes concentrate economically useful minerals, and what does extraction disrupt?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe rock cycle requires students to reconceptualise rock as temporary — any rock can become any other rock type given sufficient time and appropriate conditions. This is a significant conceptual shift from stable, fixed rock types.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMineral journey tracing, timescale comparison, and mining impact analysis each illuminate different aspects of the cycle as a dynamic Earth system.
