Rock cycle

AC9S8U04 · Year 8 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

describe the key processes of the rock cycle, including the timescales over which they occur, and examine how the properties of sedimentary, igneous and metamorphic rocks reflect their formation and influence their use

Prior knowledge

Students can compare observable properties of rocks and minerals (AC9S3U02) and describe weathering, erosion, transportation and deposition as surface processes (AC9S5U02). Plate tectonics provides heat, pressure and uplift mechanisms (AC9S8U03). Prior learning covers surface rock identification and surface processes only — rock formation processes (igneous crystallisation, metamorphism, lithification) and the rock cycle as a system are entirely new.

By the end

Students describe the rock cycle as an interconnected system linking sedimentary, igneous and metamorphic formation, explain the geological timescales involved, and examine how each rock type's properties reflect its formation history and determine appropriate use.

Waypoints

  • 1. Sedimentary rocks — weathering, transport, deposition and lithificationStudents can describe the formation of sedimentary rocks through the sequence of weathering, erosion, transportation, deposition and lithification, and connect observable properties to formation.
  • 2. Igneous rocks — magma cooling and crystallisationHingeStudents can explain the formation of igneous rocks from cooling magma or lava and connect crystal size to cooling rate.
  • 3. Metamorphic rocks — heat and pressure without meltingStudents can explain metamorphic rock formation through heat and pressure applied to existing rock, and describe observable properties that reflect these conditions.
  • 4. The rock cycle — processes connecting all three types over geological timeStudents can describe the rock cycle as a set of interconnected processes by which rocks are continuously transformed between types, and identify the geological timescales involved.
  • 5. Rock properties, formation history and useStudents can explain how the properties of each rock type reflect its formation history and evaluate the suitability of specific rocks for named uses.
