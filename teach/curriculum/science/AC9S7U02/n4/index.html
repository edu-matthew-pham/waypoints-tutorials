Biotic factors and population effects

AC9S7U02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can predict and explain how a change to a biotic factor cascades through populations in a food web

Lessons on this site

  • What if the dingo disappeared?

Success criteria

  • Correctly defines biotic factor and gives 3 examples
  • Predicts the effect of removing one species with food web reasoning
  • Traces at least one cascade effect beyond the immediate population

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Abiotic factors and population effects.

Width

Minimum
Define biotic factor; give three examples; predict the effect of removing one species from a given food web with reasoning
Typical
Analyse a trophic cascade case study (e.g. wolf reintroduction in Yellowstone); explain the chain of biotic effects with evidence
Wider
  • Predict effects on local ecosystems when pollinators or predators are removed or introduced
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' responses to invasive species and their effect on food webs communities depend on
  • Evaluate an invasive species scenario — construct food webs before and after, predict population effects, and propose a management response

Hinge

Predicting biotic cascade effects requires holding the whole food web in mind simultaneously — the same multi-step reasoning skill required in AC9S9U03 (Carbon Cycle and Earth's Spheres), where students must trace carbon flow through biotic and abiotic ecosystem components. Students who haven't secured biotic cascade reasoning here will treat carbon flow as a simple transfer rather than a web of interdependent pathways.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly predict only the immediate effect of species removal without tracing the cascade through the web. Seeing more biotic change scenarios across different ecosystem types — predator removal, invasive species, pollinator loss — forces students to practise multi-step reasoning through food web structure rather than applying a single memorised cascade pattern.
