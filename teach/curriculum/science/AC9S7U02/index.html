Food webs and ecosystems

AC9S7U02 · Year 7 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use models, including food webs, to represent matter and energy flow in ecosystems and predict the impact of changing abiotic and biotic factors on populations

Prior knowledge

Students can explain the roles of producers, consumers and decomposers in a habitat and construct simple food chains (AC9S4U01), and know that a habitat's physical conditions affect the growth and survival of living things (AC9S6U01). They have not studied energy flow quantitatively or modelled multi-factor ecosystem changes.

By the end

Students use food web models to represent matter and energy flow, and predict the impact of changes to abiotic and biotic factors on populations.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • analysing food webs to show feeding relationships between organisms in an ecosystem and the role of microorganisms
  • modelling how energy flows into and out of an ecosystem via the pathways of food webs
  • predicting the effects on local ecosystems when living things such pollinators or predators are removed from or die out in an area
  • examining how events such as seasonal changes, destruction of habitat or introduction of a species impact abiotic and biotic factors and cause changes to populations
  • investigating First Nations Australians' responses to invasive species and their effect on food webs that many communities are a part of, and depend on, for produce and medicine
  • considering how First Nations Australians' fire management practices over tens of thousands of years have changed the distribution of flora and fauna in most regions of Australia

Waypoints

  • 1. From food chains to food websHingeStudents can construct a food web and identify the roles of producers, consumers and decomposers including microorganisms
  • 2. Matter and energy flowHingeStudents can explain the direction and loss of energy through trophic levels and distinguish energy flow from matter cycling
  • 3. Abiotic factors and population effectsStudents can predict and explain how a change to an abiotic factor cascades through populations in a food web
  • 4. Biotic factors and population effectsHingeStudents can predict and explain how a change to a biotic factor cascades through populations in a food web
  • 5. Models, limitations and predictionsStudents can evaluate the limitations of food web models and explain how these affect the reliability of predictions
