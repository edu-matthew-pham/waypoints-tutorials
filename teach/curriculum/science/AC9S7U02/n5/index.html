Models, limitations and predictions

AC9S7U02 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 7 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the limitations of food web models and explain how these affect the reliability of predictions

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: abiotic factors and food web limits

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one limitation of food web models
  • Explains how that limitation affects the reliability of a specific prediction

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Biotic factors and population effects.

Width

Minimum
Identify one limitation of food web models; explain how this affects the reliability of predictions made from them
Typical
Compare a simple and complex model of the same ecosystem — explain what each can and cannot predict
Wider
  • Evaluate a real scientific food web model used in a conservation decision — what did it predict, what did it miss?
  • Consider how introduction of a species impacts abiotic and biotic factors beyond immediate food web effects
  • Research how First Nations ecological knowledge has improved or challenged scientific food web models in a specific case

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly identify model limitations as abstract statements ('it doesn't include everything') without reasoning through how a specific limitation affects a specific prediction. Scaffolding the connection — what is missing from this model, and what prediction would be wrong as a result — builds genuine model evaluation rather than generic limitation-listing.
