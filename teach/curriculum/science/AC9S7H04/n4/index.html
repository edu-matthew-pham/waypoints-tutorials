The role of science communication in society

AC9S7H04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 7–8 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how science communication plays an ongoing role in informing viewpoints and shaping policies across different contexts

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explains how communication choices connected to both viewpoint and policy outcomes
  • Identifies what made the communication effective or ineffective

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Communication shapes community policies and regulations.

Width

Minimum
Explain how science communication informed viewpoints and shaped policy in one case, connecting communication choices to outcomes
Typical
Compare two cases where science communication had different impacts — one primarily on viewpoints, one primarily on policy — and explain what made each effective
Wider
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of a science communication campaign — what worked, what didn't, and what could be improved?
  • Investigate how science communication of the impact of waste materials led to both changed viewpoints and community recycling policies
  • Construct an argument about what makes science communication effective at driving both viewpoint change and policy change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the communication forms thread (Node 1), the viewpoints thread (Node 2), and the policy thread (Node 3) into a coordinated account of science communication's role in society. Students who can address each separately but cannot connect them into a coherent explanation of how communication choices produce viewpoint and policy outcomes have not met the descriptor's exploratory demand.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe effectiveness evaluation direction — what made communication work or not work — requires explicit criteria scaffolding. Students need support in identifying what 'effective' means across the viewpoint and policy dimensions, not just narrating what happened. Without this scaffold, evaluations default to impressionistic judgements.
