Earth is a planet in the solar system

AC9S2U01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe Earth as a planet in the solar system, name other planets and the sun, and describe the shape of Earth using images from space.

Success criteria

  • Names Earth as a planet in the solar system and identifies at least two other planets
  • Describes the shape of Earth using evidence from space images

Prior knowledge

Students can describe daily and seasonal changes in the environment (AC9S1U02). They have not yet placed Earth within the solar system or examined patterns in the changing position of celestial objects as a systematic phenomena.

Width

Minimum
Students name Earth as a planet in the solar system, name at least two other planets, and describe the shape of Earth.
Typical
Students explore representations of the solar system; identify Earth and other planets; describe Earth's shape from space images; distinguish celestial objects visible during the day (sun) from those visible at night (moon, stars, planets); and name the sun as the central body.
Wider
  • Explore how cultural stories of First Nations Peoples of Australia describe patterns in the changing positions of the sun, moon and stars — comparing with Western astronomical frameworks
  • Create a class model of the solar system and name the sun and planets — representing scale and position relationships
  • Distinguish between regular sky events (full moon cycle) and irregular events (blood moon, blue moon) — examining the difference between predictable patterns and rare phenomena

Hinge

N2 requires students to know what celestial objects are and that Earth is positioned within a solar system — without this, students cannot distinguish patterns in celestial object positions from generic environmental change (already covered in Y1). The solar system framework is the necessary context for sky pattern investigation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations astronomical knowledge and solar system modelling broaden students' encounter with sky objects beyond a single framework — building the richer conceptual model of Earth's position that N2 pattern observation requires.
