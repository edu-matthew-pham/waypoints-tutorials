← AC9S6U01 Physical conditions and survival

Human activity changes physical conditions and affects living things

AC9S6U01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 6 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how human-caused changes to physical conditions affect living things, examine community and scientific responses, and predict the impact of specific condition changes on named organisms.

Success criteria

  • Describes a human-caused change to a physical condition and its effect on a named organism, with the mechanism explained
  • Describes at least one scientific or community response to a human-caused condition change

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Changing physical conditions affect growth and survival.

Width

Minimum
Students describe one example of how human activity changes physical conditions and explain the effect on at least one organism.
Typical
Students examine multiple examples of human-caused condition change (urban lighting affecting nocturnal animals, pollution changing water chemistry, habitat clearing changing temperature and moisture), explain the mechanism by which each change affects organisms, and describe scientific or community responses.
Wider
  • Research the impacts of light pollution and how communities have used scientific knowledge to reduce it — examining covered streetlights, automated systems and motion sensors as evidence-based responses (AC9S6H02)
  • Investigate how changes in physical conditions caused by human activity affect aquatic organisms — examining noise pollution, temperature change, chemical runoff
  • Examine international scientific collaboration in Antarctica to understand how extreme physical conditions affect living thing survival — connecting to global climate condition change (AC9S6H01)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents are often aware of dramatic habitat destruction but miss less visible human-caused condition changes (light pollution, noise pollution, temperature increase from urban heat islands). Varied examples help students see human impact as operating through physical condition change — a more analytical and generalisable framework than 'humans damage habitats'.
