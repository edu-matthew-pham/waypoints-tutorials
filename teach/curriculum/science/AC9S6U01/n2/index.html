Changing physical conditions affect growth and survival

AC9S6U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 6 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how changes in physical conditions affect the growth and survival of living things — using investigation data or case study evidence to support their analysis.

Success criteria

  • Describes how a change in a named physical condition affects the growth or survival of a named organism
  • Uses evidence (investigation data or case study) to support the analysis

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Habitats have measurable physical conditions.

Width

Minimum
Students describe how a change in one physical condition (e.g. temperature, salinity, light) affects the growth or survival of a named organism, using evidence.
Typical
Students investigate how changes in physical conditions (salinity, soil type, sunlight, temperature) affect plant growth; examine how temperature, light and rainfall changes affect animals; explain the mechanism by which each condition change affects the organism; and use data to support their analysis.
Wider
  • Investigate how changes in physical conditions affect the growth of bread mould colonies in sealed bags — examining temperature, moisture and light as variables in a controlled investigation
  • Examine how changes in physical conditions (temperature, light availability, acidity) affect corals, honey bees or flying foxes — connecting condition change to real threatened species
  • Recognise how environmental conditions affect life stages — ponds drying up preventing tadpole development, seeds requiring water to germinate, temperature affecting egg hatching

Hinge

Condition-change analysis (mechanism + evidence) is the direct prerequisite for Node 3 — students must be able to explain how a specific condition change affects an organism before they can analyse human-caused condition changes and predict organism impacts. Without this analytical move, N3 reduces to listing human impacts without explanatory depth.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly understand that extreme conditions kill organisms but miss the subtler effects of condition change on growth rates, reproductive success and life stage completion. Encountering condition-response relationships across plant and animal examples, and across different physical variables, builds a more complete analytical model.
