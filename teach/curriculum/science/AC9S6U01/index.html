Physical conditions and survival

AC9S6U01 · Year 6 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate the physical conditions of a habitat and analyse how the growth and survival of living things is affected by changing physical conditions

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how structural features and behaviours enable survival in specific habitats (AC9S5U01). They have not yet systematically investigated physical conditions as variables, examined how changes in conditions affect growth and survival, or analysed the impact of human-caused condition changes.

By the end

Students identify physical conditions in habitats (temperature, light, salinity, soil type, moisture), investigate how changes in those conditions affect the growth and survival of living things, and analyse how human-caused condition changes affect organisms — including predicting impacts and examining responses.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the physical conditions in an aquatic or terrestrial habitat and how they change over time
  • investigating how changes to physical conditions such as salinity, soil type, sunlight or temperature affect plant growth
  • examining how changes in physical conditions such as temperature, light availability and rainfall affect animals, such as corals, honey bees or flying foxes, and predict impacts of these changes
  • investigating changes in physical conditions that are the result of human activity and exploring the impact of these on living things, such as the impact of urban lighting on nocturnal and migratory animals
  • investigating the effect of physical conditions on the growth of bread mould colonies in sealed plastic bags
  • recognising that environmental conditions can affect stages of life, such as ponds drying up, seeds requiring water to germinate, or temperatures being too hot or cold for eggs to hatch
  • investigating First Nations Australians' knowledges and understandings of the physical conditions necessary for the survival of certain plants and animals

Waypoints

  • 1. Habitats have measurable physical conditionsHingeStudents can identify and describe the physical conditions of an aquatic or terrestrial habitat — including temperature, light availability, salinity, soil type, moisture and pH — and explain how these conditions vary over time.
  • 2. Changing physical conditions affect growth and survivalHingeStudents can analyse how changes in physical conditions affect the growth and survival of living things — using investigation data or case study evidence to support their analysis.
  • 3. Human activity changes physical conditions and affects living thingsStudents can analyse how human-caused changes to physical conditions affect living things, examine community and scientific responses, and predict the impact of specific condition changes on named organisms.
