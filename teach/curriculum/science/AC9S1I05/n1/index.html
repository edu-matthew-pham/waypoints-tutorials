← AC9S1I05 Evaluating

Observations are compared with predictions and others

AC9S1I05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Years 1–2 Science · Inquiry

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare their observation with their prediction (did it match?) and compare their observation with another student's observation, identifying at least one similarity or difference.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Compares observation with prediction and states whether it matched
  • Compares observation with at least one other student's and identifies a similarity or difference

Prior knowledge

Students have made observations and predictions (F band). They now begin to compare these with others and consider fairness — guided by the teacher.

Width

Minimum
Students compare their observation with their prediction and with one other student's observation.
Typical
Students compare observations with predictions across investigations; compare observations with others (bird counts, plant growth, object movement); consider whether differences might be due to an unfair investigation; and identify one further question prompted by an unexpected observation.
Wider
  • Consult with First Nations Australians to compare observations and evaluate identifications of animal tracks — examining how knowledge of Country informs observation comparison
  • Explore if making weather observations at different times of day makes a difference — considering fairness of comparison
  • Compare how far an object travels with different students pushing and discuss how to make the investigation fairer
