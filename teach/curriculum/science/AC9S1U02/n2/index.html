Daily and seasonal changes affect everyday life

AC9S1U02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 1 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how daily weather and seasonal changes affect decisions people make and how plants and animals respond to these changes.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one way daily weather change affects a human decision
  • Describes at least one way seasonal change affects a plant or animal

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The environment changes daily and across seasons.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least one way daily weather changes affect human decisions and one way seasonal change affects a plant or animal.
Typical
Students explain how daily weather indicators and seasonal patterns help people plan activities and clothing choices; investigate how seasonal changes affect animals (hibernation, migration) and plants; and connect their own experiences of responding to weather and season to the broader pattern.
Wider
  • Explore how people make clothing choices using weather predictions or seasonal knowledge — connecting observable pattern to everyday decision-making
  • Investigate how changes in weather affect plants and animals including humans
  • Recognising how First Nations Australians use changes in landscape and sky to answer questions about when to gather certain resources

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents readily connect seasonal change to their own clothing choices but miss the broader effects on other living things and on planning decisions. Encountering multiple ways daily and seasonal change affects daily life — human, plant and animal — builds a more complete model of environmental change and response.
