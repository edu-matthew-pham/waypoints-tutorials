← AC9S1U02 Daily and seasonal changes

The environment changes daily and across seasons

AC9S1U02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 1 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe observable daily changes (weather, temperature, daylight) and identify seasonal patterns across the year.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two observable daily changes in the environment
  • Names at least two seasons and describes one observable environmental change for each

Prior knowledge

Students can observe and describe features of their local environment (F band). They have not yet described daily and seasonal change patterns or connected those patterns to effects on living things and human decisions.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least two daily changes in the environment and name at least two seasons with one observable change for each.
Typical
Students make and record observations of daily weather changes and seasonal patterns in plants (leaf colour, flowering, dropping leaves) and weather; identify patterns across days and seasons; and explain what makes one season different from another using observable evidence.
Wider
  • Recognise the extensive knowledges of daily and seasonal change held by First Nations Australians — examining how traditional seasonal calendars describe environmental patterns beyond the four-season European model
  • Explore how First Nations Australians' concepts of time and weather patterns explain how things happen in the world around them
  • Investigate how seasonal changes affect animals including hibernation and migration — connecting seasonal pattern to animal behaviour

Hinge

N2 requires students to have identified daily and seasonal change patterns before they can reason about how those changes affect people, plants and animals. Without an established pattern model, N2 explanations reduce to isolated anecdotes rather than pattern-based reasoning.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations seasonal calendars and animal behaviour directions broaden the pattern model beyond the European four-season framework, providing culturally rich and ecologically grounded encounters with seasonal change that deepen the concept before N2 applies it.
