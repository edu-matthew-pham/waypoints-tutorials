Science knowledge influences decisions and practices

AC9S3H02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how scientific knowledge has influenced how people make decisions or change their practices — not only in designed solutions but in everyday choices and policy.

Success criteria

  • Describes an example where scientific knowledge changed how people make decisions or behave, identifying the knowledge and the change
  • Distinguishes between an engineered application (a designed product) and a behavioural or practice change as two different ways science knowledge is used

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Examining real applications of science knowledge.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least one example where scientific knowledge changed how people behave or make decisions (e.g. plastic disposal, water conservation), explaining what the knowledge was and what changed.
Typical
Students examine how knowledge of plastic properties has influenced purchasing, use and disposal practices, and how this differs from engineered applications — connecting scientific knowledge to individual and community decision-making.
Wider
  • Research a community or policy response to a scientific finding (e.g. water restrictions based on hydrological data; species protection based on ecological knowledge) and explain the knowledge-to-decision pathway
  • Compare two decisions influenced by different scientific knowledge — one where the knowledge led to a designed solution, one where it changed behaviour — and discuss the different ways science knowledge shapes human action
  • Examine how knowledges of plant biology enable First Nations Australians to sustainably harvest and use plants — tracing how deep ecological knowledge shapes sustainable practice decisions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node asks students to integrate the explanation-to-application framework with a new dimension — behavioural and policy change — alongside designed solutions. Students must coordinate the same underlying idea (knowledge informs action) across a broader range of outcomes than engineered solutions alone.
