Variation within populations

AC9S10U02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Year 10 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that individuals within a population vary in heritable characteristics and identify the sources of that variation.

Lessons on this site

  • How different are individuals?

Success criteria

  • Explains that individuals within a population vary in heritable characteristics
  • Distinguishes heritable from non-heritable variation
  • Identifies mutation and sexual reproduction as sources of heritable variation

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how specific structural features and behaviours are adaptations enabling survival in particular habitats (AC9S5U01), analyse how physical conditions affect organism survival (AC9S6U01), and explain classification systems and biodiversity (AC9S7U01). They understand sexual and asexual reproduction and the role of variation in species survival (AC9S9U02). They have not yet encountered natural selection as a mechanistic explanation for how populations change over time, or engaged with the evidence base — fossil record, anatomical and chemical similarities, geographical distribution — that supports the theory.

Width

Minimum
Students state that individuals in a population vary and that some variation is heritable, giving one example.
Typical
Students distinguish heritable from non-heritable variation; connect heritable variation to meiosis and sexual reproduction (AC9S10U01); explain that mutation introduces new variation; describe variation across a population as a distribution rather than a binary.
Wider
  • Investigate variation in a measurable characteristic across a sample population and represent the distribution graphically — what does the shape of the distribution suggest?
  • Connect to AC9S10U01 — how does the genetic recombination from meiosis generate the heritable variation that natural selection acts on?

Hinge

Natural selection cannot operate without heritable variation — it is the raw material the mechanism acts on. Students who treat all individuals in a population as identical, or who do not understand that variation must be heritable to be selected, cannot construct the selection mechanism in N2 or explain why advantageous traits accumulate over generations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly accept that 'animals adapt to their environment' as a process that happens to individuals, not populations over generations. Establishing that variation is the starting condition — present in every population before any selection pressure exists — is the prerequisite for understanding selection as a filter, not a creator.
