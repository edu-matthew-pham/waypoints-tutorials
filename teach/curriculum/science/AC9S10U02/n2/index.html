← AC9S10U02 Evolution by natural selection

Natural selection as a mechanism — variation, selection pressure, differential survival

AC9S10U02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 10 Science · Biological · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the mechanism of natural selection: how a selection pressure acts on heritable variation to produce differential survival and reproduction, leading to population change over generations.

Lessons on this site

  • Why did the beetles change?

Success criteria

  • Describes the mechanism of natural selection as variation → selection pressure → differential survival/reproduction → inherited by offspring
  • Applies the mechanism correctly to a named example
  • Distinguishes natural selection from the Lamarckian view that individuals adapt during their lifetime

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Variation within populations.

Width

Minimum
Students describe that individuals with advantageous traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing those traits to offspring, with one named example.
Typical
Students apply the mechanism across multiple examples including predation, disease and environmental change as selection pressures; explain why traits accumulate in populations over generations; distinguish natural selection from Lamarckian 'use and disuse'; describe artificial selection as a human-directed application of the same mechanism.
Wider
  • Investigate natural selection in a specific documented case — e.g. antibiotic resistance in bacteria, or industrial melanism in peppered moths — and trace each step of the mechanism
  • Compare natural selection to artificial selection in breeding programmes — what are the structural similarities and what is different?

Hinge

The mechanism of natural selection is the core explanatory tool the descriptor demands. Students who conflate natural selection with individual adaptation (Lamarckism) or who cannot articulate the variation → differential survival → inherited by offspring chain cannot explain either past diversity (N4) or present diversity (N5), and cannot evaluate any evidence for the theory (N3).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe Lamarckian misconception — that individuals change during their lifetime to suit the environment and pass those changes on — is persistent and must be explicitly addressed. Students need to reconstruct the selection mechanism step by step, identifying where Lamarck's account differs at each step.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepApplying the selection mechanism to varied, well-evidenced cases builds the explanatory fluency needed to use the theory as an analytical tool in N4 and N5. Each case exercises the same mechanistic reasoning in a new context.
