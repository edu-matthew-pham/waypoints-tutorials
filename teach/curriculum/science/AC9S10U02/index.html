Evolution by natural selection

AC9S10U02 · Year 10 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use the theory of evolution by natural selection to explain past and present diversity and analyse the scientific evidence supporting the theory

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how specific structural features and behaviours are adaptations enabling survival in particular habitats (AC9S5U01), analyse how physical conditions affect organism survival (AC9S6U01), and explain classification systems and biodiversity (AC9S7U01). They understand sexual and asexual reproduction and the role of variation in species survival (AC9S9U02). They have not yet encountered natural selection as a mechanistic explanation for how populations change over time, or engaged with the evidence base — fossil record, anatomical and chemical similarities, geographical distribution — that supports the theory.

By the end

Students use the theory of evolution by natural selection to explain both past and present biological diversity, and analyse multiple lines of scientific evidence — fossil record, anatomical and chemical similarities, geographical distribution — that support the theory.

Waypoints

  • 1. Variation within populationsHingeStudents can explain that individuals within a population vary in heritable characteristics and identify the sources of that variation.
  • 2. Natural selection as a mechanism — variation, selection pressure, differential survivalHingeStudents can describe the mechanism of natural selection: how a selection pressure acts on heritable variation to produce differential survival and reproduction, leading to population change over generations.
  • 3. Evidence for evolution — multiple lines of supportStudents can describe and explain multiple lines of evidence that support the theory of evolution by natural selection, including the fossil record, anatomical and chemical similarities, and geographical distribution.
  • 4. Using natural selection to explain past diversity — speciation and extinctionStudents can use the theory of natural selection to explain how past populations diverged into new species and how extinction results from failed adaptation to selection pressures.
  • 5. Using natural selection to explain present diversity and current changeStudents can use the theory of natural selection to explain current patterns of biodiversity and analyse examples of evolution occurring in present populations.
