← AC9S10U02 Evolution by natural selection

Evidence for evolution — multiple lines of support

AC9S10U02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 10 Science · Biological

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe and explain multiple lines of evidence that support the theory of evolution by natural selection, including the fossil record, anatomical and chemical similarities, and geographical distribution.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: evidence, new species and evolution today

Success criteria

  • Describes at least three distinct lines of evidence for evolution
  • Explains what each evidence type demonstrates about common descent or change over time
  • Identifies that multiple independent evidence types support the same conclusion

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Natural selection as a mechanism — variation, selection pressure, differential survival.

Width

Minimum
Students describe at least two distinct lines of evidence for evolution and explain what each line demonstrates.
Typical
Students explain the fossil record as evidence of change over time and species extinction; describe homologous structures as evidence of common ancestry; explain chemical similarities (DNA, protein sequences) as evidence of evolutionary relationships; describe geographical distribution patterns (island species, marsupial distribution) as evidence consistent with common descent; explain how each evidence type supports the theory independently.
Wider
  • Analyse a specific cladogram or phylogenetic tree using DNA sequence data — what does the branching pattern indicate about evolutionary relationships?
  • Investigate how the discovery of gravitational waves or other recent scientific advances parallel the process by which evolutionary evidence accumulates and gets validated

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEach evidence type — fossil record, anatomical homology, chemical similarity, geographical distribution — is a distinct line of reasoning. Students encountering only one type often believe the theory rests on that single line. Working across all four types builds the 'multiple independent lines' understanding the descriptor's 'analyse' demand requires.
