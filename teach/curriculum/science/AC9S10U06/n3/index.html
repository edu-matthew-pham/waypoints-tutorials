Periodic trends — why properties repeat across periods

AC9S10U06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 Science · Chemical

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe repeating patterns of properties across the periodic table and explain them as a consequence of repeating outer electron shell configurations.

Lessons on this site

  • Why does the periodic table repeat?

Success criteria

  • Explains that a new period begins when electrons start filling a new shell
  • Describes that properties repeat across periods because outer electron configurations repeat
  • Interprets the position of an element in the table in terms of its electron shell configuration

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Outer electrons and group properties — why elements in a group behave similarly.

Width

Minimum
Students identify that a new period begins when a new electron shell starts filling and explain that properties repeat because outer electron counts repeat.
Typical
Students describe how atomic number increases across a period while outer electrons increase from 1 to 8 before resetting; explain metallic character decreasing across a period; describe trends in reactivity within groups; explain that the period number indicates the number of occupied electron shells; interpret the table's structure as a map of electron configuration.
Wider
  • Analyse the historical development of the periodic table — why did Mendeleev's table work before electron theory was established, and what does this suggest about the relationship between pattern and explanation?
  • Examine an extended periodic table section (transition metals) and explain why their properties are more similar across periods than main group elements

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe periodicity of the table — why it has the structure it does, with periods and groups — is not obvious to students who learned the table as a given organisational chart. Making the electron-shell-reset the explicit explanation for period structure completes the descriptor's 'relate structure of atoms to organisation' demand.
