Electron shell configuration and the Bohr model

AC9S10U06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 Science · Chemical · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw or describe the electron shell configuration of elements 1–20 using the Bohr model and explain the shell-filling rules.

Lessons on this site

  • How are the electrons arranged?

Success criteria

  • Draws a correct Bohr diagram for any element 1–20
  • States the maximum electron capacity of the first three shells (2, 8, 8)
  • Identifies the number of outer-shell electrons for any element 1–20

Prior knowledge

Students can classify matter as elements, compounds or mixtures and use symbols, formulas and models to represent them (AC9S8U06). They can explain how atomic theory developed through the discovery of subatomic particles and describe how radioactive decay produces stable atoms (AC9S9U06). They have the Bohr model of the atom as prior knowledge — protons, neutrons and electrons in shells — but have not yet examined how electron shell configuration determines an element's chemical properties, or how repeating patterns in electron configuration are the basis for periodic table organisation.

Width

Minimum
Students correctly assign electrons to shells for at least five elements and state the maximum electron capacity of the first three shells.
Typical
Students draw Bohr diagrams for elements 1–20; state shell capacities (2, 8, 8); identify the number of outer-shell electrons for any element 1–20; explain that the number of outer-shell electrons is the key variable for chemical behaviour; connect to the prior atomic model from AC9S9U06.
Wider
  • Investigate emission spectra from flame tests and explain how spectral lines relate to electron energy levels — connecting the visible pattern to the Bohr model
  • Examine how the development of the spectroscope enabled scientists to probe atomic structure — what did spectral lines reveal that earlier atomic models could not explain?

Hinge

Outer electron shell configuration is the structural variable the entire periodic table organisation depends on. Students who cannot correctly assign outer electrons for a given element cannot explain why elements in the same group share properties (N2), cannot interpret periodic trends (N3), and cannot use the table predictively (N4).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsElectron shell assignment requires procedural precision — filling shells in order, knowing capacities, correctly identifying outer-shell electrons. Students need practice and error-checking at the diagram level before the pattern-recognition work of N2 is meaningful.
