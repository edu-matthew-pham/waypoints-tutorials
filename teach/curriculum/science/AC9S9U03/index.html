Carbon cycle and Earth's spheres

AC9S9U03 · Year 9 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

represent the carbon cycle and examine how key processes including combustion, photosynthesis and respiration rely on interactions between Earth's spheres (the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and atmosphere)

Prior knowledge

Students can describe Earth's surface changes (AC9S5U02), model how Earth's tilt and revolution produce seasonal change (AC9S6U02), and compare reversible and irreversible changes including combustion (AC9S6U04). They can use food web models to represent matter and energy flow through ecosystems (AC9S7U02) and explain plate tectonics and rock cycle processes (AC9S8U03, AC9S8U04). They have not yet encountered Earth as a system of interacting spheres, or carbon as a substance that cycles through those spheres via identifiable chemical and biological processes.

By the end

Students represent the carbon cycle using diagrams or models and explain how combustion, photosynthesis and respiration each move carbon between Earth's spheres — including how human activity through combustion perturbs the natural cycle.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying Earth as a system, describing Earth's spheres and discussing examples of interactions between different spheres
  • examining the carbon cycle using diagrams, animations or simulations and explaining the role of photosynthesis and respiration in that cycle
  • identifying the impact of combustion reactions as a result of human activity on the carbon cycle
  • investigating the greenhouse effect and relating it to the role carbon dioxide plays in maintaining temperatures that support life on Earth
  • conducting a field investigation to evaluate carbon sequestration in an ecosystem
  • investigating how First Nations Australians use fire-mediated chemical reactions to facilitate energy and nutrient transfer through firestick farming
  • investigating how First Nations Australians are reducing greenhouse gas emissions through traditional fire management
  • identifying how carbon dioxide is captured and stored naturally or through technologies
  • calculating an individual's carbon footprint and suggesting strategies to reduce emissions

Waypoints

  • 1. Earth's spheres — geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphereHingeStudents can name and describe Earth's four spheres and give examples of matter or processes found in each.
  • 2. Carbon as a substance cycling through Earth's spheresStudents can identify where carbon is stored in each of Earth's spheres and explain that carbon moves between these stores.
  • 3. Photosynthesis and respiration — carbon movement through the biosphereHingeStudents can explain how photosynthesis and respiration move carbon between the atmosphere and biosphere, and represent these as flows in the carbon cycle.
  • 4. Combustion — carbon release from geosphere to atmosphereStudents can explain how combustion of fossil fuels moves carbon from the geosphere to the atmosphere and describe the impact of human activity on this flow.
  • 5. Representing the carbon cycle — integrating processes across all spheresStudents can construct or annotate a carbon cycle diagram that represents all key processes (photosynthesis, respiration, combustion, decomposition) as flows between Earth's spheres.
