← AC9S9U03 Carbon cycle and Earth's spheres

Representing the carbon cycle — integrating processes across all spheres

AC9S9U03 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct or annotate a carbon cycle diagram that represents all key processes (photosynthesis, respiration, combustion, decomposition) as flows between Earth's spheres.

Lessons on this site

  • Quick check: the whole carbon cycle

Success criteria

  • Constructs or correctly annotates a carbon cycle diagram with at least four labelled flows between named spheres
  • Identifies which flows are biological, which are geological, and which are human-driven
  • Explains how human combustion has altered the balance of the natural carbon cycle

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Combustion — carbon release from geosphere to atmosphere.

Width

Minimum
Students correctly label a carbon cycle diagram showing at least four carbon flows between named spheres.
Typical
Students construct a complete carbon cycle diagram from memory or annotate a template with all major flows; explain the direction and mechanism of each flow; identify where human activity has altered natural flow rates; explain how carbon sequestration (natural or technological) works within the cycle; describe the cycle as a system in approximate balance before industrialisation.
Wider
  • Evaluate a carbon sequestration strategy (reforestation, biochar, ocean fertilisation) using the carbon cycle diagram — which flows does it target and what are the risks?
  • Investigate how First Nations Australians' traditional fire management reduces net greenhouse gas emissions — map the mechanism onto the carbon cycle diagram

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the four-sphere framework (N1), carbon stores (N2), photosynthesis and respiration flows (N3), and combustion (N4) into a single representative model. Constructing the diagram is the descriptor's 'represent the carbon cycle' demand — the integration point for all prior nodes.
