Carbon as a substance cycling through Earth's spheres

AC9S9U03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Earth and space

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify where carbon is stored in each of Earth's spheres and explain that carbon moves between these stores.

Lessons on this site

  • Where is all the carbon?

Success criteria

  • Identifies carbon stores in at least three of Earth's four spheres with examples
  • Explains that carbon moves between stores rather than being created or destroyed
  • Distinguishes fast-cycling carbon stores (atmosphere, living organisms) from slow stores (fossil fuels, carbonate rock)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Earth's spheres — geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere.

Width

Minimum
Students identify carbon stores in at least three spheres (e.g. CO₂ in atmosphere, organic matter in biosphere, carbonate rocks in geosphere) and state that carbon moves between stores.
Typical
Students describe carbon stores in all four spheres (atmospheric CO₂, dissolved carbon in oceans, organic carbon in living and dead matter, fossil fuels and carbonate rocks in geosphere); explain that carbon is neither created nor destroyed — it moves between stores; introduce carbon cycle as the map of those movements.
Wider
  • Investigate carbon sequestration in a local ecosystem — what stores exist and how large are they relative to atmospheric CO₂?
  • Compare timescales of carbon movement: carbon in a leaf vs carbon in a coal seam — why does geological storage matter for climate?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need to understand carbon as a substance with identifiable locations before they can trace its movement in the cycle. The 'stores and flows' framing — familiar from food web energy flow in AC9S7U02 — is the conceptual bridge to carbon cycle representation.
