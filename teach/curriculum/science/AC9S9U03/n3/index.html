← AC9S9U03 Carbon cycle and Earth's spheres

Photosynthesis and respiration — carbon movement through the biosphere

AC9S9U03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Year 9 Science · Earth and space · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how photosynthesis and respiration move carbon between the atmosphere and biosphere, and represent these as flows in the carbon cycle.

Lessons on this site

  • Where does the carbon in a tree go?

Success criteria

  • Explains that photosynthesis moves carbon from the atmosphere to the biosphere
  • Explains that respiration and decomposition move carbon from the biosphere to the atmosphere
  • Represents both processes as directional arrows between spheres in a diagram

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Carbon as a substance cycling through Earth's spheres.

Width

Minimum
Students state that photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the atmosphere and stores carbon in organic matter, and that respiration releases CO₂ back to the atmosphere.
Typical
Students describe photosynthesis and respiration as complementary processes; explain that photosynthesis moves carbon from atmosphere to biosphere and respiration does the reverse; connect to food web energy flow from AC9S7U02; represent both processes as arrows in a carbon cycle diagram; explain that decomposition is also a form of respiration returning carbon to the atmosphere.
Wider
  • Investigate how carbon moves through a food web — trace a carbon atom from CO₂ through photosynthesis, herbivore, carnivore and decomposer back to atmosphere
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' fire management regimes and explain how controlled burning affects the photosynthesis-respiration balance in an ecosystem

Hinge

Photosynthesis and respiration are the biological processes that dominate carbon cycling between biosphere and atmosphere. Students who cannot represent these as directional flows cannot construct a correct carbon cycle diagram in N5, and cannot explain why deforestation or ecosystem loss accelerates CO₂ accumulation in the atmosphere.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often know photosynthesis and respiration as chemical equations without locating them as sphere-exchange processes. Reframing both processes as carbon-flow arrows between specific spheres — the representation the descriptor demands — requires an explicit conceptual shift from 'what happens in a cell' to 'how carbon moves between stores'.
