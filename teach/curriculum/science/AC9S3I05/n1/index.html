Conclusions are grounded in data

AC9S3I05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Science · Inquiry · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw a conclusion from an investigation that is directly grounded in the data collected, and distinguish between a conclusion supported by data and a claim that goes beyond the data.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • States a conclusion that accurately reflects the data (does not overclaim)
  • Identifies at least one piece of data that supports the conclusion

Prior knowledge

Students can compare observations with predictions with guidance, and identify unexpected results (AC9S1I05, 1-2 band). They have not yet systematically evaluated fairness of investigations, drawn evidence-based conclusions, or posed further questions from findings.

Width

Minimum
Students draw a conclusion from a given data set and explain how the data supports the conclusion.
Typical
Students identify the difference between a conclusion that accurately reflects the data and one that overclaims, and revise an overclaiming conclusion to match the data.
Wider
  • Examine a scenario where two data sets lead to different conclusions — what does this suggest about the investigation?
  • Evaluate conclusions drawn from food chain simulations (virtual or role-play) against what would actually happen in a real habitat — what does the simulation's simplification mean for the conclusion's validity?
  • Compare how conclusions are presented in a scientific article (hedged, data-referenced) versus how they appear in a news report (often more absolute) — and discuss why scientists are cautious

Hinge

Data-grounded conclusion drawing is the payoff of the entire inquiry sequence and feeds directly into AC9S3H01. Students who cannot distinguish a supported conclusion from an overclaim produce unreliable conclusions that undermines the purpose of investigation — and carry this pattern into all subsequent inquiry work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe data-to-conclusion chain requires explicit scaffolding. Students commonly draw conclusions that go well beyond what the data shows, or that contradict the data, without recognising the mismatch. Making the chain explicit (what did the data show → what can I conclude from that data → what would I need to be able to say something stronger?) supports more disciplined conclusion-drawing.
