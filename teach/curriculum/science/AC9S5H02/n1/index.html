Scientific knowledge helps identify and define problems

AC9S5H02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Science · Human endeavour

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Science: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how scientific knowledge enables people to identify and precisely define a problem — distinguishing between a symptom (what is observed) and a cause (what the knowledge explains).

Success criteria

  • Describes one example where scientific knowledge enabled a problem to be identified
  • Explains what the knowledge was and how it transformed an observation into a defined problem with a cause

Prior knowledge

Students can examine how scientific explanations are used to meet needs or solve problems (AC9S3H02, 3-4 band). They now extend to examining how knowledge is used by communities — not just to design solutions, but to identify problems, evaluate responses and make collective decisions.

Width

Minimum
Students describe one example where scientific knowledge helped identify a problem, explaining what the knowledge was and how it enabled the problem to be recognised.
Typical
Students examine how knowledge of erosion processes enables identification of at-risk landscapes, and how knowledge of light properties enables identification of light pollution as a problem — explaining in each case how scientific knowledge transforms an observable phenomenon into a defined problem with a cause.
Wider
  • Examine how park rangers use knowledge of erosion to identify and define problems in fragile environments — distinguishing natural and human-caused erosion and predicting risk locations
  • Investigate how knowledge of light pollution and its effects on ecosystems enables communities to identify artificial lighting as a problem requiring management
  • Research how First Nations Australians use ecological knowledge to identify problems in Country — examining how deep observational knowledge enables early problem recognition
