← AC9HP8P09 Media influence on health

Understanding how media shapes health attitudes

AC9HP8P09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how different types of media sources and influential people can shape attitudes and beliefs about health, safety, relationships and wellbeing — beyond just providing information.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one media source and explains how it operates as a shaper of attitudes or beliefs
  • Distinguishes between providing information and shaping attitudes in at least one example

Prior knowledge

Students have investigated different sources and types of health information and how these apply to health choices (Y5-6). They can identify and apply health information but have not yet been expected to investigate the mechanisms by which media and influential figures shape attitudes and beliefs — not just inform decisions.

Width

Minimum
Identify one media source and explain one way it shapes attitudes or beliefs about health (not just provides information about it).
Typical
Explain how different source types (news, bloggers, influencers, social media advertising) operate differently as attitude and belief shapers; distinguish between informing, persuading and normalising as media mechanisms; connect media exposure to attitude formation across multiple health domains.
Wider
  • Analyse how social media algorithms shape what health information young people see, and evaluate what media literacy strategies help students navigate this curated environment
  • Explore how messages related to food are portrayed in media and how this shapes decision-making about health and wellbeing, examining both explicit messaging and normalisation through representation

Hinge

The descriptor asks students to investigate impact on attitudes, beliefs, decisions and behaviours — not just to evaluate source credibility. Students who only understand media as information provision cannot investigate attitudinal and behavioural influence, which is the primary focus of this descriptor.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDistinguish between media as information source and media as attitude/belief shaper. Explicitly model the mechanism: exposure → normalisation → attitude → behaviour. Without this distinction, students evaluate credibility but do not investigate influence.
