Critiquing media messaging about health

AC9HP10P09 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can critique health information and media messaging to evaluate their influence on attitudes and actions

Success criteria

  • Applies specific criteria (from Node 1) to critique a named piece of health information or media
  • Evaluates the likely influence on attitudes or actions — not just the quality of the information itself
  • Identifies what is absent or distorted in the message, not only what it gets wrong

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Criteria for critiquing health information.

Width

Minimum
Critique one piece of health information or media messaging — apply criteria to evaluate its quality, identify its likely influence on attitudes, and explain what it leaves out
Typical
Critique health information and services across multiple domains (substance use, food, fitness, mental health, sexual health, personal safety); critique how the portrayal of sexual relationships in TV, advertisements, movies, popular music and online content may influence beliefs about respectful, safe and consensual relationships; evaluate influence on individual attitudes and actions
Wider
  • Critique a specific media portrayal of sexual relationships (film, TV, pornography, advertising) using evidence-based criteria for respectful and consensual relationships — evaluate the gap between the portrayal and legal/ethical standards
  • Investigate and critique a health campaign targeting a specific community — evaluate whether it is culturally appropriate, accessible, and likely to achieve its stated goals
  • Design a counter-messaging strategy for a specific piece of misleading health information — evaluate what approach would most effectively reach the target audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepHealth media critique requires practice across different domains and message types — food advertising operates differently from sexual health messaging in social media, which operates differently from a government health campaign. Students need to apply their criteria across multiple message types to develop genuine critique capability. The sexual relationships media direction is particularly important given the elaborations' explicit inclusion of pornography and its influence on consent beliefs.
