Fair and unfair play

AC9HPFM04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe actions that would constitute fair and unfair play in minor games.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one example of fair play and one example of unfair play in a game context
  • Gives a simple reason why fair play is important (e.g. so everyone can enjoy the game)

Prior knowledge

No prior HPE curriculum. Students bring informal social experience of turn-taking and sharing from play and home contexts. No prior structured engagement with game rules or fair play.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one example of fair play and one example of unfair play in a game they have participated in.
Typical
Identify and describe fair and unfair play actions across a range of minor game contexts; explain why fair play matters to participants.
Wider
  • Explore fair play examples from First Nations Australian games and compare with other game contexts
  • Discuss scenarios where intent (accidental vs deliberate) affects judgements about fairness
  • Connect fair play to feelings — how unfair play makes participants feel

Hinge

Students who cannot identify fair and unfair play lack the conceptual foundation to follow rules purposefully (next node) — they comply mechanically at best. The Y1-2 equivalent (co-constructing rules) requires this conceptual base.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse game stoppages and brief scenario discussions to make fair/unfair distinctions explicit. Foundation students need the concepts named and exemplified concretely before they can apply them.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVaried game contexts — chasing games, target games, cooperative games — reveal that fair play principles are consistent across different rule structures.
