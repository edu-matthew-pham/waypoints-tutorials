Practising seeking, giving and denying permission

AC9HPFP04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can practise and refine how to ask for permission and how to give or deny permission respectfully, including respecting someone's right to say no.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Uses respectful language or gesture to ask permission in a play or sharing context
  • Accepts a yes or no response to a permission request without acting disrespectfully

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The importance of asking permission.

Width

Minimum
Practise asking permission in at least one play or sharing context using respectful language, and demonstrate accepting a yes or no response.
Typical
Practise and refine seeking, giving and denying permission across play and imaginative situations; demonstrate respect for different responses including 'no'; negotiate roles through imaginative and shared play while maintaining awareness of rights.
Wider
  • Practise permission-seeking in physical activity contexts involving personal space (e.g. partner games, dance, contact activities)
  • Use role-play to rehearse scenarios where permission is denied and explore how to respond respectfully
  • Discuss and practise digital scenarios involving permission (e.g. asking before taking a photo)

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPermission-seeking and denial is a practised social skill. Foundation students need repeated, structured role-play and play experiences before respectful language and acceptance of 'no' become habitual.
