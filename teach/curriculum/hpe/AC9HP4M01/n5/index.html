Movement sequences — jumping, landing and balancing

AC9HP4M01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform and connect movement sequences incorporating different jumping, landing and balancing techniques.

Success criteria

  • Performs a movement sequence that includes a jump, landing and balance with controlled transitions.
  • Maintains balance and control at the connection points between elements.
  • Describes the sequence they performed and at least one aspect they would refine.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Coordinated swimming strokes.

Width

Minimum
Perform a movement sequence that connects at least a jump, a landing and a balance, with controlled transitions.
Typical
Create and perform a sequence that incorporates multiple jumping, landing and balancing techniques with controlled connections and smooth transitions.
Wider
  • Choreograph and perform a movement sequence for an audience (class or small group), incorporating transitions that are deliberate and match a chosen theme or musical structure.
  • Compare how jumping, landing and balancing techniques feature in gymnastics, dance and outdoor/adventure activities — identifying similarities and differences in technique demands.

Hinge

Sequencing and connecting movements is the integration demand that spans the whole standard. Students who cannot connect skill components into a sequence will be unable to demonstrate the 'new movement situations' requirement at the progression endpoint, and this limitation cascades into AC9HP6M01 where more complex combined sequences are expected.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to coordinate previously practised individual skills (jump, land, balance) into a connected sequence. Teacher investment should focus on the transitions — the linking moments — rather than re-teaching individual skills.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who achieve the minimum sequence benefit from performing in different movement contexts (gymnastics, dance, outdoor) to build transferability of the sequencing capability.
