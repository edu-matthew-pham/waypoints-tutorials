Coordinated swimming strokes

AC9HP4M01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can coordinate kicking with arm movements to move their body through the water using different types of strokes.

Success criteria

  • Coordinates kicking and arm movements in at least one swimming stroke.
  • Sustains the stroke across a short distance without stopping.
  • Identifies whether their kick and arm timing felt coordinated and what they would adjust.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Weight transference in movement.

Width

Minimum
Coordinate kicking and arm movements to sustain one swimming stroke across at least a short distance.
Typical
Demonstrate coordination across two or more swimming strokes, sustaining rhythm and timing between kick and arm action across varied distances.
Wider
  • Explore how the coordination demands differ between freestyle and breaststroke — or between any two strokes with different timing patterns — and explain what makes each challenging.
  • Apply coordinated stroke technique in a minor water-based activity or challenge, adjusting technique in response to feedback.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSwimming stroke coordination requires timing precision between two independent movement chains (kick and arms). Students need repeated practice to build automaticity before they can apply skill in varied water contexts.
