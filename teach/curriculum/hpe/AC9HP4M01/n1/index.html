Combining locomotor and object control skills

AC9HP4M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform activities that combine locomotor and object control skills to complete a movement task or challenge.

Success criteria

  • Identifies the locomotor and object control components in a given movement task.
  • Performs a combined-skill movement task, demonstrating coordination between the two skill types.
  • Completes the movement task or challenge with at least partial success.

Prior knowledge

Students have practised fundamental movement skills and applied them in a variety of movement situations (AC9HP2M01). They can apply skills across locomotor and object control domains in familiar contexts.

Width

Minimum
Combine at least one locomotor skill with one object control skill to complete a set movement task.
Typical
Perform a range of activities that combine locomotor and object control skills across different movement tasks and challenges, including games contexts.
Wider
  • Design and perform their own combined-skill movement challenge, explaining the locomotor and object control components involved.
  • Compare how the combination of locomotor and object control skills differs across two distinct movement contexts (e.g. a field game vs a court game).

Hinge

If students cannot integrate locomotor and object control skills in combination, they cannot access the refinement and adaptation work in subsequent nodes — all applied movement tasks in this band require combined-skill execution.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStudents who can execute skills in isolation but not in combination need structured practice that gradually introduces dual demands — locomotor task first, then add the object control component.
