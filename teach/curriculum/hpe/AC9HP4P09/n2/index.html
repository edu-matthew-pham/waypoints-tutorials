Food and nutrition health messages

AC9HP4P09 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine health messages about food and nutrition from different sources and explore choices and behaviours conveyed.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a health message about food or nutrition from a specific source.
  • Describes the behaviour or choice the message promotes.
  • Discusses whether the message aligns with evidence-based nutrition advice.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sources of health information and accuracy.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one health message about food or nutrition from a source, and describe the choice or behaviour it is encouraging.
Typical
Examine food and nutrition health messages from multiple sources; compare the choices and behaviours promoted; discuss whether messages are aligned with evidence-based nutrition advice.
Wider
  • Audit the health messages present in a school canteen menu, supermarket shelf display or breakfast cereal packaging — identifying what behaviours are being promoted and who benefits.
  • Investigate nutritional information on food labels from a range of products and discuss what information is present, what is emphasised and what might be misleading.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepNutrition messages appear in very different contexts (advertising, food labelling, health promotion, school canteen) that serve different interests and have different effects. Exposure across contexts builds richer critical literacy.
