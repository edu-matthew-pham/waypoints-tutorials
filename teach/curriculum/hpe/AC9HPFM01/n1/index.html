Locomotor skills in play

AC9HPFM01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Foundation Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply different locomotor skills (walking, running, jumping, hopping, skipping) to move purposefully from one point to another in a play context.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Applies at least two different locomotor skills during a game or play activity
  • Moves with basic control, including stopping and changing direction when needed

Prior knowledge

No prior HPE curriculum. Students bring informal movement experience from play and daily life — walking, running, throwing, catching — but without structured FMS practice.

Width

Minimum
Move between two points using at least two different locomotor skills in a simple game or activity.
Typical
Apply a range of locomotor skills (at least four) in minor games and imaginative play; demonstrate basic control of starting, stopping and changing direction.
Wider
  • Apply locomotor skills in natural and outdoor environments, adapting movement to uneven terrain or open space
  • Combine locomotor sequences (e.g. run–jump–hop) in response to game signals or teacher cues
  • Explore locomotor skills used in First Nations Australian games such as running-based chasing games

Hinge

Locomotor control underpins safe participation in all subsequent FMS combinations. Students who cannot move purposefully between points struggle with every minor game context in this standard and in the Y1-2 band.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFoundation students need repeated practice across varied play contexts before locomotor patterns become reliable. Precision of starting, stopping and direction changes should be emphasised before combining with object manipulation.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary the play contexts (chasing games, imaginative play, natural environments) to build transferable locomotor control across settings.
