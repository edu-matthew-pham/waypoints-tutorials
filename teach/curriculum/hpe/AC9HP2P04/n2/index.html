Fair and respectful interaction regardless of difference

AC9HP2P04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can practise ways to interact with others in a fair and respectful way in play and other activities, regardless of differences in gender, abilities, race or personality.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates a fair and respectful interaction with a peer who is different from themselves
  • Can describe what they did to interact respectfully

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Seeking, giving and refusing consent.

Width

Minimum
In a play or activity context, demonstrate at least one fair and respectful interaction with a person who is different from themselves; describe what they did.
Typical
Practise fair and respectful interaction across a range of play and activity contexts involving diverse peers; explain how respecting difference contributes to the activity being positive for everyone.
Wider
  • Compare how the same respectful interaction strategy works with peers of different abilities, genders, or backgrounds
  • Explore scenarios where differences in personality or preferences create friction in activities, and practise strategies for managing these respectfully
  • Discuss how respecting difference in physical activities connects to the fair-play principles students have explored in movement contexts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepRespectful interaction across different types of difference requires practice in varied contexts. Explicitly vary peer configurations (different abilities, genders, personalities) in activity tasks so students practise the full range of the descriptor.
