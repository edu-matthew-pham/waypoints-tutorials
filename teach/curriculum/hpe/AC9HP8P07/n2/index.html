Exploring skills and strategies for consent communication

AC9HP8P07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain specific skills and strategies for seeking, giving and denying consent assertively and respectfully, including in online contexts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one skill or strategy for assertive consent communication
  • Explains why the skill or strategy is effective for seeking, giving or denying consent

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding consent and assertive communication.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one skill or strategy for communicating assertively when seeking, giving or denying consent and explain why it is effective.
Typical
Explore and refine a range of skills and strategies for assertive consent communication across in-person and online contexts; recognise and respond to harmful online content (grooming, image-based abuse); explain how digital communication creates additional challenges for consent.
Wider
  • Explore strategies for recognising and responding to online content that may be harmful — such as grooming or image-based abuse — and practise communicating needs or concerns assertively in these situations
  • Examine how power imbalances, group pressure and digital contexts complicate consent communication, and identify strategies that remain effective in these more challenging conditions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need multiple strategies across multiple contexts — no single strategy works in all consent situations. Building a repertoire that spans in-person and online contexts is essential before application.
