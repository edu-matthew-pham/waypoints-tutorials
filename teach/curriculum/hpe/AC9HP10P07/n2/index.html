Consent supporting respectful relationships

AC9HP10P07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine how consent communication strategies support the development of respectful relationships, including sexual relationships

Success criteria

  • Explains a specific connection between practising consent communication and a quality of the relationship it builds
  • Includes sexual relationships as one context — does not treat consent as relevant only to intimate relationships
  • Reflects on own responsibility — not only on what makes a relationship respectful in the abstract

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Consent communication strategies.

Width

Minimum
Explain one way in which practising consent communication supports the development of a more respectful relationship
Typical
Examine how strategies for communicating choices, seeking and denying consent, and expressing needs support respectful relationships broadly and sexual relationships specifically; reflect on potential impact of own behaviour on others; examine how consent communication connects to affirmative consent, individual rights, and mutual respect
Wider
  • Examine how the portrayal of consent in TV shows, movies, popular music and online content may influence people's beliefs about what respectful and consensual relationships look like — evaluate the gap between media representations and legal/ethical standards
  • Investigate how consent and respectful communication functions differently in relationships where power is unequal (e.g. age gaps, social status, online anonymity) and evaluate what additional strategies are needed
  • Design a peer education resource on consent for a specific audience (e.g. Year 8 students, parents/carers) — evaluate what framing and examples would be most effective

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates consent communication skills (Node 1) with the broader relationship development framework — showing how practising consent is not a procedural compliance exercise but is constitutive of respectful relationships. Students who can communicate consent but cannot explain its relational function have not reached the descriptor's command term. The integration task is connecting the skill to the relationship quality it produces.
