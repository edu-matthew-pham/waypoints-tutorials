Consent communication strategies

AC9HP10P07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe and refine strategies for communicating consent clearly and respectfully across a range of relationship scenarios

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one specific consent communication strategy with reference to a named scenario type
  • Strategy includes both verbal and non-verbal components, or explicitly addresses what to do when non-verbal cues are absent
  • Reflects on taking responsibility for own behaviour — not only on what others should do

Prior knowledge

Students can evaluate emotional responses and refine emotion management strategies (AC9HP10P06), and have previously explained and applied assertive consent communication skills (AC9HP8P07). At Y9-10 the focus expands to examining how consent communication strategies support the development of respectful relationships broadly, including sexual relationships.

Width

Minimum
Describe one strategy for communicating consent clearly in a specific relationship scenario; explain what makes it effective
Typical
Refine strategies for communicating choices, needs and opinions clearly and respectfully across peer group, family and work situations; describe strategies that enhance safety and wellbeing of sexual partners including communicating feelings, respecting boundaries, gaining affirmative consent, and assertive communication; reflect on taking responsibility for own actions
Wider
  • Investigate the legal requirements for consent in your state or territory — including seeking, giving, refusing and accepting refusal in online and offline interactions with friends, colleagues, strangers and intimate partners — and evaluate how these laws connect to respectful relationship principles
  • Rehearse consent communication in scenarios where social pressure or power imbalances are present — evaluate which strategies are most effective and why
  • Examine how consent communication strategies need to adapt for digital contexts (texting, social media, online relationships) where verbal and non-verbal cues are absent

Hinge

Students who cannot articulate and refine specific consent communication strategies cannot examine their relationship-building function in Node 2 — the examination of how strategies support relationships requires first having identified what those strategies actually are.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyConsent communication is a capability that requires a repertoire across relationship types and contexts. Peer group, family, intimate partner, and online contexts each require different communication approaches. The legal dimension (state/territory requirements) adds a factual layer that students need alongside the skills dimension. Building range across contexts is more valuable than polishing a single scenario response.
