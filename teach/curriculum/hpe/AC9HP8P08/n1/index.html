Identifying protective behaviours and when they apply

AC9HP8P08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify a range of protective behaviours appropriate to different risk or help-seeking scenarios and explain the reasoning behind each.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two protective behaviours
  • Explains the reasoning behind each behaviour in a specific scenario

Prior knowledge

Students have analysed and rehearsed protective behaviours and help-seeking strategies in online and offline situations (Y5-6). They have rehearsed protective behaviours but have not yet been expected to refine them through evaluation or to evaluate the quality and suitability of community resources.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two protective behaviours and explain the reasoning behind each in a specific scenario.
Typical
Identify protective behaviours across a range of scenarios (mental health, online abuse, substance use, sexual health, bullying); explain why each behaviour protects safety or wellbeing in its specific context; distinguish proactive from reactive protective behaviours.
Wider
  • Explore help-seeking scenarios young people may encounter — including situations linked to substance use, mental health, sexual health, safety and risk-taking — and identify which protective behaviours are most relevant to each
  • Investigate how emotions, gender norms and cultural expectations can affect a person's willingness to apply protective behaviours or seek help, and what this means for designing effective protective strategies

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyProtective behaviour application depends on having a range of behaviours available — students who only know one or two protective strategies will be unable to respond appropriately to diverse scenarios.
