← AC9HP8P08 Protective behaviours and community resources

Evaluating community resources for help-seeking

AC9HP8P08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and evaluate community resources available for help-seeking in a range of scenarios, assessing their accessibility, relevance and quality.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one community resource available for help-seeking in a named scenario
  • Evaluates the resource against at least one criterion (accessibility, reliability or relevance)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying protective behaviours and when they apply.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one community resource available for help-seeking in a specific scenario and give one reason for or against using it.
Typical
Identify and evaluate resources across multiple domains (mental health, online safety, substance use, sexual health); assess accessibility, reliability and relevance for different scenarios; examine how support services like Kidshelpline and eSafety Commissioner operate.
Wider
  • Analyse how online support services (Kidshelpline, eSafety Commissioner) are designed and evaluate their effectiveness for different types of situations young people encounter
  • Examine how gender norms and cultural expectations create barriers to help-seeking for different groups and evaluate which resources are designed to address these barriers

Hinge

Refining protective behaviours and evaluating resources are the twin demands of this descriptor — students who cannot evaluate resource quality cannot make informed help-seeking decisions for themselves or others. This evaluation capability is what separates knowing resources exist from being able to use them effectively.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProvide explicit evaluation criteria (accessibility, relevance, reliability, appropriateness for specific scenarios) before expecting evaluation judgements. Without criteria, resource evaluation defaults to surface-level familiarity.
