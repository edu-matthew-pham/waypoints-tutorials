← AC9HP4M02 Movement strategies

Adapting strategies to create scoring opportunities

AC9HP4M02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can demonstrate how movement strategies can be adapted to create scoring opportunities across different game categories.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a scoring opportunity in a game and names a strategy to exploit it.
  • Adapts their movement strategy in response to a change in the game (e.g. opponent movement, rule change, new target).
  • Explains what they changed in their strategy and the reason for the change.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning strategies in minor and small-sided games.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate one adapted strategy in a game context where scoring is involved, explaining what was changed and why.
Typical
Demonstrate adapted strategies across invasion, target, net/court, and striking/fielding game contexts, identifying how each game's rules and structure require different adaptations.
Wider
  • Design a new rule or constraint variation for a known game and trial it, explaining how the change shifts the strategy space for players.
  • Investigate how professional or community teams adapt game strategies in response to opposition — using video or direct observation — and apply one observed adaptation to their own game.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStrategy adaptation is highly context-dependent — what works in an invasion game fails in a target game. Students need exposure across game categories to build genuine strategic flexibility rather than context-specific habits.
