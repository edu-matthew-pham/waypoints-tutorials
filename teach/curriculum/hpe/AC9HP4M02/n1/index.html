Planning strategies in minor and small-sided games

AC9HP4M02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan and perform strategies to be successful in minor and small-sided games.

Success criteria

  • Describes a strategy they will use before participating in a game.
  • Performs the game using the planned strategy.
  • Reflects on whether the strategy achieved the intended outcome.

Prior knowledge

Students have investigated different ways of moving and manipulating objects and space and drawn conclusions about their effectiveness (AC9HP2M02). They can experiment with movement but have not yet applied structured strategies to game contexts.

Width

Minimum
Plan and perform a simple movement strategy in one minor or small-sided game (e.g. a tag game, target game or net/court game) and reflect on whether it achieved the intended outcome.
Typical
Plan and perform strategies in a range of minor and small-sided game types, reflecting on what worked and adjusting approach across games.
Wider
  • Compare strategies across two different game categories (e.g. tag game vs target game) and explain why the same strategy does or does not transfer.
  • Lead a small group in planning and trialling a strategy, gathering peer feedback on its effectiveness.

Hinge

Planning a strategy — even a simple one — is the prerequisite cognitive move for all subsequent adaptation work. Students who act purely reactively without planning cannot access the evaluation and adaptation demands in the next nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a scaffold for thinking before and after a strategy attempt: what are we trying to achieve, what will we do, what happened. Brief structured reflection (e.g. a verbal debrief format) is more useful at this stage than extended written analysis.
