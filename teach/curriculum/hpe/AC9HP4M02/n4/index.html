Evaluating game tactics

AC9HP4M02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the outcome of a game tactic that has been adapted to improve scoring options using set criteria.

Success criteria

  • Uses provided criteria to assess whether an adapted tactic achieved its intended effect.
  • Identifies at least one strength and one limitation of the tactic.
  • Proposes a specific change to the tactic based on their evaluation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Centre of gravity and stability.

Width

Minimum
Use a provided set of criteria to evaluate one adapted game tactic, identifying whether it improved scoring options and one change they would make.
Typical
Evaluate adapted game tactics across two or more game types using criteria, identifying strengths and areas for improvement and connecting evaluation to future strategy decisions.
Wider
  • Develop their own evaluation criteria for assessing a game tactic, trial the criteria with peers, and refine the criteria based on what they found most useful.
  • Compare tactic evaluations across two game sessions to track whether strategic decision-making improved over time and explain what evidence supports this.

Hinge

Evaluation using criteria is the capability move that connects strategy use to learning — without it, students simply repeat tactics rather than developing strategic thinking. This evaluation habit is directly required in AC9HP6M02 and underpins the more sophisticated tactical analysis expected at Y5-6.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCriteria-based evaluation is a new metacognitive layer for this age group. Providing a structured debrief format (what did we try, what happened, what would we change) and gradually releasing criteria construction to students builds the evaluation capability systematically.
