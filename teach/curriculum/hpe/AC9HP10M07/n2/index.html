← AC9HP10M07 Transfer skills to unfamiliar situations

Applying and evaluating transfer

AC9HP10M07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply transferred skills and strategies in unfamiliar situations and evaluate the success of the transfer

Success criteria

  • Attempts to apply a transferred skill or strategy in a named unfamiliar situation
  • Evaluates whether the transfer was successful — with specific reference to the outcome
  • Identifies what adaptation was needed and explains why the original form didn't transfer directly

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying transferable skills and strategies.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least one transferred skill or strategy in an unfamiliar movement situation; evaluate whether the transfer was successful and what adaptation was needed
Typical
Attempt innovative solutions to movement challenges in unfamiliar situations based on past experience; reflect on the effectiveness of movement solutions and suggest improvements; propose how the solution can be transferred to other movement situations
Wider
  • Apply transfer across multiple unfamiliar situations — compare which transfers worked better and build explanations for why some transfers succeed more readily than others
  • Evaluate a failed transfer attempt — identify what was assumed to transfer that didn't, and explain why; propose what would need to be different for the transfer to succeed
  • Lead a group through an unfamiliar movement challenge by deliberately applying and teaching transfer thinking — evaluate the group's success and your own leadership of the transfer process

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyTransfer capability builds through repeated practice of the transfer cycle across multiple unfamiliar situations. A single successful transfer does not develop the metacognitive skill — students need to experience both successful and unsuccessful transfers, and to reflect on what determined the outcome. The elaborations explicitly require reflection on effectiveness and proposals for further transfer.
