← AC9HP4P01 Resilience and identities

Stories of challenge and success

AC9HP4P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how characters or people overcome challenge and adversity and achieve success in different ways.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a challenge or setback a person faced in a story or real example.
  • Explains how the person's response to the challenge affected their resilience or identity.
  • Describes what 'resilience' means using language connected to a real example.

Prior knowledge

Students have described their personal qualities and those of others, and explained how they contribute to developing identities (AC9HP2P01). They understand personal qualities but have not yet examined how experiences of success, setback and failure shape identity and resilience.

Width

Minimum
Analyse at least one story or example of a person overcoming a challenge, identifying what the challenge was, how they responded, and what the outcome was for their identity or resilience.
Typical
Examine multiple stories and examples (from texts, sport, community, peers) of overcoming challenge; identify patterns in how people respond to adversity and what builds resilience.
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Australians' pride in cultural background strengthens identity and resilience — exploring stories of First Nations athletes, leaders or community members.
  • Explore how responses to success, challenge, setbacks and failure are shaped by cultural beliefs and values — comparing examples from different cultural contexts.

Hinge

Understanding that challenge and setback contribute to rather than undermine identity is the core conceptual shift of this standard. Without it, students' work on resilience strategies (node 2) has no conceptual grounding — they treat resilience as a technique rather than an outcome of how one relates to difficulty.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often conflate success with positive identity development and failure with negative — the key insight that challenge and failure are also identity-strengthening requires explicit discussion and counterexample. Text-based case analysis with guided questions builds this reasoning.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations Australian perspectives on resilience and identity, and cross-cultural comparisons of responses to adversity, offer substantively different frameworks for understanding how identity is built — not just additional examples. Exploring these contexts enriches the conceptual understanding meaningfully.
