Devising decision-making strategies

AC9HP10M09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Checkpoint

Students can devise explicit strategies for group decision-making that demonstrate leadership and collaboration

Success criteria

  • Articulates a specific decision-making approach — not just 'we'll discuss it'
  • Strategy includes a mechanism for incorporating different perspectives or resolving disagreement
  • Explains how leadership is distributed — who decides what, and how

Prior knowledge

Students can demonstrate fair play and reflect on how ethical behaviour influences outcomes (AC9HP10M08), and have previously practised and applied leadership, collaboration and group decision-making in physical activities (AC9HP8M09). At Y9-10 the focus shifts to devising, implementing and refining decision-making strategies — a higher-order planning and evaluation task.

Width

Minimum
Devise one strategy for making a group decision in a physical activity context; explain how it distributes leadership and incorporates different perspectives
Typical
Devise decision-making strategies that incorporate leadership, collaboration and group dynamics; analyse leadership styles and group dynamics through initiative games; use self-talk and encouragement to motivate team members; evaluate their own contribution to teamwork and enjoyable participation
Wider
  • Devise and implement a decision-making strategy for a complex group movement challenge — evaluate how the strategy performed under pressure and what adjustments were needed
  • Analyse different leadership styles (directive, collaborative, distributed) through initiative games — evaluate when each style is most effective and for which group composition
  • Design and implement self-assessment and peer-assessment tools to evaluate performance across multiple group roles — use findings to refine leadership approach

Hinge

Students who cannot devise an explicit decision-making strategy cannot implement and refine one in Node 2 — refinement requires a concrete intended approach to evaluate against actual group outcomes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly lead groups without making their decision-making strategy explicit — acting on instinct without articulating the approach. The investment is in making the strategy explicit: how will decisions be made in this group, what is each person's role, how will disagreement be handled. This articulation is prerequisite for implementation and refinement.
