Understanding community and outdoor spaces for physical activity

AC9HP8M05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the range of community and outdoor settings used for physical activity in their local area and describe key features that make them suitable or unsuitable for different activity types.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two community or outdoor settings available for physical activity
  • Describes at least one feature that supports or limits use of each setting

Prior knowledge

Students have participated in physical activities in natural and outdoor settings and analysed the steps and resources needed to promote participation (Y5-6). They can identify what supports participation but have not yet evaluated management strategies at a community level.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two community or outdoor settings available locally for physical activity and describe one feature that supports or limits use of each.
Typical
Map a range of local community and outdoor settings; describe what makes each suitable for different activity types; identify barriers to and enablers of participation in these settings.
Wider
  • Investigate how urban planning and design decisions have shaped access to outdoor and community physical activity spaces in the local area, considering equity of access across different groups
  • Explore how different community groups use the same outdoor spaces and what this means for managing competing needs

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFamiliarity with a range of community settings is foundational for evaluating strategies — students who only know one space type have a limited basis for evaluation.
