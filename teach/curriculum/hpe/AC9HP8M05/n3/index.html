Evaluating strategies for increased use

AC9HP8M05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate strategies designed to support increased or sustainable use of community and outdoor physical activity spaces, weighing their benefits against potential drawbacks.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one strategy for supporting increased use of a community or outdoor physical activity space
  • Evaluates the strategy using at least one clear criterion (e.g. effectiveness, equity, environmental impact)
  • Weighs at least one benefit and one limitation of the strategy

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Participating in community and outdoor settings.

Width

Minimum
Identify one strategy for increasing use of a community or outdoor space and give at least one reason why it would or would not be effective.
Typical
Evaluate a range of strategies for managing, protecting and increasing use of community and outdoor spaces; consider environmental impact, community equity, and sustainability; design physical activity options that reimagine or better utilise community spaces.
Wider
  • Investigate different approaches to custodial responsibility for Country/Place used by First Nations Australian communities, and evaluate how these principles could inform management of local physical activity spaces
  • Evaluate strategies used to manage and protect environments where physical activities take place (bushwalking trails, surf beaches, skate parks, sporting fields), identifying trade-offs between access and environmental protection

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluation requires students to apply criteria and weigh trade-offs — scaffold this with explicit criteria (e.g. effectiveness, equity, environmental impact, sustainability) before expecting independent evaluation.
