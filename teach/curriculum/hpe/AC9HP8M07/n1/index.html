Proposing movement strategies

AC9HP8M07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the key features of a movement situation and propose a strategy or skill selection that responds to those features with a stated rationale.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one key feature of a movement situation that informs strategy selection
  • Proposes a movement strategy or skill appropriate to that situation with a stated rationale

Prior knowledge

Students have predicted and tested the effectiveness of different skills and strategies in a range of movement situations (Y5-6). They can make predictions and test them but have not yet been expected to systematically propose and evaluate across a range of situation types.

Width

Minimum
Identify the key features of one movement situation and propose a strategy or skill most likely to be effective, giving one reason.
Typical
Identify relevant contextual features across a range of movement situations; propose strategies and skills that respond to each; articulate the rationale for each proposal in terms of what the situation demands.
Wider
  • Explain and justify the movement strategies selected in response to outdoor or nature-based activity challenges (rope courses, bushwalking, orienteering, canoeing) where environmental unpredictability is a key contextual feature
  • Investigate how movement strategy selection differs between competitive and cooperative movement contexts and what contextual features drive the differences

Hinge

Evaluation requires a proposal to evaluate — students who cannot make reasoned proposals have no object for their evaluation. The quality of proposal reasoning determines whether evaluation is substantive or superficial.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkScaffold proposal reasoning by explicitly modelling situation analysis: what are the demands of this situation, what skills or strategies address those demands, and why? Build this as a consistent thinking routine before evaluation is introduced.
