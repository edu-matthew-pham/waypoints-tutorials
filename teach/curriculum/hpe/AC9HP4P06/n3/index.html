Self-regulation strategies

AC9HP4P06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can implement self-regulation strategies to manage the expression of emotional responses.

Success criteria

  • Practises at least one self-regulation strategy.
  • Identifies when in an emotional sequence the strategy should be applied.
  • Reflects on whether the strategy was effective and what they would do differently.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Responding helpfully in emergencies.

Width

Minimum
Practise at least one self-regulation strategy (e.g. deep breathing, counting, moving away) in a scenario or real context, and reflect on its effectiveness.
Typical
Practise and compare a range of self-regulation strategies across different emotional scenarios; reflect on which strategies work best for them in different situations and explain why.
Wider
  • Explore mindfulness-based self-regulation practices (e.g. guided breathing, body scan, visualisation) and practise at least one regularly over a period of time, tracking its effectiveness.
  • Investigate how athletes and performers use self-regulation strategies in high-pressure situations — applying one technique to a physical activity context.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierSelf-regulation in practice requires integrating trigger recognition (node 1) with deliberate strategy selection. Students who have practised both separately need opportunities to apply the full sequence — notice the trigger → apply the strategy — in scenario or real contexts.
Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyNo single self-regulation strategy works in all situations. Students benefit from practising multiple strategies (breathing, movement, self-talk, seeking support) and developing personal knowledge of what works for them.
