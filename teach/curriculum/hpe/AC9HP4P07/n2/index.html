← AC9HP4P07 Consent strategies

Responding to unsafe situations and boundary violations

AC9HP4P07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify actions they can take when they or others are unsafe — including online situations — and practise response strategies.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two actions they can take when someone acts without their consent.
  • Applies at least one response strategy appropriately in a scenario.
  • Identifies who they would go to for help in an unsafe situation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Practising consent strategies — giving and seeking.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two actions they can take when something happens without their permission or consent, including at least one online scenario.
Typical
Explore and practise a range of response strategies for different unsafe situations — online and offline — including saying no, leaving, reporting and seeking help; discuss which response is appropriate in each context.
Wider
  • Analyse scenarios involving increasingly complex consent situations (e.g. someone posts an embarrassing photo online without permission, a trusted person crosses a boundary) and evaluate which response strategies apply.
  • Develop a personal safety plan that identifies trusted adults, response strategies and reporting options for different types of unsafe situations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierResponding to unsafe situations integrates consent understanding (node 1) with help-seeking skills (AC9HP4P08). Scenario-based practice that requires students to identify the situation type and select an appropriate response builds the integration needed.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepOnline and offline unsafe situations require different response strategies. Students who have only practised face-to-face responses are less prepared for the digital contexts where boundary violations are increasingly likely.
