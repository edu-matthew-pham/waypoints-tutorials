Practising consent strategies — giving and seeking

AC9HP4P07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can practise and refine strategies for seeking and giving consent in different situations.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates a strategy for asking for consent in a scenario.
  • Demonstrates a strategy for giving or denying consent clearly and respectfully.
  • Identifies a situation where seeking permission is required.

Prior knowledge

Students have practised strategies for seeking, giving or denying permission respectfully (AC9HP2P04). They have practised basic consent strategies but have not yet rehearsed them in more complex contexts including online environments and situations involving personal safety.

Width

Minimum
Practise at least one strategy for seeking consent and one for giving or denying consent in a described scenario.
Typical
Rehearse and compare strategies for seeking, giving and denying consent across varied contexts (personal space, sharing photos, physical touch); evaluate which strategies are most clear and respectful.
Wider
  • Explore how consent applies in digital contexts (e.g. photo sharing, tagging, personal information) and practise consent strategies appropriate for online interactions.
  • Investigate how consent is taught in different cultural contexts and discuss what is similar and different about the principles involved.

Hinge

Practised consent strategies are the operational foundation for all unsafe situation responses in node 2. Students who have not rehearsed clear consent language cannot access the more complex safety response skills that follow.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsConsent strategy language needs to be rehearsed to automaticity — not just known conceptually. Role-play with explicit language scaffolding ('I'd like to..., is that okay?', 'I don't want that', 'Let me check first') is the most effective practice format.
