Critiquing health strategies

AC9HP10P10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can critique health strategies — their own and others' — for effectiveness, accessibility, and cultural appropriateness

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Applies specific criteria to critique a health strategy — not just expresses a general opinion
  • Critique addresses accessibility — who can access this strategy and who cannot, and why
  • Proposes at least one concrete improvement, not only identifies what is wrong

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning and justifying health strategies.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two criteria to critique a health strategy (their own or another's) — evaluate what it achieves, what it misses, and for whom it is most and least accessible
Typical
Critique strategies for enhancing health, safety, relationships and wellbeing; analyse how social, cultural and economic factors influence the accessibility of strategies and help-seeking; evaluate the influence of stigma on access to health support (menstrual health, gender and sexual diversity, sexual health, mental illness); propose constructive improvements to online/digital health promotion strategies
Wider
  • Investigate cultural rights of First Nations Australians to maintain traditional diets; critique how mainstream health strategies address or fail to address this right, and evaluate the proven health and wellbeing benefits
  • Critique an existing school or community health strategy against criteria including cultural appropriateness, accessibility, and evidence base — propose specific improvements
  • Evaluate how stigma associated with specific health issues (mental health, sexual health, menstrual health) affects help-seeking in your community — critique current strategies for reducing stigma and propose targeted improvements

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates planning/justification (Node 1) with critique capability developed across P09 and P10 — students are now applying critical evaluation to strategies they have themselves designed or selected. The integration is between the planning perspective (what does this strategy aim to do) and the critical perspective (for whom does it work, what does it miss, what does it assume). This is the highest-order move in the P strand at this band.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCritique of health strategies requires application across different health domains and community contexts. Critiquing a mental health strategy requires different criteria than critiquing a food sustainability strategy or a First Nations traditional diet rights framework. Range across health domains builds genuine critique capability.
