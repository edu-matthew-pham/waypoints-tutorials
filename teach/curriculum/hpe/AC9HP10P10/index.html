Plan and critique health strategies

AC9HP10P10 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

plan, justify and critique strategies to enhance their own and others' health, safety, relationships and wellbeing

Prior knowledge

Students can critique health information and media messaging to evaluate their influence on attitudes and actions (AC9HP10P09). At Y9-10 this standard integrates critique capability with strategy planning and justification — moving from evaluating others' messages to designing and critically assessing their own strategies.

By the end

Students plan, justify and critique strategies to enhance health, safety, relationships and wellbeing — across individual, community and cultural dimensions, including mental health and wellbeing strategies.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating community-action initiatives young people have started with positive health and wellbeing influence
  • proposing, sharing and evaluating sustainable food preparation presentations
  • exploring how connection to Country/Place enhances health and wellbeing for First Nations Australians
  • investigating cultural rights of First Nations Australians to maintain traditional diets
  • analysing social, cultural and economic factors influencing health behaviours and help-seeking
  • investigating First Nations Australian community pharmacopeia and health practices with respect for ICIP protocols
  • proposing constructive digital health promotion actions
  • designing and evaluating mental health and wellbeing strategies

Waypoints

  • 1. Planning and justifying health strategiesHingeStudents can plan a strategy to enhance health, safety, relationships or wellbeing and justify it with evidence and reasoning
  • 2. Critiquing health strategiesStudents can critique health strategies — their own and others' — for effectiveness, accessibility, and cultural appropriateness
