Community connections through physical activity

AC9HP4M05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine how physical activity connects people to their community and explore community participation factors.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one way physical activity connects individuals to community.
  • Names at least two factors that can influence whether people participate in physical activity.
  • Explains how one identified factor specifically affected their own or someone else's participation.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Home-based and everyday physical activity.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one way physical activity connects people to community members, and describe one factor that influences whether people in their community participate.
Typical
Examine multiple ways physical activity builds community connections, and discuss a range of factors (social, cultural, environmental, logistical) that influence community participation in physical activity.
Wider
  • Research a specific community physical activity program or event and examine how it was designed to be inclusive and accessible to different community members.
  • Explore how community sport and recreation look different across urban, regional and remote contexts in Australia — including the role of physical activity in First Nations community life.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a multi-factor analysis scaffold to move beyond 'people like it' explanations of participation. A simple factors framework (social, environmental, access, cultural) helps them categorise and examine participation influences systematically.
