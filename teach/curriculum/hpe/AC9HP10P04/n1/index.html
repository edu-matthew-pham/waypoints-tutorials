Power and coercion in relationships

AC9HP10P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how power imbalances and coercion undermine respectful relationships

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific power imbalance and explains how it operates in a relationship
  • Evaluates the effect on at least one party's autonomy, rights or wellbeing
  • Proposes a specific action that could be taken when a relationship becomes coercive or disrespectful

Prior knowledge

Students can explain and apply skills to communicate assertively when seeking, giving or denying consent (AC9HP8P07). They have previously examined roles of respect, empathy, power and coercion in respectful relationships (AC9HP8P04). At Y9-10 the focus shifts to evaluation — assessing how these factors operate and what happens when the balance shifts.

Width

Minimum
Identify a power imbalance in a relationship scenario and evaluate how it affects the ability of both parties to maintain respect and autonomy
Typical
Investigate how the balance of power influences the nature of relationships; evaluate the characteristics of positive, respectful relationships against the rights and ethical responsibilities of individuals; propose actions when a relationship is not respectful
Wider
  • Investigate how historical patterns of inequity, violence and discrimination have lasting intergenerational effects on wellbeing — evaluate strategies for building cultural awareness and empathy that contribute to reconciliation
  • Analyse how bullying, racism and gender-based violence take different forms (verbal, physical, emotional, sexual, economic) in face-to-face and online environments — evaluate which forms are most commonly underrecognised and why
  • Investigate how demonstrating support for truth-telling causes can build cultural awareness, empathy and more respectful relationships at community level

Hinge

Students who cannot evaluate how power imbalances operate cannot assess the role of respect and empathy as countervailing forces in Node 2 — the constructive analysis requires first having established where and how coercion undermines relationships.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often name that power imbalances are harmful without evaluating how they operate — the mechanism by which coercion undermines respect and autonomy. The structured reasoning investment is in building the evaluative chain: what power imbalance, in what relationship context, what effect on each party's rights, what does a respectful response look like. The intergenerational inequity direction requires this same chain applied at a historical and cultural scale.
