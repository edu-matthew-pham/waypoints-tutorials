← AC9HP10P04 Evaluating respectful relationships

Respect and empathy as relational foundations

AC9HP10P04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how respect and empathy actively establish and maintain respectful relationships

Success criteria

  • Identifies specific behaviours or practices that demonstrate respect or empathy in a relationship
  • Evaluates what respect or empathy requires from each party — not just what it looks like from outside
  • Distinguishes between genuine respect/empathy and performative compliance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Power and coercion in relationships.

Width

Minimum
Explain one way in which demonstrating respect or empathy strengthens a relationship; evaluate what this requires from each party
Typical
Evaluate the rights and ethical responsibilities of individuals in relationships; investigate the characteristics of positive, respectful relationships; propose and evaluate actions that build respect and empathy across different relationship types
Wider
  • Evaluate how public acknowledgement of cultural contributions (e.g. First Nations Australians in sport and community) functions as a form of respect that builds relational trust at community level — not just in individual relationships
  • Design a scenario-based evaluation tool that assesses whether a relationship demonstrates respect, empathy and equitable power — apply it to at least two different relationship types
  • Investigate how cultural practices of empathy and respect differ across communities and evaluate what this means for respectful cross-cultural relationships

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepRespect and empathy operate differently across relationship types — peer, romantic, family, online, cross-cultural. Students who only evaluate these in one relationship context develop a narrow understanding that may not transfer. Working across multiple relationship types and cultural contexts builds a more generalisable evaluative capability.
