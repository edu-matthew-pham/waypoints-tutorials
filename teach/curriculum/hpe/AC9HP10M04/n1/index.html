Understanding individual physiological responses

AC9HP10M04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate their body's responses to physical activity at different intensities and connect these to fitness components

Success criteria

  • Names at least two physiological responses observed during activity
  • Explains what each response indicates about exertion level or fitness demand
  • Connects at least one response to a specific fitness component (e.g. cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength)

Prior knowledge

Students can design and justify strategies to increase physical activity to achieve health and wellbeing outcomes (AC9HP8M06). At Y9-10 the focus shifts to personalisation — designing, applying and evaluating strategies that incorporate specific physical activities into their own lives, including understanding individual physiological responses.

Width

Minimum
Participate in a physical activity and identify at least two physiological responses (e.g. heart rate, breathing rate, ability to talk, recovery rate); explain what each response indicates
Typical
Participate in a range of physical activities and evaluate individual responses including heart rate, breathing rate, ability to talk and recovery rate; perform activities designed to improve fitness and analyse how each improves specific fitness components
Wider
  • Investigate target training heart-rate zones for different individuals — explain how these zones are measured, how they relate to fitness level, and how they should inform activity selection for different health goals
  • Compare physiological responses across different activity types at the same perceived effort level — evaluate what this reveals about relative fitness demands
  • Analyse how individual factors (age, fitness level, health condition) affect physiological responses to the same activity — evaluate what this means for personalised plan design

Hinge

Students who cannot interpret their own physiological responses to physical activity cannot make evidence-based decisions about activity selection and intensity in their personalised plan (Node 2) — the design requires understanding what the body is signalling during and after activity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often participate in activities and report physiological responses without interpreting what they mean. The structured reasoning investment is in building the interpretation chain: this response occurred, it indicates this physiological state, this implies this about fitness demand or intensity level, therefore this about activity design. Heart rate zones add a quantitative framework that supports this reasoning.
