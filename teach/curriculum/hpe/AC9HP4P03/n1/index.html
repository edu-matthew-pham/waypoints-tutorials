Stereotypes in texts and media

AC9HP4P03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify gender stereotypes in texts and media and discuss the messages they portray and how they can influence choices and actions.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one stereotype in a text or media example.
  • Describes the message the stereotype sends about a particular group.
  • Explains how that message could influence a person's choices or actions.

Prior knowledge

Students have described their personal qualities and those of others, and explained how they contribute to developing identities (AC9HP2P01). They have a basic understanding of personal qualities but have not yet examined how external stereotypes influence choices and actions.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one stereotype in a text or media example and describe the message it sends about what is expected of a particular group.
Typical
Explore a range of texts and media examples; identify stereotypes about gender and other characteristics; discuss the messages portrayed and how they could influence a person's choices or actions.
Wider
  • Analyse how stereotypes in media targeting children (advertisements, games, toys, clothing) send messages about what is 'for' which gender, and investigate how these messages may limit choices.
  • Compare stereotyping in media across two different time periods or cultural contexts, examining how portrayals have changed or remained similar.

Hinge

Recognising a stereotype as a message about expected behaviour is the prerequisite conceptual move. Students who cannot identify a stereotype as such cannot describe how it influences choices — they will describe the influence without identifying its source.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a working definition of 'stereotype' and practice identifying examples before they can analyse influence. A define-identify-analyse sequence with concrete media examples is more effective than abstract discussion of the concept.
