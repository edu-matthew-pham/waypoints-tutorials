← AC9HP8P01 Values, beliefs and identities

Analysing influence on identity development

AC9HP8P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how specific values and beliefs — from personal, family, peer or cultural sources — influence identity development, identifying the mechanism of influence.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one source of values or beliefs and names the source (personal, family, peer or cultural)
  • Analyses at least one specific way that source influences identity development

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding values and beliefs as identity influences.

Width

Minimum
Identify one source of values or beliefs (personal, family or cultural) and analyse how it influences identity development in at least one specific way.
Typical
Analyse how values and beliefs from multiple sources influence identity development across a range of contexts; examine how cultural beliefs about puberty, gender, and physical change specifically affect the development of gender, cultural and sexual identities.
Wider
  • Analyse how naming of deceased protocols (such as kumanjayi) in First Nations Australian communities reflect community values and impact the identities of community members
  • Examine how cultural beliefs about puberty and physical change create different pathways to gender, cultural and sexual identity across different cultural communities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAnalysis of identity influence deepens when students encounter culturally diverse examples — encountering how the same mechanism (values → identity) operates differently across cultural contexts builds genuine analytical capacity rather than culturally narrow conclusions.
