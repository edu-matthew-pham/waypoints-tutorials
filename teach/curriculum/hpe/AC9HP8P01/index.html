Values, beliefs and identities

AC9HP8P01 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse and reflect on the influence of values and beliefs on the development of identities

Prior knowledge

Students have explained how identities can be influenced by people and places, and how positive self-identities can be created (Y5-6). They can identify external influences on identity but have not yet been expected to analyse how values and beliefs specifically operate as mechanisms of influence.

By the end

Students analyse and reflect on the influence of values and beliefs on the development of identities.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining how their values and beliefs, as well as those of family and friends, influence the development of identities
  • analysing how naming of deceased protocols, such as kumanjayi, can impact the identities of other members within First Nations Australian communities
  • examining how cultural values and beliefs influence the way young people view themselves, including young Australians of Asian heritage
  • examining how cultural beliefs about the physical changes experienced during puberty can influence gender, cultural and sexual identities

Waypoints

  • 1. Understanding values and beliefs as identity influencesHingeStudents can explain what values and beliefs are and describe how they can shape how a person sees themselves.
  • 2. Analysing influence on identity developmentStudents can analyse how specific values and beliefs — from personal, family, peer or cultural sources — influence identity development, identifying the mechanism of influence.
  • 3. Reflecting on personal identity developmentStudents can reflect on how their own values and beliefs — and those of the people and communities around them — have contributed to their developing sense of identity.
