Designing a personalised plan

AC9HP10M06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can design a personalised physical activity plan with justified activity selections linked to specific goals

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Plan includes at least one specific, measurable goal
  • Each activity selection is justified with reference to the goal and a fitness component
  • Plan is realistic for the intended person — accounts for their current fitness level and available time

Prior knowledge

Students can participate in activities promoting health and social outcomes and design participation strategies (AC9HP10M05), and have designed and applied personalised activity strategies (AC9HP10M04). This standard extends to explicitly implementing and evaluating multi-goal personalised plans for themselves or others, including using digital tools and heart-rate zone knowledge.

Width

Minimum
Design a personalised plan with at least two specific activities; justify why each activity was selected based on the goal and individual needs
Typical
Design a personalised physical activity plan using digital tools; include a timeframe, specific goals, and a variety of activities that meet the needs of different people; justify activity selections linked to physical activity goals and wellbeing outcomes; investigate target heart-rate zones for different people and incorporate into plan design
Wider
  • Design a personalised plan for a different person (e.g. with different fitness levels, age, or health conditions) — justify how the design differs from a plan for yourself and why
  • Incorporate heart-rate zone calculations into a multi-week plan — justify how each session's intensity is matched to a specific fitness component goal
  • Use digital planning tools to build a plan that tracks multiple variables (frequency, intensity, duration, type) and includes built-in monitoring and adjustment triggers

Hinge

Students who cannot design a plan with justified activity selections cannot implement it purposefully or evaluate it meaningfully in Node 2 — implementation without a justified design is just exercise, and evaluation requires a stated intention to evaluate against.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly design plans by listing activities they enjoy without justifying how each activity contributes to the stated goal. The investment is in building the design justification chain: goal → fitness component → activity type → intensity and frequency → how it produces the desired outcome. Heart-rate zone knowledge supports this reasoning quantitatively.
