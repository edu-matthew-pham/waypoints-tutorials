← AC9HP8P02 Changes, transitions and support

Analysing the impact of changes and transitions

AC9HP8P02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse the specific impact of one or more transitions on individuals — including emotional, relational and identity effects — and explain what factors influence how significant the impact is.

Success criteria

  • Analyses at least two specific effects of a named transition on an individual
  • Identifies at least one factor that influences how significant the impact is

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding types of changes and transitions.

Width

Minimum
Analyse the impact of one transition on an individual, identifying at least two specific effects and one factor that influences how significant the impact is.
Typical
Analyse the impact of transitions across multiple types; identify factors that mediate impact (social support, coping skills, cultural resources, individual disposition); examine how puberty-related changes affect emotional responses and relationships.
Wider
  • Investigate how accessing and assessing health and sexual health information supports young people through puberty-related transitions, evaluating the quality and relevance of available sources
  • Examine the emotional and relational impacts of 'border crossing' for First Nations young people navigating multiple cultural contexts, considering how this ongoing transition intersects with other developmental changes

Hinge

Devising strategies requires understanding what the strategies are responding to — students who cannot analyse the impact of transitions will devise generic coping strategies unconnected to the specific experience being managed.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkScaffold impact analysis by explicitly modelling the analysis of a specific transition: what changes, for whom, in what ways, and what makes the impact greater or lesser for different people? This builds the analytical structure students need before devising strategies.
