Understanding types of changes and transitions

AC9HP8P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the range of significant changes and transitions relevant to their age group and describe the types of impact — social, emotional, physical — each can have.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two changes or transitions relevant to their age group
  • Describes at least one type of impact (social, emotional or physical) for each identified transition

Prior knowledge

Students have investigated resources and strategies to manage changes and transitions, including puberty (Y5-6). They can identify strategies and resources but have not yet been expected to analyse the impact of transitions systematically or devise strategies for supporting others.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two changes or transitions relevant to their age group and describe one type of impact each can have.
Typical
Identify a range of significant transitions (puberty, changing peer/family relationships, social transitions, cultural transitions) and describe their social, emotional and physical impacts; distinguish between predictable and unexpected transitions.
Wider
  • Examine how ceremonies of First Nations Australians involve sharing of cultural knowledge and mark changes in social standing and responsibilities, comparing these transition structures to those in students' own communities
  • Explore the concept of 'border crossing' — how First Nations Australians navigate multiple cultural contexts — and how this type of ongoing transition can impact sense of belonging

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepA broad repertoire of transition types prevents students from treating only visible physical changes as significant — emotional, social and cultural transitions are equally important in this descriptor.
