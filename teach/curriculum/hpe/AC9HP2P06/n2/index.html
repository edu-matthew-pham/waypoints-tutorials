Food, health and community practices

AC9HP2P06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can discuss how eating a variety of fresh foods and community health practices influence health and wellbeing.

Success criteria

  • Describes how eating a variety of fresh foods can influence health and wellbeing
  • Identifies at least one community health practice and explains its purpose

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Health symbols, labels and community messages.

Width

Minimum
Using a reference such as the Australian Guide to Healthy Eating, describe how eating a variety of fresh foods can influence health and wellbeing.
Typical
Investigate how food choices and a range of community health practices influence health and wellbeing; discuss the purpose of these practices and why they matter.
Wider
  • Explore sustainable practices students can implement in the classroom to improve health and wellbeing, such as composting food waste, creating an edible garden, or reducing single-use plastics
  • Investigate how foods are marketed to children and discuss how marketing influences food choice and health messages
  • Compare a food or health practice from a First Nations Australian or Asian Australian community with a familiar practice and discuss similarities and differences in health purpose

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCommunity health practices vary widely — food, digital use, sustainability, medicine safety. Investigating across multiple practice types gives students a genuinely broader picture of what 'community health' encompasses.
