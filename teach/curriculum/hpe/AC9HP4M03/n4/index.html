← AC9HP4M03 Movement concepts

Manipulating space in games contexts

AC9HP4M03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore different ways of manipulating space to receive passes, maintain possession, or increase or decrease scoring opportunities in games.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a spatial opportunity or problem in a game and names a strategy to address it.
  • Demonstrates deliberate space manipulation (creating or closing space) in a game.
  • Explains how their spatial movement affected a scoring or possession outcome.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Body awareness — symmetry, shapes, angles.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate one spatial strategy in a game (e.g. moving to create space, closing space defensively) and explain what they were trying to achieve.
Typical
Demonstrate spatial manipulation strategies across at least two game categories (invasion, net/court, striking/fielding or target games); explain how space manipulation connects to the scoring or possession outcome.
Wider
  • Analyse a game play (observed or played) to identify how both teams are using space manipulation — mapping movements and explaining the spatial battle.
  • Design a small-sided game modification that forces a particular type of space manipulation (e.g. a constraint that limits defending space) and explain its intended effect.

Hinge

Spatial manipulation in games is the integrative application that demonstrates the full movement concepts standard. Without this applied understanding, the concepts of effort, space, time, objects and people remain disconnected from the games literacy demanded at Y5-6 (AC9HP6M03).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node draws together effort (when to move), space (where to move), time (how quickly) and people (relative to teammates and opponents). Teacher investment should focus on helping students connect these concepts rather than treating each separately.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSpatial manipulation works differently across game categories. Students who have only experienced invasion game space concepts may have limited transfer to target or net/court contexts.
