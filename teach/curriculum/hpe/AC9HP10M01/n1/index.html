Analysing movement performance

AC9HP10M01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse movement performance — their own and others' — identifying what needs to be refined and why

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two specific aspects of a performance that affect the outcome
  • Explains why each aspect matters — not just that it is 'good' or 'bad'
  • Analysis is specific enough to inform a refinement action — generic ('needs to be faster') is insufficient

Prior knowledge

Students can demonstrate and explain how movement concepts can be manipulated to improve movement outcomes (AC9HP8M03). At Y9-10 the focus moves to analysis of performance with feedback — using knowledge of results, digital tools, and constructive peer feedback to refine skills in challenging situations.

Width

Minimum
Observe a movement performance (their own or a peer's) and identify at least two specific aspects that affect the outcome — explain why each matters for performance quality
Typical
Use knowledge of results feedback to analyse performance with accuracy and control; analyse performances (e.g. swimming, athletics, cross-country) and propose strategies for refining technique; use digital tools to record and analyse timing of movements in relation to teammates, opposition or external stimuli
Wider
  • Use video analysis tools to compare own performance against a reference model — identify specific timing, positioning or technique differences and explain their effect on outcome
  • Analyse performance changes when equipment is varied (e.g. different bat weight, ball type, resistance) — evaluate which equipment adaptations improve performance and why
  • Provide constructive performance feedback to a peer using a structured framework (e.g. two specific observations, one improvement focus) — evaluate whether the feedback resulted in a measurable change

Hinge

Students who cannot analyse what specifically needs to change in a performance cannot meaningfully adapt or refine it in Node 2 — adaptation and refinement require a prior analytical judgement about what the current performance is producing and what it needs to produce instead.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly observe performances and describe what they see without analysing why it matters for performance outcome. The structured reasoning investment is in building the analysis chain: what is the specific movement pattern, what effect does it have on the outcome, what would a refined version look like. Digital tools support this — video allows repeated review that verbal observation cannot match.
