Evaluating emotional responses

AC9HP10P06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the consequences of emotional responses — their own and others' — in a range of situations

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific emotional response in a specific situation
  • Evaluates the consequence of that response for the situation and for at least one relationship
  • Distinguishes between the emotion itself and the behavioural response — recognises both are relevant to evaluation

Prior knowledge

Students can propose strategies to challenge biases and promote inclusion (AC9HP10P05), and have previously analysed factors that influence emotional responses and devised self-management strategies (AC9HP8P06). At Y9-10 the focus shifts from devising strategies to evaluating emotional responses and refining existing strategies based on that evaluation.

Width

Minimum
Describe one situation where an emotional response (their own or observed) had a significant impact — evaluate what the response was, why it occurred, and what effect it had
Typical
Reflect on the possible consequences of not recognising emotions in challenging situations including rejection, failure, harassment and violence; evaluate situations where extreme or uncontrolled emotion occurs and its impact on the situation and relationships; analyse how others might be affected by their own emotional responses
Wider
  • Evaluate a case study of conflict escalation — trace the emotional responses at each stage and evaluate where a different response could have changed the outcome
  • Analyse emotional responses in online environments (e.g. responding to harassment, rejection or exclusion online) and evaluate whether strategies that work face-to-face transfer effectively
  • Evaluate the consequences of emotional responses in physical activity contexts — how does uncontrolled emotion (frustration, aggression) affect team dynamics, fair play and individual performance

Hinge

Students who cannot evaluate emotional responses — what triggered them, what effects they had, what alternative responses were available — cannot refine strategies in Node 2. Strategy refinement requires prior evaluative judgement about what the current response achieved and what it failed to achieve.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe emotional responses without evaluating them — narrating what happened without assessing the consequence chain. The structured reasoning investment is in building the evaluative sequence: what was the emotional response, what triggered it, what was its effect on the situation, on relationships, on the person themselves. This evaluation is prerequisite for the refinement work in Node 2.
