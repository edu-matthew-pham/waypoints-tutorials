Transferring movement skills to new situations

AC9HP8M01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply a refined movement skill to a new or varied movement situation, adapting execution appropriately to the new context.

Success criteria

  • Applies a movement skill from a practised context to a new movement situation
  • Identifies at least one adaptation made to execution to suit the new context
  • Uses feedback (from self or others) to evaluate whether the transfer was effective

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Analysing movement skills.

Width

Minimum
Apply a movement skill practised in one context to a different movement situation and describe one adaptation made to suit the new context.
Typical
Transfer movement skills and strategies across a range of movement situations — including different game categories, performance contexts and outdoor/obstacle settings — explaining what was adapted and why.
Wider
  • Use feedback from others to safely and efficiently travel around, over, up, under and through natural or built obstacles — applying movement skills learned in structured game contexts to unpredictable outdoor environments
  • Investigate how movement skills in one game category (e.g. invasion) transfer to a different category (e.g. net/court or target), identifying shared and divergent requirements

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepTransfer is the evidence of capability — students who have only practised in one context cannot demonstrate transfer. Breadth of movement contexts is not enrichment; it is the mechanism by which transfer capability is built and assessed.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates the feedback interpretation, refinement, and analysis capabilities from earlier nodes into coordinated performance across new contexts. Instruction should explicitly support students in drawing on all three in the transfer situation.
