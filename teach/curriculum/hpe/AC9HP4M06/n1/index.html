Australian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines

AC9HP4M06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore physical activity and screen-usage time recommendations in the Australian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for Children and Young People.

Success criteria

  • States the key physical activity recommendation (minutes per day) for their age group.
  • Identifies what is meant by 'sedentary behaviour' in the guidelines.
  • Explains one connection between the guidelines and a health benefit they have studied.

Prior knowledge

Students have participated in physical activities and described how regular activity supports health and wellbeing (AC9HP4M04, same band). They have a working understanding of health benefits but have not yet engaged with formal guidelines or sedentary behaviour recommendations.

Width

Minimum
Identify the key physical activity and screen time recommendations in the Australian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines and describe in general terms what they mean for daily life.
Typical
Explore the Australian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines in detail, examining the physical activity, sedentary and sleep components and connecting each to the health benefits discussed in AC9HP4M04.
Wider
  • Compare the 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for different age groups (children vs teenagers vs adults) and explain how and why recommendations change across life stages.
  • Investigate how the 24-Hour Movement Guidelines were developed — who made them, what evidence was used — and discuss what it means for a guideline to be evidence-based.

Hinge

Understanding the guidelines is the prerequisite for all strategy-setting work in node 2. Students who do not understand what the recommendations are cannot meaningfully propose strategies to achieve them.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkGuidelines contain specific numbers and categories (daily minutes, screen limits, sleep hours) that require direct teaching and structured unpacking before students can engage with them critically. A guided reading and annotation activity is more effective than discovery.
