Strategies to meet recommendations

AC9HP4M06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can propose realistic strategies to incorporate physical activity and reduce sedentary time to meet the 24-Hour Movement Guidelines.

Success criteria

  • Proposes at least two specific strategies to meet physical activity recommendations.
  • Explains how each strategy would increase activity or reduce sedentary time.
  • Identifies one realistic barrier to the strategy and suggests how it could be overcome.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Benefits of regular activity — sleep, concentration, wellbeing.

Width

Minimum
Propose at least two specific strategies to increase physical activity or reduce sedentary time in their own daily routine.
Typical
Propose and discuss a range of strategies to meet physical activity recommendations across school, home and community contexts; evaluate the feasibility of each strategy.
Wider
  • Develop and trial a one-week 'active plan' for their own life, tracking whether they met the recommendations and reflecting on what worked and what was difficult.
  • Design an activity plan for a peer or family member who faces a specific barrier to meeting the guidelines (e.g. limited outdoor space, long screen time habits) and explain how the plan addresses the barrier.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a broad repertoire of strategy options — at school, at home, in community settings, using different activity types — so they can select strategies that fit their specific circumstances. A narrow strategy set limits real-world application.
