Explaining how qualities contribute to identity

AC9HP2P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how personal qualities contribute to developing identities.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least one personal quality contributes to their own or another person's identity
  • Links a specific quality to a specific aspect of identity (e.g. how they are seen, how they feel about themselves)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying personal qualities.

Width

Minimum
Explain how at least one personal quality contributes to their own identity or the identity of someone they know.
Typical
Explain how multiple personal qualities contribute to individual identities; discuss how different qualities shape how a person is known and how they see themselves.
Wider
  • Explore how belonging to First Nations Australian cultural groups contributes to identities and how cultural practices shape personal qualities
  • Examine examples from Australians of Asian heritage that recognise how family and friends contribute to identities
  • Describe personal achievements and explain how succeeding (or struggling) in physical activities influenced their personal identity

Hinge

The explanatory move — 'how qualities contribute to identity' — is the descriptor's primary demand and the conceptual foundation for examining group and community identity in Years 3–4. Thin treatment here (staying at description) means students carry forward only a surface understanding of identity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProvide a simple causal scaffold: 'I am [quality] and this shapes my identity because...'. Without this structure, students tend to list qualities rather than explain their contribution.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExploring identity through cultural belonging, achievement in physical activities, and stories provides genuinely different angles on how qualities shape identity — not just the same explanation in different words.
