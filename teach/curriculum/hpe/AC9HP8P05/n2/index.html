← AC9HP8P05 Community diversity and inclusion

Investigating strategies that promote community valuing of diversity

AC9HP8P05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate specific strategies or initiatives that have influenced how communities value diversity, explaining the mechanisms by which they create change.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one strategy or initiative that influences community valuing of diversity
  • Explains the mechanism by which the strategy produces change

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding diversity and its value.

Width

Minimum
Identify one strategy or initiative that influences how a community values diversity and explain how it works.
Typical
Investigate strategies at multiple levels (school, local, national, global) that challenge stereotypes and promote inclusion; examine how norms and prejudice can be shifted; investigate events that value First Nations Australian contributions and strengthen relationships.
Wider
  • Investigate events and strategies that value First Nations Australian contributions and strengthen relationships — such as Indigenous rounds in sporting codes and NAIDOC Week — evaluating the evidence for their effectiveness in shifting community attitudes
  • Research how anti-discrimination initiatives in different global contexts have addressed specific forms of prejudice, examining what made some more effective than others

Hinge

Proposing actions requires understanding how change happens — students who cannot explain the mechanisms by which strategies create shifts in community attitudes will propose actions without understanding why they would work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkWhen investigating strategies, explicitly ask students to identify the mechanism of change: who is the target of the strategy, what attitude or behaviour is being shifted, and how does the strategy produce that shift? This builds the causal reasoning needed for proposal.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepInvestigating strategies across diverse community contexts — including First Nations, Asian Australian, and other cultural community examples — builds a rich repertoire of approaches that informs more substantive proposal.
