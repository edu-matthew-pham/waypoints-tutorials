Cultural diversity — sharing and celebrating

AC9HP4P05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and celebrate the cultural diversity of their class and community.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one aspect of a cultural background different from their own.
  • Identifies something they learned from engaging with that cultural perspective.
  • Explains why cultural diversity is valued in their community.

Prior knowledge

Students have explored skills and strategies to develop respectful relationships (AC9HP2P02) and investigated emotional responses (AC9HP2P03). They have practised respectful interaction but have not yet made explicit connections between valuing diversity and wellbeing outcomes.

Width

Minimum
Identify and describe at least one cultural practice, game or tradition from their own or a classmate's cultural background.
Typical
Explore and share cultural practices, stories and games from multiple cultural backgrounds; reflect on what learning about different cultures adds to the class community.
Wider
  • Explore the diversity of backgrounds, experiences, beliefs and practices of Asian cultures, examining similarities and differences and connecting this to Australian cultural diversity.
  • Recognise the important role of cultural narratives in First Nations Australian communities — exploring how stories describe diversity and share beliefs and practices.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCultural diversity understanding requires genuine exposure to multiple cultures — not just general awareness that diversity exists. Participation in games, sharing of stories and personal accounts from within the class community grounds the concept in lived experience.
