← AC9HP2P05 Protective behaviours and help-seeking

Help-seeking strategies and safe networks

AC9HP2P05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and rehearse strategies they can use when requiring assistance, including trusted adults, safe places and problem-solving with friends.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one trusted adult and one safe place they could access for help
  • Describes at least one strategy for asking for help in a situation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Body reactions to safe and unsafe situations.

Width

Minimum
Name at least one trusted adult and one safe place they could go to for help; describe one strategy for asking for help.
Typical
Identify a network of trusted people and safe places; practise strategies for seeking help including asking an adult, reading signs, recognising safe locations, and problem-solving with friends.
Wider
  • Recognise photos and locations of safe places in the school and local community and explain why these are safe
  • Explore how to seek help in different scenarios — at school, at home, and in public — and compare which strategies are most appropriate in each context
  • Discuss the importance of seeking help when a problem is too big to solve alone, and practise how to communicate the nature of the problem to a trusted adult

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a practical repertoire of help-seeking strategies and a named network of trusted people. Build this explicitly — not all students have clear networks or know how to activate them.
