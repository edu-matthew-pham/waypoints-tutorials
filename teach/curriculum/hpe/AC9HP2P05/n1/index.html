← AC9HP2P05 Protective behaviours and help-seeking

Body reactions to safe and unsafe situations

AC9HP2P05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the body's reaction to safe and unsafe situations and compare the different emotional responses.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one body reaction that signals a safe situation and one that signals an unsafe situation
  • Identifies these reactions as signals to consider a protective response

Prior knowledge

Students have demonstrated protective behaviours, named body parts and rehearsed help-seeking strategies that help keep them safe (AC9HPFP05).

Width

Minimum
Describe how their body feels in a safe situation versus an unsafe or uncomfortable situation; identify this as a signal to act.
Typical
Identify the body's physical and emotional reactions to a range of safe and unsafe situations; explain how these reactions can help them recognise when they need to use protective behaviours.
Wider
  • Explore how characters in texts experience safe and unsafe situations and compare their emotional responses to the student's own experience
  • Identify body reactions in emergency scenarios through role play and describe what these signals mean
  • Compare how the same situation might feel different to different people, and discuss what this means for recognising when others may need help

Hinge

Recognising bodily and emotional signals as safety indicators is the conceptual anchor for all protective and help-seeking action. Students who do not understand the signal cannot reliably trigger the protective response in real situations.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse explicit language scaffolds to connect body signals to meaning: 'When I feel __ in my body, it might mean __'. This prevents the concept staying at the level of emotion labelling rather than signal interpretation.
