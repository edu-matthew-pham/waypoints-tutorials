Inclusive and exclusive behaviour

AC9HPFP02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify behaviours that may be disrespectful or cause harm during play, and discuss how excluded characters or peers could be included.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one disrespectful or hurtful behaviour in a play or story context
  • Suggests a more inclusive action the character or peer could have taken

Prior knowledge

No prior HPE curriculum. Students bring social experience from home and prior care/education settings. No prior structured curriculum practice of respectful interaction skills.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one behaviour that is disrespectful or hurtful in a play or story context, and suggest how someone could have acted more inclusively.
Typical
Explore stories and play scenarios involving exclusion; discuss why exclusion occurs, how it feels, and what more inclusive behaviour looks like; identify disrespectful behaviours in their own play contexts.
Wider
  • Explore characters from stories across different cultural contexts who navigate inclusion and exclusion
  • Discuss exclusion and inclusion in First Nations Australian community contexts using relevant stories or examples
  • Connect inclusion behaviours to fair play — how respectful social interaction and fair play reinforce each other

Hinge

Students who cannot identify disrespectful or excluding behaviour cannot apply respectful interaction skills (next node) meaningfully. This conceptual understanding of what counts as respectful vs disrespectful is the foundation for practising the skills.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse story-based scenarios and game stoppages to make inclusion/exclusion visible and nameable. Foundation students benefit from explicit naming of the concepts before practising them.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVaried story and cultural contexts broaden understanding of what respect and inclusion look like across different settings.
