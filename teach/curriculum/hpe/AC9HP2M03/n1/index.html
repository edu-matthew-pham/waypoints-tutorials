← AC9HP2M03 Physical activity enjoyment

Features of natural and outdoor settings

AC9HP2M03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise physical features of natural and outdoor settings and describe how those features relate to physical activity.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one physical feature of an outdoor or natural setting
  • Describes how that feature affects physical activity in that space

Prior knowledge

Students have participated in activities in natural and outdoor settings and explored the benefits of being physically active (AC9HPFM03).

Width

Minimum
In at least one outdoor or natural setting, identify one or two physical features and describe how they affect physical activity in that space.
Typical
Across a range of natural and outdoor settings, identify key physical features and describe how those features enable or constrain different types of physical activity.
Wider
  • Compare physical activity in an indoor setting to the same or similar activity in an outdoor or natural setting, noting how features of the environment change what's possible
  • Explore a natural setting (e.g. bushland, beach, park) and identify movement possibilities that do not exist in built spaces
  • Investigate how First Nations Australians' connections to country relate to physical activity in natural settings

Hinge

Understanding what makes a setting suitable for physical activity is the conceptual foundation for the investigation in Node 2. Students who only 'participate' without noticing environmental features cannot meaningfully investigate what makes activity enjoyable in different settings.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProvide a simple observation prompt (e.g. 'I notice __ in this space; this makes __ possible/difficult') to shift students from passive participation to active noticing of environmental features.
