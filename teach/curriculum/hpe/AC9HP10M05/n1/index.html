Physical activity for social outcomes

AC9HP10M05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and participate in physical activities that promote social outcomes alongside health benefits

Success criteria

  • Names a specific physical activity and identifies both a health and a social outcome it promotes
  • Distinguishes social outcomes (e.g. connection, belonging, inclusion) from health outcomes (e.g. fitness, wellbeing)
  • Identifies at least one community space or resource where the activity could be pursued

Prior knowledge

Students can design, apply and evaluate personalised strategies for incorporating physical activity into their lives (AC9HP10M04). At Y9-10 this standard shifts focus from individual fitness planning to the social dimensions of physical activity participation — designing strategies that promote both health and social connection for themselves and others.

Width

Minimum
Participate in at least one physical activity with a clear social dimension; identify one health and one social outcome it promotes
Typical
Participate in physical activities that promote health and social outcomes; identify local natural resources and community spaces where individuals and groups can connect through physical and social activities; investigate community-based campaigns promoting physical activity participation
Wider
  • Investigate how culturally significant physical activities create community connection and belonging — evaluate the social outcomes these activities produce beyond physical health benefits
  • Identify and visit or research local natural settings for physical activity in the community — evaluate how accessible they are for different groups and what could increase their use
  • Participate in or investigate a community campaign promoting physical activity — determine the key elements that made it successful and evaluate whether they could be replicated in a school context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSocial outcomes from physical activity are highly context-dependent — what builds community connection in one cultural or community setting differs from another. Students need to engage with multiple activity types and community contexts to develop genuine understanding of how physical activity produces social outcomes, not just health ones.
