Design physical activity strategies

AC9HP8M06 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

design and justify strategies to increase physical activity levels to achieve health and wellbeing outcomes

Prior knowledge

Students have proposed and explained strategies to increase physical activity and reduce sedentary behaviour (Y5-6). They can describe what strategies might work but have not yet been expected to design and justify strategies against specific health and wellbeing outcome targets.

By the end

Students design and justify strategies to increase physical activity levels to achieve health and wellbeing outcomes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • designing and monitoring a personal physical activity plan that proposes realistic strategies for increasing physical activity levels and reducing sedentary behaviours
  • investigating which physical activities people engage in to maintain emotional and social wellbeing, and designing a program of activities aimed at increasing social connection and wellbeing
  • comparing their current physical activity levels, amount of sleep and sedentary activity time with Australia's 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for Children and Young People and suggesting strategies for themselves and others to meet these recommendations

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying physical activity barriers and enablersHingeStudents can identify factors that support or limit physical activity participation for themselves and their peer group, including personal, social and environmental factors.
  • 2. Understanding health and wellbeing outcomesStudents can explain the health and wellbeing outcomes that increased physical activity is intended to achieve, with reference to specific outcome areas (physical, social, emotional, mental).
  • 3. Designing and justifying strategiesStudents can design a strategy to increase physical activity levels for themselves or others, with explicit justification linking design decisions to identified barriers and targeted health or wellbeing outcomes.
