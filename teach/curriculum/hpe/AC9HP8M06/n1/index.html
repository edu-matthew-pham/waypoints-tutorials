Identifying physical activity barriers and enablers

AC9HP8M06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify factors that support or limit physical activity participation for themselves and their peer group, including personal, social and environmental factors.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two factors that influence their own physical activity participation
  • Categorises factors as personal, social or environmental

Prior knowledge

Students have proposed and explained strategies to increase physical activity and reduce sedentary behaviour (Y5-6). They can describe what strategies might work but have not yet been expected to design and justify strategies against specific health and wellbeing outcome targets.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two factors (personal, social or environmental) that influence their own physical activity levels.
Typical
Identify and categorise factors that support and limit physical activity participation across personal, social and environmental domains; compare their current activity levels with Australia's 24-Hour Movement Guidelines; identify specific gaps.
Wider
  • Compare physical activity levels, sleep and sedentary time against the 24-Hour Movement Guidelines and map personal and social factors contributing to any identified gaps
  • Investigate which physical activities different peer groups engage in for social and emotional wellbeing, identifying patterns in motivation and participation

Hinge

Design decisions require a clear understanding of what is being designed against — students who cannot identify the specific barriers and enablers in their own context will produce generic strategies with no real justification grounded in evidence.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a structured analysis framework (personal/social/environmental) to ensure students map their own context systematically before designing. Strategies should be explicitly linked to identified barriers.
