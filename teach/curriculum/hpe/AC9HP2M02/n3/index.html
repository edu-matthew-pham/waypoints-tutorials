Drawing conclusions about movement effectiveness

AC9HP2M02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate different movement approaches and draw conclusions about which are most effective for a given purpose.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies which of two movement approaches was more effective for a given purpose
  • Gives at least one reason for their conclusion linked to what they observed or experienced

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Investigating object and space manipulation.

Width

Minimum
After trying at least two movement approaches (different equipment, speed, space use or game rules), identify which was more effective and give one reason.
Typical
Across multiple movement investigations, compare different approaches and draw conclusions about effectiveness against a defined purpose or set of criteria; explain reasoning.
Wider
  • Evaluate a self-created game against a set of criteria (e.g. inclusive, challenging, enjoyable) and propose changes
  • Compare movement approaches across indoor and outdoor settings and draw conclusions about which settings favour which movement types
  • Use peer feedback to evaluate the effectiveness of a movement approach and revise conclusions based on additional evidence

Hinge

The ability to draw conclusions about effectiveness — not just describe what was done — is the descriptor's primary inquiry demand. Thin treatment here leaves students able to perform movement tasks but unable to evaluate them, which is the prerequisite capability for the investigative movement standards in Years 3–4.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProvide a simple evaluative scaffold (e.g. 'I tried __ and __; __ was more effective because __') to make the conclusion-drawing move explicit. Without structure, young students often describe what happened rather than evaluating it.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepConclusions drawn about only one movement context are thin. Vary the contexts (games, sequences, outdoor settings) so students practise drawing conclusions across different types of movement investigations.
