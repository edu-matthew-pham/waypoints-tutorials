← AC9HP2M02 Moving and manipulating objects

Movement variation — speed, direction, level

AC9HP2M02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can demonstrate changes in speed, direction and level as they use locomotor and non-locomotor skills.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates a deliberate change in at least two movement variables (speed, direction, or level)
  • Can describe what they changed and how it felt different

Prior knowledge

Students have experimented with different ways of moving their body safely and manipulating objects and space (AC9HPFM02). They can practise fundamental movement skills in varied situations (AC9HP2M01).

Width

Minimum
In a movement task, deliberately change at least two of: speed, direction, or level, and describe what changed.
Typical
Demonstrate intentional changes in speed, direction and level across locomotor and non-locomotor movement sequences; describe how each change affects movement.
Wider
  • Use stimuli such as music, rhythm or words to drive changes in speed and level, and compare which stimuli produce the greatest movement variation
  • Participate in movement activities that require safe navigation of different outdoor spaces, comparing which movement patterns are most efficient
  • Create a short movement sequence that deliberately uses all three variables (speed, direction, level) and reflect on which combinations felt most effective

Hinge

The ability to deliberately vary movement parameters (speed, direction, level) is foundational to the investigative intent of this descriptor. Students who cannot vary their movement purposefully will not be able to compare effectiveness or draw meaningful conclusions (Node 3).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStudents need repeated opportunities to practise deliberate variation — first one variable at a time, then in combination. Rushing to comparison before variation is controlled produces weak conclusions.
