Community health and safety strategies

AC9HPFP06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and follow community strategies that keep children safe, including safety procedures at specific community environments.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one community safety strategy and names the environment it applies to
  • Describes what the safety strategy requires them to do in that environment

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Health and safety symbols.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one community strategy that keeps children safe (e.g. pedestrian crossing, swimming pool rules) and describe what it requires them to do.
Typical
Identify and explain a range of community safety strategies — pedestrian crossings, traffic controllers, pool and beach safety procedures, road environment rules; follow safety symbols and procedures in relevant environments.
Wider
  • Investigate the purpose behind different safety strategies — why we have pool rules, road rules, school crossing procedures
  • Explore safety strategies in natural and outdoor environments, including water safety in different contexts
  • Connect community safety strategies to personal responsibility — what their role is in following and promoting safety

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCommunity safety strategies differ by environment — road, water, home, outdoor. Students who understand pool rules may not automatically transfer reasoning to road contexts. Vary the environments.
