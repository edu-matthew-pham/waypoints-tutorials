← AC9HP10P02 Managing changes and transitions

Evaluating existing strategies

AC9HP10P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the effectiveness of strategies they have previously used or learned for managing changes and transitions

Success criteria

  • Names a specific strategy for managing a change or transition
  • Evaluates its effectiveness with reference to a specific situation or context
  • Identifies at least one condition under which the strategy works well and one where it is less effective

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse factors that shape identities and evaluate how individuals influence others' identities (AC9HP10P01). They have previously investigated resources and strategies to manage changes including puberty (AC9HP6P02) and analysed the impact of changes and transitions (AC9HP8P02). At Y9-10 the focus shifts to refining, evaluating and adapting — not introducing strategies for the first time.

Width

Minimum
Identify one strategy for managing a change or transition and evaluate its effectiveness in a specific situation — what worked, what didn't, and why
Typical
Assess behavioural expectations across different relationships and social situations; evaluate how these expectations influence decisions and actions when managing transitions; identify which strategies are most effective for different types of change
Wider
  • Analyse how changing roles and responsibilities for First Nations Australians as social standing changes within communities creates distinct transition challenges — evaluate what strategies are culturally appropriate versus imposed from outside
  • Investigate the impact of border crossing on identity and transition for First Nations Australians, evaluating the specific strategies needed when navigating between cultural contexts
  • Evaluate strategies for managing digital transitions (e.g. changing social media presence, online identity shifts) as a distinct category of contemporary transition that existing frameworks may not fully address

Hinge

Students who cannot evaluate the effectiveness of strategies cannot meaningfully refine or adapt them in Node 2 — refinement requires a prior evaluative judgement about what needs to change and why.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often describe strategies without evaluating them — listing what they would do without assessing whether it works or why. Scaffolding the evaluative move (what was the strategy, in what context, what was the outcome, what does this reveal about when it works) is the key investment here. The First Nations transition contexts require particular care — evaluation must include cultural appropriateness as a criterion, not just effectiveness by generic measures.
