Identifying and planning for risk situations

AC9HP10P08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify health risk situations and plan realistic strategies for managing them

Success criteria

  • Names a specific health risk situation and identifies who may be at risk
  • Plans a realistic response strategy — not an idealised one that ignores social or contextual constraints
  • Plan includes at least one external resource or support service that could be accessed

Prior knowledge

Students can plan and implement strategies using health resources to enhance health, safety and wellbeing (AC9HP8P10). At Y9-10 the focus moves to more complex risk situations — peer pressure around substance use, first aid including CPR, consent under impairment, and safe blood practices — with evaluation of strategies added to planning and rehearsal.

Width

Minimum
Identify one health risk situation (e.g. peer pressure, emergency, digital safety risk) and plan a realistic response strategy
Typical
Identify a range of health risk situations relevant to young people; plan realistic responses including refusal skills, communicating choices, contingency plans; critique the appropriateness and effectiveness of support services available in the local community for young people
Wider
  • Plan responses to scenarios involving peer pressure around e-cigarettes, cannabis, psychostimulants, opiates or hallucinogens — evaluate which refusal strategies are most realistic in peer group social contexts
  • Investigate safe blood practices across a range of situations (not sharing needles, precautions in physical activity, safe sexual practices including use of condoms and dams) and plan how to raise these practices with peers in an accessible way
  • Examine factors that impact ability to seek, give or deny consent when a person is affected by alcohol or other drugs, or where there is a power imbalance — plan strategies for recognising and responding to these situations

Hinge

Students who cannot plan realistic strategies for named risk situations cannot rehearse and evaluate them in Node 2 — rehearsal requires a concrete plan to enact and evaluate.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepHealth risk situations are diverse — peer pressure around substances, physical emergencies, digital safety, and consent under impairment each require different strategies. Students need to plan across multiple risk types rather than developing one polished response. The elaborations explicitly require multiple risk contexts.
