← AC9HP4P08 Protective behaviours online and offline

Warning signs — physical, emotional and external

AC9HP4P08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe warning signs (physical, emotional and external) that help them know if they are safe or unsafe.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two warning signs from at least two different categories (physical, emotional, external).
  • Explains what each warning sign might indicate about a situation.
  • Describes what they would do after noticing a warning sign.

Prior knowledge

Students have identified and demonstrated protective behaviours and help-seeking strategies to help themselves and others stay safe (AC9HP2P05). They can identify and demonstrate strategies in familiar contexts but have not yet applied them across the full range of online and offline situations.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two warning signs (across physical, emotional and/or external categories) that indicate an unsafe situation.
Typical
Describe a range of physical, emotional and external warning signs across different unsafe situations; explain what makes each sign a reliable indicator of risk.
Wider
  • Investigate how animals and humans use similar instinctive warning signals (physical responses like increased heart rate or hair standing up) as protective mechanisms, and connect this to the body's natural safety system.
  • Explore how cultural backgrounds influence what is recognised as an unsafe situation and what warning signs are prioritised — discussing how this affects protective behaviour.

Hinge

Recognising warning signs is the precondition for all protective behaviour and help-seeking. Students who cannot identify that they are unsafe cannot access or apply the strategies in the subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a classification system for warning signs (physical body responses, emotional feelings, external environmental signals) to organise their recognition and move from general unease to actionable assessment. Explicit categorisation practice with examples builds this.
