Protective behaviours across situations

AC9HP4P08 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine protective behaviours to stay safe in different situations, including near water or roads, in the park, online and when someone makes them feel uncomfortable.

Success criteria

  • Describes a protective behaviour relevant to at least two different contexts.
  • Explains how each behaviour reduces risk in that context.
  • Identifies one behaviour they already use and one they could strengthen.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Warning signs — physical, emotional and external.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one protective behaviour relevant to at least two different contexts (e.g. near water, online).
Typical
Describe and discuss protective behaviours across a full range of situations — water, roads, parks, online, personal safety — identifying what makes each behaviour protective and how it reduces risk.
Wider
  • Indicate safe places and trusted people on a local map for their own neighbourhood, creating a personalised safety reference.
  • Examine protective behaviours used by characters in texts or media across varied situations, evaluating the effectiveness of each and identifying alternatives.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepProtective behaviours look significantly different across contexts — water safety has different requirements from online safety, which differs again from personal space safety. Students need exposure across contexts to build situation-appropriate protective behaviour.
