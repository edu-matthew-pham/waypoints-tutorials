Understanding fair play and inclusion as design criteria

AC9HP8M08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what fair play and inclusive participation mean in a physical activity context and identify specific conditions that threaten or support them.

Success criteria

  • Explains what fair play means in a physical activity context
  • Explains what inclusive participation means and gives one example of a condition that limits it

Prior knowledge

Students have devised and tested alternative rules and game modifications to support fair play and inclusive participation (Y5-6). They have tested modifications but have not yet systematically investigated how and why modifications work.

Width

Minimum
Explain what fair play and inclusive participation mean and identify one condition in a physical activity that threatens either.
Typical
Explain fair play and inclusion as design criteria; identify a range of conditions (equipment, skill gaps, rule complexity, physical ability) that can threaten or support each; explain how scoring systems can be structured to advantage or disadvantage different participant groups.
Wider
  • Analyse the benefits and potential drawbacks of player-officiated games where players rather than an independent official are responsible for officiating, examining how this affects fair play
  • Explore how traditional games of First Nations Australians are structured to support fair play and inclusive participation, identifying the design principles underlying these structures

Hinge

Without understanding fair play and inclusion as criteria, modification investigations become arbitrary rather than purposeful. Students need to know what they are designing for before they can evaluate whether modifications achieve it.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExplicitly establish fair play and inclusion as design criteria with concrete indicators before investigation begins. Ask students to identify who is disadvantaged in a given game and what the modification is trying to fix.
