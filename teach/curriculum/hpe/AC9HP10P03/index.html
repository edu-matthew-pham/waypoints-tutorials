Gender equality and violence prevention

AC9HP10P03 · Years 9–10 Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate how gender equality and challenging assumptions about gender can prevent violence and abuse in relationships

Prior knowledge

Students can refine, evaluate and adapt strategies for managing changes and transitions (AC9HP10P02), and have previously examined how power, coercion and gender stereotypes influence roles and relationships (AC9HP8P03). At Y9-10, the focus moves from examining influence to investigating the causal link between gender assumptions and violence prevention.

By the end

Students investigate how gender equality and challenging gender assumptions can prevent violence and abuse in relationships, including analysing attitudes that excuse or condone gender-based violence and evaluating how equality empowers respectful relationships.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigate and challenge attitudes that lead to people enacting, excusing or condoning gender-based violence
  • analysing ways that attitudes towards gender equality and power can affect expectations and behaviour, and lead to practices of sexual coercion, harassment, violence and submission
  • evaluating how gender equality can empower people to develop equal and respectful relationships
  • exploring scenarios in texts that demonstrate how gender inequality can arise from intergenerational patterns of inequity and unequal power in relationships, and proposing strategies for challenging inequality in their communities

Waypoints

  • 1. Attitudes that enable gender-based violenceHingeStudents can analyse how attitudes towards gender equality and power lead to practices of coercion, harassment and violence
  • 2. Gender equality as preventionStudents can evaluate how gender equality and challenging gender assumptions can prevent violence and empower respectful relationships
