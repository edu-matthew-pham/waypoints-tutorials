Working cooperatively with a partner

AC9HP2M05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can work cooperatively with a partner when practising new movement skills.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates at least one cooperative behaviour (e.g. waiting turn, encouraging partner) during a paired movement activity
  • Can name what they did to cooperate with their partner

Prior knowledge

Students have followed rules to promote fair play (AC9HPFM04) and have experienced cooperative interaction in basic game contexts. There is no direct Foundation predecessor for collaborative strategy use — this is a new demand at this band.

Width

Minimum
In a paired activity, cooperate with a partner to practise a movement skill; demonstrate at least one cooperative behaviour (e.g. waiting, encouraging, sharing feedback).
Typical
Across several partner activities, consistently demonstrate cooperative behaviours including turn-taking, encouragement and mutual practice; describe what cooperation looks like in movement contexts.
Wider
  • Compare how practising with a partner differs from practising alone, and reflect on which produces better skill development and why
  • Work with a partner from a different cultural background or with different physical abilities and reflect on how to adjust cooperative strategies
  • Design a partner skill-practice routine and evaluate how well the cooperative strategies used supported both partners' learning

Hinge

Partner cooperation is the building block for group collaboration. Students who cannot sustain cooperative behaviour in a paired context will not be able to apply inclusion strategies or adapt games for group participation (Nodes 2 and 3).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsCooperative behaviour in movement contexts requires deliberate, repeated practice — students need regular paired tasks with explicit attention to the cooperative dimension, not just movement skill practice.
