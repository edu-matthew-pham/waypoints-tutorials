Personal identity and belonging

AC9HPFP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation Health and Physical Education · Personal, social and community health · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make personal connections between their identity and their sense of belonging — who they are, where they come from, the people in their lives.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Shares at least one aspect of who they are or where they come from
  • Makes a connection between something about themselves and a feeling of belonging to a group or community

Prior knowledge

No prior HPE curriculum. Students bring lived experience of family, community and personal identity. No prior structured curriculum investigation of identity and belonging.

Width

Minimum
Share something about who they are — including at least one aspect of family, culture or personal interest — and connect this to a story or text about identity and belonging.
Typical
Explore personal identity through stories, discussion and creative expression; make connections between personal qualities, family background and belonging in class and community contexts.
Wider
  • Explore identity and belonging stories from First Nations Australian contexts, discussing similarities and differences with their own experience
  • Investigate cultural practices from different cultures, including cultures of Asia, that are important to families and communities
  • Explore how belonging to groups (family, class, community) shapes identity and personal connections

Hinge

A secure sense of personal identity is the conceptual foundation for investigating others and differences (next node). Students who cannot articulate aspects of their own identity cannot meaningfully compare or connect with others' identities and family structures.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepIdentity is expressed through multiple lenses — family, culture, physical strengths, community. Varied expression modes (drawing, story, movement, talk) allow all students to access this concept from their own starting point.
