← AC9HP2M01 Fundamental movement in varied situations

Controlling objects with equipment and body parts

AC9HP2M01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform fundamental movement skills involving controlling objects with equipment and different parts of the body.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one piece of equipment to send or receive a moving object
  • Attempts object control using a non-dominant body part in a movement activity

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Selecting and applying movement skills in games.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one piece of equipment to control a moving object; attempt control using a non-dominant body part.
Typical
Use a range of equipment types and body parts to send, receive and retain objects in movement contexts; practise across both dominant and non-dominant body parts.
Wider
  • Use different types of equipment to create an original movement challenge and evaluate how difficult object control was
  • Compare controlling objects in water versus on land, noting how the medium changes the challenge
  • Explore equipment from different cultural contexts, noting similarities and differences in how objects are controlled

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsObject control requires repetition with feedback — students benefit from focused practice on one control pattern before switching equipment or body parts.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVarying equipment types (bats, racquets, hands, feet) and object types (balls of different sizes, beanbags) broadens the range of control experiences without increasing conceptual demand.
