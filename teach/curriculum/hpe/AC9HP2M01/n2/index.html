← AC9HP2M01 Fundamental movement in varied situations

Selecting and applying movement skills in games

AC9HP2M01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 1–2 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select and implement different movement skills to be successful in a range of games.

Success criteria

  • In a game situation, selects a movement skill appropriate to what the game requires
  • Can describe why they used a particular movement in at least one game

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locomotor and non-locomotor movement.

Width

Minimum
In at least one minor game, select an appropriate movement skill and use it purposefully to participate successfully.
Typical
Across a range of minor games, select and implement movement skills appropriate to the game context; describe why they chose a particular movement.
Wider
  • Invent a new game using known movement skills and reflect on whether the movement choices made the game interesting and challenging
  • Participate in children's games from First Nations Australian cultures and identify which movement skills are used
  • Modify a familiar game to require a different movement skill and evaluate whether the change improves the game

Hinge

The ability to select movement skills purposefully — not just perform them in isolation — is the core transfer move of this descriptor. Thin treatment here leaves students able to perform skills in drills but unable to apply them in context, which undermines all subsequent movement application across the band.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkMake the selection process explicit: before and after games, ask students why they chose a particular movement. Brief verbal or visual prompts help students articulate skill choice rather than just performing habitually.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary game types (chasing, fielding, net, target) so students practise selection across different movement demands rather than optimising for one game context.
