← AC9HP8M03 Manipulate movement concepts

Demonstrating deliberate manipulation

AC9HP8M03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Health and Physical Education · Movement and physical activity

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Health and Physical Education: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can demonstrate deliberate manipulation of movement concepts in a variety of movement forms, showing that the manipulation produces an improved or intended outcome.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates deliberate manipulation of at least one movement concept in a physical activity
  • Explains what manipulation was made and why it was expected to improve the movement outcome
  • Evaluates whether the demonstration achieved the intended outcome change

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Explaining how manipulation changes outcomes.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate manipulation of at least one movement concept in one activity, showing that the change produced the intended improvement.
Typical
Demonstrate manipulation of movement concepts across multiple forms (game-based performance, rhythmic sequences, outdoor movement); connect biomechanical principles (effort, angle, spatial relationships) to performance design and execution.
Wider
  • Create, perform and appraise rhythmic movement sequences that demonstrate intentional variations in flow, space and relationships — evaluating whether each manipulation achieved its intended effect
  • Design and refine performances demonstrating how to manipulate space and relationships between players to achieve successful movement outcomes in a tactical game context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDemonstration integrates conceptual understanding with physical execution and reflective evaluation. The full demand requires students to know what they are doing and why — instruction should keep all three layers visible simultaneously.
