← AC9HC9S01 Develop and modify civic inquiry questions

Modifying questions as investigation develops

AC9HC9S01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can revisit and modify their inquiry questions in response to new information gathered during investigation.

Success criteria

  • Modifies at least one inquiry question during an investigation
  • Explains what new information or understanding prompted the modification and how the revised question better serves the investigation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Developing focused civic inquiry questions.

Width

Minimum
Modify at least one inquiry question based on information gathered during investigation, and explain what prompted the change.
Typical
Revisit initial inquiry questions as investigation develops; modify questions to be more specific, more accurate, or more relevant based on what has been found; explain the reasoning for modifications (e.g. moving from 'How democratic is Australia's system of government?' to 'How representative is Australia's system of government?' as evidence about voting systems is encountered).
Wider
  • Document a full inquiry question development trail — initial question, modification trigger, revised question, and effect on investigation — and evaluate how modification improved the inquiry
  • Practise modifying questions across two or three different civic topics to build flexibility and transfer
  • Evaluate the inquiry questions used in a published report or civic inquiry and propose how they could have been better framed

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsModifying questions requires students to hold their original question in mind, identify the specific gap or imprecision the new information revealed, and articulate the revision clearly. Regular low-stakes practice — e.g. modifying questions at the end of each research session — builds this reflective habit.
