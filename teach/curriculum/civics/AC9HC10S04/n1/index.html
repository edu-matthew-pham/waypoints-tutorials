Identifying civic participation methods and strategies

AC9HC10S04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the methods or strategies used by a government, NGO or campaign group to address an issue at regional or global level or to ensure accountability to international obligations.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two methods or strategies used by a civic actor
  • Describes what each method involves with sufficient detail to enable evaluation
  • Connects the method to the specific issue or obligation being addressed

Prior knowledge

Students have evaluated the methods or strategies related to making decisions about civic participation at Year 9 (AC9HC9S04). At Year 10 the descriptor adds 'and outcomes' — students are now expected to evaluate not just the method but what the method achieved.

Width

Minimum
Identify and describe at least two methods or strategies used by a civic actor to address a specific issue, with a brief description of what each involves.
Typical
Examine the methods or strategies used by the Australian Government, a non-government organisation, or a campaigning or pressure group to address an issue at a regional or global level or to ensure accountability to Australia's international obligations — identifying the full range of strategies deployed.
Wider
  • Compare the methods used by a government actor and a non-government actor addressing the same issue and analyse why their strategic choices differ
  • Investigate how a specific pressure group or advocacy campaign has adapted its methods over time in response to changing political conditions
  • Research how international accountability mechanisms (e.g. UN treaty review processes) are used as civic participation strategies by NGOs and affected communities

Hinge

Students who have a vague understanding of what methods were used cannot evaluate the relationship between method and outcome — knowing that a campaign 'raised awareness' is insufficient for evaluating effectiveness.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents benefit from seeing a variety of civic participation methods (lobbying, public campaigns, litigation, international reporting, grassroots organising) before selecting one to evaluate in depth — a thin repertoire produces thin evaluation.
