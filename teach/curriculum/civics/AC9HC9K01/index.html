Constitution and democratic processes

AC9HC9K01 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the role of the Australian Constitution in providing the basis for Australia's federal system of government and democratic processes, including institutions, and the process for constitutional change through a referendum

Prior knowledge

Students know how Australians are informed about and participate in democracy (AC9HC8K01), and the role of political parties and independent representatives including elections and formation of governments (AC9HC8K02). They have a working understanding of democratic participation but have not yet examined the constitutional foundations underpinning these processes.

By the end

Students explain the role of the Australian Constitution in establishing Australia's federal system of government and democratic institutions, and describe the process and challenges of constitutional change through referendum.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • understanding the aims of the founding fathers prior to Federation in writing the Australian Constitution, including the need for compromise
  • describing the process by which referendums to change the Australian Constitution are initiated and decided, including the role of the Australian Electoral Commission, and discussing the advantages and disadvantages of having a constitution that can only be amended by referendum
  • investigating the extent to which the Constitution upholds and enables democratic institutions and processes, including a constitutional monarchy, a federal parliamentary system with elected legislatures, protection of states' rights and individual rights, and representation of the interests of all Australians
  • assessing the effectiveness of the process of constitutional change and the degree to which it supports popular sovereignty through examining selected referendum proposals; for example, the successful vote on the Constitution Alteration (Aboriginals) 1967 or the unsuccessful vote on the Constitution Alteration (Establishment of Republic) 1999

Waypoints

  • 1. Purpose and origins of the ConstitutionHingeStudents can explain why the Australian Constitution was written and the key compromises involved in Federation.
  • 2. Federal system and distribution of powersStudents can describe how the Constitution distributes powers between the Commonwealth and the states, and identify the democratic institutions it establishes.
  • 3. Constitutional protection of democratic processesStudents can explain how the Constitution upholds democratic institutions and processes, including protections and limitations.
  • 4. Referendum process and constitutional changeHingeStudents can describe the process for changing the Australian Constitution through referendum and assess why it is difficult.
