Federal system and distribution of powers

AC9HC9K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how the Constitution distributes powers between the Commonwealth and the states, and identify the democratic institutions it establishes.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two areas of federal government responsibility and two of state government responsibility
  • Identifies two constitutional institutions and explains their role in democratic government

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Purpose and origins of the Constitution.

Width

Minimum
Identify that the Constitution divides responsibilities between federal and state governments, and name at least two institutions established by the Constitution (e.g. Parliament, the High Court).
Typical
Describe the federal system as a division of powers between the Commonwealth and states; explain the role of key institutions established by the Constitution (Parliament, the executive, the High Court); identify examples of federal vs state responsibilities and how conflicts are resolved.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific area of federal-state conflict (e.g. environmental law, education funding, health) and analyse how constitutional division of powers has shaped that conflict
  • Examine the concept of a constitutional monarchy and how it operates in practice alongside parliamentary democracy
  • Compare federal systems in Australia and another country to explore different ways of distributing powers

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly understand the federal system in the abstract but struggle to apply it to specific policy areas. Using two or three concrete examples of divided responsibility (roads, hospitals, defence) builds the working understanding needed for later analysis.
