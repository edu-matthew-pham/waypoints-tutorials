Civic and global participation

AC9HC9K05 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how and why individuals and groups, including community, religious and cultural groups, participate in and contribute to civic life in Australia and to global citizenship

Prior knowledge

Students know how culture and religion influence individuals' and groups' perceptions and expressions of citizenship (AC9HC8K05), and have examined debates about national identity and citizenship (AC9HC8K06). They understand that citizenship is expressed through identity and culture but have not yet examined formal and informal mechanisms of civic participation at community, national and global levels.

By the end

Students explain how and why individuals and groups participate in civic life in Australia and as global citizens, including the contribution of community, religious and cultural organisations.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • researching the work of an Australian non-government organisation (NGO), philanthropist, community group or religious group and the extent to which they contribute to the Australian community; for example, volunteers that support communities ravaged by natural disasters such as the State Emergency Service (SES), the Salvation Army, the Red Cross, Sikh Volunteers Australia
  • researching examples of young people who are acting as global citizens, such as Malala Yousafzai and Greta Thunberg, and Australia's Amelia Telford, Mackinley Butson and Bassam Maaliki, or UNICEF Australia's Young Ambassadors
  • collaborating with peers to plan a citizenship campaign on an issue related to sustainability or climate change

Waypoints

  • 1. Forms and motivations of civic participationStudents can identify different forms of civic participation and explain the motivations that lead individuals and groups to participate.
  • 2. Community, religious and cultural groups in civic lifeStudents can research and explain the contribution of specific non-government organisations, community or religious groups to Australian civic life.
  • 3. Global citizenshipStudents can explain what global citizenship means and provide examples of individuals or groups acting as global citizens.
