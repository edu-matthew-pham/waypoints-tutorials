Distinguishing law types: statutory, common, delegated

AC9HC8K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish statutory law, common law and executive (delegated) law from each other, and identify how the houses of parliament can disallow delegated legislation.

Success criteria

  • Can correctly identify the source and authority of statutory, common and delegated law
  • Can explain how parliament can disallow delegated legislation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Criminal law vs civil law.

Width

Minimum
Distinguish statutory law from common law and delegated law with a brief description of each.
Typical
Distinguish statutory law (Acts of parliament), common law (judge-made law through precedent) and executive/delegated law (regulations made under parliamentary authority) from each other — explaining the source and authority of each — and identify how the houses of parliament can disallow delegated legislation through disallowance mechanisms.
Wider
  • Trace a specific regulation through the delegated legislation process — identifying the parent Act, the regulation made under it, and whether parliament has used or considered disallowance
  • Investigate how the three types of law interact in a specific area of Australian law (e.g. environmental law, employment law) — what comes from statute, what from common law, what from delegated regulation
  • Examine how the balance between statutory, common and delegated law has shifted over time as government regulation has expanded
