Democratic participation

AC9HC8K01 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how Australians are informed about and participate in democracy

Prior knowledge

Students know the key features of Australia's system of government including democracy, the Constitution, responsible government and federalism (AC9HC7K01), and the characteristics of Australia's democracy including freedoms of speech, association, assembly, religion and movement (AC9HC7K02). They can explain how values of freedom, respect, fairness and equality support social cohesion (AC9HC7K05). The gap this descriptor fills is the mechanisms by which citizens actually participate in and are informed about that democratic system — moving from structural knowledge to participatory knowledge.

By the end

Students explain how Australians are informed about and participate in democracy, including the range of participatory mechanisms, how those mechanisms have changed over time, and the role of media, interest groups and campaigns in shaping informed citizenship.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating how Australians can participate in democracy, such as voting in elections, contacting their elected representatives, joining political parties and standing as candidates, and using lobby groups and taking direct action
  • explaining how who can vote and have a say in Australian democracy has changed since Federation, and the importance of an independent electoral service such as the Australian Electoral Commission
  • examining the issues of concern to young people in Australia today and investigating different ways they are seeking to influence political processes in addressing these issues
  • analysing case studies of citizen participation in political campaigns such as the Uluru Statement from the Heart
  • explaining how citizens' understanding of issues, policies and political choices are shaped and can contribute to active and informed citizenship, including the influence of the media and social media, political parties, interest groups and election campaigns
  • examining a range of strategies used to influence citizens' electoral choices, such as public debate, media and social media, opinion polls and political consulting firms, advertising, interest groups and political party campaigns

Waypoints

  • 1. Forms of democratic participationHingeStudents can identify and describe the main formal and informal ways Australians participate in democracy, including voting, contacting representatives, joining political parties, standing as candidates, using lobby groups, and taking direct action.
  • 2. Voting rights and the AECHingeStudents can explain how voting rights in Australian democracy have changed since Federation and describe the role of the Australian Electoral Commission as an independent electoral service.
  • 3. Young people and political influenceStudents can identify issues of concern to young Australians and explain different ways young people seek to influence political processes.
  • 4. Case study: citizen participation in political campaignsStudents can analyse a case study of citizen participation in a political campaign, identifying the participatory strategies used and evaluating their significance.
  • 5. Media, interest groups and informed citizenshipHingeStudents can explain how media, social media, political parties and interest groups shape citizens' understanding of issues and their choices, and describe strategies used to influence electoral behaviour.
