How international obligations shape domestic law and policy

AC9HC10K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how at least one international convention or declaration has shaped Australian law or government policy, using specific examples.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one international convention or declaration and the obligation it creates
  • Explains how that obligation has shaped a specific Australian law or government policy
  • Uses accurate examples with correct names of treaties and policies

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Australia's key international treaty commitments.

Width

Minimum
Explain how at least one international convention or declaration has shaped a specific Australian law or government policy, using an example.
Typical
Investigate how international conventions and declarations — including the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, the Paris Agreement, the World Heritage Convention, and the Refugee Convention — have shaped Australian law and government policies, with specific examples for at least two.
Wider
  • Trace in detail how the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples has shaped (or not shaped) Australian government law and policies with respect to First Nations Australians
  • Analyse Australia's legal obligations under the Paris Agreement (2016) and evaluate the extent to which Australian policy meets those obligations
  • Investigate how the Convention Relating to the Status of Refugees and its protocol shape Australia's offshore processing policy, and analyse the issues this creates

Hinge

Students who cannot connect specific treaties to specific policy changes cannot complete the 'issues in application' analysis that is the descriptor's second key demand — they need the treaty-to-policy link established before they can examine where it breaks down.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCovering at least two distinct treaty-policy examples (e.g. UNDRIP and climate, or climate and refugees) gives students a comparative base — they see that different obligations create different kinds of issues rather than treating all treaties as equivalent.
