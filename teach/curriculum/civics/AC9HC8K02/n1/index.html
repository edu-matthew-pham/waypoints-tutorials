Structure of Australian parliaments

AC9HC8K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the structure and composition of Australian parliaments at both federal and state/territory level, including bicameral and unicameral arrangements.

Success criteria

  • Can identify the two houses of the federal parliament and describe the distinct function of each
  • Can explain the difference between a bicameral and a unicameral parliament with an Australian example of each

Prior knowledge

Students know the key features of Australia's system of government including responsible government, federalism and the structure of parliament (AC9HC7K01). They understand the characteristics of Australia's democracy including freedoms that underpin political participation (AC9HC7K02). The gap this descriptor fills is the mechanics of how political representation operates — how parliaments are structured, how representatives are elected and how government is formed and lost.

Width

Minimum
Describe the structure of the federal parliament, identifying the two houses and their key functions.
Typical
Describe the structure and composition of Australian parliaments at both federal and state/territory level — including the House of Representatives, the Senate, state lower and upper houses, and unicameral territories — and explain the role of each chamber in the legislative process.
Wider
  • Compare the structures of two state or territory parliaments, examining why Queensland and the territories have unicameral parliaments while other states are bicameral
  • Investigate the composition of the current federal parliament — the representation of parties, independents, gender and cultural background — and what this reveals about political representation
  • Examine the role of the Governor-General and state governors in the formal structure of Australian parliamentary democracy

Hinge

Understanding the structure of parliament — which chambers exist, what their roles are — is the prerequisite for understanding how government is formed, how voting systems work and what roles political parties play. A thin treatment produces confusion between the roles of upper and lower houses and prevents meaningful engagement with later nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to build an accurate working map of parliamentary structures before they can make sense of how those structures operate politically. Focus on ensuring the key components (houses, roles, federal vs state) are securely understood.
