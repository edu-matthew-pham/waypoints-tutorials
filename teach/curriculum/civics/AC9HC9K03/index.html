Court jurisdictions and tribunals

AC9HC9K03 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the key features and jurisdictions of Australia's court system, and the operations of courts and tribunals

Prior knowledge

Students know the characteristics of laws and how they are made through parliaments and courts (AC9HC8K03), and the types of law in Australia including criminal and civil law and First Nations customary law (AC9HC8K04). They understand law is made through two mechanisms but have not yet examined how the court hierarchy is structured or how different courts exercise their jurisdiction.

By the end

Students explain the key features and jurisdictions of Australia's court system, describe the operations of courts and tribunals, and explain how court judgements contribute to the development of law.

Waypoints

  • 1. Court hierarchy and jurisdictionHingeStudents can describe the structure of Australia's court hierarchy and explain what jurisdiction means in this context.
  • 2. Civil and criminal law in practiceStudents can use sample cases to explain the differences between civil and criminal law and how courts apply each.
  • 3. Precedent and the development of lawStudents can explain how court judgements impact on the development of law through statutory interpretation and the creation of precedent.
