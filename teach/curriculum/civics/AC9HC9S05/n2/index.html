← AC9HC9S05 Create evidence-based civic arguments

Incorporating evidence into civic arguments

AC9HC9S05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct a civic argument in which evidence is incorporated into the argument structure, not merely appended.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Constructs an argument on a civic issue that includes at least one claim and one piece of supporting evidence
  • Evidence is integrated into the argument (supports a claim) rather than simply listed or appended
  • Uses at least two civics and citizenship terms accurately within the argument

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Using civic concepts and terms accurately.

Width

Minimum
Construct an argument on a civic issue that includes at least one piece of evidence integrated into the body of the argument (not just listed at the end).
Typical
Develop an evidence-based argument about a civics and citizenship issue — such as a need to reform youth justice arrangements in a particular jurisdiction; incorporate evidence into the argument structure by using it to support claims, acknowledge counter-arguments, and draw conclusions; use civic concepts and terms throughout.
Wider
  • Develop arguments on the same civic issue from opposing perspectives, and evaluate which argument makes more effective use of evidence
  • Construct an argument that responds to a counter-argument — specifically addressing contrary evidence rather than ignoring it
  • Develop a multimodal civic argument (written, visual, or oral) for a specific audience (e.g. a parliamentary submission, a community presentation, a letter to a local councillor) and evaluate how audience shapes argument construction

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierEvidence incorporation is an integration task — students must combine their civic content knowledge, their source evaluation skills (AC9HC9S02), and their perspective analysis skills (AC9HC9S03) in the production of a single argument. Feedback on drafts that distinguishes evidence integration from evidence reference is the key teaching move.
