Locating relevant sources across a range

AC9HC10S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can locate print and online sources relevant to a political, legal or civic issue, selecting sources that represent varying and conflicting points of view.

Success criteria

  • Locates at least three sources relevant to the civic issue
  • Sources represent at least two different perspectives or points of view
  • Includes both print/formal and digital sources

Prior knowledge

Students have located, selected and compared information from a range of sources at Year 9 (AC9HC9S02). They can use digital tools to survey political issues and explore media for bias and representation.

Width

Minimum
Locate at least three sources on a civic issue that represent at least two different perspectives, including at least one print or formal source and one digital source.
Typical
Locate print and online sources that represent varying and conflicting points of view on a contemporary political, legal or civic issue, including traditional media, social media, government documents, and survey/polling data.
Wider
  • Use digital survey or polling tools to collect primary data on a political issue and evaluate how survey design affects the results obtained
  • Investigate how different media environments (traditional vs social media) make particular types of civic information more or less visible or accessible
  • Locate sources produced by groups whose perspectives are typically underrepresented in mainstream civic debate and evaluate what they add to the issue

Hinge

Students who locate only one perspective or who cannot deliberately seek out conflicting views will produce incomplete analysis — the comparison and evaluation in subsequent nodes depends on having sources that represent genuine variation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkTeach source-mapping as a deliberate strategy — students should plan their source search to ensure perspective diversity, not just gather whatever appears first. Model the difference between varied and echo-chamber sourcing.
