Evaluating sources for credibility and bias

AC9HC10S02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate sources for credibility, perspective and potential bias, including identifying stereotypes, over-generalisation and misrepresentation in media.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least two sources for credibility and perspective
  • Identifies a specific potential bias or limitation in at least one source
  • Distinguishes between a source's viewpoint and factual error

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating relevant sources across a range.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least two sources for credibility and perspective, identifying one potential bias or limitation in each.
Typical
Evaluate sources for credibility, perspective and potential bias — including examining traditional and social media texts for stereotypes, over-generalisation and misrepresentation, such as those related to how cultural groups or immigrants are represented.
Wider
  • Conduct a detailed media analysis comparing how two different outlets frame the same civic issue, identifying specific language choices and representation patterns
  • Investigate how algorithms shape what civic information different users see on social media and evaluate what this means for an informed citizenry
  • Compare the policies of different political groups on a contested issue and evaluate the framing choices each group makes in presenting their position

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSource evaluation requires explicit criteria. Students benefit from a structured framework (e.g. authority, accuracy, purpose, perspective) rather than general scepticism — teach the evaluative categories before applying them to specific sources.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe algorithm and social media direction gives students a systemic rather than individual-source view of bias — understanding how information environments are structured is a different and richer analytical move.
