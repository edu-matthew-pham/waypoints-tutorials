Law-making processes

AC9HC8K03 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the characteristics of laws and how laws are made in Australia through parliaments (statutory law) and through the courts (common law)

Prior knowledge

Students know the key principles and features of the Australian legal system, including the Constitution, the rule of law and the court system (AC9HC7K03). They understand that democratic systems have structures and checks. The gap this descriptor fills is specifically how law is made — the characteristics that define a law and the two distinct sources (parliament and courts) through which Australian law is created.

By the end

Students explain the characteristics of laws in Australia and how laws are made through both parliamentary processes (statutory law) and through the courts (common law), including the role of delegated legislation and the relationship between parliament and the courts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring the characteristics of laws, such as reflecting society's values; being enforceable; being known, clear and understood; and being relatively stable
  • understanding how governments can introduce new rules and regulations without parliament voting on them (for example, delegated or subordinate legislation is not made directly by an Act of parliament, but under the authority of an Act of parliament)
  • questioning whether laws are affected by different cultural or social perspectives, such as those of First Nations Australians
  • identifying the 2 chief sources of law (parliament and the courts) and analysing a case study of statute and common law
  • examining the relationship between parliament and the courts

Waypoints

  • 1. Characteristics of lawsHingeStudents can describe the key characteristics of laws in Australia — that they reflect society's values, are enforceable, are known, clear and understood, and are relatively stable.
  • 2. Two sources of law: statute and common lawHingeStudents can identify the two chief sources of Australian law — parliament (statute law) and courts (common law) — and explain how each source creates law.
  • 3. Delegated legislationStudents can explain how delegated (subordinate) legislation works — that governments can make rules under authority granted by an Act of parliament without parliament voting on each rule directly.
  • 4. Cultural and social perspectives on lawStudents can question whether laws are affected by different cultural or social perspectives, using First Nations Australians as a key example of how cultural perspective intersects with law-making.
  • 5. Relationship between parliament and the courtsStudents can explain the relationship between parliament and the courts as law-making bodies, including that parliament is supreme but courts interpret statute and can fill gaps through common law.
