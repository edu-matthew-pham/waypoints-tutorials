← AC9HC8K03 Law-making processes

Cultural and social perspectives on law

AC9HC8K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 5 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can question whether laws are affected by different cultural or social perspectives, using First Nations Australians as a key example of how cultural perspective intersects with law-making.

Success criteria

  • Can give a specific example of how cultural or social perspectives have influenced or been in tension with Australian law
  • Can explain why the law-making process may not automatically reflect the perspectives of all groups in a diverse society

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Delegated legislation.

Width

Minimum
Give one example of how a cultural or social perspective has influenced or been in tension with Australian law.
Typical
Examine whether and how Australian laws reflect or fail to reflect diverse cultural and social perspectives — with particular focus on First Nations Australians as a group whose legal traditions and social perspectives have frequently been in tension with statute and common law.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific example where First Nations Australian cultural perspectives have influenced or challenged Australian statute or common law (e.g. land rights, customary practices, cultural heritage protection)
  • Examine how social change — such as changing attitudes to marriage equality or drug policy — has driven legal change, and analyse the lag between social perspectives and legislative change
  • Investigate how multicultural Australia's diverse religious and cultural communities interact with civil and criminal law — where tensions arise and how they are managed

Response modes
