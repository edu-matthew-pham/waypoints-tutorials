Relationship between parliament and the courts

AC9HC8K03 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the relationship between parliament and the courts as law-making bodies, including that parliament is supreme but courts interpret statute and can fill gaps through common law.

Success criteria

  • Can explain the key distinction between parliament and the courts as law-making bodies — that parliament legislates and courts interpret and develop common law
  • Can explain how the relationship between parliament and courts contributes to the rule of law in Australia

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Cultural and social perspectives on law.

Width

Minimum
Explain one key aspect of the relationship between parliament and the courts as law-making bodies.
Typical
Examine the relationship between parliament and the courts — that parliament is the supreme law-making body but courts interpret statutes, develop common law in areas where statute is silent, and review executive action. Explain how this interaction maintains the rule of law.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific case where a court's interpretation of a statute led parliament to amend the legislation in response
  • Examine the role of the High Court of Australia in interpreting the Constitution and how this can limit what parliament can do
  • Investigate judicial review — what courts can and cannot do when reviewing government action, and why this matters for the rule of law

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates statute law, common law, delegated legislation and the characteristics of laws into a coherent picture of how Australia's law-making system operates. Ensure the synthesis is explicit — that students can explain how the whole system fits together.
