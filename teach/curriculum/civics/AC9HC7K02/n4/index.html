← AC9HC7K02 Characteristics of Australia's democracy

Formal and active citizenship

AC9HC7K02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can distinguish between formal citizenship (legal status) and active citizenship (engaged participation), and identify who has been included and excluded from citizenship rights in Australia, particularly in relation to First Nations Australians.

Success criteria

  • Distinguish formal citizenship from active citizenship and explain what each involves
  • Identify that not all people in Australia have historically had equal access to citizenship rights, with reference to First Nations Australians

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Ways of participating in democracy.

Width

Minimum
Distinguish formal citizenship (legal membership of the nation, with associated rights) from active citizenship (engaged participation in civic life); identify that not all people in Australia have always had equal citizenship rights.
Typical
Explain the attributes of formal and active citizenship; identify how First Nations Australians and other groups have been excluded from full citizenship rights and freedoms historically, and examine examples of First Nations Australians' active citizenship and advocacy (e.g. Neville Bonner, Adam Goodes, Noel Pearson, Ellie Gaffney).
Wider
  • Investigate the 1967 referendum and the campaign leading to it as a case of active citizenship driving constitutional change
  • Examine the contributions of specific First Nations Australian citizens (Neville Bonner, Adam Goodes, Noel Pearson, Murundoo Yanner, Charles Mene, Ellie Gaffney, Evelyn Scott, Pat O'Shane) and the significance of their advocacy
  • Explore what it means to 'become' an Australian citizen through naturalisation, and what rights and responsibilities this confers

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe exclusion of First Nations Australians from citizenship rights is not background information — it is central to understanding what Australian democracy has meant in practice. These cases must be substantively engaged with, not mentioned in passing.
