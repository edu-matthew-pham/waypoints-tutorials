Limits on democratic freedoms

AC9HC7K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that democratic freedoms are not absolute and can be limited 'within the bounds of law', and identify reasons why such limits exist.

Lessons on this site

  • Within the bounds of law

Success criteria

  • Explain that democratic freedoms can be limited by law and identify at least one reason why such limits exist
  • Give an example of a legal limit on a democratic freedom and explain the purpose of that limit

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Democratic freedoms.

Width

Minimum
Explain that democratic freedoms can be limited by law and give at least one example of a legal limit on a freedom and the reason for it (e.g. defamation law limits freedom of speech to protect individuals).
Typical
Explain that democratic freedoms are subject to legal limits and identify examples of how the law limits freedoms of speech, association, assembly, religion and movement; explain the reasons for such limits (e.g. protecting others' rights, national security, public order).
Wider
  • Investigate a specific legal case where freedom of speech was tested in Australian courts, examining how courts balance competing rights
  • Explore how the limits on democratic freedoms in Australia compare with formal constitutional protections in other countries
  • Examine First Nations Australians' experience of legally enforced limits on freedoms (e.g. historical restrictions on movement under Protection Acts) and how this shapes understanding of democratic freedom

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of freedoms that are simultaneously protected and limited requires careful reasoning — students need to understand why a freedom can be both real and bounded, and what makes a legal limit legitimate rather than arbitrary.
