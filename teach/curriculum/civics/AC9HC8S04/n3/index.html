Making decisions about civic participation strategies

AC9HC8S04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the reasoning involved in choosing civic participation strategies — how the choice of strategy is shaped by the aim, the context and the available options.

Success criteria

  • Can explain at least two factors that would influence the choice of civic participation strategy for a given issue
  • Can justify a strategic choice for a civic scenario with reference to aim and context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Democracy, active citizenship and global citizenship.

Width

Minimum
For a given civic issue, explain why one particular participation strategy would be appropriate, referencing the aim and context.
Typical
Explain the methods or strategies related to making decisions about civic participation — examining how the choice of strategy depends on the civic aim, the political and legal context, the scale of the issue, the resources available and the likely audience. Apply this reasoning to real or hypothetical civic scenarios.
Wider
  • Analyse a case where a civic group had to choose between different participation strategies (e.g. legal challenge vs direct action vs political lobbying) and evaluate the reasoning behind the choice made
  • Investigate how civic participation strategies differ in authoritarian versus democratic contexts — examining what strategies are available when formal democratic channels are restricted or absent
  • Develop and justify a civic participation plan for a current issue, explaining each strategic decision with reference to aim, context and likely effect

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates active citizenship strategies and the democracy/global citizenship links into the decision-making framework the descriptor explicitly requires. Ensure students apply reasoning — not just description — to strategic choice.
