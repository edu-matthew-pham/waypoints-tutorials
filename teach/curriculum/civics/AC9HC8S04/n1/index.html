Active citizenship strategies and their purposes

AC9HC8S04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how active citizenship strategies may contribute to informed and positive change, explaining the purpose of different strategies in building a democratically cohesive society.

Success criteria

  • Can describe at least two distinct active citizenship strategies
  • Can explain how each strategy contributes to civic change or democratic cohesion, not just describe what it involves

Prior knowledge

Students have explained methods and strategies for civic participation at Year 7 level (AC9HC7S04). The gap this descriptor fills is explaining those methods in the context of decision-making — not just naming strategies but explaining the reasoning behind choosing particular strategies for particular civic aims, and the links between democratic societies, active citizenship and global citizenship.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two active citizenship strategies and explain how each contributes to democratic participation or positive civic change.
Typical
Describe a range of active citizenship strategies — including campaigning, lobbying, community organising, public advocacy, direct action and participation in formal democratic processes — and explain how each can contribute to informed and positive change and to building a democratically cohesive society, drawing on examples such as stopping whaling or reducing carbon emissions.
Wider
  • Investigate a successful civic campaign in Australia and analyse which active citizenship strategies were used, why those strategies were chosen and what made them effective
  • Compare active citizenship strategies across different civic contexts — environmental campaigns, social justice movements, community advocacy — examining whether the same strategies transfer across contexts
  • Investigate how digital tools and social media have created new active citizenship strategies and changed the landscape of civic participation

Hinge

Understanding that civic participation requires strategy — that different methods suit different aims and contexts — is the prerequisite for explaining the decision-making aspect of this descriptor. A student who treats civic participation as a list of things people do cannot explain how or why particular strategies are chosen.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a working repertoire of active citizenship strategies with concrete examples before they can reason about choosing between them. Invest in building that repertoire across diverse contexts.
