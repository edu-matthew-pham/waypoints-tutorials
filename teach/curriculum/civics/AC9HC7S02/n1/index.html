Identifying and using civics-relevant sources

AC9HC7S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the types of sources relevant to civic investigations (government websites, media, specialist reports, statistics) and explain why different source types are appropriate for different purposes.

Success criteria

  • Identify at least three source types relevant to civic investigations and explain what each provides
  • Match a given inquiry question to the most appropriate source type and explain the choice

Prior knowledge

Students can locate, collect and organise information and data from primary and secondary sources in a range of formats (AC9HS6S02). The Y7 shift is to civics-specific source types (government websites, specialist reports, legislation) and a more deliberate approach to source selection and organisation.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three types of sources useful for civic investigations (e.g. government websites, news articles, specialist reports) and explain what kind of information each provides.
Typical
Identify the range of source types relevant to civics — government websites, media reports, articles, graphs, charts, statistics, specialist reports from bodies like the Australian Law Reform Commission — and explain which source types are most appropriate for finding information about government systems, legal principles, cultural diversity, and civic issues.
Wider
  • Evaluate the relative authority of different source types for civic claims — comparing a government website, a newspaper editorial, and a specialist report on the same issue
  • Investigate how to access official sources such as the Australian Law Reform Commission reports, parliamentary Hansard, and ABS data for civics research
  • Explore the difference between primary sources (legislation, Hansard, court decisions) and secondary sources (media, commentaries) in a civic research context

Hinge

If students cannot distinguish between appropriate and inappropriate source types for civic claims, their research (S02) and analysis (S03) will be built on poorly selected evidence. This is the source literacy gate for civic inquiry.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a working repertoire of civics-specific source types — not just 'Google it', but knowing when to look at Hansard, ABS, specialist commission reports, or government websites. Practise finding the right type for different question types.
