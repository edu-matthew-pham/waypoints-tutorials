Selecting and organising information

AC9HC7S02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select information from multiple sources on the basis of similarity and relevance to the investigation topic, and organise it coherently for analysis.

Lessons on this site

  • Keep what answers your question
  • Organise what you found

Success criteria

  • Select information from at least two sources that is relevant to a civic inquiry question
  • Organise selected information so that similar ideas from different sources are grouped, not just listed by source

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Locating information using search strategies.

Width

Minimum
Select information from at least two different sources that is relevant to a civic inquiry question, and organise it so that similar ideas from different sources are grouped together.
Typical
Select and organise information from different sources (media reports, online publications, government websites) on the basis of similarity and relevance; identify key findings from specialist sources such as Law Reform Commission reports; organise information in a format that supports analysis (e.g. comparison table, annotated notes, concept map).
Wider
  • Develop a structured note-taking system that tracks which source each piece of information came from, supporting later citation and attribution
  • Practice organising conflicting information from different sources — identifying where sources agree and disagree rather than only looking for supporting evidence
  • Organise information from a civic investigation into a structure that anticipates an argument or explanation, moving from collection to analytical organisation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsOrganising information by similarity and relevance (rather than by source) is a cognitive step that many students skip — practise is needed to build the habit of synthesising rather than summarising.
