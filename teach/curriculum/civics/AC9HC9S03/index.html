Evaluate civic perspectives and interpretations

AC9HC9S03 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse information, data and ideas about political, legal or civic issues to identify and evaluate differences in perspectives and interpretations

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse information about civic issues to identify and explain differences in perspectives and potential challenges (AC9HC8S03). At Year 9 the expectation advances from explaining differences to evaluating them — students must assess the values, motivations and contexts that underpin perspectives, not just identify that perspectives differ.

By the end

Students analyse information about political, legal or civic issues to identify and evaluate differences in perspectives and interpretations, including examining the values and motivations underlying those perspectives.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the values, motivations and contexts that underpin different perspectives about political and legal systems and processes; for example, calls for constitutional recognition for First Nations Australians, First Nations Voice and Makarrata, or the impact of reducing or extending mining activity on particular communities
  • examining information that includes a diversity of perspectives about the effectiveness of a political or legal institution; for example, 'Is Australia's parliament representative of the Australian community?' or 'Does the court system deliver justice for the victims of crime?'
  • investigating how the Australian Government responds to an issue and examining the process of policy development and implementation, such as the development of welfare changes and financial support in response to natural disasters or emergencies

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying values and motivations underlying perspectivesHingeStudents can identify the values, motivations and contexts that underpin different perspectives on a civic or political issue.
  • 2. Evaluating effectiveness and policy responsesStudents can evaluate the effectiveness of a political or legal institution and examine how government responds to civic issues.
