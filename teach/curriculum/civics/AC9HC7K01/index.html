Australia's system of government

AC9HC7K01 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the key features of Australia's system of government, including democracy, the Australian Constitution, responsible government and federalism

Prior knowledge

Students know the key institutions of Australia's system of government, that it is based on the Westminster system, and the key values and beliefs of Western democracies (AC9HS6K06). They understand the roles and responsibilities of the three levels of government (AC9HS6K07) and the events leading to Federation and the Constitution (AC9HS6K01).

By the end

Students explain the key features of Australia's system of government, including democracy, the Australian Constitution, responsible government and federalism.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • describing Australia's system of responsible and representative government set out in the Australian Constitution, with a focus on the roles of the Governor-General, the Senate and the House of Representatives, the Prime Minister and Cabinet, the Opposition and the members of parliament
  • explaining the origins of the Australian system of government by acknowledging the aspects of the Westminster and Washington systems in the overall model of Australia's Federation
  • exploring key democratic concepts, including popular sovereignty, representation, accountability and a partial separation of powers through the election of members of a legislative branch who answer to the people and from whom the executive branch is drawn
  • investigating Australia as a federation with a division of powers and responsibilities between Commonwealth and state/territory governments

Waypoints

  • 1. The Australian Constitution as foundationHingeStudents can explain the role of the Australian Constitution as the foundational legal document establishing the structure and powers of Australia's national government.
  • 2. Representative and responsible governmentHingeStudents can explain the principles of representative and responsible government, including the roles of the Governor-General, Parliament, Prime Minister and Cabinet, and the relationship between the executive and the legislature.
  • 3. Democratic principlesStudents can explain key democratic concepts including popular sovereignty, representation, accountability, and the partial separation of powers in Australia's system.
  • 4. Federalism and division of powersStudents can explain Australia as a federation with a constitutional division of powers and responsibilities between Commonwealth and state/territory governments.
  • 5. Westminster and Washington influencesStudents can explain the origins of Australia's system of government by identifying the aspects drawn from Westminster (British) and Washington (US) systems in the overall federation model.
