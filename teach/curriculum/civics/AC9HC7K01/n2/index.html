← AC9HC7K01 Australia's system of government

Representative and responsible government

AC9HC7K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the principles of representative and responsible government, including the roles of the Governor-General, Parliament, Prime Minister and Cabinet, and the relationship between the executive and the legislature.

Lessons on this site

  • We elect them, they answer to us

Success criteria

  • Explain how the Prime Minister and Cabinet are drawn from and accountable to Parliament
  • Describe the role of at least two institutions established by the Constitution (e.g. Governor-General, Senate, House of Representatives)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: The Australian Constitution as foundation.

Width

Minimum
Explain that in a representative democracy citizens elect members of parliament, and that the executive (Prime Minister and Cabinet) is drawn from and accountable to the legislature.
Typical
Explain the roles of the Governor-General, the Senate, the House of Representatives, the Prime Minister, Cabinet, and the Opposition; describe how the executive is accountable to Parliament and how this distinguishes responsible government from other systems.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific constitutional crisis (such as 1975) to examine how the principles of responsible government can be tested in practice
  • Compare Australia's responsible government model with a presidential system (e.g. USA) where the executive is separate from the legislature
  • Explore how question time, motions of no confidence, and Senate estimates function as mechanisms of parliamentary accountability

Hinge

The accountability relationship between the executive and legislature is central to understanding how responsible government works and why it differs from other democratic models. Thin understanding here means students misread how political power actually operates in Australia.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of the executive being drawn from and accountable to the legislature is genuinely counter-intuitive for students — invest in the logical chain: election → parliament → government formation → accountability.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExamining concrete accountability mechanisms (question time, votes of no confidence) and historical examples helps students understand how the principle operates in practice.
