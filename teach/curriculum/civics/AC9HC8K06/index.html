National identity debates

AC9HC8K06 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

different experiences of, perspectives on and debates about Australia's national identity and citizenship, including the perspectives of First Nations Australians as owners of their respective nations, and of different migrant groups

Prior knowledge

Students understand how Australia's pluralist multi-faith society draws on diverse cultural origins (AC9HC7K04), the values supporting social cohesion (AC9HC7K05), and from K05 in this year level, how culture and religion shape citizenship expressions. The gap this descriptor fills is the explicitly contested dimension of national identity — not just that Australia is diverse, but that there are genuine, ongoing debates about what it means to be Australian, who belongs, and whose perspectives are centred or marginalised.

By the end

Students analyse different experiences of, perspectives on and debates about Australia's national identity and citizenship, including the perspectives of First Nations Australians as owners of their respective nations and of different migrant groups.

Waypoints

  • 1. Meaning of national identity and citizenshipHingeStudents can explain what is meant by 'national identity', what it means to be an Australian citizen, and why national identity matters in a globalised world.
  • 2. Different representations of Australian identityStudents can analyse different representations of Australian identity — historical and current — examining how they reflect particular perspectives and whose experiences they include or exclude.
  • 3. Factors shaping national identity debatesStudents can investigate the factors shaping debates about Australian identity, including place, immigration and standard of living.
  • 4. Migrant experiences and citizenship choicesStudents can explain why some migrants choose to become Australian citizens while others do not, and what this reveals about the meaning of citizenship.
  • 5. National symbols and identity: citizenship test and anthemStudents can analyse how national symbols such as the citizenship test and the national anthem contribute to and reflect debates about Australian national identity.
