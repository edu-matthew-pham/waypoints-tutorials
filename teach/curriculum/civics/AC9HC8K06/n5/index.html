National symbols and identity: citizenship test and anthem

AC9HC8K06 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how national symbols such as the citizenship test and the national anthem contribute to and reflect debates about Australian national identity.

Success criteria

  • Can explain how either the citizenship test or the national anthem reflects a particular view of Australian identity
  • Can identify at least one perspective (e.g. First Nations, migrant) that raises questions about whether those symbols adequately represent all Australians

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Migrant experiences and citizenship choices.

Width

Minimum
Explain how either the citizenship test or the national anthem reflects a particular vision of Australian national identity.
Typical
Examine the citizenship test — the types of questions asked and what vision of Australian identity they embed — and discuss how the national anthem contributes to national identity, examining whose stories and perspectives it does and does not include. Connect both to broader debates about Australian identity.
Wider
  • Analyse debates about changing the Australian national anthem — examining different perspectives on whether the anthem adequately reflects contemporary Australia, including First Nations perspectives
  • Investigate how citizenship tests in different countries vary and what values different nations embed in their citizenship requirements
  • Examine how First Nations Australians relate to national symbols such as the Australian flag and anthem — and what debates about changing these symbols reveal about unresolved questions of national identity
