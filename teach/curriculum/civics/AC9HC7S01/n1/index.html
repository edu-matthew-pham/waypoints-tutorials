Characteristics of effective civic inquiry questions

AC9HC7S01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the characteristics of effective civic inquiry questions — those that are focused, investigable, and connected to genuine issues in political or legal systems.

Success criteria

  • Identify at least two characteristics of an effective civic inquiry question
  • Distinguish between a too-broad question and a focused, investigable question and explain the difference

Prior knowledge

Students can develop questions to investigate people, events, developments, places and systems (AC9HS6S01). At Y7 the shift is from general HASS inquiry questions to discipline-specific civics questions grounded in understanding of political and legal systems.

Width

Minimum
Distinguish between a too-broad question (e.g. 'What is democracy?') and a focused inquiry question (e.g. 'How does Australia's federal system divide powers?') and explain what makes the second more useful for investigation.
Typical
Identify the characteristics of effective civic inquiry questions: focused scope, investigable with available sources, connected to real issues in political or legal systems; practise identifying which questions are well-scoped vs too broad or too narrow.
Wider
  • Evaluate a set of example inquiry questions against quality criteria, ranking and justifying which would lead to the most productive investigations
  • Compare civic inquiry questions with scientific or historical inquiry questions to understand what disciplinary thinking looks like in each context
  • Develop criteria for assessing the quality of an inquiry question and apply them to evaluate questions generated by others

Hinge

If students cannot distinguish productive from unproductive inquiry questions, the investigation phase (S02, S03) will be misdirected — either scope creep or investigation of trivially answerable questions. This is the phase-entry gate for civic inquiry work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUnderstanding what makes an inquiry question 'good' is a meta-cognitive step — students need to reason about the question itself before they can use it as an investigation tool. Invest in the criteria explicitly.
