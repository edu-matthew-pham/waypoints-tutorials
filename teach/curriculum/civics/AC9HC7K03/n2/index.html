← AC9HC7K03 The Australian legal system

Rule of law and principles of justice

AC9HC7K03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the rule of law and the principles of justice, fairness, equality, presumption of innocence, burden of proof, and the right to a fair trial.

Success criteria

  • Explain the rule of law and what it means for how law is applied in Australia
  • Identify at least two principles of a fair legal system (e.g. presumption of innocence, burden of proof) and explain why each matters

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Origins of Australia's legal system.

Width

Minimum
Explain the rule of law (that everyone is subject to the same law, applied equally) and identify at least two principles of a fair legal system (e.g. presumption of innocence, right to a fair trial).
Typical
Explain the rule of law and its relationship to democratic principles; describe the principles of justice, fairness, equality, presumption of innocence, burden of proof, and the right to a fair trial and legal representation; make connections between these principles and democratic values.
Wider
  • Investigate examples where the rule of law has been challenged in Australia, such as cases involving the legality of government actions, and examine how the courts resolved them
  • Examine how the principle of equal access to justice operates in practice through legal aid, community legal centres, and self-represented litigants
  • Explore how Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have experienced the Australian legal system, including historical inequalities in the application of law

Hinge

The rule of law and fair trial principles are the conceptual core of the legal system. Without understanding these, students cannot assess whether a legal system is working as intended or evaluate proposed reforms. The concepts in K04 and K05 (social cohesion, democratic values) rely on students grasping why impartial law matters.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe rule of law needs careful unpacking — students need to understand why equal application of law is a principled achievement, not a given, and what happens when it fails.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations Australians' historical experience of unequal application of law provides concrete context for understanding why rule of law principles matter and what is at stake when they are not upheld.
