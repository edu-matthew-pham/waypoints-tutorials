The Australian legal system

AC9HC7K03 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the key principles and features of the Australian legal system, including the Australian Constitution, the rule of law and the court system

Prior knowledge

Students understand that Australia's Constitution established the framework of government (AC9HC7K01). They know about democratic values including fairness and equality from primary HASS (AC9HS6K06). This is the first systematic treatment of Australia's legal system as a distinct institution.

By the end

Students explain the key principles and features of the Australian legal system, including the Constitution, the rule of law, principles of justice and the court hierarchy.

Waypoints

  • 1. Origins of Australia's legal systemHingeStudents can explain the origins of Australia's legal system, including British common law, the Magna Carta, and the establishment of an independent judiciary.
  • 2. Rule of law and principles of justiceHingeStudents can explain the rule of law and the principles of justice, fairness, equality, presumption of innocence, burden of proof, and the right to a fair trial.
  • 3. Court hierarchy and fair trialStudents can describe Australia's court hierarchy (federal and state/territory) and explain the elements of a fair trial, including the roles of witnesses, jurors, legal representation, and due process.
  • 4. Legal system and democratic principlesStudents can explain the connections between a fair and just legal system and democratic principles, and understand how access to justice is supported in Australia.
