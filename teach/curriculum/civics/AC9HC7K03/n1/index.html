Origins of Australia's legal system

AC9HC7K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the origins of Australia's legal system, including British common law, the Magna Carta, and the establishment of an independent judiciary.

Success criteria

  • Identify that Australia's legal system is based on British common law
  • Explain the key principle that no person is above the law and trace its origins to the Magna Carta

Prior knowledge

Students understand that Australia's Constitution established the framework of government (AC9HC7K01). They know about democratic values including fairness and equality from primary HASS (AC9HS6K06). This is the first systematic treatment of Australia's legal system as a distinct institution.

Width

Minimum
Identify that Australia's legal system is based on British common law and explain the key principle inherited from the Magna Carta that no person is above the law.
Typical
Explain the origins of Australia's legal system in British common law and the Magna Carta; describe the role of an independent judiciary in applying the law impartially; identify the federal and state/territory court hierarchies.
Wider
  • Investigate the historical significance of the Magna Carta (1215) and trace how its principles of limiting government power and protecting individual rights developed into modern Australian law
  • Explore how Australian common law has developed differently from UK law since 1901, including landmark cases that changed Australian law
  • Examine the concept of legal precedent (stare decisis) and how decisions made in higher courts bind lower courts

Hinge

The concept of common law and its basis in precedent is foundational for understanding how Australian law develops and why court hierarchy matters. Students who don't grasp the origin story will treat the court system as arbitrary bureaucracy rather than a principled institution.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe idea that law can be 'made' through judicial decisions (common law) rather than only through legislation is genuinely novel — invest in the reasoning scaffold that shows how precedent accumulates into binding legal principles.
