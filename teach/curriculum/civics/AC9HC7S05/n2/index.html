← AC9HC7S05 Creating civic descriptions, explanations and arguments

Developing evidence-referenced explanations

AC9HC7S05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop explanations that use civics knowledge and research to present an authoritative position on an issue, referencing specific evidence.

Success criteria

  • Write an explanation that references at least one piece of specific evidence
  • Connect the evidence to the explanation — showing why the evidence supports the claim rather than just listing it

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Using civics concepts and terms accurately.

Width

Minimum
Write an explanation of a civic issue or system that references at least one piece of specific evidence (e.g. a law, a statistic, an example) to support the explanation.
Typical
Develop explanations using civic and citizenship knowledge or research that present an authoritative position; reference specific evidence (laws, data, examples, cases); connect evidence to explanation rather than listing facts alongside claims.
Wider
  • Develop an explanation that integrates evidence from multiple source types (e.g. legislation + statistical data + expert report) and explains how the evidence sources support each other
  • Evaluate an explanation written by someone else to identify where evidence is well-integrated and where claims are unsubstantiated
  • Revise an explanation after receiving feedback on evidence use — practising the iterative writing process

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsConnecting evidence to claims (not just including evidence alongside claims) is a writing skill that requires practice and iterative feedback — students need to write, receive feedback, and revise to develop this skill.
