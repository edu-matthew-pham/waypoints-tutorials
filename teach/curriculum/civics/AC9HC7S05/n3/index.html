Constructing civic arguments

AC9HC7S05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct a civic argument — a structured claim with supporting evidence and reasoning — appropriate to the purpose and audience, using relevant concepts and terms.

Lessons on this site

  • Make an argument

Success criteria

  • Construct a structured civic argument with a clear claim, at least two supporting points, and referenced evidence
  • Use at least two civics concepts or terms accurately and present ideas appropriate to the intended audience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Developing evidence-referenced explanations.

Width

Minimum
Write a short argument (claim + at least two supporting points + evidence) on a civic topic, using at least two relevant civics terms and presenting ideas appropriate to the audience.
Typical
Develop civic arguments using appropriate terms and concepts; present ideas appropriate to purpose and audience (e.g. digital material promoting democratic participation, a written case for constitutional change); structure arguments with a clear position, supporting points, evidence, and coherent reasoning.
Wider
  • Develop an argument for a simulated civic context (e.g. a submission to a parliamentary committee, a speech for a school community forum) and adapt tone and structure to that specific audience and purpose
  • Compare two arguments taking opposing positions on the same civic issue, evaluating which is better supported and identifying the weaknesses in each
  • Develop a multi-modal civic communication (e.g. digital presentation promoting participation in democracy) that integrates text, evidence, and visual elements

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierArgument construction integrates accurate term use (N1), evidence referencing (N2), and knowledge of civic systems — the argument is the performance that draws on all previous capabilities and knowledge nodes.
