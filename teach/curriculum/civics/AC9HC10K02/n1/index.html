Forms of regional and global participation

AC9HC10K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe the main ways Australia contributes to and participates in the Asia-Pacific region and globally, including membership of international organisations and agreements.

Success criteria

  • Names at least three distinct forms of Australia's regional or global participation
  • Provides a specific example for at least two of those forms
  • Distinguishes between regional (Asia-Pacific) and global participation

Prior knowledge

Students know how individuals and groups participate in civic life in Australia and contribute to global citizenship (AC9HC9K05). They understand legislative processes shaping government policy (AC9HC9K02).

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three specific forms of Australia's regional or global participation (e.g. peacekeeping, development aid, UN involvement) with brief descriptions of each.
Typical
Explain the full range of ways Australia participates in the Asia-Pacific region and globally — exchange programs, peacekeeping, election monitoring, development aid, health programs, disaster management, and international treaty participation including the UN Security Council — using accurate examples.
Wider
  • Investigate Australia's participation in a specific multilateral body (e.g. UN Security Council, G20) and evaluate what membership requires and delivers
  • Research how Australia's engagement with the Asia-Pacific has changed over time and what this reveals about shifting foreign policy priorities
  • Examine how Australia's international development commitments compare with those of comparable nations (e.g. aid spending as percentage of GNI)

Hinge

Students who have only vague knowledge of participation forms cannot complete the evaluative analysis in subsequent nodes — they lack the factual base to assess effectiveness or compare responses.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuild a concrete repertoire of participation types and examples before moving to evaluation. Students should be able to name specific programs or agreements, not just categories.
