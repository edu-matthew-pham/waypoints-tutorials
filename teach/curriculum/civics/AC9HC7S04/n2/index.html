Responsibilities of active citizenship

AC9HC7S04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the core responsibilities of active citizenship and evaluate how citizens enacting democratic values contribute to a cohesive society.

Success criteria

  • Identify at least two responsibilities of active citizenship and explain how they support democratic life
  • Give an example of active citizenship contributing to social cohesion and explain the connection

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Features of civic participation methods.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two responsibilities associated with active citizenship (e.g. staying informed, contributing to community, participating in democratic processes) and explain how they contribute to democratic life.
Typical
Identify the core responsibilities of active citizenship; evaluate how the contributions of citizens who enact democratic values (e.g. through disability advocacy services, Landcare groups, community organisations) demonstrate active citizenship and contribute to social cohesion; connect individual action to broader democratic outcomes.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific community organisation or advocacy group (e.g. Landcare, disability advocacy services) and evaluate the contribution it makes to both local community and democratic participation
  • Examine what is meant by 'civic responsibility' in different cultural contexts — are the responsibilities of citizenship understood differently across communities?
  • Explore First Nations Australian conceptions of responsibility to community and Country as a form of active citizenship that predates colonial democratic institutions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExamining diverse examples of active citizenship (disability advocacy, environmental action, First Nations community organisations) helps students understand that active citizenship takes many forms and is not limited to formal political participation.
