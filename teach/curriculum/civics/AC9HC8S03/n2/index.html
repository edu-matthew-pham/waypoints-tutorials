Explaining reasons for perspective differences

AC9HC8S03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain possible reasons for differences in perspective about a civics and citizenship issue — connecting perspective differences to underlying values, experiences, roles or positions.

Success criteria

  • Can explain at least two reasons why perspectives on a specific civic issue differ
  • Can connect each reason to specific features of the issue or the perspectives involved

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying differences in perspective.

Width

Minimum
Explain at least two possible reasons why perspectives on a civic issue differ, grounded in the specific issue being examined.
Typical
Identify and explain possible reasons for differences in perspectives on a civic issue — such as marriage equality — drawing on factors such as religious beliefs, cultural background, lived experience, political ideology, professional role (e.g. politician, jurist, advocate) or personal interest.
Wider
  • Investigate how power relationships affect whose perspectives are heard in civic debate — examining why some perspectives are more prominent in official discourse than others
  • Examine how the same individual can hold different perspectives in different roles (e.g. as a politician vs as a parent vs as a member of a faith community) and what this reveals about the complexity of civic perspective
  • Analyse the Australian of the Year and Young Australian of the Year criteria and the different ways recipients have met them — examining how different expressions of 'good citizenship' reflect different values and priorities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepReasons for perspective differences vary significantly by issue type. Widening across different civic contexts — legal issues, identity issues, political issues — builds transferable analytical skill.
