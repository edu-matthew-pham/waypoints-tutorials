Collective identities in multicultural Australia

AC9HC8K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore the collective identities of several different groups in Australia's multicultural society, and examine how those identities are represented and perceived.

Success criteria

  • Can describe the collective identity of at least one group in Australia's multicultural society
  • Can explain how that identity is represented in public life and how it may be perceived differently by different audiences

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Multiple community memberships and citizenship expression.

Width

Minimum
Describe the collective identity of one group in Australia's multicultural society and explain how it is represented in public life.
Typical
Explore the collective identities of several different groups in Australia's multicultural society — examining how these identities are formed, how they are represented (e.g. in media, cultural events, advocacy) and how they are perceived by others within Australian society.
Wider
  • Examine how First Nations Australian collective identities — as owners of their respective nations — are represented and sometimes misrepresented in mainstream Australian media and public discourse
  • Investigate how a specific migrant community has maintained and adapted its collective identity in Australia — examining what has been preserved, what has changed and what tensions arise
  • Examine how the representation of collective identities in Australia's national symbols (flag, anthem, public holidays) reflects some groups and not others, and what debates this produces

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepGenuine understanding of how identity and citizenship intersect requires encounter with diverse groups. Widening across First Nations Australian identities, migrant community identities and other cultural groups prevents students from working with a single representative case.
