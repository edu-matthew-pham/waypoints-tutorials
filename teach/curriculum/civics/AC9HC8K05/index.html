Culture, religion and citizenship

AC9HC8K05 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how culture and religion may influence individuals' and groups' perceptions and expressions of citizenship and their actions as citizens

Prior knowledge

Students understand how Australia's secular democracy draws on diverse cultural origins including Christian and Western heritage, distinct First Nations Australian histories and cultures, and migrant communities (AC9HC7K04). They understand how values of freedom, respect, fairness and equality support social cohesion (AC9HC7K05). The gap this descriptor fills is the mechanism by which culture and religion shape how individuals and groups enact citizenship — moving from understanding Australia's diverse society to understanding how that diversity plays out in lived citizenship.

By the end

Students explain how culture and religion may influence individuals' and groups' perceptions and expressions of citizenship and their actions as citizens, including through multiple community memberships, collective identities and the distinctions between types of citizenship.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring how people express different aspects of their personal identity through membership of multiple communities, such as cultural groups, faith groups, professional associations, trade unions, and sporting and social clubs, and how this is reflected in expressions of citizenship; for example, fund-raising for community services or supporting people experiencing health concerns
  • exploring the collective identities of several different groups in Australia's multicultural society, and how these identities might be represented and perceived
  • discussing the differences between legal citizenship, active citizenship and global citizenship

Waypoints

  • 1. Multiple community memberships and citizenship expressionHingeStudents can explain how membership of multiple communities (cultural, faith, professional, sporting, social) shapes how individuals express and enact citizenship.
  • 2. Collective identities in multicultural AustraliaStudents can explore the collective identities of several different groups in Australia's multicultural society, and examine how those identities are represented and perceived.
  • 3. Types of citizenship: legal, active and globalStudents can explain the distinctions between legal citizenship, active citizenship and global citizenship.
