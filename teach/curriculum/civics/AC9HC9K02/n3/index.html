Influences on policy formulation

AC9HC9K02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and analyse multiple influences on how federal policy is formulated and implemented.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two types of influence on policy formulation
  • Uses a specific federal policy to illustrate how at least one influence shaped the policy's development or implementation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: From idea to enacted legislation.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two different types of influences on federal policy formulation (e.g. political parties, interest groups, citizens) using a specific federal policy as an example.
Typical
Use a specific federal policy to understand and analyse the range of influences on policy formulation and implementation, including: political parties, interest groups, citizens and public opinion, international influences, and the public service; explain how these influences interact and sometimes conflict.
Wider
  • Investigate a policy area where international obligations (e.g. UN conventions, trade agreements) have shaped domestic federal legislation and evaluate the extent of that influence
  • Analyse the role of media campaigns or public advocacy in shifting federal policy on a contemporary issue
  • Compare the relative influence of different stakeholders (industry lobby vs community groups vs public service) in a specific policy case and assess whose interests were most reflected in the final legislation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAnalysing influences on policy formulation becomes meaningful only when applied to a specific, concrete policy case. Using two different policy examples (e.g. one with strong industry influence, one with significant community pressure) helps students see that the influence mix varies by case.
