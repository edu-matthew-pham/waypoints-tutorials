Sustaining democracy and cohesion

AC9HC10K05 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the challenges to and ways of sustaining a resilient democracy and a cohesive society in Australia and/or in our region or globally

Prior knowledge

Students know how individuals and groups participate in and contribute to civic life in Australia and to global citizenship (AC9HC9K05). They understand the influence of media, including social media, in shaping identity and attitudes to diversity (AC9HC9K06).

By the end

Students explain the challenges to and ways of sustaining a resilient democracy and a cohesive society, examining threats and responses in the Australian and/or global context.

Waypoints

  • 1. Characteristics of a resilient democracy and cohesive societyHingeStudents can identify the key characteristics of cohesive, democratic societies and explain why an active and informed citizenry is important for sustaining them.
  • 2. Threats to democratic resilienceStudents can identify and discuss a range of threats to Australian democracy and democratic societies more broadly, including vested interests, corruption, and the impact of social media on civic debate.
  • 3. Ways of sustaining democracy and social cohesionStudents can discuss ways of sustaining democratic resilience and social cohesion, including through public debate, civic participation, and engagement with controversial issues.
  • 4. Evaluating Australia's democratic resilienceStudents can evaluate Australia's democratic resilience, considering both threats and responses, and connect this to the responsibilities of active citizenship.
