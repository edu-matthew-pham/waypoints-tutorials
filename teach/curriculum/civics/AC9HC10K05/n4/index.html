Evaluating Australia's democratic resilience

AC9HC10K05 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate Australia's democratic resilience, considering both threats and responses, and connect this to the responsibilities of active citizenship.

Success criteria

  • Makes a supported evaluative claim about Australia's democratic resilience
  • Identifies at least one significant threat and at least one effective response
  • Connects the evaluation to the responsibilities of active citizenship

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Ways of sustaining democracy and social cohesion.

Width

Minimum
Offer a supported judgement about Australia's democratic resilience, identifying both a significant threat and an effective response.
Typical
Evaluate Australia's democratic resilience by weighing significant threats against existing responses and mechanisms, and connect this evaluation to what active, informed citizenship requires in the current civic environment.
Wider
  • Evaluate the adequacy of Australia's democratic institutions in addressing reconciliation, truth-telling and First Nations sovereignty, and assess what a more resilient, cohesive democracy would require
  • Compare Australia's democratic resilience with that of another democracy in the region, drawing evidence-based conclusions
  • Argue a position on a specific controversial issue (e.g. call for a treaty, Uluru Statement) as an exercise in democratic participation, using evidence and acknowledging opposing views

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierIntegration node — characteristics of democracy, threat analysis, and responses come together in an evaluative argument about Australia's current situation. Teaching investment is in supporting students to move from description to supported evaluation.
