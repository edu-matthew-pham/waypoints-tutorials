Framing an investigable civic question

AC9HC10S01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop an overarching inquiry question about Australia's political or legal system or a contemporary civic issue that is specific, investigable, and productively open.

Success criteria

  • Develops an overarching inquiry question about a political, legal or civic issue
  • The question is neither too broad nor a yes/no question — it is genuinely investigable
  • Can explain what the question is asking and why it matters civically

Prior knowledge

Students have developed and modified questions to investigate Australia's political and legal systems and contemporary civic issues at Year 9 (AC9HC9S01). They can formulate sub-questions and revisit questions based on new information.

Width

Minimum
Develop one overarching inquiry question about a political, legal or civic issue that is investigable — not too broad, not yes/no.
Typical
Develop an overarching inquiry question about Australia's political or legal system or a contemporary civic issue that is specific, investigable, and productively open — able to sustain sub-questions and structured inquiry.
Wider
  • Practise framing questions across multiple civic domains (political systems, legal rights, civic participation) and evaluate which framing opens the richest inquiry
  • Examine examples of civic inquiry questions that led to significant public policy change and analyse what made them productive
  • Develop and compare overarching questions from the perspectives of different civic actors (e.g. a citizen, an NGO, the government) on the same issue

Hinge

A poorly framed overarching question makes the entire inquiry phase structurally unsound — sub-questions cannot compensate for a question that is too broad, too narrow, or not genuinely investigable. Phase collapse begins here.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkTeach the criteria for a productive inquiry question explicitly — scope, investigability, openness — before students attempt to frame one. Model the difference between strong and weak framing.
