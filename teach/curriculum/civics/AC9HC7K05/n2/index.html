Values and social cohesion

AC9HC7K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how shared values can promote social cohesion and reconciliation in Australia's diverse society, and evaluate the extent to which these values are reflected in Australian society.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explain what social cohesion means and identify how shared values support it in a diverse society
  • Evaluate at least one area where Australian values are strongly reflected and one area where there is a gap between aspiration and reality

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Australian values and their expression.

Width

Minimum
Explain what 'social cohesion' means and identify at least one way shared values (e.g. fairness, inclusion) support people from diverse backgrounds living together in Australian society.
Typical
Explain how shared values support social cohesion in a diverse society; evaluate the extent to which values like freedom, respect, fairness and equality are genuinely reflected in Australian society, including examining where gaps between aspiration and reality exist; consider how these values can promote reconciliation.
Wider
  • Evaluate the extent to which Australian values are reflected across different groups — examining evidence of inequality, discrimination or exclusion that challenges claims about shared values
  • Investigate how reconciliation between First Nations and non-Indigenous Australians is understood as a values-driven process, and examine specific initiatives (e.g. Sorry Day, Reconciliation Action Plans)
  • Explore how migrants and refugees experience Australian values when arriving in Australia — what aspects of Australian civic life help or hinder their participation

Hinge

The evaluation of whether values are actually reflected in society — not just stated — is the conceptual core of this descriptor. Students who only identify values without evaluating their application cannot meet the descriptor's command term ('can support') which implies a conditional, evaluative claim.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating the gap between stated values and lived reality requires careful reasoning — students need a scaffold for assessing evidence of both alignment and failure, and for making conditional claims about what values 'can' (but do not always) achieve.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepReconciliation as a values-driven process offers rich context for exploring how social cohesion is actively built rather than passively assumed — examine specific initiatives alongside the ongoing gaps.
