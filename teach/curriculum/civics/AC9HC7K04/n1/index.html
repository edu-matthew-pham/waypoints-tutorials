← AC9HC7K04 Secular democracy and pluralist society

Key concepts: secular, pluralist, multi-faith

AC9HC7K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can define and explain the terms 'secular', 'pluralist', 'multi-faith', and 'diverse society', and discuss their relevance to contemporary Australia.

Success criteria

  • Define 'secular', 'pluralist', and 'multi-faith' and explain each term's relevance to Australia
  • Explain what it means for Australia to have a secular government within a multi-faith society

Prior knowledge

Students understand Australia's system of government and democratic freedoms (AC9HC7K01, AC9HC7K02). From primary HASS they know about migration and Australian society (AC9HS6K03) and diversity in communities. This descriptor extends that to the concept of secular democracy and the religious and cultural dimensions of Australian identity.

Width

Minimum
Define 'secular' (government separate from religion), 'multi-faith' (many religious traditions present), and 'pluralist' (diversity of beliefs and cultures respected), and give one example of each concept in Australian life.
Typical
Define and discuss the terms 'secular', 'multi-faith', 'diverse society', 'culturally diverse', 'Christian heritage', 'Western heritage', and 'pluralist'; explain what it means for Australia to be simultaneously secular (in government) and multi-faith (in society).
Wider
  • Investigate the distinction between a secular state (government does not favour any religion) and a secular society (religion plays little role in public life) — is Australia both, or just the former?
  • Compare Australia's secular constitutional arrangements with countries that have state religions, examining what difference this makes to religious freedom
  • Explore ABS census data on religious observance in Australia over recent decades to identify trends and discuss what they suggest about Australian society

Hinge

Without clear understanding of what 'secular' means in Australian constitutional terms, students conflate secular government with irreligion or with hostility to religion — this leads to misreading subsequent nodes about Christian heritage and religious diversity in a pluralist society.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe distinction between a secular state and a secular society is genuinely subtle — invest in unpacking why a secular government can coexist with high religious diversity and why this is seen as a democratic achievement.
