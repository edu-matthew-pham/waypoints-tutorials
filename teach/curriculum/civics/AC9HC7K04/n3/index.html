First Nations Australian histories and cultures

AC9HC7K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the diversity of First Nations Australian communities and their distinct histories and cultures, including the distinctiveness of connections to Country/Place.

Success criteria

  • Describe the diversity of First Nations Australian communities and explain why they cannot be treated as a single homogeneous group
  • Explain the significance of connection to Country/Place in First Nations Australian cultures

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Christian and Western heritage.

Width

Minimum
Describe the diversity of First Nations Australian communities and explain the significance of connection to Country/Place as a distinctive feature of First Nations cultures.
Typical
Explain the diversity of First Nations Australian communities, including the distinctiveness of their connections to Country/Place; explore the diversity of spiritualities among First Nations communities, from traditional spirituality to adoption of other religions; describe First Nations Australians as one of the foundational cultural origins of Australia's pluralist society.
Wider
  • Investigate the diversity of First Nations Australian spiritualities and how these have interacted with introduced religions since colonisation
  • Examine how connection to Country/Place functions in First Nations cultures as a framework for relationships, responsibilities and law, and compare this with Western legal concepts of land ownership
  • Explore specific First Nations communities' histories and cultures to understand the diversity within the category 'First Nations Australians' — avoiding homogenisation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAvoiding homogenisation of First Nations cultures requires deliberate breadth — students need encounters with the diversity of communities, languages, spiritualities and connections to Country to resist treating 'First Nations Australians' as a monolithic category.
