Migrant communities and contemporary diversity

AC9HC7K04 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 7 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how migrant communities contribute to Australia's pluralist, multi-faith society, and use data to identify trends in religious observance and cultural heritage.

Success criteria

  • Identify at least two religious or cultural traditions beyond Christianity that are part of contemporary Australian society
  • Explain how migrant communities contribute to Australia's pluralist, multi-faith identity

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: First Nations Australian histories and cultures.

Width

Minimum
Identify that migrant communities are a significant source of cultural and religious diversity in Australia, and identify at least two religious traditions practised in contemporary Australia beyond Christianity.
Typical
Explain how migrant communities have contributed to Australia's cultural and religious diversity; identify trends regarding religious observance and/or cultural heritage using ABS or similar data; describe religious traditions practised in contemporary Australia including Judaism, Buddhism, Islam and Hinduism.
Wider
  • Analyse ABS census data on religious diversity across different postcode areas, age groups or decades to identify patterns and changes in Australia's religious landscape
  • Investigate the experiences of a specific migrant community in Australia — examining how they maintain cultural and religious identity while participating in Australian civic life
  • Explore how Australia's approach to multiculturalism has evolved as a policy framework for managing cultural and religious diversity

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node brings together the Christian/Western heritage, First Nations cultures, and migrant communities as the three foundational streams of Australia's pluralist society — it is the synthesis point where students see how Australia's multi-faith, diverse character emerged from multiple historical layers.
