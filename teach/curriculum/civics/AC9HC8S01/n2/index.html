← AC9HC8S01 Develop civic inquiry questions

Key question and related sub-questions

AC9HC8S01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop a key inquiry question and a set of related sub-questions to investigate a civic issue, demonstrating that the sub-questions scaffold investigation toward the key question.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can develop a key inquiry question about a civic issue
  • Can write at least two sub-questions that clearly scaffold toward the key question rather than restating it or going off on tangents

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Closed and open-ended civic questions.

Width

Minimum
Develop a key inquiry question about a civic issue and at least two related sub-questions that help investigate it.
Typical
Develop a key question and related sub-questions to investigate a civic issue — for example, 'How do young people effectively participate in democratic processes to influence issues that impact on them?' or 'How did public opinion help in persuading the government on the importance of providing a National Disability Insurance Scheme?' — ensuring the sub-questions build cumulatively toward the key question.
Wider
  • Develop a question framework for investigating a current civic issue, evaluating whether the sub-questions together cover the factual, perspective and evaluative dimensions needed to fully address the key question
  • Critique a provided set of inquiry questions for a civic issue — identifying gaps, overlaps or sub-questions that don't scaffold toward the key question
  • Revise an initial question framework after a first round of research, adding sub-questions that address gaps uncovered during investigation

Hinge

The ability to structure a key question supported by related sub-questions is the organisational framework for all subsequent inquiry work in S02–S05. If students cannot generate a coherent question structure, their research, analysis and argument tasks lack direction and focus.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe relationship between a key question and sub-questions is a scaffolding relationship — sub-questions should cumulatively build the investigation needed to address the key question. This requires explicit reasoning about inquiry design, not just question writing.
