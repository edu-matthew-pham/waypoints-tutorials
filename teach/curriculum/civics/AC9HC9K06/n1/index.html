How media constructs representations

AC9HC9K06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how media construct representations of groups and why these representations matter for community attitudes.

Success criteria

  • Explains that media representations involve active choices about framing and selection
  • Identifies a specific example of media representation of a group and explains how it shapes community attitudes

Prior knowledge

Students have examined different experiences of and debates about Australia's national identity and citizenship including perspectives of First Nations Australians and migrant groups (AC9HC8K06). They understand identity as shaped by culture and community but have not yet examined how media actively constructs and circulates representations that shape identity and attitudes.

Width

Minimum
Explain that media do not simply report reality but make choices about how to represent groups, and identify at least one example of a media representation of a specific group.
Typical
Analyse how media represent different groups in Australian society — including First Nations Australians, immigrant groups, male and female sporting figures, and people with disability; explain how these representations are constructed through selection, framing and emphasis; identify the impact those representations can have on community attitudes.
Wider
  • Compare how the same group (e.g. a refugee community, First Nations Australians) is represented across different media outlets and evaluate what drives the differences
  • Trace how representations of a specific group have changed over time and assess what has driven that change
  • Examine representations in advertising or entertainment media and assess how they shape or challenge stereotypes about gender, ethnicity or disability

Hinge

If students treat media representations as neutral descriptions rather than constructed artefacts, they cannot undertake the social media analysis in Node 2, which depends on understanding media choices as active shaping forces rather than mirrors of community attitudes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need explicit scaffolding on the distinction between representation and reality — that the same event or community can be represented in multiple ways, and that choices about framing carry consequences. A comparative analysis exercise (same event, two different outlets) makes this concrete.
