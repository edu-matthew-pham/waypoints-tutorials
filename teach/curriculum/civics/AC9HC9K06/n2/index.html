← AC9HC9K06 Media influence on identity and diversity

Social media, identity and human rights campaigns

AC9HC9K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how social media influences identity formation and explain how human rights campaigns use social media to engage the public.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one way social media influences identity or attitudes to diversity
  • Describes a human rights campaign that used social media and explains how it sought to engage public participation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How media constructs representations.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one way social media influences identity and one way it has been used in a human rights campaign.
Typical
Investigate how social media shapes identity (including the construction of personal and group identity online) and attitudes to diversity; investigate a human rights campaign that uses social media and how members of the public have engaged with the issue; assess both the reach and the limitations of social media as a tool for civic engagement.
Wider
  • Analyse a specific social media campaign on a human rights or diversity issue (e.g. #BlackLivesMatter, #MeToo, LGBTIQ+ campaigns) and evaluate its impact on public attitudes and policy
  • Investigate how algorithms shape what content people see and how this can create filter bubbles that reinforce existing identity and attitudes
  • Examine how social media platforms are used by both those advocating for diversity and those spreading divisive or hateful content, and evaluate what responsibilities platforms have

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepSocial media's role in identity is experienced differently by students in different communities. Using both a national-scale campaign and a local or youth-focused example makes the analysis personally anchored and more critical.
