Developing and evaluating a civic participation plan

AC9HC9S04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 9 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop a plan to address a contemporary civic issue that incorporates a consultation process and evaluation criteria.

Success criteria

  • Develops a plan that includes at least one action, one consultation mechanism, and one evaluation criterion
  • Explains how the consultation process ensures a range of views will be represented

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Examining civic action plans.

Width

Minimum
Develop a basic plan to address a civic issue that includes at least one action, one measure of success, and one way to consult stakeholders.
Typical
Develop a plan of action to address a contemporary civic issue that incorporates a consultation process to ensure a range of views are heard, and participants are provided with opportunities to contribute; include evaluation criteria for assessing effectiveness; consider examples such as a local safety campaign, a community sustainability initiative, or an educational resources strategy.
Wider
  • Develop and peer-review plans for addressing the same civic issue using different methods (e.g. media campaign vs community consultation vs policy advocacy) and evaluate which approach best fits the context
  • Evaluate government media campaigns and community activities used over time to address a recurring civic issue (e.g. littering, road safety) and determine which have been most effective and why
  • Incorporate feedback loops into a civic plan — specifying how the plan would be revised if early evaluation data indicated it was not working

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDeveloping a plan integrates the analytical skills from Node 1 (examining existing plans) with the capability skills of civic planning. Students are executing a genuine mode B task — the thinking is only legible through the plan they produce.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPlans developed for contrasting civic contexts (a local personal safety issue, an international development or aid context) build transferability — students learn that civic planning methods must be adapted to context, not just applied formulaically.
