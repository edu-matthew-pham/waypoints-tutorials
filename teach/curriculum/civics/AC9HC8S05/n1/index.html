Civic concepts and terminology

AC9HC8S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use appropriate civics and citizenship concepts and terms accurately in their descriptions and explanations — including terms such as 'freedoms', 'responsibilities', 'common law', 'statutory law' and 'customary law'.

Success criteria

  • Can use at least three Year 8 civics terms accurately in a description or explanation
  • Can distinguish between two related terms (e.g. statutory law vs common law, or civil law vs criminal law) in writing

Prior knowledge

Students have created civic descriptions, explanations and arguments at Year 7 level (AC9HC7S05). The gap this descriptor fills is creating civic communication that references evidence — moving from basic descriptions and explanations to arguments that are grounded in evidence and use subject-specific terminology accurately. The command term 'create' encompasses a range of output types — not just written arguments but also descriptions and explanations across different civic contexts.

Width

Minimum
Use at least three subject-specific civics terms accurately in a short description or explanation of a civic concept.
Typical
Demonstrate accurate use of appropriate civics and citizenship terms and concepts — including 'freedoms', 'responsibilities', 'common law', 'statutory law', 'customary law' and other Year 8 civics vocabulary — in descriptions and explanations of political and legal concepts.
Wider
  • Investigate how the same civic concept (e.g. 'rule of law' or 'democratic participation') is defined and used differently in different contexts — academic, legal, journalistic, political — and analyse what precision of terminology matters in each context
  • Practise writing for different audiences about the same civic concept, adjusting terminology use while maintaining accuracy (e.g. for a Year 8 peer vs for a formal submission vs for a general public audience)
  • Build a personal civic vocabulary reference — tracking how terms are defined, used in context and applied across different civics topics throughout the year

Hinge

Accurate vocabulary is the prerequisite for all subsequent civic argument creation. Arguments that misuse civic terms cannot be evaluated as civic arguments — the evidence cannot be connected to the claim if the claim is not expressed with precision. This foundation underpins all outputs in S05.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate term use requires repeated, varied practice before it becomes fluent. This is not a concept students understand once — it needs consolidation across multiple description and explanation tasks before the vocabulary is truly usable.
