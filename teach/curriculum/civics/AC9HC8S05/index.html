Create civic arguments

AC9HC8S05 · Year 8 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

create descriptions, explanations and arguments using civics and citizenship knowledge, concepts and terms that reference evidence

Prior knowledge

Students have created civic descriptions, explanations and arguments at Year 7 level (AC9HC7S05). The gap this descriptor fills is creating civic communication that references evidence — moving from basic descriptions and explanations to arguments that are grounded in evidence and use subject-specific terminology accurately. The command term 'create' encompasses a range of output types — not just written arguments but also descriptions and explanations across different civic contexts.

By the end

Students create descriptions, explanations and arguments using civics and citizenship knowledge, concepts and terms that reference evidence — including persuasive content on contemporary civic issues and accurate use of subject-specific legal and civic terminology.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • persuading the public on a contemporary issue or about a need for action, using evidence; for example, to argue the case for a constitutional change or an advertisement promoting participation in Australia's democracy
  • using appropriate terms and concepts such as "freedoms", "responsibilities", "common law", "statutory law" and "customary law"

Waypoints

  • 1. Civic concepts and terminologyHingeStudents can use appropriate civics and citizenship concepts and terms accurately in their descriptions and explanations — including terms such as 'freedoms', 'responsibilities', 'common law', 'statutory law' and 'customary law'.
  • 2. Evidence-referenced civic descriptions and explanationsStudents can create civic descriptions and explanations that reference evidence — connecting claims to sources or examples rather than making unsupported assertions.
  • 3. Persuasive civic arguments with evidenceStudents can create civic arguments — including persuasive writing — that use knowledge, concepts and terms accurately and that reference evidence to support the position argued.
