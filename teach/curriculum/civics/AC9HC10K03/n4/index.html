← AC9HC10K03 Parliament, High Court and rights

Comparing and evaluating both institutions' roles

AC9HC10K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare the roles of parliament and the High Court in protecting rights, explaining how they interact and evaluating the effectiveness of Australia's overall approach to rights protection.

Success criteria

  • Compares parliament and the High Court's roles in rights protection with at least one similarity and one difference
  • Explains how the two institutions interact (e.g. parliament can respond to court decisions; courts interpret parliamentary legislation)
  • Offers a supported judgement about the effectiveness of Australia's approach to rights protection

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Role of parliament in legislating rights.

Width

Minimum
Compare the roles of parliament and the High Court in protecting rights, identifying at least one key similarity and one key difference in how each institution contributes to rights protection.
Typical
Compare the roles of parliament and the High Court across constitutional, common law, and statute law contexts, explaining how the two institutions interact and evaluating the strengths and limitations of Australia's approach to protecting rights.
Wider
  • Evaluate whether Australia's patchwork approach to rights protection (no national bill of rights; relying on courts, parliament and common law) adequately protects all citizens, including marginalised groups
  • Analyse the Stolen Generations case and the native title pathway together as a set, drawing conclusions about what each institution can and cannot achieve
  • Compare Australia's approach with the USA or New Zealand and argue which model better protects citizens' rights, with evidence

Response modes
