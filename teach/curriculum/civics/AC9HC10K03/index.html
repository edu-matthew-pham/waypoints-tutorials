Parliament, High Court and rights

AC9HC10K03 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the role of the parliament and the High Court of Australia in protecting rights under the Constitution, common law, and through federal and state statute law

Prior knowledge

Students know the key features and jurisdictions of Australia's court system and the operations of courts and tribunals (AC9HC9K03). They understand the role of courts, judges, lawyers and juries in trials, and the rights of the accused and victims (AC9HC9K04).

By the end

Students explain the roles of both parliament and the High Court in protecting rights through the Constitution, common law, and statute law, comparing different mechanisms and their effectiveness.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • explaining the role of the High Court in interpreting and applying the law, using contemporary Australian examples
  • exploring the concepts of express and implied rights through High Court cases such as the Stolen Generations Case (Kruger v the Commonwealth [1997]) and the Vicki Lee Roach Case (Roach v Electoral Commissioner [2007])
  • investigating understanding of terra nullius and how the Native Title Act 1993 property rights were developed through High Court interpretation of common law and enshrined in statutory law
  • comparing the effectiveness of a constitutional bill of rights, such as in the USA, with a statutory bill of rights, such as in New Zealand

Waypoints

  • 1. Sources of rights protection in AustraliaHingeStudents can identify the three main sources of rights protection in Australia — the Constitution, common law, and statute law — and explain how each operates.
  • 2. Role of the High Court in interpreting and applying lawHingeStudents can explain the role of the High Court in interpreting and applying the law, and identify examples of High Court decisions that have shaped rights protection.
  • 3. Role of parliament in legislating rightsStudents can explain how parliament protects rights through federal and state statute law, and how legislative rights differ from constitutional rights.
  • 4. Comparing and evaluating both institutions' rolesStudents can compare the roles of parliament and the High Court in protecting rights, explaining how they interact and evaluating the effectiveness of Australia's overall approach to rights protection.
