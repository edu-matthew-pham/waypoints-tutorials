← AC9HC10K01 Comparing systems of government

Structured comparison

AC9HC10K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare the key features and values of Australia's system with the selected comparison system, identifying specific similarities and differences across multiple dimensions.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two structural similarities and two structural differences between the two systems
  • Connects at least one difference to an underlying democratic or non-democratic value
  • Uses specific evidence or examples from both systems to support the comparison

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Features of a comparison system.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two similarities and two differences between Australia's system and the comparison system, using a structured framework (e.g. table, structured paragraph).
Typical
Compare Australia's system with the selected system across multiple dimensions — electoral processes, structure of power, rule of law, and rights protections — explaining both structural differences and the values that underpin them.
Wider
  • Compare the experiences of First Nations Australians in engaging with the Australian political system with those of Indigenous peoples in the comparison nation
  • Evaluate which system better protects human rights for minority groups, drawing on specific evidence
  • Investigate how global interconnectedness (international agreements, trade, migration) has influenced political similarities between the two systems

Hinge

The comparison move is the core demand of this descriptor. Students who produce surface-level comparison (listing features without connecting them to underlying values) will not satisfy the descriptor's 'key features and values' framing — the integration node cannot recover this gap.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often list differences without explaining the values or institutional logic behind them. Use structured scaffolds (e.g. comparative frameworks) to push students from feature-matching to values-based analysis.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepWidening to include First Nations political participation or rights-minority analysis gives students comparison across multiple values dimensions, not just structural features.
