Comparing systems of government

AC9HC10K01 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the key features and values of Australia's system of government compared with at least one other democratic or non-democratic system of government in the Asia-Pacific region

Prior knowledge

Students know the role of the Australian Constitution in providing the basis for Australia's federal system and democratic processes, including institutions and the referendum process (AC9HC9K01). They understand the legislative processes through which federal government policy is shaped and implemented (AC9HC9K02).

By the end

Students compare the key features and values of Australia's system of government with at least one other system of government in the Asia-Pacific region, explaining similarities, differences and cultural influences.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing the key institutional features of Australia's system of government (for example, free, fair and frequent elections, the party system, the partial separation of powers, the rule of law, and the protection of human rights and freedoms) with the key features of a system of government of a country in Asia
  • researching and comparing the experiences of First Nations Australians in engaging with the political system with those of the Indigenous peoples in an Asian nation
  • exploring and reflecting on the cultural influences shaping the Australian political system and that of a comparison country

Waypoints

  • 1. Key features of Australia's systemHingeStudents can identify and explain the key institutional features and values of Australia's system of government, including free and fair elections, the party system, separation of powers, the rule of law, and protection of human rights.
  • 2. Features of a comparison systemStudents can describe the key features of at least one other system of government in the Asia-Pacific region, identifying whether it is democratic or non-democratic.
  • 3. Structured comparisonHingeStudents can compare the key features and values of Australia's system with the selected comparison system, identifying specific similarities and differences across multiple dimensions.
  • 4. Cultural influences on political systemsStudents can explain how cultural influences have shaped Australia's political system and that of the comparison country, reflecting on what this reveals about the relationship between culture and governance.
