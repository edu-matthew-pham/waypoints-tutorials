Analyse civic perspectives and interpretations

AC9HC10S03 · Year 10 Civics and Citizenship · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Civics and Citizenship: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse information, data and ideas about political, legal or civic issues to identify and evaluate differences in perspectives and interpretations

Prior knowledge

Students have analysed information to identify and evaluate differences in perspectives and interpretations at Year 9 (AC9HC9S03). They can examine media and information sources for varying views on contemporary issues.

By the end

Students analyse information, data and ideas about political, legal or civic issues to identify and evaluate differences in perspectives and interpretations, including assessing the effectiveness of global governance institutions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining information from traditional and social media sources to identify examples of different views on and responses to a contemporary Australian political, legal or civic issue, such as limiting freedoms, placing restrictions on movement, trade or association, or debates about the use of public resources
  • examining the role of institutions of global governance, such as the United Nations or the International Criminal Court, and evaluating their effectiveness in upholding democratic norms and human rights
  • investigating a contemporary international event or situation, such as armed conflict, development of an international agreement, or natural or humanitarian disaster, to assess the effectiveness and values positions of global actors such as NGOs, and the response of the Australian Government to the event or situation

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying differences in perspectivesHingeStudents can identify different perspectives and interpretations in information about a political, legal or civic issue, explaining what positions different actors hold and why.
  • 2. Evaluating perspectives and interpretationsStudents can evaluate differences in perspectives and interpretations, assessing the quality of evidence and reasoning behind each, including identifying value positions.
  • 3. Assessing effectiveness of global governance actorsStudents can investigate a contemporary international event or situation and assess the effectiveness and values positions of global actors, including the Australian Government's response.
