Compose and notate music

AC9AMU8C02 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compose using the elements of music and compositional devices to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning, and notate, document and/or record the music

Prior knowledge

Students can manipulate elements of music and use compositional devices to communicate ideas when composing and practising music for performance (AC9AMU6C01). They can notate, document and/or record their compositions. They have not yet focused on communicating specific ideas, perspectives or meaning as a compositional intent, nor on arranging or re-imagining existing works.

By the end

Students compose music using elements of music and compositional devices to communicate specific ideas, perspectives or meaning, and notate, document or record the composition in a format appropriate to the genre, style or intended performers.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • writing songs or instrumental music to communicate ideas or opinions about themes or issues, such as their perspectives on local issues relating to sustainable ways of living or relationships such as friendship
  • combining and manipulating the elements of music in ways that are characteristic of selected styles, creating and notating, documenting and/or recording a composition, using available digital tools
  • using patterns such as harmonic, rhythmic or melodic patterns as the basis for improvisation or composition; for example, creating a composition entirely from loops or recording a series of loops (ostinati/repeating patterns) and then composing/improvising additional melodic lines
  • composing music for different purposes, such as repertoire for an ensemble to perform or music for a theatrical, site-specific or media work; for example, a fanfare or other composition that will grab the audience's attention at the start of an event, ritual or ceremony
  • using software to notate, document and/or record compositions in formats that are appropriate to a genre, style or instrument, such as chord charts or percussion notation
  • arranging a familiar music piece into a different music style by manipulating the elements of music to convey meaning, and documenting the arrangement in a form that is appropriate, considering the style and the preferences of the musicians who might be performing the arrangement; for example, arranging an instrumental work for choral performance
  • developing an arrangement or re-imagining of an existing melody/tune by improvising, using given material such as a melody, riff or chord progression

Waypoints

  • 1. Establishing compositional intentStudents can identify an idea, perspective or meaning they want to communicate through music and describe how musical choices might serve that intent
  • 2. Using patterns and devices as compositional materialStudents can use harmonic, rhythmic or melodic patterns and compositional devices as the basis for a composition or improvisation
  • 3. Shaping music for purpose and contextStudents can compose music shaped by a specific purpose or context, making decisions about form, style and instrumentation appropriate to that context
  • 4. Notating, documenting and recordingStudents can notate, document or record their composition in a format appropriate to the genre, style or intended performers
  • 5. Integrated composition communicating intentStudents can produce a complete composition that integrates compositional intent, use of patterns/devices, contextual shaping and appropriate documentation into a unified work
