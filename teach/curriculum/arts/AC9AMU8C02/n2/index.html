← AC9AMU8C02 Compose and notate music

Using patterns and devices as compositional material

AC9AMU8C02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use harmonic, rhythmic or melodic patterns and compositional devices as the basis for a composition or improvisation

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one pattern or device as compositional material
  • Composes or improvises additional material that relates to the pattern

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Establishing compositional intent.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one pattern (e.g. ostinato, riff, chord progression) as compositional material; compose or improvise additional material over or around it
Typical
Use multiple patterns or devices (e.g. loops, harmonic progressions, melodic motifs) in combination; make deliberate choices about how patterns are combined, varied or contrasted
Wider
  • Create a composition built entirely from loops or layered ostinati, then compose or improvise additional melodic lines that interact with the looped material — evaluate how the layers interact
  • Develop an arrangement or re-imagining of an existing melody by improvising over or around given material (melody, riff or chord progression) and documenting the choices made

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a working vocabulary of patterns and devices before they can combine them purposefully. Experiencing loops, ostinati, chord progressions and melodic motifs as distinct compositional tools — with their own affordances — builds the palette that compositional decision-making draws on.
