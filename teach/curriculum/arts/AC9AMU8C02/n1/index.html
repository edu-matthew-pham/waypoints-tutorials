← AC9AMU8C02 Compose and notate music

Establishing compositional intent

AC9AMU8C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify an idea, perspective or meaning they want to communicate through music and describe how musical choices might serve that intent

Success criteria

  • States a compositional intent beyond general mood description
  • Identifies at least one musical element or device connected to the intent

Prior knowledge

Students can manipulate elements of music and use compositional devices to communicate ideas when composing and practising music for performance (AC9AMU6C01). They can notate, document and/or record their compositions. They have not yet focused on communicating specific ideas, perspectives or meaning as a compositional intent, nor on arranging or re-imagining existing works.

Width

Minimum
State a compositional intent (idea, perspective, theme or purpose) and identify at least one musical element or device that could help communicate it
Typical
Articulate a compositional intent with enough specificity to guide musical decisions; identify multiple elements or devices that could serve the intent and explain the connection
Wider
  • Investigate how composers have used music to communicate perspectives on social issues (e.g. protest songs, nationalistic music) — analyse the musical choices made and connect them to the intended message
  • Compare how the same broad intent (e.g. 'friendship' or 'belonging') could be communicated differently depending on genre — what changes and what stays the same?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often default to vague or generic compositional intents ('I want it to sound happy') that don't drive meaningful musical decisions. Encountering a range of real compositional purposes — protest, ceremony, film scoring, community — helps students understand that intent shapes musical choices, not just mood.
