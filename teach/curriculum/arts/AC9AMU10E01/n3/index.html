Experimental music and sound installations

AC9AMU10E01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate why music might be described as 'experimental' and analyse how elements of music are manipulated in sound designs for installations or site-specific artworks.

Lessons on this site

Success criteria

  • Can explain why a piece might be described as experimental with at least two musical reasons
  • Can describe how at least one element of music is manipulated in an unusual or context-specific way

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Music in times of conflict, change and social context.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two reasons why a specific piece of music might be described as experimental; describe how at least one element of music is manipulated in an unusual way.
Typical
Investigate the concept of experimental music — how composers combine techniques from different cultural or historical contexts, how elements are manipulated in installations or site-specific works, how the space and physical context shape the music; discuss what distinguishes experimental music from conventional practice and what it aims to achieve.
Wider
  • Investigate how a specific experimental composer combines techniques from two or more cultural or historical traditions — describe what personal style emerges and what makes it distinct from each source tradition
  • Investigate how elements of music are manipulated in a sound installation — analyse how the physical space, listener movement and context shape the musical experience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExperimental music challenges students' assumptions about what music is and does. Guided questioning — 'Why do you think this was made this way?', 'What is the composer trying to achieve that conventional approaches could not?', 'What does the space add to the music?' — builds the analytical flexibility to engage with unfamiliar sonic contexts.
