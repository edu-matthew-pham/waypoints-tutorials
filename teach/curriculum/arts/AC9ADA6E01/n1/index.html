← AC9ADA6E01 Elements of dance communicate meaning

Reading element use in observed dances

AC9ADA6E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe how elements of dance and choreographic devices are combined in dances they experience — connecting element choices to communicative effect.

Success criteria

  • Student can describe how at least two elements combine in an observed dance to create a specific communicative effect
  • Student connects at least one element choice to the mood, meaning or audience relationship in the dance

Prior knowledge

Students can explore where, why and how dance is choreographed and/or performed across cultures, times, places and/or other contexts (Y3-4). They can compare dances and identify purpose and meaning using Viewpoints questions.

Width

Minimum
Identifying how at least two elements of dance are used together in one observed dance — and describing the communicative effect of those choices.
Typical
Exploring how dances use elements and choreographic devices (repetition, unison, contrast); discussing purpose of movements, elements, production elements, technical and expressive skills and their effect on mood and audience relationship; using Viewpoints to explore similarities and differences across choreographers and contexts.
Wider
  • Explore how dance communicates cultural traditions — examining a dance from a country or region in Asia and asking how it reflects cultural ways of being
  • Investigate how elements of dance in a specific dance style (e.g. hip hop, contemporary, classical) reflect the cultural or historical context of that style

Hinge

Reading element combinations as communicative — not just aesthetic — is the analytical move that distinguishes appreciation from analysis. Students who cannot yet do this will engage with dances at a descriptive surface level in Y7-8 investigating work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkElement-to-meaning reading requires an explicit interpretive move — students need Viewpoints questions with analytical scaffolding: 'How does the use of slow dynamics here change the mood?', 'What does the unison say about the relationship between dancers?'
