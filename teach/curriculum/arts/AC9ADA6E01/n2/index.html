← AC9ADA6E01 Elements of dance communicate meaning

Comparing element use across cultural contexts

AC9ADA6E01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare how elements of dance are combined across different cultural and temporal contexts — identifying how context shapes element use.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can compare element use in two dances from different cultural or temporal contexts
  • Student offers a culturally contextualised explanation for at least one difference observed

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Reading element use in observed dances.

Width

Minimum
Comparing element use in two dances from different cultural or temporal contexts — identifying at least one difference in how elements are combined and suggesting why.
Typical
Exploring similarities and differences in how choreographers use elements across genres, styles, cultures and times; using Viewpoints to develop questions about how main ideas are communicated; seeking feedback about how audience perceived choreographic intent; considering how cultural traditions shape element use.
Wider
  • Investigate how dance-on-screen (film, music video) uses production elements alongside dance elements — examining how the combination changes what is communicated
  • Explore how a dance from a country or region in Asia uses elements in ways that differ from familiar Western dance styles — discussing what this reveals about cultural values and aesthetics

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepGenuine comparison requires genuine diversity of examples. Students who have encountered only one or two cultural dance contexts cannot make meaningful comparative observations.
