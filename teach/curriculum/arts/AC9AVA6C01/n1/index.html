← AC9AVA6C01 Plan and create artworks that communicate ideas and meaning

Planning with intention

AC9AVA6C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan an artwork systematically — generating ideas, identifying visual conventions and processes that will serve their intent, and documenting decisions before making.

Success criteria

  • Produces a plan that identifies the idea, at least one convention, and a material or process
  • Explains how at least one planned choice will serve the communicative intent

Prior knowledge

Students can create artworks that communicate specific ideas and perspectives using compositional choices and deliberate selection of conventions and materials (AC9AVA4C01). They can generate and select ideas and explain their choices. They have not yet planned artworks systematically, applied aesthetic criteria, or created works that combine digital and analog materials intentionally.

Width

Minimum
Produce a simple plan for an artwork — identify the idea, at least one convention to be used, and one material or process — before beginning the work.
Typical
Plan artworks using brainstorming, Viewpoints questions and compositional sketches; identify and document how visual conventions, materials and processes will serve the communicative intent; make and justify choices before making.
Wider
  • Create a logbook comparing analog and digital versions of the same material — document what each can and cannot do and explain which will serve the artwork's intent
  • Plan an artwork inspired by a text's illustrative approach — analyse how the illustrator's choices serve the narrative, then design your own approach for a different scene

Hinge

Systematic planning is the new demand at Y5-6 compared to Y3-4. Students who skip planning and rely on intuition produce works that can communicate but cannot be refined or defended — subsequent nodes about refining and evaluating choices are inaccessible.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPlanning is a reasoning process — students need to think forward (what will this produce?) not just backward (why did I make this?). Structured planning prompts build the habit of intentional decision-making before the work begins.
