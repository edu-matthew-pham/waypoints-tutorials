Plan and create artworks that communicate ideas and meaning

AC9AVA6C01 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use visual conventions, visual arts processes and materials to plan and create artworks that communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning

Prior knowledge

Students can create artworks that communicate specific ideas and perspectives using compositional choices and deliberate selection of conventions and materials (AC9AVA4C01). They can generate and select ideas and explain their choices. They have not yet planned artworks systematically, applied aesthetic criteria, or created works that combine digital and analog materials intentionally.

By the end

Students use visual conventions, visual arts processes and materials to plan and create artworks that communicate ideas, perspectives or meaning — making and documenting deliberate choices about how elements serve aesthetic and communicative intentions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying their understanding of visual arts processes and materials to create artworks that are both robust and communicate intended aesthetic qualities
  • using visual conventions and visual arts processes to communicate ideas and meaning in their artwork; for example, exploring the ways that different brushstrokes can communicate meaning in a painting
  • examining a series of diverse artworks that employ frames, windows, mirrors or sections as containers or portals into space
  • trying and testing a range of analog visual arts materials, such as paints, drawing materials or clay, and comparing virtual simulations of these materials
  • creating artworks that are the product of ideas they have developed through experimentation and planning
  • forming modular patterns, images or letters with grid paper or an online digital system to combine glyphs, pixels and shapes

Waypoints

  • 1. Planning with intentionHingeStudents can plan an artwork systematically — generating ideas, identifying visual conventions and processes that will serve their intent, and documenting decisions before making.
  • 2. Aesthetic choices and material knowledgeStudents can make and explain choices about materials, joining techniques and visual conventions based on the aesthetic qualities they want the artwork to have.
  • 3. Creating with coordinated intentStudents can create an artwork in which planning, aesthetic choices and communicative intent are coordinated — the work delivers what was intended.
