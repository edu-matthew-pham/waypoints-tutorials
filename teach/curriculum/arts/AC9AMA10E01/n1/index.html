Technical and symbolic codes — ideology and positioning

AC9AMA10E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how technical and symbolic codes communicate social beliefs, values and ideology — and evaluate how audiences are positioned to respond.

Success criteria

  • Student can analyse how at least three technical or symbolic codes in a media arts work communicate social beliefs or values
  • Student can explain how the work positions its audience to respond and whose perspectives are centred or marginalised

Prior knowledge

Students can compare media representations across contexts and explain how context shapes values and stereotypes. They have analysed power relationships in representations. They have not yet investigated at the level of evaluating social, cultural and ethical implications — or analysing how media platforms use data to shape audience information.

Width

Minimum
Analyse how at least three technical or symbolic codes in a media arts work communicate specific social beliefs or values, and explain how the work positions its audience to respond.
Typical
Analyse how social beliefs and values are communicated through technical and symbolic codes; evaluate how audiences are positioned by representations; investigate stereotypical representations across historical and contemporary contexts; examine how media institutions and practices influence audience perceptions.
Wider
  • Analyse and evaluate how a specific media arts genre has communicated and evolved its ideological positioning across different historical periods — identifying the technical and symbolic code changes that correspond to social changes, and evaluating what this reveals about media's relationship to ideology
  • Investigate how media platforms use data to influence the breadth and depth of information provided to audiences — examining algorithmic curation through media feeds and evaluating the implications for audience agency, representation and ideological positioning

Hinge

Understanding how codes communicate ideology and position audiences is the conceptual foundation for this standard at Y9-10 — without it, subsequent evaluation of social, cultural and ethical implications has no analytical grounding.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need an explicit analytical framework: 'What values does this code communicate? Who is positioned to agree with this representation? Whose perspective is marginalised?' This is the critical media literacy reasoning chain at Y9-10.
