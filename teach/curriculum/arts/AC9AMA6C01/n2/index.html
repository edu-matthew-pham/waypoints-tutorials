Production planning for audiences

AC9AMA6C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a media arts production using pre-production tools — designing for a specific audience and making pre-production decisions that serve that audience relationship.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a pre-production plan (storyboard, shot list, or equivalent) for a media arts work
  • Student can explain at least two audience-directed decisions in the plan

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Audience and genre.

Width

Minimum
Produce a storyboard or shot list for a key scene in a planned media arts work — including at least two audience-directed decisions (e.g. camera angle choice, sound choice) with written justifications.
Typical
Design using pre-production tools (storyboard, shot list, game design plan, script) for specific audiences; apply genre conventions to planning decisions; reflect on the storyboard using Viewpoints questions ('What is working well?', 'What do I need to improve?'); plan permissions and documentation requirements.
Wider
  • Design a storyboard for a short genre film — including camera angles, transitions, edits, voice and soundtrack decisions — and evaluate the storyboard against the intended audience relationship before producing
  • Design a game character for a specific genre — considering costume, mannerisms, cultural background and typical dialogue for the target audience — and justify each design decision in terms of genre conventions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPre-production planning is a discipline habit that requires practice — students who skip or rush storyboarding produce works that drift from their communicative intent. Invest in thorough planning cycles, not just capture.
