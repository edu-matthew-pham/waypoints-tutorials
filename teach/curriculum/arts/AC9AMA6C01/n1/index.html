Audience and genre

AC9AMA6C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the relationship between audience, purpose and genre — explaining how codes and conventions of an established genre serve specific audiences.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify the genre of a media arts work and name at least two defining codes or conventions
  • Student can explain how those conventions serve the intended audience

Prior knowledge

Students can construct representations using media languages and production processes, communicating ideas and perspectives. They have applied shot types, sound, sequencing and pre-production planning. They have not yet designed works specifically for audiences — applying genre codes and conventions deliberately, or using chroma key, special effects and more advanced editing.

Width

Minimum
Identify the genre of one media arts work, name two codes or conventions that define it, and explain how those conventions serve the intended audience.
Typical
Analyse genre codes and conventions across two or more media forms (genre film, music video, advertisement, game design); explain how conventions create audience expectations and relationships; identify story structures and technical/symbolic elements that contribute to genre formation.
Wider
  • Identify the genre conventions of a music video and explain how they structure the audience experience differently from a narrative film — applying findings to original genre analysis or production planning
  • Analyse game genre conventions (character design, level design, dialogue conventions) and investigate how each convention builds a specific audience relationship — applying analysis to original character or level design

Hinge

Understanding the audience-genre relationship is the prerequisite for all subsequent production decisions — without it, production choices serve personal preference rather than communicative intent for a specific audience.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse Viewpoints questions to build the reasoning chain: 'How can I adapt and manipulate story principles to make my intention clear for this audience?' Students need explicit practice connecting genre conventions to audience expectation.
