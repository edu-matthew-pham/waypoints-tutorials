← AC9ADR10C01 Improvise, devise and interpret

Working within a framework to develop dramatic intention

AC9ADR10C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Drama: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a teacher-provided or self-generated framework to develop a clear dramatic intention — specifying audience, viewpoint, purpose, style and form before devising.

Success criteria

  • Student articulates a dramatic intention addressing audience, purpose and style/form
  • Student can explain how their devising decisions are driven by that intention

Prior knowledge

Students can improvise, devise and interpret scripted drama, manipulating elements and applying conventions relevant to style/form (Y7-8). They can evaluate and refine dramatic work and develop performance skills for specific styles.

Width

Minimum
Student articulates a clear dramatic intention that addresses at least audience, purpose and style/form before beginning devising or interpretation work.
Typical
Students use frameworks (stimulus material, imagery, character lists, narrative outlines) to develop drama; they consider contexts such as audiences, viewpoints, intentions, purpose and style/form when planning interpretations; they work collaboratively to devise and develop dramatic action.
Wider
  • Students script scenes initially developed through improvisation, applying play-building techniques to refine them into coherent dramatic work
  • Students develop devised drama that presents a range of perspectives on an event — using non-linear structures, narrator/chorus conventions, or disruptive characters to challenge 'agreed' understandings
  • Students devise drama responding to an issue identified from another learning area, building purposeful interdisciplinary dramatic work

Hinge

At Y9-10, devising and interpretation without a clear intentional framework produces technically competent but purposeless work; the aesthetic-choice refinement in the second strand (AC9ADR10C02) depends entirely on having a clear unified intention to refine toward.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkRequire an explicit 'intention statement' before devising work begins — audience, purpose, style, key message. This is not bureaucracy; it is the analytical foundation for all subsequent refining decisions.
