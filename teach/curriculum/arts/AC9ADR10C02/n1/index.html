Deliberate aesthetic choice-making

AC9ADR10C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Drama: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the aesthetic choices operating in their drama — and articulate why each choice serves the unified dramatic meaning.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least three aesthetic choices in their drama
  • Student explains how each choice contributes to the unified dramatic meaning — not just what it does technically, but why it belongs in this work

Prior knowledge

Students can evaluate and refine use of elements and conventions to shape dramatic action (Y7-8). They can plan, organise and rehearse, and adjust performance based on feedback and audience response.

Width

Minimum
Student identifies at least three aesthetic choices (elements, conventions, design) in their drama and explains how each contributes to unified meaning.
Typical
Students evaluate how ideas, emotions and audience response can be used to refine the drama; they consider the impact of design elements (costume, prop, set, lighting, sound) on meaning and aesthetic effect; they manipulate elements and use conventions to create and manipulate actor-audience relationships.
Wider
  • Students take on specific production roles (director, designer, stage manager) and articulate aesthetic decisions from within that role
  • Students evaluate how actor-audience relationship conventions (fourth wall, direct address, immersive staging) change the nature of audience experience and adjust their choices accordingly

Hinge

The concept of aesthetic unity — all choices serving a single coherent meaning — is what distinguishes sophisticated drama-making at this band from technically competent but unfocused work; without this frame, refining is just fixing individual problems rather than shaping a unified work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkIntroduce 'aesthetic unity' as an explicit concept before the refinement phase; use analysis of existing works to model what it looks like, before applying the lens to students' own drama.
