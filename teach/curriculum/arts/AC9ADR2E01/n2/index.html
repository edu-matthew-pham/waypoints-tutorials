How and why drama communicates — across cultures

AC9ADR2E01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Drama: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how drama communicates feelings and ideas, and identify that different communities and cultures use drama for different purposes.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student explains at least one reason why people in a given context make and experience drama
  • Student identifies that drama can communicate different things for different cultures or communities

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Where drama happens in life and community.

Width

Minimum
Student can explain one reason why people in a specific context make and experience drama.
Typical
Students recognise how drama shows feelings and ideas people have about the world; they ask and discuss questions ('How and why are these people making drama?', 'What is the drama about?'); they explore how drama communicates cultural knowledge in their own and other communities.
Wider
  • Students investigate how drama communicates stories from a country or region in Asia, exploring what that drama is about and why it matters to that community
  • Students compare two different drama contexts — one familiar and one from another culture — and identify similarities and differences in purpose and form

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse guiding questions ('What is the drama about?', 'How do you think the people making it feel?', 'Why would a community share this story?') to scaffold simple causal and cultural reasoning.
