Where drama happens in life and community

AC9ADR2E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Drama: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify places and occasions where drama is experienced — at school, in the community, and beyond.

Success criteria

  • Student names at least two different contexts where drama is experienced (e.g. at school, at a community event, on screen)
  • Student asks or responds to at least one question about where or why drama is made

Prior knowledge

Students can identify arts works in their lives and explain a simple reason why the arts are important for people and communities (Foundation). They have not yet explored drama specifically across different cultures and contexts.

Width

Minimum
Student identifies at least one context where drama is experienced beyond their classroom (a school performance, a community event, a screen-based drama).
Typical
Students share experiences of attending drama performances or participating in drama at school and in their community; they discuss where and why drama is made and experienced.
Wider
  • Students explore drama contexts from another culture or region (e.g. shadow puppetry, Asian performance traditions, community storytelling theatre) and compare with familiar drama
  • Students consider how drama communicates cultural knowledge in their own communities through stories, traditions and shared experience

Hinge

If students only understand drama as a school classroom activity, they cannot engage with the 'across cultures and contexts' scope of the descriptor; understanding that drama exists widely is the foundation for the 'why' inquiry.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDeliberately expand students' awareness of drama contexts — community theatre, cultural ceremonies, puppetry, screen drama — before narrowing to 'why' questions.
