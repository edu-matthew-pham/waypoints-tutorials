← AC9AMU4P01 Perform learnt and composed music in informal settings

Using technology in rehearsal and performance

AC9AMU4P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use rehearsal recordings and (with assistance) sound reinforcement to evaluate and improve their performances.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Records a rehearsal and identifies at least one specific thing to improve from the recording
  • Can describe the difference between how they thought they sounded and how they actually sounded

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Performing from notation.

Width

Minimum
Make at least one recording of a rehearsal, listen back, and identify one specific thing to improve.
Typical
Use rehearsal recordings systematically to evaluate performance and make documented adjustments; with assistance, apply basic sound reinforcement (PA, microphones, foldback) in a performance context and adjust to ensure the audience and performers can hear the music clearly.
Wider
  • Record two rehearsals at different stages of preparation, listen back to both, and document what improved and what still needs work
  • Learn to set up and adjust basic PA equipment — practise the process before a performance so it is efficient and reliable on the day

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkListening back to recordings requires students to listen as audience rather than as performers. Build a structured listening framework: 'Listen first for timing, then for balance, then for tone.' Without structure, students either hear only overall impression or listen only for their own part.
