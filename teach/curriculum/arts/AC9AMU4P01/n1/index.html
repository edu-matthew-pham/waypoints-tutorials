Performing from notation

AC9AMU4P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read from and follow notation or documentation (lead-sheet, staff or graphic notation) when practising and performing music.

Success criteria

  • Can navigate a notation document during rehearsal and find their place
  • Follows at least one specific instruction from the notation (e.g. a dynamic marking or repeat sign) during performance

Prior knowledge

Students can sing and play in informal settings, follow agreed performance decisions, introduce performances and contribute to post-performance discussion (AC9AMU2P01). They can make basic staging decisions. They have not yet performed from notation in rehearsal/performance, used sound reinforcement technology, or iteratively refined a performance using recordings.

Width

Minimum
Read from at least one notation format during a rehearsal and apply at least one instruction from it during the performance.
Typical
Read from a range of notation formats (lead-sheet, staff and graphic notation) across different repertoire; use notation to guide element manipulation decisions during rehearsal; revise notation when performance decisions change.
Wider
  • Perform from a lead-sheet (lyrics and chords) for a song — navigate the notation in real time and discuss where reading helped and where it required additional preparation
  • Compare rehearsing from memory vs from notation for the same piece — what are the advantages and limitations of each?

Hinge

Performing from notation is a new performance skill at Y3-4 and the primary new technical demand of this standard relative to Y1-2. Students who cannot follow notation in performance cannot access the full range of repertoire available in Y5-6 and beyond, and cannot participate in ensemble contexts where notation is shared.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsReading notation while simultaneously singing, playing and following group cues is a compound skill that requires the notation-reading component to be sufficiently automatic. Targeted practice of notation reading before full rehearsal integration — practise reading the notation first, then add the performance — reduces cognitive load.
