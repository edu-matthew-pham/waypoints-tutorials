Providing and responding to peer feedback

AC9AMU6P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can provide specific, music-based feedback on a peer's performance and use feedback received to make documented refinements.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Provides at least one music-specific piece of feedback on a peer's performance
  • Makes at least one documented change to their performance in response to feedback received

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Expressive elements in rehearsal and performance.

Width

Minimum
Provide at least one specific music-based piece of feedback on a peer's performance; respond to at least one piece of feedback received by making a documented change.
Typical
Provide and respond to feedback systematically — discussing how the composer indicates dynamics and how the performer/s interpret this; describing how tone colour and articulation communicate mood; making specific adjustments based on feedback and evaluating their effect.
Wider
  • Conduct a structured peer feedback session using a specific framework (e.g. 'I noticed... I wondered... I suggest...') and track how feedback is used across three rehearsals
  • Compare feedback from a peer vs a recording vs the teacher — how does each source offer different perspectives on the performance?

Hinge

Peer feedback as a rehearsal tool — rather than post-performance commentary — is a new practice at Y5-6. Students who cannot give or use specific music-based feedback remain dependent on teacher evaluation for performance improvement. This skill underpins the self-directed rehearsal and evaluation practices required in Y7-8 and Y9-10.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEffective peer feedback requires a framework that keeps observations music-specific. Without structure, students give vague or personal feedback ('I liked it', 'it was too quiet'). Build a feedback protocol: 'Name a specific moment, describe what you heard using a music term, suggest one specific change.' Practise before applying in performance contexts.
