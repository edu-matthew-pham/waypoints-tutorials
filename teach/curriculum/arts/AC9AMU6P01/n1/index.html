Expressive elements in rehearsal and performance

AC9AMU6P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can focus on specific expressive elements (e.g. dynamics and balance) during rehearsal and demonstrate them in performance to engage audiences and communicate ideas.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least one expressive element targeted in rehearsal
  • Demonstrates that element in performance with a recognisable effect on the audience experience

Prior knowledge

Students can perform from notation in informal settings, use rehearsal recordings and basic sound reinforcement, and plan/introduce performances (AC9AMU4P01). They have not yet performed in formal settings, presented via digital platforms, or engaged in systematic peer feedback cycles.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one expressive element to focus on in rehearsal; demonstrate that element in the subsequent performance.
Typical
Rehearse and refine performances with a focus on expressive elements — dynamics, balance, tone colour, articulation (phrasing, staccato, legato); connect expressive choices to the mood and meaning of the music; focus on songs from cultural groups or in a language being learnt.
Wider
  • Rehearse the same piece with different expressive interpretations — compare recordings and decide which interpretation best communicates the mood, with justification
  • Focus on tone colour and articulation across a cultural context the class is exploring — how do the expressive conventions of that style differ from what students typically apply?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsExpressive performance requires the technical aspects to be automated enough that expressive intention can be maintained throughout. Rehearsal that explicitly separates technical accuracy work from expressive focus work — drilling technique first, then adding expression — builds this layered competence.
