Dance across cultures and contexts

AC9ADA2E01 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explore where, why and how people across cultures, communities and/or other contexts experience dance

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how arts are important for communities at Foundation. They recognise that arts experiences — including dances — bring people together and carry cultural meaning.

By the end

Students explore where, why and how people across cultures, communities and/or other contexts experience dance.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying features of dances that communicate cultural knowledge such as stories, traditions or ceremonies
  • using Viewpoints to ask questions about dances experienced
  • recognising and describing patterns of movement using own words and learnt terminology
  • identifying similarities and differences in dances by sharing, viewing and learning dances from families and communities
  • identifying how dance can show what feelings people have about places, people or experiences

Waypoints

  • 1. Observing and describing danceHingeStudents can observe a dance and describe its features — movements, music, costume — using their own words and some dance terminology.
  • 2. Where, why and how people danceStudents can identify that people dance for different reasons in different places and contexts — and that dance can communicate feelings, knowledge and cultural identity.
