Develop, practise and refine aural and instrumental skills

AC9AMU10D01 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop, practise and refine listening/aural skills and style-specific vocal/instrumental skills

Prior knowledge

Students have developed ensemble listening skills, vocal and instrumental techniques for expressive effect, and used digital tools for skill development (AC9AMU8D01 — Y7-8). They can make real-time ensemble adjustments and monitor technique using recordings. The Y9-10 step adds: identifying challenging sections and building stamina, mapping and realising dynamic interpretations, and developing technical and expressive facility and control at a higher level.

By the end

Students develop, practise and refine listening/aural skills and style-specific vocal/instrumental skills, demonstrating increasing technical and expressive facility and control.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • working collaboratively to develop ensemble skills, such as listening carefully to establish and maintain balance across parts
  • identifying challenging sections in music they are learning and developing necessary techniques and stamina
  • mapping a planned approach to interpret dynamics and practising to realise the plan
  • developing technical and expressive facility and control when using voice and/or instrument/s

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying challenges and building staminaHingeStudents can identify the technically or musically challenging sections in music they are learning, develop targeted strategies for those sections, and build the stamina to perform the whole work.
  • 2. Ensemble skills and collaborative listeningStudents can work collaboratively to develop ensemble skills — listening carefully to establish and maintain balance, intonation and blend across parts.
  • 3. Mapping and realising a dynamic interpretationStudents can map a planned dynamic interpretation for a piece (identifying loudest and softest points, shaping dynamic arcs) and practise to realise the plan in performance.
