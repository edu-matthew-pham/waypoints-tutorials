Identifying challenges and building stamina

AC9AMU10D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the technically or musically challenging sections in music they are learning, develop targeted strategies for those sections, and build the stamina to perform the whole work.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least two technically or musically challenging sections in a piece being prepared
  • Has applied a targeted strategy for each and can describe what they tried and what changed

Prior knowledge

Students have developed ensemble listening skills, vocal and instrumental techniques for expressive effect, and used digital tools for skill development (AC9AMU8D01 — Y7-8). They can make real-time ensemble adjustments and monitor technique using recordings. The Y9-10 step adds: identifying challenging sections and building stamina, mapping and realising dynamic interpretations, and developing technical and expressive facility and control at a higher level.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two challenging sections in a piece being prepared; develop and apply a different targeted strategy for each.
Typical
Systematically identify challenges across a piece — technical challenges (speed, range, coordination), musical challenges (expressive nuance, stylistic conventions), and stamina challenges (maintaining quality across the full work); develop and apply targeted practice strategies for each; monitor progress and adjust strategies as needed.
Wider
  • Document a practice log over a rehearsal period — record challenges identified, strategies tried, and progress observed; use the log to inform subsequent practice
  • Focus specifically on stamina: identify where quality drops across a full-length performance and develop conditioning strategies targeted at those points

Hinge

Self-directed practice — identifying challenges and developing strategies without teacher direction — is the foundational skill of independent musicianship. Students who cannot identify and address their own technical weaknesses remain permanently dependent on teacher diagnosis. This is the defining difference between a student who improves in teacher-led rehearsal and one who improves between sessions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkBuild the diagnostic habit explicitly: before each practice session, students identify one specific challenge to work on, one strategy to try, and how they will know if it worked. After the session, brief reflection: 'Did the strategy work? What did you discover?' This moves practice from running-through to deliberate improvement.
