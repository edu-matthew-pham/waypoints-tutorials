First Nations music and ICIP rights

AC9AMU8E02 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate the diversity of music composed and/or performed by First Nations Australians, considering culturally responsive approaches to Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights

Prior knowledge

Students can explore ways First Nations Australians use music to continue and revitalise culture (AC9AMU6E02). They have explored rather than investigated — they have not yet engaged with the specific question of Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP) rights or considered the ethical responsibilities of listeners, performers and composers when engaging with First Nations music.

By the end

Students investigate the diversity of music composed and/or performed by First Nations Australians and engage with culturally responsive approaches to Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights as they are relevant to their practice as listeners, composers and performers.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring ways that First Nations Australian composers and/or performers communicate their ideas and messages across communities and cultures; for example, by asking questions such as "What are the impacts of this musician's/these musicians' music?" and "What music styles or genres is this musician drawing on in creating their own music?"
  • investigating when, how and why copyright and/or Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights might be relevant to their practice as, for example, listeners, songwriters, composers and/or performers
  • exploring how protocols for protecting Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights exist to help students make ethical choices about how they use music, as listeners or creators; for example, by asking questions such as, "Who wrote this music?", "May I use ideas from this song, and do I need permission to do so?"
  • investigating issues relating to the use of First Nations Australian languages or stories in lyrics and songs or music for screen-based works; for example, considering case studies that illustrate protocols relating to Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights

Waypoints

  • 1. Diversity of First Nations Australian musicStudents can describe the diversity of music composed and/or performed by First Nations Australians, including the range of styles, genres and cultural contexts represented
  • 2. Understanding ICIP rightsStudents can explain what Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights are and why they exist, including when and how they might be relevant to music
  • 3. Applying culturally responsive approachesStudents can make ethical choices about how they engage with First Nations Australian music, using protocols for ICIP rights as a guide
