Understanding ICIP rights

AC9AMU8E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights are and why they exist, including when and how they might be relevant to music

Success criteria

  • Explains what ICIP rights are
  • Identifies at least one context in which ICIP rights are relevant to music practice

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Diversity of First Nations Australian music.

Width

Minimum
Explain what ICIP rights are and give at least one example of when they might be relevant to music — as a listener, songwriter, composer or performer
Typical
Explain why ICIP rights exist and how copyright and ICIP rights interact; identify scenarios relevant to music where ICIP rights are relevant and explain what they require
Wider
  • Investigate a case study that illustrates protocols relating to ICIP rights in music — consider how the case clarifies when permission is required and what respectful engagement looks like
  • Investigate issues relating to the use of First Nations Australian languages or stories in lyrics, songs or screen-based music — identify the ethical questions these raise and the protocols that apply

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often understand ICIP rights in the abstract but don't know how to apply them in practice. Encountering multiple specific contexts — listening, sampling, songwriting, performing — where the rights question arises helps students move from abstract understanding to practical ethical reasoning.
