Responsibility for Country/Place

AC9AVA4E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that First Nations Australian visual arts communicate not only connection to Country/Place but also responsibility for caring for it.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one way a First Nations Australian artwork communicates responsibility or care for Country/Place
  • Distinguishes between connection (belonging to a place) and responsibility (caring for a place)

Prior knowledge

Students have explored artworks by First Nations Australians, identified visual conventions and observed how Country/Place is central to First Nations art-making (AC9AVA2E02). They understand that place is connected to identity. They have not yet explored the concept of responsibility for Country/Place, or examined how visual arts communicate this responsibility.

Width

Minimum
Look at one First Nations Australian artwork and identify at least one way it communicates responsibility or care for Country/Place (not only connection).
Typical
Access accounts by contemporary First Nations Australian artists about their ideas concerning Country/Place; use Viewpoints questions to investigate what the works communicate about responsibility; discuss spiritual and intellectual connections to land, sea, sky and waterways.
Wider
  • Explore visual arts that share cultural accounts of Country/Place — investigate how specific visual conventions, symbols and patterns communicate responsibility for specific places
  • Examine artworks addressing the diversity of First Nations Nations — investigate how distinct communities communicate their relationships to their own Country/Place

Hinge

The shift from connection to responsibility is the conceptual advance in this descriptor over Y1-2 — students who understand only connection miss the relational and ethical dimension that gives First Nations visual arts their full meaning at this level.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkResponsibility for Country/Place is an unfamiliar concept for most students. Explicit discussion with targeted prompts is needed before students can identify it in artworks — the concept must be introduced before artworks can demonstrate it.
