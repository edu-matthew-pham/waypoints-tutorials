Seeking and using feedback

AC9ADR8D02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can seek, accept and respond to feedback to inform choices about elements and conventions in own drama

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Responds to at least one piece of feedback with a documented change
  • Explains what the feedback revealed about a specific dramatic choice
  • Change is specific — connected to elements or conventions, not just 'I tried harder'

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Reflecting on own dramatic action.

Width

Minimum
Seek and respond to at least one piece of feedback; document how the feedback changes a specific choice in own drama
Typical
Seek, accept and respond to feedback from peers and teachers; use Viewpoints questions to generate specific feedback about elements and conventions; document how feedback informs choices
Wider
  • Use Viewpoints questions to generate feedback from observers — 'How did the performer use language/movement to make character believable?', 'What relationships did you observe between characters?' — evaluate how each question reveals different dimensions of the drama
  • Develop criteria for giving and receiving useful drama feedback — distinguish feedback that identifies specific dramatic choices from feedback that is merely evaluative
  • Compare feedback from multiple sources (self, peer, teacher, video) — evaluate what each source reveals that others miss

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly receive feedback passively without reasoning through how it applies to specific dramatic choices. Scaffolding the process — what is the feedback pointing to, what specific choice is at stake, what would I change and why — builds the habit of productive feedback integration rather than vague acknowledgment.
