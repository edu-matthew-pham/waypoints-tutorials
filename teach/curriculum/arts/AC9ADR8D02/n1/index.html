Reflecting on own dramatic action

AC9ADR8D02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can reflect on the effectiveness of own dramatic action and make specific decisions about what to do differently

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one aspect that was effective and one that would be done differently
  • Reflection is specific — connected to elements and dramatic action, not just general impressions
  • Documentation uses drama terminology

Prior knowledge

Students can use teacher feedback to reflect on own drama practice (AC9ADR6D02). They have not yet systematically investigated others' drama as a source of influence, or used reflection to document and direct practice development over time.

Width

Minimum
Reflect on at least one aspect of own dramatic action; identify what was effective and what would be done differently; document the reflection
Typical
Reflect independently and in class discussion on dramatic action created; evaluate what was effective, whether the purpose was achieved, whether elements could have been managed differently; document decisions
Wider
  • Use Viewpoints questions to deepen reflection — 'Was meaning clear?', 'How could elements be used differently for better effect?', 'Which elements worked well to make meaning clear?' — evaluate how structured questions reveal things self-observation misses
  • Reflect on how performance skills communicate perspectives — investigate how drama can perpetuate or challenge unequal power relationships
  • Develop a sustained reflective journal across a unit — trace how reflection on own dramatic action has changed practice decisions over time

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsProductive self-reflection in drama — going beyond 'it went well/badly' to specific evaluation of what elements worked, what purpose was achieved, and what specific changes would improve the work — is a disciplined habit that develops through repeated cycles. Students need sustained practice with the reflection cycle, not more dramatic contexts.
