← AC9AVA6D01 Experiment, document and reflect on visual arts practice

Systematic documentation

AC9AVA6D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can document experiments systematically — recording what was tried, what resulted, and what it means for future making.

Success criteria

  • Produces at least one documented experiment that records what was tried and what resulted
  • Draws at least one implication for future making from the documentation

Prior knowledge

Students can experiment with a range of materials and processes, document discoveries through annotation, and use visual arts language to reflect (AC9AVA4D01). They have begun investigating composition. They have not yet produced systematic logbook-style documentation, experimented with Augmented Reality or cross-arts visual mapping, or applied observational drawing across multiple representational modes.

Width

Minimum
Document at least one experiment in a logbook or visual diary — record what was tried, what the result was, and one implication for future making.
Typical
Maintain a developing documentation record across multiple experiments; use Viewpoints questions to annotate processes; connect discoveries to informed future choices; build a body of documented practice.
Wider
  • Create a systematic comparison of analog and digital simulations of the same material — record findings in a logbook and draw conclusions about when each is preferable
  • Document the steps of a printmaking process — record variations (colour changes, progressive texture additions) and reflect on what each variation produces

Hinge

Systematic documentation is the key new demand at Y5-6 — students who do not maintain records of experiments cannot make informed choices in subsequent making. The documentation is what converts experiments into a knowledge base.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDocumentation habits require consistent expectation across multiple sessions to become genuinely systematic. Students who document only when required develop superficial records — the habit needs consolidation through repeated practice.
