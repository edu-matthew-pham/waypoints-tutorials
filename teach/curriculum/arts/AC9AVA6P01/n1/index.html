Preparing artworks for display

AC9AVA6P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can prepare artworks for physical display — attending to measurement, framing, labelling and spatial arrangement.

Success criteria

  • Prepares at least one artwork for display, attending to at least one presentation element
  • Labels the artwork with relevant information about the work and the artist

Prior knowledge

Students can share and display artworks in informal settings, produce artist statements and make audience-driven decisions about format (AC9AVA4P01). They have curated basic displays and produced written or spoken statements. They have not yet prepared artworks for formal display, created professional exhibition materials, or used digital encoding methods to accompany their work.

Width

Minimum
Prepare one artwork for physical display — attend to at least one presentation element (framing, mounting, positioning, labelling).
Typical
Prepare artworks for display within the school environment; measure and make frames or stands using repurposed materials; position artworks at consistent heights and intervals; write and position labels with appropriate information.
Wider
  • Plan and prepare a class exhibition space — make decisions about layout, spacing, label design and audience flow; reflect on how spatial choices affect the audience's experience
  • Adapt a physical display for a digital platform — consider what changes and what can be preserved; add QR codes or image coding to link audiences to additional information

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPhysical display preparation involves practical skills (measurement, framing, labelling) that require practice to execute at a level that serves the artwork. Consolidation across multiple display tasks builds the precision that formal exhibition demands.
