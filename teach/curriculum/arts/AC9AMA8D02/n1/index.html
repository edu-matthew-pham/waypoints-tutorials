Documenting and explaining creative choices

AC9AMA8D02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Media arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can document and explain their creative choices in relation to representations, genre codes and conventions with reference to intended meaning

Success criteria

  • Documents at least two creative choices with reasoning
  • Explains how each choice constructs a representation for the intended audience
  • Documentation goes beyond 'I decided to...' — explains why the choice serves the intended meaning

Prior knowledge

Students can use feedback to reflect on own media arts practice (AC9AMA6D02). They have not yet investigated others' media arts works and practices systematically as a source of influence, or used structured reflection to document and direct production decisions.

Width

Minimum
Document at least two creative choices in own media arts work; explain how each constructs a representation for the intended audience
Typical
Document and explain creative choices throughout the production process in relation to representations, genre codes and conventions; use Viewpoints questions to interrogate own decisions
Wider
  • Use Viewpoints questions to interrogate own representational choices — 'How can I represent a range of views?', 'What do I need to change to create stronger audience connection?' — document how each question drives production refinement
  • Use a storyboarding or editing template to annotate creative thinking — document the reasoning behind decisions, not just the decisions themselves
  • Develop a production journal that traces how creative choices have evolved across pre-production, production and post-production — evaluate what the documentation reveals about own decision-making

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDocumenting creative choices with genuine reasoning — explaining why a decision serves the intended representation rather than just recording what was decided — is a reflective practice habit that develops through sustained iteration. Students who document after the fact without reasoning through the decisions haven't built the habit of reflective production practice.
