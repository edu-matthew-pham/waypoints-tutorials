← AC9ADR2P01 Share drama in informal settings

Performing and reflecting

AC9ADR2P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Drama: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform their drama in an informal setting and reflect on the experience as performer and as audience member.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student performs their drama in an informal setting to at least one audience member
  • Student responds to at least one reflection question about what the drama made them think or feel
  • Student identifies at least one thing they liked in their own or a peer's drama and says why

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What the audience should experience.

Width

Minimum
Student performs their drama to an audience (even a partner) and responds to at least one reflection question afterwards.
Typical
Students retell a familiar story or share a drama through acting in an informal setting; they perform sequences (including frozen images or transitions) to communicate key scenes; they reflect using questions about what they liked, what the drama made them think about, and how they felt making and watching it.
Wider
  • Students perform a drama based on a community story, family experience or exploration of First Nations Australians' connection to place, sharing with their class
  • Students use digital recording to capture and watch back their performance, noticing what communicated well

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPerformance and reflection integrate all prior skills. Post-performance discussion ('What did you like best? Why? What did this drama make you think about?') scaffolds the reflective habit that builds over the later bands.
