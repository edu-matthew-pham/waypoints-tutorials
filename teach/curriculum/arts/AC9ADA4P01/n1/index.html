Expressive skills in performance

AC9ADA4P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Dance: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use expressive skills — projection and focus — to communicate dance ideas to an audience.

Success criteria

  • Student demonstrates projection or focus in their performance — visible to an observer
  • Student can name the expressive skill they are using and describe what it involves

Prior knowledge

Students have shared dance sequences in informal settings (Y1-2). They can present with expressive intent and reflect on performances using Viewpoints questions.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrating at least one expressive skill (projection or focus) in a performance — student can name and describe what they are doing.
Typical
Using expressive skills of projection and focus in informal performances: looking out and up to communicate joy; extending movements outwards; maintaining focus to communicate intention; practising these skills across multiple performances.
Wider
  • Practise the same sequence with deliberate variation in expressive skill (e.g. with and without projection) — comparing what changes in the audience's experience
  • View recordings of own performances to identify where expressive skills are strong and where they could be developed

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyExpressive performance skills require repeated practice across many performance opportunities. Single performances don't build the consistency and confidence that genuine performance presence requires.
