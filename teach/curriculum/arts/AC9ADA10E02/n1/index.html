Celebrating and challenging Australian identity

AC9ADA10E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate how First Nations Australian choreographers and/or performers use dance to celebrate and challenge perspectives of Australian identity.

Success criteria

  • Student can describe how a specific First Nations Australian dance work celebrates or challenges a perspective of Australian identity
  • Student connects the choreographer's community position and lived experience to specific choices in the work

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate the diversity of dance choreographed and/or performed by First Nations Australians, considering ICIP rights (Y7-8, AC9ADA8E02). They understand cultural appropriation and can apply culturally responsive approaches to First Nations dance.

Width

Minimum
Investigating at least one example of a First Nations Australian dance work that either celebrates or challenges a perspective of Australian identity — describing the choreographer's approach.
Typical
Evaluating ways that First Nations Australian performers and/or choreographers work respectfully and collaboratively with communities to explore local histories, cultures and identities; investigating dance works that challenge understandings about 'first contacts' between First Nations Peoples and colonisers; exploring how choreographers and performers communicate ideas, messages and lived experiences to the broader community.
Wider
  • Investigate a First Nations Australian choreographer's sustained practice — examining how their work has evolved in response to historical and contemporary events
  • Compare how two different First Nations Australian dance practitioners address Australian identity — analysing what different community positions produce in terms of choreographic choices

Hinge

Understanding dance as a site of cultural celebration and political challenge — not merely cultural expression — is the conceptual shift that enables genuine critical engagement with First Nations Australian art. Without it, students remain in appreciation mode rather than critical analysis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of challenging identity requires students to understand that art can be a form of advocacy, resistance and reframing — not just representation. Explore specific examples where the work explicitly challenges dominant narratives. Use resources that centre First Nations voices.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations Australian choreographic practices are diverse — no single example represents the whole. Investigate multiple practitioners from different communities and contexts.
