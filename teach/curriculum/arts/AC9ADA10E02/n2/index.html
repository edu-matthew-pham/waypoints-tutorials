Communicating lived experience through dance

AC9ADA10E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how First Nations Australian choreographers and/or performers communicate lived experiences and ideas to the broader community through dance.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least two choreographic choices in a First Nations Australian work that communicate lived experience to a broad audience
  • Student can explain what the work communicates and to whom — recognising that different audiences may read the work differently

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Celebrating and challenging Australian identity.

Width

Minimum
Identifying how at least two specific choreographic choices in a First Nations Australian work communicate a lived experience or idea to a broad audience.
Typical
Exploring the ways that First Nations Australian choreographers and/or performers use their practice to communicate ideas, messages and lived experiences to the broader community; considering how the work addresses audiences who may have no prior relationship to First Nations Australian cultures.
Wider
  • Investigate how a First Nations Australian dance work was received by different audiences — comparing how community audiences and broader Australian audiences read the same work
  • Explore how a First Nations Australian choreographer's relationship to both First Nations and broader Australian cultural contexts shapes their communicative choices

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCommunicating lived experience is both a choreographic and a political act. Help students understand the layers of address in First Nations Australian dance — to community, to country, and to a broader national audience — and how each layer produces different interpretive demands.
