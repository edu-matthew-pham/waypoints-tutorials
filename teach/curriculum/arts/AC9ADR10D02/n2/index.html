Using reflection to shape and refine practice

AC9ADR10D02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use reflective analysis to make informed, documented refinements to their dramatic practice — demonstrating that reflection has changed specific choices.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student documents at least one change to dramatic practice explicitly motivated by reflective analysis
  • Student evaluates the effect of that change on the drama's meaning or effectiveness
  • Student draws at least one lesson for future practice

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Analytical reflection on elements, conventions and approaches.

Width

Minimum
Student documents at least one change to their dramatic practice that is explicitly motivated by reflective analysis.
Typical
Students shape their use of elements, conventions and styles to achieve intended meaning or effect; they reflect on and evaluate how successfully intentions, meaning and effects were achieved; they draw lessons for future dramatic practice.
Wider
  • Students maintain a reflective practice journal or portfolio across a substantial drama project, analysing how their thinking evolved across multiple iterations
  • Students engage in peer-critique sessions using a shared analytical framework, developing capacity to give and receive high-quality developmental feedback

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierReflection-into-practice is the integration move at Y9-10: analysis informs choice, choice produces work, work is analysed again. Structured cycles of make-reflect-revise are the key pedagogical scaffold.
Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuilding a repertoire of reflective approaches — personal journal, peer critique, video review, critical writing — gives students multiple lenses for examining their own practice.
