Analytical reflection on elements, conventions and approaches

AC9ADR10D02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse their own and others' drama at the level of specific element, convention and approach choices — identifying what is working, what is not, and why.

Success criteria

  • Student analyses at least two specific dramatic choices with reasoning — not just description
  • Student's reflection distinguishes intention from effect and addresses the gap between them

Prior knowledge

Students can reflect on their own and others' drama to inform choices when manipulating elements and conventions (Y7-8). They can seek, accept and respond to feedback, and develop understanding of human behaviours and emotions in performance contexts.

Width

Minimum
Student produces a reflection that analyses at least two specific choices (element, convention, or approach) in their own or a peer's drama, with reasoning for each assessment.
Typical
Students compare use of conventions to explore and challenge power and oppression across performance styles; they analyse and make choices about manipulating elements and experimenting with conventions (subtext, beats) to affect meaning; they shape their use of elements and conventions to achieve intended meaning.
Wider
  • Students write, pod-cast or video-cast a critical analysis of their own rehearsal process — documenting decisions, testing alternatives, and evaluating outcomes
  • Students analyse how their own dramatic practice responds to (or resists) cultural influences, naming specific choices as culturally shaped

Hinge

Reflective practice at the analytical level — not just 'what happened' but 'why this choice and what was its effect' — is the mechanism through which Y9-10 students develop independent dramatic judgment; without it, refining is reactive rather than intentional.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProvide analytical frameworks — 'What was the intention? What choice was made? What was the effect? What would you change and why?' — to scaffold the move from descriptive to analytical reflection.
