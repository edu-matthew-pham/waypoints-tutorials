Develop skills for manipulating elements

AC9AMU4D01 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop listening skills and skills for manipulating elements of music when singing and playing instruments

Prior knowledge

Students have developed listening skills for specific purposes and basic singing/instrumental technique (AC9AMU2D01). They can match pitch with developing accuracy and demonstrate correct technique for classroom instruments. They have not yet focused on manipulating elements deliberately to create and control intended effects.

By the end

Students develop listening skills and skills for manipulating elements of music to create controlled expressive effects when singing and playing instruments.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using speaking, singing, voice/vocalisation and movement to develop skills for singing/vocalisation
  • manipulating elements of music to create and control intended effects, using sounds from a range of sources
  • singing and playing learnt pitch and rhythm patterns and varying elements of music within them to create different effects
  • listening to the effects they create by manipulating elements of music in different ways
  • listening to diverse examples of music to explore how rhythm and pitch patterns, structures or timbres are used
  • practising reading staff, graphic and/or invented notation as they rehearse and perform
  • exploring options for representing sounds in a score

Waypoints

  • 1. Manipulating elements during performanceHingeStudents can manipulate at least one element of music (e.g. dynamics, tempo, articulation) during singing or playing to create a specific intended effect.
  • 2. Listening to evaluate and compare effectsStudents can listen to the effects they create by manipulating elements and evaluate which manipulations work better for the musical intention.
  • 3. Notation as a performance toolStudents can read from staff, graphic and/or invented notation during rehearsal and performance, using it to guide element manipulation decisions.
