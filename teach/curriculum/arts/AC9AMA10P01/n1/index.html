Distribution planning and audience relationships

AC9AMA10P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan a distribution approach for a media arts work — identifying the target audience, platform, and strategies for creating audience relationships in that distribution context.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify a target audience, platform, and at least two audience relationship strategies for a planned distribution
  • Student can explain how the platform choice serves the intended audience relationship

Prior knowledge

Students can present media arts works in informal and formal settings applying responsible media practices. They can reflect on how different audiences receive the same work. They have not yet planned distribution strategies, investigated classification systems, or engaged with government regulation, self-regulation and institutional media producer relationships.

Width

Minimum
Plan a distribution approach for a media arts work — identifying one platform, the target audience, and at least two strategies for creating audience relationships in that context.
Typical
Plan distribution approaches across personal, community and institutional contexts; research how different audiences relate to media arts works and how those relationships inform production and distribution; investigate how intended audiences and the communities they form influence the creation of new works.
Wider
  • Investigate the relationships created between intended audiences and media arts works — including the communities formed between audience members — and use this research to plan a distribution strategy that deliberately cultivates specific audience relationships
  • Plan distribution for a media arts work across two different contexts (e.g. school LMS and community event) — comparing how each context creates different audience relationships and evaluating which best serves the work's purpose

Hinge

Distribution planning is prerequisite to actual distribution — without a plan that addresses audience relationships, responsible media practice is applied reactively rather than strategically, and the 'plan approaches' command term is not met.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse structured planning: 'Who is the intended audience? How does this platform create or limit the relationships I want? What responsible practice requirements apply in this context?' Distribution decisions are relationship decisions.
