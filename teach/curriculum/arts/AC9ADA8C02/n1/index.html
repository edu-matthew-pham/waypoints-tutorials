Applying technical skills to intensify communication

AC9ADA8C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply specific technical skills to intensify the communication of mood, atmosphere or meaning in a dance

Success criteria

  • Applies at least one technical skill with a specific communicative purpose
  • Explains how the skill contributes to mood or meaning
  • Technical choices are connected to intent — not just demonstrations of ability

Prior knowledge

Students can use technical and expressive skills in own dance works (AC9ADA6C02). They have not yet applied skills with a clear rationale for how each contributes to communicating specific ideas, perspectives or meaning.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least one technical skill (e.g. balance, strength, coordination) with a specific communicative purpose; explain how the skill contributes to the mood or meaning communicated
Typical
Apply a range of technical skills across a dance to communicate choreographic intent; explain how each technical choice intensifies or clarifies the intended communication
Wider
  • Investigate how mastery of a specific technical skill (e.g. controlled stillness, sustained movement quality) changes what a dance communicates — experiment with developing that skill to expressive purpose
  • Apply strength, balance and coordination simultaneously — evaluate the challenge of coordinating multiple technical demands while maintaining expressive clarity
  • Experiment with applying style-specific techniques from a dance genre that uses those techniques with different communicative purposes than own familiar style

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly apply technical skills for their own sake — demonstrating what they can do — without connecting technical choices to communicative intent. Scaffolding the reasoning (what am I trying to communicate, which technical quality serves that, how does this skill intensify the intended effect?) builds the habit of purposeful technical application.
