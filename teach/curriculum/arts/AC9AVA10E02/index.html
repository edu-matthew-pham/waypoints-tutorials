First Nations artists celebrate and challenge Australian identity

AC9AVA10E02 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate the ways that First Nations Australian artists celebrate and challenge multiple perspectives of Australian identity through their artworks and visual arts practice

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how First Nations Australian arts continue and revitalise cultures, and engaged with site-specific and community-based works (AC9AVA8E02, implied from progression). They understand arts as ongoing cultural practice. They have not yet engaged with First Nations Australian visual arts as explicitly political, activist or identity-challenging, or investigated 'first contacts' and colonisation from multiple perspectives within this frame.

By the end

Students investigate ways that First Nations Australian artists celebrate and challenge multiple perspectives of Australian identity through their artworks and visual arts practice.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • researching a diverse range of artworks or designs such as artworks in galleries, protest posters or street art in order to analyse how and why First Nations Australian artists have used visual communication as a tool to express activism for change
  • analysing the ways that First Nations Australian artists use their practices to challenge and inform community debate
  • comparing, analysing and evaluating ways that contemporary First Nations Australian artists and designers are exploring and challenging concepts and histories of Australia and Australian identity
  • exploring the ways that First Nations Australian visual artists use their practices to communicate ideas, messages and lived experiences to the broader community
  • understanding ways that the arts provide opportunities for First Nations Australian artists and designers to continue and develop their culture
  • investigating how First Nations Australian visual artists are caring for Country/Place, culture and people through the visual arts

Waypoints

  • 1. Visual arts as activismHingeStudents can investigate how First Nations Australian artists use visual communication as a tool for activism — expressing calls for change through artworks, protest posters and street art.
  • 2. Multiple perspectives on Australian identityStudents can investigate how First Nations Australian artists present multiple perspectives of Australian identity — including perspectives that challenge dominant narratives about 'first contacts' and colonisation.
  • 3. Cultural continuation, development and community voiceStudents can investigate how First Nations Australian visual arts provide opportunities to continue and develop culture, communicate lived experiences, and care for Country/Place and people.
