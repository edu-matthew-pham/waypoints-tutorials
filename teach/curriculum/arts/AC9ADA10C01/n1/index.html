Developing motifs and innovative manipulation

AC9ADA10C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Dance: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop movement motifs and manipulate elements and choreographic devices in innovative ways to communicate a clear choreographic intent.

Success criteria

  • Student can develop a movement motif and generate at least three variations using named manipulation techniques
  • Student can explain how the manipulation choices serve the choreographic intent

Prior knowledge

Students can choreograph dance by selecting and manipulating elements of dance and choreographic devices to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning (Y7-8, AC9ADA8C01). They have worked with binary, ternary and narrative structures, transitions, variation and contrast, and can document their process.

Width

Minimum
Developing at least one movement motif; applying at least two forms of manipulation (e.g. addition, diminution, force, tempo) to generate material from the motif.
Typical
Developing movement motifs and using choreographic devices or manipulating elements in innovative ways; experimenting with addition and diminution, force and tempo, different body parts; developing motifs from dances shared via social media or other sources; using style-specific forms to structure work.
Wider
  • Develop motifs that synthesise influences from dances of different cultures, times and places — using respectful dance practice to adapt rather than appropriate
  • Use digital tools or social media-inspired movement (e.g. viral movement motifs) as starting points — manipulating these into original work that communicates a personal perspective

Hinge

Motif development and innovative manipulation are the compositional engine of senior dance. Without this, students produce choreography that is movement arrangement rather than genuine compositional craft — which limits expressive range and structural sophistication in the final work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkInnovative manipulation requires students to move beyond familiar devices — scaffold with explicit manipulation menus (addition, diminution, inversion, retrograde, fragmentation) and encourage systematic exploration before selection.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who develop motifs from diverse cultural influences and contemporary movement sources build wider compositional range and more personal choreographic voice.
