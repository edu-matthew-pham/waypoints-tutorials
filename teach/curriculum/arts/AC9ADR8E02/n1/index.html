Understanding ICIP rights in drama

AC9ADR8E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what ICIP rights mean in drama contexts and describe what culturally responsive engagement with First Nations drama looks like in practice

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explains what ICIP rights mean in a drama context
  • Gives at least one specific example of respectful engagement
  • Does not reduce ICIP to a checklist — explains the reasoning behind protocols

Prior knowledge

Students have some awareness that First Nations Australians have diverse storytelling and performance traditions. They have not yet investigated this diversity systematically or engaged with ICIP rights frameworks in drama contexts.

Width

Minimum
Explain what ICIP rights are in a drama context; give one example of what respectful engagement with First Nations drama looks like
Typical
Investigate ICIP rights frameworks and explain how they apply to drama-making — using cultural stories, developing characters from First Nations contexts, representing historical events
Wider
  • Investigate a specific case of cultural appropriation in drama where ICIP rights were denied — explain what happened, who was harmed, and what a respectful alternative would have been
  • Use ICIP protocols to reason through whether specific ideas from a cultural source can be used in new drama work — document the reasoning process including 'Whose story is it?' and 'Is this my story to share?'
  • Explore how drama practitioners can communicate First Nations Australian perspectives on resilience or colonisation respectfully — develop criteria for ethically engaging with this material

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly understand ICIP rights abstractly without being able to reason through specific drama scenarios — they know permission is needed without being able to work out whose story it is, what counts as cultural expression, or what respectful engagement looks like in practice. Scenario-based scaffolding builds actionable understanding.
