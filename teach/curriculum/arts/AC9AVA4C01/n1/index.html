Generating and selecting ideas

AC9AVA4C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can generate multiple ideas for an artwork, select one, and document their reason for the selection.

Success criteria

  • Generates at least two distinct ideas for an artwork
  • Selects one and gives a specific reason for the choice (not just 'I like it')

Prior knowledge

Students can use visual conventions, processes and materials to create artworks with deliberate intent (AC9AVA2C01). They make choices about conventions and materials based on communicative purpose. They have not yet planned artworks in advance, used compositional strategies, or created works that communicate specific perspectives.

Width

Minimum
Generate at least two possible ideas for an artwork and choose one, giving a reason for the choice.
Typical
Use mind-mapping, Viewpoints questions and brainstorming to generate multiple compositional ideas linked to a topic; evaluate options and select one; document reasons for selection.
Wider
  • Generate ideas for an artwork inspired by a performance (dance, drama) — explore how to capture movement, gesture or emotion through visual means
  • Brainstorm how an illustrator's choices in a familiar text communicate perspective — generate and evaluate different ways to illustrate a specific moment

Hinge

Students who cannot generate and evaluate ideas create from the first option that comes to mind. The selection habit is the prerequisite for intentional composition — without it, later nodes about communicating specific perspectives are undermined.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents at this level need structured tools (mind-mapping, Viewpoints questions) to generate genuine options rather than defaulting to one idea immediately. The reasoning move — evaluating and choosing — requires explicit scaffolding.
