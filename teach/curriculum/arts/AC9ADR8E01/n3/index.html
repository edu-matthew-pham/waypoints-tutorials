← AC9ADR8E01 Investigate drama across cultures and contexts

Connecting investigation to own drama practice

AC9ADR8E01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use investigation of drama across contexts to inform specific decisions in own devising, interpreting or performing

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one insight from investigating other works that influenced own practice
  • Explains the connection — not just 'I was inspired by'
  • Distinguishes informed adaptation from imitation or appropriation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Investigating context and meaning.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one insight from investigating other drama works that has influenced a decision in own practice; explain the connection
Typical
Analyse how investigating drama across contexts informs own choices about elements, conventions and dramatic action; document specific influences and how they are adapted rather than imitated
Wider
  • Develop a journal documenting how investigation of diverse drama traditions has opened new possibilities in own practice
  • Investigate how drama from other cultural traditions uses specific conventions — experiment with how those conventions might inform own devising without cultural appropriation
  • Construct an argument about why investigating diverse drama traditions produces stronger and more distinctive own practice than drawing on a single tradition

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to bring together element/convention analysis (Node 1) and contextual investigation (Node 2) and connect both to own practice decisions. Students who can analyse other works but cannot use that analysis to inform own drama have not met the standard's full investigative demand.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly describe what they have investigated without articulating how it changes specific decisions in own work. Scaffolding the connection — what did I learn, what decision did it change, how is my adaptation different from imitation — builds the analytical-to-practical transfer.
