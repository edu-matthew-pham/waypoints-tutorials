What audiences do

AC9AVAFP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation The Arts · Visual arts: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify what it means to be an audience — that others look at, listen to or watch arts works, and that the audience receives something from the work.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Participates as an audience member for a peer's work
  • Shares at least one observation about the work

Prior knowledge

Students have no formal sharing or presenting experience assumed. They bring familiarity with informal show-and-tell and play-based sharing with peers.

Width

Minimum
Participate as an audience member for a peer's work; share one thing noticed or felt.
Typical
Participate as audience and maker in informal sharing contexts; identify features of artworks when looking at peers' work (similarities, differences, use of materials, visual elements).
Wider
  • Walk around a class gallery and use positive language to comment on specific features of peers' works (line, colour, shape, materials)
  • Watch a performance or sharing of a different arts form — describe what the audience does and what the experience is like

Hinge

Students who have no concept of an audience share work without communicative intent — they cannot make meaningful choices about how to present or explain their work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyThe audience role needs to be experienced from both sides — as receiver and as presenter. Students who have only made, without experiencing what it is to receive, lack the perspective needed for intentional sharing.
