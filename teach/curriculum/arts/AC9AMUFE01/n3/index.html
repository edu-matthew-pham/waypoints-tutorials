Arts as important for communities

AC9AMUFE01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe at least one way that an arts work is important for a community — beyond personal enjoyment.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can describe one way an arts work is important to a community (e.g. it brings people together, it celebrates something, it tells a story about who we are)
  • Can give at least one example from a community context beyond their own personal experience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: How arts communicate emotions and ideas.

Width

Minimum
Identify one way an arts work (e.g. a ceremony song, a school anthem, a community celebration) is important to a group of people, not just to themselves personally.
Typical
Sing songs used for celebrations and discuss why they are used; explore how arts experiences are shared in their school community; listen to First Nations Australians talk about the importance of the arts for connecting to People, Culture and Country/Place.
Wider
  • Investigate how a local cultural event (e.g. a school concert, a community festival) uses music — discuss what it does for the people who participate
  • Listen to First Nations Australians discuss the role of music and arts in connecting to Country/Place — compare this to how music is used in the students' own community

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe concept that arts matter to communities (not just individuals) benefits from encountering multiple community contexts — school, family, cultural, ceremonial. Each new context adds evidence for why the arts have collective importance.
