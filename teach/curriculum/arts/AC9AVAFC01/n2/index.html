← AC9AVAFC01 Create artworks that communicate ideas

Making with communicative intent

AC9AVAFC01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make a deliberate choice in their artwork — choosing what to include, how to arrange it, or what material to use — because of what they want to communicate.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Makes at least one deliberate choice about what to include or how to arrange the artwork
  • Explains the choice in terms of what the work is meant to show or communicate

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Materials and imagination as starting points.

Width

Minimum
Make one deliberate choice (what to put in, what colour, what shape) and explain why it helps communicate the idea.
Typical
Make multiple deliberate choices across materials, arrangement and content; explain choices in terms of what the artwork is communicating.
Wider
  • Respond to First Nations Australian visual arts or designs encountered through culturally authoritative resources — explore how specific choices (colour, symbol, pattern) communicate meaning
  • Create a work in response to another arts form (music, dance) — make choices about what visual elements express the feeling or story

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkYoung students often make without reflecting on why. Simple scaffolding — 'What does your artwork show? Why did you choose that?' — builds the habit of connecting choice to intent.
