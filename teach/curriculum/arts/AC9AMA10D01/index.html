Construct representations with intention

AC9AMA10D01 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

experiment with ways to construct representations that reflect ideas, perspectives and/or meaning, and/or use of media conventions, media languages and media technologies

Prior knowledge

Students can manipulate media languages to change meaning and develop production skills across multiple forms. They can apply genre conventions deliberately. They have not yet experimented systematically with how an auteur, genre or film movement shapes a distinctive representational language — or used target audience research to inform production.

By the end

Students experiment with ways to construct representations that reflect ideas, perspectives and/or meaning using media conventions, languages and technologies — informed by analysis of practitioners and target audience research.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the way technical and symbolic codes are shaped by a media artist such as a social media influencer, auteur, game designer or music video director
  • researching target audiences and conventions used in successful online media arts works
  • experimenting with use of mise-en-scène, camera work, sound, editing and media technologies in ways that are typically used in a genre, auteur or film movement
  • manipulating technical and symbolic media codes and conventions of specific film, television or radio genres to produce a media arts work for a target audience

Waypoints

  • 1. Auteur, genre and representational languageHingeStudents can analyse how a specific media artist (auteur, social media influencer, game designer, music video director) uses technical and symbolic codes to construct a recognisable representational language — and apply this analysis to inform their own construction experiments.
  • 2. Audience research and production experimentationStudents can research a target audience and use findings to design and produce a media arts work — experimenting with multiple production approaches and selecting the most effective for the intended audience relationship.
