Exploring through play

AC9AMUFD01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Foundation The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use play as a genuine tool for discovering musical or arts possibilities — trying things out without a fixed endpoint.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Engages in open-ended play involving sound, voice or movement without requiring a predetermined outcome
  • Can name one thing they discovered or tried during play

Prior knowledge

No formal prior arts learning assumed. Students bring informal experience with play, voice and movement from early childhood. This is their first formal encounter with arts skill-development in a school context.

Width

Minimum
Engage in at least one open-ended play activity involving sound, voice or movement; identify one discovery or surprise from the play.
Typical
Use play to explore tactile qualities, voice possibilities, movement options and rhythm patterns across multiple contexts; describe what they discovered and how it might be used in an arts work.
Wider
  • Explore how tactile qualities of materials (rough, smooth, spikey) can be translated into sound or voice — e.g. 'This rough texture sounds like...'
  • Use dramatic play scenarios to generate musical or movement ideas — ask 'What would the sound of this place/character be?'

Hinge

If students treat arts activities as instruction-following rather than play-based exploration, they miss the imaginative generativity that underpins the descriptor. Later skill development (including improvisation and composition) depends on students being comfortable with exploratory, non-predetermined making. A class that bypasses play at Foundation arrives at composition without the habits of experimentation it requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExplicitly frame play as productive: 'We're going to try things and see what happens — there's no wrong answer here.' Debrief after play by asking 'What did you discover?' to help students articulate that play generates usable ideas.
