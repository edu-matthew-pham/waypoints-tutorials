Developing and extending an idea

AC9AMUFD01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Foundation The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a discovered possibility to develop a musical or arts idea — taking one thing they found through play and doing something intentional with it.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Takes a discovery from play and uses it deliberately in a subsequent arts activity
  • Can describe the connection between their earlier exploration and what they are now doing intentionally

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Responding to and interpreting music.

Width

Minimum
Take one discovery from play or exploration (a vocal quality, a rhythm, a movement) and use it deliberately in a simple arts activity.
Typical
Use discoveries from play to develop ideas across multiple forms — improvising new movements for action songs and interpreting them visually; using a discovered rhythm pattern as the basis for a chant; taking a vocal quality and using it to represent a character.
Wider
  • Improvise rhythmic clapping patterns and then represent them visually using shapes and lines — make explicit connections between the sound and the image
  • Use improvised movement to explain the steps in a process, then capture it as a photographic story with sound commentary

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates play, exploration and skill development into an intentional arts act. The teaching investment is in helping students see the connection: 'You discovered that earlier — now you're using it deliberately.'
