Language revitalisation through music

AC9AMU6E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how music is being used to maintain and revitalise knowledge and use of First Nations Australian languages.

Success criteria

  • Can describe at least one way music is being used to maintain or revitalise a First Nations Australian language
  • Can explain why music serves this purpose effectively

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Diverse contemporary styles and instruments.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of First Nations Australian music that uses or teaches a specific language; describe how the music contributes to language knowledge.
Typical
Investigate how First Nations Australian musicians are using music to maintain and revitalise languages — songs that teach vocabulary, performances that model pronunciation and usage, recordings that preserve language from Elders; discuss the role music plays in keeping languages alive that might otherwise be lost.
Wider
  • Research a specific First Nations Australian language that music is helping to revitalise — find and listen to examples of that music, then discuss how music serves language learning differently from formal instruction
  • Investigate how musicians balance the use of English and First Nations Australian languages in their music — what choices do they make and why?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkLanguage revitalisation through music is a concept most students will encounter for the first time at Y5-6. Build understanding through specific examples and guided questioning: 'How does singing a song in a language help you learn that language?' 'Why might music be a more effective vehicle for language preservation than textbooks?' Connect to what students know about how music helps people remember things.
