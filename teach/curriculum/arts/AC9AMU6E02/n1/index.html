← AC9AMU6E02 First Nations music and cultural continuity

Diverse contemporary styles and instruments

AC9AMU6E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate the diverse music styles and instruments that living First Nations Australian composers and performers are using, including in contemporary genres.

Success criteria

  • Can name at least two different music styles used by living First Nations Australian artists
  • Can describe how each style is connected to First Nations Australian identity or cultural expression

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how First Nations Australians use music to communicate connection to and responsibility for Country/Place (AC9AMU4E02), including engaging with First Nations Australian voices directly. The new focus is cultural continuity and revitalisation — music's role in maintaining living culture, language, and knowledge, including in contemporary contexts.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two different music styles or genres being used by living First Nations Australian composers or performers; describe one instrument or musical feature of each.
Typical
Investigate the range of music styles used by living First Nations Australian composers and performers — contemporary pop, hip-hop, classical, orchestral, ceremonial, folk; identify instruments including both traditional instruments and contemporary ones; recognise the diversity of what First Nations Australian music is and can be.
Wider
  • Compare a First Nations Australian artist working in a contemporary genre (e.g. hip-hop or pop) with one working in a more traditional context — describe how each serves cultural continuity in different ways
  • Investigate the work of a specific First Nations Australian composer or performer in depth — identify their style, instruments, cultural context and what they say about their music

Hinge

A narrow image of what First Nations Australian music 'is' — limited to traditional or ceremonial contexts — prevents students from engaging with living cultural continuity. If students associate First Nations Australian music only with the past, they cannot understand how music continues and revitalises culture in the present. This conceptual block makes the rest of the descriptor inaccessible.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDeliberately expose students to First Nations Australian musicians working across diverse contemporary genres alongside traditional contexts. The breadth itself challenges the assumption that First Nations Australian music is a single, historical thing — each new genre context adds evidence for living, diverse cultural expression.
