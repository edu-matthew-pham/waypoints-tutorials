Design to examine and communicate

AC9AMA10C01 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

design and structure media arts works that examine and communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning

Prior knowledge

Students can design and structure media arts works using pre-production processes and apply media arts concepts to construct representations for specific audiences. They have applied genre codes and conventions and reflected on communicative intent. They have not yet designed works that specifically examine and communicate ideas — applying critical analysis of existing conventions to produce works that confirm or challenge them.

By the end

Students design and structure media arts works that examine and communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning using pre-production processes and deliberate application of technical and symbolic codes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using media technologies, languages and representations when designing a media arts work such as a computer game, community service announcement, website or music video
  • using media technologies to design a media arts work that communicates stereotypical representations for a specific target audience
  • designing the camera work, sound, editing or mise-en-scène to construct alternative representations of people, places, ideas and events
  • examining the way technical and symbolic codes and conventions of specific film, television or radio genres have been used in a media arts work

Waypoints

  • 1. Critical analysis of genre conventionsHingeStudents can analyse how technical and symbolic codes in a specific genre, auteur or film movement communicate values, themes or ideas — and identify how those codes could be used, confirmed or challenged in original work.
  • 2. Pre-production designStudents can use pre-production tools to design and structure a media arts work — making deliberate decisions about how technical and symbolic codes will examine and communicate ideas for an intended audience.
