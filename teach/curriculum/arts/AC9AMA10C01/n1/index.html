Critical analysis of genre conventions

AC9AMA10C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how technical and symbolic codes in a specific genre, auteur or film movement communicate values, themes or ideas — and identify how those codes could be used, confirmed or challenged in original work.

Success criteria

  • Student can analyse how at least two technical and symbolic codes in a genre communicate values, themes or ideas
  • Student can identify how those codes could be used or challenged in original design work

Prior knowledge

Students can design and structure media arts works using pre-production processes and apply media arts concepts to construct representations for specific audiences. They have applied genre codes and conventions and reflected on communicative intent. They have not yet designed works that specifically examine and communicate ideas — applying critical analysis of existing conventions to produce works that confirm or challenge them.

Width

Minimum
Analyse how technical and symbolic codes in one genre communicate at least two specific values, themes or ideas, and identify how those codes could be replicated or challenged in an original work.
Typical
Examine technical and symbolic codes in specific film, television or radio genres; analyse how codes construct stereotypical or alternative representations; identify conventions that confirm or challenge audience expectations; use pre-production processes (shot list, treatment, storyboard) to trial ideas before designing.
Wider
  • Analyse how a specific auteur's use of technical and symbolic codes constructs a recognisable visual/audio language — and design an original work that either pays homage to or deliberately subverts that style, documenting the design decisions
  • Examine how the same genre conventions have been used differently in film works from different decades or cultural contexts — identifying how social forces shaped the conventions, and designing a response that positions itself in relation to that history

Hinge

Critical analysis of genre conventions is the conceptual prerequisite for designing works that examine ideas — without it, production is replication rather than examination, and the 'examine' command term is not met.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need a structured analytical framework: 'What values does this code communicate? Who benefits from this representation? What alternative representation would challenge it?' This reasoning chain grounds design decisions in critical analysis.
