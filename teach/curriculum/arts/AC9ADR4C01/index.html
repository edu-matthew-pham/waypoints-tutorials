Improvise and devise drama

AC9ADR4C01 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Drama: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

improvise and/or devise and shape drama using the elements of drama to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning

Prior knowledge

Students can create and co-create fictional situations using elements of drama in dramatic play and process drama (Y1-2). They can take on roles, contribute ideas, and communicate non-verbally within a group improvisation.

By the end

Students improvise and/or devise and shape drama using elements of drama to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using learning from First Nations Australians' drama to devise drama communicating connection to place
  • creating drama by exploring roles and situations in a 'miniature world'
  • improvising drama to explore issues, using empathy to take on roles and 'stand in the shoes' of another
  • devising dialogue for a previously 'wordless story'
  • using elements of drama and principles of story to shape improvisations
  • working in groups to devise sections of drama and combining them using play-building
  • communicating Acknowledgement of Country before a performance

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying dramatic potentialHingeStudents can identify a situation, issue or story worth exploring through drama and name what ideas or perspectives it raises.
  • 2. Using elements to shape improvised dramaStudents can use elements of drama — time, place, role, language, movement — to shape improvisations and give them meaning and structure.
  • 3. Devising and presenting drama with communicative intentStudents can work collaboratively to devise a structured drama that communicates an intended idea, perspective or meaning.
