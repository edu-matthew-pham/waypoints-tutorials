Exploring and varying sound sources

AC9AMU2D01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how sounds from a range of sources (voice, classroom instruments, found objects, digital instruments) can be varied using elements of music.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Explores at least two different sound sources
  • Demonstrates at least one variation of a musical element on each source explored

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Listening for specific purposes.

Width

Minimum
Explore at least two different sound sources; demonstrate one way each can be varied (e.g. played louder/softer, faster/slower, or with a different technique).
Typical
Explore and improvise ways to create and vary sounds across multiple source types — voice/vocalisation, classroom instruments, found/environmental objects and digital instruments; ask and answer questions such as 'Which way of playing sounds best?' and 'What is the way I want it to sound?'
Wider
  • Compare how the same element (e.g. dynamics) is varied differently on different instruments — what techniques are used on a drum vs a xylophone vs voice?
  • Explore found and environmental objects as instruments — investigate the range of sounds each can produce and how elements like articulation and tone colour vary

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyA rich repertoire of sound source experiences builds the tonal imagination that underpins composition and performance decisions. Students who have only explored one or two instruments default to those instruments in composition; broader exploration opens up more deliberate choices.
