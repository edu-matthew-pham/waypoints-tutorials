Personal music preferences and experiences

AC9AMU2E01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can discuss their own music preferences and compare them with peers and family members, linking preferences to where and how music is experienced.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can share a music preference and give at least one reason
  • Can connect their preference to a context in which they experience that music (e.g. 'I listen to it at home', 'my family plays it at celebrations')

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Music from diverse cultures.

Width

Minimum
Share one music preference (a type of music, a song, or a context they like music in) and give a reason.
Typical
Discuss music preferences with peers and family; identify what music they encounter daily and in what contexts; compile or imagine a playlist that combines favourites from different people or generations; practise active listening (moving with the beat, clapping rhythmic ideas).
Wider
  • Compile a family or community playlist combining music from different generations — discuss what each piece means to the people who love it
  • Investigate how music preferences relate to cultural identity — interview a family member about music important to them and share findings with the class

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates context awareness with personal response — students connect what they know about how music works in different contexts to their own experience of music. The teaching investment is in making the connection between 'music happens in many contexts' and 'I encounter music in specific contexts for specific reasons.'
