Manipulate elements to communicate ideas

AC9AMU4C01 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

manipulate elements of music to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning when composing and practising for performance

Prior knowledge

Students can select and combine elements of music when composing (AC9AMU2C01) and have practised documentation using signs, symbols and recordings. They can combine two elements for expressive effect and justify their selections. They have not yet manipulated elements strategically to communicate specific ideas, perspectives or meaning across a sustained composition.

By the end

Students manipulate elements of music to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning when composing and practising for performance, using notation, documentation or recording to capture their work.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • writing lyrics and/or melodies for songs, raps or chants and using formats such as graphic or staff notation or a lead-sheet or an audio recording to notate/document/record their work
  • using available technologies to create accompaniments; for example, improvising patterns and recording as a loop or using digital tools to notate/document music
  • improvising phrases of music on an instrument to practise known notes and rhythm patterns
  • learning songs and/or instrumental music associated with specific cultures
  • listening to live or recorded performances and gathering ideas they can use in their performance
  • manipulating the elements of music to express different characters or dramatic action when composing music for a screen-based work or to accompany drama
  • combining composed and improvised sections to create a complete work
  • working in pairs or groups to create ostinatos or accompaniments

Waypoints

  • 1. Manipulating vs selecting elementsHingeStudents understand that manipulating elements means actively changing, shaping and controlling them throughout a composition — not just choosing them at the start.
  • 2. Communicating ideas through manipulationHingeStudents can manipulate elements of music specifically to communicate an idea, perspective or meaning — e.g. expressing dramatic action, emotion or character.
  • 3. Notation and documentation for practiceStudents can use notation or documentation to capture their compositions and use this during rehearsal to practise and refine the work.
