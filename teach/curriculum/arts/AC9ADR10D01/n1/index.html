Physical and psychological character preparation

AC9ADR10D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse character to identify both physical and psychological dimensions, and use that analysis to ground performance choices.

Success criteria

  • Student identifies at least two physical and two psychological dimensions of a character through analysis
  • Student demonstrates at least one performance choice grounded in psychological analysis (not just physical imitation)

Prior knowledge

Students can develop performance skills relevant to selected drama styles and forms (Y7-8). They can develop vocal quality, movement skills, and use improvisation to explore character.

Width

Minimum
Student uses text analysis or improvisation to identify at least two physical and two psychological dimensions of a character, and applies these in a short scene.
Typical
Students use analysis of examined text and performances to provide information for exploring the implied and underlying aspects of character; they refine understanding of human behaviours and emotions using appropriate boundaries; they use character analysis to ground voice, physicality and relationship choices.
Wider
  • Students explore how contemporary performance styles and non-realistic conventions can reshape meaning for a character from a classic text
  • Students investigate how comedy forms (physical comedy, satire, irony) require specific physical and psychological approaches distinct from dramatic character work

Hinge

The move from externally-imposed role-playing to internally-grounded character work is the key development at Y9-10; without psychological grounding, physical performance choices are decorative rather than expressive — the character lacks coherence across the full range of dramatic action.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse character analysis frameworks (Stanislavski objectives, given circumstances, subtext) or equivalent structured approaches before performance work; make the interior life explicit before asking students to express it physically.
