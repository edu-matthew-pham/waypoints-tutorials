Creating and co-creating fictional situations

AC9ADR2C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Drama: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create and co-create fictional situations, using non-verbal and verbal communication to establish role and situation with others.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student creates or co-creates a fictional situation in collaboration with at least one other person
  • Student uses non-verbal communication (facial expression, movement, gesture) within the drama
  • Student can describe the role or situation they were exploring

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Drawing on imagination and experience.

Width

Minimum
Student participates in creating a fictional situation with at least one other person, contributing roles or scenarios and communicating within the drama.
Typical
Students contribute ideas and actions within whole-group or small-group improvisations; they communicate non-verbally through facial expressions and movement; they use costume items, props or materials to establish roles and situations; they accept and build on others' contributions.
Wider
  • Students participate in Readers' Theatre, using costume items and props to share information about their character while 'in role'
  • Students co-create dramatic play with teacher-in-role, responding to the teacher's contributions and extending the fictional world
  • Students devise short scenes communicating their feelings about a place or how they care for their community, drawing on exploration of First Nations Australians' drama about Country/Place

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCo-creation integrates the pretence-acceptance and idea-sourcing moves — students must simultaneously contribute, accept, and maintain the fictional world. Teacher scaffolds with process drama strategies and teacher-in-role.
