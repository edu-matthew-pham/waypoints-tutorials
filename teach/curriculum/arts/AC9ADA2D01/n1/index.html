Safe dance awareness

AC9ADA2D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Dance: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe and practise safe dance — being aware of self and others in the space, moving with care, and taking responsibility for their own physical wellbeing.

Success criteria

  • Student consistently maintains awareness of others in the dance space during movement activities
  • Student can describe at least one safe dance practice (e.g. checking space around them, landing softly)

Prior knowledge

Students have used play and imagination to discover movement possibilities at Foundation. They can vary movement in simple ways and are building awareness of their body in space.

Width

Minimum
Consistent demonstration of spatial awareness and care for self and others during movement activities in a defined space.
Typical
Developing responsibility for safe dance practice: awareness of self and others in the space; recognising the boundaries of the dance space; understanding body care (hydration, rest); moving safely in open and crowded spaces; using props such as scarves without endangering others.
Wider
  • Reflect explicitly on moments of safe and unsafe movement — discussing what made movement feel safe or risky and identifying strategies
  • Practise safe movement in varied spatial configurations — small groups, scattered, lines — building adaptable spatial awareness

Hinge

Safe dance awareness is a prerequisite for all physical engagement in dance. Students who do not internalise spatial awareness and body care cannot participate safely in subsequent technical or expressive movement work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSafe practice must become habitual — not a checklist that requires conscious effort. Repeated practice across many sessions builds the automatic awareness and responsibility that safety in dance requires.
