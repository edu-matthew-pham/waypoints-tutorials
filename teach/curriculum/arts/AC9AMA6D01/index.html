Develop production skills for meaning

AC9AMA6D01 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Media arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop media production skills to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning through manipulation of media languages, including images, sounds, texts and/or interactive elements, and media technologies

Prior knowledge

Students can experiment with production techniques and evaluate how changes affect meaning. They can apply Foley sound, storyboarding and image review. They have not yet manipulated media languages across multiple forms to communicate perspectives — or applied genre conventions deliberately to create humour, mood or meaning.

By the end

Students develop media production skills to communicate ideas, perspectives and/or meaning through manipulation of media languages and media technologies.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • experimenting with approaches to capturing and sequencing images and text to create comic books, animations or stop motion videos that retell familiar stories
  • trialling options for applying the conventions of an established genre to an ordinary everyday event in order to create humour
  • using Viewpoints to develop questions when solving problems or making decisions about preferred options
  • identifying story structures and technical or symbolic elements that contribute to the formation of genre in media arts works
  • creating a script for a radio production and documenting the appropriate permissions that may be required for music and voice talents in a real-world community radio setting

Waypoints

  • 1. Manipulating media languages to change meaningHingeStudents can manipulate at least two media language elements (sound, framing, editing, text, augmented environment) and explain how each manipulation changes the meaning or perspective communicated.
  • 2. Production skills across formsStudents can apply production skills across multiple media forms — capturing, sequencing, scripting or documenting with deliberate communicative intent in at least two different task contexts.
