Investigating diversity of First Nations Australian dance

AC9ADA8E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate the diversity of First Nations Australian dance practices across regions and contexts with cultural responsiveness

Success criteria

  • Investigates at least one First Nations Australian dance or practice in genuine depth
  • Explains cultural significance — not just describes what the dance looks like
  • Demonstrates cultural responsiveness throughout

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding ICIP rights in dance contexts.

Width

Minimum
Investigate at least one First Nations Australian dance work or choreographer in depth; explain what makes it culturally significant
Typical
Investigate First Nations Australian dance from at least two different regional or cultural contexts; analyse what is distinctive about each and how ICIP responsibilities apply
Wider
  • Use a map of First Nations Australian language groups to identify where choreographers and performers are working — investigate the geographic and cultural breadth of First Nations dance practice
  • Analyse how linear and non-linear narrative is used in specific First Nations Australian dance works to explore issues of identity, relationships or empathy
  • Investigate a contemporary First Nations Australian choreographer working in contemporary or cross-cultural contexts — analyse how they navigate cultural identity and ICIP responsibilities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly treat First Nations Australian dance as a single tradition rather than understanding the enormous diversity across hundreds of distinct cultural groups, regions, and contemporary/traditional contexts. Multiple distinct investigation contexts — different regions, contemporary vs traditional, cross-cultural — resolve this misconception through genuine breadth.
