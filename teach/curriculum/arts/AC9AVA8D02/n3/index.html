Using reflection to develop visual arts practice over time

AC9AVA8D02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use reflection on influences — their own and other artists' — as a sustained practice that develops over time and produces increasingly informed and distinctive artistic choices

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Shows at least one example where reflection led to a changed practice direction
  • Documentation demonstrates development over time — not a single reflection moment
  • Reflection on influences is connected to increasingly distinctive and informed choices

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Reflecting on own influences and practice choices.

Width

Minimum
Show at least one example where reflection on an influence led to a changed practice direction; document the before/after and explain what changed and why
Typical
Document how practice has developed over a unit as a result of sustained reflection on influences; show how reflection has produced increasingly informed and distinctive choices
Wider
  • Build on understanding of composition to explore multiple approaches to representing space and depth — document how each investigation changes subsequent compositional decisions
  • Develop a personal artistic statement that articulates the influences currently shaping own practice and how those influences are being engaged and adapted
  • Reflect on the work of multiple artists alongside own explorations — document convergences and divergences and explain what they reveal about own developing practice identity

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates influence identification (Node 1), reflective practice cycles (Node 2), and evidence of development over time into a coherent account of own practice development. Students who can reflect on single influences but cannot demonstrate sustained development over a unit have not met the descriptor's demand for reflection that informs practice.
