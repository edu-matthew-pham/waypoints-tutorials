Experimenting with materials and processes

AC9AVA2C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can experiment with a range of materials and visual arts processes to discover what effects are possible.

Success criteria

  • Experiments with at least two different materials or processes
  • Describes at least one effect discovered — what the material or process can do

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What visual conventions are.

Width

Minimum
Try at least two materials or processes; describe one effect discovered.
Typical
Experiment with a range of materials and processes (mark-making, printing, layering, 3D construction, digital tools); explore accidental and intentional effects; reflect on what each material can do.
Wider
  • Explore layering as a visual technique — build a surface using pastels, then watercolour, ink and wax; identify how each layer changes the work
  • Combine digital and analog tools in one experiment — use a drawing app alongside traditional media and compare what each can do

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who experiment with only one or two materials develop limited material vocabulary. A wider range of processes (printing, layering, 3D, digital) reveals that different materials serve different communicative purposes.
