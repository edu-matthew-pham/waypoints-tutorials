Investigating choreographic practice in context

AC9ADA10E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate how and why choreographers and performers work within specific cultural, social, historical and environmental contexts — analysing how context shapes practice.

Success criteria

  • Student connects at least two choreographic choices to the practitioner's contextual position (cultural, social, historical)
  • Student can articulate how context shapes the way a dance communicates or challenges ideas

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate dance works, performers and/or choreographers across cultures, times, places and contexts (Y7-8, AC9ADA8E01). They can analyse element and device use, investigate digital tool influence, and evaluate choreographic intent.

Width

Minimum
Investigating one choreographer or dance practitioner — connecting their contextual position (cultural, historical, social) to at least two identifiable choices in their work.
Typical
Investigating how and why choreographers work with communities to advocate for social justice or sustainable living; exploring specific examples of practitioners working with communities in Australia with cultural links to Asia; analysing how traditions, styles and contexts affect experience and interpretation; extending own movement vocabulary through analysis of others' stylistic preferences.
Wider
  • Investigate a choreographer whose practice is grounded in a specific cultural community — analysing how community context shapes every aspect of the work
  • Compare how two choreographers from different cultural or historical contexts address the same theme — analysing how context determines approach, form and reception

Hinge

Context-driven analysis — understanding why a choreographer made specific choices given their position — is the analytical depth that distinguishes investigating from describing. Students without this move cannot engage critically with cultural diversity or social justice in dance.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkContextual analysis requires an explicit methodology — teach students to move from 'what I see' to 'why this choice, given this context'. Use a structured inquiry model: context → choice → effect → interpretation.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepInvestigating practitioners across genuinely diverse cultural, temporal and geographic contexts builds analytical range and prevents single-tradition dominance of the student's interpretive frame.
