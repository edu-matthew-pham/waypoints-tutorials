Evaluating personal responses with reasons

AC9AMU6E01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate and share personal preferences for music from diverse contexts, using both their own words and music terminology, and giving reasons.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Shares a personal response to a piece using at least one music term
  • Gives at least one reason grounded in a specific musical feature

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How elements are combined in diverse music.

Width

Minimum
Share a preference for or response to one piece of music using at least one music term and give at least one reason grounded in the music.
Typical
Evaluate and discuss personal preferences across music from multiple contexts — leisure music, ensemble music, music important to family or community, music from specific genres; use music terminology to describe what creates the preference; give reasons grounded in the music's features rather than vague impressions.
Wider
  • Compare preferences for music from two different cultural contexts — discuss what each communicates and why one is preferred, using specific terminology
  • Evaluate a piece of game or screen music — describe what the music does for the product and whether it succeeds, using element analysis as evidence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPersonal response becomes analytically meaningful when grounded in specific musical evidence. Build a response framework: 'I like/don't like this piece because [specific musical feature] creates [specific effect]. I know because [what I listened for].' This bridges felt response and analytical observation.
