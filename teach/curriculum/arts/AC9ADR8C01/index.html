Improvise, devise and interpret drama

AC9ADR8C01 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

improvise and devise drama and/or interpret scripted drama, manipulating elements of drama and applying conventions relevant to the style/form

Prior knowledge

Students can use elements of drama and simple conventions in own drama work (AC9ADR6C01). They have not yet selected and manipulated elements with clear rationale for how each serves the intended dramatic action, nor applied style-specific conventions with deliberate intent.

By the end

Students improvise, devise and interpret drama where manipulation of elements and application of conventions is deliberate and connected to intended dramatic action and meaning.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying dramatic potential in starting points and using improvisation and processes such as 'mantle of the expert' to develop situations and characters
  • using play-building to develop scenes as a class response to an inquiry question
  • working collaboratively to interpret scripted drama — using improvisation to explore characters, applying conventions, blocking space
  • using Viewpoints to frame questions to interpret, analyse and evaluate dramatic forms, elements, design and conventions
  • employing voice/vocalisation and movement appropriate to situation, manipulating space and time to heighten tension and shape meaning
  • using feedback, reflection or evaluation to develop and extend ideas when improvising, devising and/or scripting

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying and developing dramatic potentialStudents can identify dramatic potential in a starting point and develop it into dramatic action using elements of drama
  • 2. Manipulating elements and applying conventionsStudents can manipulate elements of drama and apply style-relevant conventions to shape and communicate dramatic action
  • 3. Using feedback to develop and refine dramaStudents can use feedback, reflection and evaluation to develop and extend ideas when improvising, devising or interpreting drama
