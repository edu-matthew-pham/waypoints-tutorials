Identifying and developing dramatic potential

AC9ADR8C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify dramatic potential in a starting point and develop it into dramatic action using elements of drama

Success criteria

  • Identifies dramatic potential in a given starting point
  • Develops it into dramatic action using at least one element deliberately
  • Dramatic action has a clear direction — not just loosely connected scenes

Prior knowledge

Students can use elements of drama and simple conventions in own drama work (AC9ADR6C01). They have not yet selected and manipulated elements with clear rationale for how each serves the intended dramatic action, nor applied style-specific conventions with deliberate intent.

Width

Minimum
Identify dramatic potential in a given starting point (stimulus, text, image, scenario); develop it into a short dramatic action using at least one element of drama deliberately
Typical
Identify dramatic potential in diverse starting points; use improvisation to explore situations, characters and relationships; develop dramatic action using elements of drama with a clear purpose
Wider
  • Use 'mantle of the expert' or play-building to develop dramatic action from an inquiry question — evaluate how the collaborative devising process constrains and enables dramatic choices
  • Respond to the dramatic potential of an image using the questions 'What's happening? What has happened? What's going to happen next?' — develop improvised scenes from each response
  • Identify dramatic potential in research or issues and use improvisation to develop situations and characters that represent multiple perspectives on the issue

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to build a repertoire of starting points — stimulus types, devising approaches, dramatic structures — before they can identify and develop dramatic potential reliably. Each approach (mantle of the expert, image response, play-building, inquiry question) produces different kinds of dramatic material and builds different generative skills.
