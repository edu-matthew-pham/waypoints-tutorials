Social and historical context in artworks

AC9AVA6E01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate how social, historical and environmental concerns are expressed in artworks, and how artworks can develop social awareness.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one social, historical or environmental concern expressed in an artwork
  • Explains how at least one visual choice (convention, material, composition) communicates that concern

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Multiple perspectives on artworks.

Width

Minimum
Identify one social, historical or environmental concern expressed in an artwork; explain how the visual choices communicate it.
Typical
Investigate how Australian and international artists have expressed historical, social and environmental concerns; compare how different artists approach the same concern from different contexts; explore artworks from countries in Asia relevant to class topics.
Wider
  • Explore a range of artworks from different cultural contexts that address sustainability — compare how land art, recycled-material works and climate visualisations each use visual conventions to communicate environmental concern
  • Explore artworks that communicate perspectives on migration to Australia — investigate how artists' backgrounds shape what they emphasise and how they use visual conventions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who examine only Australian artists or only one issue type develop limited understanding of how visual arts function socially. International and cross-cultural comparisons are essential for understanding that the relationship between art and social concern is universal.
