← AC9AVA2D01 Experiment and play with visual conventions and materials

Playing within limits

AC9AVA2D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can work with constraints on materials or processes to focus experimentation and notice what the material can do.

Success criteria

  • Engages with at least one material or process constraint
  • Describes at least one discovery — what worked, what failed, or what could be used again

Prior knowledge

Students can use play and imagination to discover possibilities with materials (AC9AVAFD01). They have begun noticing visual qualities and developing ideas through experimentation. They have not yet deliberately experimented with visual conventions as named elements.

Width

Minimum
Work with one material or process constraint (e.g. only one colour, only folding and tearing) and describe what was discovered.
Typical
Play with a range of constrained experiments (limited materials, limited tools, limited time, silent working); describe discoveries and failures; identify what could be used in a future artwork.
Wider
  • Work in small groups to make something from a surprise bag of everyday materials — compare what different groups discovered about the same materials
  • Set a timed challenge to find as many ways as possible to create lines using a wide range of materials — compare results and discuss what each option is good for

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepConstrained play focuses attention on what materials actually do rather than defaulting to familiar habits. Different constraint types (colour, tool, process) reveal different material properties.
