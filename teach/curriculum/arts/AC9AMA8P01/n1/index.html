Applying responsible media practices in presentation

AC9AMA8P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Media arts: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply copyright, ICIP and ethical obligations when presenting media arts works

Success criteria

  • Identifies and applies at least one responsible practice obligation in presentation
  • Explains why the obligation exists and how it applies
  • Responsible practice is genuinely applied — not just acknowledged

Prior knowledge

Students can present media arts works to an audience (AC9AMA6P01). They have not yet made deliberate presentation decisions about distribution context, audience relationships, copyright/ICIP obligations, or evaluated how different presentation contexts change audience reception.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one responsible media practice obligation relevant to presenting own work; explain how it is applied in the presentation
Typical
Apply copyright, ICIP and ethical obligations when presenting media arts works; explain specific decisions made to comply with responsible practice requirements
Wider
  • Research copyright laws and ICIP rights relevant to specific content in own work — document how permission was obtained or why specific material was not included
  • Develop a presentation plan that explicitly addresses all responsible practice obligations before distribution — evaluate how responsible practice shaped presentation decisions
  • Investigate how media arts makers navigate copyright and ICIP obligations in professional distribution contexts — apply those practices to own work

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly treat copyright and ICIP obligations as administrative hurdles rather than reasoned professional practice. Scaffolding the reasoning — what is the obligation, why does it exist, how does it apply to this specific content in this specific context — builds genuine understanding of responsible practice rather than compliance-as-checklist.
