← AC9AMA8P01 Present media arts works

Considering audience relationships in presentation

AC9AMA8P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Media arts: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make deliberate presentation decisions that consider the potential relationship the work creates with the intended audience

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Makes at least one presentation decision with explicit audience relationship reasoning
  • Explains how the presentation context shapes the audience relationship
  • Presentation is audience-centred — not just platform selection

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Applying responsible media practices in presentation.

Width

Minimum
Make at least one deliberate presentation decision (platform, timing, context) with an explicit consideration of the audience relationship it creates; explain the reasoning
Typical
Plan presentation with deliberate consideration of audience relationships; reflect on how different presentation contexts affect interpretation and engagement; document reasoning
Wider
  • Distribute a media arts work to a specific audience using appropriate platforms — evaluate how the distribution context shapes audience engagement and relationship
  • Present media arts works in multiple contexts (online platform, school exhibition, community screening) — evaluate how each context changes audience interpretation and response
  • Reflect on how relationships develop between media arts makers and audiences across a cohort — evaluate how shared or differentiated audience experiences change reception

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsThinking deliberately about audience relationships in presentation — not just choosing a platform but reasoning through what relationship the presentation creates — is a production practice habit that develops through repeated consideration cycles. Students who present without this reasoning don't develop the audience-centred presentation thinking the descriptor requires.
