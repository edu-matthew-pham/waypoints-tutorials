Developing an idea from exploration

AC9ADRFD01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can extend an initial discovery into a developing idea — asking 'what if?' or 'what next?' to push the play further.

Success criteria

  • Student makes at least one variation or extension on an initial discovery
  • Student can describe or show what they were exploring and one thing they tried

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Exploring through play and materials.

Width

Minimum
Student makes at least one 'what if?' move — e.g. changes a voice quality, adds a movement, or repurposes a material in a new way.
Typical
Students extend their exploratory play by imagining characters, situations or possibilities ('What would happen if...?'); they try at least two variations and choose one to develop further.
Wider
  • Students use inquiry questions about a story or image ('What is happening? What will happen next?') to generate new imaginative directions
  • Students document a step in their making process using a digital photo or drawing, creating a simple record of how the idea developed
  • Students collaborate with a partner to build on each other's ideas in dramatic play

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkTeacher prompts ('What else could happen?', 'What if your character could fly?') scaffold the move from random play to intentional idea-development.
