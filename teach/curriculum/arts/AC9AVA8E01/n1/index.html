Identifying visual conventions and their purpose

AC9AVA8E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify specific visual conventions used in an artwork and describe what role they play in communicating ideas or meaning

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two visual conventions in a given artwork
  • Describes what role each convention plays in communicating meaning — not just names the convention
  • Does not confuse describing what an artwork looks like with explaining how it makes meaning

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how artists use visual conventions and materials to communicate ideas (AC9AVA6E01). They have not yet investigated how cultural, historical and contextual factors shape these choices or analysed how the same subject is represented differently across cultures and times.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two visual conventions in a given artwork and describe what each contributes to the meaning or effect
Typical
Analyse a range of artworks to identify visual conventions and explain how each has been manipulated to communicate specific ideas or perspectives
Wider
  • Compare how two artists working in different media use the same visual convention (e.g. line, colour, composition) to achieve different expressive effects
  • Investigate how a visual convention carries different meanings in different cultural contexts — e.g. how white is used in Western vs East Asian funerary traditions
  • Analyse how a First Nations Australian artist uses visual conventions that carry cultural knowledge — investigate what those conventions mean within their cultural context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a range of visual convention examples before they can analyse how manipulation of conventions produces meaning. Exposure to multiple artworks, media and cultural contexts builds the visual literacy needed for genuine analysis rather than surface description.
