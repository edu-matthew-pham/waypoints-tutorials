Investigate conventions and meaning

AC9AVA8E01 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate ways that visual conventions, visual arts processes and materials are manipulated to represent ideas, perspectives and/or meaning in artworks created across cultures, times, places and/or other contexts

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how artists use visual conventions and materials to communicate ideas (AC9AVA6E01). They have not yet investigated how cultural, historical and contextual factors shape these choices or analysed how the same subject is represented differently across cultures and times.

By the end

Students investigate how visual conventions, processes and materials are manipulated to represent ideas, perspectives and meaning across cultures, times, places and contexts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating arts works by First Nations Australian artists that cross over multiple artforms, analysing how the arts forms come together
  • researching the ways that artists from different times and places have represented a particular subject or theme
  • analysing the ways that artists represent subject matter and ideas in their artworks when developing ideas for own representations
  • creating written accounts such as catalogue entries or reviews that discuss how artists have shown their ideas
  • investigating and trialling techniques and visual arts processes used by artists, thinking about how processes have changed over time
  • exploring the ways that artists are inspired and influenced by the practice of artists from other countries or cultures

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying visual conventions and their purposeStudents can identify specific visual conventions used in an artwork and describe what role they play in communicating ideas or meaning
  • 2. Investigating across cultures and contextsStudents can investigate how visual conventions and processes are shaped by cultural, historical and contextual factors and explain how these factors influence meaning-making
  • 3. Connecting investigation to own practiceStudents can use their investigation of other artists' use of visual conventions to inform decisions in their own visual arts practice
