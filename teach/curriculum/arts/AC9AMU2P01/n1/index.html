Following agreed performance decisions

AC9AMU2P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can follow agreed decisions about how music should sound during a performance (e.g. using singing voice, performing at the agreed tempo, holding instruments correctly).

Success criteria

  • Follows at least two agreed performance decisions during a group performance
  • Can identify whether they met the agreed decisions when reflecting after the performance

Prior knowledge

Students can share arts works with audiences (AC9AMUFP01), understand the audience relationship, and have practised basic music performance skills (staying in time, using singing voice). They have not yet performed with formal introductions, staging decisions or post-performance reflection.

Width

Minimum
Follow at least two agreed performance decisions (e.g. use singing voice AND perform at the agreed tempo) in a group performance.
Typical
Follow a full set of agreed performance decisions across singing and instrumental music — using correct voice type, agreed tempo, agreed dynamics, correct instrument hold; begin to self-monitor whether they are meeting the agreed decisions during performance.
Wider
  • Discuss which performance decisions are most important for the audience experience — prioritise and explain choices
  • Practise a section of a performance specifically focused on one agreed decision, then perform the whole piece and evaluate whether the decision was maintained

Hinge

Following agreed decisions is the foundational collective skill of ensemble performance. Students who cannot subordinate personal impulse to group agreement cannot function in ensemble contexts, which underpins all performance work in Y3-4 and beyond. This is also where performance as a social and communicative act — not just individual music-making — is first established.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAgreed performance decisions need to be established early in rehearsal and reinforced repeatedly before performance. Consolidation here means using rehearsal time to practise the specific decisions — stop, name the decision, try it again — rather than running through the piece from start to finish.
