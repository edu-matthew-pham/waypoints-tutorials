Introducing a performance

AC9AMU2P01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can introduce a performance by sharing information about the music — including at least the title and one piece of contextual information (e.g. who composed it, when or why it was created).

Success criteria

  • Contributes to or delivers an introduction that includes the title and at least one piece of contextual information
  • Can explain why introducing a performance is useful for the audience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Staging and presentation.

Width

Minimum
Introduce a performance with at least a title and one piece of contextual information about the music.
Typical
Write or contribute to a spoken or visual introduction to a performance sharing: title, composer, and where/when/why the music was composed; deliver or display the introduction before or during the performance.
Wider
  • Create a projected slide introduction for a performance — include title, composer, context, and one personal reflection from the performers
  • Introduce a performance by connecting the music to its cultural context — share one thing about where this music comes from and why it matters

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkIntroducing a performance is a significant step from Foundation sharing — it requires students to frame their work in context for an audience. Support with sentence starters and a simple introduction template: 'The name of our piece is... It was composed by... We chose it because...'
