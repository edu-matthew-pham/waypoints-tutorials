Staging and presentation

AC9AMU2P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make and justify at least one decision about how to position themselves in a performance space.

Success criteria

  • Can give at least one reason for a staging choice (related to seeing cues, hearing others, or audience visibility)
  • Contributes to a staging decision in a group performance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Following agreed performance decisions.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one reason why where they stand/sit during a performance matters (e.g. to see the conductor, to hear others, for the audience to see them).
Typical
Contribute to decisions about how/where to sit/stand in a performance space — consider whether curved or straight rows allow them to see cues, whether standing or sitting affects their ability to perform accurately; try different options and discuss which works better.
Wider
  • Try two different staging arrangements for the same performance and compare: which made it easier to see cues? Which looked better for the audience?
  • Discuss how the size and shape of a performance space (classroom vs hall vs outdoors) changes staging decisions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStaging decisions are a concrete, discussable case of performance planning. Use guided questions: 'Can you see the teacher/conductor from there?', 'Can the audience see you?', 'Can you hear the other performers?' This builds the habit of thinking about performance holistically rather than just about the music itself.
