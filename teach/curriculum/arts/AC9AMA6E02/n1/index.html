Media arts for cultural continuity

AC9AMA6E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how and why First Nations Australian media arts works are critical for sharing, maintaining, continuing and revitalising cultures.

Success criteria

  • Student can explain at least one way a First Nations Australian media arts work contributes to sharing, maintaining or revitalising culture
  • Student grounds their explanation in specific features of the work (not general statements about First Nations culture)

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how First Nations Australians use media arts to communicate connection to and responsibility for Country/Place, and can compare multiple works across forms. They have not yet explored how media arts works are used specifically to continue and revitalise cultures — or investigated how cultural expressions change over time.

Width

Minimum
Explore one First Nations Australian media arts work and explain at least one specific way it contributes to sharing, maintaining or revitalising culture.
Typical
Explore how and why First Nations Australian cultural expressions and media arts works are critical for cultural continuity; investigate specific examples using community-published resources; discuss what is communicated and what would be lost if these works were not made; engage with accredited First Nations Australian resources.
Wider
  • Using resources created or co-created by First Nations Australian organisations, investigate how and why cultural expressions are shared and maintained through media arts — discussing specific examples and what would be at risk if these forms were not continued
  • Explore how First Nations Australian media arts works created for different purposes (language maintenance, ceremonial recording, community storytelling, popular culture) each contribute differently to cultural continuity

Hinge

Understanding the cultural continuity function of First Nations Australian media arts is the conceptual foundation for this standard — without it, analysis of specific representations (identity, visibility, change over time) has no grounding.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need guided inquiry with community-published resources to understand cultural continuity — Viewpoints questions help: 'What do I already know about this issue?', 'Is there more to this story than is being presented?'
