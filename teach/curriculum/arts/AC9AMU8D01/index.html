Develop listening and performance skills

AC9AMU8D01 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop and practise listening/aural skills and vocal and/or instrumental skills/techniques for manipulating elements of music to achieve expressive effects

Prior knowledge

Students can develop listening/aural skills and skills for manipulating elements of music to achieve expressive effects when composing, singing and playing instruments (AC9AMU6D01). They have not yet focused on documenting their developing skills, experimenting with digital tools for arranging, or extending and varying harmonic, rhythmic and melodic patterns systematically.

By the end

Students develop and practise listening/aural skills and vocal/instrumental techniques for manipulating elements of music to achieve expressive effects, documenting their ideas and progress as a reference for performance and composition.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • experimenting with ways in which vocal/instrumental techniques can be used to manipulate elements of music to achieve intended effects, such as an expressive sound that is typical of a particular style, and transcribing or documenting their ideas as a reference for future performances or composition
  • practising technical and expressive skills such as intonation, pitch/rhythmic accuracy, phrasing or shaping dynamics and articulation to communicate expressive intent using voices and/or instruments
  • developing techniques for extending/varying patterns such as harmonic, rhythmic or melodic patterns; for example, identifying qualities of chords in isolation and experimenting with different ways to order the chords to create a chord progression and improvising over the progression, or using the chord progression in a song or instrumental music
  • experimenting with ways in which digital tools can be used when arranging musical ideas or creating effects; for example, using digitally produced parts alone or in combination with voice/vocalisation or other instruments
  • developing ability to manipulate and control elements of music to enhance performance and communicate intentions; for example, recording alternative approaches to interpreting a phrase or melody and evaluating it to identify preferred options
  • using a listening diary/journal format to document ideas for compositions, interpretations, re-imaginings or performances
  • writing/notating a chart or lead-sheet to record ideas from a songwriting workshop or using software to write additional parts such as a bassline to accompany a melody

Waypoints

  • 1. Listening with intent — aural skill developmentStudents can use focused listening to identify how elements of music are being manipulated and how this creates expressive effects
  • 2. Technical and expressive performance skillsStudents can practise technical and expressive performance skills including intonation, pitch/rhythmic accuracy, phrasing, dynamics and articulation to communicate expressive intent
  • 3. Extending and varying patternsStudents can extend and vary harmonic, rhythmic or melodic patterns, including developing chord progressions and improvising over them
  • 4. Using digital tools in practiceStudents can experiment with digital tools to arrange musical ideas or create effects, using digitally produced parts alone or in combination with voice or acoustic instruments
  • 5. Documenting practice and intentionsStudents can document their musical ideas, practice progress and performance or compositional intentions in a format useful for future reference
