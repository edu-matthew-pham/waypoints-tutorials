Technical and expressive performance skills

AC9AMU8D01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can practise technical and expressive performance skills including intonation, pitch/rhythmic accuracy, phrasing, dynamics and articulation to communicate expressive intent

Success criteria

  • Practises at least one technical or expressive skill with an identified intent
  • Can describe what changed through practice and what remains to work on

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Listening with intent — aural skill development.

Width

Minimum
Practise at least one technical or expressive skill (e.g. intonation, phrasing, dynamic control) with a focus on communicating expressive intent; identify what improved and what remains to work on
Typical
Practise multiple technical and expressive skills in combination; use deliberate practice strategies to address specific weaknesses; evaluate progress against intended expressive outcomes
Wider
  • Record alternative approaches to interpreting a phrase or melody and evaluate each to identify which best communicates the intended expressive effect — explain what the ear notices that playing alone doesn't reveal
  • Experiment with different ways to control tone and volume in solo and ensemble contexts — evaluate how balance and blend affect the expressive character of the music

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTechnical and expressive performance skills require deliberate, sustained practice with feedback — this is where the cognitive work is repetitive and iterative by design. Breadth of skills matters, but the learning mechanism is practice-and-evaluate, not exposure to more contexts.
