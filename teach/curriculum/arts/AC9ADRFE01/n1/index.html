Noticing arts in life and community

AC9ADRFE01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation The Arts · Drama: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify arts works they have experienced in their lives and describe what they noticed.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student names at least one arts experience from their own life
  • Student can say something (however simple) about what they noticed or felt

Prior knowledge

No formal prior Drama curriculum. Students have personal experience of arts in their lives — songs, stories, performances — but have not yet been prompted to consider why people make and share arts works.

Width

Minimum
Student names at least one arts experience from their life (song, story, dance, game) and says something about it.
Typical
Students identify arts works and experiences from school, home and community (songs, animations, dances, drama); they share ideas and feelings about those works with peers and teachers.
Wider
  • Students explore arts from different cultures in their community — including cultural dances, songs or stories — noticing how these carry different meanings for different people
  • Students engage with First Nations Australians' perspectives on why the arts matter for connecting to people, culture and Country/Place, using resources created or co-created by First Nations Australians

Hinge

If students cannot yet identify or recall an arts experience, they have no grounded example from which to explore the 'why'; the second node — understanding purpose and meaning — cannot proceed without a concrete referent.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCast the net widely: songs, animations, games, performances at community events all count. Students who don't recognise formal theatre often strongly identify with games or songs — breadth of examples makes this accessible to all.
