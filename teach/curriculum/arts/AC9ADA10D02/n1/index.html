Analytical reflection using Viewpoints

AC9ADA10D02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Dance: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use Viewpoints-based evaluation to analyse element and device use in their own and others' work — connecting analysis to specific choreographic or performance choices.

Success criteria

  • Student uses at least two Viewpoints questions to analyse specific element or device use in a dance work
  • Student connects the analysis to a specific choreographic or performance decision

Prior knowledge

Students can reflect on own and others' dance works and/or practices to inform choreographic choices and use of technical and expressive skills (Y7-8, AC9ADA8D02). They can use journals and peer feedback as reflective tools.

Width

Minimum
Using at least two Viewpoints questions to reflect analytically on a specific aspect of own or another's work — connecting analysis to at least one decision.
Typical
Using Viewpoints to reflect on and evaluate forms and elements: 'How can I use space and dynamics to convey my ideas?'; evaluating choreographic and performance choices ('How successful were the dancers in conveying the stated intent?', 'What was the impact of choosing gesture to communicate emotion?'); documenting in reflective records.
Wider
  • Maintain a systematic reflective journal across an extended choreographic project — analysing key decision points and their outcomes
  • Analyse a professional dance work for element and device use — applying the same analytical frame to their own choreographic process

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkAnalytical reflection — not just impressionistic response — requires a consistent evaluative framework. Viewpoints questions provide this frame; students need practice using them analytically before using them generatively.
