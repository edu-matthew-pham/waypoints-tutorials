Applying musculoskeletal knowledge to safe technique

AC9ADA10C02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Dance: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply knowledge of the musculoskeletal system to execute movement techniques safely and prevent injury.

Success criteria

  • Student can explain how at least one musculoskeletal principle applies to the safe execution of a specific technique
  • Student consistently applies safe practice principles when executing demanding techniques

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Genre/style-specific technique application.

Width

Minimum
Explaining at least one musculoskeletal principle relevant to a technique they are practising; applying it to modify or refine execution.
Typical
Applying knowledge of the structure and function of the musculoskeletal system to execute movements safely; preventing injury to self and others; connecting anatomical understanding to technique decisions in a specific genre/style.
Wider
  • Analyse a physically demanding technique in their chosen style through an anatomical lens — identifying injury risks and safe practice modifications
  • Research how professional dance practitioners in their chosen style approach injury prevention and recovery — applying this knowledge to their own practice

Hinge

At Y9-10, technical demands increase significantly and students work with genre-specific technique that carries real injury risk if executed without anatomical awareness. Students who lack this knowledge cannot safely pursue the level of technical challenge expected.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkAnatomical knowledge requires explicit instruction — it does not arise naturally from movement practice alone. Teach relevant musculoskeletal concepts in direct connection to the techniques being practised, not as standalone science content.
