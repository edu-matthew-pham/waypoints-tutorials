Investigating others' dance works as influence

AC9ADA8D02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how other dancers' and choreographers' use of elements, devices and techniques influences own practice choices

Success criteria

  • Investigates at least one example of another artist's use of an element or technique
  • Explains how the example informs a specific choice in own practice
  • Does not reduce influence to 'inspiration' — connects specific observation to specific decision

Prior knowledge

Students can use teacher feedback to reflect on own dance practice (AC9ADA6D02). They have not yet systematically investigated others' dance works as a source of influence, or used reflection to document and direct practice development over time.

Width

Minimum
Investigate at least one example of how another dancer or choreographer uses an element of dance or style-specific technique; explain how this informs a specific choice in own practice
Typical
Document a range of examples of how others use elements of dance or style-specific techniques in a journal; explain how each example informs choices in own choreography or performance
Wider
  • Develop a personal archive of influences — journal entries documenting how specific examples of others' work open new directions in own practice
  • Investigate how two choreographers with contrasting approaches to the same element of dance make different expressive choices — analyse what each reveals and use the comparison to sharpen own approach
  • Research a choreographer whose work has directly influenced a dance style and trace the chain of influence into contemporary practice

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to build a personal repertoire of influences — documented examples of how others use elements and techniques — before reflection on influences can become a genuine practice habit. A single example is insufficient; accumulated references build the vocabulary for informed choreographic decision-making.
