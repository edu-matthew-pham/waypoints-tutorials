Develop performance skills for drama

AC9ADR8D01 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

develop performance skills relevant to selected drama styles and/or forms

Prior knowledge

Students can use voice and movement to communicate in drama (AC9ADR6D01). They have not yet developed skills systematically through deliberate practice, applied style-specific conventions, or used improvisation to extend technique.

By the end

Students develop vocal and physical performance skills relevant to selected drama styles and forms, using improvisation and deliberate practice to extend technique.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • developing vocal qualities of projection, clarity and contrast through control of pace, pitch, dynamics and use of pause and silence
  • developing use of the body to communicate through movement, stillness, realistic and non-realistic movement; exploring ways to transition between scenes
  • experimenting with ways to use conventions associated with a specific style/form
  • experimenting with elements of drama and stylistic conventions to create, shape and sustain dramatic action through improvisation and basic devising
  • using improvisation to explore techniques associated with specific styles to heighten and release tension
  • experimenting with ways to manipulate elements and performance skills to communicate status/power relationships
  • understanding the importance of and applying respectful relationships and empathy when developing roles and characters

Waypoints

  • 1. Developing vocal performance skillsStudents can develop vocal skills — projection, clarity, pace, pitch, dynamics, pause — with deliberate intent to communicate in drama
  • 2. Developing physical performance skillsStudents can develop physical skills — movement, stillness, gesture, use of space — to communicate relationships, status and meaning in drama
  • 3. Applying style-specific conventions through improvisationStudents can experiment with conventions associated with specific drama styles/forms using improvisation to develop technique
