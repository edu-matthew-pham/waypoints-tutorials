Identifying elements and devices in dance works

AC9ADA8E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify elements of dance and choreographic devices in a given work and describe how each contributes to the meaning communicated

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two elements or devices in a given work
  • Describes how each contributes to meaning — not just names the element
  • Does not confuse describing what a dance looks like with explaining how it communicates

Prior knowledge

Students can identify how elements of dance and choreographic devices are used to communicate ideas (AC9ADA6E01). They have not yet investigated how cultural, historical and contextual factors shape these choices across diverse dance traditions.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two elements of dance or choreographic devices in a given work; describe how each contributes to the meaning or effect
Typical
Analyse a range of dance works to identify elements and devices; explain how specific choices communicate intended ideas or perspectives
Wider
  • Compare how the same element of dance (e.g. repetition, use of space) is used in two works from different genres or cultural contexts — analyse what the comparison reveals about how meaning is made differently
  • Use Viewpoints questions to investigate choreographic intention — 'What was the choreographer's intention?', 'What ideas did the dance express?' — and evaluate whether the choreographic choices achieve the intention
  • Analyse how a single realistic movement can be manipulated from representational to symbolic — investigate examples across different dance traditions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a repertoire of dance works and the elements/devices used within them before they can reason about how context shapes meaning-making choices. A single example gives too narrow a lens for genuine comparative analysis across cultures and times.
