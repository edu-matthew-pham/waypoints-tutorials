Choreographing and refining a sequence

AC9ADA2C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Dance: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can choreograph a dance sequence with a beginning, middle and end, using the elements of dance, and can reflect on and refine it using peer/teacher feedback.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a dance sequence with a beginning, middle and end
  • Student can identify at least two element choices in their sequence
  • Student can describe what their sequence communicates and incorporate at least one piece of feedback

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Improvising and selecting movement.

Width

Minimum
A choreographed sequence with clear start and end; at least two identifiable element choices; can describe what the sequence is communicating.
Typical
A dance sequence that represents an idea (weather, place, story, music) using multiple element choices; devised starting shape, locomotor movement through space, and ending position; combined with a partner or group to extend the sequence; feedback sought and used to refine.
Wider
  • Choreograph a sequence that communicates care for a place that is important to the student — using element choices to express relationship and responsibility
  • Devise a collaborative sequence where each person's contribution is identifiable — using contrast and connection between individual parts

Hinge

Completing a choreographed sequence — with intentional element use and reflective refinement — is the prerequisite for all subsequent Creating strand work. Students who have not produced a whole sequence lack the foundational production experience.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierChoreographing integrates element knowledge (N1) with improvised material (N2) into a structured, communicative whole. Scaffold with structural prompts: 'How will it begin?', 'How do you move through the space?', 'How does it end?'
