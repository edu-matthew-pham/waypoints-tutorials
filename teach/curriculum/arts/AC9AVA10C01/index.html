Evaluate critical feedback in visual arts practice

AC9AVA10C01 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

evaluate critical feedback when planning, developing and refining their visual arts practice

Prior knowledge

Students have created planned artworks using coordinated visual choices and can explain their decisions (AC9AVA8C01/AC9AVA8C02, implied from progression). They have engaged with peer and teacher feedback at an informal level. They have not yet systematically evaluated feedback, tracked its influence on decision-making, or distinguished between seeking impressions and confirming intended responses.

By the end

Students evaluate critical feedback from teachers, peers and audiences when planning, developing and refining their visual arts practice — documenting how feedback influences decisions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • uncovering meaning, interpretations or reactions from audiences by seeking impressions without confirming or leading the audience towards intended responses
  • tracking success using self-assessment and reflection guided by questions based on Viewpoints in order to solve creative challenges
  • experimenting with and evaluating ways of structuring ideas in sketches, studies, maquettes, annotated digital images or journal entries
  • evaluating and responding to feedback on works in process from teachers and peers, in discussions or reviews, and documenting their decisions and responses in written, oral or multimedia, physical or digital journals/diaries
  • collaborating with other students, groups or agencies, such as community groups, to generate ideas for artworks on design or artistic projects for curated exhibitions or events

Waypoints

  • 1. Seeking impressions without leadingHingeStudents can seek feedback in a way that uncovers genuine audience impressions rather than confirming their own intended response.
  • 2. Evaluating and responding to feedbackStudents can evaluate feedback from teachers and peers and document how it influences their decisions — accepting, adapting or consciously setting aside feedback with reasoning.
  • 3. Structuring ideas and tracking developmentStudents can use structured documentation (sketches, maquettes, annotated digital images, journals) to experiment with ways of developing ideas and to track the evolution of a work.
