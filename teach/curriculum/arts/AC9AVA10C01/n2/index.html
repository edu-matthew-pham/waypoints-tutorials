Evaluating and responding to feedback

AC9AVA10C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate feedback from teachers and peers and document how it influences their decisions — accepting, adapting or consciously setting aside feedback with reasoning.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least one piece of feedback on a work in progress
  • Documents a decision made in response — including whether it was accepted, adapted or set aside, with a reason

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Seeking impressions without leading.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least one piece of feedback on a work in progress; document one decision made in response to it — including whether they accepted, adapted or set it aside and why.
Typical
Evaluate and respond to feedback from multiple sources across multiple works; document decisions and reasoning in written, oral or multimedia journal; track changes made as a result of feedback; use self-assessment with Viewpoints questions alongside external feedback.
Wider
  • Collaborate with a community group or external agency to generate ideas and receive feedback on an artwork project — evaluate how external feedback differs from peer/teacher feedback
  • Develop a systematic reflective process — use Viewpoints questions to self-assess work in progress alongside external feedback and document the relationship between the two

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating feedback requires weighing its validity against intentions — students need a reasoning framework (Does this align with my intent? Is this a perception issue or an execution issue? Should I change the work or my communication of it?) rather than simply accepting all feedback.
