Evaluate exhibits and curate exhibitions

AC9AVA10P01 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Visual arts: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

evaluate art exhibits to inform the curation and exhibition of their own and/or others' artworks and/or visual arts practice

Prior knowledge

Students can prepare artworks for formal display, create exhibition materials and make deliberate presentation decisions for different audiences (AC9AVA8P01, implied from progression). They have produced artist statements and curated class exhibitions. They have not yet evaluated existing exhibitions as a research base for their own curatorial decisions, or planned exhibitions that communicate a broader curatorial argument.

By the end

Students evaluate art exhibitions to inform the curation and exhibition of their own and/or others' artworks and visual arts practice.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating ways in which artworks are displayed and how artists and curators work together to create a visual narrative
  • selecting and presenting their own artworks for exhibitions in class, in the school or in the wider community
  • planning and curating exhibitions of their own work, community installations, projects or imagined exhibitions of artworks by other artists, considering, as appropriate, Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property rights
  • developing material to accompany exhibitions, such as didactic panels, artists' or curatorial statements, branding, advertising or press statements
  • visiting and evaluating exhibitions of work in physical or virtual galleries, museums or in community sites
  • developing responses to exhibitions of work; for example, in written/oral/multimedia explanations or reviews
  • constructing explanations of how artists, designers, architects or craftspeople communicate ideas and meaning in artworks

Waypoints

  • 1. Evaluating exhibitionsHingeStudents can visit or investigate exhibitions (physical or virtual) and evaluate the curatorial and presentational choices made — analysing how these choices create meaning and communicate artists' intentions.
  • 2. Planning and curating exhibitionsStudents can plan and curate an exhibition of their own or others' work — making deliberate decisions about selection, arrangement, sequence and spatial narrative.
  • 3. Exhibition materials and curatorial voiceStudents can develop exhibition materials — didactic panels, artist statements, branding, press statements — that communicate the curatorial argument and serve the audience.
