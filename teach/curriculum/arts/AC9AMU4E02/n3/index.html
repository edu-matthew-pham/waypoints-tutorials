← AC9AMU4E02 First Nations music and country/place

Performance protocols and audience responsibilities

AC9AMU4E02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the expectations and responsibilities of performers and audiences when experiencing First Nations Australian music that communicates connection to Country/Place.

Success criteria

  • Can describe at least one protocol or expectation relevant to a First Nations Australian performance context
  • Can explain why that protocol exists in relation to the music's cultural significance

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Learning from First Nations Australians.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one protocol or expectation relevant to a specific context of First Nations Australian music performance (e.g. a Welcome to Country).
Typical
Learn about expectations and requirements for both performers and audiences in contexts where First Nations Australian music communicates connection to Country/Place — music for Welcome to Country, ceremonial music, community events; discuss why these expectations exist and what they protect.
Wider
  • Compare the expectations for a Welcome to Country with those for a school concert — discuss why the responsibilities are different and why those differences are important
  • Investigate what it means to engage respectfully as an audience with culturally significant music — identify two or three specific behaviours and explain why each matters

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierProtocol knowledge integrates the conceptual understanding of connection to Country/Place with practical, respectful engagement. Students who understand why protocols exist are more likely to follow them authentically than those who follow rules without understanding their cultural basis.
