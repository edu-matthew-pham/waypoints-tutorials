Imagining possibilities

AC9AMAFD01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Media arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use imagination to extend what they observe or know — asking 'what if?' questions to develop new ideas for arts works.

Success criteria

  • Student can use at least one imagining strategy to extend a story, character or situation beyond what was given
  • Student can describe the new possibility they imagined

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Playing to discover.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one imagining strategy (asking 'What happens next?', taking the point of view of a character, or imagining an environment) to extend an observed story or situation.
Typical
Use imagination to explore how characters in stories might look, move or speak; make still or moving images to communicate ideas about characters; ask questions of stories to develop new scenarios; improvise rhythms, movements or melodies and interpret them visually.
Wider
  • Imagine and trial ways to use geometric and organic shapes creatively — collage, movement traces of shapes, or melodies that follow a shape's contour; exploring multiple representations of the same shape
  • Use a camera to document a process (e.g. mixing colours, building a structure) — sequence and annotate the images as a record of imaginative problem-solving
  • Explore speaking and singing voices to discover new ways to communicate ideas and feelings — experimenting with pitch, volume, rhythm and tone

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuild students' repertoire of imagining strategies — 'What happens next?', character perspective, environment transformation. A wider repertoire means more generative possibilities when making.
