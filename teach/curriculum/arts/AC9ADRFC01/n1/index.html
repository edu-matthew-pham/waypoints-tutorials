← AC9ADRFC01 Create arts works that communicate ideas

Ideas from imagination and experience

AC9ADRFC01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Drama: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify an idea from play, a story or personal experience to express through making.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can name or show (through action or words) what their drama or artwork is about
  • Student's making has a recognisable intent — it is not purely random activity

Prior knowledge

No formal prior Drama curriculum. Students bring imaginative play, storytelling through objects and movement, and early experience using voice, gesture and repurposed materials to express ideas.

Width

Minimum
One example: a student names or shows what their dramatic play or object manipulation is about.
Typical
Students draw on stories, characters or personal experience to identify what they want to make and communicate; they can describe their idea simply to a partner or teacher.
Wider
  • Students explore multiple potential ideas across different starting points (e.g. a story, an object, a place) before choosing one to develop
  • Students ask 'what if' questions to extend a familiar story into a new scenario

Hinge

Without a communicative intent — even a simple one — students produce activity rather than art-making; the next node (choosing ways to express the idea) has no anchor.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse teacher-in-role or guided questioning ('What's happening? What happens next?') to help students find and name an idea before they begin making.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepOffer a range of imaginative entry points — objects, boxes, puppets, images — so students who don't connect with story-based prompts can engage through material play.
