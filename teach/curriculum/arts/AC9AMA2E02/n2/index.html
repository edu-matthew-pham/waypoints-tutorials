Elements and cultural communication

AC9AMA2E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify at least one visual, audio or interactive element in a First Nations Australian media arts work and describe what cultural knowledge it communicates.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify at least one visual, audio or interactive element in a First Nations Australian media arts work
  • Student can describe what cultural knowledge that element communicates (using community resources or accredited materials to ground the description)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: First Nations media arts works.

Width

Minimum
Identify one element (colour, symbol, pattern, sound, or image) in a First Nations Australian media arts work and describe what it communicates about culture, identity or story.
Typical
Observe and describe how visual, audio and/or interactive elements (colours, symbols, patterns, sounds, voices, language) are used in First Nations Australian media arts works to communicate cultural knowledge; work with community representatives or accredited resources to understand the significance of specific elements.
Wider
  • Investigate how colours, symbols and patterns are used in First Nations Australian media arts works — working with community representatives or accessing resources created or co-created by First Nations Australians to understand their cultural significance
  • Explore media arts works created by First Nations Australians that communicate cultural narratives — comparing how the same story or knowledge is communicated through different media elements in different works

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need explicit scaffolding to connect observable elements (colour, symbol, sound) to cultural meaning — this requires guided inquiry with community resources or accredited materials, not independent interpretation.
