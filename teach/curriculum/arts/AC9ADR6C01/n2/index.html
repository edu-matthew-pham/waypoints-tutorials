Shaping dramatic action across forms

AC9ADR6C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Drama: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can shape dramatic action — developing situation, tension and resolution — across improvised, devised and/or scripted forms.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student shapes a dramatic sequence with a recognisable dramatic arc (beginning, complication, resolution)
  • Student demonstrates sustained dramatic action across a complete piece in at least one form
  • Student can articulate what idea, perspective or meaning the drama is communicating

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Developing character.

Width

Minimum
Student shapes a dramatic sequence with an identifiable beginning, complication and resolution across one form.
Typical
Students shape and sustain dramatic action across improvised, devised and/or scripted drama; they use rehearsal, interaction and negotiation with others; they use props, costumes and available technologies (light, sound, multimedia) as dramatic symbols; they develop drama for school rituals or community events with a specific purpose.
Wider
  • Students devise drama based on research about a school or local community project, using drama to present different perspectives in a debate or issue
  • Students explore how a devised drama for a specific community audience (e.g. school assembly, cultural ceremony) requires adapting form and content to purpose
  • Students use Viewpoints to frame questions about their own drama: 'How did the performers use elements effectively?', 'What was the purpose?'

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierShaping sustained dramatic action across forms is the integration point — character, situation, tension, purpose and audience awareness all converge. Structured reflection and feedback during rehearsal is the key scaffold.
