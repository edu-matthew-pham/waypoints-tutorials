Taking on role in dramatic play

AC9ADR2D01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can take on a role purposefully in dramatic play or process drama, developing ideas through that role.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student takes on a named or implied role and sustains it for the duration of a short dramatic play sequence
  • Student makes at least one decision or contribution from within the role

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding the elements of drama.

Width

Minimum
Student takes on a specific role in dramatic play and makes decisions from within that role.
Typical
Students take on roles in dramatic play focusing on creating situations; they stay in role when participating in improvised drama; they develop ideas from texts (children's literature, images) into character and situation.
Wider
  • Students explore a range of role types — from characters in familiar stories to imagined roles in invented scenarios — and consider how different roles produce different perspectives
  • Students participate in process drama with teacher-in-role, accepting and responding to the teacher's character within the fictional world

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsStaying in role requires repeated practice; students need multiple short experiences of role-taking before they can sustain it through a more complex dramatic sequence.
