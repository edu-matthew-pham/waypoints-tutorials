Understanding the elements of drama

AC9ADR2D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Drama: developing practices and skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name and recognise the basic elements of drama — role, place, situation, language, movement — in drama they experience and create.

Success criteria

  • Student correctly uses at least two drama element terms when discussing drama
  • Student can identify an element of drama (e.g. role, place) in a drama they have participated in or observed

Prior knowledge

Students can use play and imagination to discover possibilities and develop ideas (Foundation). They can engage in purposeful exploration of materials, voice and movement.

Width

Minimum
Student can use at least two drama element terms appropriately when describing their own or others' drama.
Typical
Students understand and use appropriate drama terminology (role, place, situation, language, movement, dramatic play, process drama, pretending, audience, theatre, performance, actors) when discussing drama they have made and/or experienced.
Wider
  • Students explore how elements combine differently in different forms — comparing dramatic play to a class performance — and name what changes
  • Students use props from familiar contexts (empty containers, clothing, toys) to explore how physical objects create or signal role and place

Hinge

If students lack element vocabulary and conceptual awareness, they cannot intentionally use elements in their drama-making — subsequent nodes about taking roles and developing situations lack a shared language.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExplicitly introduce each element with concrete examples from drama students have just experienced; use questioning ('What role were you playing? What was the place?') to make the elements visible.
