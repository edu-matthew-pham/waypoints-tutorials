Interpret music

AC9AMU8C01 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret music in a variety of forms and/or styles, manipulating elements of music and employing relevant vocal/instrumental techniques

Prior knowledge

Students can manipulate elements of music and use compositional devices to communicate ideas when composing and practising music for performance (AC9AMU6C01). They can notate, document and/or record music they compose. They have not yet focused on interpretation of existing works across a variety of forms and styles, nor on deliberately employing style-specific vocal/instrumental techniques to realise stylistic features.

By the end

Students interpret music in a variety of forms and/or styles by manipulating elements of music and employing relevant vocal/instrumental techniques to realise stylistic features and achieve intended expressive effects.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating and trialling ways of realising stylistic features when rehearsing; for example, using effects such as distortion, decay or delay, or using accent/stress to achieve a stylistic feel when interpreting melodies or rhythmic patterns
  • interpreting music that is notated/documented in a range of formats, such as graphic or interactive scores, staff notation or tablature (TAB) and recordings; for example, using a recording and a chart that includes melody, form and harmony/chords to develop an arrangement of a song
  • using student and teacher set goals as a focus for practising and rehearsing a range of solo and/or ensemble music
  • manipulating sound quality by applying understanding of the range of sounds/timbres that different instruments and voice types can produce; for example, manipulating dynamics and timbre in voice or acoustic or digital instruments to create a specific effect

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying stylistic featuresStudents can identify the stylistic features of a piece of music that need to be realised in performance, including relevant elements of music and technical demands
  • 2. Trialling techniques for stylistic effectStudents can trial and select vocal/instrumental techniques that realise identified stylistic features, including manipulation of dynamics, timbre, articulation and accent/stress
  • 3. Rehearsing toward interpretation goalsStudents can use student and teacher set goals to focus rehearsal, tracking progress toward an intended interpretation
  • 4. Working from notation and documentationStudents can interpret music notated or documented in a range of formats, using the notation as a basis for developing their interpretation
  • 5. Integrated interpretation across a pieceStudents can produce an interpretation of a piece in a chosen style that integrates stylistic feature decisions, technique choices, notation reading and rehearsal goals into a coherent expressive whole
