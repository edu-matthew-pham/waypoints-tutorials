Identifying stylistic features

AC9AMU8C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the stylistic features of a piece of music that need to be realised in performance, including relevant elements of music and technical demands

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two stylistic features of a piece
  • Describes what each feature requires from a performer (technical or expressive demand)

Prior knowledge

Students can manipulate elements of music and use compositional devices to communicate ideas when composing and practising music for performance (AC9AMU6C01). They can notate, document and/or record music they compose. They have not yet focused on interpretation of existing works across a variety of forms and styles, nor on deliberately employing style-specific vocal/instrumental techniques to realise stylistic features.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two stylistic features of a given piece and describe what they require from a performer
Typical
Identify stylistic features across a range of forms/styles; describe what each requires technically and expressively from the performer
Wider
  • Analyse recordings of expert performers to identify how specific stylistic features are realised differently across interpretations of the same work
  • Compare stylistic features across two contrasting genres (e.g. jazz and classical) to identify what changes technically and what remains common

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a range of stylistic feature examples before they can reliably identify and target them in their own interpretation. Exposure to multiple styles and forms builds the reference bank that makes identification purposeful rather than guesswork.
