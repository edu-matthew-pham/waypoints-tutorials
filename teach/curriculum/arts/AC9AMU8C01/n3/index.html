Rehearsing toward interpretation goals

AC9AMU8C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use student and teacher set goals to focus rehearsal, tracking progress toward an intended interpretation

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one explicit rehearsal goal
  • Can describe whether and how the goal was met

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Trialling techniques for stylistic effect.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one explicit goal to focus rehearsal; identify whether the goal was met and what still needs work
Typical
Set and use multiple rehearsal goals across technical and expressive dimensions; adjust goals as rehearsal progresses; document decisions
Wider
  • Record alternative approaches to interpreting a phrase or melody and evaluate each against the intended stylistic effect — use the recording to identify what the ear notices that playing alone doesn't reveal
  • Limited width — rehearsal goal-setting is a process skill; breadth comes from the variety of repertoire and styles rehearsed, not from multiplying goal-setting procedures.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsGoal-directed rehearsal is a precision and persistence skill — students need sustained practice with the feedback loop (set goal → rehearse → evaluate → adjust) rather than exposure to more contexts. The cognitive work is in the iteration, not the variety.
