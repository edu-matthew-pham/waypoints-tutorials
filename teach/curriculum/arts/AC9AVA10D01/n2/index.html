Interrogating multiple approaches

AC9AVA10D01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interrogate multiple approaches to representing a topic — using different media to express different interpretations or emotions.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two different media to represent the same topic
  • Describes what each approach expresses differently — not just what it looks like but what it communicates

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: In-depth investigation of a medium.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two different media to represent the same topic differently; describe what each approach expresses.
Typical
Interrogate multiple approaches to the same activity using diverse media; produce several works on the one topic each exploring a different emotion or interpretation; compare approaches and document what each reveals.
Wider
  • Develop an 'inspiration' journal — collect photographs, found objects, media experiments, thoughts and ideas from everyday life; use Viewpoints questions to identify what you are drawn to and why
  • Investigate artists from a different cultural context (e.g. a country in the Asia region) through accounts of their influences — reflect on how their accounts inform your understanding of what can shape your own practice

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepUsing a single approach to represent a topic produces one reading. The comparison across multiple media reveals how mode shapes meaning — students who only use one approach cannot see the expressive choices available to them.
