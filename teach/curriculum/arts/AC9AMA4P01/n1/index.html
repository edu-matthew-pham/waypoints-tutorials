Responsible media practice in presentation

AC9AMA4P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Media arts: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and apply at least two responsible media practices when preparing to share a work — including one permission/credit consideration and one audience consideration.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify at least two responsible media practice requirements relevant to their sharing context
  • Student applies both requirements when sharing their work and can explain why each is needed

Prior knowledge

Students can share media arts works with audiences in informal settings and apply basic permission principles. They have not yet applied a full suite of responsible media practices (credits, formal permission workflows, audience and relationship considerations) when presenting.

Width

Minimum
Identify and apply two responsible media practice requirements before sharing (e.g. create credits, obtain permission for photographs) — and explain why each is required.
Typical
Apply responsible media practices across sharing contexts — creating credits for title sequences (font, colour, display time), seeking and acknowledging permission for photographs, considering audience and relationship when formatting a publication; apply media concepts of audience and relationships to layout decisions.
Wider
  • Document a school excursion through photography — practising the full permission-to-publication workflow: seeking permission from class members, selecting and sequencing images, creating credits, and publishing to the school intranet
  • Develop and apply a class protocol for responsible sharing that addresses: individual permission, cultural material permission, credits, and platform-appropriate sharing

Hinge

Responsible media practice habits established here underpin all subsequent sharing and publication work — failure to establish the habit at Y3-4 creates compliance gaps in later years when stakes increase.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsResponsible media practice is a procedural habit that requires repeated application across different contexts — not just conceptual understanding. Students need practice applying permission and credits workflows, not just knowing they exist.
