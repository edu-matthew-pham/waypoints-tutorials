← AC9ADR10P01 Perform to audiences with intention

Evaluating performance and drawing forward

AC9ADR10P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Drama: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how successfully their performance achieved its intentions and draw specific lessons for future dramatic practice.

Success criteria

  • Student evaluates their performance across at least three dimensions: intention, execution, audience effect
  • Student draws at least one specific lesson for future practice
  • Student demonstrates appropriate cultural protocols when performing material connected to community ownership or cultural knowledge

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Performing with sustained intentionality.

Width

Minimum
Student produces a performance evaluation that addresses at least three dimensions (intention, execution, audience effect) and draws at least one lesson for future practice.
Typical
Students reflect on and evaluate how successfully intentions, meaning and effects were achieved in context; they consider use of elements, conventions, performance skills and design; they identify what can be learnt from this for future dramatic practice.
Wider
  • Students compare their own evaluation of the performance with collected audience feedback, analysing the gap between intended and experienced meaning
  • Students use a structured performance review framework consistently across multiple performances, tracking their development across the year

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPost-performance evaluation is the culminating integration of performance skills, reflective practice and dramatic intention; it closes the make-perform-reflect cycle and generates the foundation for continued development.
