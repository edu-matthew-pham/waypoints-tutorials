Evaluating experimentation with feedback

AC9AVA8D01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate their own experimentation using teacher and peer feedback, and use evaluation to set future practice directions

Success criteria

  • Responds to at least one piece of feedback with a documented change in practice direction
  • Evaluation uses visual arts terminology — not just 'I liked it' or 'it didn't work'
  • Documents the feedback-evaluation-decision cycle

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Learning from other artists to inform own experimentation.

Width

Minimum
Respond to at least one piece of teacher or peer feedback on own experimentation; document how the feedback changes subsequent practice direction
Typical
Use teacher and peer feedback to evaluate own experimentation outcomes; set future goals based on evaluation; document the feedback-evaluation-decision cycle using visual arts terminology
Wider
  • Develop a systematic evaluation process for own studio practice — use Viewpoints to generate questions that push reflection beyond surface assessment
  • Evaluate own experimentation against the work of artists investigated — identify specific points of comparison and what they reveal about own skill development
  • Develop criteria for evaluating own experimentation that go beyond 'it worked/didn't work' — use visual arts vocabulary to articulate what success looks like at multiple levels

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsUsing feedback productively — receiving it, interpreting it in relation to visual arts criteria, and translating it into changed practice — is a reflective habit that develops through repeated cycles. Students need sustained practice with the feedback loop rather than exposure to more experimentation contexts.
