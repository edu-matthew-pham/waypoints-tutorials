Purposeful experimentation with materials and processes

AC9AVA8D01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can experiment with a visual convention, process or material with a clear purpose — to discover what it can do — and document what the experimentation reveals

Success criteria

  • Experiments with at least one convention, process or material with a clear purpose
  • Documents at least two outcomes using visual arts terminology
  • Describes what the experimentation reveals — not just what was tried

Prior knowledge

Students can use visual conventions, processes and materials with increasing control (AC9AVA6D01). They have not yet systematically experimented to develop skills through deliberate practice, nor documented and evaluated that process using visual arts terminology.

Width

Minimum
Experiment with at least one visual convention, process or material; document at least two outcomes and describe what each reveals about what the material or process can do
Typical
Experiment with a range of materials, processes or conventions across the unit; approach each experiment with a question or purpose; document outcomes using visual arts terminology
Wider
  • Investigate how artists use encryption, layering or symbolic content and experiment with these approaches — document what the technique adds to meaning-making in own work
  • Experiment with sustainable materials (natural dyes, recycled materials) and document how material constraints generate rather than limit creative possibilities
  • Study how a specific artist uses a particular material or process and experiment with replicating and then diverging from that approach — document what is learned through the divergence

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPurposeful experimentation — setting a question, trying approaches, documenting outcomes using correct terminology — is a studio practice habit that develops through repeated cycles, not through exposure to more material types. The skill is in the discipline of the process itself: students need to practise the documentation cycle until it becomes habitual.
