Learning from other artists to inform own experimentation

AC9AVA8D01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate how other artists use materials and processes and use these insights to direct their own experimentation

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Investigates at least one artist's use of a material or process
  • Uses the investigation to set a specific experimentation direction — not just 'I was inspired'
  • Documents the connection between the investigation and own experimentation outcomes

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Purposeful experimentation with materials and processes.

Width

Minimum
Investigate how at least one artist uses a specific material or process; use this as a starting point for own experimentation; document the connection
Typical
Investigate multiple artists' approaches to materials and processes; use the investigation to set specific experimentation goals; document how the investigation informed the direction of own experimentation
Wider
  • Explore how artists respond to sensory qualities of materials as inspiration — experiment with allowing material properties to direct the work rather than imposing a plan
  • Compare how artists working in different cultural traditions respond to the same material — use the comparison to develop own informed approach
  • Investigate how an artist's choice of material carries meaning beyond technical function — experiment with making material choice a deliberate expressive decision in own work

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly look at other artists' work and describe what they see without reasoning through how it informs their own decisions — the investigation stays at appreciation rather than becoming analytical. Scaffolding the connection (what specific technique or choice am I investigating, what does it do, how would I try it, what would I change?) builds the transfer from investigation to practice.
