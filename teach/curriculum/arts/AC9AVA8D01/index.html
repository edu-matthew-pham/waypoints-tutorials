Experiment to develop skills

AC9AVA8D01 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

experiment with visual conventions, visual arts processes and materials to develop skills

Prior knowledge

Students can use visual conventions, processes and materials with increasing control (AC9AVA6D01). They have not yet systematically experimented to develop skills through deliberate practice, nor documented and evaluated that process using visual arts terminology.

By the end

Students experiment with visual conventions, processes and materials to develop skills, documenting and evaluating their practice using visual arts terminology.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • selecting and appropriately acknowledging the use of found images when making and responding to artworks
  • investigating the choices that other artists have made when structuring their representation of ideas
  • developing an understanding of how and why artists choose to use encryption in their artworks — analog and digital approaches
  • evaluating feedback from teachers and peers and documenting subsequent decisions in journals using visual arts terminology
  • developing an awareness of cultural and artistic conventions for representing subjects through contextual study, observing protocols for First Nations Australian cultural property
  • reflecting on learning and evaluating choices to represent their ideas as the artwork progresses
  • exploring the ways that a range of artists use materials and visual conventions to communicate concepts
  • observing the different ways that artists respond to sensory stimuli to generate ideas

Waypoints

  • 1. Purposeful experimentation with materials and processesStudents can experiment with a visual convention, process or material with a clear purpose — to discover what it can do — and document what the experimentation reveals
  • 2. Learning from other artists to inform own experimentationStudents can investigate how other artists use materials and processes and use these insights to direct their own experimentation
  • 3. Evaluating experimentation with feedbackStudents can evaluate their own experimentation using teacher and peer feedback, and use evaluation to set future practice directions
