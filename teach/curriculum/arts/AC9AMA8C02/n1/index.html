Applying production processes across the pipeline

AC9AMA8C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply pre-production, production and post-production processes to produce a media arts work with deliberate intent at each stage

Success criteria

  • Applies at least two production stages with deliberate intent
  • Explains how each stage contributed to intended meaning
  • Production decisions are connected to representational intent — not just technical execution

Prior knowledge

Students can use production processes to create media arts works (AC9AMA6C02). They have not yet applied media arts concepts deliberately across the full production pipeline, nor maintained responsible media practice as a consistent production discipline.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two stages of the production pipeline (pre-production, production, post-production); explain how each stage contributed to the intended meaning of the work
Typical
Apply production processes across all three stages; document decisions made at each stage and how they contribute to the intended representation and audience relationship
Wider
  • Produce a work that represents sustainable futures or social justice — evaluate how each production stage decision serves the communicative intent
  • Use Viewpoints questions at each production stage — 'What elements define this genre?', 'How am I creating mood?' — document how questioning drives production decisions
  • Transfer established technical codes across media types (framing in stills and moving image, sound in radio and audio-visual) — evaluate how the transfer changes meaning and what it reveals about how codes work

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly move through production stages without reasoning about how each stage contributes to or detracts from the intended meaning. Scaffolding the stage-by-stage reasoning — what is the intended meaning, what does this stage produce, is it serving the intent — builds deliberate production practice rather than mechanical pipeline execution.
