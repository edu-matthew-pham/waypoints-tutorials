What it means to have an audience

AC9AMUFP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Music: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students understand that sharing arts works involves an audience with a role to play — watching, listening and responding.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Participates appropriately as an audience member (watching/listening during the performance)
  • Can make at least one specific positive comment about another student's work

Prior knowledge

No formal prior arts learning assumed. Students bring informal experience with showing things to others (e.g. showing a drawing to a parent). Formal experience of sharing arts works with an audience is new.

Width

Minimum
Participate as an audience member during a peer sharing: listen or watch quietly, then make one positive comment about what they noticed.
Typical
Experience both roles — performer/sharer and audience member; practise using positive language to comment on others' works; understand that the audience's role is to engage with and respond to the work, not just to watch.
Wider
  • Discuss the difference between being an audience at a school performance vs watching a cartoon at home — what does the performer/artist need from each?
  • Practise responding to an arts work with specific observations (e.g. 'I noticed the chant got faster') rather than only general praise

Hinge

Understanding that performance involves an audience relationship — not just doing something in front of people — is foundational to all presenting and performing work. Students who see sharing as 'showing and sitting down' do not develop the intentional communication of later performance work. This concept underpins AC9AMU2P01's requirements around staging, introduction and post-performance discussion.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExplicitly teach the audience role before sharing events: 'When you are the audience, your job is to...' Debrief after sharing: 'What was it like to perform? What was it like to watch?' Build language for responding to arts works.
