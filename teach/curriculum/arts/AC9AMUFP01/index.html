Share arts works with audiences

AC9AMUFP01 · Foundation The Arts · Music: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

share their arts works with audiences

Prior knowledge

No formal prior arts learning assumed. Students bring informal experience with showing things to others (e.g. showing a drawing to a parent). Formal experience of sharing arts works with an audience is new.

By the end

Students share their arts works with an audience, introduce what the work is about, and respond to questions or comments about it.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • sharing their artworks with the class via a 'gallery' or 'performance walk'
  • performing songs, soundscapes or chants/raps, using music skills they have learnt and practised
  • taking images of class arts events, creating an image sequence to document the occasion, and adding sound
  • talking about ideas such as themes, processes used to make the work, or features of the work
  • presenting retellings of stories to peers through dramatic play using repurposed objects, toys or puppets
  • using digital devices to record their arts explorations; curating a sequence of digital images to show the steps in a process

Waypoints

  • 1. What it means to have an audienceHingeStudents understand that sharing arts works involves an audience with a role to play — watching, listening and responding.
  • 2. Introducing and sharing a workStudents can introduce their arts work to an audience by naming one feature of it, then perform or display it.
  • 3. Performing with music skillsStudents can perform a song, soundscape or chant using at least one practised music skill (e.g. matching starting pitch, staying in time with the group, following a simple graphic score).
