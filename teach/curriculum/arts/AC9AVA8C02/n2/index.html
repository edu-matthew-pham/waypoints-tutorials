← AC9AVA8C02 Select and manipulate for meaning

Working across media and processes

AC9AVA8C02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select processes and materials appropriate to their intended meaning and explain why those choices serve the artistic concept better than alternatives

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Selects process or material with a documented rationale connected to intended meaning
  • Explains what the choice enables that an alternative would not
  • Material/process choice is connected to concept, not just to familiarity or convenience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Making deliberate choices with visual conventions.

Width

Minimum
Select at least one process or material with a clear rationale for why it suits the intended meaning; explain what this choice enables that an alternative would not
Typical
Create artworks using selected processes and materials that are connected to intended meaning; evaluate whether material and process choices are serving the concept; document decision-making
Wider
  • Create a work using digital tools (AR assets, photographic manipulation, digital drawing) and evaluate how digital processes extend or constrain expressive possibilities compared to analog approaches
  • Transform an analog artwork into digital versions and evaluate which transformation best serves the intended meaning — document the decision using visual arts terminology
  • Consider sustainable design practices as a material constraint — explore sustainable fabrics or recycled materials and evaluate how constraints generate creative possibilities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly select materials and processes based on what they know how to use rather than what serves the intended meaning. Scaffolding the selection reasoning (what does this material allow, what does it prevent, how does that connect to what I'm trying to communicate?) builds the habit of purposeful material thinking rather than defaulting to the familiar.
