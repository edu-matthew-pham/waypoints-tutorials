Arts in communities

AC9AVAFE01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how and why arts are important for people and communities — identifying examples and explaining their importance in terms of shared meaning, celebration or connection.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one example of arts important to a community (not just an individual)
  • Explains why the arts work or event matters to that community

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Why arts matter to people.

Width

Minimum
Identify one example of arts that is shared by or important to a community (not just an individual) and explain why.
Typical
Explore arts works and events that are important to communities — songs, public art, cultural celebrations, shared stories; discuss the role arts play in bringing people together and communicating shared values.
Wider
  • Investigate arts works that serve specific community functions (school songs, sports team anthems, ceremonial dances) — compare different communities' arts traditions
  • Explore how facial expressions, gesture and visual imagery communicate feelings across cultures — discuss how this shared function makes arts important everywhere

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierStudents bring together their personal examples and their understanding of why arts matter to arrive at the broader claim: arts are important for communities, not just individuals.
