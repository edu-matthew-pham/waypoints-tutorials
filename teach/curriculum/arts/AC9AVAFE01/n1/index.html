Identifying arts in daily life

AC9AVAFE01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify arts works and experiences in their own lives — naming songs, artworks, dances, animations or performances they know.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one arts work or experience from their own life
  • Can categorise it as visual art, music, dance, drama or digital

Prior knowledge

Students bring lived experience of arts in daily life (songs, drawings, stories, celebrations) but have not yet been invited to reflect on why arts matter to communities.

Width

Minimum
Name at least one arts work or experience from their own life (a song, a drawing, a dance).
Typical
Identify arts works and experiences across multiple forms (visual, music, dance, drama, digital) encountered at school, at home and in community settings.
Wider
  • Identify arts works from different cultural celebrations (school songs, ceremonial dances, birthday traditions) — compare which forms appear in different communities
  • Use dramatic play to explore a text or image — act out 'what is happening?' and 'what will happen next?' to understand how arts communicate story

Hinge

Students who cannot identify arts in their own lives have no foundation from which to reason about why arts matter — the subsequent conceptual move (importance) requires concrete examples to anchor it.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a sufficient inventory of examples before they can generalise about why arts matter. Broadening the range of recognised arts forms ensures the concept applies beyond a single familiar type.
