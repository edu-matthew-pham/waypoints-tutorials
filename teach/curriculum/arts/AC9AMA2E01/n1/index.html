Media arts across contexts

AC9AMA2E01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify where they encounter media arts works in their lives and communities — naming types of media and contexts.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify at least two types of media arts works from their own life
  • Student can describe where and when they encounter each type

Prior knowledge

Students can identify arts works in their own lives and explain why they matter to people. They have not yet explored media arts works across different cultures or communities, or considered the roles and responsibilities of media artists.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two different types of media arts works encountered in their own life and explain where and when they experience each.
Typical
Identify a range of media types and contexts (podcasts, animated shows, advertisements, games, news) encountered in school, home and community settings; describe the purpose of each type; ask Viewpoints questions: 'What story is this work telling?', 'Who made this?', 'Where is it from?'
Wider
  • Compare media arts works encountered at home vs at school vs in the community — investigating how different contexts produce different types of media and different audience experiences
  • Investigate how media arts works in different cultural contexts (e.g. animated shows from different countries) communicate cultural knowledge and community values

Hinge

If students cannot identify the range of media contexts they inhabit, the 'across cultures and communities' exploration has no concrete grounding — subsequent questions about purpose and meaning have no examples to work from.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary the media contexts encountered — podcasts, animated shows, advertisements, games, news — so students develop a broad understanding of what counts as media arts, not just the forms most familiar to them.
