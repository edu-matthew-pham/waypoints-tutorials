Roles and responsibilities of media artists

AC9AMA2E01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the role of media artists and explain what permission means in relation to using and sharing images, sounds and text.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can describe at least one role a media artist plays
  • Student can explain what permission means in at least one media context (e.g. taking photos of people, using someone else's music)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Purpose and cultural knowledge.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one role a media artist plays (e.g. photographer, animator, filmmaker) and explain one permission consideration for using images or sounds created by others.
Typical
Discuss the roles of media artists across different types of media; develop class rules for using and creating images, sounds and text; apply permission principles to their own creation and sharing activities; understand that some cultural material belongs to a group rather than an individual.
Wider
  • Develop a class set of rules for using and creating images, sounds and text in media arts — drawing on media artist role examples and permission principles to ground each rule in a real scenario
  • Investigate a specific case of permission in context: obtaining permission to share a class story as a news bulletin, understanding that permission from individuals and from cultural groups may be different

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierConnecting media artist roles to permission responsibilities integrates the 'what people do' with the 'what they owe' — support students in seeing permission as an ethical dimension of the media artist's role, not a separate compliance requirement.
