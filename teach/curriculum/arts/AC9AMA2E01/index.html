Media arts across cultures and contexts

AC9AMA2E01 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explore where, why and how people across cultures, communities and/or other contexts experience media arts

Prior knowledge

Students can identify arts works in their own lives and explain why they matter to people. They have not yet explored media arts works across different cultures or communities, or considered the roles and responsibilities of media artists.

By the end

Students can explore where, why and how people across cultures, communities and/or other contexts experience media arts, and describe the role of media artists and what permissions mean.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying where they might experience media arts works in their lives and communities; for example, considering how media arts works communicate cultural knowledge and the purpose of a range of media types
  • using Viewpoints to ask questions about societies and cultures; for example, "What story is this media arts work telling me/us?", "Who made this media arts work?", "Where is this media arts work from?"
  • discussing the roles of media artists and what permission means; for example, deciding on a class set of rules for using and creating images, sounds and text in media arts works
  • considering how media arts works communicate and celebrate cultural knowledge in their own communities; for example, through podcasts or animated children's shows

Waypoints

  • 1. Media arts across contextsHingeStudents can identify where they encounter media arts works in their lives and communities — naming types of media and contexts.
  • 2. Purpose and cultural knowledgeStudents can explain why a media arts work was made and how it communicates cultural knowledge — connecting purpose to content and form.
  • 3. Roles and responsibilities of media artistsStudents can describe the role of media artists and explain what permission means in relation to using and sharing images, sounds and text.
