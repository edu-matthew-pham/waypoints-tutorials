Music across cultures, times and places

AC9AMU4E01 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explore where, why and how music is composed and/or performed across cultures, times, places and/or other contexts

Prior knowledge

Students have explored music from diverse cultural contexts and discussed preferences (AC9AMU2E01). They can identify where and how people experience music and connect personal preferences to contexts. They have not yet compared music across times and places for similar purposes, or examined audience/performer expectations across different settings.

By the end

Students explore where, why and how music is composed and performed across cultures, times, places and other contexts, comparing purposes, notation formats, performance settings and audience expectations.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring examples of music created for similar purposes across different times and/or places, such as music for dancing, songs that communicate messages, or narrative songs
  • examining the purpose for which music is used in their lives and community
  • comparing the expectations and requirements of performers and audiences in different performance settings
  • exploring ways of notating or documenting forms of music
  • exploring ways to make instruments from a range of materials

Waypoints

  • 1. Music for similar purposes across different contextsStudents can compare music created for similar purposes across different cultural or historical contexts, identifying similarities and differences in how elements of music are used.
  • 2. Performer and audience expectations in different settingsStudents can describe different expectations for performers and audience members in at least two different performance settings.
  • 3. Notation and documentation across contextsStudents can explore different ways of notating or documenting music and explain how each type conveys information to performers.
