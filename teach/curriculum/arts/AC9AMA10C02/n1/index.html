Production to confirm or challenge expectations

AC9AMA10C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply production processes to produce a media arts work — making deliberate choices about whether to confirm or challenge genre conventions and audience expectations, and explaining how each choice serves this intent.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a media arts work making deliberate choices about genre conventions and audience expectations
  • Student can explain how at least two production choices confirm or challenge those expectations

Prior knowledge

Students can design media arts works using pre-production processes. They can apply genre conventions deliberately. They have not yet produced works that specifically confirm or challenge audience expectations — applying full production processes with critical awareness of how representations position audiences.

Width

Minimum
Produce one media arts work that either confirms or challenges at least two audience expectations — documenting the production choices made and explaining how each serves the intended relationship with the audience.
Typical
Produce media arts works across forms (silent film, music video, stop motion, animation, genre film) using full production processes; use mise-en-scène, camera work, sound, editing and media technologies in genre-typical or genre-disrupting ways; apply ethical, legal and cultural safety considerations; evaluate how production choices position audiences.
Wider
  • Produce a genre film that either conforms to or deliberately challenges the conventions of its genre — documenting the production decisions made and evaluating how each positions the audience, then producing an alternative opening or sequence that takes the opposite approach
  • Create multiple representations of the same person, place or concept in different media forms and for different intentions — evaluating how each medium, genre choice and audience relationship shapes the representation produced

Hinge

The ability to consciously position production choices in relation to audience expectations is the core capability of this standard — without it, production is competent craft but not critical media practice.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse Viewpoints questions to make the confirmation/challenge decision explicit: 'How does this camera choice confirm what the audience expects? What would happen if I did the opposite?' The reasoning behind each choice is as important as the choice.
