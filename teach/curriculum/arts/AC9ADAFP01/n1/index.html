Being an audience

AC9ADAFP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Foundation The Arts · Dance: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can participate as an attentive audience for peers' arts works — watching, noticing, and responding.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student watches a peer's work with sustained attention
  • Student uses at least one positive, specific comment about a peer's work

Prior knowledge

Students have created simple movement sequences and arts works communicating personal ideas (AC9ADAFC01). They have some experience of physical play and movement exploration (AC9ADAFD01).

Width

Minimum
Participating as audience for at least one class sharing event; using positive language to comment on a peer's work.
Typical
Participating in class gallery or performance walks; observing peers' works with attention; using positive language to identify features — shapes, movements, patterns — and sharing observations with peers and teachers.
Wider
  • Take and curate digital images of class arts events (with permission), sequencing them to document the occasion
  • Respond to peers' work through a different arts form — e.g. drawing a response image or creating a sound to accompany a movement

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need repeated practice as audience members to build observational vocabulary and respectful viewing habits. Multiple sharing events across the year build this repertoire.
