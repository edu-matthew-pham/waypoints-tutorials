← AC9ADA8C01 Choreograph dance for meaning

Selecting and developing movement with choreographic devices

AC9ADA8C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select movement from improvisation and develop it using choreographic devices to communicate intended ideas or meaning

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one choreographic device to develop selected movement
  • Explains how the device serves the intended meaning or idea
  • Device choice is deliberate — not just applied by default

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Improvising to generate choreographic material.

Width

Minimum
Select movement from improvisation and develop it using at least one choreographic device; explain how the device serves the intended meaning
Typical
Select, combine, refine and sequence movement using choreographic devices such as transitions, variation and contrast, and forms such as binary, ternary or narrative; document how each device choice serves the choreographic intent
Wider
  • Experiment with multiple structural forms (binary, ternary, narrative) and evaluate how each changes what the dance communicates — select the form that best serves the intended meaning
  • Develop movement sequences that portray characters or social relationships respectfully and empathetically — evaluate whether the choreographic choices achieve the intended portrayal
  • Create choreography for a specific production context (music video, immersive VR experience) — analyse how the context constrains and shapes choreographic decisions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly apply a single familiar choreographic device (e.g. repetition) without exploring whether other devices better serve the intended meaning. Working across multiple devices and structural forms — in different contexts with different communicative goals — builds the habit of purposeful device selection rather than defaulting to the familiar.
