Improvising to generate choreographic material

AC9ADA8C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use improvisation as a generative tool to produce movement material that can be selected and developed for choreographic purposes

Success criteria

  • Generates movement material through at least one focused improvisation
  • Selects at least two movement phrases with a reason for their potential
  • Improvisation has a clear focus — not just free movement

Prior knowledge

Students can use elements of dance and simple choreographic devices in own dance works (AC9ADA6C01). They have not yet selected and manipulated devices with a clear rationale for how each choice communicates specific ideas, perspectives or meaning.

Width

Minimum
Generate movement material through at least one improvisation with a specific focus (e.g. mood, theme, relationship); select at least two movement phrases worth developing
Typical
Use improvisation with different focuses (mood, social relationships, thematic ideas) to generate a range of choreographic material; document and evaluate what each improvisation produces
Wider
  • Improvise using movements and techniques from diverse styles — evaluate how importing movement vocabulary from other traditions generates unexpected choreographic possibilities
  • Use improvisation focused on communication of specific emotions or social relationships — document how the improvisation generates movement that carries expressive content
  • Experiment with using improvisation for a specific production context (music video, VR experience) — evaluate how the intended context shapes what movement is worth generating

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to build a repertoire of improvisation approaches — focused on different themes, emotions, contexts and movement sources — before they can select intelligently from improvised material. A single improvisation produces a narrow range of material; diverse improvisation approaches build the generative bank needed for purposeful choreographic selection.
