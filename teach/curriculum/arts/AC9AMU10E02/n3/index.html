Mapping First Nations Australian music geographically

AC9AMU10E02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use an annotated map of First Nations Australian language groups to identify where specific composers and performers are working and connect their music to their cultural and geographic context.

Success criteria

  • Can locate at least two First Nations Australian artists on a language group map
  • Can describe how each artist's work connects to their Country/Place or cultural context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Music that advocates, educates and challenges.

Width

Minimum
Locate at least two First Nations Australian artists on a language group map; describe how each artist's work connects to their Country/Place or language group.
Typical
Use and annotate a map of First Nations Australian language groups to identify where composers and performers are working; research and present descriptions of specific artists' music and its cultural and geographic context; make connections between Country/Place, language group and musical practice.
Wider
  • Compare two First Nations Australian artists from different regions and language groups — how does the specificity of their Country/Place shape their music differently?
  • Research a First Nations Australian artist whose work directly references their language group or Country/Place — investigate how the specific geographic and cultural context is present in the music

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierGeographic mapping integrates the understanding of music as culturally and geographically situated with the investigation of specific artists and their work. The map makes visible the diversity of First Nations Australian cultures that the music represents — and challenges any residual assumption that First Nations Australian music is a single tradition from a single place.
