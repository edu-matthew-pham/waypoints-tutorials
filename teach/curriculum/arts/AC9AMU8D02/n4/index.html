Reflection informing compositional and performance choices

AC9AMU8D02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use reflection on their own and others' music to make deliberate, informed choices as a composer or performer about how to manipulate elements of music or use compositional devices

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one deliberate compositional or performance choice that was informed by reflection
  • Can explain the connection between the reflection and the decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Using ensemble cues in performance.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one choice made in their own composing or performing that was directly informed by reflection on music (own or others'); explain the connection between what they heard and what they decided
Typical
Demonstrate a consistent reflective practice in which observations about music (own and others') lead to deliberate compositional or performance choices; document the reflection-to-decision chain
Wider
  • Develop a structured reflection process used across both composition and performance work — compare how reflection functions differently in each context (retrospective in composition, anticipatory and real-time in performance)
  • Use aural reflection to identify a pattern in their own musical tendencies (e.g. always choosing the same dynamic range, defaulting to similar rhythmic patterns) and deliberately experiment with alternatives

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates terminology, focused listening, and ensemble awareness into a deliberate reflective practice that feeds back into making. The coordination of reflection and decision-making as a unified practice — rather than separate activities — is the learning.
Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe reflection-to-decision chain needs explicit scaffolding — students often reflect and then make their next choice without connecting the two. Structuring the reasoning (what I heard → what it tells me → what I will do differently) makes the feedback loop visible and habitual.
