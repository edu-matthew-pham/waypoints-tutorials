Investigating First Nations Australian media arts

AC9AMA8E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate the diversity of First Nations Australian media arts works and practices with cultural responsiveness

Success criteria

  • Investigates at least one First Nations Australian media work in genuine depth
  • Explains cultural significance — not just describes content
  • Demonstrates cultural responsiveness throughout

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding ICIP rights in media arts.

Width

Minimum
Investigate at least one First Nations Australian media arts work or practitioner in depth; explain what makes it culturally significant and how ICIP principles are evident
Typical
Investigate diverse First Nations Australian media arts works and practices; analyse choices artists make about technical elements, story principles and audience relationships; explain how ICIP principles shape those choices
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian media artists construct representations of resilience in response to colonisation — analyse how technical and symbolic choices serve that communicative purpose
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian media arts works use story principles that may differ from dominant Western narrative structures — analyse what those differences communicate
  • Research how to select and use historical materials that accurately communicate First Nations perspectives — develop own criteria for responsible use in media arts practice

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents commonly treat First Nations Australian media arts as a single tradition rather than understanding the diversity across hundreds of distinct cultural groups and a wide range of contemporary media forms. Multiple distinct investigation contexts — different practitioners, forms, and thematic focuses — resolve this misconception through genuine breadth.
