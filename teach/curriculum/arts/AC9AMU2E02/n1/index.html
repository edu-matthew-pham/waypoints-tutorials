Where First Nations Australian music is heard

AC9AMU2E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify at least two contexts in which they can experience music composed or performed by First Nations Australians.

Success criteria

  • Can name at least two contexts where First Nations Australian music can be experienced
  • Can identify at least one First Nations Australian musician, group or musical context they have encountered

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how arts are important for communities (AC9AMUFE01) including listening to First Nations Australians talk about arts and Country/Place. They have explored music from diverse cultural contexts (AC9AMU2E01). This is their first focused exploration of music specifically by First Nations Australian composers and performers.

Width

Minimum
Name at least two contexts where they could hear First Nations Australian music (e.g. mainstream streaming, school ceremony, local festival, television).
Typical
Identify and explore multiple contexts — mainstream music platforms, live performance, music for school or community ceremonies, state and national events, local festivals, choirs and orchestras, Welcome to Country and cultural openings.
Wider
  • Access First Nations Australian music through different platform types (streaming service, live performance or community event, television) and compare how each context shapes the listening experience
  • Identify a local First Nations Australian musician or group and find out where their music can be experienced

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often assume First Nations Australian music is only encountered in ceremonial or historical contexts. Broadening awareness to include mainstream media, contemporary performance and everyday community life challenges this assumption and opens up ongoing engagement.
