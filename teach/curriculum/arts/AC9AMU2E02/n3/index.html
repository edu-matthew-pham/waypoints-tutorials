Music communicating messages about Country/Place

AC9AMU2E02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how First Nations Australian music communicates messages about Country/Place, asking questions about how musical elements are used to carry those messages.

Success criteria

  • Can identify how at least one element of music (e.g. tempo, dynamics) is being used in a First Nations Australian piece to create a specific effect
  • Can ask or respond to a question about how the music communicates a message or feeling about Country/Place

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Listening to diverse First Nations Australian music styles.

Width

Minimum
Listen to one piece of First Nations Australian music connected to Country/Place; identify one way the music might be communicating a message or feeling about a place.
Typical
Explore screen-based or theatrical works that communicate First Nations Australian cultural knowledge — view animations or cartoons retelling traditional stories; use questions to explore how texture, melody, tempo and dynamics are used to represent characters, mood and narrative; ask 'How is this music telling me about this story/place?'
Wider
  • Watch an animation or cartoon that retells a First Nations Australian story and focus on how the music changes when the setting or character changes — map the musical choices to the narrative
  • Use Viewpoints questions to analyse how specific elements of music (texture, tempo, dynamics, melody) are used to communicate specific aspects of a First Nations Australian story or place

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe connection between music and cultural message is not self-evident to Y1-2 students. Guided Viewpoints questioning ('How is the music telling you about this character/place/moment?') builds the analytical framework that underpins deeper engagement with First Nations Australian cultural knowledge through music in subsequent bands.
