Listening to diverse First Nations Australian music styles

AC9AMU2E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can listen to examples of First Nations Australian music across diverse styles and identify instruments, voices and features of the music.

Success criteria

  • Can identify at least one instrument or voice in a First Nations Australian music example
  • Can describe at least one feature of the music (e.g. tempo, mood, instrumentation) and ask at least one question about it

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Where First Nations Australian music is heard.

Width

Minimum
Listen to at least one example of First Nations Australian music; identify one instrument or voice and describe one feature of the music.
Typical
Listen to First Nations Australian music across diverse styles (mainstream media, local festival, choir/orchestra, ceremonial); identify instruments and voices; ask questions such as 'What instruments can I hear?', 'How is this music similar or different to other music I listen to?', 'How is this music communicating a message about Country/Place?'
Wider
  • Compare two examples of First Nations Australian music from different styles or contexts — describe what is similar and what is different in the instruments, voices and feeling of each
  • Focus on one example and listen multiple times for different things — first for instruments, then for mood, then for what message the music might be communicating

Hinge

The quality of listening engagement here determines whether students engage with First Nations Australian music as genuine music-making or as cultural background. If students do not develop attentive, observational listening skills in this standard, they cannot access the more analytical exploration of cultural knowledge and connection to Country/Place in AC9AMU4E02.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse explicit listening frames before and after each listening experience: 'We're going to listen for instruments first — put up your hand when you hear something you recognise.' After listening: 'What did you hear? What was surprising? What questions do you have?' Build observation habits rather than passive reception.
