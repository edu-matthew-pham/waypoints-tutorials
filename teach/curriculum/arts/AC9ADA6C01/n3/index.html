Structuring a complete dance

AC9ADA6C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Dance: creating and making · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can structure a dance using choreographic devices that communicate a clear idea, perspective or meaning — with technical and expressive skill considerations integrated.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student produces a dance that uses at least one choreographic device to structure communication of an idea
  • Student can identify how technical-expressive skill choices contribute to the dance's communicative effect
  • Student can describe what their dance communicates and explain at least one structural decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Generating material through manipulation.

Width

Minimum
A structured dance using at least one device; clear communicative intent; attention to at least one technical-expressive skill (control, coordination, balance).
Typical
Creating structured dances: narrative dances retelling a story, dances exploring themes or issues; using choreographic devices to create sections; focusing on technical and expressive skills; reflecting on and refining using Viewpoints questions ('How is the body used to represent the story?', 'How can space communicate meaning?').
Wider
  • Create a dance using production elements (music, costume, props) to enhance communication of the theme or idea
  • Choreograph a dance that explores a sustainability or social issue — using device choices to structure an argument or narrative arc

Hinge

Producing a complete structured dance using devices — not just movement arranged in order — is the capability milestone of this standard. Without this, students enter Y7-8 without the compositional experience to work with more complex structural forms (binary, ternary, narrative) and genre-specific techniques.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierStructuring integrates device knowledge (N1), generated material (N2), and technical-expressive attention into a communicative whole. Use Viewpoints questions throughout the process — not just at the end.
