Recognising arts in everyday life

AC9ADAFE01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify arts works and experiences — including dances and songs — that are present in their own lives and communities.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can name at least one arts experience from their own life
  • Student can share an idea or feeling about an arts work they have experienced

Prior knowledge

No formal prior knowledge assumed. Students bring awareness of arts experiences from family and community life.

Width

Minimum
Identifying at least one type of arts work or experience from their own life — songs they sing, dances they watch or perform, animations, community events.
Typical
Identifying a range of arts experiences across multiple forms — songs for celebrations, dances, public art, digital works — and sharing ideas and feelings about these with peers and teachers.
Wider
  • Listen to First Nations Australians talk about the importance of arts for connecting to People, Culture and Country/Place (using resources created or co-created by First Nations Australians)
  • Explore dances or songs from students' cultural communities — identifying what they are used for and who participates

Hinge

Without recognising arts as present and purposeful in their own lives, students lack a reference point for understanding arts as communicative and culturally meaningful — the conceptual grounding for N2.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents from different cultural backgrounds have access to different arts experiences. Sharing across the class builds collective recognition of arts' diversity and omnipresence.
