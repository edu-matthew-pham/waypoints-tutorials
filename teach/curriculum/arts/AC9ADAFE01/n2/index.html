Understanding arts communicate emotion and ideas

AC9ADAFE01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Foundation The Arts · Dance: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe how facial expressions, gestures, movement and voice in arts works communicate emotions and feelings.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify an emotion being communicated in a simple arts work
  • Student can point to at least one feature (face, movement, voice) that communicates that emotion

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Recognising arts in everyday life.

Width

Minimum
Identifying how a character or performer in a simple arts work (cartoon, song, dance) communicates an emotion through their face, voice or movement.
Typical
Exploring how facial expressions, gestures, movement and voice communicate emotions and feelings across a range of arts forms — portraits, cartoons, songs, chants, dances from different times and cultures.
Wider
  • Explore how illustrations and images in picture books communicate mood, character, and narrative without words
  • Compare how the same emotion (e.g. joy, sadness) is communicated differently across two different arts forms or cultural contexts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need language and observational frames to read arts works — prompt with 'How do you know they are happy/sad?', 'What does the dancer do to show that?'. Viewpoints-style questions scaffold the looking.
