← AC9AMA8C01 Design and structure media arts works

Designing for specific audiences with empathy

AC9AMA8C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Media arts: creating and making

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can design media arts works that consider the intended audience relationship and approach representations of characters and cultural content with empathy and responsibility

Success criteria

  • Designs with at least one explicit consideration of the audience relationship
  • Explains how design choices create that relationship
  • Representations of cultural or social groups demonstrate empathy and responsibility

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Designing representations with media languages.

Width

Minimum
Design a media arts work with at least one explicit consideration of the audience relationship; explain how design choices create or shape that relationship
Typical
Design media arts works with deliberate attention to audience relationships; apply empathy and respect when designing representations of cultural or social groups; document reasoning
Wider
  • Design characters or representations that convey historical or cultural ideas with genuine empathy — evaluate whether design choices honour the people or cultures represented
  • Plan how to create relationships with audiences when developing characters — investigate how different design approaches create different emotional and cognitive responses
  • Design a work that explores how people are engaging in online social justice campaigns — evaluate how design choices can build solidarity or reinforce distance with the intended audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly design for an abstract 'audience' without reasoning through who the specific audience is, what relationship the work should create with them, and how design choices build or undermine that relationship. Scaffolding the audience design reasoning — who are they, what do I want them to feel/think/do, which design choices serve that — builds audience-centred design thinking.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates pre-production planning (Node 1), deliberate media language design (Node 2), and investigative work from E01/E02 into a coherent design that serves a specific audience with appropriate ethical responsibility. Students who can design technically but cannot reason through the audience relationship and representational ethics have not met the standard's full demand.
