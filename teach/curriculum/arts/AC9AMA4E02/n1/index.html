Connection to Country/Place in media arts

AC9AMA4E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 3–4 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how a First Nations Australian media arts work communicates connection to and responsibility for Country/Place — naming at least one specific media arts concept used (framing, language, sound, symbols).

Success criteria

  • Student can identify how a First Nations Australian media arts work communicates connection to Country/Place
  • Student can name at least one media arts concept (framing, language, sound, symbols) used to communicate that connection

Prior knowledge

Students can identify media arts works produced by First Nations Australians and describe how visual, audio and interactive elements communicate cultural knowledge. They have not yet explored specifically how media arts communicates connection to and responsibility for Country/Place.

Width

Minimum
Explore one First Nations Australian media arts work that communicates connection to Country/Place and identify at least one media arts concept (framing, language, symbols, sound) used to communicate that connection.
Typical
Explore First Nations Australian media arts works across forms that communicate connection to and responsibility for Country/Place — identifying how cultural expressions include: naming Country and Traditional Owners, acknowledging Ancestors, singing/speaking in language, communicating knowledge about seasons, land, sea, sky and waterways; recognise how screen-based storytelling maintains language and ways of life.
Wider
  • Explore First Nations Australian media arts works created for ceremonial or festival contexts — collecting and reflecting on how the occasion, purpose and media arts choices communicate knowledge about Country/Place
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian media artists use screen-based storytelling to maintain language and unique ways of life — examining specific examples and discussing what would be at risk if these works were not made

Hinge

Understanding the specific connection between First Nations Australian media arts and Country/Place is the conceptual foundation for this standard — without it, exploration of specific elements and practices has no cultural grounding.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need guided inquiry with community resources or accredited materials to connect observable media elements to their Country/Place significance — this is not interpretable from the text or images alone.
