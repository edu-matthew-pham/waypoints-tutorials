← AC9ADR8P01 Rehearse and perform drama

Performing with style-appropriate skills and conventions

AC9ADR8P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Drama: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform drama using performance skills and conventions relevant to the style or form with clear communicative intent

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one performance skill and one convention appropriate to the style
  • Choices are connected to communicative intent — not just demonstrations of ability
  • Performance demonstrates empathy and appropriate respect for material and audience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Rehearsing with performance intent.

Width

Minimum
Perform drama using at least one performance skill and one convention relevant to the style or form; explain how each serves the intended dramatic communication
Typical
Perform improvised, devised or scripted drama using performance skills and conventions appropriate to the style; suit choices to the underlying dramatic structure; demonstrate communicative intent throughout
Wider
  • Perform using conventions that match the underlying dramatic structure — evaluate how structural choices constrain and enable performance decisions
  • Perform drama that explores First Nations Australian perspectives on identity, resilience or environmental practices — apply ICIP responsibilities and communicate with respectful and empathetic intent
  • Develop performance choices that demonstrate genuine empathy for the characters and situations portrayed — evaluate how empathy is expressed through performance skills rather than just described

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly perform using familiar skills without reasoning through whether those choices serve the style or the intended communication. Scaffolding the reasoning — what does this style/form require, what performance skill or convention serves that, am I making choices that communicate to the audience or just demonstrating what I can do — builds purposeful performance thinking.
