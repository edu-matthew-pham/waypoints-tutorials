Presenting across settings and audiences

AC9AMA6P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Media arts: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can present a media arts work to audiences in more than one setting — reflecting on how and why the work may be received differently across settings.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Student presents a media arts work to at least one audience applying relevant responsible media practices
  • Student can reflect on at least one aspect of how the audience or setting shaped the reception of the work

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Responsible media practice for formal and digital settings.

Width

Minimum
Present a media arts work to at least one audience and reflect on at least one way the setting or audience affected how the work was received.
Typical
Present media arts works in informal and formal settings (classroom, school assembly, online platform); reflect on differences in reception across settings; engage with audience responses on digital platforms applying online safety; consider how understandings of culture shape audience experience.
Wider
  • Present a short film to multiple audiences in different settings (classroom, school assembly, digital platform) — reflecting systematically on how and why the work was received differently in each context, and what this reveals about the relationship between audience, setting and meaning
  • Present a media arts work using internet-based technologies on a curated digital platform (school LMS) — applying online safety and responsible media practice, engaging with audience responses thoughtfully, and reflecting on how the online audience differs from a live audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPresenting across settings integrates production, responsible media practice and audience awareness — support students in treating reception differences as information about communication, not just approval ratings.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary the presentation settings — informal classroom, formal assembly, digital platform, community context — so students develop genuine experience of how setting and audience shape reception across multiple contexts.
