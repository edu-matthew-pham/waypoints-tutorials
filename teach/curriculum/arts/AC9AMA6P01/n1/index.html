Responsible media practice for formal and digital settings

AC9AMA6P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Media arts: presenting and performing · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and apply responsible media practices appropriate to formal or digital presentation contexts — including legal, ethical and cultural considerations.

Success criteria

  • Student can identify at least two responsible media practice requirements for their planned presentation context
  • Student applies both requirements when presenting and can explain why each is needed in this context

Prior knowledge

Students can share media arts works in informal settings applying permission and credits. They have not yet presented in formal settings, used internet-based platforms for distribution, or reflected systematically on how different audiences receive the same work.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two responsible media practice requirements for a formal or digital sharing context (e.g. online safety, cultural protocols, copyright) and apply them when preparing to present.
Typical
Apply responsible media practices across informal and formal contexts and digital platforms — legal, ethical and moral considerations; online safety protocols; cultural protocols for representing community or cultural stories; copyright and attribution; engaging appropriately with audience responses online.
Wider
  • Investigate protocols for representing community or cultural stories in media arts works before creating and presenting a work — documenting the protocol decisions made and reflecting on how cultural understandings shaped the final presentation choices
  • Research responsible media practice requirements for sharing on a specific digital platform (school LMS, community website) — developing a checklist that addresses legal, ethical and cultural considerations specific to that platform

Hinge

Responsible media practice for digital and formal settings requires explicit application — without it, online sharing creates legal, ethical and cultural compliance gaps that cannot be remedied after the fact.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse scenario-based reasoning to build transferable responsible media practice: 'Before we upload this to the school website, what do we need to check? Why does each check matter?' Apply to real planned sharing activities.
