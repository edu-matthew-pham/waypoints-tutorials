Extending movement vocabulary through diverse styles

AC9ADA8D01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Dance: developing practices and skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can extend their movement vocabulary by experimenting with movements and techniques from diverse dance styles

Success criteria

  • Experiments with at least one style beyond own familiar range
  • Describes what the experimentation added to own movement vocabulary
  • Movement vocabulary extension is connected to expressive possibility — not just technical novelty

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Developing technical competence through deliberate practice.

Width

Minimum
Experiment with movements or techniques from at least one dance style beyond own familiar style; describe what the experimentation added to own movement vocabulary
Typical
Improvise using movements and techniques from diverse dance styles; document what each style adds to available expressive range; reflect on how extended vocabulary informs own choreography and performance
Wider
  • Develop a personal movement vocabulary archive — document movements and techniques from different styles with annotations about their expressive qualities and technical demands
  • Investigate how a specific dance style from another cultural context uses expressive skills (gesture, facial expression, use of space) differently — experiment with integrating these into own movement vocabulary
  • Create an improvisation that deliberately draws on movements from three or more distinct dance styles — evaluate how the combination creates new expressive possibilities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who improvise only within familiar movement vocabulary cannot access the expressive range the descriptor requires. Experimenting with diverse styles — each with different technical and expressive demands — genuinely expands what students can do and say through movement. Breadth here is the mechanism of growth, not just enrichment.
