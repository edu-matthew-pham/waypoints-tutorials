Describing stylistic features of genres and styles

AC9AMU8E01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Music: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the stylistic features of a musical style or genre using music terminology, based on analytical listening

Success criteria

  • Describes at least two stylistic features of a genre or style using music terminology
  • The description is specific enough to distinguish the style from at least one other

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Accessing and analysing music across contexts.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two stylistic features of one genre or style using music terminology; the description is specific enough to distinguish this style from others
Typical
Describe the stylistic features of multiple genres or styles; use terminology consistently and accurately; identify what makes each style distinctive
Wider
  • Compare the stylistic features of two genres that share historical roots (e.g. blues and rock, folk and country, Western classical and film scores) — identify what was inherited and what diverged
  • Research the social, cultural and historical developments that shaped a specific style or tradition (e.g. 32-bar song form, chamber music, singer-songwriter) — explain how these developments are reflected in the music's stylistic features

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents frequently describe stylistic features in generic terms ('it sounds old-fashioned', 'it has a strong beat') that don't distinguish between styles. Encountering multiple styles and practising comparative description — what this style does that others don't — builds the specificity that useful stylistic description requires.
