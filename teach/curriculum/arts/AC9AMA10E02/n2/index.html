← AC9AMA10E02 First Nations media and identity

Celebrating and challenging — multiple perspectives

AC9AMA10E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 The Arts · Media arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare and critique multiple First Nations Australian media artists' use of their practice to celebrate and challenge perspectives about Australian identity — evaluating implications for community debate and audience relationships.

Success criteria

  • Student can compare at least two First Nations Australian media artists' approaches to celebrating or challenging Australian identity
  • Student can evaluate how each approach might affect community debate or audience understanding

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: First Nations media arts and Australian identity.

Width

Minimum
Compare how two First Nations Australian media artists celebrate or challenge Australian identity — identifying at least one shared and one different strategy, and evaluating how each might affect community understanding.
Typical
Compare and critique multiple practitioners; analyse how media arts provide a voice for First Nations Australians through institutions and media forms; evaluate potential impact on audiences and community debate; investigate how First Nations Australians use media arts to highlight care for Country/Place and challenge community debate.
Wider
  • Compare and critique the work of three or more First Nations Australian media artists or producers — examining how each uses their practice to communicate multiple ways of understanding an issue, and evaluating which approaches are most effective at creating productive community debate
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian media arts representations of Australian identity have changed across different platforms and periods — analysing what forces (institutional, technological, social) have enabled or constrained the range of perspectives expressed

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepVary the practitioners, platforms and issues investigated — multiple artists, multiple forms, multiple issues — to ensure students develop a genuinely diverse understanding of First Nations Australian media arts practice, not a single representative example.
