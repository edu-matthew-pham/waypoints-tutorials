← AC9AVA6E02 First Nations visual arts continuing and revitalising cultures

Identity, resilience and storytelling

AC9AVA6E02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how First Nations Australian artists embed identity, storytelling and resilience in artworks as a visual declaration — across a diverse range of forms and contexts.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least one First Nations Australian artwork serves as a declaration of identity, storytelling or resilience
  • Uses language that reflects understanding of arts as active cultural revitalisation — not only preservation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Contemporary forms and contexts.

Width

Minimum
Identify how one First Nations Australian artwork serves as a declaration of identity, storytelling or resilience.
Typical
Explore how diverse First Nations Australian artists embed identity, storytelling and resilience across multiple forms; investigate how visual conventions carry cultural messages; discuss what cultural continuation and revitalisation mean in practice.
Wider
  • Compare how First Nations Australian artists in different contemporary forms (gallery, public, fashion, popular culture) use similar visual conventions to communicate the same cultural messages
  • Use Viewpoints questions to investigate what you already know, what you need to learn, and what questions you would ask a First Nations Australian artist about their practice and community

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierStudents bring together their understanding of multiple purposes, contemporary forms and the concept of revitalisation to reach the full descriptor demand: First Nations visual arts as an ongoing, living practice of cultural continuation.
