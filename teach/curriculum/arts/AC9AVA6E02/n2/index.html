← AC9AVA6E02 First Nations visual arts continuing and revitalising cultures

Contemporary forms and contexts

AC9AVA6E02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate a range of contemporary First Nations Australian visual arts forms — site-specific work, street art, murals, installation, textiles — and explore how they communicate and celebrate cultural knowledge.

Success criteria

  • Explores at least one contemporary First Nations Australian artwork in a non-gallery form
  • Identifies how it communicates cultural knowledge or identity

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Multiple purposes of First Nations visual arts.

Width

Minimum
Explore one contemporary First Nations Australian artwork in a non-gallery form (street art, mural, installation, textile); identify how it communicates cultural knowledge.
Typical
Investigate a range of site-specific artworks, murals, street art and installations by First Nations Australian artists; explore how these forms communicate cultural knowledge; use Viewpoints questions to deepen questioning; investigate meaning and purpose.
Wider
  • Investigate site-specific artworks by First Nations Australian artists — explore how the site itself shapes the meaning of the work and how placement communicates cultural significance
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian artists use fashion and textiles to share cultural knowledge with the broader community — examine specific visual conventions used

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who are only exposed to gallery-based or historical First Nations Australian artworks develop an incomplete picture of how these arts function. Contemporary forms — street art, textiles, popular culture — show that cultural revitalisation is ongoing.
