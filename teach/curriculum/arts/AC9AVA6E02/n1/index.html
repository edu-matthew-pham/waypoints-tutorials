← AC9AVA6E02 First Nations visual arts continuing and revitalising cultures

Multiple purposes of First Nations visual arts

AC9AVA6E02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and explain that First Nations Australian visual arts serve multiple purposes — cultural practice, narrative, identity declaration, community communication and cultural revitalisation.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two different purposes of a First Nations Australian artwork
  • Explains the difference between arts as cultural practice and arts as cultural revitalisation

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how First Nations Australians use visual arts to communicate connection to and responsibility for Country/Place (AC9AVA4E02). They can identify cultural symbols and understand that arts serve community functions. They have not yet examined how First Nations arts serve as active cultural continuation and revitalisation, or explored contemporary applications such as street art, popular culture and textiles.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two different purposes of one First Nations Australian artwork (e.g. cultural practice and narrative, or community communication and identity).
Typical
Explore how First Nations Australian artworks may have multiple purposes simultaneously; use Viewpoints questions to interrogate what each work is doing and for whom; discuss the difference between art as cultural practice and art as revitalisation.
Wider
  • Investigate how First Nations Australian artists embed themselves and their communities in artworks in diverse forms — explore representations of First Nations Australians as superheroes in comics and popular culture
  • Explore how visual arts created by First Nations Australians communicate cultural knowledge to the broader community through textiles, fashion and applied design

Hinge

The multiple-purposes concept is new at Y5-6 — students who understand only 'connection to Country/Place' from Y3-4 are not equipped to engage with the full range of functions the descriptor requires, including revitalisation as an active, ongoing practice.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of revitalisation — arts actively continuing and renewing culture — needs explicit introduction. Students often assume arts only document culture; discussion prompts help them see arts as a living, forward-looking practice.
