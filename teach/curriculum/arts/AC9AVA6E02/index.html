First Nations visual arts continuing and revitalising cultures

AC9AVA6E02 · Years 5–6 The Arts · Visual arts: exploring and responding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explore ways that First Nations Australians use visual arts to continue and revitalise cultures

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how First Nations Australians use visual arts to communicate connection to and responsibility for Country/Place (AC9AVA4E02). They can identify cultural symbols and understand that arts serve community functions. They have not yet examined how First Nations arts serve as active cultural continuation and revitalisation, or explored contemporary applications such as street art, popular culture and textiles.

By the end

Students explore ways that First Nations Australians use visual arts to continue and revitalise cultures — examining the range of purposes, forms and contexts in which this occurs.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring the ways that a diverse range of First Nations Australian artists embed themselves and their communities in artworks in a range of representative forms as a visual declaration of identity, storytelling and resilience
  • exploring the ways that visual arts created by First Nations Australians may have multiple purposes; for example, art as cultural practice, art to communicate cultural understandings, or as narrative
  • investigating a range of site-specific artworks created by First Nations Australian artists, exploring how they communicate and celebrate cultural knowledge and understandings
  • using Viewpoints to question understandings about artists and artworks
  • investigating how meaning and purpose are communicated in artworks and design created or co-created by First Nations Australians; for example, learning about how visual conventions are used in artworks and designs, such as textiles and fashion, to share cultural knowledge with the broader community

Waypoints

  • 1. Multiple purposes of First Nations visual artsHingeStudents can identify and explain that First Nations Australian visual arts serve multiple purposes — cultural practice, narrative, identity declaration, community communication and cultural revitalisation.
  • 2. Contemporary forms and contextsStudents can investigate a range of contemporary First Nations Australian visual arts forms — site-specific work, street art, murals, installation, textiles — and explore how they communicate and celebrate cultural knowledge.
  • 3. Identity, resilience and storytellingStudents can explore how First Nations Australian artists embed identity, storytelling and resilience in artworks as a visual declaration — across a diverse range of forms and contexts.
