Understanding curation and audience

AC9AVA8P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 The Arts · Visual arts: presenting and performing

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in The Arts: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what curation involves and how curation choices affect what an audience understands about the artworks and the artist's ideas

Success criteria

  • Explains what curation involves
  • Identifies at least two curation decisions and explains how each affects audience experience
  • Does not treat presentation as merely displaying artworks — understands it as a communicative act

Prior knowledge

Students can present artworks with basic accompanying documentation (AC9AVA6P01). They have not yet made deliberate curation decisions about which works to present, how to organise them, or how documentation can connect artworks to ideas and meaning for an audience.

Width

Minimum
Explain what curation involves; describe at least two decisions a curator makes and how each affects audience experience
Typical
Investigate examples of curated exhibitions; analyse the curation decisions made (selection, arrangement, labelling, space) and explain how each shapes audience experience and understanding
Wider
  • Visit and critically evaluate an exhibition in a gallery, museum or community site — analyse specific curation decisions and evaluate their effectiveness for the intended audience
  • Investigate how digital curation (webpage, walkthrough, online showcase) makes different choices than physical curation — analyse what each enables and constrains
  • Research how artists describe their artistic vision in exhibition contexts — analyse how language choices in labels and statements shape audience understanding

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need exposure to multiple curation contexts — physical and digital, school and professional, different audience types — to understand that curation is a design practice with genuine decisions and consequences. A single example of a school display doesn't build the conceptual understanding needed for deliberate curation choices.
