What personal data is stored in accounts

AC9TDI4P09 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify examples of personal data stored in at least two accounts they use and state who can access each

Success criteria

  • Names at least two types of personal data in at least one account
  • Identifies who can access that data

Prior knowledge

Students can discuss how websites and apps store personal data online (AC9TDI2P07). They know data is stored online. They have not yet identified specifically what data is in their own accounts, how that data forms a digital identity, or systematically discussed the risks associated with that data.

Width

Minimum
Name at least two types of personal data stored in one account they use (e.g. school documents in cloud storage, gaming nickname) and state who can see it
Typical
Identify personal data stored across different account types (school cloud storage, gaming accounts, communication apps); state who can access the data in each (teacher only, all players, public); distinguish between data the user chose to share and data stored automatically
Wider
  • Investigate what information is automatically stored in an account without the user actively entering it (e.g. login times, device used, location) — discuss why this data exists and who can access it
  • Compare what data is stored in a school account versus a social/gaming account — discuss why the access levels are different and what that means for privacy

Hinge

Students who cannot identify what personal data exists in their accounts cannot reason about digital identity formation in N2 or associated risks in N3 — risk reasoning requires knowing what data is there first.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to think of personal data as only information they deliberately entered. Multiple account types — especially those revealing automatically stored data — help students build a more complete picture of their digital footprint.
