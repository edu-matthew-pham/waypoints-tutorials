Boolean logical operators

AC9TDI10P05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the behaviour of AND, OR and NOT and evaluate compound Boolean expressions.

Success criteria

  • Correctly evaluates a compound expression with at least two operators
  • Correctly identifies when an AND expression is false (either operand false) vs when an OR expression is false (both operands false)

Prior knowledge

Students can design algorithms involving nested control structures and represent them as flowcharts and pseudocode (AC9TDI8P05). They have experience with sequence, selection and iteration. They have not yet used Boolean logical operators (AND, OR, NOT) to express compound conditions in control structures.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate the truth value of at least three compound Boolean expressions involving AND, OR and NOT; explain what each operator does.
Typical
Evaluate compound Boolean expressions for multiple input combinations; describe in plain language what condition each compound expression represents; identify when a condition will be true and when false for AND, OR and NOT combinations.
Wider
  • Use truth tables to systematically evaluate all combinations for two-variable AND, OR and NOT expressions
  • Translate a plain-language rule (e.g. 'a person can vote if they are 18 or older AND are a registered citizen') into a Boolean expression and verify it

Hinge

Boolean operator errors (confusing AND with OR, misapplying NOT) propagate directly into N2 — students who cannot reliably evaluate compound expressions cannot write correct IF conditions, making algorithmic design at N4 unreachable.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsBoolean operator evaluation requires precision — students commonly confuse AND (both must be true) with OR (at least one must be true), and misapply NOT. Sufficient practice with varied expressions, including edge cases, builds reliable operator intuition before algorithm design.
