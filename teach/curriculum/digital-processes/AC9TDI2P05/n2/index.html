Recognising personal information and online safety

AC9TDI2P05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify examples of personal information and recognise that sharing personal information online requires care

Success criteria

  • Names at least one type of personal information correctly
  • Explains at least one reason why that information should be shared carefully

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Applying agreed behaviours when sharing.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one type of personal information (photo, name, address) and state why it should be shared carefully online
Typical
Give examples of personal information that could be used inappropriately if shared; explain that some information (photos, addresses, phone numbers) needs permission before being shared; identify who trusted adults are and their role in guiding online sharing
Wider
  • Discuss scenarios where personal information was shared without permission — explain what could go wrong and what to do if it happens
  • Compare what information it is safe to share with classmates on a classroom platform versus what should only be shared with family — identify who the trusted adult is in each context

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkRecognising personal information risk requires students to reason about consequence — not just 'what is personal information' but 'why does sharing it matter and to whom'. Scaffolding the reasoning (who could see it? what could they do with it? who should I ask?) prevents students from treating all sharing as equally safe or equally dangerous.
