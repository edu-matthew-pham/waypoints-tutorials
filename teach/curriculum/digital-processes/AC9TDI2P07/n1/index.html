What data websites and apps collect

AC9TDI2P07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 1–2 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can give examples of the types of personal data that websites and apps commonly collect

Lessons on this site

Success criteria

  • Names at least two types of data that apps or websites collect
  • Gives at least one specific example from an app or website they know

Prior knowledge

Students understand that credentials are personal and private (AC9TDI2P06). They can identify some types of personal information (AC9TDI2P05). They have not yet discussed the concept that websites and apps collect and store personal data online — or that entering details online creates a stored record.

Width

Minimum
Name at least two types of data that websites or apps collect (e.g. username, email address, date of birth) from examples they commonly encounter
Typical
Give examples of data collected by apps and websites they commonly use — usernames and email addresses for school websites and games; explain that entering this information creates a record that is stored by the website or app
Wider
  • Compare what data two different platforms collect — for example a school website versus an online game — and explain what each collects and why
  • Discuss what information is needed just to use an app versus what might be optional — identify what is necessary and what might be sharing more than needed

Hinge

Students who cannot name the types of data that apps collect cannot reason in Node 2 about why permission is needed before entering specific details — the permission reasoning requires knowing what is at stake.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need to accumulate a range of examples of data that apps collect — a single example (username) is insufficient to build a reliable understanding of the range of personal data at stake. Repeated examples across different platforms build the recognition that many different types of information are collected across different contexts.
