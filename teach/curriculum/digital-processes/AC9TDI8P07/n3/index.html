Wireframes and storyboards

AC9TDI8P07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use wireframes and storyboards to plan and communicate the user experience across multiple pages or screens of a digital system

Success criteria

  • Produces wireframes showing layout and navigation for at least two connected pages
  • Shows how a user moves between the pages

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Users and their needs.

Width

Minimum
Sketch a wireframe for at least two connected pages of a digital system; show how a user navigates between them
Typical
Create wireframes and a storyboard that maps the user journey across multiple pages; annotate design decisions with references to user stories
Wider
  • Compare wireframes at different fidelity levels (sketch vs digital tool) and explain when each is appropriate in the design process
  • Limited width — wireframing is a technique. More pages or more detail is practice volume, not broader application. The genuine breadth is in the variety of user journeys the wireframes serve, which is captured through user stories at Node 2.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsWireframing is a practical skill that requires building comfort with visual layout conventions — what to show, what to annotate, how to indicate navigation. More wireframing practice is more useful than varying the type of application being wireframed.
