← AC9TDI10P01 Acquire, store and validate data

Privacy-compliant data acquisition

AC9TDI10P01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can acquire and store data in accordance with the Australian Privacy Principles, protecting personally identifiable information from unauthorised access.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one personally identifiable data element in a system
  • Explains at least one APP requirement that applies to that data and how the system satisfies or violates it

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Automated validation techniques.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one personally identifiable element in a data collection system and explain what the Australian Privacy Principles require for that data.
Typical
Design a data collection system that acquires data according to the Australian Privacy Principles — limiting collection to what is necessary, protecting PII from public exposure, and ensuring data is not shared without consent; explain the specific APP requirements applied.
Wider
  • Evaluate an existing data collection system (e.g. a school app, community platform) against the Australian Privacy Principles — identify gaps and recommend improvements
  • Investigate how systems collecting data from or about First Nations Australians must also address Indigenous data sovereignty principles, and how these extend beyond the APPs
  • Compare how data protection requirements differ across jurisdictions (APPs in Australia, GDPR in Europe) and explain the implications for systems used globally

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPrivacy compliance is abstract without concrete cases. Students need to apply APP requirements to different data types — health data, location data, contact data — to understand how the principles operate in practice rather than as a compliance checklist.
