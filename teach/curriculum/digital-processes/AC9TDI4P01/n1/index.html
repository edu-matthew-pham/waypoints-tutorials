What design criteria are

AC9TDI4P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Checkpoint

Students can read a set of given design criteria and explain what a solution must do to meet them

Success criteria

  • Reads given criteria and restates what a solution must do
  • Does not confuse criteria (what it must do) with a solution (what it will be)

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate simple problems for known users that can be solved with digital systems (AC9TDI2P01). They have explored what makes a problem solvable. They have not yet worked with explicit design criteria or user story formats as structured tools for problem definition.

Width

Minimum
Given a set of design criteria for a simple problem, list what a solution would need to do to satisfy them
Typical
Read given design criteria across different problem contexts (a game, a tool, a communication solution); explain in plain language what each criterion means for what the solution must do
Wider
  • Explore design criteria for digital tools used by First Nations rangers — discuss how criteria like reliability in remote areas or low data usage reflect the specific context of users
  • Compare two sets of design criteria for the same problem — discuss how different criteria lead to different solutions and why criteria choices matter

Hinge

Students who cannot interpret design criteria cannot check in N3 whether their user story and criteria are consistent — the integration task requires reading criteria correctly.
