Generate and evaluate designs

AC9TDI8P08 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Generate, modify, communicate and evaluate alternative designs

Prior knowledge

Students can generate, modify, communicate and evaluate design ideas (AC9TDI6P04). They have experience producing and sharing designs but have not yet systematically generated alternatives, used iterative modification, or evaluated designs against user stories for diverse users.

By the end

Students generate multiple alternative designs using appropriate tools (concept maps, wireframes, diagrams, AI), communicate design ideas clearly, modify a preferred design iteratively based on evaluation, and evaluate alternatives against design criteria and user stories.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • reviewing and modifying a preferred design as part of the iterative development approach, for example making changes to overcome limitations of the design or better satisfy the user stories
  • using concept maps, wireframes or other diagrams to record and discuss the generated ideas, for example creating and discussing wireframes of a music streaming service, evaluating it against design criteria and user stories, such as the needs of diverse users
  • comparing multiple outputs from a generative model to determine the most suitable; for example, using AI tools to generate multiple prototypes of a user interface and selecting the design or features that best address users' needs

Waypoints

  • 1. Generating alternative designsHingeStudents can use strategies to generate multiple alternative design ideas rather than committing to a single idea
  • 2. Evaluating against criteria and user storiesStudents can evaluate alternative designs against design criteria and user stories, identifying strengths and weaknesses of each
  • 3. Iterative modificationStudents can modify a preferred design iteratively based on evaluation feedback, documenting what changed and why
