Generating alternative designs

AC9TDI8P08 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use strategies to generate multiple alternative design ideas rather than committing to a single idea

Success criteria

  • Produces at least two genuinely different design alternatives for the same brief
  • Communicates each design clearly enough for someone else to understand the idea
  • Explains why generating alternatives is valuable before selecting one

Prior knowledge

Students can generate, modify, communicate and evaluate design ideas (AC9TDI6P04). They have experience producing and sharing designs but have not yet systematically generated alternatives, used iterative modification, or evaluated designs against user stories for diverse users.

Width

Minimum
Generate at least two alternative designs for a given brief; communicate each using sketches, diagrams or digital tools
Typical
Use multiple generation strategies (brainstorming, sketching, concept mapping) to produce alternatives; explain why generating multiple options before committing leads to better outcomes
Wider
  • Use generative AI to produce multiple design prototypes and compare them against user needs — evaluate what the AI generated well and what it missed
  • Research how professional design teams use divergent thinking techniques to generate alternatives before converging on a solution

Hinge

Students with only one generation strategy produce variants of the same idea rather than genuine alternatives. Node 2 evaluation requires meaningfully different designs to evaluate — if all designs are the same idea with surface variation, the evaluation at Node 2 has nothing to compare.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyAlternative generation requires students to resist committing to the first idea — the repertoire of generation strategies (brainstorming, sketching, concept mapping) is what enables genuine alternatives. More strategies practised = less likely to produce variants of the same idea.
