← AC9TDI6P04 Generate, modify and evaluate designs

Generating a range of design ideas

AC9TDI6P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can generate multiple distinct design ideas for a given problem rather than pursuing the first idea

Success criteria

  • Produces at least two design ideas that are meaningfully different
  • Each idea is represented clearly enough to be compared

Prior knowledge

Students can generate, communicate and compare designs (AC9TDI4P03). They have produced and communicated design ideas and made comparisons. They have not yet formally evaluated designs against criteria or made and documented modifications to a preferred design based on evaluation.

Width

Minimum
Generate at least two design ideas for the same problem that are genuinely different (not minor variations)
Typical
Ideate a range of possible design solutions; represent each idea clearly enough that it can be compared to others; produce sketches, diagrams or descriptions for each
Wider
  • Use a structured ideation method (e.g. sketching six thumbnails, mind-mapping from the user story) to generate ideas beyond the first obvious solution
  • Generate design ideas that each prioritise a different design criterion — e.g. one idea optimises for cost, another for accessibility, another for ease of use — making trade-offs explicit from the start

Hinge

Genuine ideation in N1 is a prerequisite for evaluation in N2 — if students produce only one idea or near-identical variations, N2's comparative evaluation against criteria collapses into description. The evaluate step requires at least two meaningfully different options.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents who default to their first idea lack ideation repertoire — they do not know how to generate alternatives. Accumulated experience with ideation methods (thumbnail sketches, constraint-flipping, stakeholder-first framing) builds the repertoire needed for genuine comparison.
