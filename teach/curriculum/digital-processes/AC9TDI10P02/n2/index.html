Drawing conclusions and making predictions

AC9TDI10P02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Checkpoint

Students can draw conclusions from data analysis and make predictions, including identifying the limits of the prediction.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • States a conclusion that is grounded in the data (not just restating the data)
  • Makes a prediction and identifies at least one assumption or limitation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Summarising data and identifying trends.

Width

Minimum
Draw at least one conclusion from a dataset and make one prediction; note one limitation of the prediction.
Typical
Draw multiple conclusions from a dataset with different variables; make predictions by extending observed trends; identify what would need to be true for the predictions to hold and where they might fail.
Wider
  • Investigate emerging trends in Australia's industries using ABS data — make predictions about future employment patterns and identify what assumptions underpin each prediction
  • Analyse historical data to predict a future outcome (election, environmental trend, economic indicator) and explain what factors could cause the prediction to be wrong

Hinge

Students who cannot identify limiting assumptions for predictions cannot meaningfully design interactive visualisations at N3 — the analytical intent of the visualisation depends on knowing what question is being asked and what its limitations are.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often confuse correlation with causation when making predictions, or state predictions without identifying their limiting assumptions. A reasoning scaffold — what does the trend show? → what would have to be true for this to continue? → what could break this pattern? — builds rigour in prediction-making.
