Summarising data and identifying trends

AC9TDI10P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can summarise a dataset by identifying its attributes, relationships between variables, trends and outliers.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one trend in a dataset and describes its direction
  • Identifies at least one outlier and explains whether it represents an error or a genuine data point

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse and visualise data using a range of software including spreadsheets and databases (AC9TDI8P02). They can identify trends and patterns in data and draw conclusions from visualisations. They have not yet developed interactive visualisations, applied machine learning as a form of analysis, or adjusted model parameters to observe effects on predictions.

Width

Minimum
Given a dataset, identify at least one trend and one outlier; describe what each indicates about the data.
Typical
Summarise a dataset's attributes and the relationships between variables; identify multiple trends and outliers; explain what each finding suggests and whether outliers are errors or genuinely interesting data points.
Wider
  • Summarise electoral data including demographics, historical swings and exceptions — identify what trends predict outcomes and where exceptions undermine simple predictions
  • Investigate why the same dataset can show different trends depending on how it is grouped or filtered — connecting this to statistical concepts of aggregation

Hinge

Students who cannot identify trends and outliers in a dataset cannot draw conclusions or make predictions at N2 — the N2 checkpoint is directly dependent on the data summary produced at N1.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkTrend and outlier identification requires systematic data reading — students who scan data intuitively often miss outliers or misread trend direction. Scaffolding the analysis (describe the range → identify central tendency → find exceptions → name the direction) produces reliable summary before prediction.
