Eliciting stakeholder needs through interviews

AC9TDI10P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can design and conduct an interview with a stakeholder to elicit their needs, capturing information relevant to defining the problem.

Success criteria

  • Conducts an interview using prepared questions
  • Records the stakeholder's expressed needs accurately
  • Identifies at least one insight that came from the interview rather than assumption

Prior knowledge

Students can define and decompose real-world problems with design criteria and by considering the needs of users (AC9TDI8P04). They can identify constraints and requirements. They have not yet conducted stakeholder interviews, formulated user stories from primary research, or defined problems with explicit scope awareness.

Width

Minimum
Conduct at least one stakeholder interview using prepared questions; record the key needs and perspectives the interviewee expressed.
Typical
Design interview questions that elicit both specific needs and open-ended responses; conduct the interview; synthesise findings to identify the core stakeholder needs and distinguish explicit requests from underlying goals.
Wider
  • Conduct interviews with stakeholders who have different or conflicting needs — synthesise findings to identify where requirements agree and where they must be reconciled
  • Explore how interviewing First Nations Australian community members to develop digital solutions requires additional considerations: cultural protocols, community consent, and understanding community-defined needs rather than imposing external problem frames
  • Compare interview-elicited user needs with assumptions made without interviews — reflect on what would have been designed wrongly without the primary research

Hinge

Inadequate stakeholder interviews cascade through the entire design process — user stories at N2 built on unelicited or assumed needs, and a problem definition at N3 that doesn't reflect real stakeholder requirements, produce design criteria at N4 that cannot evaluate whether the right problem is being solved.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStakeholder interview quality varies enormously with question design. Students who only interview one type of stakeholder (e.g. a peer) often miss how different perspectives create different requirements. Interviewing stakeholders with different roles or needs reveals the problem space more fully.
