Sharing content selectively and safely

AC9TDI4P07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can share content with a specific, appropriate audience while keeping personal information appropriately private

Success criteria

  • Shares content only with the agreed audience
  • Does not include specific personal details (exact dates, specific names, location) in shared content

Prior knowledge

Students can use basic features of digital tools to share content and collaborate, demonstrating agreed behaviours guided by trusted adults (AC9TDI2P05). They have shared content in simple, supervised contexts. They have not yet used digital tools to plan tasks collaboratively or manage shared content across a group project.

Width

Minimum
Share content with an agreed audience (e.g. a class folder, the teacher) without revealing specific personal details
Typical
Share content in different collaboration contexts (shared folder, class survey, group document); explain which details should be kept private when sharing with different audiences; demonstrate agreed behaviours when sharing
Wider
  • Discuss a scenario where content shared for a legitimate purpose (e.g. sharing a holiday adventure) could inadvertently reveal personal information — identify what should be removed and why
  • Compare the audience reach of different sharing methods (shared folder vs link vs public post) — discuss which is appropriate for different collaboration contexts

Hinge

Students who have not developed contextual sharing judgement will make inappropriate disclosures in N2 and N3 collaborative tasks — knowing what to share with which audience is prerequisite to participating safely in any collaborative digital activity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often focus on whether they are allowed to share rather than what information sharing reveals. Different sharing contexts (class only, school, public) help students develop a contextual understanding of appropriate sharing rather than a binary 'allowed/not allowed' rule.
