Collaborating online with agreed behaviours

AC9TDI4P07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can participate in an online collaboration following agreed behaviours — taking turns, respecting others' contributions, and adding to shared content constructively

Success criteria

  • Adds content to a shared digital space following agreed behaviours
  • Responds to others' contributions in a way that builds on rather than ignores them

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Using digital tools to plan tasks collaboratively.

Width

Minimum
Participate in one collaborative online task (e.g. adding to a shared slide deck or document), following at least two agreed behaviours
Typical
Participate in collaborative online tasks across different formats; listen to and respond to others' contributions; add content that builds on what others have contributed rather than replacing or ignoring it
Wider
  • Collaborate on a task with students from a different class or year level using digital tools — discuss how agreed behaviours need to be explicitly established when collaborating with people you do not know well
  • Reflect on a collaborative experience and identify one thing that went well and one that could be improved — discuss how agreed behaviours help resolve conflicts in online collaboration

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAgreed behaviours in online collaboration need to transfer across tools and audiences. Students who only practise within their own familiar class context may not understand why behaviours need to be re-established with new collaborators or in new tools.
