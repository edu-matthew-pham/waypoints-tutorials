Share content and collaborate

AC9TDI4P07 · Years 3–4 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use the core features of common digital tools to share content, plan tasks, and collaborate, following agreed behaviours, supported by trusted adults

Prior knowledge

Students can use basic features of digital tools to share content and collaborate, demonstrating agreed behaviours guided by trusted adults (AC9TDI2P05). They have shared content in simple, supervised contexts. They have not yet used digital tools to plan tasks collaboratively or manage shared content across a group project.

By the end

Students use core features of digital tools to share content, plan tasks and collaborate with peers, following agreed behaviours and supported by trusted adults.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using an agreed folder to make it easy for students to collaborate on shared content in a group project
  • demonstrating safe sharing of content with a select audience, for example sharing a holiday adventure without revealing exact dates, specific names or other personal information
  • listening to others when participating in online environments to share content, for example respecting the rights of others by taking turns to suggest and add words or images to a factual slide deck to share with the teacher
  • interacting cooperatively in a group in an online environment to plan and complete a task, for example writing and responding to others' views on canteen products
  • using digital tools to plan an event as a class, for example jointly creating a class survey to help plan an end-of-year party, where responses conform to the class's accepted behavioural expectations

Waypoints

  • 1. Sharing content selectively and safelyHingeStudents can share content with a specific, appropriate audience while keeping personal information appropriately private
  • 2. Using digital tools to plan tasks collaborativelyStudents can use a digital tool to plan and organise a collaborative task with peers
  • 3. Collaborating online with agreed behavioursStudents can participate in an online collaboration following agreed behaviours — taking turns, respecting others' contributions, and adding to shared content constructively
