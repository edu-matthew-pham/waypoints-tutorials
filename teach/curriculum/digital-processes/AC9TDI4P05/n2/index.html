Evaluating against design criteria

AC9TDI4P05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can check a solution against specific design criteria and state whether each criterion is met with a reason

Success criteria

  • States clearly whether a criterion is met
  • Gives a reason that references a specific feature of the solution, not just a general impression

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Describing what a solution does for users.

Width

Minimum
Given a solution and one or two criteria, state whether each criterion is met and give one reason
Typical
Evaluate an existing or student solution against a set of design criteria; for each criterion, state whether it is met and provide a reason; identify criteria the solution meets well and criteria it partially or does not meet
Wider
  • Evaluate a digital solution against criteria that include accessibility or equity considerations — for example, a solution that works well for urban users but fails for users in areas with limited connectivity
  • Evaluate a student-created game against criteria from both the designer's and a classmate's perspective — discuss how the same solution can be judged differently depending on who is evaluating

Hinge

Students who evaluate by general impression rather than criterion-by-criterion reasoning cannot engage with N3's user story evaluation — both require the same structured judgement move applied to different frameworks.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluation against criteria requires students to make a criterion-by-criterion judgement move rather than a general impression. Scaffolding the process (identify the criterion → check the solution against it → state whether it is met → give a reason) prevents students from evaluating by opinion alone.
