Acquire and validate data

AC9TDI8P01 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Acquire, store and validate data from a range of sources using software, including spreadsheets and databases

Prior knowledge

This is the first standard in the Managing Data sub-strand (begins at 7-8). Students bring general digital literacy from the Collaborating and Managing sub-strand and basic data representation understanding from the K strand. They have not yet formally acquired, stored or validated data using spreadsheets or databases.

By the end

Students acquire data from a range of sources to answer a specific question, store it using appropriate software (spreadsheets or databases), and validate the data by judging its relevance, correctness, currency and potential bias.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • acquiring data to answer questions for their own investigations, for example answering "Does the canteen sell the right food?" by designing a survey to collect food preferences data and accessing canteen sales data
  • judging how meaningful data is to a question, its correctness and how up to date the data is, for example "Does age affect the chance of cyclist injury?", "Are self-reported accidents reliable?" and "Is the data before cycleways existed relevant?"
  • storing acquired data using specialised and general software appropriate for how it will be accessed and manipulated, for example a spreadsheet for visualisation or a pre-defined database for filtering and queries
  • acquiring, storing and validating data from a reputable source, such as the Australian Bureau of Statistics, to analyse the geographic distribution of First Nations Australians, with the aim to highlight past and emerging trends
  • ensuring that the data used to train an AI model minimises any potential biases in its output and is representative of the target audience; for example, training a model on data collected from a single demographic group may not produce correct outputs for a more diverse population

Covered in these courses

Main focus: Data detective

Touches on it: SQL and databases, Deeper Python in the console, Web apps with Flask, The DOM and the browser console

This is general coverage, not a lesson tagged to each waypoint. A course that only touches a standard meets it in passing; it may not be the course's main focus, and a teacher could extend it to cover the standard more fully.

Waypoints

  • 1. Defining a question that data can answerHingeStudents can identify a question that requires data to answer and determine what data is needed
  • 2. Acquiring data from sourcesStudents can acquire data from a range of sources including surveys, existing datasets and digital platforms
  • 3. Storing data appropriatelyStudents can store acquired data using software appropriate for how it will be accessed and analysed
  • 4. Validating dataStudents can validate data by judging its relevance, correctness, currency and potential bias
