Defining a question that data can answer

AC9TDI8P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify a question that requires data to answer and determine what data is needed

Success criteria

  • States a question that requires data to answer, not just opinion
  • Identifies at least one type of data needed to investigate the question

Prior knowledge

This is the first standard in the Managing Data sub-strand (begins at 7-8). Students bring general digital literacy from the Collaborating and Managing sub-strand and basic data representation understanding from the K strand. They have not yet formally acquired, stored or validated data using spreadsheets or databases.

Width

Minimum
Given a scenario, state a question that data could help answer; identify what data would be needed to investigate it
Typical
Frame a question for their own investigation; identify what data is needed, where it might come from, and what format it should be in
Wider
  • Compare how the same broad question (e.g. 'Is the canteen selling the right food?') requires different data depending on how the question is framed — food preferences data vs nutritional data vs sales data
  • Limited width — question framing is a single skill. The breadth comes from the variety of investigations at later nodes, not from framing more questions.

Hinge

Students who cannot articulate what data is needed to answer their question will acquire irrelevant data or store data without purpose at Nodes 2 and 3. The question drives every subsequent phase — without a clear question, the data pipeline has no direction.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkDefining a researchable question requires students to reason from 'I want to know about X' to 'I need data of type Y from source Z to answer a specific question'. Without scaffolding this chain, students frame questions that cannot be answered with data — the question must precede and constrain every subsequent phase of the pipeline.
