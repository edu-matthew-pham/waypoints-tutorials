Acquiring data from sources

AC9TDI8P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can acquire data from a range of sources including surveys, existing datasets and digital platforms

Success criteria

  • Acquires data from at least one source relevant to the question
  • Records where the data came from

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Defining a question that data can answer.

Width

Minimum
Acquire data from at least one source relevant to the question; record the source
Typical
Acquire data from multiple sources (e.g. a survey they design and an existing dataset such as ABS); compare what each source provides
Wider
  • Acquire and compare data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics to analyse geographic distribution of First Nations Australians, identifying past and emerging trends
  • Investigate how the source of data affects its reliability — compare self-reported survey data with independently measured data for the same question

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepAcquiring from multiple sources (survey + ABS) across different question types helps students see that source selection shapes what data is available — different sources answer different aspects of the same question. The width here is the variety of source types and their trade-offs.
