Apply Australian Privacy Principles

AC9TDI10P14 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply the Australian Privacy Principles to critique and manage the digital footprint that existing systems and student solutions collect

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate and manage the digital footprint that existing systems and student solutions collect (AC9TDI8P14). They can identify what data systems collect about them. They have not yet applied the Australian Privacy Principles as a formal framework to critique systems, or used cryptographic techniques to protect user data in student solutions.

By the end

Students apply the Australian Privacy Principles to critique existing systems and manage the digital footprint collected by student solutions, including using appropriate technical controls to protect user data.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • critiquing the extent to which online services allow them to control access to their data in line with the Australian Privacy Principles
  • using the Australian Privacy Principles as a reference to evaluate the steps they are taking to protect user information in their application, for example explaining how they are storing passwords using cryptographic hashing algorithms so that a data breach does not expose their users to security vulnerabilities due to password re-use

Waypoints

  • 1. Understanding the Australian Privacy PrinciplesHingeStudents can identify the key Australian Privacy Principles relevant to digital systems and describe what each requires.
  • 2. Critiquing existing systems against the APPsStudents can critique the extent to which existing digital systems allow users to control their data in line with the Australian Privacy Principles.
  • 3. Protecting user data in student solutionsStudents can apply technical controls in their own solutions to protect user data in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, including using cryptographic techniques for passwords.
