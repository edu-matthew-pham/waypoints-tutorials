Understanding the Australian Privacy Principles

AC9TDI10P14 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the key Australian Privacy Principles relevant to digital systems and describe what each requires.

Lessons on this site

  • The Australian Privacy Principles

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies at least two APPs and their requirements
  • Can distinguish between requirements for collection, use and disclosure

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate and manage the digital footprint that existing systems and student solutions collect (AC9TDI8P14). They can identify what data systems collect about them. They have not yet applied the Australian Privacy Principles as a formal framework to critique systems, or used cryptographic techniques to protect user data in student solutions.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two Australian Privacy Principles and describe what each requires a system to do or refrain from doing.
Typical
Identify the APPs most relevant to digital systems (including those governing collection, use, disclosure, quality, security and access); describe what each requires; explain what type of data each applies to.
Wider
  • Compare the Australian Privacy Principles with the EU GDPR — identify the key differences and explain what each framework is trying to achieve
  • Investigate what the APPs require for data collected about minors and explain how student-facing systems must address these requirements

Hinge

APP knowledge is directly prerequisite for N2 and N3 — students who cannot identify relevant principles cannot critique systems against them or implement compliant technical controls.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyThe APPs are a set of distinct principles each with different requirements. Students need to accumulate sufficient examples of each principle in action before they can apply them to critique systems at N2 — a single APP in a single context does not build the evaluative toolkit N2 requires.
