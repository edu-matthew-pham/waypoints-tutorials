Investigate problems for users

AC9TDI2P01 · Years 1–2 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

investigate simple problems for known users that can be solved with digital systems

Prior knowledge

Students have not yet formally investigated problems. They have encountered digital systems in familiar contexts and can recognise when a digital system helps them do something. They have not yet structured a problem investigation — identifying a user, a problem, and how a digital system could address it.

By the end

Students investigate simple, well-defined problems for a known user and identify how a digital system could solve the problem.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating internet-based translation tools and how similar digital tools could promote the use of First Nations Australians' languages or assist communications, for example responding to cultural stories of Australian First Nations Peoples
  • investigating familiar and easily understood problems with few complications or steps needed to solve them, for example deciding what to wear by checking the forecast on a weather app
  • identifying how digital systems are used to solve problems at school, for example taking attendance or borrowing a library book
  • exploring how a familiar problem could be solved using a robot, for example creating a model robot using cardboard boxes and explaining how it could be used to clean up the classroom floor at the end of the day

Waypoints

  • 1. Identifying a user and their problemHingeStudents can identify a known user and describe a simple, specific problem that user has
  • 2. Establishing what the problem involvesHingeStudents can investigate a simple problem by finding out what the user needs and what would count as solving it
  • 3. Identifying how a digital system could solve the problemStudents can investigate how a digital system could solve a simple, known problem for a specific user
