Follow and describe algorithms

AC9TDI2P02 · Years 1–2 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

follow and describe algorithms involving a sequence of steps, branching (decisions) and iteration (repetition)

Prior knowledge

This is the first algorithmic thinking descriptor in the DT curriculum (no Foundation predecessor). Students bring general sequencing experience from everyday tasks — they can order familiar actions. They have not yet used computational vocabulary (sequence, branching, iteration) or applied algorithmic structure to a problem.

By the end

Students follow and describe algorithms that include a sequence of steps, at least one decision point (branching), and at least one repetition (iteration).

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • following a short, ordered sequence of steps and making decisions to solve a simple problem, for example follow a recipe or directions to reach a location
  • describing the steps and decisions (in the correct order) needed to solve a simple problem, for example writing, saying, drawing or photographing the steps needed to make a sandwich
  • rearranging into the correct order a series of mixed-up pictures that describe a story, for example stories authored and published by First Nations Australians
  • identifying the steps needed to solve a problem, and understanding when their order is important, for example socks must be put on before shoes but a jumper can be put on before or after shoes
  • identifying the decisions needed to solve a problem and the next steps to follow in each case, for example if it is raining, take a raincoat, otherwise take a hat
  • following algorithms that repeat a single step a fixed number of times, for example practise spelling a word 5 times or throw and catch a ball with a partner 10 times

Waypoints

  • 1. Following and describing sequencesHingeStudents can follow a short ordered sequence of steps and describe the steps in correct order
  • 2. Identifying and following branching (decisions)HingeStudents can identify decision points in an algorithm and follow the correct path based on the condition
  • 3. Identifying and following iteration (repetition)HingeStudents can identify when steps are repeated in an algorithm and follow algorithms that include a fixed-count repetition
  • 4. Describing algorithms with all three structuresStudents can follow and describe an algorithm that combines sequence, branching and iteration
