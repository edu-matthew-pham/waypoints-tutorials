Collaborate and manage projects

AC9TDI6P08 · Years 5–6 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Select and use appropriate digital tools effectively to share content online, plan tasks and collaborate on projects, demonstrating agreed behaviours

Prior knowledge

Students can use the core features of common digital tools to share content, plan tasks and collaborate, following agreed rules (AC9TDI4P07). They have used shared tools with provided rules. They have not yet selected tools themselves or taken ownership of defining and moderating collaborative behaviours.

By the end

Students select appropriate tools for sharing content and project collaboration, manage files and project tasks, and demonstrate agreed online behaviours including cultural protocols and respect for others' work.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • following a previously created plan to report back to the class on a given problem, using digital tools, for example small groups reporting on the best location for a new skate park in the local government area
  • defining and acting collectively using online community standards and valuing the work of others, for example moderating language and behaviour in an online class forum, not deleting the work of collaborators, and respecting others' intellectual property
  • naming, organising and storing files in a way that allows for easy retrieval of shared content, for example labelling the main folders by topic, such as school garden project, and then creating sub-folders for each section of the project, such as 'plans', 'designs' and 'implementation'
  • demonstrating agreed behaviours; following cultural protocols, including relevant permissions and attributions; acknowledging diversity, capability and strength; and addressing risks and responsibilities such as privacy, security, and accuracy of data; for example when sharing images of First Nations Australians' cultural artefacts
  • using a range of communication tools to share ideas and information with stakeholders, for example presenting content for a school celebration such as a graduation celebration with the parents and citizens association or school executive in an online forum

Waypoints

  • 1. Organising and managing files for shared accessStudents can organise and store shared files in a way that allows collaborators to locate and use them easily
  • 2. Demonstrating agreed online behavioursStudents can define and follow agreed online community standards when collaborating in a digital environment
  • 3. Sharing content with stakeholders using appropriate toolsStudents can select and use an appropriate communication tool to share content with a specific stakeholder audience
