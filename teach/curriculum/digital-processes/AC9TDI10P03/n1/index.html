Modelling entities and their attributes

AC9TDI10P03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify entities, their attributes, and the relationships between them for a given domain.

Lessons on this site

No lesson is tagged to this waypoint. The standard is generally covered by some courses; see the standard's page for which are the main focus and which only touch it.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two distinct entities with their respective attributes
  • Correctly identifies at least one relationship between entities (including its type — one-to-many or many-to-many)

Prior knowledge

Students can model and query the attributes of objects and events using structured data (AC9TDI8P03). They have experience with single-table databases and basic queries. They have not yet modelled multi-entity relationships, created relational data schemas, or written SQL queries with JOIN operations.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two entities in a given scenario, list their attributes, and describe one relationship between them.
Typical
Model a multi-entity domain (e.g. movies, users and reviews) by identifying all entities, their attributes, and the relationships between them; represent the model as an entity-relationship diagram or equivalent.
Wider
  • Model a community data scenario (e.g. a local First Nations language archive with speakers, words and recordings) — identify entities, attributes and relationships specific to the community's needs
  • Compare how the same domain can be modelled differently depending on the questions the database needs to answer — explain the implications of each modelling choice

Hinge

Entity and relationship modelling errors cascade directly into N2 schema design — a student who misidentifies entities or omits a relationship cannot design a correct schema, and all downstream database build and query work inherits the error.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEntity modelling requires systematic domain reasoning — what things exist? what are their properties? how do they relate? Students who approach this intuitively often omit entities or model relationships as attributes. Scaffolding the domain analysis step by step prevents schema design from inheriting modelling errors.
