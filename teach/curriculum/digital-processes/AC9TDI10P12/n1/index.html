Planning with agile decomposition

AC9TDI10P12 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can decompose a project into tasks, schedule them, and assign responsibilities to team members.

Success criteria

  • Project is decomposed into distinct tasks with assignments and due dates
  • Plan is recorded in a project management tool and accessible to all team members

Prior knowledge

Students have experience with collaborative digital tools and sequencing tasks (AC9TDI8P12). They can assign roles and track progress. They have not yet applied agile project management techniques, worked with version control in a collaborative coding context, or systematically accounted for project risks.

Width

Minimum
Decompose a project into at least four tasks; assign each task to a team member with a due date; record in a project management tool.
Typical
Decompose a project into sequenced tasks with dependencies; assign roles and responsibilities to all team members; use scheduling and revision to produce a realistic plan; explain the reasoning behind the task sequence.
Wider
  • Use project management tool features (e.g. AI-assisted summaries, risk forecasting) to identify potential delays or bottlenecks and explain how the plan was adjusted
  • Apply an agile sprint structure — plan a sprint, execute, review, adjust — and reflect on what the sprint review revealed about the original plan

Hinge

A project without a decomposed task structure makes N2 version control contributions uncoordinated (no tasks to commit against) and N3 risk identification abstract (no specific tasks to identify risks for).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProject decomposition requires reasoning from the project goal backward through phases to specific tasks with dependencies — students who create flat task lists without sequencing or ownership produce plans that cannot be executed or tracked.
