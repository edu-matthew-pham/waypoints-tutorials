Design algorithms with branching and iteration

AC9TDI6P02 · Years 5–6 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Design algorithms involving multiple alternatives (branching) and iteration

Prior knowledge

Students can follow and describe algorithms involving sequencing, comparison operators (branching) and iteration (AC9TDI4P02). They have worked with existing algorithms and described how they work. They have not yet designed algorithms themselves — the shift from reading/describing to creating is the new demand at this band.

By the end

Students design algorithms that include multiple branches (more than two alternatives) and loops (repetition until a condition is met), representing their logic as structured steps.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • designing an algorithm or understanding and modifying an existing algorithm to fix an error or change functionality, for example exploring issues in drought-prone areas to decide when to water a garden, taking into account humidity as well as soil moisture level
  • creating the steps, decisions and loops in algorithms and knowing what step they are up to, for example repeating the steps to add 2 digits for each column in multi-digit addition, knowing which column they are adding and when to stop
  • constructing more than one sequence of steps that solve the same problem and explaining why one is better than the other, for example specifying the exact route through a maze versus using the right-hand rule that works for all mazes
  • modelling a decision that has more than 2 options to select the next step, for example selecting transport IF distance is less than 2 km THEN walk, ELSE IF the distance is less than 5 km THEN ride a bike, ELSE catch the bus
  • planning algorithms that repeat until a condition is met, for example keep mixing UNTIL the ingredients are combined or subtracting a number UNTIL the result reaches zero
  • designing an algorithm including branching and iteration which responds to data, for example how First Nations Australian rangers use structured procedures to respond to live tracking data that indicates feral buffalo are approaching an environmentally or culturally significant site

Waypoints

  • 1. Iteration — loops until a condition is metHingeStudents can design an algorithm that repeats steps until a condition is met
  • 2. Multiple alternatives — branching beyond yes/noHingeStudents can design an algorithm with more than two branches (multiple alternatives, not just binary if/else)
  • 3. Algorithms with both branching and iterationStudents can design an algorithm that combines branching and iteration, responding to data
