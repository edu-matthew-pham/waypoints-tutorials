Decompose real-world problems

AC9TDI8P04 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Define and decompose real-world problems with design criteria and by creating user stories

Prior knowledge

Students can define problems using given or co-developed design criteria and independently created user stories (AC9TDI6P01). They have not yet decomposed complex real-world problems into sub-problems or justified their decomposition decisions.

By the end

Students define real-world problems by framing what is known and what outcome is desired, decompose them into manageable sub-problems, and create user stories that capture user needs to guide the design process.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • framing a problem in terms of what we know, why it is important and the outcome we want, for example matching the items in your fridge to possible recipes to reduce food waste
  • asking a series of questions and sub-questions to understand the problem and breaking it down into manageable parts, for example "How do we keep track of what items are in the pantry? Are there any dietary requirements that need to be considered?"
  • making predictions about future population distribution of First Nations Australians based on identified trends, for example analysing and visualising data using spreadsheets and databases on their population growth in metropolitan areas

Waypoints

  • 1. Framing a problemHingeStudents can frame a real-world problem in terms of what is known, why it is important, and what outcome is desired
  • 2. Decomposing into sub-problemsStudents can break a defined problem into manageable parts by asking a series of questions and sub-questions
  • 3. Creating user storiesStudents can create user stories that capture user needs and use them as design criteria to guide the solution
