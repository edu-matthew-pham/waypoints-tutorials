Framing a problem

AC9TDI8P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Checkpoint

Students can frame a real-world problem in terms of what is known, why it is important, and what outcome is desired

Success criteria

  • States what is known about the problem clearly
  • Explains why the problem matters
  • Describes what a successful outcome looks like

Prior knowledge

Students can define problems using given or co-developed design criteria and independently created user stories (AC9TDI6P01). They have not yet decomposed complex real-world problems into sub-problems or justified their decomposition decisions.

Width

Minimum
Given a real-world scenario, state what is known, why the problem matters, and what a successful outcome looks like
Typical
Frame a problem from a scenario that has multiple possible interpretations; justify why the chosen framing matters for the direction of the solution
Wider
  • Apply problem framing to a context sourced from a First Nations community — how does the community's perspective shape what the problem actually is?
  • Investigate how problem framing affects solution direction — frame the same scenario two different ways and predict how each framing leads to a different solution

Hinge

In a Mode B design cycle, every downstream phase (decomposition, user stories, design, evaluation) flows from the problem frame. A weak or absent frame produces decompositions that solve the wrong problem, user stories that don't connect to a defined need, and designs that cannot be evaluated against criteria.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProblem framing requires students to make explicit what is known, why it matters, and what success looks like — three distinct questions that students commonly conflate or skip. Scaffolding the framing structure prevents students from jumping to solutions before the problem is defined.
