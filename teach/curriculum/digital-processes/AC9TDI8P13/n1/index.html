When passwords fail

AC9TDI8P13 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how a password can be compromised and why password-only security is insufficient

Lessons on this site

  • When passwords fail

Success criteria

  • Names at least two ways a password can be compromised
  • Explains why password-only security is insufficient

Prior knowledge

Students can use unique passphrases across accounts and explain the risks of password re-use (AC9TDI6P09). They understand password-based security but have not yet explored what happens when passwords are compromised, how MFA adds a second layer of protection, or how phishing attacks exploit human behaviour.

Width

Minimum
Name at least two ways a password can be compromised (e.g. data breach, guessing, shoulder surfing); explain why this means passwords alone are not enough
Typical
Explain how password reuse amplifies the impact of a single breach; connect to prior knowledge about unique passphrases
Wider
  • Limited width — password compromise is a small set of scenarios. The genuine breadth comes from how MFA and phishing awareness respond to these scenarios at later nodes.

Hinge

Students who do not understand how passwords can be compromised cannot reason about why MFA is necessary at Node 2. The attack scenario (password obtained → attacker tries to log in → MFA blocks them) only makes sense if students understand the first step. Without it, MFA appears as arbitrary additional friction rather than a principled response.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who understand only one attack vector (e.g. guessing) do not grasp why password-only security is fundamentally insufficient. Seeing multiple compromise vectors (breach, phishing, shoulder surfing, reuse amplification) establishes that no single password hygiene practice is enough — which is why MFA exists.
