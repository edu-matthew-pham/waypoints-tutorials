Manage digital footprint data

AC9TDI8P14 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

Investigate and manage the digital footprint existing systems and student solutions collect and assess if the data is essential to their purpose

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how their digital footprint is created and permanent, and consider privacy when collecting user data (AC9TDI6P10). They understand that a footprint exists but have not yet investigated what specific data systems collect, assessed whether that data is essential, or managed their own footprint settings.

By the end

Students investigate what digital footprint data existing systems and student solutions collect, manage their own footprint by reviewing privacy settings, and assess whether the data collected is essential to the system's purpose.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • investigating the ethical obligations of individuals and organisations regarding ownership and privacy of data and information by researching an online platform's privacy policy for data collection, use and storage information and discussing impacts on digital footprint
  • reviewing and managing their digital footprint across online digital tools that they use, for example selecting their default privacy and sharing settings on social media accounts
  • investigating how recommendation algorithms used in media services rely on data that tracks user habits, for example how music streaming services generate playlists that contain songs from genres and artists that are similar to those you listen to regularly
  • assessing the appropriateness and relevance of data collected by surveys from other students and organisations they complete online, for example identifying that providing your address data is not necessary for a survey asking about your food preferences but providing the address for the Census would be appropriate
  • explaining the risks associated with sharing personal data due to the ease with which generative AI models can create new content; for example, from short videos and audio recordings it is possible for convincing deepfake videos to be generated and distributed for malicious purposes

Waypoints

  • 1. What data systems collectHingeStudents can investigate what digital footprint data a system collects about its users
  • 2. Assessing whether data is essentialStudents can assess whether the data a system collects is essential to its purpose, or whether it exceeds what is needed
  • 3. Managing your digital footprintStudents can review and manage their own digital footprint by adjusting privacy settings and making informed decisions about data sharing
