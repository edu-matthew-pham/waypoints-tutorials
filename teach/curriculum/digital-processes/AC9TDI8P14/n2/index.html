Assessing whether data is essential

AC9TDI8P14 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can assess whether the data a system collects is essential to its purpose, or whether it exceeds what is needed

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one essential and one non-essential data item for a given system
  • Explains why each is or is not essential to the system's purpose

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What data systems collect.

Width

Minimum
Given a system and the data it collects, identify at least one piece of data that is essential to its purpose and one that is not; explain the difference
Typical
Assess the appropriateness of data collected by surveys or platforms — for example, identifying that providing your address is not necessary for a food preferences survey but is appropriate for a Census
Wider
  • Evaluate whether the data collected by a student-built solution is essential — apply the same critical lens to their own design decisions
  • Research how the Australian Privacy Principles define data minimisation and apply this standard to a real platform

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkAssessing whether data is essential requires students to reason from purpose: what does this system do? → what data does it need to do that? → is this collected item necessary for that purpose? Students who cannot articulate the system's purpose cannot make the essential/non-essential distinction — they need the reasoning chain, not just the binary label.
