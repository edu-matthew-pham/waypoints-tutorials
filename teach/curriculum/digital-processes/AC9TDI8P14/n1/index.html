What data systems collect

AC9TDI8P14 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate what digital footprint data a system collects about its users

Lessons on this site

  • Your digital footprint

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three types of data a platform collects
  • Includes at least one type of data collected passively (not explicitly provided by the user)

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how their digital footprint is created and permanent, and consider privacy when collecting user data (AC9TDI6P10). They understand that a footprint exists but have not yet investigated what specific data systems collect, assessed whether that data is essential, or managed their own footprint settings.

Width

Minimum
Given a platform or app, identify at least three types of data it collects about users (e.g. location, browsing history, contacts, usage patterns)
Typical
Research a platform's privacy policy to identify what data is collected, how it is used, and how long it is stored; compare data collection across two platforms
Wider
  • Investigate how recommendation algorithms used in music or video streaming services rely on tracking user habits to generate personalised content
  • Research how data collection practices differ between platforms marketed to young people vs adults

Hinge

Students who cannot name what categories of data a system collects cannot assess whether that data is essential (Node 2) or make informed decisions about privacy settings (Node 3). The investigation at Node 1 is the factual foundation for both subsequent analytical tasks.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepInvestigating data collection across different platform types (social media, streaming services, youth-targeted apps) reveals that collection practices vary systematically with business model and audience. Multiple platform contexts help students see the pattern rather than treating a single platform as representative.
