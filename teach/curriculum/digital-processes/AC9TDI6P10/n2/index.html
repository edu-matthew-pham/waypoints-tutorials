Data minimisation — collecting only what is needed

AC9TDI6P10 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why collecting the minimum data needed for a purpose protects user privacy

Success criteria

  • Explains why collecting unnecessary data creates privacy risk
  • Identifies at least one example of unnecessary data collection and what could be collected instead

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How digital footprints are created and persist.

Width

Minimum
Give one example where collecting unnecessary personal data creates a privacy risk — and identify what could be collected instead
Typical
Explain the principle that systems should collect the minimum data needed for their purpose; apply this to at least two scenarios — identifying what data is necessary, what is unnecessary, and what harm unnecessary collection creates
Wider
  • Evaluate a sign-up form for an app and identify which fields are necessary for the stated purpose and which could be removed without affecting function — applying the data minimisation principle as a designer
  • Explain how collecting personal data to train generative AI models creates a long-term privacy risk — personal information shared once could surface in AI outputs far in the future

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents typically think of privacy as about keeping secrets, not about what should be collected in the first place. The data minimisation framing is counter-intuitive — it requires students to think as designers, not just users. Multiple scenarios (form design, AI training data) help students apply the principle from a design perspective rather than a personal-risk perspective.
