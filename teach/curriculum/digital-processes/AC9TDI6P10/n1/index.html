How digital footprints are created and persist

AC9TDI6P10 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Digital Technologies (processes) · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Digital Technologies (processes): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how a digital footprint is created and why it persists over time

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one digital action and explains how it creates a persistent record
  • Explains why deleting a post may not remove the digital footprint it created

Prior knowledge

Students can identify what personal data is stored and shared in their online accounts and discuss associated risks (AC9TDI4P09). They understand that accounts hold personal data. They have not yet understood the concept of digital footprint as cumulative and permanent, or considered data minimisation as a design principle when collecting user data.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one scenario where a digital action creates a persistent record that could be resurfaced later
Typical
Explain how online actions — comments, posts, images, search history — create records that persist and can be associated with a person later; describe at least two different ways a digital footprint is created and explain why deletion is not always effective
Wider
  • Investigate how a comment made on a social media post or video links the commenter's identity to both the comment and the content — explaining why that association is permanent even if the post is later deleted
  • Explore how digital footprints accumulate over time — explaining that data from multiple platforms and years can be combined to build a detailed profile

Hinge

Students who do not understand that digital footprints are permanent and cumulative cannot engage meaningfully with N2's data minimisation reasoning — the design principle of 'collect only what is needed' requires understanding why unnecessary data collection has lasting consequences.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to think of digital actions as ephemeral — 'I can just delete it'. Multiple contexts where deletion does not remove the trace (comment linked to another user's post, cached search results, data already shared with a third party) help students understand permanence as a structural feature, not a privacy setting.
