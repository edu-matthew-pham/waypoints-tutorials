Locating and identifying information in texts

AC9LCH4C03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can locate familiar words and key information in a range of text types and identify the purpose, main content and key features of the text.

Success criteria

  • Locates at least three key pieces of information from a familiar text
  • Identifies the main purpose of the text and at least one key feature of its format

Prior knowledge

Students can locate key information in familiar texts and respond with opinions and retelling using modelled language (AC9LCH2C03). They can identify main points and make personal connections. They have not yet gathered, organised and re-presented information from multiple sources, nor conducted interviews or produced structured summaries or responses for an audience.

Width

Minimum
Locate at least three key pieces of information in a familiar text and identify the text's main purpose.
Typical
Locate familiar characters, words or information in spoken, written and multimodal texts; identify main purpose and meaning; discuss key features of different text types (stories, informative texts, songs, packaging, educational programs); notice how First Nations Australian languages change according to relationships.
Wider
  • Compare how the same information is presented in two different text types or formats — identify what is the same and what is different about how meaning is conveyed
  • Investigate a real-world text (packaging, sign, advertisement) in the target language — identify all the information it contains and explain how the visual and written elements work together

Hinge

Locating and identifying information is the prerequisite for all text response and re-presentation. Students who cannot extract key information from texts cannot organise, summarise or present it in a different form — the core demand of this descriptor.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepInformation location strategies transfer across text types — stories, songs, packaging, educational media, informative texts. Practising with varied text types builds flexible comprehension strategies that work beyond a single familiar format.
