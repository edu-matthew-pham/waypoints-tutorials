Gathering and organising information from multiple sources

AC9LCH4C03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can gather information from more than one source and organise it to present findings in a different form.

Success criteria

  • Gathers information from at least two sources
  • Organises and presents the information in a different form — timeline, graph, table, or summary

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Locating and identifying information in texts.

Width

Minimum
Gather information from at least two sources and organise findings into a different format — for example, a timeline, graph, table, or summary list.
Typical
Gather information from a range of sources — interviews, texts, surveys, media — and organise findings in a new format; create timelines, picture graphs, annotated displays or summaries; interview family or peers to collect information about cultural practices, languages or experiences.
Wider
  • Compare findings gathered from different sources — identify where sources agree, differ, or complement each other, and discuss why
  • Design and conduct a class survey, analyse the results, and present findings in an annotated target language graph or display

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkGathering from multiple sources and re-presenting requires students to hold information across sources and decide how to organise it. Scaffolded organisational tools — templates, graphic organisers, guided interview questions — support this cognitive work before students can manage it independently.
