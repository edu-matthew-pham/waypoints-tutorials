Evaluating formal and policy texts

AC9LCH10C03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can access and evaluate formal text types — policies, procedures, official documents, news — assessing their validity, reliability and credibility, and citing sources in target language.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least two formal texts using explicit credibility criteria
  • Cites at least one source appropriately in target language

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret and analyse texts with cultural and critical awareness, evaluate source credibility, and respond with empathy and critical stance (AC9LCH8C03). They have analysed register, humour and cultural reference. They have not yet evaluated texts using formal analytical frameworks, used public speaking and debating strategies, accessed formal text types such as official policies, or referenced sources in target language academic contexts.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least two formal target language texts for validity, reliability and credibility using explicit criteria, and cite at least one source appropriately in target language.
Typical
Access formal text types — emergency procedures, policies, news, academic resources; evaluate validity, reliability and credibility; gather and cite references in target language; use formal analytical language to discuss and evaluate; compare formal target language sources with English equivalents.
Wider
  • Conduct a research task using formal target language sources — evaluate source quality, synthesise across sources, and present findings with citations in target language
  • Compare how credibility is signalled differently in target language and English formal texts — identify differences in citation conventions, author authority markers, and evidence presentation

Hinge

Formal text evaluation with citation is the prerequisite for all analytical and academic response work at Y9-10. Students who cannot evaluate formal sources or cite them appropriately cannot produce the evaluative, evidence-based responses the descriptor and creating strand require.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkFormal text evaluation and citation require explicit instruction on the criteria and conventions — these are not intuitive. Explicit instruction on formal source evaluation frameworks and target language citation conventions, followed by guided practice, builds the academic literacy capacity needed.
