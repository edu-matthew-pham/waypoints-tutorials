Similarities and differences between target language and English using metalanguage

AC9LCH8U03 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

reflect on and explain similarities and differences between the target language and English language structures and features, using metalanguage

Prior knowledge

Students can compare language structures and features across the target language and English using metalanguage, and produce comparative analyses (AC9LCH6U03). They have compared text types, grammar and cohesive devices. They have not yet taught younger students using comparative frameworks, explored metaphorical versus literal meaning across languages, or applied comparative understanding to unlock unfamiliar content.

By the end

Students reflect on and explain similarities and differences between the target language and English across a range of text types and grammatical features — using metalanguage to extend and apply comparative understanding, including to unfamiliar content and to support others' learning.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • extending understanding of metalanguage about a range of textual features in target language and English
  • identifying and comparing language features in target language and English texts
  • teaching younger students about some key differences between the target language and English using support resources
  • applying understanding of key features of familiar text types to understand unfamiliar content
  • exploring the way content is developed in different types of texts
  • reading samples of formal texts and identifying how language compares with everyday speech
  • exploring metaphorical and literal meaning in target language and English texts

Waypoints

  • 1. Extended metalanguage for text and grammar analysisHingeStudents can use extended metalanguage to describe and explain a range of text-level and grammatical features in both the target language and English.
  • 2. Metaphorical and literal meaning across languagesStudents can identify and compare metaphorical and literal meaning in target language and English texts, and explain how metaphor functions differently across the two languages.
  • 3. Applying comparative knowledge to support learningStudents can apply comparative language knowledge to access unfamiliar content and to support younger learners' understanding of key language differences.
