Apply target language sound system

AC9LS10EU01 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply features of the target language sound system, including pitch, rhythm, stress, pronunciation and intonation, and show how these are represented in familiar and some unfamiliar contexts

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and use features of the target language sound system including vowels, consonants, intonation and stress in familiar contexts (AC9LS8EU01). They have not yet applied these features independently in unfamiliar contexts or reflected on regional pronunciation variation across target language communities.

By the end

Students apply features of the target language sound system — including pitch, rhythm, stress, pronunciation and intonation — in familiar and some unfamiliar spoken contexts, and demonstrate understanding of how these are represented in the writing system, including awareness of regional variation.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising the importance of appropriate intonation, stress, tone and pronunciation for making meaning
  • pronouncing consonants according to vowel combinations and double consonants
  • understanding variation in pronunciation across the target language speaking world
  • using the target language pronunciation of letters to spell words and phrases aloud
  • listening to and reciting texts such as poems, stories, tongue twisters or song lyrics to familiarise with rhythm and intonation
  • identifying the use of pitch, rhythm, stress and intonation in different audio tracks and how they affect the spoken message

Waypoints

  • 1. Accurate and fluent pronunciationStudents can produce target language sounds, stress and intonation with sufficient accuracy and consistency to be understood in familiar and some unfamiliar contexts.
  • 2. Intonation, rhythm and communicative meaningStudents can use pitch, rhythm and intonation to convey communicative meaning — distinguishing mood, emphasis and intention in spoken target language production.
