← AC9LS10EU01 Apply target language sound system

Accurate and fluent pronunciation

AC9LS10EU01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce target language sounds, stress and intonation with sufficient accuracy and consistency to be understood in familiar and some unfamiliar contexts.

Success criteria

  • Produces a prepared spoken text with consistent and accurate vowel sounds, target consonants and sentence stress
  • Can apply phonological knowledge to attempt pronunciation of an unfamiliar word in the target language

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and use features of the target language sound system including vowels, consonants, intonation and stress in familiar contexts (AC9LS8EU01). They have not yet applied these features independently in unfamiliar contexts or reflected on regional pronunciation variation across target language communities.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate accurate production of the key phonological features of the target language (vowels, target consonants, stress patterns) in a prepared spoken text.
Typical
Apply accurate pronunciation, stress and intonation across prepared and semi-prepared spoken tasks; use phonological knowledge to support pronunciation of unfamiliar words; apply correct terminology for phonological features and marking conventions.
Wider
  • Record and critically review own spoken production — identify specific phonological features to improve and practise targeted correction
  • Research and compare pronunciation across two or more regional varieties of the target language — discuss what the variation reveals about language change and cultural identity

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsPhonological accuracy at Y9-10 is primarily a fluency issue — students have learned the rules but need continued practice to maintain accuracy under communicative pressure. Performance tasks with self-assessment and recording support the ongoing consolidation this requires.
