Intonation, rhythm and communicative meaning

AC9LS10EU01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use pitch, rhythm and intonation to convey communicative meaning — distinguishing mood, emphasis and intention in spoken target language production.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces at least two spoken utterances with audibly different intonation patterns conveying different communicative meanings
  • Can identify how intonation shifts meaning in at least one heard example from a target language audio source

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Accurate and fluent pronunciation.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate at least two distinct intonation patterns that convey different communicative meanings (e.g. certainty vs doubt, statement vs question, excitement vs neutrality) in the target language.
Typical
Use pitch, rhythm and intonation to convey mood, emphasis and communicative intent; recognise and reproduce intonation patterns from authentic audio sources; identify how intonation affects the meaning of target language utterances.
Wider
  • Analyse the use of pitch, rhythm and intonation in target language audio or video texts intended for different purposes (advertisement, news, narrative, conversation) — identify how prosodic features serve each purpose
  • Practise prosodic features through performance texts (poems, songs, speeches) and reflect on how prosody shapes audience response

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepIntonation as a meaning-making resource is only apparent across varied communicative purposes. Exposure to target language texts across multiple genres and registers allows students to observe how prosodic choices serve different communicative ends.
