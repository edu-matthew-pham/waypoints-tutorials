Apply grammar and punctuation to compose texts

AC9LS6U02 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use knowledge of modelled grammatical structures and formulaic expressions to compose and respond to texts, using appropriate punctuation and textual conventions

Prior knowledge

Students recognise and use agreement patterns, verb forms, prepositions, adverbs and conjunctions (AC9LS4U02). They apply familiar grammatical structures in modelled contexts. They have not yet used past tense in composition, near-future structures, frequency adverbs, comparatives or consolidated agreement across complex word groups.

By the end

Students apply modelled grammatical structures to compose and respond to texts — including tense variation, comparatives, frequency adverbs, expanded connectors and consolidated agreement patterns.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying appropriate writing conventions and punctuation
  • experimenting with multiple forms of 'to be' (or equivalent) in own texts to show different uses
  • constructing sentences using modelled forms of present and past tenses in context, and describing intended actions using near future structures
  • using conditional mode as a formulaic, modelled expression
  • using a range of prepositions of location to give directions
  • using a range of questions to make requests or ask for specific information
  • linking ideas and providing more information using conjunctions
  • indicating frequency using adverbs, noticing flexibility of word order
  • using ordinal numbers
  • consolidating the use of gender and number agreement
  • using a wide range of comparatives
  • applying the omission of subject pronouns by looking at verb endings
  • recognising similar spelling stems and composite words

Waypoints

  • 1. Tense variation and key verb structuresHingeStudents can use present, past and near-future tense in composition — selecting the appropriate tense for temporal meaning — and apply key verb distinctions with awareness.
  • 2. Agreement consolidation and comparativesStudents can apply agreement patterns consistently across complex word groups and use comparative structures to make comparisons.
  • 3. Frequency adverbs, expanded connectors and conventionsStudents can use frequency adverbs with flexible word order, an expanded range of connectors, and apply appropriate punctuation and textual conventions in composition.
