Facilitating and contributing to group discussions

AC9LCH10EC02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can contribute productively to group discussions with diverse views — facilitating equitable participation, reaching agreement, and managing competing perspectives.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two facilitation or negotiation strategies during a group discussion
  • Helps move the group toward a shared outcome

Prior knowledge

Students can plan, transact and negotiate with peers and produce bilingual community resources (AC9LCH8EC02). They have collaborated on cultural events and community-facing projects. They have not yet led or facilitated live or online events for community audiences, contributed to sister school negotiations, or produced bilingual community initiatives on social issues.

Width

Minimum
Contribute to a group discussion using at least two facilitation or negotiation strategies to support equitable participation and move toward a shared outcome.
Typical
Plan and facilitate live or online events; collaborate to reach agreement by equally contributing ideas; facilitate student-led activities; contribute to collaborative reports and articles on social issues; negotiate and justify decisions with diverse peers.
Wider
  • Lead a discussion where views conflict — facilitate negotiation and bring the group to a productive outcome using target language
  • Compare group discussion facilitation conventions in the target language community with Australian school contexts — identify cultural differences in how participation, turn-taking and consensus-building are managed

Hinge

Group discussion facilitation is the prerequisite for all community-facing collaborative activity. Students who can only contribute without facilitating cannot lead the community events and collaborative productions this descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkFacilitation requires students to manage group dynamics explicitly — ensuring inclusion, managing conflict, building consensus. Explicit facilitation strategy instruction followed by practice in genuinely diverse discussion contexts builds this capacity.
