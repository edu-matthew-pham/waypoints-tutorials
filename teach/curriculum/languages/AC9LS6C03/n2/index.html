← AC9LS6C03 Locate, process and respond to information in texts

Responding to texts with personal reactions and comparisons

AC9LS6C03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can respond to target language texts in varied ways — expressing personal reactions, opinions and comparisons about characters, ideas and cultural elements.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Expresses a personal reaction to a text using evaluative language
  • Produces at least one comparison across two texts, characters or ideas

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Researching and processing information across sources.

Width

Minimum
Express a personal reaction to at least one text using target language evaluative language; produce at least one comparison across two texts or characters.
Typical
Compare main characters, events and ideas across texts; discuss key messages and cultural elements; respond to artworks and images with opinions; respond to First Nations Australian authors' stories in the target language; respond to questions about characters, values and ideas.
Wider
  • Produce a comparative written response identifying similarities and differences between two texts — using comparison language structures in the target language
  • Respond to a First Nations Australian cultural story — addressing both the story's content and its cultural significance

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyEvaluative and comparative response language develops through encountering varied texts and practising different response types. Students need a bank of evaluative and comparative expressions before they can respond genuinely.
