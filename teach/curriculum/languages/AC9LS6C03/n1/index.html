← AC9LS6C03 Locate, process and respond to information in texts

Researching and processing information across sources

AC9LS6C03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can research topics of interest across multiple target language sources, extracting and organising information for a specified purpose.

Success criteria

  • Locates and extracts information from at least two sources without direct teacher prompting
  • Organises information in a format appropriate to the research purpose

Prior knowledge

Students can locate and respond to key information in familiar texts and categorise it using graphic organisers (AC9LS4C03). They extract information from prepared texts. They have not yet researched independently across multiple sources, processed information for specific purposes, or compared ideas across imaginative texts.

Width

Minimum
Research a topic using at least two sources and organise key information in an appropriate format.
Typical
Research topics across varied source types — print, digital, spoken, multimodal; extract key points about life in target language communities; answer questions about researched content; extract procedural language; use graphic organisers suited to purpose; read texts about real-world situations to inform decisions.
Wider
  • Research a topic across target language and English sources — compare what each includes and excludes, and explain what each perspective adds
  • Evaluate two target language sources on the same topic for reliability — identify which is more credible and explain why

Hinge

Independent multi-source research is the prerequisite for the evaluation and synthesis demands of subsequent bands. Students who can only process teacher-prepared texts cannot develop source evaluation and synthesis skills.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepResearch skills develop through varied source types and topic conditions. Each new source type builds a new strategy — varied conditions build transferable research capacity.
