← AC9LCH4U03 Compare features of target language and English texts

Genre features and text purpose

AC9LCH4U03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify features of different text genres in the target language and English, and discuss what makes each genre appropriate for its purpose.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two text genres in the target language with at least two distinguishing features each
  • Can explain which genre would be most appropriate for a given purpose and why

Prior knowledge

Students can compare structural features, vocabulary and text features across the target language and English using simple metalanguage (AC9LCH2U03). They have used metalanguage for basic word types. They have not yet compared texts systematically by genre or feature type, used metalanguage to describe grammar rules, or analysed how English influences everyday target language use.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two different text genres in the target language (e.g. narrative, procedural, informative) and describe at least two features that distinguish each genre.
Typical
Recognise features of narratives, procedural and explanatory texts; identify language features and symbols associated with different text types; compare the features of the same genre across target language and English texts; discuss which genres suit different purposes and audiences.
Wider
  • Compare the same genre text in target language and English — identify all the structural and language feature differences and discuss what they reveal about how the genre convention differs across the two languages
  • Create a short genre comparison guide for a peer — explaining how to recognise a particular genre in both languages and what conventions must be followed

Hinge

Genre feature knowledge is the prerequisite for all text comparison work. Students who cannot identify genre-level features cannot compare texts systematically — they can only notice surface differences. Genre awareness also underpins all text creation in the creating strand.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkGenre analysis requires students to apply a framework — what are the features of this genre? — to both familiar and new texts. Explicit genre study with named features (orientation, complication, resolution; materials, steps, result) builds the analytical vocabulary before students can compare independently.
