Identifying key words and main points in texts

AC9LCH2C03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify key words and main points in familiar spoken and written texts, demonstrating understanding through gesture, image, ordering or brief response.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three key words from a familiar text when prompted
  • Demonstrates understanding of a main point through gesture, image, sequencing or spoken response

Prior knowledge

Students can participate in classroom interactions and collaborative activities using modelled expressions (AC9LCH2C01, AC9LCH2C02). They can follow spoken instructions and respond through action. They have not yet systematically located and extracted key information from texts, made connections between text content and their own knowledge, or responded to texts with structured opinions or retelling.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three key words or one main point from a familiar spoken or written text, demonstrating understanding through gesture, image selection or spoken response.
Typical
Listen to, read or view familiar texts and identify key words; demonstrate understanding of main points through sequencing pictures, creating captions, drawing scenes or responding to guided questions; use world knowledge to infer meaning of unfamiliar words from context.
Wider
  • Identify key words and main points across different text types — spoken story, rhyme, cartoon, and written text — comparing how information is conveyed in each
  • Use knowledge of the world to infer the meaning of unfamiliar target language words in context, explaining the reasoning used

Hinge

Identifying key words and main points is the prerequisite for all text response work. Students who cannot locate information in a text cannot make personal connections, express opinions, or retell storylines — the higher-order demands of this descriptor and the creating strand.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyKey word identification requires students to encounter a growing range of text types — stories, rhymes, songs, cartoons, informative texts — to build the reading and listening strategies that transfer across formats. Repeated exposure with varied texts builds this generalised comprehension capacity.
