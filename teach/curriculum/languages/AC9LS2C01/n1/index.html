Recognising and responding to greetings and farewells

AC9LS2C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and respond appropriately to modelled greetings and farewells — selecting responses appropriate to time of day, relationship and context.

Success criteria

  • Responds correctly to at least three different greeting or farewell exchanges using appropriate target language or action
  • Uses at least two formulaic expressions (thanks, apology, praise) correctly in context

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise meaning and communicate using modelled words, gestures and formulaic phrases with support (AC9LSF01). They have no formal curriculum experience with structured classroom routines, following instructions, or producing personal introductions in the target language.

Width

Minimum
Respond correctly to at least three different greeting and farewell exchanges using appropriate target language or action.
Typical
Participate in greetings and farewells across varied time-of-day and relationship contexts; use formulaic expressions for thanks, apologies, praise and well wishes; recognise and use expressions for special occasions.
Wider
  • Initiate a greeting independently using the appropriate contextual form without teacher prompting
  • Explain to a peer or family member the meaning of two or three greeting expressions and when they are used

Hinge

Greeting and farewell routines are the daily entry point for all classroom target language use. Students who cannot manage these exchanges are excluded from the social fabric of the language classroom and cannot engage with instruction or personal introduction work.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsGreeting and farewell language becomes automatic only through daily repetition in real routine contexts — the routine itself is the practice vehicle.
