Articles, agreement and determiner refinement

AC9LS10EU02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply article and agreement rules with increased accuracy, including in cases where the target language differs from expected patterns.

Success criteria

  • Applies article and agreement rules correctly in complex noun phrases including at least one exception or nuanced case
  • Self-corrects agreement errors during drafting when reviewing own work

Prior knowledge

Students have developed knowledge of and can use key grammatical structures and writing system features of the target language — including agreement, verb conjugation, sentence construction and orthographic conventions (AC9LS8EU02). They have not yet selected and used more complex grammatical structures (comparatives, multiple tenses, complex sentences) to enhance meaning purposefully.

Width

Minimum
Apply definite and indefinite article rules correctly in at least five phrases, including at least one case that involves an exception or nuance from the basic rule.
Typical
Use articles, possessives and demonstratives with accurate gender and number agreement; handle common exceptions and nuanced cases (e.g. omission rules, collective nouns, irregular patterns); apply agreement across complex noun phrases.
Wider
  • Investigate how article use in the target language differs from English across specific contexts — create a reference guide for cases that are consistently challenging
  • Analyse a native speaker text for article usage — identify patterns that go beyond the rules taught and hypothesise about the additional conventions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAgreement accuracy is a fluency issue at Y9-10 — students typically understand rules but apply them inconsistently in production. Focused practice with nuanced cases and self-correction in drafting builds reliable application.
