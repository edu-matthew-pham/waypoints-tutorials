Negotiating outcomes with diverse views

AC9LCH10C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can contribute productively to discussions where diverse views must be negotiated — managing disagreement, incorporating different perspectives, and working toward a shared outcome.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two negotiation strategies during a discussion with diverse perspectives
  • Works toward a shared outcome without abandoning the target language

Prior knowledge

Students can collaborate using transacting, negotiating and justifying language to plan events (AC9LCH8C02). They have conducted community interviews and planned community-facing events. They have not yet organised authentic cultural experiences for school or community contexts, contributed to collaborative digital platforms, or collected and analysed community data for presentations.

Width

Minimum
Contribute to a discussion with diverse perspectives, using at least two negotiation strategies (acknowledging a different view, finding common ground, or proposing a compromise) to work toward an outcome.
Typical
Participate in discussions involving diverse cultural experiences and views; manage disagreements productively; incorporate different perspectives into planning; negotiate outcomes that address competing interests; promote inclusive approaches to community activities.
Wider
  • Lead a discussion where views conflict — facilitate productive negotiation and bring the group to a shared outcome using target language
  • Compare negotiation strategies across cultural contexts — identify how the target language community manages disagreement differently from Australian contexts and discuss implications for intercultural collaboration

Hinge

Negotiating diverse views is the core demand of this descriptor and the prerequisite for all community-facing collaborative activity. Students who can only interact with like-minded partners cannot manage the genuine diversity of perspectives that real community and cultural engagement requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProductive negotiation with diverse views requires students to understand others' positions before responding — a listening-first, reasoning-second discipline. Explicit negotiation strategy instruction followed by practice in genuinely diverse discussion contexts builds this capacity.
