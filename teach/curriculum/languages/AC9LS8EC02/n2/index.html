← AC9LS8EC02 Develop language for classroom exchanges

Asking for help, information and permission

AC9LS8EC02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use modelled phrases to ask for help, clarification or permission in the target language.

Success criteria

  • Can produce at least two contextually appropriate asking phrases in the target language without prompting
  • Can distinguish between formal and informal address when making a request

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Following and giving classroom instructions.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two modelled phrases for asking for help or permission in a classroom context.
Typical
Use a set of modelled phrases to ask for help, check understanding, request repetition, ask for information or seek permission; distinguish appropriately between formal and informal forms of address when making requests.
Wider
  • Extend asking strategies to unfamiliar contexts — e.g. a simulated phone call, an email to a teacher, or a transaction scenario
  • Develop a personal 'survival phrase' card listing key asking strategies and add to it across the unit

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a bank of asking strategies rather than one memorised phrase. Exposure to multiple modelled forms across varied asking scenarios builds the flexibility needed to manage breakdowns in communication.
