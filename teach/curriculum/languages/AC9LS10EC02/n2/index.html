← AC9LS10EC02 Question, compare and discuss ideas

Offering and justifying opinions in discussion

AC9LS10EC02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can express and justify opinions on topics of current or social interest in the target language, using appropriate discussion language.

Success criteria

  • Expresses an opinion with at least one justification in the target language
  • Can identify and respond to a counter-argument rather than simply restating their original position

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Questioning and probing ideas.

Width

Minimum
Express and briefly justify at least one opinion on a current or social topic in the target language, using at least one justification structure.
Typical
Use a range of structures to express, justify and nuance opinions; identify pros, cons and limitations of ideas; use persuasion language and slogans or rhetorical devices where appropriate.
Wider
  • Participate in a structured class debate on a current issue in the target language — argue an assigned position and respond to counter-arguments
  • Compose a short opinion text (letter, post, spoken statement) for a real or simulated audience on a topic of personal or social relevance

Hinge

Justified opinion expression is the communicative core of EC02. Students who can only assert ('I think it's good') without justification ('because...', 'however...', 'this shows that...') cannot participate meaningfully in discussion or debate. This also underpins EC06's persuasive text creation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkJustified opinions require explicit reasoning scaffolds — students need modelled examples of how justification structures work in the target language before they can deploy them independently. Structured debate and opinion frames provide the scaffold.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepOpinion expression should be practised across a range of topics so that the structures transfer beyond one familiar topic area.
