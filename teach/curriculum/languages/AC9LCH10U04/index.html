Identity shaped by language, culture and values — evaluate

AC9LCH10U04 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

reflect on and evaluate how their own and others' identity is shaped by language(s), culture(s), attitudes, beliefs and values, and how these affect ways of communicating

Prior knowledge

Students can reflect on and explain how their own and others' identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes and values, and can identify stereotyping risks in intercultural interaction (AC9LCH8U04). They have examined cultural participation and communication adaptation. They have not yet evaluated the impact of globalisation on cultural identity, analysed how title and address conventions reflect cultural values, or reflected on non-verbal cultural behaviour and dress codes as communication.

By the end

Students reflect on and evaluate how their own and others' identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values — including the impact of globalisation, loan words, title conventions, non-verbal behaviour, and the ongoing relationship between language change and cultural identity.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • reflecting on how own identity is shaped by attitudes, beliefs, values and languages used every day
  • showing how the use of titles indicates respect or formality compared with Australian culture
  • indicating the impact of globalisation on cultural change and own identity and beliefs
  • reflecting on and explaining the protocols required to authentically co-create an Acknowledgement of Country/Place with a First Nations Australian
  • demonstrating the impact of loan words and internet language on changes in modern languages
  • reflecting on how non-verbal cultural behaviour such as dress codes affect communication in different cultural settings

Waypoints

  • 1. Evaluating globalisation's impact on language and identityHingeStudents can evaluate how globalisation has shaped modern target language use — including loan words, internet language, and cultural change — and reflect on the impact on their own identity and beliefs.
  • 2. Title conventions and non-verbal cultural behaviourStudents can explain how title and address conventions in the target language reflect cultural values of respect and formality, and how non-verbal cultural behaviour — dress codes, body language, space — affects communication.
  • 3. Evaluating own multilingual identityStudents can evaluate how their own multilingual and multicultural identity has been shaped by the languages, cultures, attitudes and values they have encountered, and how this shapes their ways of communicating.
