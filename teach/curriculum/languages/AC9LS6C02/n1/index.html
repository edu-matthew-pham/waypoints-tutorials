← AC9LS6C02 Plan and negotiate with others

Expressing preferences and ideas in planning

AC9LS6C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can express preferences and ideas in collaborative planning contexts — contributing personal views and negotiating with others about options and decisions.

Success criteria

  • Expresses at least two preferences or ideas in a collaborative planning task
  • Responds to a partner's contribution — building on, questioning or negotiating rather than simply accepting

Prior knowledge

Students can participate in collaborative planning activities using familiar phrases and modelled structures (AC9LS4C02). They allocate tasks and follow sequences. They have not yet used language to express and negotiate preferences and ideas — only to coordinate task allocation.

Width

Minimum
Express at least two preferences or ideas in a planning activity and respond to at least one partner's preference or idea.
Typical
Express and negotiate preferences across planning contexts; check understanding and progress during tasks; delegate roles and tasks; develop questions for guests or interviews; promote participation in events using target language.
Wider
  • Lead a planning negotiation — ensure all group members contribute a preference and that the final decision reflects genuine compromise
  • Conduct a simulated transaction involving genuine negotiation over options and decisions

Hinge

Expressing preferences in collaborative contexts — contributing personal views not only allocating tasks — is what distinguishes planning from coordination. Students who cannot negotiate preferences cannot engage with the opinion-exchange demands that continue through subsequent bands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPreference expression in planning requires students to move from describing tasks to advocating for options. Structured planning tasks with open decision points scaffold this move.
