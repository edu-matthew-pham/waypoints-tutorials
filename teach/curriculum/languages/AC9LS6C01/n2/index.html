← AC9LS6C01 Initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar contexts

Sustaining exchanges using conversational strategies

AC9LS6C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can sustain a modelled exchange using conversational strategies — showing interest, asking follow-up questions and using fillers and reaction expressions.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Sustains an exchange for at least four turns using at least two conversational strategies
  • Uses at least one reaction expression or filler that shows genuine response to a partner's contribution

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Expressing feelings, emotions and opinions.

Width

Minimum
Sustain an exchange for at least four turns using at least two different conversational strategies.
Typical
Use conversational maintenance strategies — asking follow-up questions, using fillers and acknowledgement expressions, showing reactions; sustain exchanges using modelled conversation patterns; use target language for expressing agreement, disagreement and interest.
Wider
  • Sustain an exchange on an unexpected topic — using maintenance strategies to keep the conversation going without preparation
  • Analyse a recorded exchange and identify every maintenance strategy used — categorise by type and evaluate which were most effective

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepConversational sustainability develops through varied partner and topic conditions. Maintenance strategies need to transfer across varied topics and partners before they become genuinely available conversational tools.
