Expressing feelings, emotions and opinions

AC9LS6C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can express feelings, emotions, preferences and opinions in the target language — going beyond information exchange to personal response.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Expresses at least two different feelings or emotions using correct target language structures
  • Expresses at least one preference or opinion with a supporting reason or elaboration

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate exchanges and respond to modelled questions about self, others and classroom environment (AC9LS4C01). They use formulaic expressions and modelled structures. They have not yet sustained exchanges beyond information exchange — including expressing feelings, opinions, preferences and using conversational maintenance strategies.

Width

Minimum
Express at least two feelings or emotions and at least one preference or opinion using appropriate target language structures.
Typical
Express feelings and emotions about experiences; express preferences and opinions with supporting reasons; apologise and express concern or sympathy; use appropriate register for formal and informal contexts; share personal information about hopes and future plans.
Wider
  • Express a nuanced emotional response — not only a simple feeling but an explanation of why, connected to a specific experience
  • Compare how opinions and preferences are expressed differently in the target language and English — identify where the target language requires more explicit grammatical marking

Hinge

Moving beyond information exchange to expressing feelings and opinions is the developmental leap between 3-4 and 5-6. Students who can only exchange factual information cannot sustain exchanges about personal worlds and cannot reach the opinion and negotiation demands of subsequent standards.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyExpressing feelings and opinions requires a bank of structures across emotional and evaluative domains. Students need varied practice expressing feelings about different experiences before this vocabulary becomes spontaneously available.
