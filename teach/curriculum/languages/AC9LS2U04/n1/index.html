Diversity of target language communities and variation

AC9LS2U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 1–2 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can notice that the target language is spoken in many different communities and that language use varies across these communities.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two target language communities and locates them on a world map
  • Identifies at least one way language varies across target language communities

Prior knowledge

Students have noticed connections between language and culture (AC9LSF03) and that greetings carry cultural meaning (AC9LS2C04). They have not yet explored the diversity of target language communities, regional language variation, or the relationship between language use and cultural identity more broadly.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two target language communities or countries and at least one way language varies between them.
Typical
Locate target language communities on a world map; recognise that the target language is spoken with cultural and linguistic variation across many communities; notice vocabulary or expression differences across regions; understand that some people speak more than one language.
Wider
  • Research one specific target language community — find two or three cultural practices reflected in the language used there and share findings
  • Compare the symbols and expressions of two target language communities — identify what they reveal about each community's identity

Hinge

Understanding that the target language is spoken by diverse communities — not a single monolithic culture — is the conceptual foundation for all subsequent cultural and intercultural work. Students who imagine a single homogeneous target language culture cannot engage with genuine cultural complexity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepDiversity awareness is built by encountering multiple communities and language variation examples. Single-community exposure produces a narrow and misleading picture of the target language.
