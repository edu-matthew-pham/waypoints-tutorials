← AC9LS10U01 Apply spoken conventions for fluency in familiar and unfamiliar contexts

Fluency in unfamiliar contexts and spoken text creation

AC9LS10U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can respond and create with adequate fluency in unfamiliar spoken contexts — applying all prior spoken conventions in extended unrehearsed production.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces an extended spoken text in an unfamiliar context with fluency adequate for communicative purpose
  • Applies natural speech features in at least one unrehearsed spoken context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Natural speech features and extended sentence stress.

Width

Minimum
Produce an extended spoken text in an unfamiliar context with fluency adequate for the communicative purpose.
Typical
Apply spoken conventions in unfamiliar and spontaneous contexts; create extended spoken texts — presentations, debates, interviews, performances — applying prosodic features deliberately; sustain spoken interaction without excessive hesitation or code-switching.
Wider
  • Participate in a fully spontaneous spoken exchange on an unfamiliar topic — sustaining fluency for at least five minutes
  • Create and perform an original spoken text with deliberate prosodic design — demonstrate that intonation, stress and rhythm serve the communicative and expressive purpose

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFluency in unfamiliar contexts requires encountering genuinely varied and unpredictable spoken conditions. Rehearsed performance does not build the spontaneous spoken fluency that authentic interaction demands.
