← AC9LS10U01 Apply spoken conventions for fluency in familiar and unfamiliar contexts

Natural speech features and extended sentence stress

AC9LS10U01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply natural speech features — contractions, elision, rhythm — and place stress correctly in extended and complex sentences.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates at least two natural speech features in connected speech
  • Places stress correctly across at least two complex sentences

Prior knowledge

Students apply spoken target language conventions including consonant nuances, sentence-level stress, written doubling conventions and extended intonation (AC9LS8U01). They apply these in familiar contexts. They have not yet applied spoken conventions in genuinely unfamiliar contexts, nor created extended spoken texts for real audiences.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate at least two natural speech features in connected speech; place stress correctly across at least two complex sentences.
Typical
Apply contractions, elision and natural rhythm in connected speech; maintain stress patterns in complex sentences; develop fluency through tongue twisters, reading aloud and performance; apply spoken conventions in unrehearsed contexts.
Wider
  • Produce a recording of connected speech demonstrating natural rhythm, elision and stress — compare with a native speaker sample and identify remaining differences
  • Apply spoken conventions in a genuine unrehearsed context — demonstrating that prosodic features are automatic rather than deliberate

Hinge

Natural speech features — contractions, elision, natural rhythm — are what distinguish fluent from stilted spoken production. Students who speak only in citation forms sound unnatural and are difficult to understand in authentic interaction.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsNatural speech features become automatic only through high-frequency production in varied spoken contexts — including unrehearsed conditions. Rehearsed accuracy does not transfer automatically to natural speech.
