Further tense forms and conditional structures

AC9LS10U02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use further tense forms for continuous or recent action, and conditional or hypothetical structures in composition.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least one further tense form correctly in composition
  • Uses at least one conditional or hypothetical structure correctly

Prior knowledge

Students apply grammatical structures including irregular verbs across tenses, expanded cohesive devices, quantity expressions and adverbial modification (AC9LS8U02). They compose in past, present and compound tenses. They have not yet worked with further tense forms for continuous or recent action, conditional or subjunctive structures, or passive constructions.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one further tense form for continuous or recent action correctly; use at least one conditional or hypothetical structure correctly.
Typical
Apply further tense forms for continuous action or recently completed events; use conditional structures for real and hypothetical scenarios; use hypothetical or subjunctive forms where appropriate in the target language; apply perfect aspect forms for completed action.
Wider
  • Write a text using at least three different tense and aspect forms — each with a margin note explaining the temporal or aspectual purpose
  • Produce a conditional scenario — describing what would happen if something were different — using the correct conditional structure throughout

Hinge

Further tense and conditional structures are the grammatical tools for expressing continuous, recently completed and hypothetical meaning — all required for sophisticated narrative, analytical and persuasive composition at this band.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsComplex tense and conditional accuracy requires repeated production practice in varied composition contexts. Understanding the distinction intellectually does not prevent production errors under pressure.
