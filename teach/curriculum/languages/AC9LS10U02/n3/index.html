Using grammar to predict meaning in complex texts

AC9LS10U02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use grammatical knowledge to predict and confirm meaning in complex target language texts — including texts with unfamiliar vocabulary.

Success criteria

  • Uses grammatical knowledge to predict the meaning of at least two unfamiliar items in a complex text
  • Explains the grammatical reasoning used for at least one prediction

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Passive voice and complex sentence types.

Width

Minimum
Use grammatical knowledge to predict the meaning of at least two unfamiliar words or structures in a complex text — explain the reasoning used.
Typical
Apply grammatical knowledge to interpret complex texts; use sentence structure and word class knowledge to predict unfamiliar meaning; identify how grammatical features signal relationships between ideas in complex texts; use grammar as a comprehension tool in authentic reading and listening.
Wider
  • Work through a complex authentic target language text — for every unfamiliar word, use grammar and context to predict meaning before checking a reference
  • Explain the grammatical reasoning behind at least three predictions made in a complex text — demonstrating explicit metalinguistic awareness of the prediction process

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUsing grammar as a comprehension tool requires students to articulate the reasoning — not only guess intuitively. Structured prediction tasks with explicit reasoning make the metalinguistic process visible.
