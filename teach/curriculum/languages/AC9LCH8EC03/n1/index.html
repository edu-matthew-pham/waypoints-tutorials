Applying comprehension strategies across text types

AC9LCH8EC03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply listening and reading comprehension strategies — note-taking, key fact identification, source evaluation — across a range of target language texts.

Success criteria

  • Applies at least two comprehension strategies across different text types
  • Locates key information from at least one spoken and one written text

Prior knowledge

Students entering at Y7-8 can access and comprehend target language texts in familiar registers from home and community exposure. They can identify key information in known contexts. They have not yet applied systematic information-gathering strategies, evaluated source credibility, or produced structured academic responses to a range of text types.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two comprehension strategies (e.g. note-taking, key fact identification, main idea extraction) across at least two different text types.
Typical
Listen for key facts in spoken texts — phone messages, broadcasts, announcements — using note-taking strategies; access digital and print texts to gather information; evaluate possible sources before undertaking research; read imaginative texts and keep a reading journal.
Wider
  • Evaluate a set of sources for a research task — apply explicit credibility criteria and justify source selection before gathering information
  • Compare comprehension strategies used in the target language with those used in English academic contexts — identify where the strategies transfer and where they need adaptation

Hinge

Systematic comprehension strategy use is the prerequisite for all information processing and response work. Entry students with strong oral comprehension but limited academic text strategy will struggle to access the full range of text types this descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyComprehension strategy repertoire must be built across varied text types — spoken broadcasts, digital texts, imaginative literature, formal documents. Single text type practice produces narrow transfer; varied encounters build flexible strategy use.
