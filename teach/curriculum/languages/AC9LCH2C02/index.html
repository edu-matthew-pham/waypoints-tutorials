Collaborate in play-based activities

AC9LCH2C02 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

collaborate with others in a range of play-based activities using modelled expressions and visual and spoken cues

Prior knowledge

Students can interact in classroom routines and personal introductions using familiar formulaic expressions (AC9LCH2C01). They can share personal information and respond to routine classroom language. They have not yet sustained collaborative activity using the target language — taking turns, giving instructions, leading games, or working with peers toward a shared goal in the target language.

By the end

Students collaborate with others across a range of play-based activities, using modelled expressions, visual and spoken cues to participate, take turns, give instructions and work toward shared outcomes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • participating in singing songs, and reciting rhymes and chants that use repetitive language and actions
  • participating in play-based experiences using props or dress-up costumes
  • using pictures or prompts to participate in speaking and listening activities with peers
  • collaborating in games that involve taking turns, making choices or swapping items
  • playing matching games with target language and English words from big books
  • taking turns leading games or activities
  • giving instructions such as taking the role of a physical education teacher
  • working collaboratively to identify and label objects or places on a word wall or map

Waypoints

  • 1. Participating in songs, rhymes and structured language playHingeStudents can participate in songs, rhymes, chants and structured language games that use repetitive language and physical actions.
  • 2. Taking turns, giving instructions and leading activitiesStudents can take turns in games and collaborative activities, give simple instructions in the target language, and take on a leading role in familiar activities.
  • 3. Collaborating in role-play and shared creative activitiesStudents can collaborate in play-based role-play and creative activities, using modelled expressions and props to communicate within a shared scenario.
