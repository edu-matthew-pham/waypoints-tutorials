Digital and written interaction formats

AC9LCH4C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create short texts in digital or written formats to interact with others — inviting, congratulating, thanking or sharing information — using appropriate format conventions.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Creates a short interactive text using at least three target language phrases and appropriate format conventions
  • The text has a clear communicative purpose — invitation, thanks, or information sharing

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Asking for clarification and seeking help.

Width

Minimum
Create at least one short interactive text (email, letter, message, or invitation) using appropriate format conventions and at least three target language phrases.
Typical
Create short texts in digital and written formats — emails, letters, text messages, invitations — to interact with others; use appropriate conventions for different formats; share information about school, community or personal experiences through written interaction.
Wider
  • Compare the conventions of a formal and an informal written or digital interaction in the target language — identify what changes (greeting, closing, vocabulary, register) and explain why
  • Create a digital interaction text for a specific purpose and audience — for example, a message to a peer in a target language community — selecting format and language appropriately

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDigital and written interaction integrates initiation skills (node 1) and clarification/help-seeking strategies (node 2) into a different mode — written production for a genuine communicative purpose. The teaching investment is in making the format conventions explicit and in creating real or realistic interaction partners.
