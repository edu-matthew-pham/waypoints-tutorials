Initiating and sustaining conversations

AC9LCH4C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can initiate a conversation by asking a question in a familiar context and sustain it through at least three turns, using appropriate turn-taking strategies.

Success criteria

  • Initiates at least one exchange with an opening question appropriate to the context
  • Sustains the exchange for at least three turns without teacher prompting

Prior knowledge

Students can interact in classroom routines, personal introductions and collaborative activities using modelled expressions (AC9LCH2C01, AC9LCH2C02). They can make polite requests, express thanks and preferences. They have not yet independently initiated exchanges, maintained multi-turn conversations, asked for clarification, or used digital communication formats in the target language.

Width

Minimum
Initiate at least one exchange by asking an opening question and sustain it for at least three turns, using at least one turn-taking strategy (suggestion, follow-up question, or response to a peer's question).
Typical
Initiate conversations in different contexts by asking questions about familiar topics — home, school, interests, daily routines; maintain exchanges by taking turns, making suggestions and responding to peers; use emoticons or digital conventions in appropriate digital communication contexts.
Wider
  • Initiate and sustain a conversation on a topic introduced by the partner — demonstrate flexibility in responding to an unexpected topic direction
  • Compare conversation initiation conventions in the target language with English — identify different ways of opening a conversation and what makes each appropriate

Hinge

Initiating exchanges is the entry condition for all subsequent interaction development. Students who can only respond to teacher-led prompts cannot participate in the peer-to-peer and student-led exchanges that the Y3-4 and Y5-6 descriptors require.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyConversation initiation requires a growing bank of opening questions and turn-taking strategies across different topics and contexts. Repeated practice across varied familiar topics — not only one scripted exchange — builds the flexible initiation capacity that the descriptor requires.
