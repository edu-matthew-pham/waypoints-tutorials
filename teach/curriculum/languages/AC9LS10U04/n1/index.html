Language, belonging, exclusion and cultural change

AC9LS10U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how language choices create belonging or exclusion and reflect on how cultural change affects language and identity.

Success criteria

  • Explains how at least one language feature creates belonging for some and exclusion for others
  • Identifies at least one example of cultural change affecting language or identity and explains the connection

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how language variation signals identity and social relationships, and how co-official languages relate to community identity (AC9LS8U04). They have not yet evaluated critically how language shapes belonging and exclusion, engaged in intercultural projects for real audiences, or reflected on language learning as identity experience.

Width

Minimum
Explain how at least one language feature creates belonging for some and exclusion for others; identify at least one example of cultural change affecting language or identity.
Typical
Reflect on how language choices signal group membership and cultural belonging; explore how jargon, slang and specialist language create inclusion and exclusion; reflect on how cultural change — migration, globalisation, digital culture — affects identity and language; explore assumptions in daily expressions; analyse how language changes with speakers' age, relationship and setting.
Wider
  • Examine a target language community discourse where language choice signals belonging or exclusion — explain the mechanism and evaluate the social implications
  • Research how a significant cultural change has shifted language or identity in a target language community — explain the before and after, and what the shift reveals about the community's values

Hinge

Understanding language as a vehicle for belonging and exclusion is the critical conceptual leap that moves cultural understanding from description to analysis. Students who only describe cultural practices cannot engage in evaluative reflection on language and power.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkBelonging and exclusion analysis requires explicit evaluative frameworks — not only descriptive observation. Students need to articulate the mechanism by which language creates inclusion or exclusion.
