Initiate, sustain and extend exchanges

AC9LCH10EC01 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

initiate, sustain and extend exchanges in a range of contexts, responding to ideas, opinions and perspectives about their own and others' personal worlds

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges in culturally appropriate ways across digital and social contexts (AC9LCH8EC01). They can manage invitations, personal exchanges, and online interaction. They have not yet extended exchanges into debate and argument contexts, used hypothetical and spontaneous role-play scenarios, or responded formally in professional or institutional communication contexts.

By the end

Students initiate, sustain and extend exchanges in a range of contexts — including debate, formal online communication, hypothetical scenarios, and professional interaction — responding analytically to ideas, opinions and perspectives.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying strategies to sustain face-to-face and secure online conversations
  • applying effective strategies in a debate, summarising key information and presenting a balanced and reasoned argument
  • sharing opinions and responding to a range of school-related topics linking ideas to support a view
  • acknowledging strengths in others' ideas and providing evidence to contradict the ideas
  • engaging with educational social media to document own experiences and achievements
  • expressing apology, appreciation or gratitude in diverse ways and comparing practices across contexts
  • interacting in spontaneous, hypothetical role-plays relating to a range of contexts
  • responding to a range of texts such as personal letters, emails or job advertisements

Waypoints

  • 1. Debate strategies and reasoned argumentHingeStudents can apply effective debate strategies — summarising key information, presenting balanced arguments, acknowledging and challenging others' views — in structured and semi-structured discussion.
  • 2. Hypothetical and spontaneous interactionStudents can participate in spontaneous and hypothetical role-plays — unfamiliar or unexpected scenarios — using target language flexibly without pre-prepared scripts.
  • 3. Formal and professional communicationStudents can respond formally in professional or institutional contexts — job applications, formal correspondence, apology and gratitude conventions — using appropriate register.
