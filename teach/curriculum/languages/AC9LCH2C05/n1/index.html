Copying and producing written target language

AC9LCH2C05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can copy and produce written target language words and phrases with attention to writing conventions, using known characters or script features.

Success criteria

  • Copies at least five target language words with correct script conventions
  • Can produce at least two words from memory with recognisable accuracy

Prior knowledge

Students can interact in classroom routines, collaborate in play-based activities, and locate information in familiar texts (AC9LCH2C01–C03). They can use formulaic expressions and communicate personal meaning. They have not yet created their own texts — combining target language words, phrases and writing with images and multimodal elements — for a defined purpose and audience.

Width

Minimum
Copy at least five target language words or a short phrase with attention to script conventions (stroke order, character formation, or script-specific features as applicable), demonstrating understanding of what the words mean.
Typical
Copy and trace words and phrases with attention to writing conventions; make and form characters or script features using varied materials; connect written forms to their spoken equivalents and meanings; begin to write short known phrases from memory.
Wider
  • Explore the relationship between the written form and meaning in the target language writing system — for example, how components or character elements contribute to meaning
  • Compare writing conventions in the target language with English — identify what is similar and what requires different knowledge or attention

Hinge

Producing written target language is the prerequisite for all text creation. Students who cannot form written words with attention to script conventions cannot create written or multimodal texts — the core demand of this descriptor.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsWriting script conventions require repeated practice for accuracy and fluency. Varied production activities — chalk, sand, whiteboards, digital tools — build the muscle memory and visual attention needed before students can write with confidence in text creation tasks.
