← AC9LCH2C05 Create spoken, written and multimodal texts

Performing and presenting created texts

AC9LCH2C05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can perform or present a created spoken or multimodal text — a role-play, short play, or oral presentation — using the target language with support.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Performs or presents a created text using at least four target language phrases appropriate to the context
  • Demonstrates awareness of audience through manner of delivery — for example, appropriate volume, pace or expression

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Creating short texts for a purpose.

Width

Minimum
Perform or present at least one created text (role-play script, short play, oral presentation) using at least four target language phrases appropriate to the context.
Typical
Role-play modelled conversations incorporating personal information; perform short plays based on familiar stories using puppets and props; practise and present oral texts using toys, costumes or visual supports; create and present a birthday card, self-portrait or greeting in the target language.
Wider
  • Create and perform an original short play or role-play — not based on a modelled script — using known language for a new scenario
  • Present a created text to a real audience (another class, family members, school community) and adapt the presentation for that audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPerformance and presentation integrates written production (node 1) and purposeful text creation (node 2) into a full communicative act. The teaching investment is in creating real or realistic audience conditions — students performing for peers, younger students or family — that give the communication genuine stakes.
