Adapting texts for purpose and audience

AC9LCH6C05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can adapt language register, content and format deliberately to suit a specific purpose and audience when creating a text.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Creates a text with at least two deliberate language or format adaptations appropriate to purpose or audience
  • Can explain the choices made and why they suit the specified audience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sequencing and cohesion in extended texts.

Width

Minimum
Create a text with at least two deliberate adaptations for a specific purpose or audience — for example, formal language for an adult audience, simplified language for younger students, or persuasive language for a campaign.
Typical
Create informative and imaginative texts adapted for different purposes and audiences — community newsletters, travel brochures, procedures, advertisements, stories; select vocabulary, register and format appropriate to the audience; support content with visual elements where appropriate.
Wider
  • Create the same content in two different formats for two different audiences — compare the language and format choices made and explain how each serves its intended audience
  • Research and adopt the conventions of a specific real-world text type (e.g. a formal report, a digital advertisement) in the target language and produce a text that authentically follows those conventions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkAudience and purpose adaptation requires students to make deliberate, reasoned language choices — not default to familiar patterns. Explicit audience analysis before drafting — who is this for? what do they need? — builds the planning capacity that underlies purposeful text creation.
