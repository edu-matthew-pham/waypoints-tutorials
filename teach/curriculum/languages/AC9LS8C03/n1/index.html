← AC9LS8C03 Interpret information, ideas and opinions in texts

Evaluating sources and interpreting opinion

AC9LS8C03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the reliability and accuracy of target language information sources and express opinions about text content.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least one source for reliability using at least one explicit criterion
  • Shares at least one opinion about text content using appropriate agreement or evaluative language

Prior knowledge

Students can locate and process information across multiple sources and respond in varied ways (AC9LS6C03). They have not yet evaluated source reliability, interpreted humour and cultural nuance, or compared perspectives across diverse community contexts.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least one source for reliability using at least one explicit criterion; share at least one opinion about text content using appropriate language.
Typical
Access and evaluate information sources for reliability and accuracy; compare different source types for validity and perspective; share opinions on topics of interest to young people; express agreement and disagreement with authors; exchange preferences and perspectives on characters and events in imaginative texts.
Wider
  • Compare the same event covered in two different target language sources — identify where they agree, where they diverge, and explain what the divergence reveals about each source's perspective
  • Evaluate a target language social media post on a factual topic — identify specific indicators of reliability and explain the evaluation criteria used

Hinge

Source evaluation is the prerequisite for the synthesis and evaluation demands of subsequent bands. Students who accept all sources at face value cannot synthesise across multiple perspectives or recognise that texts serve particular purposes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSource evaluation requires explicit criteria and reasoning — students need to articulate what makes a source credible or biased before they can apply this spontaneously.
