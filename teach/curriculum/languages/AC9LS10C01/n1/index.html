← AC9LS10C01 Initiate, sustain and extend exchanges

Exchanging accounts of experiences

AC9LS10C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can exchange accounts of their own and others' significant experiences — using narrative, question and response strategies across familiar and unfamiliar contexts.

Success criteria

  • Shares at least one account of a significant experience using narrative structure
  • Asks and responds to at least two questions about another person's experience

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar and some unfamiliar contexts, adjusting register and structuring arguments (AC9LS8C01). They have not yet extended exchanges into fully unfamiliar territory, sustained discussion of others' experiences, or used a full range of discussion management strategies.

Width

Minimum
Share at least one account of a significant experience using narrative structure; ask and respond to questions about another person's experience.
Typical
Exchange accounts of significant events, milestones and personal experiences; use narrative techniques to develop accounts; ask follow-up questions about others' experiences; identify common themes across shared accounts.
Wider
  • Exchange accounts with a target language community peer — adapting language and cultural references for the audience
  • Compare two accounts of the same type of experience from different cultural perspectives — identify what each reveals about the values of each community

Hinge

Exchanging accounts of others' experiences — not only one's own — requires perspective-taking and responsive questioning that is a prerequisite for the discussion and debate work in node 2.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyNarrative and experiential exchange requires a bank of discourse-level strategies — not only sentence-level structures. Students need varied practice exchanging accounts across different topic areas.
