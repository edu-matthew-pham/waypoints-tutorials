Initiate, sustain and extend exchanges

AC9LS10C01 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

initiate, sustain and extend exchanges in familiar and unfamiliar contexts related to students' own and others' experiences of the world, adjusting their language in response to others

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar and some unfamiliar contexts, adjusting register and structuring arguments (AC9LS8C01). They have not yet extended exchanges into fully unfamiliar territory, sustained discussion of others' experiences, or used a full range of discussion management strategies.

By the end

Students initiate, sustain and extend exchanges in familiar and unfamiliar contexts about their own and others' experiences — using structured argument, discussion management, register adjustment and spontaneous interaction strategies.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying appropriate register and modifying behaviour, language and etiquette in familiar and unfamiliar contexts
  • exchanging messages with peers in a target language context to share views about aspects of teenage life
  • interviewing class members to elicit opinions on personal experiences and significant events
  • maintaining and extending conversations by following up on others' contributions
  • using language to achieve different purposes in interactions, including contractions and slang for speed and economy
  • interacting in role-plays or spontaneous skits about travelling and service situations
  • playing a true and false game relating to past experiences, future aspirations or topics of interest
  • participating in a discussion, exchanging different viewpoints using strategies such as sentence starters, asking for repetition or inviting elaboration

Waypoints

  • 1. Exchanging accounts of experiencesHingeStudents can exchange accounts of their own and others' significant experiences — using narrative, question and response strategies across familiar and unfamiliar contexts.
  • 2. Structuring arguments and managing discussionStudents can structure arguments, invite contributions and manage discussion flow using a range of discourse strategies.
  • 3. Adjusting language across unfamiliar contextsStudents can adjust their language across familiar and unfamiliar contexts — using appropriate register, slang or formal language for varied interlocutors and purposes.
  • 4. Sustaining exchanges about complex and topical subjectsStudents can initiate and sustain exchanges about complex and topical subjects — including social issues, global topics and shared experiences — drawing on a full range of interaction strategies.
