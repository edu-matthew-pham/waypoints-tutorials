Using digital tools for fluency development

AC9LCH6U01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use digital tools — recording apps, pronunciation checkers, digital texts — to monitor and develop their own spoken fluency.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Uses a digital tool to record and review at least one spoken production
  • Identifies at least one specific fluency target from the review and demonstrates a practice attempt

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Tone change patterns and phrasing.

Width

Minimum
Use a digital tool to record and review at least one spoken production, identifying at least one specific feature to improve and practising improvement.
Typical
Use digital tools to check pronunciation and tonal accuracy; record own speech and compare with models; use voice-recording apps and online resources to develop fluency; check pronunciation of target language words using digital dictionaries or reference tools.
Wider
  • Conduct a systematic self-review of a recorded spoken performance — identify specific tonal, intonation or fluency targets, practise, re-record, and compare the two versions
  • Use digital tools to investigate regional accent variation in the target language — compare recordings from different communities and identify phonological differences

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDigital tool use for fluency development integrates intonation awareness (node 1) and tone change knowledge (node 2) into a self-directed improvement cycle. The teaching investment is in establishing a sustainable self-review practice — not a one-off recording task but a regular cycle of production, review and improvement.
