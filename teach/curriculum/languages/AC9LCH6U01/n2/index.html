Tone change patterns and phrasing

AC9LCH6U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and apply tone change patterns in the target language and use phrasing — pausing, grouping, rhythm — to support fluent and expressive spoken production.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one tone change pattern and demonstrates correct application in at least three examples
  • Uses phrasing and grouping to support comprehension in at least one spoken text

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Intonation and stress for emotional register.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one tone change pattern in the target language (e.g. where a standard tone changes in a specific phonological context) and demonstrate correct application in at least three examples.
Typical
Recognise and apply tone change patterns; use phrasing — grouping words, pausing at appropriate points — to support fluency and intelligibility; understand how tone marks affect pronunciation and meaning; use context to distinguish between homophones or tonal minimal pairs.
Wider
  • Analyse a spoken text for phrasing patterns — identify where the speaker pauses, groups words, and varies stress, and explain how these choices support comprehension
  • Investigate tone sandhi or tone change rules in the target language — compile examples and explain the phonological context for each change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkTone change patterns require explicit instruction — they are not intuitive and background learners who know spoken language may not have analysed these patterns. Systematic explanation of phonological contexts for tone change, followed by targeted practice, prevents persistent errors.
