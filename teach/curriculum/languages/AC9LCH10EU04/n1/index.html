Cultural change, globalisation and identity

AC9LCH10EU04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how cultural traditions and globalisation affect identity and communication, and explain how cultural beliefs shape communication differences across Chinese-speaking communities.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates how at least two cultural traditions or globalisation changes have affected communication
  • Explains the impact on own identity or beliefs with reasoned argument

Prior knowledge

Students can reflect on and explain how their own and others' identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values (AC9LCH8EU04). They have examined cultural values in language use, reflected on identity across communities, and identified stereotyping risks. They have not yet evaluated how globalisation and cultural change affect identity, examined how language is used to empower or marginalise, or reflected on the academic and professional opportunities opened by bilingualism.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate how at least two cultural traditions or globalisation-related changes have affected communication in the target language community, and explain the impact on own identity or beliefs.
Typical
Evaluate differences in perceptions, behaviours and communication across cultures; explain how cultural traditions and beliefs affect communication across regions; explore modesty, personal space and respect for authority in communication; reflect on how globalisation has affected own identity and beliefs.
Wider
  • Research how a specific wave of globalisation has affected target language cultural practices and identity — evaluate whether the changes strengthen or challenge cultural continuity
  • Compare own intergenerational family experience of cultural change — identify what has been maintained, what has changed, and discuss the factors that drive cultural continuity or change

Hinge

Evaluating the impact of cultural change and globalisation on identity is the conceptual prerequisite for the most sophisticated identity analysis at this level. Students who can only describe cultural-language connections cannot evaluate the dynamic, contested relationship between language, culture and identity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating cultural change requires students to reason about cause and effect, continuity and change, and competing values — a multi-step analytical process. Explicit frameworks for evaluating cultural impact build the critical analytical capacity the descriptor requires.
