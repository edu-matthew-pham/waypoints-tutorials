Language, power and marginalisation

AC9LCH10EU04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine how language may be used to empower or marginalise groups in society, and identify how access to sources and critical awareness can build mutual understanding.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two examples of language used to empower or marginalise
  • Explains the impact of each with specific reference to language choices

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Cultural change, globalisation and identity.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two examples of how language is used to empower or marginalise in the target language community or across cultures, and explain the impact of each.
Typical
Examine how language empowers or marginalises groups; identify content that creates bias or stereotypes; build mutual understanding through critical source evaluation; explore how group labels influence perception; examine how cultural assumptions are embedded in language; analyse gender language conventions.
Wider
  • Conduct an analysis of a target language text or media source for embedded bias or marginalising language — identify specific language choices and explain how they position readers or particular groups
  • Compare how the same social group is labelled and discussed across target language and English media — identify differences in framing and discuss the cultural values that underlie each

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepLanguage and power analysis must be applied across varied contexts — media, group labels, gender conventions, community discourse — before students can generalise the critical framework. Varied examples build the analytical lens that transfers across new texts and contexts.
