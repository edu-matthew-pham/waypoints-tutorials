Evaluate identity shaped by language and culture

AC9LCH10EU04 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

reflect on and evaluate how their own and others' identify is shaped by language(s), culture(s), attitudes, beliefs and values, and how these affect ways of communicating

Prior knowledge

Students can reflect on and explain how their own and others' identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values (AC9LCH8EU04). They have examined cultural values in language use, reflected on identity across communities, and identified stereotyping risks. They have not yet evaluated how globalisation and cultural change affect identity, examined how language is used to empower or marginalise, or reflected on the academic and professional opportunities opened by bilingualism.

By the end

Students reflect on and evaluate how their own and others' identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values — including the impact of globalisation, power and marginalisation in language, and the opportunities created by bilingual identity.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • evaluating differences in perceptions, understandings and behaviours across cultures
  • explaining how cultural traditions and beliefs affect communication of speakers from various regions
  • exploring how concepts such as modesty, personal space and respect influence communication styles
  • reflecting on and explaining the protocols required to authentically co-create an Acknowledgement of Country/Place
  • examining how language may be used to empower or marginalise groups in society
  • accessing a range of sources and identifying content that creates bias or stereotypes
  • identifying how knowledge of Chinese and English opens up opportunities to expand world views
  • exploring cultural assumptions in language such as how terms for 'getting married' differ by gender
  • analysing own language and cultural backgrounds through conversations with relatives

Waypoints

  • 1. Cultural change, globalisation and identityHingeStudents can evaluate how cultural traditions and globalisation affect identity and communication, and explain how cultural beliefs shape communication differences across Chinese-speaking communities.
  • 2. Language, power and marginalisationStudents can examine how language may be used to empower or marginalise groups in society, and identify how access to sources and critical awareness can build mutual understanding.
  • 3. Bilingual identity and world view expansionStudents can evaluate how their bilingual identity opens opportunities to expand world views and pursue heritage and cultural connections, reflecting on the academic and personal significance of language knowledge.
