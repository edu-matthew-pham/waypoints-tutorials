Locate and process information in texts

AC9LCH6C03 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

locate and process information and ideas in a range of spoken, written and multimodal texts, and respond in different ways to suit purpose and audience

Prior knowledge

Students can locate, organise and respond to key information in familiar texts across multiple sources (AC9LCH4C03). They can retell, summarise and present findings. They have not yet processed texts analytically — identifying author perspective, cultural positioning, or variations in language register — or responded in ways that are deliberately adapted for different purposes and audiences.

By the end

Students locate and process information and ideas in a range of spoken, written and multimodal texts — including analysing perspective and language variation — and respond in different ways appropriate to purpose and audience.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • listening to, reading or viewing informative texts and discussing opinions
  • identifying and describing key elements of informative or imaginative texts by asking and responding to questions
  • reading or viewing imaginative texts and performing scenes illustrating aspects of a character's attitude or emotion
  • listening to or viewing First Nations Australian authors' stories in English and responding to them in a range of text types in Chinese
  • researching, comparing and presenting information about aspects of life in China and Australia
  • identifying variations in aspects of language use such as tone, gesture and word choice in different contexts
  • interviewing family, friends or peers to collect stories of migration, identifying words and expressions that reflect important values
  • reading extracts from culturally significant children's literature and discussing personal responses
  • illustrating an extract of text by selecting images to express the content, mood or key message

Waypoints

  • 1. Analysing perspective and language variation in textsHingeStudents can identify variations in language use across texts — including tone, register, word choice and perspective — and discuss what these reveal about the text's purpose and audience.
  • 2. Processing and responding to imaginative and cultural textsStudents can read, view or listen to imaginative texts in the target language and respond — including through performance, discussion or creative response — demonstrating understanding of content and cultural context.
  • 3. Responding to suit purpose and audienceStudents can produce a response to a text that is deliberately adapted for purpose and audience — selecting format, language and content appropriate to the communicative context.
