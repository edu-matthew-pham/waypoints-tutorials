Responding to suit purpose and audience

AC9LCH6C03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce a response to a text that is deliberately adapted for purpose and audience — selecting format, language and content appropriate to the communicative context.

Success criteria

  • Produces a response demonstrating at least one deliberate language or format choice appropriate to audience or purpose
  • Can explain the choice made and why it suits the communicative context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Processing and responding to imaginative and cultural texts.

Width

Minimum
Produce at least one response to a text that demonstrates a clear deliberate choice about format, language or content appropriate to a specific purpose or audience.
Typical
Respond to informative and imaginative texts in different ways for different purposes and audiences — summaries, discussions, performances, creative responses, presentations; adapt language register, format and content to suit the response context; compare information across texts and present findings.
Wider
  • Produce two different responses to the same text for different audiences — for example, a summary for a peer and a performance for younger students — then compare the language and format choices made in each
  • Create a multimodal response to a text that combines language, image and another mode — explain how each mode contributes to the overall meaning and why this format suits the purpose

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPurposeful, audience-aware response integrates analytical text processing (node 1) and imaginative text engagement (node 2) into a full communicative act. The teaching investment is in making audience and purpose explicit as design decisions — not default choices — before students produce their response.
