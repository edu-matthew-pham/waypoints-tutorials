← AC9LCH6C03 Locate and process information in texts

Processing and responding to imaginative and cultural texts

AC9LCH6C03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can read, view or listen to imaginative texts in the target language and respond — including through performance, discussion or creative response — demonstrating understanding of content and cultural context.

Success criteria

  • Produces a response to an imaginative text demonstrating understanding of content
  • Identifies at least one cultural element in the text and connects it to their own experience or knowledge

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Analysing perspective and language variation in texts.

Width

Minimum
Read, view or listen to an imaginative text and produce at least one response (performance, discussion, or creative work) demonstrating understanding of content and at least one cultural element.
Typical
Read, view or listen to imaginative texts — poems, stories, folk tales, cartoons, films — and respond through performance, discussion or creative work; identify cultural context and language choices; connect texts to personal experience and cultural background; explore migration stories and cross-cultural perspectives.
Wider
  • Create a performance or creative response that reimagines a key scene or moment in a familiar text from a different cultural perspective
  • Compare the cultural values embedded in a target language imaginative text with those of an equivalent Australian text — discuss what the comparison reveals about cultural difference and similarity

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepResponse to imaginative texts develops through encounter with a range of text types and cultural contexts. Students who have responded to only one type of imaginative text cannot generalise their response strategies. Varied text encounters — different genres, media, cultural contexts — build flexible response capacity.
