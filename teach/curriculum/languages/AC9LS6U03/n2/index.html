← AC9LS6U03 Compare target language and English structures using metalanguage

Persuasive language, informal varieties and vocabulary patterns

AC9LS6U03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and compare persuasive language in target language and English texts, and explore informal language varieties and vocabulary patterns.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two persuasive language features in a target language text and names each using metalanguage
  • Identifies at least one informal language feature or vocabulary pattern and explains its social or communicative function

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Comparing grammatical structures and text conventions.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two persuasive language features in a target language text and compare them with English equivalents; identify at least one example of informal language variety or vocabulary pattern.
Typical
Compare advertisements and public signs for persuasive and directive language across both languages; explore informal language used by young people in target language communities; compare affective language features across the two languages; notice vocabulary patterns for extending word knowledge.
Wider
  • Create a target language persuasive text using at least three persuasive techniques — annotate each with metalanguage
  • Research informal language used in target language communities — explain each term, find any English parallel and describe what each reveals about youth culture

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPersuasive language and informal variety awareness develops through exposure to varied authentic target language texts — advertisements, signs, social media, informal speech.
