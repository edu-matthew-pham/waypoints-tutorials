Cultural identity across community and national contexts

AC9LCH4U04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how cultural identity is expressed through language in community, regional and national contexts within the target language speaking world.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two ways cultural identity is expressed through language in different target language communities
  • Can explain what at least one cultural practice or ceremony reveals about the values of that community

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Language encoding feelings, beliefs and values.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two ways cultural identity is expressed through language in different target language communities or regions — for example, dialect, ceremony, cultural events, or community-specific expression.
Typical
Explore how cultural identity is expressed across different target language communities — regional dialects, national cultural practices, community-specific language use; identify language associated with national or community identity markers; compare cultural practices across different target language communities.
Wider
  • Research cultural identity expression in a specific target language community — identify the language practices, ceremonies or events that mark belonging to that community
  • Compare how cultural identity is expressed through language in a target language community with how First Nations Australian identity is expressed through language and cultural practice — identify similarities and differences

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCultural identity expression varies across communities, regions and contexts within the target language world. Exposure to varied examples — different regions, communities, ceremonies, art forms — builds the insight that cultural identity is not monolithic but diverse within the target language community.
