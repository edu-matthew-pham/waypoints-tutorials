← AC9LCH4U04 Connections between language and cultural practices

Language encoding feelings, beliefs and values

AC9LCH4U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify specific language choices in the target language that encode cultural feelings, beliefs or values, and explain the cultural significance.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two target language expressions that encode cultural values or beliefs
  • Explains what at least one of these expressions reveals about the culture

Prior knowledge

Students notice that people use language in ways that reflect cultural practices, and can identify cultural symbols and connect language to personal identity (AC9LCH2U04). They have explored how language reflects values and community belonging. They have not yet systematically identified how feelings, beliefs and values are embedded in specific language choices, or examined cultural identity across community and national levels.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two examples of target language expressions or terms that encode cultural feelings, beliefs or values, and explain what each reveals about the culture.
Typical
Examine how specific language choices reflect cultural values — terms of affection, occasion-specific language, honorifics, culturally-valued qualities encoded in naming or expression; notice differences in cultural protocol for specific events and the language associated with them; compare cultural protocols with Australian conventions.
Wider
  • Research a cultural protocol or ceremony associated with the target language and identify all the language elements that encode its values — explain what the language choices reveal about what the culture considers important
  • Compare how the same value (e.g. respect for elders, hospitality, community) is encoded differently in the target language versus English — discuss what this reveals about cultural difference

Hinge

Understanding how language encodes cultural values is the foundational insight for the entire language-culture strand. Students who treat language and culture as separate phenomena cannot develop the intercultural competence that this descriptor and subsequent bands require.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe connection between language choices and cultural values requires explicit scaffolded discussion — students need prompts to move from 'they say it this way' to 'this reveals what the culture values.' Guided cultural analysis develops the explanatory capacity before students can reason independently.
