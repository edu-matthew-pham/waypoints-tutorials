Selecting vocabulary and structures for purpose

AC9LS8C05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select vocabulary, expressions and grammatical structures deliberately to suit the purpose of a text — moving beyond default or most familiar choices.

Success criteria

  • Creates a text with at least three deliberate vocabulary or structural choices explained in terms of purpose
  • The text is consistently appropriate in register and vocabulary for its specific purpose

Prior knowledge

Students can create texts using varied structures and text-type conventions (AC9LS6C05). They create for familiar formats and purposes. They have not yet selected vocabulary and structures deliberately for a specific purpose or created biographical and narrative texts integrating multiple text features.

Width

Minimum
Create a text and identify at least three vocabulary or structural choices made because of the text's specific purpose — explaining the reason for each.
Typical
Create texts with deliberate vocabulary and structure selection across varied formats — event materials, travel texts, biographical accounts, social media content; use contemporary and emerging vocabulary appropriate to context; select tense and register for purpose; apply text-type conventions consistently.
Wider
  • Create two versions of the same content for different purposes — explicitly contrast the vocabulary, structure and register choices made for each
  • Annotate a produced text — marking every deliberate vocabulary or structural choice and explaining the purpose reason for each

Hinge

Deliberate vocabulary and structure selection for purpose is the move from producing any text to producing the right text for a given context. Students who produce default texts regardless of purpose cannot meet the audience diversity demands of subsequent bands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSelection awareness requires metacognitive attention to language choices. Annotation tasks and contrastive production scaffold the metalinguistic awareness needed for deliberate selection.
