← AC9LCH10EU01 Features of spoken language for fluency

Comprehension and discrimination across accents and speeds

AC9LCH10EU01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop strategies for understanding unfamiliar voices at varied speeds and discriminating between unfamiliar homophones and regional variation.

Success criteria

  • Demonstrates comprehension of at least two spoken texts from unfamiliar voices or speeds
  • Identifies at least two homophone pairs and describes a strategy for discrimination

Prior knowledge

Students can examine phonological features, monitor pronunciation against standard models, and perform target language texts (AC9LCH8EU01). They have compared own speech with standard forms. They have not yet applied spoken language features to extend fluency in debate and argument contexts, prepared formal speeches for real audiences, or analysed rap, comedy and performance forms for phonological technique.

Width

Minimum
Demonstrate comprehension of at least two spoken texts from unfamiliar voices or speeds, identify at least two unfamiliar homophone pairs, and describe at least one strategy used for each.
Typical
Listen to interactions between unfamiliar voices at varied speeds; apply comprehension strategies; discriminate between unfamiliar homophones; enhance fluency through rap songs or stand-up comedy; identify regional variation in pronunciation across Chinese-speaking environments.
Wider
  • Research a specific regional variety of the target language — describe the phonological features, find examples, and explain the comprehension strategies most useful for that variety
  • Compare comprehension strategies across multiple regional accents encountered — identify which strategies transfer and which are variety-specific

Hinge

Comprehension across diverse speakers and speeds is the prerequisite for authentic spoken language use at Y9-10. Students who can only understand familiar speakers or slow, teacher-directed speech cannot function in real-world target language listening contexts.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyAcoustic comprehension across diverse speakers is built through varied, repeated listening exposure. Systematic encounter with different voices, speeds, accents and registers builds the flexible processing capacity needed.
