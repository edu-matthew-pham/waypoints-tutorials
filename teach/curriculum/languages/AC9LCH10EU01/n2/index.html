← AC9LCH10EU01 Features of spoken language for fluency

Performance techniques for rhythm and fluency

AC9LCH10EU01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can experiment with rhythm, pace, tone and stress in performance contexts — rap, comedy, poetry, drama — and develop strategies to enhance communication for specific audiences.

Success criteria

  • Produces a performance demonstrating deliberate use of at least two prosodic features
  • Can explain the intended communicative or artistic effect of each prosodic choice

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Comprehension and discrimination across accents and speeds.

Width

Minimum
Produce at least one performance demonstrating deliberate use of at least two prosodic features (rhythm, pace, tone variation, stress) for communicative or artistic effect.
Typical
Experiment with rhythm, pace, tone and stress; take on the role of a news reader or performer; practise rap or stand-up comedy in the target language; read poems in multiple ways to convey different meanings; watch and perform versions of target language cultural performances.
Wider
  • Create an original performance text — rap, spoken word, or monologue — demonstrating deliberate prosodic design choices, with annotation explaining each choice
  • Compare the prosodic techniques used in target language and English performance genres — identify what is shared and what is culturally specific to each

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepPerformance technique requires practice across varied performance forms. Students who have only practised in one format — only poetry, or only formal speech — cannot generalise their prosodic skills. Varied performance contexts build flexible prosodic production capacity.
