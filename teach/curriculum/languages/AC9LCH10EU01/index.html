Features of spoken language for fluency

AC9LCH10EU01 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

demonstrate how features and conventions of spoken Chinese can extend fluency when responding to and creating texts appropriate to contexts, purposes and audiences

Prior knowledge

Students can examine phonological features, monitor pronunciation against standard models, and perform target language texts (AC9LCH8EU01). They have compared own speech with standard forms. They have not yet applied spoken language features to extend fluency in debate and argument contexts, prepared formal speeches for real audiences, or analysed rap, comedy and performance forms for phonological technique.

By the end

Students demonstrate how features and conventions of spoken target language extend fluency — including comprehension of diverse accents, performance techniques, and formal speech delivery for real audiences.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • listening to interactions between unfamiliar voices at different speeds to develop auditory and comprehension skills
  • discriminating between unfamiliar homophones and enhancing fluency through rap songs or stand-up comedy
  • identifying regional variation in pronunciation across different target language environments
  • experimenting with rhythm, pace, tone and stress and developing strategies to enhance communication
  • using dictionaries or digital tools to help with learning characters and pronunciation
  • presenting an argument in a debate using emphasis and persuasion with focus on fluency and audience
  • reading aloud a poem in different ways using intonation to convey happiness, sarcasm or jealousy

Waypoints

  • 1. Comprehension and discrimination across accents and speedsHingeStudents can develop strategies for understanding unfamiliar voices at varied speeds and discriminating between unfamiliar homophones and regional variation.
  • 2. Performance techniques for rhythm and fluencyStudents can experiment with rhythm, pace, tone and stress in performance contexts — rap, comedy, poetry, drama — and develop strategies to enhance communication for specific audiences.
  • 3. Formal speech delivery for real audiencesStudents can deliver formal speeches to real or realistic audiences using appropriate intonation, stress and phrasing — including debate presentations, welcome speeches, and argumentative oral texts.
