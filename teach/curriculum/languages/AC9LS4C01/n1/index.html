Initiating and responding in personal exchanges

AC9LS4C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can initiate an exchange about themselves or others using appropriate formulaic expressions, and respond to questions about personal life, family, interests and routines.

Success criteria

  • Initiates at least one exchange using an appropriate greeting or opening question without teacher prompting
  • Responds to at least three different personal questions using modelled sentence structures

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and respond to modelled greetings, instructions and routines, and produce personal introductions using modelled sentence structures (AC9LS2C01). They respond to questions but have not yet initiated exchanges independently or used a range of interrogatives.

Width

Minimum
Initiate at least one exchange using an appropriate greeting or question without teacher prompting; respond to at least three personal questions using modelled sentence structures.
Typical
Use formal and informal forms of address across contexts; exchange information about family, friends and interests using simple statements and connecting words; use question-answer patterns about school and home life; describe routines using time expressions; use a range of question words.
Wider
  • Conduct a short unrehearsed exchange with a partner — initiating, sustaining and closing without teacher scripting — using at least four different question types
  • Exchange information about routines across different time references and explain the pattern of a typical week

Hinge

Initiating exchanges — rather than only responding — is the core progression from 1-2 to 3-4. Students who can only respond cannot engage in the planning, collaborative and text comprehension tasks at this band, all of which require interactive initiation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyInitiation requires a bank of question forms and opening strategies across personal domains. Students need varied practice asking and answering across different topic areas to build the range needed for genuine initiation.
