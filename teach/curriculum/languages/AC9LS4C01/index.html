Initiate exchanges and respond about self and environment

AC9LS4C01 · Years 3–4 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

initiate exchanges and respond to modelled questions about self, others, and classroom environment, using formulaic expressions

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and respond to modelled greetings, instructions and routines, and produce personal introductions using modelled sentence structures (AC9LS2C01). They respond to questions but have not yet initiated exchanges independently or used a range of interrogatives.

By the end

Students initiate exchanges and respond to questions about self, others and the classroom environment using formulaic expressions and modelled structures — including routines, interests, family, location and time.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using formal and informal greetings in a variety of contexts
  • exchanging information about family, friends or interests using simple descriptive statements and conjunctions
  • using question-answer patterns to share information about school and home life
  • responding to simple questions about the class environment or daily routine
  • explaining routines and favourite activities using expressions related to time and days of the week
  • using a range of interrogatives to obtain further details
  • describing features, characteristics and locations of particular things or places
  • making and responding to requests such as asking for items, requesting help, clarification, rephrasing or repetition

Waypoints

  • 1. Initiating and responding in personal exchangesHingeStudents can initiate an exchange about themselves or others using appropriate formulaic expressions, and respond to questions about personal life, family, interests and routines.
  • 2. Describing environment, features and locationsStudents can describe the features, characteristics and locations of things and places in the classroom environment using location language and descriptive expressions.
  • 3. Making requests and managing communicationStudents can make and respond to requests — including requests for items, help, clarification and repetition — using modelled expressions.
