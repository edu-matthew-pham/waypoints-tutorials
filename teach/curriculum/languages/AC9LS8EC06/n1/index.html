← AC9LS8EC06 Create informative and imaginative texts

Structuring a text for purpose and audience

AC9LS8EC06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the structural conventions of a familiar target language text type and apply them in their own text production.

Success criteria

  • Produces a text with a recognisable structure appropriate to the chosen text type (not just a list of sentences)
  • Can identify the intended audience for the text and explain at least one structural choice that reflects that audience

Prior knowledge

Students can locate and process information in familiar texts (AC9LS8EC04) and interact using modelled language (AC9LS8EC01–03). They have not yet created extended spoken, written or multimodal texts in the target language for specific purposes and audiences.

Width

Minimum
Produce one text in the target language that follows a recognisable structural convention (e.g. a presentation with greeting, introduction and sequenced ideas; a narrative with beginning, middle and end).
Typical
Prepare and produce texts across at least two formats (e.g. a spoken presentation and a written informative text); apply structural conventions appropriate to each text type and identify audience for each text.
Wider
  • Compare the structural conventions of equivalent text types (e.g. a presentation, a notice, a story) in the target language and in English — identify where conventions align and where they diverge
  • Produce a bilingual text for a mixed-language audience — consider how structural and linguistic choices change when the audience changes

Hinge

Text structure awareness is the precondition for all subsequent text creation. Students who produce language without any structural frame produce utterances, not texts — they cannot meet the descriptor's demand for textual conventions or demonstrate audience awareness, both of which require a sense of the text as a whole.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need explicit instruction in the structural conventions of target language text types before they can apply them. Annotated model texts, structural frameworks and co-constructed exemplars help students see text organisation as a deliberate choice, not a default.
