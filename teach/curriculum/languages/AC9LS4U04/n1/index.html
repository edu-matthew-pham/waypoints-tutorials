← AC9LS4U04 Connections between language and cultural practices

Using conceptual vocabulary for language-culture analysis

AC9LS4U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 3–4 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use conceptual terms to discuss connections between target language and cultural practices — explaining the connection, not only naming it.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two conceptual terms when discussing a target language language-culture connection
  • Explains at least one connection between a target language feature and a cultural practice — not only identifies it

Prior knowledge

Students have noticed that language reflects cultural practices and that the target language is spoken across diverse communities (AC9LS2U04). They can notice connections. They have not yet used analytical vocabulary to discuss these connections or reflected on cross-cultural comparisons systematically.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two conceptual terms (identity, behaviour, celebration, belief) in a discussion about a target language language-culture connection — explaining the connection.
Typical
Discuss cultural practices using terms like identity, behaviour, celebrations, beliefs; respond to discussion prompts about cultural meaning; explore how language features carry cultural values (e.g. naming conventions, forms of address, affective expressions); identify words that reflect cultural priorities.
Wider
  • Research a target language cultural practice and explain the language associated with it — using at least three conceptual terms
  • Identify a culturally specific target language expression and explain what value or belief it encodes — providing an English equivalent or explaining why one does not exist

Hinge

Conceptual vocabulary for discussing language and culture is the analytical tool for all subsequent cultural reflection. Students who can only describe cultural practices without the conceptual frame cannot engage with the increasingly analytical cultural work from subsequent bands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUsing conceptual vocabulary requires students to move from description to analysis. Structured discussion prompts with conceptual vocabulary scaffold this analytical move.
