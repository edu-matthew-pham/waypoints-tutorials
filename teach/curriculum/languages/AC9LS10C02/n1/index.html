← AC9LS10C02 Contribute to discussions involving diverse views

Expressing agreement, disagreement and encouraging participation

AC9LS10C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can express agreement and disagreement, encourage others' contributions, and use turn-taking strategies in group discussion.

Success criteria

  • Expresses at least one agreement and one disagreement using appropriate target language conventions
  • Uses at least one strategy to invite another person's contribution

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges using argument structures and discussion management (AC9LS10C01). They have not yet contributed systematically to multi-perspective discussions, used persuasive language for topical issues, or participated in collaborative media or broadcast contexts.

Width

Minimum
Express at least one agreement and one disagreement using target language conventions; use at least one strategy to invite another person's contribution.
Typical
Use varied expressions for agreement and disagreement; provide encouragement and acknowledgement in discussion; take turns and manage overlapping contributions; negotiate shared decisions in collaborative contexts.
Wider
  • Facilitate a group discussion — ensuring balanced contribution and summarising diverse views at the end
  • Respond to a position that conflicts with their own — using target language conventions for polite but direct disagreement

Hinge

Expressing agreement and disagreement through target language conventions — not only through the logic of what is said — is the social foundation for genuine multi-perspective discussion. Students who only express opinion without these conventions cannot participate in the collaborative and persuasive tasks that follow.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyAgreement and disagreement conventions require a bank of expressions for varied levels of alignment and contexts. Students need varied practice across different discussion types.
