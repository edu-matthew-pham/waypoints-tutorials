Using persuasive language for topical issues

AC9LS10C02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use argument and persuasive language to contribute to discussion of topical issues — including social, environmental and cultural topics.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two persuasive language moves in a discussion of a topical issue
  • Acknowledges at least one opposing view in the course of the discussion

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Negotiating outcomes and reaching shared decisions.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two persuasive language moves in a discussion of a topical issue; acknowledge at least one opposing view.
Typical
Use argument and persuasive language to discuss topical issues; structure contributions to introduce, support and conclude a position; acknowledge and respond to opposing views; participate in collaborative community media or broadcast projects.
Wider
  • Contribute to a multi-perspective discussion on a topical issue where participants hold genuinely different positions — sustaining the argument through at least three exchange turns
  • Produce a persuasive contribution to a community media project — presenting a position on a real issue to a real audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierUsing persuasive language for topical issues integrates all prior discussion capabilities — agreement/disagreement (N1), negotiation (N2) — and applies them in an argument context with genuine intellectual stakes.
