Evaluative and persuasive texts

AC9LCH10EC05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce evaluative and persuasive texts — issue analyses, debate speeches, community presentations — using evidence, balanced perspective and persuasive language structures.

Success criteria

  • Produces an evaluative or persuasive text with a clear position supported by at least two pieces of evidence
  • Uses at least two persuasive structures across the text

Prior knowledge

Students can create spoken, written and multimodal informative and imaginative texts for purpose and audience (AC9LCH8EC05). They have drafted, revised and produced texts for community audiences. They have not yet created evaluative and persuasive texts at extended length, produced formal procedural or policy-based texts, or created literary texts drawing on specific target language literary traditions.

Width

Minimum
Produce an evaluative or persuasive text of at least ten sentences with a clear position, at least two pieces of supporting evidence, and at least two persuasive structures.
Typical
Create presentations on contemporary issues analysing pros and cons; produce persuasive texts for community audiences; write evaluations comparing alternative positions; present on population, environmental, educational or community issues; use statistical data and research evidence.
Wider
  • Produce an extended persuasive text on a topic of genuine community relevance, with a documented drafting and revision process showing deliberate language choices
  • Compare persuasive text conventions in target language and English — identify how argument structure, evidence use and rhetorical strategy differ across the two languages

Hinge

Evaluative and persuasive text production is the foundational capability for all advanced text creation at Y9-10. Students who can only produce narrative or informative texts cannot meet the analytical and advocacy production demands that follow.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPersuasive text production requires deliberate structural planning — position, evidence, counter-argument, conclusion. Explicit argument structure instruction followed by scaffolded production builds the planning capacity that produces genuinely persuasive texts.
