Sustaining exchanges in unfamiliar contexts

AC9LCH8C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can sustain an exchange in an unfamiliar or open-ended context — on topics beyond scripted school and home content — using strategies to maintain conversation when language resources are stretched.

Success criteria

  • Sustains an exchange for at least five turns in a partially unfamiliar context
  • Uses at least one maintenance strategy (clarification, paraphrase, or follow-up question) when needed

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges related to experiences and perspectives, using active listening strategies and opinion-sharing language (AC9LCH6C01). They can sustain face-to-face and digital exchanges. They have not yet adjusted language in real-time response to others, sustained exchanges in genuinely unfamiliar contexts, used descriptive and persuasive language to discuss contrasting perspectives, or summarised others' views in interaction.

Width

Minimum
Sustain an exchange for at least five turns on a topic that is partially or fully unfamiliar, using at least one strategy (clarification, paraphrase, or follow-up question) when language resources are insufficient.
Typical
Sustain face-to-face and online exchanges in familiar and some unfamiliar contexts; use open-ended questions to maintain conversation; use clarification, repetition and paraphrase strategies; use descriptive language to discuss contrasting perspectives on personal topics; contribute to online forums and discussions.
Wider
  • Sustain an exchange on a topic introduced spontaneously by the partner — demonstrate real-time adaptation to an unplanned topic direction across at least six turns
  • Participate in an online or written forum exchange where the topic and participants are partially unfamiliar — sustain contribution across multiple turns

Hinge

Sustaining exchanges in unfamiliar contexts is the prerequisite for all higher-order interaction work. Students who can only manage scripted or familiar exchanges cannot meet the 'adjusting language in response to others' demand or develop the interactional flexibility that the Y9-10 descriptors require.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyConversational resilience in unfamiliar contexts is built through accumulated experience across varied topics and partners. Structured practice with progressively less familiar topics — combined with explicit strategy instruction for when language resources run short — builds the confidence and flexibility the descriptor requires.
