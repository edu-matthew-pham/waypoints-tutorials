Initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar and unfamiliar contexts

AC9LCH8C01 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar and unfamiliar contexts related to students' experiences, feelings and views, adjusting their language in response to others

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges related to experiences and perspectives, using active listening strategies and opinion-sharing language (AC9LCH6C01). They can sustain face-to-face and digital exchanges. They have not yet adjusted language in real-time response to others, sustained exchanges in genuinely unfamiliar contexts, used descriptive and persuasive language to discuss contrasting perspectives, or summarised others' views in interaction.

By the end

Students initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar and unfamiliar contexts about their experiences, feelings and views, adjusting their language in response to others and demonstrating active, responsive listening.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • initiating and sustaining conversations to share aspects of personal worlds such as recent holidays, future plans or achievements
  • asking and responding to open-ended questions about learning activities or strategies
  • sustaining face-to-face and secure online conversations, for example, encouraging responses through questions
  • using descriptive language to discuss contrasting aspects of personal worlds such as home and school, study and entertainment
  • using digital technology to maintain contact and exchange thoughts and experiences
  • requesting repetition or asking for and providing clarification
  • acknowledging others' ideas and indicating agreement or disagreement in non-judgemental ways
  • contributing to secure online forums or discussions offering solutions or personal ideas
  • summarising different opinions in interactions

Waypoints

  • 1. Sustaining exchanges in unfamiliar contextsHingeStudents can sustain an exchange in an unfamiliar or open-ended context — on topics beyond scripted school and home content — using strategies to maintain conversation when language resources are stretched.
  • 2. Acknowledging views and using persuasive languageStudents can acknowledge others' ideas, indicate agreement or disagreement non-judgementally, and use language to persuade or influence others during an exchange.
  • 3. Real-time language adjustmentStudents can adjust their language in real-time in response to others — summarising views, rephrasing for clarity, or shifting register — as an exchange develops.
