Target language and English similarities and differences

AC9LS2U03 · Years 1–2 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

notice that the target language has features that may be similar to or different from English

Prior knowledge

Students have noticed some similarities and differences between target language and English sounds and features with support (AC9LSF02). They are developing early English literacy. They have not yet systematically compared structural features across the two languages.

By the end

Students notice and describe features of the target language that are similar to or different from English — including structural patterns, shared vocabulary, text conventions and written features — drawing on both languages as reference points.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • observing features of familiar types of texts such as stories and greeting cards, and comparing them with English equivalents
  • understanding grammatical features present in the target language but absent in English (e.g. grammatical gender, different word order)
  • noticing plural forms similar to or different from English
  • noticing there are cognates in the target language and English, but pronunciation may differ
  • creating an identity card with parallel personal information in the target language and English, noticing similarities and differences
  • comparing how target language words used in Australian English are pronounced by speakers of each language
  • noticing the differences between punctuation marks in the target language and English
  • identifying words in the target language borrowed from other languages, and vice versa

Waypoints

  • 1. Shared vocabulary and structural comparisonsHingeStudents can identify vocabulary shared across the target language and English, and notice structural patterns that are similar to or different from English.
  • 2. Text features and vocabulary borrowing across languagesStudents can notice similarities and differences in text features across the target language and English — including text type conventions, written features and borrowed vocabulary.
