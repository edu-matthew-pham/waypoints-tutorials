Comprehension strategies for unfamiliar language

AC9LS4C04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 3–4 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply strategies to work out the meaning of unfamiliar target language — using context, vocabulary patterns, surrounding words and available reference tools.

Success criteria

  • Applies at least two comprehension strategies to work out the meaning of unfamiliar words in a text
  • Can identify which strategy was used for at least one word and explain the reasoning

Prior knowledge

Students have noticed that language carries cultural meaning (AC9LS2C04) and use formulaic expressions in context. They have not yet developed explicit strategies for comprehending unfamiliar language or adjusting language to convey cultural meaning.

Width

Minimum
Apply at least two different strategies to work out the meaning of unfamiliar words or expressions in a given text — and identify which strategy was used.
Typical
Use context and surrounding words to infer meaning; apply vocabulary pattern recognition; use dictionaries and reference tools; apply literacy skills from English to support comprehension; notice word families and patterns; consider audience and purpose as comprehension aids.
Wider
  • Apply multiple strategies to the same unfamiliar word and compare which gives the most accurate result — develop a personal strategy priority for target language comprehension
  • Explain to a partner how they worked out the meaning of an unfamiliar word — articulating the reasoning strategy explicitly

Hinge

Comprehension strategies are what allow students to function with authentic and partially unfamiliar texts. Students who can only understand pre-taught vocabulary are dependent on teacher pre-loading and cannot access texts independently — which is required for research and text engagement tasks from this band onwards.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStrategy application must be made explicit before it becomes automatic. Students need to name and practise each strategy deliberately before they can deploy them spontaneously.
