Interpret and translate texts with cultural explanation

AC9LCH10C04 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret and translate a range of non-verbal, spoken and written texts, and explain how meaning is conveyed to reflect cultural context, purpose and audience

Prior knowledge

Students can interpret and translate across cultural contexts, manage register conversion, and produce bilingual texts for public purposes (AC9LCH8C04). They have interpreted for visitors and created bilingual resources. They have not yet interpreted puns, sarcasm and malapropisms, created multimodal bilingual resources, or justified cultural communication decisions in diverse social scenarios.

By the end

Students interpret and translate a range of texts — including humour, sarcasm, puns and culturally-complex language — explaining how meaning is conveyed to reflect cultural context, purpose and audience.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • applying strategies to overcome challenges in the translation of culturally unique concepts
  • demonstrating politeness, respect and formality in a range of cultural contexts
  • interpreting puns, idioms, humour or sarcasm between the target language and English
  • applying non-verbal and spoken communication strategies and justifying own actions and reactions
  • creating and developing multimodal target language resources incorporating bilingual subtitles
  • discussing and explaining malapropisms and translating hidden meanings
  • using secure online tools to understand emerging new words and applying knowledge in communication
  • composing multimodal bilingual texts for a range of audiences
  • changing informal spoken language into formal register to suit particular purposes and audiences

Waypoints

  • 1. Interpreting humour, sarcasm and wordplayHingeStudents can interpret puns, idioms, humour, sarcasm and malapropisms between target language and English, applying strategies to explain meaning including hidden or layered meanings.
  • 2. Justifying cultural communication decisionsStudents can apply non-verbal and spoken communication strategies across diverse social scenarios and justify their own communication decisions in terms of cultural context.
  • 3. Multimodal bilingual text productionStudents can create multimodal bilingual resources — vlogs, educational blogs, subtitled video, interactive guides — that explain cultural meaning and serve a specific audience purpose.
