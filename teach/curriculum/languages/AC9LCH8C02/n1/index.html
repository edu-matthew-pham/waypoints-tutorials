Justification language in collaborative planning

AC9LCH8C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use justification language — reasoning, evidence, consequence framing — to support positions and proposals in collaborative planning contexts.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two justification structures during a collaborative planning task
  • Supports at least one proposal with a reason or evidence

Prior knowledge

Students can plan and negotiate with others using modelled and idiomatic language, reaching agreement and resolving differences (AC9LCH6C02). They have collaborated on community-facing projects. They have not yet used justification language in collaborative planning, conducted community interviews, or led events involving external community members.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two justification structures during a collaborative planning task — for example, giving a reason for a proposal, citing evidence for a decision, or framing a consequence.
Typical
Use justification language — reasoning, consequence framing, evidence reference — in collaborative planning; provide metalanguage-supported feedback on peers' contributions; negotiate and justify decisions for events including place, time, program, and participants.
Wider
  • Lead a planning discussion — facilitating justification-based decision making and managing competing proposals to a shared outcome using target language
  • Compare how justification is expressed in collaborative contexts in the target language with English — identify where the conventions differ in directness or formality

Hinge

Justification language distinguishes collaborative negotiation from simple preference exchange. Students who can only assert preferences cannot engage in the evidence-based, reasoned planning that this descriptor requires and that the Y9-10 discussion descriptors build upon.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkJustification in collaborative contexts requires students to construct and deploy reasoned arguments — not just express preferences. Explicit modelling of justification structures followed by scaffolded planning tasks builds the argumentative capacity needed.
