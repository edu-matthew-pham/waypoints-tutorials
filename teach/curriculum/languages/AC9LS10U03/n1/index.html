Evaluating text effectiveness using metalanguage

AC9LS10U03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate target language texts for effectiveness — analysing how specific language features contribute to or undermine the text's purpose.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two specific language features in a text and evaluates how each contributes to or undermines the purpose
  • Uses metalanguage accurately to frame at least two evaluative claims

Prior knowledge

Students can use metalanguage to compare whole-text coherence features, text-type conventions, vocabulary patterns and historical influences (AC9LS8U03). They have not yet evaluated target language texts critically for effectiveness, analysed texts that challenge norms, or connected language register variation to social positioning.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least one target language text for effectiveness — identify at least two specific language features and explain how each contributes to or undermines the purpose.
Typical
Evaluate the effectiveness of texts in achieving their purpose; analyse how vocabulary, sentence structure, cohesion and text conventions contribute to effectiveness; compare effective and ineffective texts in the same genre; use metalanguage to frame evaluative claims.
Wider
  • Evaluate a target language community media text — identify every language choice that serves the text's purpose and every one that does not, with metalanguage framing each claim
  • Rewrite a section of a target language text to be more effective for its stated purpose — annotate every change with a metalinguistic justification

Hinge

Text effectiveness evaluation — not only feature identification — is the analytical foundation for all critical text work. Students who only identify features without evaluating their effectiveness cannot engage in genuine text criticism.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEffectiveness evaluation requires a move from identification (this feature is present) to evaluation (this feature serves or undermines the purpose). Structured evaluation frameworks scaffold this analytical move.
