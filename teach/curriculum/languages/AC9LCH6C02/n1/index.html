← AC9LCH6C02 Plan and negotiate with others

Language for suggesting, agreeing and resolving

AC9LCH6C02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use the language of suggestion, agreement and resolution — including idiomatic expressions — to navigate collaborative decision-making.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least three structures for suggesting, agreeing, or resolving during a collaborative task
  • Reaches a negotiated outcome without abandoning the target language

Prior knowledge

Students can plan and transact with others in familiar contexts using a range of phrases and modelled sentences (AC9LCH4C02). They can take on roles, give instructions, and participate in cultural presentations. They have not yet used idiomatic language in collaborative planning, negotiated competing preferences to reach resolution, or participated in community-facing collaborative projects.

Width

Minimum
Use at least three language structures for suggesting, agreeing, or resolving in a collaborative planning task.
Typical
Use a range of planning and negotiation language — making suggestions, responding to suggestions, proposing alternatives, reaching agreement, resolving differences; include idiomatic expressions appropriate to the context; provide feedback on peers' contributions.
Wider
  • Compare how suggestions and disagreements are managed in collaborative contexts in the target language community with Australian conventions — identify differences in directness, face-saving, and resolution strategies
  • Analyse the language of a target language negotiation or planning text — identify the structures used at each stage (proposal, response, counter-proposal, agreement) and discuss what makes them effective

Hinge

Language for suggestion, agreement and resolution is the functional prerequisite for all collaborative planning activity. Students who can only assert preferences without using negotiation language cannot participate meaningfully in the planning tasks this descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyNegotiation language requires a bank of structures across different positions — suggesting, countering, conceding, agreeing. Repeated practice across varied collaborative contexts builds the flexible deployment needed for genuine negotiation.
