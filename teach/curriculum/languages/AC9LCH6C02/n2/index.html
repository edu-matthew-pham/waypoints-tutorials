Idiomatic language in collaborative contexts

AC9LCH6C02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and use idiomatic expressions appropriate to collaborative and planning contexts, understanding that idiomatic meaning differs from literal meaning.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two idiomatic expressions used in collaborative contexts
  • Uses at least one idiom correctly in a collaborative or planning exchange

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Language for suggesting, agreeing and resolving.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two idiomatic expressions used in planning or collaborative contexts and use at least one correctly in context.
Typical
Recognise and use idiomatic expressions in collaborative planning contexts; understand that idiomatic meaning is not derived from literal word-for-word interpretation; discuss the social function of particular idioms in collaborative interaction.
Wider
  • Research a set of idioms used in collaborative or persuasive contexts in the target language — explain their meaning, origin (where known), and compare with English equivalents or absence of equivalents
  • Compare how idioms are used differently in formal and informal collaborative contexts in the target language — identify when idiomatic language is appropriate and when it would be inappropriate

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepIdiomatic language is understood and deployed through varied contextual encounters — the same idiom used in different registers, situations and with different speakers. Varied exposure across collaborative contexts builds genuine understanding beyond memorised definitions.
