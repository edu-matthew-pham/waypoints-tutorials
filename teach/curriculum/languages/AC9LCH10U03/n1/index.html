Stylistic devices and rhetorical analysis

AC9LCH10U03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and analyse stylistic devices used in target language texts — including rhetorical, figurative and structural devices — and evaluate their effectiveness.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three stylistic devices in a target language text using metalanguage
  • Evaluates the effectiveness of at least one device with explanation

Prior knowledge

Students can reflect on and explain similarities and differences between target language and English, support younger learners, and analyse metaphorical meaning (AC9LCH8U03). They have applied comparative metalanguage across text types. They have not yet analysed stylistic devices in target language texts, compared genre organisation across languages, described genres unique to target language literary tradition, or used metalanguage to explain linguistic, textual and cultural elements simultaneously.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three stylistic devices in a target language text, explain how each works using metalanguage, and evaluate the effectiveness of at least one.
Typical
Analyse stylistic devices in target language written texts — parallel structures, double negatives, figurative devices, rhetorical questions; use metalanguage to explain their function; evaluate the effectiveness of specific devices; compare stylistic conventions in target language and English texts.
Wider
  • Produce a full stylistic analysis of a target language text — annotating every stylistic device with metalanguage and evaluating how each contributes to the text's overall effect
  • Compare how the same rhetorical effect is achieved differently in target language and English texts — identify genre-specific and culturally-specific stylistic conventions

Hinge

Stylistic device analysis is the prerequisite for full text analysis at Y9-10. Students who cannot identify and name stylistic devices cannot produce the linguistic, textual and cultural analysis that this descriptor requires — nor can they apply these insights to their own text creation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStylistic device analysis requires students to identify a device, name it using metalanguage, explain its function, and evaluate its effectiveness — a multi-step analytical process. Explicit stylistic analysis instruction with varied model texts builds this systematic capacity before students can analyse independently.
