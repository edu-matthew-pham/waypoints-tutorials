Source credibility and critical information evaluation

AC9LCH8C03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the credibility of information sources in the target language and apply strategies to assess and compare information across sources.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least two target language sources using at least two explicit credibility criteria
  • Explains which source is more reliable for a given purpose and why

Prior knowledge

Students can locate and process information in texts analytically and respond for purpose and audience (AC9LCH6C03). They can analyse perspective and language variation. They have not yet evaluated credibility of sources, analysed humour and cultural reference, identified register variation across formal and informal contexts, or responded with empathetic or critical personal stances.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate the credibility of at least two target language sources using at least two criteria, and explain which is more reliable for a given purpose.
Typical
Use target language digital and print sources to research topics; evaluate source credibility using appropriate criteria; compare information across sources; use target language search tools; distinguish between fact, opinion and interpretation in informative texts.
Wider
  • Research a contested topic using target language sources — compare how different sources frame the same information, evaluate their credibility, and present a synthesis that acknowledges source differences
  • Investigate what makes a source credible in the target language information context — discuss whether credibility criteria differ across languages or communities

Hinge

Source credibility evaluation is the prerequisite for all analytical text work at Y7-8. Students who cannot evaluate sources cannot distinguish reliable from unreliable information — which undermines the quality and integrity of all analytical and research-based responses.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSource credibility evaluation requires students to apply explicit criteria — not just judge intuitively. Explicit instruction on credibility criteria (author, currency, purpose, evidence) followed by comparative source analysis tasks builds the systematic evaluative capacity.
