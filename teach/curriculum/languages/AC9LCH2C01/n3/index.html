Requests, thanks and everyday social expressions

AC9LCH2C01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use polite requests, thanks, apologies and expressions of likes and dislikes in familiar classroom and social contexts.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two different social expressions (request, thanks, apology, or preference) appropriately in a real or role-play context
  • Demonstrates appropriate manner or gesture alongside the verbal expression

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Personal information and introductions.

Width

Minimum
Use at least two formulaic social expressions appropriately in context — for example, making a polite request, thanking someone, or expressing a like or dislike.
Typical
Use a range of social expressions across everyday classroom interactions — polite requests, thanks, apologies, expressions of preference — with appropriate manner and register; begin to use exclamations and formulaic responses spontaneously in peer interaction.
Wider
  • Compare the politeness conventions of the target language with English — identify where the same communicative act (e.g. requesting, thanking) requires different forms or gestures
  • Role-play a series of short social interactions (e.g. asking to borrow something, thanking a peer, apologising) demonstrating appropriate language and gesture for each

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierSocial expression use integrates greeting conventions (node 1) and personal exchange skills (node 2) into the broader repertoire of classroom social language. The teaching investment is in creating authentic occasions for these expressions — not drills, but real moments of requesting, thanking and responding.
