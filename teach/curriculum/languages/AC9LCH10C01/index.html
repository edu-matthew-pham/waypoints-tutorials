Initiate, sustain and extend exchanges

AC9LCH10C01 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

initiate, sustain and extend exchanges in a range of contexts, responding to ideas, opinions and perspectives

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges in familiar and unfamiliar contexts, adjusting language in response to others and using persuasive and influencing language (AC9LCH8C01). They can summarise others' views and manage real-time language adjustment. They have not yet extended exchanges into wider community and issue-based contexts, used analytical language to evaluate rules and regulations, or communicated in formal contexts such as phone calls, official enquiries, or advocacy writing.

By the end

Students initiate, sustain and extend exchanges in a range of contexts — including community issues, formal communication, and advocacy — responding analytically to ideas, opinions and perspectives.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • initiating and sustaining conversations to share issues from the wider target language community such as global warming
  • asking and responding to open-ended questions about promoting language learning
  • sustaining communications in a wide range of contexts such as meetings or phone calls
  • using analytical language to discuss and evaluate aspects of rules and regulations
  • using digital resources to maintain contact, express concerns or make enquiries
  • confirming understanding and requesting clarification
  • contributing to discussions and dealing with potential conflicts
  • writing to different audiences in a wide range of contexts to address real-life issues

Waypoints

  • 1. Extending exchanges into wider community and issue contextsHingeStudents can initiate and sustain exchanges about issues beyond personal experience — community, social, or global topics — using language appropriate to the scope of the discussion.
  • 2. Formal communication contextsStudents can communicate in formal contexts — phone calls, official meetings, formal written enquiries — using appropriate register, structure and language for each context.
  • 3. Advocacy and conflict management in exchangesStudents can advocate for a position, address conflicting views, and use digital platforms to express concerns and contribute to wider community discussions.
