Formal communication contexts

AC9LCH10C01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can communicate in formal contexts — phone calls, official meetings, formal written enquiries — using appropriate register, structure and language for each context.

Success criteria

  • Participates in or produces a formal communication using appropriate register throughout
  • Uses at least two formal register conventions appropriate to the communication context

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Extending exchanges into wider community and issue contexts.

Width

Minimum
Participate in or produce at least one formal communication (phone call simulation, formal meeting contribution, or formal written enquiry) using appropriate register throughout.
Typical
Sustain communication in formal contexts — meetings, phone calls, official enquiries; use formal register and appropriate structural conventions; confirm understanding and request clarification in formal contexts; write to different audiences addressing real-life issues such as visa applications or scholarship enquiries.
Wider
  • Compare formal communication conventions in the target language with Australian English equivalents — identify differences in register, structure, opening and closing conventions
  • Draft a formal written communication on a real issue of personal relevance — apply target language formal register and structural conventions appropriately

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkFormal communication contexts require students to understand what makes formal register different from informal, and to apply this consistently. Explicit register analysis of model formal texts, followed by structured production practice, builds the contextual awareness that formal communication demands.
