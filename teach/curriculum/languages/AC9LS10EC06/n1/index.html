Creating for a specific audience

AC9LS10EC06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can make deliberate language and textual decisions in a target language text based on a clearly identified audience.

Success criteria

  • Produces a text where at least two deliberate language choices are explicitly justified with reference to audience
  • Can explain how a different audience would require different language or format choices for the same content

Prior knowledge

Students can create spoken, written and multimodal texts using appropriate vocabulary, grammar and some textual conventions (AC9LS8EC06). They have not yet created texts for deliberately varied or unfamiliar audiences, or made strategic language selections to engage different audiences across different contexts and purposes.

Width

Minimum
Produce one target language text where at least two deliberate choices (vocabulary, structure, register, format) are explicitly made with reference to audience.
Typical
Create informative and imaginative texts with a clearly identified audience; make deliberate vocabulary, register and format choices that reflect audience knowledge, expectations and needs; produce bilingual texts for mixed-language audiences where appropriate.
Wider
  • Create the same content for two significantly different audiences (e.g. peers vs community members; second language learners vs fluent speakers) — compare the language choices required for each
  • Produce a promotional or community text in the target language for a real audience — evaluate its effectiveness based on actual reception

Hinge

Audience-oriented text creation is the central move of EC06 at Y9-10 — it distinguishes text creation from language practice. Students who produce target language texts without reference to audience are still doing EC06 Y7-8 tasks. The entire descriptor's demand for 'engaging different audiences' collapses if audience awareness is not established as a production criterion.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkAudience awareness in production requires students to hold a mental model of the reader or viewer while making language choices — a metacognitive demand that requires explicit scaffolding. Audience analysis tasks and annotation of deliberate choices make the process visible.
