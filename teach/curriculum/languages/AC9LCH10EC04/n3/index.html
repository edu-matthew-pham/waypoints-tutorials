← AC9LCH10EC04 Interpret and translate texts

Extended text translation and multimodal bilingual production

AC9LCH10EC04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can translate extended texts and produce multimodal bilingual resources — subtitled videos, captioned presentations, bilingual cultural products — that accurately convey meaning across both languages.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Translates a short extended text or produces a multimodal bilingual resource with accurate meaning transfer
  • Demonstrates at least two deliberate translation decisions beyond word-for-word substitution

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Interpreting for cross-cultural events.

Width

Minimum
Translate a short extended text (at least 8 sentences) or produce a multimodal bilingual resource, demonstrating accurate meaning transfer and at least two deliberate translation decisions.
Typical
Experiment with translations of extended texts using dictionaries, translation tools and own knowledge; compare bilingual editions of stories or poems; produce subtitles for Chinese or English programs; create multimodal bilingual products for cultural audiences; apply translation strategies in extended contexts.
Wider
  • Produce a fully subtitled bilingual video — evaluate accuracy and cultural appropriateness with a target audience and revise
  • Compare own translation of an extended text with an existing published translation — identify every difference, evaluate the choices, and discuss what the comparison reveals about translation as cultural interpretation

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierExtended text translation integrates comparison and evaluation skills (node 1) and cross-cultural interpretation (node 2) into a full sustained translation act. The teaching investment is in creating real-audience conditions — actual subtitling, real community events — where translation accuracy has genuine communicative stakes.
