Character form and complex grammatical structures

AC9LCH10EU02 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply knowledge of character form and function, and complex grammatical structures and features, to predict meaning and to compose a wide range of texts

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse character components, apply stroke conventions, and use a range of sentence structures including complex constructions (AC9LCH8EU02). They have inferred meaning from morphological knowledge. They have not yet analysed sequences of unfamiliar characters using component patterns to predict meaning, compared simplified and traditional forms systematically, or applied complex literary language and grammatical features in extended texts.

By the end

Students apply knowledge of character form and function and complex grammatical structures to predict meaning and compose a wide range of texts — including analysis of simplified/traditional forms, polysemy, literary language, and advanced grammatical features.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • analysing sequences of unfamiliar characters to determine features of form and function and predict meanings
  • comparing and contrasting characters in simplified and traditional forms
  • analysing and comparing unfamiliar characters in print media and online texts
  • applying understanding of word formation when comparing translations
  • discriminating correct forms and sounds of characters with multiple readings
  • inferring the meaning of unfamiliar words containing a familiar morpheme
  • explaining how morphology works to convey subtle and precise meanings
  • inferring meaning of terminology based on character analysis
  • applying punctuation conventions in writing and reading aloud
  • using language features to extend and elaborate a point of view
  • exploring functions of key grammatical forms in different contexts
  • exploring examples of literary language and how grammatical features differ from everyday speech
  • experimenting with abstract nouns to create procedural or creative texts

Waypoints

  • 1. Advanced character analysis and predictionHingeStudents can analyse sequences of unfamiliar characters using component patterns to predict meaning, compare simplified and traditional forms, and determine precise morpheme meanings from context.
  • 2. Complex grammatical features and literary languageStudents can apply complex grammatical features — modal and auxiliary verbs, abstract nouns, literary particles — and identify how grammatical features differ between literary and everyday language.
  • 3. Composing a wide range of textsStudents can apply full character and grammatical knowledge to compose a wide range of texts — formal, informal, procedural, literary, multimodal — across diverse contexts and purposes.
