Target language sound system

AC9LS8EU01 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise and use features of the target language sound system, including pitch, rhythm, stress, pronunciation and intonation, and demonstrate understanding of how these are represented in familiar contexts

Prior knowledge

Students enter the Y7-10 sequence with no formal prior study of the target language sound system. They bring awareness of English phonology and may have incidental exposure to the target language's sounds. They have not yet systematically learned the phonological features of the target language or how these are represented in its writing system.

By the end

Students recognise and use the key features of the target language sound system — vowels, consonants, intonation patterns, stress and pitch — and demonstrate understanding of how these are represented in familiar written and spoken contexts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising that many letters of the target language and English have the same sounds, with some exceptions
  • understanding that in some target languages there is only one sound for each vowel
  • recognising and practising different vowel and consonant sounds through simple rhymes
  • spelling own name aloud in the target language
  • recognising variations in intonation required to form statements, questions and exclamations
  • listening to statements, exclamations and questions read aloud, using intonation cues and transcribing what is heard
  • using own phonetic notes to help pronunciation and intonation
  • applying pronunciation rules to common performance texts such as tongue twisters

Waypoints

  • 1. Target language vowel and consonant soundsHingeStudents can recognise and produce the key vowel and consonant sounds of the target language, including those that differ from English.
  • 2. Intonation and sentence melodyStudents can recognise and use appropriate intonation patterns for statements, questions and exclamations in the target language.
  • 3. Stress, rhythm and phonological representationStudents can recognise and apply stress and rhythm patterns in the target language and understand how phonological features are represented in the writing system.
