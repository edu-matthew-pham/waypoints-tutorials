Initiate and sustain exchanges

AC9LCH6C01 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

initiate and sustain exchanges related to students' experiences and perspectives

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate exchanges and sustain multi-turn conversations about home and classroom environments using familiar expressions (AC9LCH4C01). They can ask for clarification, seek help, and use digital interaction formats. They have not yet exchanged opinions and perspectives, used idiomatic language in exchanges, demonstrated active listening strategies, or sustained conversations on topics of personal significance beyond immediate school and home contexts.

By the end

Students initiate and sustain exchanges related to their experiences and perspectives — including face-to-face and digital contexts — using strategies for active listening, expressing agreement and disagreement, and sharing opinions and feelings.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • initiating and sustaining conversations including face-to-face and secure online conversations by asking peers about personal interests
  • playing guessing games and practising modelled sentences and question words
  • developing conversation skills such as staying on topic, recognising non-verbal cues and using interjections
  • exchanging ideas and opinions in class discussions, expressing agreement, disagreement, negotiating and demonstrating active listening
  • using secure digital resources to write a daily or weekly journal recording events or highlights
  • expressing gratitude, apologies, sympathy or concern when interacting with family, friends or peers
  • checking on the progress of others during learning tasks using questions
  • exchanging text messages and emails using appropriate formats to negotiate weekend plans

Waypoints

  • 1. Expressing opinions and perspectivesHingeStudents can express opinions, agree and disagree, and exchange perspectives with peers using appropriate target language structures.
  • 2. Active listening and conversation managementStudents can use active listening strategies — non-verbal cues, interjections, follow-up questions — to sustain and manage conversations, recognising and responding to conversational signals.
  • 3. Sharing feelings and personal experiencesStudents can express gratitude, sympathy, concern and personal feelings in context, and exchange information about personal experiences including past events and future plans.
