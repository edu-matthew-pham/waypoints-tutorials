Expressing opinions and perspectives

AC9LCH6C01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can express opinions, agree and disagree, and exchange perspectives with peers using appropriate target language structures.

Success criteria

  • Expresses at least two opinions using target language structures
  • Responds to a peer's view with at least one agreement or disagreement expression

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate exchanges and sustain multi-turn conversations about home and classroom environments using familiar expressions (AC9LCH4C01). They can ask for clarification, seek help, and use digital interaction formats. They have not yet exchanged opinions and perspectives, used idiomatic language in exchanges, demonstrated active listening strategies, or sustained conversations on topics of personal significance beyond immediate school and home contexts.

Width

Minimum
Express at least two opinions on a topic and respond to a peer's view with at least one agreement or disagreement expression.
Typical
Exchange ideas and opinions in class discussions — expressing views, agreeing, disagreeing, negotiating, and demonstrating active listening; use a range of opinion and perspective expressions appropriate to the topic; engage in digital written exchanges using appropriate formats.
Wider
  • Sustain a discussion on a topic where views diverge — manage disagreement respectfully using target language and reach a negotiated position or summary
  • Compare how opinions and disagreement are expressed in the target language with English — identify where conventions differ and what this means for intercultural communication

Hinge

Opinion exchange is the entry condition for all perspective-based interaction at Y5-6 and beyond. Students who can only share information cannot participate in the discussions, negotiation and argument-based exchanges that this and subsequent descriptors require.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyOpinion and perspective language requires a growing bank of structures across different positions — agreement, partial agreement, disagreement, qualification. Repeated practice across varied topics and discussion formats builds the spontaneous deployment that genuine exchange demands.
