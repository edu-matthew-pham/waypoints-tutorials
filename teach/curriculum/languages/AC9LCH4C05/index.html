Create and present informative and imaginative texts

AC9LCH4C05 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

create and present informative and imaginative spoken, written and multimodal texts, using a range of sentence structures and modelled textual conventions, characters appropriate to context, and/or the target language writing system

Prior knowledge

Students can create short spoken, written and multimodal texts using modelled phrases and sentences (AC9LCH2C05). They can produce written target language and perform created texts for a purpose and audience. They have not yet created extended texts using a range of sentence structures, produced informative texts for real audiences, or created imaginative texts such as alternative story endings or original performances.

By the end

Students create and present a range of informative and imaginative spoken, written and multimodal texts using sentence structures and textual conventions appropriate to purpose and context.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • presenting a visual display with supporting text on a topic of personal interest
  • supporting presentations using pictures, charts and graphs
  • conveying information about school and community events in written and multimodal texts
  • creating simple descriptions in Chinese and matching them to appropriate First Nations Country/Place locations
  • presenting a show and tell about an object, activity or person special to them
  • using familiar words, phrases or idioms to create raps or songs
  • creating alternative endings to familiar stories
  • creating and performing simple imaginative texts to share with teachers and peers
  • creating a mini-book relating to a familiar topic

Waypoints

  • 1. Using a range of sentence structuresHingeStudents can construct sentences using a range of structures — including descriptive, compound and complex sentences — appropriate to the text type being created.
  • 2. Creating informative texts for a purpose and audienceStudents can create informative texts in spoken, written or multimodal form using appropriate textual conventions, with supporting visual elements where relevant.
  • 3. Creating imaginative texts and performancesStudents can create and perform imaginative texts — stories, songs, plays, dialogues — adapting familiar narratives or creating original work for a specific audience.
