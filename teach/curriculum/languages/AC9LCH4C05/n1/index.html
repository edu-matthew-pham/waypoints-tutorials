← AC9LCH4C05 Create and present informative and imaginative texts

Using a range of sentence structures

AC9LCH4C05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can construct sentences using a range of structures — including descriptive, compound and complex sentences — appropriate to the text type being created.

Success criteria

  • Produces at least three sentences using at least two different sentence structures
  • Applies correct writing system conventions in at least one written text

Prior knowledge

Students can create short spoken, written and multimodal texts using modelled phrases and sentences (AC9LCH2C05). They can produce written target language and perform created texts for a purpose and audience. They have not yet created extended texts using a range of sentence structures, produced informative texts for real audiences, or created imaginative texts such as alternative story endings or original performances.

Width

Minimum
Produce at least three sentences in the target language using different structures (e.g. a simple statement, a sentence with a modifier, and a question or compound sentence).
Typical
Use a range of sentence structures in created texts — simple, compound, descriptive, and question forms; use familiar words, phrases and idioms to add variety and expression; apply appropriate writing system conventions (stroke order, direction, punctuation) to written texts.
Wider
  • Analyse a model text and identify the sentence structures used — then deliberately vary the structures in an original text to achieve different effects
  • Experiment with how the same information reads differently depending on sentence structure choice — compare two versions of the same content and discuss the effect of each

Hinge

Sentence structure range is the prerequisite for creating extended, coherent texts. Students who can only produce simple or formulaic sentences cannot create the informative or imaginative texts with textual conventions that this descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSentence structure range requires repeated production practice across different text types before it becomes flexible and reliable. Scaffolded writing tasks with explicit structure modelling — then progressive reduction of scaffolding — build this productive fluency.
