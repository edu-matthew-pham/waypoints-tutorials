Language use across communities

AC9LCH2U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify ways in which language use differs across different communities and contexts where the target language is spoken.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two communities or regions where the target language is spoken
  • Identifies at least one way language use differs across those communities

Prior knowledge

Students have explored connections between language and culture and identified cultural practices associated with the target language (AC9LCHF03). They have recognised that language carries cultural meaning in everyday interactions (AC9LCH2C04). They have not yet systematically noticed how language use across a community reflects cultural practices, values and identity — or compared their own community's language use with others'.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two communities or regions where the target language is spoken and describe at least one way language use differs across them — for example, greeting conventions, vocabulary differences, or dialect features.
Typical
Compare and contrast classroom language use with target language use in other communities; identify countries and regions where the target language is used; notice that the same language is used differently across communities; explore how language varies across contexts within the target language community.
Wider
  • Research how the target language is used in different countries or regions — identify specific words, expressions or conventions that differ across communities and discuss why
  • Compare how First Nations Australian languages and the target language are each associated with specific communities and places — discuss what language tells us about belonging and connection

Hinge

Understanding that language use varies across communities is the conceptual prerequisite for all subsequent work on language, culture and identity. Students who assume all target language speakers use language the same way cannot appreciate the role of cultural context in shaping communication.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepCommunity language variation is understood through encounter with varied examples — images, media, guest speakers, maps. Single examples of target language use give an incomplete picture. Varied community contexts build the insight that language is socially and culturally situated.
