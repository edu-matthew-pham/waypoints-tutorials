Language reflects cultural practices

AC9LCH2U04 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

notice that people use language in ways that reflect cultural practices

Prior knowledge

Students have explored connections between language and culture and identified cultural practices associated with the target language (AC9LCHF03). They have recognised that language carries cultural meaning in everyday interactions (AC9LCH2C04). They have not yet systematically noticed how language use across a community reflects cultural practices, values and identity — or compared their own community's language use with others'.

By the end

Students notice that people use language in ways that reflect cultural practices — including how language use differs across communities, the role of cultural symbols and language in identity, and the relationship between language and cultural belonging.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing and contrasting classroom interactions with those in target language communities
  • using gestures, words and phrases that reflect aspects of target language culture
  • identifying countries and regions where the target language is used as a language of communication
  • exploring symbols and language used by First Nations Australians and in target language communities in a range of contexts
  • discussing the role of the target language in their own lives — cultural events, food preferences, overseas travel
  • exploring cultural symbols and practices through stories, songs, dances, games and crafts
  • sharing information about their family — country of origin, languages spoken, current locations of extended family

Waypoints

  • 1. Language use across communitiesHingeStudents can identify ways in which language use differs across different communities and contexts where the target language is spoken.
  • 2. Cultural symbols, practices and languageStudents can identify cultural symbols and practices associated with the target language and describe how language is used in those contexts.
  • 3. Language, identity and personal connectionStudents can reflect on the role of the target language in their own identity and daily life, and share information about their family's language history and cultural connections.
