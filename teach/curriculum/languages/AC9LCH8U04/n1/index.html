Attitudes, beliefs and values shaping communication

AC9LCH8U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how attitudes, beliefs and values — in both their own and others' backgrounds — shape communication choices in the target language and English.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two ways attitudes, beliefs or values shape communication in the target language community
  • Compares these with at least one parallel example from English-speaking contexts

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise how language reflects cultural practices and identity, and have reflected on their own intercultural positioning (AC9LCH6U04). They have explored language variation across communities. They have not yet examined how attitudes, beliefs and values shape communication style, reflected on how cultural events and practices change across contexts, or critically examined stereotyping in intercultural interaction.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two ways that attitudes, beliefs or values shape communication style in the target language community, and compare with at least one way these factors shape communication in English-speaking contexts.
Typical
Reflect on how attitudes, beliefs and values are reflected in language and gesture in everyday conversations and media; examine how cultural background shapes communication style; compare communication norms across cultural contexts; identify how First Nations Australian languages connect to Country and compare with target language cultural connections.
Wider
  • Analyse a real or fictional intercultural interaction — identify every moment where attitudes, beliefs or values are visible in a language or gesture choice, and explain the cultural logic behind each
  • Compare how a specific belief or value (e.g. about respect, family, community) shapes language use differently in the target language community and in own family or community context

Hinge

Understanding how attitudes and values shape communication is the conceptual prerequisite for all intercultural reflection at this level. Students who cannot see the value system behind communication choices cannot reflect meaningfully on their own identity or explain others' behaviour.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkValues-to-communication analysis requires explicit scaffolding — students need a framework for moving from 'they communicate differently' to 'this is the belief or value that produces this communication pattern.' Guided analysis with cultural context information builds this explanatory capacity.
