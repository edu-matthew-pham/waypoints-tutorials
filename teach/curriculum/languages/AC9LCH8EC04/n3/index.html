← AC9LCH8EC04 Interpret, translate and convey meaning

Producing bilingual texts

AC9LCH8EC04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce bilingual texts — subtitles, translated resources, cross-language explanations — that accurately convey meaning across both languages for a specific purpose.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces a bilingual text with accurate meaning transfer in both languages
  • Demonstrates at least one deliberate translation decision beyond word-for-word substitution

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Interpreting for others and managing untranslatables.

Width

Minimum
Produce at least one bilingual text with accurate meaning transfer, demonstrating at least one deliberate translation decision beyond word-for-word substitution.
Typical
Produce bilingual texts — subtitles, translated materials, cross-curricular explanations for target language peers; apply translation strategies; rewrite lyrics or story lines in the target language; use resources to create Chinese translations of English texts.
Wider
  • Produce a bilingual text for a real audience — evaluate accuracy and cultural appropriateness with a target audience member and revise
  • Reflect on the translation decisions made — identify which were straightforward, which required cultural judgment, and what the process revealed about the two languages

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierBilingual text production integrates register conversion (node 1) and interpreting strategies (node 2) into a full translation act. The teaching investment is in creating real or realistic audience conditions where translation decisions carry genuine communicative weight.
