← AC9LCH8EC04 Interpret, translate and convey meaning

Interpreting for others and managing untranslatables

AC9LCH8EC04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can act as interpreter for target language and English speakers, applying clarifying and rephrasing strategies, and handle idioms and culturally-embedded language that cannot be translated directly.

Success criteria

  • Interprets an exchange using at least one clarifying or rephrasing strategy
  • Handles at least one idiom or untranslatable expression with an appropriate strategy and explanation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Register conversion and cultural connotation.

Width

Minimum
Interpret at least one exchange between target language and English speakers using at least one clarifying or rephrasing strategy, and handle at least one idiom or untranslatable expression with an appropriate strategy.
Typical
Act as interpreter for visitors — clarifying, rephrasing and explaining cultural concepts; apply strategies to translate idioms, fables and culturally-embedded stories; read bilingual texts and identify expressions that cannot be directly translated; use dictionaries and digital tools to support translation.
Wider
  • Interpret in a scenario involving a cultural concept that has no English equivalent — explain the concept to both parties and discuss the cultural background required to understand it
  • Compare own informal interpreting experience from home and community with the structured interpreting strategies introduced in this descriptor — identify what is already intuitive and what requires explicit development

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepInterpreting and untranslatable expression handling must be practised across varied cultural contexts — different audiences, different types of culturally-embedded language. Varied interpreting scenarios build flexible, audience-aware mediation capacity.
