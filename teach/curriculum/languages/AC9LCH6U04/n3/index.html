← AC9LCH6U04 Language reflects cultural practices, values and identity

Intercultural positioning and personal reflection

AC9LCH6U04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can reflect on their own experience of navigating between two or more language-culture communities, and identify how this shapes their communication and identity.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Reflects on at least one experience of navigating between two cultural-language contexts
  • Identifies at least one cultural value or communication difference that shaped that experience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Regional and community variation in language and culture.

Width

Minimum
Reflect on at least one specific experience of navigating between two cultural-language contexts — identifying what felt different, what caused difficulty, or what new perspective emerged.
Typical
Reflect on how interacting in the target language feels different from interacting in English; identify culturally-specific behaviours and communication styles; explore how students' multilingual experience shapes their perspective on cultural difference; discuss miscommunication experiences and what caused them.
Wider
  • Describe a specific intercultural communication experience in depth — identifying the cultural values at play, what was assumed, what caused misunderstanding, and what was learned
  • Compare own intercultural navigation experience with that of a peer or community member — identify what is shared and what is specific to the student's own language history and community

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierIntercultural reflection integrates cultural value analysis (node 1) and community variation awareness (node 2) into a personally grounded synthesis. The teaching investment is in creating a reflective, non-judgmental environment where students can examine their own intercultural experiences honestly — including moments of discomfort or misalignment.
