Language choices and cultural values in communication

AC9LCH6U04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how specific language choices in spoken and non-verbal communication reflect cultural practices and values, and explain the impact on interaction.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two examples of language choices reflecting cultural values
  • Explains the impact of at least one such choice on an interaction

Prior knowledge

Students can identify connections between the target language and cultural practices, and understand how language encodes values and identity (AC9LCH4U04). They can examine music, arts and cultural expression. They have not yet analysed how language variation across regional and community contexts encodes cultural identity, examined the impact of cultural language values on miscommunication, or reflected on their own intercultural positioning.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two examples of how language choices in the target language (verbal or non-verbal) reflect specific cultural values, and explain the impact on an interaction.
Typical
Examine how language choices across contexts — address forms, politeness conventions, topic selection, non-verbal behaviour — reflect cultural values; discuss how the same interaction would be managed differently across cultural contexts; identify the interconnections between cultural practices and language use.
Wider
  • Analyse a recorded or simulated intercultural interaction — identify the specific moments where cultural language values are visible and discuss how each shapes the interaction
  • Compare how a specific cultural value (e.g. respect for elders, directness, hospitality) is encoded in language and non-verbal behaviour differently in the target language community versus Australian English-speaking contexts

Hinge

Understanding that language choices reflect cultural values is the conceptual prerequisite for all intercultural communication awareness. Students who cannot see the cultural logic behind language choices cannot identify miscommunication risks or develop the intercultural competence that this descriptor targets.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCultural value encoding in language requires explicit analytical scaffolding — students need to move from observing a language choice to explaining the cultural logic behind it. Guided analysis with cultural context information builds this explanatory capacity before students can reason independently.
