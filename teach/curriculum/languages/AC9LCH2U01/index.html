Sound patterns and tones

AC9LCH2U01 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise and use sound patterns and tones of the target language

Prior knowledge

Students have noticed distinctive sounds and sound patterns in the target language and compared them with English (AC9LCHF02). They can imitate familiar sounds and identify some similarities and differences. They have not yet systematically recognised and used tones or tone-marking conventions, identified syllable structure, or applied sound pattern knowledge to their own spoken production with accuracy.

By the end

Students recognise and use the sound patterns and tones of the target language — identifying tones, matching tones to meaning, representing sounds in written form, and developing spoken fluency through attention to rhythm, tone and stress.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • listening to and performing nursery rhymes and stories with rhythms, tones and sound patterns
  • participating in tone-guessing activities identifying the 4 tones and the neutral tone
  • writing syllables in the phonetic system with correct tone marks
  • recording own pronunciation of different tones and playing games with peers
  • singing a Pinyin alphabet song understanding that Pinyin is the romanisation of spoken sounds
  • playing pair-matching card games matching tones, initial to final
  • identifying syllables that make up target language words
  • developing fluency of sentences using gesture to demonstrate tone and stress

Waypoints

  • 1. Recognising and distinguishing tonesHingeStudents can recognise and distinguish the tonal system of the target language, identifying how different tones on the same syllable produce different meanings.
  • 2. Representing sounds in written formStudents can represent target language sounds in written form using the romanisation or phonetic system of the target language, including tone marks where applicable.
  • 3. Syllable structure and spoken fluencyStudents can identify syllables in target language words and apply sound pattern knowledge to develop spoken fluency through rhymes, poems and familiar texts.
