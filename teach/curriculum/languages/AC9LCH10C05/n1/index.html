Evaluative and persuasive writing

AC9LCH10C05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce evaluative and persuasive texts — reviews, debates, analytical responses, advocacy pieces — selecting language and structure deliberately to make a case.

Success criteria

  • Produces an evaluative or persuasive text with a clear position supported by evidence
  • Uses at least three persuasive structures across the text

Prior knowledge

Students can create and present extended multimodal texts with language manipulation, drafting and editing processes (AC9LCH8C05). They have produced community-facing texts with deliberate stylistic choices. They have not yet produced evaluative and persuasive writing at extended length, created procedural texts, produced literary or reflective texts for publication, or selected and applied conventions from specific literary styles.

Width

Minimum
Produce an evaluative or persuasive text of at least ten sentences using at least three persuasive structures and demonstrating a clear position supported by evidence or reasoning.
Typical
Produce evaluative writing — reviews, analytical responses, feedback; write and debate persuasive texts on a range of topics; use evidence, logical structure and persuasive language; create texts for school newspapers and community publications; analyse and evaluate pros and cons of issues.
Wider
  • Produce an extended persuasive text on a topic of genuine personal significance — with a documented drafting and revision process showing deliberate language choices
  • Compare persuasive text conventions in target language and English — identify how argument structure, evidence presentation and rhetorical strategies differ, and apply target language conventions authentically

Hinge

Evaluative and persuasive writing is the foundational productive capability for the full range of texts this descriptor requires. Students who can only produce narrative or informative texts cannot meet the evaluative, argumentative and procedural demands that follow.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPersuasive and evaluative texts require deliberate structural planning before drafting — students need to identify their position, organise evidence, and select appropriate language before producing. Explicit argument structure instruction followed by scaffolded writing practice builds this planning capacity.
