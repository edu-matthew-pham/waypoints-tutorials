Character form and function, sentence structures and syntax

AC9LCH8U02 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply knowledge of character form and function, sentence structures, syntax, and writing system features to infer meaning and to compose a range of texts

Prior knowledge

Students can use morphological knowledge to infer meaning in unfamiliar words and apply complex grammatical structures in extended texts (AC9LCH6U02). They have composed texts across familiar and some unfamiliar contexts. They have not yet applied grammatical knowledge to modal, tense-expression, exclamatory and response-to-negative-question structures, used digital input methods for the writing system, or composed a full range of sentence types — affirmative, negative, interrogative, imperative — systematically.

By the end

Students apply knowledge of character form and function, sentence structures, syntax and writing system features to infer meaning and compose a range of texts — including modal structures, tense-expression patterns, and varied sentence types.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • using knowledge of components to select a character when inputting the target language digitally
  • categorising words containing a common morpheme to understand the relationship between morphemes and specific word meanings
  • applying knowledge of morphemes to create new words and confirming combinations using dictionaries
  • applying knowledge of punctuation in writing
  • understanding elements of grammar such as word order and sentence construction
  • understanding and using verbs to express modality
  • comparing how tense is expressed in the target language — completion, experience, intention, negation
  • recognising the purpose and effect of exclamatory sentences as opposed to statements
  • constructing a range of sentences in affirmative, negative, interrogative and imperative forms
  • recognising differences in Chinese and English responses to negative tag questions

Waypoints

  • 1. Advanced morphological application and digital inputHingeStudents can apply morphological knowledge to create new words and use digital input methods for the target language writing system.
  • 2. Modal, tense-expression and exclamatory structuresStudents can recognise and use modal verbs, tense-expression patterns, and exclamatory structures in the target language, understanding how these differ from English equivalents.
  • 3. Full sentence-type range in composed textsStudents can compose texts using the full range of sentence types — affirmative, negative, interrogative, imperative — and apply full writing system and syntactic knowledge across a range of text types.
