← AC9LCH2C04 Language carries cultural meaning

Non-verbal and visual cues as cultural meaning

AC9LCH2C04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how non-verbal cues — gesture, facial expression, body language — carry culturally-specific meaning in the target language, and interpret these in interaction and media texts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two non-verbal cues in the target language and explains their cultural meaning
  • Demonstrates or describes the correct use of at least one culturally-specific gesture or non-verbal convention

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Culturally-specific expressions and untranslatable language.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two non-verbal cues in the target language (gesture, facial expression, body language) and explain what they mean in their cultural context.
Typical
Interpret cultural gestures and non-verbal communication conventions; identify how facial expressions and visual cues in animations, songs and stories contribute to meaning; compare non-verbal conventions between the target language community and English-speaking contexts.
Wider
  • Analyse a short animated or video text and identify all the non-verbal cues that carry meaning — compare with a peer's reading to discuss what is shared and what is culturally interpreted
  • Discuss how non-verbal communication in the target language compares with gesture and body language in First Nations Australian communication — identify similarities and differences in how gesture carries meaning

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierNon-verbal meaning interpretation integrates politeness conventions (node 1) and cultural expression awareness (node 2) into a holistic reading of communicative acts. The teaching investment is in creating analysis tasks that require students to notice the full communicative package — verbal, non-verbal and contextual — not only words.
