Politeness, address and social etiquette conventions

AC9LCH2C04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and use language conventions for politeness, appropriate address and social etiquette in familiar everyday contexts.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two politeness or address conventions in the target language and can explain why each is used
  • Demonstrates the correct form in at least one context — for example, a polite request or appropriate address for an adult

Prior knowledge

Students have used formulaic social expressions in classroom and play contexts (AC9LCH2C01, AC9LCH2C02). They have noticed that gestures and body language contribute to meaning. They have not yet examined why specific language forms are used — the cultural conventions behind politeness, address, etiquette — or identified expressions that carry cultural meaning with no direct English equivalent.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two conventions of politeness or address in the target language and use them correctly in context — for example, distinguishing a polite request from a demand, or using appropriate address for an adult.
Typical
Demonstrate levels of politeness in different contexts — requests, thanks, address of adults and peers; identify cultural gestures and etiquette conventions; distinguish between polite and impolite forms of the same communicative act; use appropriate language when addressing people of different ages or authority.
Wider
  • Compare politeness conventions in the target language with English — identify where the conventions differ and what this reveals about cultural values around respect and relationship
  • Explore a range of everyday social contexts (greeting an elder, receiving a gift, entering someone's home) and identify the specific language and gesture conventions for each

Hinge

Politeness and address conventions are the foundation for all culturally appropriate social interaction. Students who do not understand why specific language forms are used cannot interpret or produce culturally appropriate communication — a prerequisite for the cultural translation and mediation work that develops across Y3-4 and beyond.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPoliteness conventions require students to move from observing language use to understanding the cultural logic behind it. Explicit discussion of why a particular form is used — not just what is said — builds the cultural reasoning capacity that distinguishes background learners' insight from surface imitation.
