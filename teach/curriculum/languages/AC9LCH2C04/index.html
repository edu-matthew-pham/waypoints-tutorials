Language carries cultural meaning

AC9LCH2C04 · Years 1–2 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise that language carries cultural meaning in everyday social interactions

Prior knowledge

Students have used formulaic social expressions in classroom and play contexts (AC9LCH2C01, AC9LCH2C02). They have noticed that gestures and body language contribute to meaning. They have not yet examined why specific language forms are used — the cultural conventions behind politeness, address, etiquette — or identified expressions that carry cultural meaning with no direct English equivalent.

By the end

Students recognise that language choices in everyday social interactions carry cultural meaning — including conventions of politeness, address, etiquette, and culturally-specific expressions — and can identify examples and explain their significance.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • discussing the different implications of direct versus polite request forms
  • showing politeness in different contexts, making requests with appropriate register
  • learning about etiquette in everyday social contexts such as how to address adults
  • understanding cultural gestures, for example, standing up to greet a teacher, receiving an object with both hands
  • creating bilingual resources such as illustrated word charts or digital flashcards
  • discussing words or expressions that appear to have no equivalent in English
  • discussing meanings of colloquial phrases used on specific occasions
  • interpreting language, facial expressions and other visual cues in animations or songs

Waypoints

  • 1. Politeness, address and social etiquette conventionsHingeStudents can identify and use language conventions for politeness, appropriate address and social etiquette in familiar everyday contexts.
  • 2. Culturally-specific expressions and untranslatable languageStudents can identify expressions in the target language that carry cultural meaning — including phrases with no direct English equivalent or phrases used on specific cultural occasions.
  • 3. Non-verbal and visual cues as cultural meaningStudents can identify how non-verbal cues — gesture, facial expression, body language — carry culturally-specific meaning in the target language, and interpret these in interaction and media texts.
