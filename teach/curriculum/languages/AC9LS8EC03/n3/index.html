Simulated and real transactions

AC9LS8EC03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can participate in simulated or real transactional exchanges (e.g. purchasing, requesting services, following directions) in the target language.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can complete a basic transactional exchange (request, respond, clarify) in a modelled scenario using target language
  • Integrates at least one asking or clarifying strategy from prior learning into the exchange

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Non-verbal communication and multimodal exchanges.

Width

Minimum
Participate in one scripted or semi-scripted transactional role-play in the target language (e.g. a simulated purchase, asking for and giving directions).
Typical
Engage in a range of simulated transactional scenarios; use modelled language for requesting, responding, clarifying and completing a transaction; integrate asking strategies from EC02.
Wider
  • Navigate a real or simulated digital service (e.g. a map, timetable or menu from a target language community) to locate and act on information
  • Produce a class resource (signs, posters, reference materials) in the target language that could support real transactions in a target language environment

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierTransactional exchanges require students to simultaneously manage topic vocabulary, asking strategies (EC02), appropriate register (EC01) and non-verbal conventions (node 2). Role-play scenarios that require all of these to operate together are the key teaching structure.
