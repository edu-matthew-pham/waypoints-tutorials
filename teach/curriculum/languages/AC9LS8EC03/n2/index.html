← AC9LS8EC03 Organise activities with peers

Non-verbal communication and multimodal exchanges

AC9LS8EC03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise the role of non-verbal communication in the target language context and combine non-verbal, spoken and written elements in exchanges.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can combine at least two communication modes (spoken, written, non-verbal) in a single organised exchange
  • Can identify at least one non-verbal convention in the target language community that differs from Australian English norms

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Coordinating and planning with peers.

Width

Minimum
Participate in one exchange that requires combining at least two modes (e.g. spoken + gesture, written + visual) in the target language.
Typical
Engage in modelled scenarios (e.g. simulated transactions, directions, collaborative tasks) that require coordinating spoken, written and non-verbal elements; recognise culturally specific non-verbal conventions in the target language community.
Wider
  • Participate in a charades or mime scenario and reflect on which meanings transfer across cultures and which require cultural knowledge to interpret
  • Produce a bilingual resource (poster, flyer, digital notice) that combines visual and written elements to communicate to a mixed-language audience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepNon-verbal conventions in the target language vary significantly across cultural communities. Exposure to varied scenarios prevents students from assuming that non-verbal cues are universal — a misconception with practical intercultural consequences.
