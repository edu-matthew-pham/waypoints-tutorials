← AC9LS8EC04 Locate and process information in texts

Processing information across text types

AC9LS8EC04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can process information from spoken, written and multimodal texts in the target language, summarising or classifying key ideas.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Can identify and summarise key points from a short spoken or multimodal text in the target language
  • Can compare or classify information from at least two texts on the same topic

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying key information in familiar texts.

Width

Minimum
Process information from at least one spoken or multimodal text (not only written) — e.g. identify key points from a short recording or video.
Typical
Locate and process information from spoken, written and multimodal texts; summarise, classify or compare information across sources; conduct and report simple information-gathering tasks such as surveys or brief interviews.
Wider
  • Compare information across two or more sources on the same topic — identify where sources agree or differ
  • Locate and process information from a First Nations Australian context presented in or translated into the target language — reflect on what is gained or lost in the shift

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepProcessing spoken texts requires different strategies from written text processing — prosody, pace and speaker variation all carry meaning. Ensuring students process across modalities prevents over-reliance on written text as the primary comprehension mode.
