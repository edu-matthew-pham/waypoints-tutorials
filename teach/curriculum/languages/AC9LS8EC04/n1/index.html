Identifying key information in familiar texts

AC9LS8EC04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify specific details and key points of information in familiar spoken, written or multimodal texts in the target language.

Success criteria

  • Can locate at least three specific pieces of information from a familiar target language text using comprehension strategies
  • Can identify the purpose and intended audience of a familiar text type in the target language

Prior knowledge

Students enter the Y7-10 sequence without prior formal target language study. They bring general literacy strategies from English and may have incidental exposure to the target language. They have not yet applied information-locating strategies to target language texts across spoken, written and multimodal modes.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three specific pieces of information from one familiar text type (e.g. a menu, timetable, short announcement or simple written notice) in the target language.
Typical
Locate and classify key information from a range of familiar text types (menus, notices, timetables, packaging, schedules); use comprehension strategies (skimming, scanning, using contextual cues) to locate specific details.
Wider
  • Compare information located in equivalent text types from the target language community and Australia — identify differences in how information is structured or presented
  • Navigate an authentic digital resource (map, timetable, website) from a target language community to locate specific information for a real purpose

Hinge

Locating specific information is the prerequisite for all processing and responding tasks in EC04. Students who rely only on global comprehension (understanding 'roughly what it is about') cannot satisfy the descriptor's demand to locate and process specific ideas. EC05's translation and interpretation tasks also depend on this capacity.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents need explicit instruction in comprehension strategies (scanning for key words, using layout and headings, making predictions from context) before they can apply them independently to target language texts. Modelling the strategy process — not just assigning texts — is the lever.
Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepExposing students to a range of familiar text types ensures comprehension strategies are not locked to one text format. Scanning a menu requires different cues than scanning a timetable.
