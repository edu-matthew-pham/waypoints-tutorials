Language manipulation for effect

AC9LCH8C05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can deliberately manipulate language — through word choice, register variation, humour, persuasion, or stylistic devices — to create intended effects in a text.

Success criteria

  • Produces a text with at least two deliberate language manipulation choices
  • Can explain the intended effect of each choice

Prior knowledge

Students can create and present texts adapted for purpose and audience using cohesive devices and a range of sentence structures (AC9LCH6C05). They have created community-facing texts and performed imaginative work. They have not yet manipulated language for deliberate effect — through humour, persuasion, irony, stylistic experimentation — or produced extended multimodal texts with developed drafting and editing processes.

Width

Minimum
Produce a text demonstrating at least two deliberate language manipulation choices — for example, a persuasive structure, a humorous element, a register shift, or a descriptive device — and explain the intended effect of each.
Typical
Manipulate language for deliberate effect — persuasive structures, humour, irony, descriptive and emotive language, jingles, wordplay; adapt characters and scenarios to new contexts; experiment with stylistic choices; discuss how language manipulation serves different purposes.
Wider
  • Create two versions of the same content — one neutral, one manipulated for effect — and compare the impact of each on a reader or audience
  • Analyse how a published target language text manipulates language for effect, then apply the same technique in an original piece

Hinge

Language manipulation is the defining capability of this descriptor. Students who can only produce functional, neutral text cannot meet the 'manipulating language to suit context, purpose and audience' demand — which is the core distinction from Y5-6 text creation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyLanguage manipulation techniques — persuasion, humour, irony, stylistic variation — are built through encounter with varied models and repeated experimental production. Students need exposure to how published writers use these techniques before they can deploy them deliberately.
