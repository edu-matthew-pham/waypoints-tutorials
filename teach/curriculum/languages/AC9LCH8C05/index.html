Create and present informative and imaginative texts

AC9LCH8C05 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

create and present spoken, written and multimodal informative and imaginative texts, manipulating language to suit context, purpose and audience

Prior knowledge

Students can create and present texts adapted for purpose and audience using cohesive devices and a range of sentence structures (AC9LCH6C05). They have created community-facing texts and performed imaginative work. They have not yet manipulated language for deliberate effect — through humour, persuasion, irony, stylistic experimentation — or produced extended multimodal texts with developed drafting and editing processes.

By the end

Students create and present spoken, written and multimodal informative and imaginative texts, manipulating language to suit context, purpose and audience — including persuasive, humorous and stylistically distinctive texts developed through drafting and editing.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • producing multimodal presentations using tables, maps, charts or graphs to deliver information
  • producing a poster or digital brochure using persuasive language to promote aspects of life in a target language region
  • creating spoken or written texts such as advertisements promoting travel
  • writing a journal entry or contributing to a school newsletter reflecting on a school excursion
  • creating captions, commentaries or subtitles for short video clips of intercultural experiences
  • creating jingles or songs by manipulating language to match tunes
  • adapting stories or films to new settings and contexts
  • creating the next scene or a new character for target language imaginative texts
  • creating written and multimodal narrative accounts of historical figures or literary characters
  • collaborating to prepare and present short dramatic or humorous performances
  • developing drafting and editing skills considering stylistic and creative features

Waypoints

  • 1. Language manipulation for effectHingeStudents can deliberately manipulate language — through word choice, register variation, humour, persuasion, or stylistic devices — to create intended effects in a text.
  • 2. Drafting, editing and stylistic developmentStudents can develop informative and imaginative texts through a drafting and editing process, making deliberate revisions to improve stylistic and creative features.
  • 3. Extended multimodal presentationStudents can create and present extended multimodal texts — combining spoken, written and visual elements — for informative or imaginative purposes, with appropriate production choices for context and audience.
