← AC9LCH8C05 Create and present informative and imaginative texts

Drafting, editing and stylistic development

AC9LCH8C05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop informative and imaginative texts through a drafting and editing process, making deliberate revisions to improve stylistic and creative features.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces a text through a documented drafting process with at least two deliberate revisions
  • Can explain the reason for each revision in terms of intended effect or audience impact

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Language manipulation for effect.

Width

Minimum
Produce at least one text through a documented drafting process — showing at least two deliberate revisions that improve a stylistic or creative feature.
Typical
Develop drafting and editing skills in own writing; revise texts for stylistic and creative improvement; seek and apply peer feedback; compare draft and final versions and explain revision choices; produce multimodal texts with developed visual and textual elements.
Wider
  • Conduct a structured peer editing session — providing metalanguage-supported feedback on a peer's text and applying equivalent feedback to own work
  • Produce a portfolio of drafts showing the development of a single text from first draft to final version, with annotations explaining each revision decision

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEffective drafting and editing requires students to evaluate their own texts analytically — identifying what is working and what needs revision — and to make deliberate improvements rather than surface corrections. Explicit revision frameworks and peer feedback structures build this self-evaluative capacity.
