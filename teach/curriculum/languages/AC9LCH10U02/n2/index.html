Complex modality and advanced grammatical features

AC9LCH10U02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply knowledge of modal and auxiliary verbs, complex tense expression, and advanced punctuation conventions across a range of text types.

Success criteria

  • Produces a text demonstrating correct use of at least three different complex grammatical features
  • Applies advanced punctuation conventions correctly in at least one text

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Advanced character analysis and print resource use.

Width

Minimum
Produce a text demonstrating correct use of at least three different complex grammatical features — modal verbs, auxiliary verbs, or advanced punctuation — with appropriate accuracy for the text type.
Typical
Apply modal verbs and auxiliary verbs in a range of text types; apply knowledge of tense in formal and informal contexts; use advanced punctuation including quotation marks for sarcasm, brackets, ellipsis; respond appropriately to sarcasm, exclamatory sentences and factual statements; identify how sentence structures convey messages effectively.
Wider
  • Analyse how modal and auxiliary verbs create different degrees of certainty, obligation or possibility — create a set of examples showing the full range and explain the distinctions
  • Investigate advanced punctuation conventions in the target language that have no direct English equivalent — explain the function of each and create examples demonstrating correct use

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsComplex modality and advanced grammatical features require repeated production across varied text types before they become reliable. Targeted writing tasks across different genres — formal report, personal narrative, persuasive text — build the flexible grammatical competence the descriptor requires.
