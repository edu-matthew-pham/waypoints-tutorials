Identity shaped by language and culture

AC9LS8EU04 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise how identity is shaped by language(s), culture(s), attitudes, beliefs and values

Prior knowledge

Students enter the Y7-10 sequence without formal prior study of target language cultures. They bring lived experience of identity shaped by their own language(s) and cultural contexts, but have not yet examined how language and culture interact to shape identity in the target language community or reflected on how learning the target language affects their own sense of identity.

By the end

Students recognise how identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values — in target language communities and in their own experience — and identify specific language features, cultural practices and texts that reflect and reinforce identity.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • viewing interactions between target language speakers and recognising register as well as comparing culturally determined behaviours
  • listening to and reading traditional texts, identifying key messages, beliefs and values
  • identifying that a sense of identity and belonging is important across all cultures and discussing how it may be expressed differently
  • examining how First Nations Australian languages have strong connections to Country/Place location
  • exploring language features that reflect and embody cultural values and history, including regional differences
  • identifying elements of ways of communicating and behaving in the target language that may be unfamiliar to people from different cultural backgrounds
  • considering language use depending on context and relationship
  • understanding that languages and cultures change continuously due to contact with other languages and cultures
  • collecting examples of word borrowings from other languages
  • observing that many target language speakers may be multilingual and can move between languages to achieve different purposes
  • examining what is most important to them and their way of life

Waypoints

  • 1. Language and cultural identityStudents can identify ways in which the target language reflects and reinforces cultural identity, attitudes and values.
  • 2. Register, context and social identityStudents can recognise how language use varies according to social context, relationship and identity, and identify examples in the target language.
  • 3. Personal identity and language learningStudents can reflect on how their own identity is expressed through language and consider how learning the target language affects or expands their sense of identity.
