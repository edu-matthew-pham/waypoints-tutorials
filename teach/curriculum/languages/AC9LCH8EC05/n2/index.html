Imaginative and creative texts

AC9LCH8EC05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create imaginative texts — stories, songs, role-plays, cultural narratives — using descriptive language, cultural references, and deliberate creative choices.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Creates an imaginative text with at least one deliberate creative choice
  • Demonstrates cultural knowledge through at least one reference, convention or language choice in the text

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Informative texts for real purposes.

Width

Minimum
Create at least one imaginative text demonstrating at least one deliberate creative choice (language, structure, cultural reference, or performance element).
Typical
Create stories about Chinese-speaking Australians; compose songs, jingles or video clips; write and perform role-plays using cultural stories; create original stories using common expressions or traditional literary idioms; produce bilingual creative texts.
Wider
  • Create an imaginative text that draws on a specific target language literary or cultural tradition — explain the creative choices made and how they reflect that tradition
  • Produce an imaginative text for a real community audience — performing or publishing for Chinese-speaking community members

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepImaginative text creation requires encounter with varied creative forms — stories, songs, role-plays, videos — before students can make independent genre and format choices. Varied creative tasks build the repertoire of forms available for deliberate selection.
