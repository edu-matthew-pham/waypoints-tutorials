← AC9LCH8EU03 Compare language structures using metalanguage

Text-type and sequencing comparison

AC9LCH8EU03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare how different text types sequence and develop ideas in the target language and English, identifying structural and language feature differences.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Compares the structure of at least one text type across target language and English
  • Identifies at least three structural or language feature differences

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Metalanguage for grammatical comparison.

Width

Minimum
Compare the structure of at least one text type (e.g. blog, email, argument, report) across target language and English, identifying at least three differences.
Typical
Compare different ways of sequencing ideas for different audiences and purposes; compare blog versus email conventions; compare argument structure in speech versus essay; read formal target language texts and compare with everyday speech; compare traditional and contemporary literary expressions.
Wider
  • Analyse how argument structure differs across target language and English texts — identify where the writer's position appears, how evidence is organised, and discuss the cultural logic behind the difference
  • Create the same text type in both languages, then compare the structural and language choices made — identify what is transferred and what is adapted

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepText-type comparison must be applied across varied genres before students can generalise their comparative analytical capacity. Single-genre comparison produces narrow analytical skills that don't transfer.
