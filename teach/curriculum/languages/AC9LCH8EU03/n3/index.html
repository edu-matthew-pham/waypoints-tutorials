Teaching and applying comparative knowledge

AC9LCH8EU03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply comparative language knowledge to access unfamiliar content and explain key language differences to others.

Success criteria

  • Applies comparative knowledge to access at least one unfamiliar text
  • Produces an explanation or resource about a key language difference for another learner

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Text-type and sequencing comparison.

Width

Minimum
Apply comparative knowledge to access at least one unfamiliar text, and produce at least one explanation or resource about a key language difference for a peer or younger learner.
Typical
Apply understanding of text and language features to access unfamiliar content; read texts and select appealing expressions to explain their choices; apply knowledge of formal text features to understand news articles and reports; explain key language differences using support resources.
Wider
  • Create a teaching resource about a specific language difference — test it with a real or simulated learner audience and revise based on feedback
  • Apply comparative linguistic knowledge to analyse a genuinely unfamiliar text type — document the strategies used and evaluate their effectiveness

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierTeaching and applying comparative knowledge integrates metalanguage (node 1) and text-type comparison (node 2) into a transfer task. The teaching investment is in creating genuine transfer conditions — real unfamiliar texts, real teaching audiences — that require functional application of comparative knowledge.
