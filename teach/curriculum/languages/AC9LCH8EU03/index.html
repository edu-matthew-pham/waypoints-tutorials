Compare language structures using metalanguage

AC9LCH8EU03 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

compare and explain similarities and differences between Chinese and English language structures and features, using familiar metalanguage

Prior knowledge

Students entering at Y7-8 have intuitive grammatical knowledge of the target language from home use, and English grammar knowledge from schooling. They have not previously been asked to compare the two systems analytically, name grammatical features using metalanguage, or examine text-type conventions across languages.

By the end

Students compare and explain similarities and differences between the target language and English language structures and features, using familiar metalanguage across text types and grammatical categories.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • discussing aspects of grammar, using metalanguage in Chinese or English
  • comparing different ways of sequencing ideas for different audiences and purposes
  • reading texts and choosing expressions or sentences that appeal and explaining choices
  • reading samples of formal texts and identifying how language compares with everyday speech
  • comparing the expression of ideas in diverse traditional and contemporary literary texts

Waypoints

  • 1. Metalanguage for grammatical comparisonHingeStudents can use metalanguage to describe grammatical features in both the target language and English, and compare how specific features function across the two languages.
  • 2. Text-type and sequencing comparisonStudents can compare how different text types sequence and develop ideas in the target language and English, identifying structural and language feature differences.
  • 3. Teaching and applying comparative knowledgeStudents can apply comparative language knowledge to access unfamiliar content and explain key language differences to others.
