Characters, sentence structures and syntax

AC9LCH8EU02 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use components and/or characters, sentence structures, syntax and writing system features to infer meaning and to compose and respond to familiar and some unfamiliar texts and contexts

Prior knowledge

Students entering at Y7-8 have variable literacy in the target language writing system — ranging from strong character recognition from home literacy to limited exposure. They have oral syntactic knowledge but have not necessarily analysed sentence structures explicitly, applied stroke order systematically, or used morphological knowledge to infer meaning in unfamiliar texts.

By the end

Students use component and character knowledge, sentence structures and writing system features to infer meaning and compose a range of familiar and some unfamiliar texts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the nature of components, and their arrangement and function in a range of characters
  • inferring characters and meanings in phrases representing auspicious meanings
  • applying rules of stroke direction and stroke order when writing characters
  • using digital tools to write unknown characters and assist with stroke order
  • identifying the possible placement of specific radicals and components in compound characters
  • examining the relationship between characters in a word to identify subtle differences in meaning
  • recognising differences between characters of similar appearance
  • analysing how words containing a common morpheme are related
  • using punctuation conventions to express different meanings
  • joining, contrasting and sequencing using cohesive devices
  • constructing sentences in affirmative, negative, interrogative and imperative forms
  • differentiating characters based on the meanings of each morpheme
  • comparing known simplified characters with traditional characters and discussing differences

Waypoints

  • 1. Character analysis and morphological inferenceHingeStudents can analyse character components and morphemes to infer meaning, identify radicals and their placement, and recognise relationships between characters sharing common components.
  • 2. Writing system conventions and sentence constructionStudents can apply stroke order and direction rules, use punctuation correctly, and construct a range of sentence types — affirmative, negative, interrogative, imperative — using cohesive devices.
  • 3. Composing and responding to unfamiliar textsStudents can apply full character and syntactic knowledge to compose and respond to texts in some unfamiliar contexts, using morphological inference and writing system knowledge strategically.
