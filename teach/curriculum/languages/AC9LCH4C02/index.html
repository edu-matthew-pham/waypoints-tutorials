Plan and transact with others

AC9LCH4C02 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

participate in activities that involve planning and transacting with others, using a range of phrases, structures and modelled sentences in familiar contexts

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges, collaborate in play-based activities, and use digital formats for interaction (AC9LCH4C01, AC9LCH2C02). They can take turns, give instructions and participate in role-play. They have not yet participated in transactional exchanges (buying, ordering, negotiating), planned and presented cultural items, or interacted with guest speakers in structured contexts.

By the end

Students participate in activities that involve planning and transacting with others — including role-plays involving transactions, planning and presenting cultural performances, and interacting with guests — using a range of phrases, structures and modelled sentences.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • planning and presenting a cultural item such as a festive occasion by reciting a poem, singing a song or performing a dance
  • performing plays with allocated roles within groups, using puppets and props
  • participating in group activities and role-plays involving scenarios such as buying food or ordering a meal
  • negotiating details in a transaction, such as stating preferred size, quantity or price
  • participating in traditional or contemporary games involving taking turns, active listening and information exchange
  • following procedures or giving modelled instructions
  • reading their own work to others
  • working in teams to create word or sentence games using digital applications
  • interacting with guest speakers or classroom visitors during special occasions
  • planning and discussing cultural items to present for a school event

Waypoints

  • 1. Transactional role-play languageHingeStudents can participate in transactional role-plays — buying, ordering, negotiating — using appropriate request forms, quantity and price language, and transaction-closing expressions.
  • 2. Planning and presenting cultural itemsStudents can plan and present a cultural item — a performance, cultural display, or community event contribution — collaborating with peers and using appropriate target language.
  • 3. Interacting with guests and in community contextsStudents can interact with guest speakers or classroom visitors using appropriate target language, and adapt their language to the context of the interaction.
