← AC9LS4U01 Sounds, pronunciation and intonation patterns

Intonation across sentence types

AC9LS4U01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 3–4 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can recognise and use different intonation patterns across statement, question, command and exclamation sentence types.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces three distinct sentence types with correct intonation patterns
  • Demonstrates different intonation for at least one statement-question pair

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sound patterns, spelling and consonant conventions.

Width

Minimum
Produce at least three different sentence types with correct intonation; demonstrate intonation variation in spoken production.
Typical
Use correct intonation for statements, questions, commands and exclamations; apply varied pace, stress and rhythm in spoken performance; understand how pitch and stress modify meaning; apply prosodic features in songs, chants and oral activities.
Wider
  • Demonstrate how changing only intonation changes the meaning of the same sentence — produce at least three intonation variants and explain the communicative difference
  • Perform a familiar chant or spoken text with deliberate prosodic control — demonstrating attention to rhythm, stress and pace beyond word-level accuracy

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyIntonation control across sentence types develops through performance across varied communicative contexts. Each context demands different intonation conditions; varied performance builds prosodic range.
