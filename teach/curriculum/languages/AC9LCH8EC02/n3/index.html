Bilingual community-facing production

AC9LCH8EC02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can create bilingual promotional or informational materials for community audiences, and produce resources to help others.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Creates a bilingual resource with accurate meaning transfer in both languages
  • The resource is appropriate for its specified community audience

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Transactional and negotiating language.

Width

Minimum
Create at least one bilingual resource (promotional material, helpful guide, or community information) that accurately conveys meaning in both languages for a specific audience.
Typical
Produce bilingual promotional materials for community events; create resources for Chinese-speaking families to engage with school activities; produce helpful guides for other students; collaborate to create displays and performances for school and community audiences.
Wider
  • Create a bilingual resource for a real community need — evaluate it with a target audience member and revise based on feedback
  • Design an 'ideal school' or community initiative with peers — drawing on research and own cultural experience to compare aspects across Australian and Chinese-speaking contexts

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierBilingual community production integrates collaborative planning (node 1) and transactional language (node 2) into a genuine community-facing task. The teaching investment is in creating real community audiences — actual events, real family and community contexts — that give the production task authentic purpose.
