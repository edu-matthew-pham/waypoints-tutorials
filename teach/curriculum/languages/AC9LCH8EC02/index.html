Plan, transact and negotiate with peers

AC9LCH8EC02 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

engage in activities that involve planning, transacting and negotiating with peers, using a range of phrases, sentences and structures and features

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges using culturally appropriate conventions (AC9LCH8EC01). They can participate in digital and written interaction. They have not yet engaged in structured collaborative planning, transactional exchanges, or bilingual community-facing production in an academic context.

By the end

Students engage in activities involving planning, transacting and negotiating with peers — including cultural events, transactions, collaborative projects, and bilingual community resources.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • planning a Chinese cultural day to support cultural diversity within the school or local community
  • collaborating to create displays, presentations or performances to showcase learning
  • producing bilingual versions of promotional materials to assist the target language community to engage with school activities
  • making real or simulated transactions such as purchasing goods or planning an itinerary
  • negotiating with peers about potential purchases based on collective opinions
  • designing an 'ideal school' in groups using research and own experiences to compare education in Australia and target language regions
  • organising a class or school celebration explaining cultural practices and comparing with practices in other communities
  • providing feedback using metalanguage to support peers
  • creating resources to help others such as helpful hints for school

Waypoints

  • 1. Collaborative planning and cultural event organisationHingeStudents can collaborate with peers to plan cultural events or school activities, making decisions, negotiating roles, and using target language throughout.
  • 2. Transactional and negotiating languageStudents can make real or simulated transactions — purchasing, requesting services, planning itineraries — negotiating details and reaching decisions as a group.
  • 3. Bilingual community-facing productionStudents can create bilingual promotional or informational materials for community audiences, and produce resources to help others.
