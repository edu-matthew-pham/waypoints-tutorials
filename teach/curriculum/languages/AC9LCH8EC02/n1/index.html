← AC9LCH8EC02 Plan, transact and negotiate with peers

Collaborative planning and cultural event organisation

AC9LCH8EC02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Languages · Background speakers, from Year 7: communicating · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can collaborate with peers to plan cultural events or school activities, making decisions, negotiating roles, and using target language throughout.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Contributes at least two planning decisions in target language
  • Justifies at least one decision with a reason

Prior knowledge

Students can initiate and sustain exchanges using culturally appropriate conventions (AC9LCH8EC01). They can participate in digital and written interaction. They have not yet engaged in structured collaborative planning, transactional exchanges, or bilingual community-facing production in an academic context.

Width

Minimum
Contribute to planning at least one event or activity in target language, making at least two planning decisions and justifying at least one.
Typical
Plan Chinese cultural days and community events; organise class or school celebrations — explaining cultural practices and comparing with Australian practices; design group projects using research and personal experience; negotiate event formats, promotion strategies and participant roles.
Wider
  • Lead the planning of a cultural event — coordinating decisions, managing peer input, and driving the group to a shared outcome using target language
  • Compare how community events are organised and promoted in target language community contexts versus Australian school contexts — identify the cultural differences in planning conventions

Hinge

Collaborative planning in target language is the prerequisite for all subsequent community-facing production and negotiation. Students who cannot sustain target language through a planning process cannot participate in the collaborative projects and bilingual resource creation this descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkCollaborative planning requires students to contribute reasoned positions — not only preferences — and to manage competing views toward a shared outcome. Explicit planning task structures with justification requirements build this capacity.
