Tonal features and syllable representation

AC9LCH4U01 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise and use the tonal features of the target language in different contexts and understand how syllables are represented

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise and distinguish tones, represent sounds in written phonetic form, and identify syllables in target language words (AC9LCH2U01). They can recite familiar rhymes and poems with attention to tone and rhythm. They have not yet used tones across different spoken contexts with developing accuracy, explained the romanisation or phonetic system as a learning tool, or investigated dialect diversity in the target language.

By the end

Students recognise and use tonal features of the target language in different contexts with developing accuracy, understand how syllables are represented in the phonetic or romanisation system, and are aware of dialect diversity in the spoken language.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • reading aloud and reciting, focusing on phrasing, stress and intonation
  • engaging in activities to consolidate tonal awareness using hand gestures
  • differentiating the 4 tones when speaking and knowing when and how to use the neutral tone
  • explaining the use of Pinyin as a learning tool to reflect spoken sounds
  • playing pair-matching card games matching Pinyin to syllables
  • investigating diversity in spoken Chinese and identifying differences in phonology across dialects

Waypoints

  • 1. Tonal accuracy in speechHingeStudents can differentiate and produce the tones of the target language with increasing accuracy in speech, including in multi-syllable words and familiar sentences.
  • 2. The phonetic or romanisation system as a learning toolStudents can explain the purpose and use of the target language phonetic or romanisation system, and identify sounds that differ from their English equivalents.
  • 3. Dialect diversity in the spoken languageStudents can identify that the target language has dialect diversity and describe at least one example of phonological difference across dialects.
