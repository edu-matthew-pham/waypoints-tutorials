Translation strategies and dictionary use

AC9LCH4C04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop and apply strategies for translating between the target language and English, including the use of bilingual dictionaries and awareness of translation limitations.

Success criteria

  • Uses a bilingual dictionary to locate at least three words, selecting context-appropriate meanings
  • Produces a translation that accounts for context — not only the first dictionary entry

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Untranslatable expressions and cultural symbols.

Width

Minimum
Use a bilingual dictionary to locate at least three words and produce a translation that accounts for context — not just the first entry.
Typical
Use print and digital bilingual dictionaries to assist with translation; recognise that words can have multiple meanings depending on context; create bilingual resources — glossaries, picture dictionaries, signs — for classroom use; develop awareness of when and how to use translation tools.
Wider
  • Translate a short text from the target language to English and explain at least two translation decisions — where a direct translation would be misleading and what approach was taken instead
  • Compare the output of a digital translation tool with a human translation of the same text — identify differences and discuss what the human translator understood that the digital tool missed

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierTranslation strategies integrate register awareness (node 1) and cultural expression knowledge (node 2) into a practical mediation task. The teaching investment is in making explicit that translation is a cultural act — not a word-for-word substitution — through tasks that require genuine translation decisions.
