Interpret and adjust language for cultural contexts

AC9LS8C04 · Years 7–8 Languages · Second language, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret and adjust non-verbal, spoken and written language to convey meaning in the target language in familiar and some unfamiliar cultural contexts

Prior knowledge

Students can apply strategies to interpret and convey meaning — including managing non-equivalent expressions and creating bilingual resources (AC9LS6C04). They have not yet compared their own translations with others, interpreted context-dependent vocabulary, or created cultural responses for community audiences.

By the end

Students interpret and adjust language to convey meaning in familiar and some unfamiliar cultural contexts — including translation comparison, context-dependent meaning, and cultural response production.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing own translation of a text with classmates, commenting on similarities and differences and possible reasons
  • creating informative responses to cultural representations of target language communities in Australia, explaining language or images in terms of cultural associations
  • interpreting short messages or communications, reflecting on challenges of transferring meaning between languages
  • viewing authentic material of target language speakers discussing their culture and language, then responding with personal perspectives
  • selecting and explaining individual words or phrases whose meaning can be affected by context
  • presenting information gathered from different types of texts and modes to create an overview of a topic of interest

Waypoints

  • 1. Context-dependent meaning and translation comparisonHingeStudents can interpret words and expressions whose meaning varies by context, and compare their translations with others' — reflecting on the choices made.
  • 2. Creating cultural responses and explanationsStudents can create informative responses to cultural representations — reviews, vlogs, explainers — that convey cultural meaning alongside content description.
