Evaluating translation tools and resources

AC9LCH6C04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use and evaluate a range of print and digital translation tools, understanding their strengths and limitations for different translation tasks.

Success criteria

  • Compares at least two translation approaches for the same text and identifies at least two differences
  • Can explain when a digital translation tool is or is not appropriate for a given task

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Managing idiom, colloquial language and literal translation risk.

Width

Minimum
Compare the output of at least two translation approaches (e.g. digital tool and human translation, or two different digital tools) for the same text and identify at least two differences.
Typical
Use a range of translation resources — print and digital bilingual dictionaries, digital translation tools — and evaluate their accuracy and limitations; discuss the differences between machine and human translation; create bilingual resources for classroom or community use.
Wider
  • Conduct a systematic comparison of digital translation tool output with a human translation — document specific errors or inadequacies, categorise them (idiom, register, cultural reference), and discuss why the tool failed
  • Create a guide for using digital translation tools appropriately — including when to use them, when not to, and how to evaluate the output

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepTranslation tool evaluation is context-dependent — tools that work adequately for some text types fail for others. Evaluating tools across different text types and translation tasks builds nuanced understanding of when and how to use them appropriately.
