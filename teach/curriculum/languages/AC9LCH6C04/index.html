Apply strategies to interpret and translate

AC9LCH6C04 · Years 5–6 Languages · Background speakers, F–10: communicating

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

apply strategies to interpret and translate language in non-verbal, spoken and written cultural contexts

Prior knowledge

Students have developed strategies to interpret culturally-loaded language and can use bilingual dictionaries for translation (AC9LCH4C04). They understand register, untranslatable expressions, and cultural symbols. They have not yet applied translation strategies to extended texts, evaluated translation tools critically, or produced bilingual resources for community purposes.

By the end

Students apply strategies to interpret and translate language in non-verbal, spoken and written cultural contexts — including managing idiom and colloquial language, evaluating translation tools, and producing bilingual resources for real purposes.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • examining the risks of literal or word-for-word translation when translating expressions, idioms and proverbs
  • recognising different levels of formality in language and using language appropriate to context
  • interpreting examples of colloquialisms or slang typically used by young people
  • identifying context-specific language used in different settings such as supermarket, doctor or visiting a friend
  • creating and using bilingual resources for language learning, such as glossaries of cultural terms
  • reading children's stories in bilingual form and reflecting on ideas that may not be readily translated
  • using a range of resources to assist in creating a target language translation of a text
  • composing bilingual resources for the school community such as posters for class or school performances

Waypoints

  • 1. Managing idiom, colloquial language and literal translation riskHingeStudents can identify the risks of literal translation for idiomatic and culturally-embedded language, and apply strategies to interpret such expressions accurately.
  • 2. Evaluating translation tools and resourcesStudents can use and evaluate a range of print and digital translation tools, understanding their strengths and limitations for different translation tasks.
  • 3. Producing bilingual resources for real purposesStudents can produce bilingual resources — community posters, event information, cultural guides — that accurately convey meaning across target language and English for a specific purpose and audience.
