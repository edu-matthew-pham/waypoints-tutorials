Evaluate identity and communication

AC9LS10EU04 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

reflect on and explain how identity is shaped by language(s), culture(s), attitudes, beliefs and values, and how these affect ways of communicating

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise how identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values (AC9LS8EU04). They have not yet reflected on and explained these relationships analytically — moving from recognition of examples to explanation of mechanisms, and considering how identity affects ways of communicating.

By the end

Students reflect on and explain how identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values — in target language communities and in their own experience — and analyse how identity affects ways of communicating, including in multilingual and cross-cultural contexts.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • recognising that the target language continues to evolve due to exposure to other cultures, globalisation, trends, technology and social change
  • recognising that many target language speaking countries have indigenous languages that are co-official languages
  • recognising that the target language co-exists with multiple indigenous languages and dialects in many communities
  • reflecting on and explaining the protocols required to authentically co-create an Acknowledgement of Country/Place with a First Nations Australian community
  • exploring the influence of popular culture in the target language speaking world
  • considering how regional and national perspectives influence the use of language and sense of identity relating to place/country
  • interviewing bilingual peers, family members or guests about the experience of using more than one language
  • discussing ways in which learning a different language can lead to new ways of thinking or interpreting experiences
  • analysing texts that show different representations of cultural identity and sense of belonging
  • discussing and reflecting on the nature and role of culture and its expression through language

Waypoints

  • 1. Language as a carrier of cultural identityStudents can explain how specific features of the target language carry and reinforce cultural values, beliefs and identity — moving beyond recognition to explanation.
  • 2. Identity and ways of communicatingStudents can explain how identity — including cultural background, social role and language repertoire — shapes the ways people communicate in the target language.
  • 3. Reflecting on own identity as a language learnerStudents can reflect on and explain how learning the target language has affected their own sense of identity, perspectives and ways of communicating.
