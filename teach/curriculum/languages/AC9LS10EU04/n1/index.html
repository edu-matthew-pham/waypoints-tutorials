Language as a carrier of cultural identity

AC9LS10EU04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how specific features of the target language carry and reinforce cultural values, beliefs and identity — moving beyond recognition to explanation.

Success criteria

  • Explains at least two connections between specific target language features and cultural values or identity
  • Explanation identifies a mechanism — not only an example — for how language carries or reinforces identity

Prior knowledge

Students can recognise how identity is shaped by language, culture, attitudes, beliefs and values (AC9LS8EU04). They have not yet reflected on and explained these relationships analytically — moving from recognition of examples to explanation of mechanisms, and considering how identity affects ways of communicating.

Width

Minimum
Explain at least two ways in which specific target language features (vocabulary, forms of address, cultural references) carry or reinforce cultural identity.
Typical
Examine a range of target language features and explain how each reflects underlying cultural values, beliefs or identity; investigate how the target language co-exists with other languages in multilingual communities; discuss how language encodes connection to place, community and history.
Wider
  • Investigate how indigenous languages in target language communities co-exist with the target language — research the history and current status of at least one co-official or community language
  • Research how a target language community's engagement with globalisation, migration or political change has affected language use and identity — identify specific language changes and their causes

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkExplaining (rather than recognising) the relationship between language and identity requires students to articulate a mechanism — 'this feature exists because this cultural value operates here'. Discussion scaffolds and case studies support the move from observation to explanation.
