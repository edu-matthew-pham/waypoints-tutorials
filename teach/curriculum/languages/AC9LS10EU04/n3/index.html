Reflecting on own identity as a language learner

AC9LS10EU04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Languages · Second language, from Year 7: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can reflect on and explain how learning the target language has affected their own sense of identity, perspectives and ways of communicating.

Success criteria

  • Produces a reflection that explains (not only describes) at least one way language learning has affected their perspectives or sense of identity
  • Reflection connects personal experience to broader insight about language, culture and identity — not only reports events

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identity and ways of communicating.

Width

Minimum
Produce one reflection in the target language that explains at least one way in which language learning has introduced a new perspective or affected their sense of identity.
Typical
Reflect on the experience of operating across languages and cultures; explain how learning the target language has created new ways of thinking, new cultural empathy or new awareness of own cultural assumptions; consider the role of the target language in potential future life experiences.
Wider
  • Produce a personal text (essay, spoken reflection, digital presentation) that traces the development of own identity and perspectives across the language learning journey
  • Compare own experience of language learning with that of a bilingual or multilingual person — discuss what is shared and what is unique to each experience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierPersonal identity reflection integrates the cultural insights from nodes 1–2 with the student's own experience as a language learner. This is the most personally meaningful dimension of EU04 — the teaching investment is in creating genuine reflective space, not scripted self-description tasks.
