Sounds and rhythms of the target language

AC9LS2U01 · Years 1–2 Languages · Second language, F–10: understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Languages: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

recognise and imitate the sounds and rhythms of the target language

Prior knowledge

Students have noticed target language sounds and imitated some with support (AC9LSF02). They have no prior systematic experience with target language phonology, vowel patterns or the full sound inventory.

By the end

Students recognise and imitate the sounds and rhythms of the target language — including vowel patterns, consonant features, intonation and sounds not present in English.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • becoming familiar with the sounds of the alphabet in the target language, noticing any letters not found in English
  • imitating target language sounds that do not exist in English
  • recognising that some sounds in the target language do not exist in English, so they may be challenging to pronounce
  • recognising and applying target language vowel sounds and vowel-consonant combinations
  • identifying additional consonants and digraphs in the target language and experimenting with tongue positioning
  • listening to familiar vocabulary and identifying silent letters
  • noticing that statements and questions have different intonation
  • developing pronunciation, phrasing and intonation skills by singing, reciting and repeating words in context
  • experimenting with sounds in onomatopoeic words related to animal sounds and actions

Waypoints

  • 1. Target language sounds and alphabet featuresHingeStudents can recognise and imitate distinctive sounds of the target language, including sounds not present in English, and apply correct sound patterns to familiar vocabulary.
  • 2. Intonation, rhythm and statement/question distinctionStudents can recognise and imitate target language intonation patterns — including the distinction between statements and questions — and produce spoken language with appropriate rhythm and pace.
