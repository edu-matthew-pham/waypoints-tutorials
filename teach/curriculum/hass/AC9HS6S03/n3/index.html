Evaluating past attitudes and proposing reasons for change

AC9HS6S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate attitudes and actions of the past that seem strange or unacceptable by contemporary standards, and propose reasons why values and social norms change over time.

Success criteria

  • Students identify at least one past attitude or practice that is no longer considered acceptable.
  • Students explain why it was accepted at the time and why values have changed.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identifying patterns using graphic organisers and maps.

Width

Minimum
Identify one past attitude or practice that is no longer acceptable and propose one reason why values changed.
Typical
Evaluate attitudes and actions of the past that now seem unacceptable (e.g. deportation of South Sea Islanders, denial of voting rights, institutional child care practices), propose reasons why these were accepted at the time and why values changed; reflect on what aspects of current society might be viewed similarly in the future.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific historical shift in social values (e.g. attitudes toward immigration, gender equality) and trace the events, movements and arguments that drove the change
  • Reflect on a current issue and propose which aspects of contemporary society might be judged negatively by future generations — and what this suggests about the nature of social progress

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating past attitudes requires students to hold two temporal frames simultaneously (past context and present standards) — scaffold explicitly by asking students to explain both what was accepted then and why it is not now.
