← AC9HS6S03 Identify patterns and relationships

Identifying patterns using graphic organisers and maps

AC9HS6S03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use graphic organisers, maps and concept maps to identify patterns, trends and cause-effect relationships in HASS data.

Success criteria

  • Students use a graphic organiser or map to identify a pattern, trend or relationship.
  • Students describe what the pattern, trend or relationship shows about the HASS topic.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Causes, effects and significance in historical data.

Width

Minimum
Use one graphic organiser or map to identify a pattern, trend or relationship in HASS data.
Typical
Use graphic organisers (maps, concept maps, tables, charts) to identify patterns — such as settlement in regional agricultural areas — trends (e.g. changes in Australian immigration statistics) and cause-effect relationships (e.g. war and refugee movement, consumer decisions and environmental sustainability).
Wider
  • Create a concept map that traces the system of causes and effects for a complex Year 6 topic (e.g. how consumer decisions affect environment and community), identifying second and third-order effects
  • Analyse Australian immigration statistics across multiple decades using a graph, identifying trends and proposing explanations for each change observed

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents benefit from using multiple graphic tools across different topics — each tool reveals different features of the data. Build a repertoire of analytical tools rather than deepening expertise in one.
