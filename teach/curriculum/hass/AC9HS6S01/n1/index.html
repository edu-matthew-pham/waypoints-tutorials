← AC9HS6S01 Develop questions about systems

Ethical and systemic question types

AC9HS6S01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop ethical questions about cultural protocol and sensitivity, and system-mapping questions that reveal connections between economic, political and environmental systems.

Success criteria

  • Students develop at least one ethical question relevant to a HASS inquiry.
  • Students develop at least one question that reveals a connection between two systems or disciplines.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS5S01 (Develop system investigation questions): Students develop purposeful inquiry questions using probing, open-ended and practical question types. Students can frame inquiry questions across HASS stages.

Width

Minimum
Develop one ethical question and one question that reveals a connection between two HASS systems.
Typical
Develop a range of question types for Year 6 contexts: probing questions, open-ended questions, practical questions for enterprising behaviours, and ethical questions about cultural sensitivities and protocol. Develop questions that reveal connections across systems (e.g. 'How do the purchases my family makes influence the environment?').
Wider
  • Mind-map a complex HASS concept (e.g. consumer decisions and environmental impact) and generate questions that reveal all significant system connections
  • Develop an inquiry question set that requires investigation across at least two HASS disciplines and explain how each question targets a different disciplinary lens

Hinge

Ethical questions and system-level questions are the Year 6 extensions beyond Year 5 practice — students who cannot form these question types will conduct surface-level inquiries that miss the systemic connections that Year 6 content demands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkModel ethical and system-level questions explicitly, showing the difference between 'what happened?' and 'who is affected and why?' — students need to see the form of these questions before generating them independently.
