Multiple connections to Country/Place

AC9HS3K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 3 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that First Nations Australians can have connections to multiple Countries/Places and describe how these connections are formed.

Success criteria

  • Explains that a person can have connections to more than one Country/Place
  • Names at least two ways connections to Country/Place are formed (e.g. birth, marriage, residence)

Prior knowledge

From Y2 (AC9HS2K04): Students explained interconnections of First Nations Australians to a local Country/Place and the relationship between language, Country/Place and spirituality.

Width

Minimum
Explain that First Nations Australians can have connections to more than one Country/Place and give at least one reason why.
Typical
Explain that some First Nations Australians have connections to many Countries/Places through marriage, birth, parents, residence and chosen or forced movement; discuss how some people are connected to one Country because it is Mother's or Father's Country.
Wider
  • Investigate how forced removal and the Stolen Generations disrupted Country/Place connections and what efforts are made to restore them
  • Explore how First Nations Australian people maintain connections to Country/Place when living away from it

Hinge

Understanding multiple connection types is prerequisite to appreciating the diversity of interconnections across Australia — the next node requires this conceptual flexibility.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe concept of belonging to multiple Countries/Places challenges conventional notions of 'one home' — scaffold carefully using concrete examples from stories and community members.
