Spatial representation and scale

AC9HS2K03 · Year 2 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how places can be spatially represented in geographical divisions from local to regional to state/territory, and how people and places are interconnected across those scales

Prior knowledge

From Y1 (AC9HS1K03): Students located natural, managed and constructed features on local maps using direction and location terms.

By the end

Students explain how places can be spatially represented from local to state/territory scale and how people and places are interconnected across those scales.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying where they live in Australia, including in relation to their nearest capital city, the region and state/territory on a map and a globe
  • investigating the places locally and at a broader scale that they and their families visit for shopping, health, recreation, religious or ceremonial activities, or other reasons
  • identifying links they and other people in their community have with people and places at the regional and/or state/territory scale; for example, where produce in their supermarket comes from or produce from their farms goes to, relatives they visit, places they go for holidays
  • describing how communication and transport technologies connect their place to other places at the regional and/or state/territory level; for example, online communication, phone, road, rail, planes, ferries

Waypoints

  • 1. Geographical divisions and scalesHingeStudents can identify the geographical divisions from local to regional to state/territory and locate where they live within this hierarchy using maps and a globe.
  • 2. Interconnections across scalesStudents can identify how they and their community are connected to people and places at regional and state/territory scale, including through movement, shopping, communication and transport.
