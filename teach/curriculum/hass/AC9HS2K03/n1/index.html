Geographical divisions and scales

AC9HS2K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify the geographical divisions from local to regional to state/territory and locate where they live within this hierarchy using maps and a globe.

Success criteria

  • Locates home in relation to nearest capital city and state/territory on a map
  • Names the state/territory they live in and at least one neighbouring state

Prior knowledge

From Y1 (AC9HS1K03): Students located natural, managed and constructed features on local maps using direction and location terms.

Width

Minimum
Locate their home in relation to their nearest capital city and state/territory on a map.
Typical
Identify where they live in Australia in relation to nearest capital city, region and state/territory on maps and a globe; use geographical tools to explore scale from local to national.
Wider
  • Locate Australia's position relative to neighbouring regions and countries on a globe
  • Compare the size and population of different states and territories and discuss why they differ

Hinge

Understanding the local-to-state/territory scale hierarchy is prerequisite to understanding interconnections across scales — you cannot explain a connection between scales you cannot name or locate.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsScale is a new concept at Year 2 — repeated practice with maps, globes and digital tools at different scales builds the spatial vocabulary needed for interconnection discussions.
