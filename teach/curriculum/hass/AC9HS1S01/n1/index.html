Questions about the past and present

AC9HS1S01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can generate questions about objects, people, places and events in both the past and present, using 'where', 'what', 'how' and 'why' stems.

Success criteria

  • Generates at least one question about the past and one about the present
  • Uses at least two different question stems (Where, What, How, Why)

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFS01): Students posed questions about familiar objects, people, places and events using basic stems (What, Where, Who, Why, How).

Width

Minimum
Generate at least two questions about a provided source — one about the present and one about the past — using at least two different question stems.
Typical
Pose questions using Where, What, How and Why stems about families and places when provided with everyday objects, photos, maps and observation sketches; ask questions before, during and after listening to stories about people and places.
Wider
  • Prepare a set of questions for parents or grandparents about how and where they lived in the past and the places they value
  • Sort questions about the past and present and discuss what type of source would best answer each question

Hinge

Year 1 inquiry requires students to direct questions to the past as well as the present — students who only ask about now cannot drive the historical comparison tasks that HASS Year 1 requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPast-directed questions require students to imagine a time they cannot directly observe — model the cognitive move explicitly: 'To find out about the past, we ask questions like...'
