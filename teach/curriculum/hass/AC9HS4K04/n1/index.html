Contact before 1788

AC9HS4K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 4 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe contact between First Nations Australians and people from Asia and Europe before 1788, including trade and conflict.

Success criteria

  • Describes at least one example of pre-1788 contact between First Nations Australians and non-British visitors
  • Identifies the nature of that contact (e.g. trade, conflict, exchange)

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K01-K03: Students have explored First Nations Australian diversity and First Fleet establishment. They now examine contact from First Nations Australian perspectives.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one example of contact between First Nations Australians and non-British visitors before 1788.
Typical
Investigate contact between First Nations Australians and people from different continents (Asia, Europe) before 1788; examine examples of trade, sharing of ideas and technologies; explore the repulsion of the Dutch at Cape Keerweer (1606).
Wider
  • Compare First Nations Australian interactions with Macassans and with early European visitors, noting differences in the nature of contact
  • Investigate rock paintings depicting contact with Macassans and discuss what these reveal from a First Nations perspective

Hinge

Establishing that First Nations Australians had prior agency in contact situations provides essential context for understanding the qualitative shift that British colonisation represented.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkPre-1788 agency must be established explicitly or students will naturalise colonial disruption as inevitable — this counter-narrative framing requires conscious scaffolding.
