Comparing accounts and perspectives

AC9HS5S04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare primary and secondary sources on the same topic to identify similarities and differences in their accounts, explaining why different sources present different perspectives.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Students compare at least two sources on the same topic and identify at least one similarity and one difference.
  • Students explain why the sources might present different accounts.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identifying bias and stereotypes.

Width

Minimum
Compare two sources on the same topic and identify at least one similarity and one difference in their accounts.
Typical
Compare sources of evidence (primary and secondary, from different creators and perspectives) to identify similarities and differences in accounts of the past or present; explain why sources differ and what each perspective reveals; give examples from historical events (e.g. Eureka Stockade, colonial exploration, gold rush).
Wider
  • Compare how an event is described in a source created at the time with how it is described in a recently published account, and evaluate what has changed in the interpretation and why
  • Examine accounts of the same event from First Nations Australian and European perspectives, and evaluate what each perspective reveals that the other omits

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepComparing perspectives across multiple source types and historical actors is the core skill — students benefit from practising comparisons across different kinds of differences (time period, cultural background, social position, source type).
