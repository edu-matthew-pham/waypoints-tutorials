Evaluating civic participation

AC9HS5K07 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate the importance of civic participation in a democratic society, explaining how citizens working together for civic goals contributes to community wellbeing and democratic health.

Success criteria

  • Students explain why civic participation matters for community wellbeing and democracy.
  • Students give at least one example of citizens working together to achieve a civic goal.
  • Students use relevant civic terms accurately (e.g. 'civic', 'volunteer', 'community organisation', 'shared values').

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Why citizens participate in civic life.

Width

Minimum
Explain why civic participation is important in a democracy, using at least one example.
Typical
Evaluate the importance of civic participation for community wellbeing and democracy, explaining how collective action around shared values achieves goals that individuals or governments acting alone cannot, and give examples from Australian and global contexts.
Wider
  • Investigate how civic participation has changed over time in Australia (e.g. changes in volunteering rates, new forms of civic action) and evaluate what this means for the health of Australian democracy
  • Assess whether digital forms of civic participation (e.g. online petitions, social media campaigns) are as effective as traditional forms, and what each contributes differently to civic goals

Response modes
