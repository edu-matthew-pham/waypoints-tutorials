What is a civic goal?

AC9HS5K07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the concept of a civic goal — a shared aim that benefits a community or society — and distinguish civic goals from personal or commercial goals.

Success criteria

  • Students explain the meaning of 'civic goal' and distinguish it from personal or commercial goals.
  • Students give at least one example of a community pursuing a civic goal.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K09 (Community identity and diversity): Students explain how communities are diverse and how people within communities have shared identities and belonging. Students understand that community groups serve different purposes.

Width

Minimum
Define 'civic goal' and give one example of a community pursuing a civic goal.
Typical
Explain what a civic goal is — an aim that benefits the broader community or society — and give examples of civic goals pursued by different types of community groups, distinguishing these from personal goals (benefiting an individual) or commercial goals (benefiting a business).
Wider
  • Investigate the relationship between civic goals and democratic values — how do shared values motivate citizens to work together for community benefit?
  • Explore examples of civic goals at different scales: local (school/neighbourhood), regional, national and global, and compare the challenges of pursuing civic goals at each scale

Hinge

Students who cannot distinguish civic goals from personal or commercial goals will describe community groups without understanding what makes their aims distinctively civic. This distinction is the conceptual foundation for understanding citizenship and collective action.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a three-column sorting framework (personal / commercial / civic) with real examples to make the distinction concrete before moving to analysis of how civic goals are pursued.
