← AC9HS5K07 Citizens working for civic goals

Why citizens participate in civic life

AC9HS5K07 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why citizens choose to participate in civic life and community groups, including the connection between shared beliefs and values and the motivation to act for community benefit.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least two reasons citizens participate in civic organisations.
  • Students connect at least one motivating reason to a shared value or belief.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: How citizens organise and work together.

Width

Minimum
Explain two reasons why citizens might volunteer or participate in a civic organisation.
Typical
Explain the motivations for civic participation: shared values and beliefs, concern for community wellbeing, cultural and religious obligation, desire to make a difference, and the social benefits of belonging to a community organisation.
Wider
  • Survey members of a local community organisation to investigate their motivations for participation, and compare their responses with the elaboration examples
  • Explore how civic participation is connected to broader democratic values — making the link between individual participation and the health of democracy explicit

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe link between values and motivation is an inferential move students may not make automatically — use examples that make the value-to-action pathway explicit (e.g. 'We believe in protecting life, so we volunteer with the SES').
