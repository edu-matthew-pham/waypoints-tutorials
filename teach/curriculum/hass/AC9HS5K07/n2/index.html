← AC9HS5K07 Citizens working for civic goals

How citizens organise and work together

AC9HS5K07 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how citizens with shared beliefs and values organise themselves into groups and work collectively to pursue civic goals, including through volunteering and community organisations.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Students describe how at least one community group is organised around shared values and goals.
  • Students explain at least two ways members of the group work together to pursue their civic goal.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What is a civic goal?.

Width

Minimum
Describe how one community group organises itself and works toward a civic goal.
Typical
Explain how different types of community groups (religious and other charities, rural fire services, surf life-saving clubs, emergency services, First Nations community groups, youth groups) organise themselves around shared values and work collectively to achieve civic goals.
Wider
  • Investigate a First Nations Australian community organisation and explain the services it provides, the values it is built around, and how it supports community goals
  • Examine how digital platforms and social media are used by citizens to organise around shared civic goals at local, national and global levels

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe range of community group types in the elaborations is important — students benefit from seeing how civic organisation occurs across very different contexts, from emergency services to cultural organisations to digital advocacy.
