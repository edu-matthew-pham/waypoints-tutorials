← AC9HS5S05 Develop evidence-based conclusions

Identifying valid evidence

AC9HS5S05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify valid evidence from primary and secondary sources that can be used to form a conclusion, explaining why some evidence is more valid than other evidence for a specific inquiry purpose.

Success criteria

  • Students select at least two pieces of valid evidence from sources for an inquiry question.
  • Students explain why each piece of evidence is relevant and reliable.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What makes a conclusion evidence-based?.

Width

Minimum
Select two pieces of valid evidence from provided sources and explain why each is relevant to the inquiry question.
Typical
Identify valid evidence from primary and secondary sources (e.g. images, posters, newspaper articles, books, websites) for a HASS inquiry; explain why some sources are more valid than others; consider the reliability, recency and relevance of each source as evidence.
Wider
  • Compare the validity of evidence from a government agency website versus a private blog on the same topic — evaluate the criteria for judging reliability in digital sources
  • Investigate how the date a secondary source was created affects its validity — exploring how a history textbook written in 1960 may reflect different values from one written in 2020

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.
