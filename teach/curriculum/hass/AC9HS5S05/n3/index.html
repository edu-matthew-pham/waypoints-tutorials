← AC9HS5S05 Develop evidence-based conclusions

Forming conclusions from evidence

AC9HS5S05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw conclusions from identified evidence that consider differing information, reflect disciplinary concepts, and address their inquiry question.

Success criteria

  • Students write at least one conclusion that addresses an inquiry question.
  • Students cite at least two sources as evidence for their conclusion.
  • Students distinguish their conclusion from personal opinion.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Identifying valid evidence.

Width

Minimum
Write one conclusion grounded in identified evidence that addresses an inquiry question.
Typical
Draw conclusions from primary and secondary sources that: address the inquiry question, reflect understanding of disciplinary concepts (historical, geographic, civic, economic), consider evidence from different sources including those with differing viewpoints, and are clearly distinguished from personal preference.
Wider
  • Draw conclusions about a community or environment (e.g. changing democratic values, patterns of human consumption, environmental change) using evidence from multiple source types
  • Construct conclusions that explicitly acknowledge uncertainty or conflicting evidence, and explain how this affects confidence in the conclusion

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsWriting evidence-based conclusions is a precision skill — students need practice forming conclusions that make the evidence-to-claim link explicit. Errors (missing citation, over-claiming, under-claiming) need targeted feedback loops.
