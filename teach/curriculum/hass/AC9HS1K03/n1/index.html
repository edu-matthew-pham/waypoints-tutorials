← AC9HS1K03 Features of local places

Natural, managed and constructed features

AC9HS1K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can classify features of local places as natural (hills, rivers, vegetation, weather), managed (farms, parks, gardens) or constructed (roads, buildings), and give examples of each.

Success criteria

  • Classifies at least two features as natural, two as managed and two as constructed
  • Gives a reason for each classification

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFK03): Students identified features of familiar places and why some places are special. They used basic place vocabulary (near, far, natural, built).

Width

Minimum
Classify at least six features across all three categories (natural, managed, constructed) with at least one example in each.
Typical
Identify and classify a full range of natural, managed and constructed features in the local area; locate them on a provided map; explore Dreaming and Creation stories that identify natural features of place.
Wider
  • Listen to and view First Nations Australian Dreaming and Creation stories that identify and explain the origin of natural features, discussing what these reveal about connections between people and place
  • Classify features of a contrasting place (e.g. a beach, a farm, a mountain) and compare with the local area

Hinge

The three-category classification is the analytical framework for this standard — without it, students cannot locate or discuss features coherently in subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe natural/managed/constructed distinction is conceptually new at Year 1. Managed features are often confused with constructed ones — use sorting activities and decision trees to make the categories explicit.
