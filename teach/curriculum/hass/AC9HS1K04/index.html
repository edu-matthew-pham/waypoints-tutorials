Changing and caring for places

AC9HS1K04 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

how places change and how they can be cared for by different groups including First Nations Australians

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFK03): Students identified features of places and how places can be looked after. From AC9HS1K03: Students classified local place features as natural, managed or constructed.

By the end

Students explain how places change and how they can be cared for by different groups including First Nations Australians.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • observing changes in natural, managed and constructed features in their place; for example, recent erosion, revegetated areas, planted crops or new buildings
  • identifying which resources they can recycle, reduce, re-use or none of these, and what local spaces and systems support these activities; for example, rules, signs, waste collection truck routes
  • describing how local places change due to changing weather and seasons, and how we can care for places because of those changes; for example, not walking in muddy areas during wet weather, and watering plants in dry weather
  • describing local features that people look after, finding out why and how these features need to be cared for, and who provides this care; for example, bushland, wetlands, a park or a heritage building
  • investigating examples of how First Nations Australians manage and care for places

Waypoints

  • 1. How places changeHingeStudents can identify and describe examples of how local places change, including changes caused by weather, seasons, erosion and human activity.
  • 2. Caring for places — why and howStudents can describe how and why local places are cared for, and identify who is responsible for different types of care.
  • 3. Reduce, reuse, recycle — local systemsStudents can identify what local resources can be recycled, reduced or reused, and describe the local systems (rules, signs, collection) that support these activities.
