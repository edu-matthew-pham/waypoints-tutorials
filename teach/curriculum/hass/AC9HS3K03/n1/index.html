States, territories and capitals

AC9HS3K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 3 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name and locate Australian states and territories and their capital cities on a map.

Success criteria

  • Names at least four states or territories correctly
  • Locates at least four on a map

Prior knowledge

From Y2 (AC9HS2K03): Students located their home within geographical divisions from local to state/territory and explored interconnections across scales.

Width

Minimum
Name at least four states or territories and locate them on a map.
Typical
Use geographical tools (globe, wall map, digital source) to locate and name all states and territories and their capital cities; label them on a map.
Wider
  • Research differences between states and territories in terms of population, size and climate
  • Locate Australian states and territories on a globe and discuss Australia's position relative to the equator and hemispheres

Hinge

Locating states and territories is prerequisite to comparing them with Countries/Places of First Nations Australians — you cannot compare boundaries you cannot locate.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsAccurate map knowledge requires repeated practice — use map quizzes, labelling and digital tools to build locational fluency.
