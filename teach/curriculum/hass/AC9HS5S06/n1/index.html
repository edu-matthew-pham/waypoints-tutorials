Identifying issues and proposed actions

AC9HS5S06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills · Hinge

Checkpoint

Students can identify a HASS issue or challenge, describe why it matters, and propose at least one possible action or response.

Success criteria

  • Students identify a specific HASS issue or challenge and explain why it matters.
  • Students propose at least one possible action or response to the issue.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4S06 (Propose responses with effects): Students propose responses to issues and identify possible effects. Students can suggest actions but do not yet apply formal criteria to assess effects.

Width

Minimum
Identify one HASS issue or challenge and propose one possible action in response to it.
Typical
Identify a current or historical HASS issue or challenge (environmental, civic, economic, historical), describe its significance, and propose a range of possible actions or responses, including actions taken in the past and possible future actions.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific contemporary challenge (e.g. environmental hazard, community need, democratic issue) using a problem-finding process that examines causes before proposing actions
  • Compare how different groups have proposed different responses to the same challenge, explaining why their proposed actions differ

Hinge

Students who propose actions without clearly identifying the issue first tend to address symptoms rather than causes — their effects assessment will be shallow because they have not established what problem the action is meant to solve. Issue identification is the prerequisite for meaningful effect assessment.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a problem-framing scaffold (What is the issue? Who is affected? What causes it? What would a good response address?) before students propose actions — this prevents the common error of jumping to solutions before understanding the problem.
