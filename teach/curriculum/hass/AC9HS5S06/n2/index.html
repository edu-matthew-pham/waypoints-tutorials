Using criteria to assess effects

AC9HS5S06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and apply criteria to assess the possible effects of proposed actions, explaining what each criterion measures and why it matters.

Success criteria

  • Students identify at least two criteria for assessing the effects of an action.
  • Students apply each criterion to a proposed action and describe the likely effect.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying issues and proposed actions.

Width

Minimum
Name two criteria for assessing the effects of an action and apply each to one proposed action.
Typical
Identify a set of criteria (e.g. economic factors: needs, wants and costs; environmental factors; health factors; social factors) and apply them to assess the possible effects of proposed actions or responses to a HASS challenge.
Wider
  • Investigate how criteria are used in real decision-making contexts (e.g. council planning decisions, government environmental policies) and evaluate whether the criteria used reflect the right values
  • Develop and justify a custom set of criteria for a specific HASS challenge, explaining why these criteria are more appropriate than a generic set

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkApplying criteria requires students to hold the action, the criterion, and the likely effect in mind simultaneously — use a structured evaluation table (action × criterion = likely effect) to make this thinking visible.
