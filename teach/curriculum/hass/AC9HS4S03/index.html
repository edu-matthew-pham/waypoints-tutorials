Interpret information in different formats

AC9HS4S03 · Year 4 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

interpret information and data displayed in different formats

Prior knowledge

From Y3 (AC9HS3S03): Students distinguished fiction from non-fiction and interpreted climate data, graphs and maps.

By the end

Students interpret information and data from symbols, graphs, maps, satellite images and other display formats.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • decoding the meaning of symbols and emblems associated with Australian history, geography and civic life and applying an understanding of conventions, vocabulary and symbols when interpreting large-scale maps
  • interpreting the data presented in picture, line, bar or column graphs to identify trends; for example, explaining survey results about types of waste produced in the school or how people participate in the community
  • interpreting thematic maps and using online satellite images to describe the environmental characteristics of a continent or region, or to identify a particular characteristic, such as equatorial rainforests or clearance of natural vegetation for farming and settlement
  • comparing environments in places of similar climate and vegetation that are located on different continents; for example, sandy, icy and stony deserts of Australia, Africa and South America

Waypoints

  • 1. Decoding symbols and map conventionsStudents can decode the meaning of symbols and emblems associated with Australian history, geography and civic life and apply understanding of conventions when interpreting large-scale maps.
  • 2. Interpreting graphs and comparing environmentsStudents can interpret data in graphs to identify trends and compare environments across continents.
