Features of familiar places

AC9HSFK03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Foundation HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify and describe features of familiar places using observation and simple place vocabulary.

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three features of a familiar place
  • Uses at least two place vocabulary terms (e.g. natural, built, near, far)

Prior knowledge

Students have lived experience of places they inhabit. No formal prior HASS curriculum.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three features of one familiar place (e.g. the school) using provided vocabulary (natural, built, people, activities).
Typical
Explore and describe features of multiple familiar places (home, school, neighbourhood, local park); use senses-based observation to identify natural and constructed features.
Wider
  • Compare features of familiar places with places students have visited or seen in stories or on television
  • Identify features of places in First Nations Australian stories and Dreaming stories, discussing what is special about those places

Hinge

Without feature vocabulary for places, students cannot explain what makes a place special or how to care for it — nodes 2 and 3 both require this observational foundation.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyBuild a working vocabulary of place features through observation walks, photographs and drawings before asking students to describe or evaluate places.
