Collecting from a range of sources

AC9HS1S02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can collect relevant information from a range of provided sources (stories, photographs, maps, observations) for a HASS inquiry.

Success criteria

  • Collects information from at least two different source types
  • Identifies at least one piece of relevant information from each source

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFS02): Students sorted and recorded information using pictorial timelines and located features on pictorial maps or models.

Width

Minimum
Collect information from at least two different types of sources (e.g. a photograph and an observation) and identify what each source tells them.
Typical
Explore stories from the past and present about people, families and places (fiction, letters, diaries, songs, Dreaming stories, films); gather evidence from local observations, aerial photographs and sketches; identify relevant information from each source type.
Wider
  • Compare what different source types (photograph vs oral history vs observation) reveal about the same topic and discuss why sources differ
  • Identify which sources are primary (from the time) and which are secondary (created later) and discuss why this matters

Hinge

Recording on timelines and maps (Nodes 2–3) requires students to have collected information first — collecting is the prerequisite; without it, there is nothing to record.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyYear 1 students need to encounter multiple source types before they can select and use them — build a class repertoire through whole-class exploration of each source type.
