← AC9HS5K01 Causes of british colonies after 1800

Integrating multiple causes

AC9HS5K01 n5 · waypoint 5 of 5 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the economic, political and social causes of the establishment of British colonies in Australia after 1800, identifying how different causes applied to different colony types.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least one economic, one political and one social cause in an integrated account.
  • Students show how the mix of causes differed between at least two colony types.
  • Students' explanation uses relevant historical terminology (e.g. 'penal colony', 'free settlers', 'transportation').

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 4: Social causes.

Width

Minimum
Explain at least one cause from each category (economic, political, social) and link each to the establishment of at least one colony.
Typical
Explain the full range of economic, political and social causes, distinguishing how different causes applied more strongly to penal versus free settler colonies, and identify which causes were most significant.
Wider
  • Construct a comparative analysis of two different colonies, examining how the combination of causes shaped the character of each colony differently
  • Evaluate which category of cause (economic, political or social) was most significant overall, using evidence from multiple colonies to support the argument

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe integration node requires students to bring all three cause categories together into a coherent explanation. Use structured tasks such as cause-and-effect matrices or comparative colony profiles to support this synthesis.
