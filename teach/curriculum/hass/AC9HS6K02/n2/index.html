Women and the advance of democracy

AC9HS6K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain key developments in advancing democracy and citizenship for women in Australia across the 20th century, including the suffragette movement, right to vote, equal pay and anti-discrimination legislation.

Success criteria

  • Students describe at least two developments that advanced democratic rights for women.
  • Students place each development in approximate chronological context and explain its significance.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: First Nations Australians and political rights.

Width

Minimum
Describe two developments that advanced the rights of women in Australia and explain why each was significant.
Typical
Explain key developments in advancing democracy for women: the suffragette movement and winning the right to vote, the bar on married women working in the public service, the equal pay campaign, and the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 — placing each in chronological context and explaining its significance.
Wider
  • Investigate the life and contribution of a specific figure in the Australian women's rights movement and explain their role in achieving a particular change
  • Compare the pace and nature of democratic change for women in Australia with that in another country, and assess what factors influenced the timing and form of change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe timeline of change for women spans the full 20th century — students benefit from placing individual developments in chronological sequence to understand that democratic inclusion was a long, contested process.
