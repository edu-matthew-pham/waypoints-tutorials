← AC9HS6K02 Citizenship and political change

Migrants, children and expanding citizenship

AC9HS6K02 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain changes to citizenship and democratic participation for migrants and children in Australia across the 20th century, including the minimum wage, anti-discrimination legislation and multicultural policy.

Success criteria

  • Students describe at least two changes that expanded citizenship rights for migrants or children.
  • Students explain the significance of each change for the groups affected.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Women and the advance of democracy.

Width

Minimum
Describe two changes that expanded democratic rights or citizenship for migrants or children.
Typical
Explain key developments advancing democracy for all citizens, including migrant groups: establishment of the minimum wage, anti-discrimination legislation, official multicultural policy — and for children: experiences in orphanages and homes, protections introduced over time.
Wider
  • Investigate the experience of a specific migrant group in Australia during the 20th century and assess how legislation and policy affected their citizenship rights
  • Explore the history of child welfare legislation in Australia, including the Stolen Generations, and evaluate the long-term impacts on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe range of groups whose citizenship expanded across the 20th century is the key breadth dimension — students benefit from comparison across groups to see democratic change as a broad social process rather than isolated events.
