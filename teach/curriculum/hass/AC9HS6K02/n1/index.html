First Nations Australians and political rights

AC9HS6K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the significance of the 1962 right to vote federally and the 1967 referendum for First Nations Australians, and explain what these changes meant for citizenship and political recognition.

Success criteria

  • Students explain what changed for First Nations Australians in 1962 and 1967.
  • Students describe why these changes were significant for citizenship and political recognition.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS5K06 (Democracy, elections and representatives): Students understand the key values and features of Australian democracy. From AC9HS6K01 (Federation and democracy): Students have just studied the founding of Australia's democratic system.

Width

Minimum
Explain what happened in 1962 and 1967 for First Nations Australians and why these events were significant.
Typical
Describe the significance of the 1962 right to vote federally and the importance of the 1967 referendum for First Nations Australians — what changed, what had been in place before, and why these changes mattered for citizenship, political recognition and the relationship between First Nations peoples and the Australian state.
Wider
  • Investigate the campaign for the 1967 referendum — who organised it, what arguments were made, and how the Yes vote result was achieved
  • Explore the ongoing campaign for constitutional recognition of First Nations Australians, and assess what further changes advocates argue are still needed

Hinge

The exclusion and then inclusion of First Nations Australians in civic and political life is foundational to understanding Australia's democratic history — students who treat democracy as having always been inclusive will misunderstand both the struggles that created change and the significance of what was achieved.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a before/after framework to make the political exclusion visible before the change — students need to understand what the situation was before 1962 and 1967 to appreciate the significance of the change.
