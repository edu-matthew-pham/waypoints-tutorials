Citizenship and political change

AC9HS6K02 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

changes in Australia's political system and to Australian citizenship after Federation and throughout the 20th century that impacted First Nations Australians, migrants, women and children

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS5K06 (Democracy, elections and representatives): Students understand the key values and features of Australian democracy. From AC9HS6K01 (Federation and democracy): Students have just studied the founding of Australia's democratic system.

By the end

Students explain the changes in Australia's political system and citizenship that impacted First Nations Australians, migrants, women and children after Federation and throughout the 20th century, assessing the significance of key changes.

Waypoints

  • 1. First Nations Australians and political rightsHingeStudents can describe the significance of the 1962 right to vote federally and the 1967 referendum for First Nations Australians, and explain what these changes meant for citizenship and political recognition.
  • 2. Women and the advance of democracyStudents can explain key developments in advancing democracy and citizenship for women in Australia across the 20th century, including the suffragette movement, right to vote, equal pay and anti-discrimination legislation.
  • 3. Migrants, children and expanding citizenshipStudents can explain changes to citizenship and democratic participation for migrants and children in Australia across the 20th century, including the minimum wage, anti-discrimination legislation and multicultural policy.
  • 4. Democracy as an evolving systemStudents can explain that Australia's political system and citizenship have changed significantly since Federation, assessing the significance of key changes for different groups and describing the nature of democracy as an evolving rather than fixed system.
