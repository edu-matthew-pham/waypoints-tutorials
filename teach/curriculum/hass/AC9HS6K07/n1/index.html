← AC9HS6K07 Roles of government

Three levels and their primary responsibilities

AC9HS6K07 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can name the three levels of government and describe the primary roles and responsibilities of each level.

Success criteria

  • Students name the three levels of government.
  • Students correctly categorise at least two responsibilities for each level.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K08 (Local government and services): Students explain that local government provides specific services to communities. From AC9HS5K06 (Democracy, elections and representatives): Students know that elected representatives exist at different levels of government.

Width

Minimum
Name the three levels of government and describe at least two responsibilities of each.
Typical
Sort and categorise the roles and responsibilities of local (roads, rubbish, parks, local planning), state/territory (schools, hospitals, police, state roads, public transport) and federal (defence, immigration, foreign affairs, social security, national laws) governments.
Wider
  • Investigate the specific responsibilities of your local council and compare them with the responsibilities of your state government — identifying what is managed at each level and why
  • Examine a local community issue and identify which level of government is primarily responsible for addressing it, explaining the reasoning

Hinge

Without a clear map of which level is responsible for what, students cannot analyse overlapping responsibilities, resolve confusion about who is accountable for specific issues, or understand how the federal system operates.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a sorting activity (card sort or three-column table) to categorise responsibilities across the three levels — make the categories concrete before moving to analysis of overlap and interaction.
