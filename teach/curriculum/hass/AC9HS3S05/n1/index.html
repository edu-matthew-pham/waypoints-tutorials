Drawing evidence-based conclusions

AC9HS3S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Year 3 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw conclusions about how their place and community have changed, the preservation of environmental features, and how people are connected to places, based on analysed data and information.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • States at least one conclusion supported by at least two pieces of evidence
  • Conclusion responds to an inquiry question rather than restating a fact

Prior knowledge

From Y2 (AC9HS2S05): Students drew evidence-based conclusions about significance, technology change and place connections.

Width

Minimum
Draw at least one conclusion about community change, an environmental feature or a place connection, supported by at least two pieces of analysed evidence.
Typical
Reach conclusions about: how place and community have changed based on settlement patterns, local species changes, historic events or cultural celebrations; preservation of unique natural features; similarities and differences of places in Australia and neighbouring countries.
Wider
  • Draw conclusions that connect community change to broader patterns (e.g. immigration, environmental policy) beyond the local area
  • Compare conclusions drawn from different source types on the same topic and discuss which are strongest

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkYear 3 conclusions must be grounded in analysis, not just impression — use structured evidence-to-conclusion templates to make the reasoning visible.
