Family structures and roles over time

AC9HS1K01 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

differences in family structures and roles today, and how these have changed or remained the same over time

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFK01): Students identified people in their family, where they were born and raised, and how family members are related. They used relationship vocabulary and created concept maps of family.

By the end

Students explain differences in family structures and roles today, and describe how these have changed or remained the same over time.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying the similarities and differences of a range of family structures; for example, nuclear families, one-child families, large families, single-parent families, extended families, blended or step families, adoptive and foster parent families and grandparent families, and kinship groups
  • comparing families in the present with those from the recent past, for example, the size of families across the generations
  • examining the roles of family members over time, such as roles of parents, children and extended family members, and comparing these with family roles today

Waypoints

  • 1. Variety of family structures todayHingeStudents can identify a range of family structures in contemporary Australia and describe how they differ from each other.
  • 2. Roles of family membersStudents can describe roles played by different family members (parents, children, extended family) and note that these roles can vary across families.
  • 3. Change and continuity over timeStudents can compare family structures and roles from the past (parents' and grandparents' generations) with the present, identifying what has changed and what has remained the same.
