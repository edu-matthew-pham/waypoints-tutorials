Types of local significance

AC9HS2K01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 2 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify different types of significance (social, cultural, spiritual, historical) and give examples of each from their local area.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two types of significance by name
  • Gives at least one local example of each type

Prior knowledge

From Y1 (AC9HS1K01-K02): Students compared family structures and daily life across generations. They understand continuity and change and comparative language.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two types of significance and give one local example of each.
Typical
Explore a range of local individuals, groups, places and buildings; classify each by type of significance; discuss why particular sites have heritage significance or cultural value for present generations.
Wider
  • Identify local sites of significance to First Nations Australians (engraving sites, rock paintings, natural features) and explain their significance from a First Nations perspective
  • Compare why the same local site might have different significance to different community groups

Hinge

Without a conceptual vocabulary for types of significance, students cannot explain why a specific local place matters — the explanatory task in Node 2 requires these categories.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkSocial, cultural and spiritual significance are abstract at Year 2 — anchor each type to a concrete local example before asking students to classify independently.
