Time sequence vocabulary

AC9HS1S06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use time sequence terms (then, now, yesterday, today, past, present, later on, before I was born, in the future, generations) correctly in spoken and written communication.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least four time sequence terms correctly in context
  • Terms are used to express temporal relationships, not randomly inserted

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFS05): Students shared narratives using sources and basic time/place terms.

Width

Minimum
Use at least four time sequence terms accurately in a spoken or written description of a change over time.
Typical
Use a full range of time sequence terms in speech and writing when describing family life over time and changes to places; use terms to order events and connect past to present.
Wider
  • Explore how time sequence terms translate across languages represented in the class and discuss how different cultures describe the passage of time
  • Create a glossary of time sequence terms with student-generated definitions and examples

Hinge

Developing a historical narrative requires the vocabulary of time sequence — without these terms, students cannot express the relationship between past and present that HASS Year 1 demands.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTime sequence vocabulary requires deliberate, frequent practice across multiple contexts — use routines (morning meeting time talk, sequencing activities) to build fluency before expecting narrative use.
