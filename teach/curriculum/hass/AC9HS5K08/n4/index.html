Sustainable resource use

AC9HS5K08 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why sustainable resource use is important, and describe how resources might be used more sustainably to meet current and future needs and wants.

Success criteria

  • Students explain what sustainable resource use means and why it matters.
  • Students describe at least one strategy for using resources more sustainably.
  • Students connect sustainable resource use to meeting both current and future needs and wants.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: How resources satisfy needs and wants.

Width

Minimum
Explain what 'sustainable resource use' means and give one example of a more sustainable practice.
Typical
Explain why sustainable resource use is important — that resources are finite and that current use patterns affect future availability — and describe strategies for using resources more sustainably, with examples from natural, human and capital resource categories.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific resource management challenge (e.g. water scarcity, soil degradation, energy transition) and evaluate proposed solutions for sustainability
  • Explore how First Nations Australian resource management practices (e.g. seasonal harvesting, firestick farming) model sustainable use, and consider what modern economies can learn from these approaches

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierSustainable resource use integrates the needs/wants distinction (Node 1), the resource types (Node 2), and the production process (Node 3). Students need to connect all three to make a reasoned sustainability judgment.
