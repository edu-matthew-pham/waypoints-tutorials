Experiences of individuals in establishing the first british colony

AC9HS4K03 · Year 4 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the experiences of individuals and groups, including military and civilian officials, and convicts involved in the establishment of the first British colony

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K02: Students explained causes of the first British colony. They now examine who was involved and what they experienced.

By the end

Students describe the experiences of diverse individuals and groups involved in establishing the first British colony.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • describing the events around the establishment of the First Fleet and the key people involved, such as Captain Arthur Phillip, marines, Reverend Richard Johnson and convicts
  • describing the events and experiences of groups and individuals on the journey of the First Fleet
  • examining the wide range of crimes punishable by transportation and looking closely at the people who were transported
  • investigating attitudes to the poor, the treatment of prisoners and the social standing of those who travelled to Australia on the First Fleet, including families, children and convict guards
  • investigating daily life in the Port Jackson penal settlement, the challenges experienced by the people there and how they were managed

Waypoints

  • 1. Who was on the First FleetHingeStudents can identify the different groups of people on the First Fleet and describe the range of backgrounds and reasons for their presence.
  • 2. Experiences during the journey and early settlementStudents can describe the experiences of specific groups and individuals on the journey of the First Fleet and in the early Port Jackson settlement.
