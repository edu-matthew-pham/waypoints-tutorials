Sustainable use of resources

AC9HS4K06 · Year 4 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

sustainable use and management of renewable and non-renewable resources, including the custodial responsibility First Nations Australians have for Country/Place

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K05: Students explained the importance of environments and vegetation to people and animals. From Y3 (AC9HS3K06): Students explored rules and community responsibility.

By the end

Students explain how resources can be used and managed sustainably, including First Nations Australian custodial responsibility for Country/Place.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • exploring how some resources are used and managed in sustainable and non-sustainable ways; for example, auditing use of renewable and non-renewable resources in the classroom, investigating recycling and waste disposal of non-renewable resources in the school and by local government, reducing waste through "nude food" lunch boxes and using recycled toilet paper, examining how renewable resources such as timber are managed
  • investigating how First Nations Australians adapted ways using knowledge and practices linked to the sustainable use of resources and environments (for example, rotational use and harvesting of resources; mutton-bird harvesting in Tasmania; the use of fire; the use of vegetation endemic in the local area for food, shelter, medicine, tools and weapons; and the collection of bush food from semi-arid rangelands), and how this knowledge can be taught through stories and songs, reflecting their inherent, custodial responsibilities

Waypoints

  • 1. Renewable and non-renewable resourcesHingeStudents can distinguish between renewable and non-renewable resources and describe sustainable and non-sustainable ways resources are used.
  • 2. First Nations Australian custodial responsibilityStudents can explain how First Nations Australians have practised sustainable resource use through knowledge and practices linked to custodial responsibility for Country/Place.
