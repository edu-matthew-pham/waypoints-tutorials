How environments are used and managed

AC9HS5K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how Australian environments are managed through practices and laws designed to balance human use with environmental protection.

Success criteria

  • Students name at least two environmental management approaches and explain the purpose of each.
  • Students give a specific Australian example for at least one management approach.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K06 (Resource management and sustainability): Students explain why environments are important and how communities manage resources for sustainability. Students understand that environments can be affected by human actions.

Width

Minimum
Explain one way Australian environments are managed (e.g. national parks, zoning, wildlife corridors) and why that management is needed.
Typical
Explain the range of environmental management approaches: protective laws and practices, zoning systems, creation of wildlife corridors and national parks, and land-use planning that aims to balance economic use with environmental protection.
Wider
  • Investigate the management of a specific Australian national park or protected area, including the rules, zoning and management challenges it faces
  • Explore how First Nations Australian management practices (e.g. cultural burning, seasonal harvesting protocols) are being incorporated into contemporary environmental management frameworks

Hinge

Students who don't understand that environments are actively managed through deliberate decisions will treat environmental hazards as purely natural events disconnected from human choice. The concept of management is the prerequisite for evaluating management responses in subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a framework that distinguishes 'what the environment provides', 'how humans use it', and 'how management balances these' — this three-part structure scaffolds the conceptual move from environment as given to environment as managed.
