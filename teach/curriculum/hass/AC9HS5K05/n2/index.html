Types of severe weather events

AC9HS5K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can describe the types of severe weather events that affect Australian environments — bushfires, floods, droughts and cyclones — and explain their causes and typical locations.

Success criteria

  • Students name at least three types of severe weather events that affect Australia and the regions at risk.
  • Students explain at least one cause of a severe weather event and its typical consequences.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How environments are used and managed.

Width

Minimum
Describe one type of severe weather event, explain where in Australia it typically occurs, and identify one consequence.
Typical
Describe the range of severe weather events affecting Australia (bushfires, floods, droughts, cyclones), explain the environmental and geographical factors that cause them, and identify the typical regions most at risk from each.
Wider
  • Investigate how climate patterns and climate variability (including El Niño and La Niña) affect the frequency and intensity of severe weather events in Australia
  • Compare the experience of severe weather events in Australia with those in other countries, and explain why different regions face different types of natural hazards

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often have uneven prior experience of different hazard types depending on where they live — deliberately addressing all four types ensures the breadth needed for comparative management analysis.
