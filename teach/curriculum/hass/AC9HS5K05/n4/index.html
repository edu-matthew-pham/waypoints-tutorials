← AC9HS5K05 Managing environments and hazards

Consequences and evaluating responses

AC9HS5K05 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the consequences of severe weather events on communities and environments, and evaluate how effectively management approaches reduce those consequences.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least two consequences of a severe weather event across different domains (e.g. environmental, economic, social).
  • Students evaluate the effectiveness of at least one management strategy with reference to evidence.
  • Students use relevant geographic and environmental terms accurately.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Management strategies for hazards.

Width

Minimum
Explain two consequences of a severe weather event and assess whether one management strategy was effective in reducing harm.
Typical
Explain the environmental, social and economic consequences of severe weather events; evaluate how effective management strategies (e.g. building codes, controlled burns, irrigation) have been in reducing harm; acknowledge that not all consequences can be mitigated.
Wider
  • Evaluate the long-term consequences of a recurring environmental hazard (e.g. drought) on a specific community or industry, and propose management improvements
  • Examine how environmental mismanagement can create or intensify hazards (e.g. fire fuel loads from lack of burning, flood risk from inadequate drainage planning), and propose how management practices should change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierEvaluation requires students to bring together their understanding of hazard types, management strategies, and consequences. Use structured evaluation criteria (e.g. economic cost, lives saved, environmental protection) to scaffold judgment.
