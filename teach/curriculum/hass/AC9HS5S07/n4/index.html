Presenting evidence-supported descriptions and explanations

AC9HS5S07 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can present descriptions and explanations that draw ideas, findings and viewpoints from sources, use subject-specific terms accurately, and are communicated in a format appropriate to purpose and audience.

Success criteria

  • Students reference at least two sources in their description or explanation.
  • Students use subject-specific HASS terms accurately across at least two discipline areas.
  • Students present in a format that is appropriate for the content and clearly communicated.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Descriptions and explanations in different formats.

Width

Minimum
Present a description or explanation that references at least two sources, uses appropriate subject-specific terms, and is in a format suited to the purpose.
Typical
Present descriptions and explanations that: draw on sourced ideas and viewpoints across a HASS topic, use accurate subject-specific terms from relevant disciplines, apply appropriate conventions for the chosen format, and communicate findings in a way suited to purpose and audience.
Wider
  • Present findings from an extended HASS inquiry in a form suitable for a real audience (e.g. a school assembly presentation, an article for a school newsletter, a letter to a local council)
  • Reflect on the effectiveness of a completed presentation — evaluate what communicated well and what could be improved, and identify specific revisions that would strengthen it

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThe integrated presentation task brings together source referencing, disciplinary vocabulary and format selection. Students need to coordinate all three simultaneously — scaffold with a planning template before drafting.
