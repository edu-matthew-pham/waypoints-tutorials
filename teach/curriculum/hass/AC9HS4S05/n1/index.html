Drawing analytical conclusions

AC9HS4S05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 1 · Year 4 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can draw conclusions about historical, geographical and civic topics by analysing sources, with conclusions grounded in evidence rather than opinion.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • States at least one conclusion grounded in analysis of sources
  • Conclusion goes beyond restating facts to explain a relationship or pattern

Prior knowledge

From Y3 (AC9HS3S05): Students drew evidence-based conclusions about community change, environmental features and place connections.

Width

Minimum
Draw at least one conclusion from analysis of at least two sources on a HASS topic.
Typical
Describe risks in past times (sea travel, exploration, colonisation); explain how seeking resources connects to trade, exploration, colonisation and environmental change; analyse sources to answer questions about ecosystems, local government services and school rules.
Wider
  • Draw conclusions that connect historical events (e.g. colonisation) to present-day circumstances (e.g. land ownership, environmental change)
  • Compare conclusions drawn from historical and geographical sources about the same topic and identify where they converge or diverge

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkYear 4 conclusions must integrate multi-source analysis — use structured templates that require evidence from at least two sources before a conclusion can be stated.
