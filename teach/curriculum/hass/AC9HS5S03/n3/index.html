Inferring relationships from visual and written sources

AC9HS5S03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine visual and written sources to infer relationships — explaining what a relationship means and how evidence supports it.

Success criteria

  • Students infer at least one relationship from a visual or written source.
  • Students explain the evidence in the source that supports their inferred relationship.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Reading and analysing graphs and maps.

Width

Minimum
Identify one relationship inferred from a visual or written source and explain the evidence that supports it.
Typical
Examine photographs, maps and written sources to infer relationships: how drought shapes enterprising behaviour, how place characteristics correlate with settlement patterns, how colonial social structures were shaped by economic roles. Explain how the evidence supports each inferred relationship.
Wider
  • Examine maps and sources showing First Nations Australians' language groups and Countries/Places, and explain the diversity of connections to Country/Place that these sources reveal
  • Investigate multiple sources on the same phenomenon and compare the relationships each source implies — identifying where sources agree and where they suggest different causal patterns

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe move from 'observation' to 'inferred relationship' is inferential and requires explicit scaffolding — students need to practise articulating 'I see X, which suggests Y because Z' as a structured reasoning step.
