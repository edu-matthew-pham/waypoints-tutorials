Importance of rules and consequences

AC9HS3K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Year 3 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain why rules are important (protecting rights, enabling fairness, maintaining safety) and describe consequences when rules are not followed.

Success criteria

  • Explains why at least one rule is important
  • Describes at least one consequence of breaking a rule

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Who makes rules and why.

Width

Minimum
Explain why at least one rule is important and describe at least one consequence of breaking it.
Typical
Identify familiar rules; explain how rules protect the rights of others and define responsibilities; describe consequences of not following rules; discuss situations where one rule for all is unfair (e.g. students with disability).
Wider
  • Develop and justify a set of fair rules for the class, considering different student needs
  • Discuss whether all rules are always fair and what should be done when a rule seems unfair

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.
