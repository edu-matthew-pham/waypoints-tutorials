← AC9HS5K04 People shape places

How people change place characteristics

AC9HS5K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain that people influence the characteristics of places through land use, resource management and development decisions, and give examples of how this happens.

Success criteria

  • Students name at least two ways people influence the characteristics of places.
  • Students give a specific example for at least one of these influences.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K05 (Importance of environments): Students explain the importance of environments and the connections people have to them. Students understand that places have natural and human characteristics.

Width

Minimum
Explain two ways people change the characteristics of a place.
Typical
Explain the range of ways people change place characteristics: vegetation clearance, fencing, urban development, drainage, irrigation, farming, introduction of livestock, mining, and forest plantations, with examples drawn from Australian contexts.
Wider
  • Investigate how the characteristics of a local place have changed over time by comparing historical and current photographs, maps or records
  • Explore examples of human influence on place characteristics in countries in Asia, Europe or North America, and compare with Australian examples

Hinge

Students who understand only physical/natural characteristics of places (from earlier years) need to clearly grasp that human decisions actively and intentionally reshape places. This conceptual shift from 'places have characteristics' to 'people create characteristics' is foundational for the evaluative work in subsequent nodes.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkUse a 'before and after' framework to make the human influence visible — explicitly connecting the decision (what people did) to the characteristic change (what the place became).
