First Nations Australians and place management

AC9HS5K04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how First Nations Australians have influenced place characteristics through land and resource management practices such as firestick farming.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least one First Nations land management practice and describe its effect on a place.
  • Students distinguish First Nations land management from post-colonial approaches in at least one example.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: How people change place characteristics.

Width

Minimum
Explain one way First Nations Australians managed land or resources and describe the effect on the place's characteristics.
Typical
Explain First Nations Australian land management practices including firestick farming, and describe how these practices sustained environments and shaped place characteristics over thousands of years.
Wider
  • Investigate a specific First Nations community's land management practices and compare them with post-colonial land use in the same area
  • Explore how contemporary land management in Australia is incorporating First Nations knowledge and practices, and the effect this is having on the characteristics of specific places

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFirst Nations land management is a distinct and important context — treating it as equivalent enrichment to other land use examples risks reducing it to tokenism. Give it dedicated time and treat the knowledge system as significant in its own right.
