Rules and laws

AC9HS4K07 · Year 4 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

the differences between 'rules' and 'laws', why laws are important and how they affect the lives of people

Prior knowledge

From Y3 (AC9HS3K06): Students explored who makes rules, why rules are important and the consequences of not following them.

By the end

Students distinguish between rules and laws, explain why laws are important and describe how they affect people's lives.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • distinguishing between "laws", such as not speeding in school zones, and "rules", such as practising sun safety in the school
  • exploring examples of laws and their purpose, recognising that laws apply to everyone in society and why they are important to students' lives
  • investigating the impact of laws, such as environmental laws, native title laws and laws concerning sacred sites, on specific groups, including First Nations Australians
  • investigating the customary lore of First Nations Australians and how they relate to people and places; for example, the lore covers rules of living, skin groups, broad roles of men and women, economic affairs, marriage and other activities

Waypoints

  • 1. Distinguishing rules from lawsHingeStudents can distinguish between rules (contextual, limited scope) and laws (universal, enforceable) and give examples of each.
  • 2. How laws affect people's livesStudents can explain how specific laws affect the lives of people, including the impact of environmental and native title laws on First Nations Australians.
