Daily life continuity and change

AC9HS1K02 · Year 1 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

continuity and change between aspects of their daily lives and their parents' and grandparents' childhoods

Prior knowledge

From Foundation (AC9HSFK02): Students explored celebrations and commemorations and their significance. From AC9HS1K01: Students compared family structures and roles across generations.

By the end

Students identify continuity and change between aspects of their daily lives and their parents' and grandparents' childhoods.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • comparing and commenting on photographs and oral histories to find out how daily lives have changed and remained the same over time; for example, talking to parents, grandparents and other elders
  • comparing what has changed in daily life over time; for example, homes, family traditions, diverse religious and cultural practices, leisure, school life, rules, and shopping/consumer habits
  • examining the traditional toys used by First Nations Australian children to play and learn; for example, Arrernte children learn to play string games so they can remember stories they have been told
  • identifying similarities and differences in classroom equipment, learning activities, school playgrounds and playground games through observations of provided artefacts and photos, and asking questions of adults from different generations

Waypoints

  • 1. Aspects of daily lifeHingeStudents can identify key aspects of daily life (home, family traditions, leisure, school, shopping) and describe what these look like today.
  • 2. Comparing past and present daily lifeStudents can compare aspects of their daily life with the same aspects in their parents' or grandparents' childhoods, identifying similarities and differences using sources.
  • 3. Using comparative language for changeStudents can use comparative language to describe change and continuity across generations, applying terms from provided vocabulary.
