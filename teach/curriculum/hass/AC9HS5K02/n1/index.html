Impact on First Nations Australians

AC9HS5K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the impact of colonial development on the lives of First Nations Australians, including dispossession, conflict, disruption to cultural practices and loss of life.

Success criteria

  • Students explain at least two specific impacts on First Nations Australians with reference to historical evidence.
  • Students describe how colonial development changed the relationship between First Nations Australians and their Country/Place.

Prior knowledge

From AC9HS4K03 (Experiences of colonisation) and AC9HS4K04 (Effects of contact and invasion): Students explain the experiences of colonisation for First Nations Australians and early colonists, and the effects of contact and invasion.

Width

Minimum
Explain at least two impacts of colonial development on First Nations Australians.
Typical
Explain the range of impacts on First Nations Australians: dispossession from Country, frontier conflict, disruption to cultural and spiritual practices, loss of life, and the destruction of established economic systems (including food sources and land management).
Wider
  • Investigate the experience of a particular First Nations community or nation during colonial expansion, using primary and secondary sources to describe changes to their way of life
  • Examine First Nations Australian perspectives and responses to colonisation, including resistance movements and efforts to maintain cultural practices

Hinge

Students who understand colonial impacts only from a settler perspective will struggle to engage with the multi-perspective analysis required in subsequent nodes and in later HASS learning. A thin account here distorts the historical record and limits the sophistication of future causal analysis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepMultiple First Nations perspectives and case studies are essential — not as enrichment but as the corrective to a default settler-centred account. Use sources that foreground First Nations voices and experiences.
