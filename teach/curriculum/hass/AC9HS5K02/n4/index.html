← AC9HS5K02 Impacts of colonial development

Integrating multiple perspectives on impact

AC9HS5K02 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the impact of colonial development in Australia on First Nations Australians, colonists and convicts, and the natural environment, presenting a multi-perspective account that acknowledges intended and unintended consequences.

Success criteria

  • Students explain the impact on at least three groups or entities (e.g. First Nations Australians, convicts, environment) in an integrated account.
  • Students distinguish between intended and unintended consequences of colonial development.
  • Students demonstrate awareness that different groups experienced colonial development very differently.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Impact on the natural environment.

Width

Minimum
Describe the impact on at least two different groups and on the environment, and identify at least one unintended consequence.
Typical
Explain the contrasting impacts on First Nations Australians, different colonial groups, and the environment; identify which impacts were intended by colonial authorities and which were unintended; acknowledge different perspectives on these impacts.
Wider
  • Evaluate which group experienced the most significant negative impact from colonial development and justify the claim with evidence
  • Investigate how the long-term environmental impacts of colonial settlement continue to affect Australia today, and how First Nations land management practices are now being recognised and applied

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis integration node requires students to hold multiple perspectives simultaneously — use structured comparison tasks (e.g. perspective matrices) that make visible the different experiences and their causes.
