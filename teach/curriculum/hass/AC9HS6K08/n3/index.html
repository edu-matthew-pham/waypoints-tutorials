Types of financial transactions and their implications

AC9HS6K08 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the different types of financial transactions (notes, coins, credit and debit cards, barter) and describe the advantages and disadvantages of each.

Success criteria

  • Students name at least three types of financial transactions.
  • Students explain at least one advantage and one disadvantage for at least two transaction types.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Selling strategies and advertising.

Width

Minimum
Name three types of financial transactions and explain one advantage and one disadvantage of each.
Typical
Explain the range of financial transaction types: notes and coins, credit cards, debit cards, digital payments, and barter — describe the advantages and disadvantages of each type and consider how the type of transaction available may influence purchasing decisions.
Wider
  • Investigate how digital and cashless payment systems have changed consumer behaviour, and assess the implications for record-keeping, budgeting and privacy
  • Explore the concept of credit and debt — when is using credit a sound financial decision and when might it lead to financial difficulty?

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe advantages and disadvantages framework for transaction types requires careful analysis — avoid oversimplification (cash = bad, digital = good) by exploring real tradeoffs including access, privacy, spending behaviour and record-keeping.
