← AC9HS5K03 Individuals and groups in colonial development

Assessing impact and 'what if' reasoning

AC9HS5K03 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can assess the economic, social or political impact of a significant individual or group on an Australian colony, and reason about how events might have developed differently.

Success criteria

  • Students explain the economic, social or political impact of their chosen individual or group with reference to evidence.
  • Students construct a plausible 'what if' scenario and explain why the actual outcome occurred instead.
  • Students use appropriate historical terms (e.g. 'significance', 'impact', 'consequence') in their response.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Roles of groups including migrants and First Nations Australians.

Width

Minimum
Explain the impact of one individual or group and identify one alternative outcome that might have occurred if events had been different.
Typical
Assess the economic, social or political impact of a chosen individual or group; construct a 'what if' scenario that explores an alternative outcome for a key event; evaluate which factors made the actual outcome more likely than the alternative.
Wider
  • Evaluate the relative significance of multiple individuals or groups in shaping the same colonial event, and justify a claim about whose role was most significant
  • Examine how 'what if' thinking is used by historians to understand causation, and apply this method to assess the role of contingency versus inevitability in colonial history

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkThe 'what if' reasoning and impact assessment require explicit scaffolding — provide sentence frames or structured planning templates that distinguish between describing what happened and reasoning about what might have been different and why.
