← AC9HS6S06 Assess possible effects

Proposing and planning responses with assessed effects

AC9HS6S06 nnull · waypoint null of 2 · Year 6 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students propose or plan a response to an identified issue or challenge and assess the possible effects of that response using criteria, including consideration of risks and alternative views.

Success criteria

  • Proposal is clearly linked to an identified issue or challenge.
  • At least two criteria are used to assess possible effects.
  • At least one possible negative effect or risk is identified alongside strategies to address it.
  • Demonstrates that criteria (not personal preference alone) shaped the assessment.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint null: Identifying and applying criteria to evaluate options.

Width

Minimum
Propose one response to a real HASS issue and assess its possible effects using at least two criteria, identifying one risk or limitation of the response.
Typical
Plan a response to a HASS challenge (civic, environmental, economic) with a specification of actions and rationale; assess possible social, cultural, economic and environmental effects using identified criteria; acknowledge alternative views and identify strategies to minimise negative effects.
Wider
  • Plan, execute and evaluate a school-based or community response project (e.g. environmental action, school council proposal, fundraising campaign), assessing its effects against predetermined criteria throughout.
  • Use the elaboration's negotiation and consensus-building approach: present a proposed response, incorporate critique from peers representing alternative stakeholder views, and revise the proposal accordingly.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepWider students can apply the propose-assess-effects process across multiple issue domains (civic, environmental, economic), with each domain bringing different criteria and effects into focus.
Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents who engage with the full project cycle (plan, execute, evaluate against criteria) are building a practitioner repertoire for civic and environmental response — the thinking is only fully legible through enacted work.
