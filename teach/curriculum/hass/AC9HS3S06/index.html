Propose actions or responses

AC9HS3S06 · Year 3 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

propose actions or responses to an issue or challenge that consider possible effects of actions

Prior knowledge

From Y2 (AC9HS2S05): Students proposed positive futures for familiar places. From Y1 (AC9HS1S05): Students made proposals for caring for places.

By the end

Students propose actions or responses to an issue or challenge, considering possible effects of those actions.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • contributing ideas to a group response to a community challenge; for example, ways their class or school could contribute to recycling or conserving energy
  • considering anticipated effects of actions designed to protect and improve places that people perceive as important, such as places of environmental, cultural or religious value, or historic significance
  • developing a plan of action to achieve a set goal; for example, to protect a place, to participate in a community festival or commemoration, to raise awareness about an issue, to raise money for a purpose
  • arguing a point of view on a civics and citizenship issue relevant to their lives (for example, the consequences of breaking school rules, the value of contributing to their community, the need to preserve an endangered species) and making effective use of persuasive language such as "I think" and "I dis/agree that" to gain the support of others

Waypoints

  • 1. Considering effects of actionsHingeStudents can anticipate the effects of proposed actions before committing to them, distinguishing between positive and negative effects.
  • 2. Proposing and justifying responsesStudents can develop a plan of action for a community challenge and argue for their proposal using persuasive reasoning.
