Features of Australia's electoral system

AC9HS5K06 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Year 5 HASS (Foundation to Year 6) · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in HASS (Foundation to Year 6): treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain the key features of Australia's electoral system, including the secret ballot, compulsory voting, preferential voting, and the role of the Australian Electoral Commission.

Success criteria

  • Students describe at least two features of Australia's electoral system and explain the purpose of each.
  • Students explain the role of the Australian Electoral Commission.

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: What is democracy?.

Width

Minimum
Describe two features of Australia's electoral system and explain the purpose of each.
Typical
Explain all key features of Australia's electoral system: the secret ballot, compulsory voting, preferential voting system, and the role of the Australian Electoral Commission in administering free and fair elections.
Wider
  • Investigate how Australia's voting system has changed over time, including when women and First Nations Australians were first allowed to vote, and evaluate what these changes reveal about democracy as an evolving system
  • Compare Australia's electoral system with that of another country, identifying similarities and differences in features such as voting age, compulsory voting and vote-counting methods

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe historical expansion of voting rights (women, First Nations Australians) adds essential context to understanding electoral features as choices made and contested, not natural givens.
