Strategies to enhance production

AC9TDE10P05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate manufacturing processes to identify strategies that enhance production — reducing waste, cutting costs, speeding up processes or forming beneficial production partnerships.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one production enhancement strategy that is specific to the design project
  • Explains what it improves and how it would be implemented — not just names the strategy

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Collaborative planning with equitable role distribution.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one strategy to enhance a production process; explain what it reduces or improves and why.
Typical
Investigate manufacturing processes to identify multiple production enhancement strategies — waste reduction, cost reduction, speed improvement, process optimisation or beneficial partnerships; evaluate each strategy for its practical applicability to the design project; incorporate selected strategies into the project plan.
Wider
  • Investigate lean manufacturing or similar production improvement frameworks — identify which principles apply to the design project and explain how they would change the production plan
  • Identify where in the production process beneficial partnerships (e.g. with specialist manufacturers, suppliers or fabricators) could reduce cost, time or complexity — evaluate the trade-offs
  • Map production waste at each stage of a design project and identify reduction strategies — evaluate the sustainability benefit of each strategy

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkProduction enhancement requires students to reason from current process → identify inefficiency or waste → identify strategy → evaluate applicability. Without this structure, students suggest generic improvements without analysing whether they apply to their specific production context.
