Planning and interpreting production timelines

AC9TDE10P05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can produce, explain and interpret drawings and production timelines using digital tools for a design project.

Success criteria

  • Production plan is produced using a digital tool with at least three sequenced tasks
  • At least one task dependency is identified and explained

Prior knowledge

Students can develop project plans to individually and collaboratively manage time, cost and production (AC9TDE8P05). They have experience with sequencing and tracking tasks. They have not yet developed plans for specific intended purposes and audiences, incorporated formal risk and contingency planning, or investigated manufacturing process strategies for production enhancement.

Width

Minimum
Produce a production timeline using a digital tool; include at least three tasks with timeframes and dependencies; explain one dependency between tasks.
Typical
Produce and interpret production timelines and flowcharts using digital tools (spreadsheets, planning software); include task dependencies, resource requirements and sequencing logic; explain how the timeline supports efficient, safe and sustainable workflows.
Wider
  • Create a production flowchart that maps all decision points in a production process — explain how the flowchart identifies where safety checks, quality reviews or sustainability decisions occur
  • Produce a materials and equipment needs analysis using a spreadsheet — calculate quantities, costs and sourcing requirements for the production plan
  • Compare two approaches to sequencing the same production tasks — explain which produces a more efficient workflow and why

Hinge

A production plan that cannot be interpreted by collaborators makes N2 equitable role distribution impossible — if the timeline and task structure are unclear, team members cannot negotiate distribution or identify their responsibilities.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsProduction timeline creation requires precision — task sequence, dependencies, durations and milestones must be specific enough to be actionable. Students who produce vague plans cannot use them for collaboration or risk identification.
