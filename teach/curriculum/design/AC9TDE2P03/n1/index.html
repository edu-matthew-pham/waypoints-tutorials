Evaluating against personal preferences

AC9TDE2P03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can reflect on and record a judgement about a design idea or solution based on personal preferences, identifying strengths and areas for improvement

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one strength of the design or solution
  • Names at least one area for improvement — not just 'make it better' but a specific change

Prior knowledge

Students can evaluate what they made using personal preferences (AC9TDEFP01). They have shared opinions about their design. They have not yet evaluated against sustainability considerations, reflected on environmental impacts, or compared design ideas for strengths and weaknesses in a structured way.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least one strength and one weakness of a design or solution using personal preferences as the basis
Typical
Reflect on design ideas and solutions across the full design process — describe what meets the design need and what could be improved; record judgements using audio, video or written/drawn responses; share with others
Wider
  • Describe how design ideas meet the needs of those who will use the solution — use audio or video recording to capture the evaluation; review the recording and identify changes
  • Share design strengths and weaknesses with the class — explain how playground equipment might be unsuitable for some children and suggest specific improvements

Hinge

Evaluative reasoning against personal preferences is the prerequisite for Node 2's sustainability evaluation — Node 2 extends the same reasoning structure to environmental criteria. Students who cannot evaluate against any criterion cannot take on a second, less familiar one.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluation requires students to apply a criterion to their work — but at Y1-2, the criterion of personal preference is itself vague without scaffolding. A structure (what did you want it to do? does it do that? what would you change and why?) moves evaluation from 'I like it/I don't like it' to reasoned judgement about design success.
