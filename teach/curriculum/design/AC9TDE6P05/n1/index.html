Planning production steps and sequencing tasks

AC9TDE6P05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can plan and sequence the production steps needed to complete a designed solution — using digital tools to record the sequence

Success criteria

  • Sequences production steps in a logical order
  • Identifies at least one dependency between steps
  • Represents the plan using a digital tool

Prior knowledge

Students can sequence steps to individually and collaboratively make designed solutions (AC9TDE4P05). They have sequenced production steps. They have not yet developed full project plans that include resources, roles, timelines, milestones and digital planning tools — the shift is from step sequencing to project management.

Width

Minimum
List the production steps for a design project in a logical sequence; identify at least one dependency between steps (this step must happen before that one)
Typical
Plan production steps needed to produce a product, service or environment using digital tools — creating a flowchart, digital planner or task list that sequences tasks and includes deadlines; explain why steps must occur in a specific order
Wider
  • Create a digital flowchart or planner that records the sequence of tasks and deadlines for a design project — including contingency steps for when a task takes longer than expected
  • Use a cloud-based or server-based document to record production steps and timelines — explaining the document sharing settings applied and why
  • Plan the production of a community meal or event, sequencing food preparation and service tasks — identifying where tasks must be done in parallel versus sequentially

Hinge

Task sequencing in N1 is prerequisite for N2's resource allocation — you cannot specify when a resource is needed without knowing when in the sequence the task requiring it occurs. Students who cannot sequence tasks cannot produce a valid resource plan.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents who list tasks without considering sequencing constraints don't yet understand project planning as dependency management. Making dependencies explicit — this step needs the output of that step — is the key reasoning move that distinguishes planning from task listing.
