Tools, equipment and production methods affect outcomes

AC9TDE6K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how the tools, equipment and techniques used in production affect the quality and characteristics of the designed solution

Success criteria

  • Describes how at least one production tool or technique affects the outcome
  • Identifies at least one trade-off between different production approaches

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Material properties determine selection.

Width

Minimum
Describe how at least one tool or production technique affects the quality or characteristics of a designed solution
Typical
Compare tools, equipment and techniques across different production contexts (factory, local enterprise, handmade); explain how production scale and methods affect cost, quality and design outcomes — including environmental and social impacts
Wider
  • Compare tools, equipment and techniques used in factories with those used by local and regional enterprises — explaining how scale, cost and design intent differ between mass production and local craft production
  • Compare design and production of products in Australia and a country in Asia (e.g. Indonesia) — exploring how the availability and preferred properties of materials, and the design of public structures like shelters and housing, differ by context
  • Describe how systems in public places (community exercise environments, water treatment plants, waste management services) are designed to benefit community life — identifying the material and system properties that make each fit for purpose

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents tend to see production tools as neutral — any tool does the job. Comparing production across scales (factory vs handmade) and cultural contexts (Australia vs Asia) makes visible how tool choice and technique are themselves design decisions with trade-offs for quality, cost and cultural fit.
