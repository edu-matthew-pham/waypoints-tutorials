Understanding design criteria as evaluation tools

AC9TDE4P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what design criteria are and describe what a solution would need to do to meet a given criterion

Success criteria

  • Explains what at least two criteria require of a solution
  • Distinguishes between criteria and solution (criteria state what the solution must do; they are not the solution itself)

Prior knowledge

Students can evaluate the success of design ideas and solutions based on personal preferences and including sustainability (AC9TDE2P03). They have evaluated based on personal judgement. They have not yet used explicit design criteria as the basis for evaluation or co-developed criteria with others.

Width

Minimum
Read a set of given design criteria and explain in plain language what a solution must do to satisfy at least two of them
Typical
Read and interpret design criteria for different design problems; explain what each criterion requires of a solution; distinguish between criteria that are met and criteria that are not met for a given solution
Wider
  • Examine design criteria that include universal design principles (flexible use, low physical effort, intuitive use) — explain what each principle means as a design requirement and how it addresses social sustainability
  • Compare two sets of criteria for the same problem — discuss how different criteria lead to different design priorities and potentially different solutions

Hinge

Students who cannot interpret what a criterion requires of a solution cannot co-develop meaningful criteria in N2 or evaluate against them in N3 — the evaluation task requires correctly reading the criteria first.
