Evaluate with design criteria

AC9TDE4P04 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

use given or co-developed design criteria including sustainability to evaluate design ideas and solutions

Prior knowledge

Students can evaluate the success of design ideas and solutions based on personal preferences and including sustainability (AC9TDE2P03). They have evaluated based on personal judgement. They have not yet used explicit design criteria as the basis for evaluation or co-developed criteria with others.

By the end

Students use given or co-developed design criteria — including sustainability — to evaluate design ideas and solutions, with reasons grounded in the criteria.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • developing design criteria with others including considering universal design principles to address social sustainability, for example a criterion that specifies flexible or intuitive use or low physical effort
  • using design criteria to evaluate, revise and select design ideas, for example when designing an e-textile toy for a young child to ensure it will be safe
  • comparing the amount of waste that would be produced from different design ideas and the potential for recycling waste, for example exploring the choice of materials to construct a toy and whether these materials are repairable or able to be recycled once the toy breaks or is no longer wanted
  • reflecting on how well their designed solution meets design criteria, such as ensuring safety and wellbeing of users and meeting the needs of communities or different cultures, for example reviewing and discussing the choice of fabrics used to make re-usable bags and how they could be made more appealing to all cultural groups by considering modifications to style

Waypoints

  • 1. Understanding design criteria as evaluation toolsHingeStudents can explain what design criteria are and describe what a solution would need to do to meet a given criterion
  • 2. Co-developing design criteriaStudents can contribute to developing design criteria with others, including at least one criterion related to sustainability
  • 3. Evaluating ideas and solutions against criteriaStudents can evaluate a design idea or solution against criteria — including sustainability — and provide reasons grounded in the criteria
