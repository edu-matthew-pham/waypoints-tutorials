Co-developing design criteria

AC9TDE4P04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can contribute to developing design criteria with others, including at least one criterion related to sustainability

Success criteria

  • Contributes at least one criterion with a stated reason
  • The contributed criterion addresses something a solution must do, not a preference

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Understanding design criteria as evaluation tools.

Width

Minimum
Contribute at least one criterion to a co-developed set, explaining why it matters for the design problem
Typical
Co-develop design criteria with others for a design problem; include criteria addressing function, safety, sustainability and user needs; explain the reasoning behind each criterion
Wider
  • Develop design criteria that include a social sustainability criterion addressing accessibility for different user groups — explain how this criterion reflects universal design thinking
  • Develop criteria for a product that will be evaluated by users from different cultural backgrounds — discuss how criteria might need to reflect diverse needs and what this reveals about how criteria are never fully neutral

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who only develop criteria for one type of problem (e.g. a toy) may not see that criteria development is always contextual. Developing criteria across different problem types and user groups builds the habit of thinking from the user's perspective rather than the designer's preferences.
