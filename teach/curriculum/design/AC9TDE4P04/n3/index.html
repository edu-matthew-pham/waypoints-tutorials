Evaluating ideas and solutions against criteria

AC9TDE4P04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate a design idea or solution against criteria — including sustainability — and provide reasons grounded in the criteria

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • States whether each criterion is met with a reason
  • At least one criterion involves sustainability
  • Evaluation references the solution's features, not just personal preference

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Co-developing design criteria.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate one design idea or solution against at least two criteria and state whether each criterion is met, with a reason for each judgement
Typical
Evaluate design ideas and solutions against criteria including sustainability across different design contexts; use evaluation to revise and select between design ideas; reflect on how well the final solution meets the criteria
Wider
  • Compare the waste produced by different design ideas and the potential for recycling — use sustainability criteria to evaluate and select between design ideas based on material choices
  • Evaluate a designed solution against criteria that include safety, sustainability and community/cultural appropriateness — discuss cases where criteria compete (e.g. a more sustainable material is less durable) and how to reason through trade-offs

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating against criteria requires criterion-by-criterion reasoning, not an overall impression. Scaffolding the process prevents students from simply stating a preference. The reasoning move (identify criterion → check solution → state match/gap → give reason) needs to become habitual.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierEvaluation integrates criteria knowledge (node 1), co-developed criteria (node 2), and knowledge of materials and sustainability (AC9TDE4K01, K02) into a practical judgement. The coordination of multiple knowledge sources in an evaluation task is the descriptor's full demand.
