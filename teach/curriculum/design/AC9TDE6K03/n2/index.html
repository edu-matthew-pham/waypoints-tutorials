← AC9TDE6K03 Managed food and fibre production

Supply chains — from production to retail

AC9TDE6K03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can sequence and explain the process of converting on-farm food or fibre products into a product suitable for retail sale

Lessons on this site

Success criteria

  • Sequences at least three stages in a food or fibre supply chain in correct order
  • Explains what happens at each stage

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Why food and fibre production requires active management.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three stages in a paddock-to-plate or fibre-to-garment supply chain and explain what happens at each stage
Typical
Sequence the full supply chain for a food or fibre product — from production through processing to retail; explain what value is added at each stage and why each step is necessary; represent the chain visually (e.g. flowchart, diagram)
Wider
  • Create a digital flowchart representing a paddock-to-plate supply chain for a locally grown product — annotating each stage with the management decisions and technologies involved
  • Sequence the fibre-to-garment life cycle (fibre → yarn → fabric → garment) and explain how decisions at each stage affect the sustainability and quality of the final product
  • Visit or virtually tour a farm and investigate how on-site decisions (breed selection, soil management, harvesting timing) affect the quality and quantity of product reaching the consumer

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents commonly know the end product and the raw material but cannot sequence the transformation steps. Tracing the supply chain as a connected sequence — where the output of each stage is the input of the next — makes the managed system legible as a designed process.
