How enterprise responds to technological change

AC9TDE10K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how commercial enterprises respond to the challenges and opportunities of technological change.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific technological change and describes how an enterprise responded
  • Explains what drove the response (market opportunity, competitive pressure, regulation, etc.)

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse the impact of innovation and the development of technologies on designed solutions and sustainability (AC9TDE8K02). They can identify how innovation responds to community needs. They have not yet examined enterprise responses to technological change, used forecasting or backcasting techniques, or analysed innovation for global preferred futures.

Width

Minimum
Describe one way a commercial enterprise has responded to a technological change — either adapting an existing model or creating a new one; explain what drove the response.
Typical
Analyse how commercial enterprises respond to technological change — including changes to business models (e.g. e-commerce), carbon footprint considerations, and the competitive dynamics that drive innovation; examine at least two examples across different sectors.
Wider
  • Compare how two enterprises in the same sector responded differently to the same technological shift — analyse why their responses differed and which was more effective
  • Investigate the carbon footprint implications of e-commerce compared with traditional retail — evaluate whether the convenience benefit is offset by the environmental cost
  • Examine how a First Nations enterprise has used emerging technology to scale, protect or commercialise traditional knowledge — for example, biodegradable polymers using spinifex grass

Hinge

Students who don't understand how enterprises respond to technological change cannot productively apply forecasting and backcasting at N2 — the scenarios have no grounding in how real design and enterprise decisions are made.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyEnterprise response patterns need to accumulate across examples before students can reason about them — a single case study doesn't provide the range needed to apply enterprise thinking to novel futures. Multiple enterprise contexts build the schema that forecasting and backcasting can then work with.
