Testing and selecting combinations

AC9TDE8K06 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can test and select the most appropriate materials, components, tools and equipment for a design solution, justifying selections based on how properties combine

Success criteria

  • Tests at least two options for a design task
  • Justifies the selection by referencing how properties serve the design purpose

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: How combining creates new possibilities.

Width

Minimum
Test at least two material or component options for a design task; select one and justify the selection based on properties
Typical
Test and select across multiple options; justify by explaining how the selected combination of properties serves the design purpose; identify risks and safe work practices for the selected materials and equipment
Wider
  • Investigate a broad range of technologies — tools, equipment, processes, materials, systems, components — when designing for a specific specialisation context such as architecture or medicine
  • Evaluate how the choice of material affects the entire production process — for example, how choosing hardwood vs softwood determines joining techniques, finishing methods and durability

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkJustifying material and component selection requires students to reason from properties to purpose: identify what the design needs → identify which properties serve those needs → compare options → explain why the selected combination is most appropriate. Students who skip this chain produce intuitive rather than evidence-based justifications.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates property knowledge (Node 1) and combination analysis (Node 2) into a selection and justification task. Students must apply both — knowing properties enables the comparison, understanding combination effects enables the justification.
