What makes good design criteria

AC9TDE8P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 5 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what design criteria are and why they need to cover multiple dimensions including sustainability

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least three dimensions that design criteria should address
  • Explains why criteria-based evaluation is more useful than personal preference

Prior knowledge

Students can negotiate design criteria, including sustainability, to evaluate design ideas, processes and solutions (AC9TDE6P04). They have used given or negotiated criteria but have not yet developed criteria collaboratively from scratch, incorporated Safety by Design principles, or used evaluation to drive iterative modification of processes.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least three dimensions that design criteria should address (e.g. accessibility, aesthetics, functionality, sustainability); explain why evaluating against criteria is more useful than evaluating by personal preference
Typical
Explain how criteria across different dimensions can complement or conflict; identify Safety by Design principles (prevention, protection, proactive change) as a specific criteria framework
Wider
  • Apply life cycle assessment as a criteria framework — how does evaluating across the full product lifecycle change what criteria are prioritised?
  • Limited width — understanding what criteria are is a single conceptual insight. The breadth comes from applying criteria to different design challenges at later nodes.

Hinge

Students who develop vague or single-dimension criteria at Node 2 will produce evaluations at Nodes 3–5 that miss trade-offs between dimensions — the multi-dimensional criteria framework must be understood before it can be applied collaboratively or used to drive iteration.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who develop vague criteria ('make it good') or single-dimension criteria ('make it sustainable') will produce evaluations that miss important trade-offs. Understanding that criteria must be specific, multi-dimensional and assessable is the foundation for useful evaluation at Nodes 2 and 3.
