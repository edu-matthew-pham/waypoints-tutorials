Applying criteria to analyse existing products

AC9TDE8P04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 5 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can apply collaboratively developed criteria to evaluate existing designed solutions and extract insights that inform their own design brief.

Success criteria

  • Evaluates at least one existing product against collaboratively developed criteria
  • Identifies at least one design insight that will inform their own solution

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Developing criteria collaboratively.

Width

Minimum
Evaluate at least one existing product against two collaboratively developed criteria; identify at least one design insight that will inform your own solution.
Typical
Evaluate multiple existing products against all developed criteria; compare what works and what doesn't; document insights as design constraints or opportunities for your own solution.
Wider
  • Compare existing products from different cultural or geographic contexts against the same criteria — how does context change what 'good design' means?
  • Evaluate products at different price or quality tiers and explain how criteria satisfaction varies.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkApplying criteria to existing products requires students to reason from evidence: criterion → what does the product do → does it satisfy the criterion → what does this tell us about our own design. Students who describe products without applying criteria produce observations rather than insights.
