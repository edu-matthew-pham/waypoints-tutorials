Testing components, tools and processes

AC9TDE4P01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can test components, tools or processes and describe what testing reveals about which are needed for a designed solution

Success criteria

  • Tests at least one component, tool or process
  • Connects the test finding to a specific design decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Testing materials for suitability.

Width

Minimum
Test one tool, component or process and describe how the test informed a design decision
Typical
Test a range of components, tools, equipment and processes for suitability; use test results alongside material testing to make decisions about what is needed to create the designed solution; document test findings
Wider
  • Test and compare joining techniques (adhesives, stitching, fasteners) for a specific design purpose — evaluate which technique best balances strength, aesthetics and ease of use, and justify the choice
  • Explore how virtual reality or simulations can be used to test assembling processes before physical production — discuss what testing digitally reveals and what it does not

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierTesting components and processes alongside materials (node 2) into a coherent set of design decisions is the integration challenge. Students often treat material testing and tool/process testing as separate activities — this node asks them to use all test findings together to inform what the solution will need.
