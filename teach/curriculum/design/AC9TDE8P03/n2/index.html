Justifying selections

AC9TDE8P03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 5 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can justify their material, tool and process selections by explaining why each choice is the most appropriate option for the design requirements

Success criteria

  • Justifies at least one selection by comparing against an alternative
  • Explains why the chosen option better meets the design requirements

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Selecting materials and processes.

Width

Minimum
Justify at least one selection by comparing it against an alternative; explain why the chosen option better meets the requirements
Typical
Justify selections across the design — materials, tools and processes — by referencing test results, properties, sustainability considerations and fitness for purpose; acknowledge trade-offs made
Wider
  • Use online tutorials or digital tools to develop expertise with an unfamiliar technique, then justify why the new technique is more appropriate than a familiar one
  • Limited width — justification is the same analytical skill regardless of what is being justified. The breadth comes from the variety of materials and contexts, which is captured at Node 1.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepJustification is the command term shift from 5-6 ('select and use') to 7-8 ('select, justify and use'). Students who can select but not justify will make reasonable choices they cannot explain — which means they cannot improve their choices based on feedback or transfer their reasoning to new contexts. Seeing justification modelled across different types of selections (materials, tools, processes) helps build the habit.
