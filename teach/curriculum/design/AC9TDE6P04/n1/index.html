Negotiating design criteria collaboratively

AC9TDE6P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can collaborate to develop and agree on design criteria that will be used to evaluate a designed solution — including at least one sustainability criterion

Success criteria

  • Contributes at least one criterion to a collaboratively developed set
  • Criteria set includes at least one sustainability criterion
  • Each criterion is specific enough to be applied when evaluating a solution

Prior knowledge

Students can use given or co-developed design criteria including sustainability to evaluate design ideas and solutions (AC9TDE4P04). They have applied provided or jointly created criteria. They have not yet negotiated criteria — the new demand is taking ownership of developing the criteria collaboratively, including sustainability as a non-optional factor.

Width

Minimum
Collaboratively develop at least three design criteria for a specific designed solution, including at least one sustainability criterion; explain how each criterion connects to the design's purpose
Typical
Decide on design criteria collaboratively — discussing what the solution must achieve, what sustainability means in this context (e.g. recyclable, low waste, durable), and how to make criteria specific enough to be testable; develop criteria that distinguish between requirements and preferences
Wider
  • Develop design criteria for a beeswax wrap product — negotiating which material properties, tool requirements and sustainability factors to include as criteria, and explaining trade-offs between competing criteria
  • Develop criteria for a community fundraising event — including waste reduction, attendance, funds raised and consideration of how to improve future events; explain how each criterion was negotiated among the group
  • Include a Safety by Design criterion in the criteria set — discussing what prevention, protection or proactive change would look like as a measurable design requirement

Hinge

Negotiated criteria are the prerequisite for N2 — if students have not developed criteria, they have nothing to evaluate against. N2's systematic evaluation task cannot proceed without the output of N1.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents negotiating criteria for the first time tend to produce vague or unmeasurable criteria (e.g. 'it should be good'). The criteria development scaffold — what must the solution do? → how will we know it's done? → what counts as sustainable here? — produces criteria that can actually be used for evaluation.
