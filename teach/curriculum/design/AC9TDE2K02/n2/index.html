← AC9TDE2K02 Materials affect movement

Combining materials and forces to create movement

AC9TDE2K02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how combining materials with forces creates movement in a product

Success criteria

  • Identifies both a material and a force involved in a product's movement
  • Explains how the material and force combine to produce the movement

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Material properties and movement.

Width

Minimum
Using one product (e.g. a toy boat, kite or puppet), identify the materials used and explain how force and material combine to create movement
Typical
Manipulate materials using tools and techniques to create different types of movement across multiple products; explore how combining materials with forces (push, pull, gravity, wind) creates and controls movement; explain the relationship between materials, forces and movement
Wider
  • Explore a marionette or Indonesian wayang kulit shadow puppet — identify how combining materials with forces creates movement; explain how the design enables the operator to control the movement
  • Construct a toy boat that floats or a kite that flies — select materials deliberately for their movement-enabling properties and explain the material and force choices

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierAt this node, material properties (node 1) and forces must be coordinated simultaneously to understand movement in a product. Neither alone explains movement — it is the interaction of material properties with applied forces that produces the outcome. Integration failure looks like: identifying the material or the force separately without explaining how they combine.
