Material properties and movement

AC9TDE2K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify how a material's properties affect how a product moves

Success criteria

  • Names at least one material property
  • Explains how that property affects how a product moves

Prior knowledge

Students can explore how products are designed by people (AC9TDEFK01). They have made simple solutions using materials (AC9TDEFP01). They have not yet systematically connected material properties to movement outcomes, or explored how changing materials or forces changes how a product moves.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least one material property (e.g. smooth, rough, flexible, rigid) and explain how it affects movement in a product or on a surface
Typical
Explore multiple material properties (smooth/rough, light/heavy, flexible/rigid) and explain how each affects movement; select materials to show how properties are appropriate for particular movement outcomes (sliding, floating, flying)
Wider
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' instructive toys and how they are designed and made to produce movement — for example propeller toys made from pandanus across northern Australia; identify which materials are used and how their properties create the movement
  • Test a wheeled toy on different surfaces (timber, carpet, rubber, plastic) — measure or observe differences in movement and speed; explain how surface material properties affect movement

Hinge

Without the material-property concept, Node 2's integration of material properties with forces has no anchor — students who cannot generalise from specific examples to a property cannot reason about how property and force interact.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents at Y1-2 may connect a specific material to a specific toy without generalising to the property relationship — 'the smooth floor makes it go fast' without understanding that smoothness (low friction) is the relevant property. Multiple material-movement contexts where the same property appears in different products builds the generalised property concept.
