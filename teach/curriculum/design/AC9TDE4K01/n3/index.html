Factors that influence design outcomes

AC9TDE4K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can examine a product, service or environment and identify factors (beyond sustainability) that influenced its design

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one design factor beyond sustainability
  • Explains how that factor influenced a specific design decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Sustainability as a design factor.

Width

Minimum
Examine one product or environment and identify at least one factor other than sustainability that influenced how it was designed (e.g. who it is for, available materials, cost, culture)
Typical
Examine a range of products and environments to discover the factors that shaped their design — including community need, material availability, cultural context, energy efficiency and aesthetic considerations; explain how these factors interact
Wider
  • Examine products from an Asian country alongside Australian products — compare the design factors each reflects (materials, cultural context, production methods) and discuss why similar needs are met with different designs
  • Examine a system or service proposed for improvement (e.g. a water-saving bathroom system, a school traffic management system) — analyse what factors constrain the design and what factors offer opportunity for change

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents at this level can identify features of a product but need scaffolding to move from observation ('it has wheels') to design factor analysis ('wheels were chosen because the product needs to move over different surfaces'). Structured examination routines help students develop analytical habits.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates occupational knowledge (node 1) with sustainability knowledge (node 2) into a complete factor analysis — students are now examining how multiple factors interact to shape a design outcome. The coordination across factors is the learning.
