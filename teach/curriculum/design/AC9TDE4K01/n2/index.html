Sustainability as a design factor

AC9TDE4K01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what sustainability means in a design context and give an example of a design decision that reflects sustainability considerations

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Names at least one sustainability factor (e.g. recycled materials, minimal waste, energy use)
  • Connects that factor to a specific design decision

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Design and technologies occupations.

Width

Minimum
Describe one example of a design decision that was shaped by sustainability (e.g. using recycled materials, minimal water use) and explain why the designer made that choice
Typical
Explain how Australian designers consider sustainability when designing products, services and environments; describe examples across different design types (products from recycled materials, services using minimal energy, landscapes requiring minimal water)
Wider
  • Examine how First Nations Australians use sustainable practices when designing and producing products from local materials — explore how sustainability is embedded in traditional design knowledge and how this knowledge continues today
  • Investigate a specific sustainability challenge in the local community (e.g. water saving, traffic management, accessibility) and examine what design factors would need to be considered to address it

Hinge

Students without a working understanding of sustainability as a design factor cannot complete the multi-factor analysis in N3 — sustainability is one of the named factors and must be identified alongside others.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often reduce sustainability to recycling. Examining sustainability across different design domains — products, services, environments, energy, water, materials — builds a broader and more accurate understanding of what sustainability means as a design consideration.
