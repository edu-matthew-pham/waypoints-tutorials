← AC9TDE4P02 Generate and communicate design ideas

Using technical terms and annotations

AC9TDE4P02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can label a design drawing using appropriate technical terms and explain what each component is and does

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Uses at least two technical terms correctly in annotations
  • Annotations explain function, not just name the part

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Generating design ideas through sketching and modelling.

Width

Minimum
Label at least one design drawing with technical terms for two or more components, with a brief explanation of each
Typical
Communicate design ideas using annotated diagrams with technical terms for all major components; include labels and descriptions explaining materials used, their properties, and the intended function of components or the whole system
Wider
  • Annotate a design for a more complex system (e.g. a pushcart with chassis, axle, wheels and steering) using technical terms for each component — explain not just what each part is but why it was designed that way
  • Research and apply technical terms from a related design field (e.g. engineering, textile design) to annotate a student design — discuss how technical language helps communicate design decisions to different audiences

Hinge

Technical annotation and attributions are required components of the N3 digital communication task — students who have not consolidated annotation practice cannot produce the complete communication the descriptor requires.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsTechnical vocabulary in design communication needs fluency — students who can label a design correctly and consistently are better positioned for the more complex communication tasks in subsequent years. Precision and accuracy in annotation matters more than breadth of context here.
