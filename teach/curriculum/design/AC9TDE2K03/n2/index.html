How and where plants and animals are grown

AC9TDE2K03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how and where plants and animals are grown for food, clothing and shelter in Australia

Success criteria

  • Names at least one location in Australia where a plant or animal is grown
  • Describes how it is grown or managed for its purpose

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Plants and animals as sources for products.

Width

Minimum
Identify where at least one plant or animal is grown in Australia and describe how it is grown or managed for a purpose
Typical
Explore how plants and animals are grown in different Australian regions for different purposes — citrus in South Australia, cotton in NSW, timber in Tasmania; describe how tools and equipment are used in growing and harvesting; connect growing methods to the product produced
Wider
  • Compare traditional and contemporary growing methods for the same product — for example hand tools versus GPS-guided tractors for wheat; identify how technology changes what is possible
  • Consider a range of tools used to grow plants for a purpose — from home garden tools (spade, rake) to large-scale equipment (seed spreader, drone); explain what each tool enables and why it is used

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often have a home-garden mental model for how plants are grown — small, familiar, manual. Encountering growing at different scales (home garden vs cotton farm vs forestry plantation) in different Australian regions builds a more accurate and varied understanding of food and materials production.
