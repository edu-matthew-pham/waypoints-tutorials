Plants and animals as sources for products

AC9TDE2K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify which plants and animals provide food, materials for clothing, or materials for shelter

Success criteria

  • Names at least one plant and one animal used as a source for food, clothing or shelter
  • Identifies at least one product derived from each source

Prior knowledge

Students have explored how products are designed and produced to meet community needs (AC9TDE2K01). They understand that design decisions connect to purpose. They have not yet examined the origins of the materials used in products — specifically that plants and animals are deliberately grown and managed as sources for food, clothing and shelter.

Width

Minimum
Sort at least three familiar items into plant-source or animal-source categories; name one plant and one animal used as a source for food, clothing or shelter
Typical
Identify a range of plants and animals as sources across food, clothing and shelter categories; give examples of products derived from each source; explain the relationship between the plant or animal and the product
Wider
  • Explore how First Nations Australians grow plants and animals for food — for example the aquaculture industry of the Kombumerri Peoples farming mangrove worms, or land transformation through terraces at Wagadagam; identify specific plants or animals and the products they provide
  • Identify products that can be designed and produced from plants and animals — food products, paper and wood products, fabrics and yarns; trace one product from source to finished item

Hinge

Without a populated repertoire of plant and animal sources, Node 2 (how and where they are grown) has no examples to explore — students cannot investigate growing methods for sources they have not yet identified.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents need a range of plant-source and animal-source examples before they can generalise the concept that living things are grown and managed for production purposes. A single example (cows for milk) does not build the category. Accumulating examples across food, clothing and shelter contexts builds the breadth needed for the 'explore' command term.
