Managing time and resource allocation

AC9TDE4P05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify roles and resources needed in a collaborative production task and explain how these are managed to complete the task

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Names at least two roles in a collaborative task
  • Identifies the resources each role needs to complete its tasks

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Planning and recording production steps.

Width

Minimum
In a collaborative making task, identify at least two roles and the resources each role needs; contribute to a plan that allocates tasks
Typical
Discuss the importance of managing time and resources throughout production; identify roles different people might take in a team; map tasks to roles and identify the resources each needs; contribute to a shared plan
Wider
  • Plan a collaborative production event (e.g. food distribution for a community event) — identify all roles, resources and the sequence of tasks; discuss how delays or resource shortages in one role affect others
  • Reflect on a completed collaborative task — identify where time or resource management broke down and propose how the plan could be improved for next time

Hinge

Students who cannot reason about role dependencies and resource needs cannot coordinate a collaborative production in N3 — they will block other roles or run out of resources mid-task.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkResource and role allocation requires students to reason about dependencies — what does each person need, and what happens if a resource is unavailable? Scaffolding this reasoning (identify role → identify resource needed → identify what could block progress) builds production planning thinking rather than just task-listing.
