← AC9TDE2P01 Describe, draw and model ideas

Communicating design ideas through labelled drawings and modelling

AC9TDE2P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can communicate a design idea through a labelled 2D drawing or model, including showing different views and describing materials and features

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces a drawing or model that others can interpret
  • Includes at least one label or description of a feature or material

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Generating ideas from existing products.

Width

Minimum
Produce a drawing or model of a design idea that includes at least one label or description of a feature
Typical
Produce labelled 2D drawings of design ideas using different views (top and side); describe materials and features; produce a model or drawing that someone else could understand; include dimensions or scale where appropriate
Wider
  • Design a new environment (e.g. cubbyhouse or animal shelter) — show different views (top and side) with descriptions of materials and features for each view
  • Design felt finger puppets — communicate design ideas with labels showing how movable parts work; include notes about changes made to the original idea and why

Hinge

Labelled drawings are the communication vehicle for Node 3's digital tool integration — without fluency in producing interpretable labelled drawings, there is no design idea for students to communicate using digital tools.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDrawing with labels and showing multiple views requires spatial reasoning and drawing skills that develop through repeated practice. Students need to practise producing labelled drawings across different design contexts before the combination of views, labels and descriptions becomes fluent.
