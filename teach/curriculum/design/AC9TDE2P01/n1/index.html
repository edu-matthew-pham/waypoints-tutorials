Generating ideas from existing products

AC9TDE2P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can compare and contrast features of existing products to generate new design ideas

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one feature of an existing product
  • Generates a design idea that connects to or builds on what was observed

Prior knowledge

Students can generate and communicate design ideas through drawing or modelling (AC9TDEFP01). They have communicated ideas for a purpose. They have not yet labelled drawings, shown multiple views, used digital tools to communicate design ideas, or compared existing products to generate new ideas.

Width

Minimum
Examine at least one existing product and identify at least one feature that could inform a new design idea
Typical
Compare and contrast features of multiple existing products to develop new ideas; identify what works well and what could be different; use observations to generate at least one design idea that builds on or modifies what they have seen
Wider
  • Compare features of several toys with movable parts — identify what makes the movement work; use this to design a new product (e.g. a puppet) with a movable part inspired by observations
  • Record the results from exploring design ideas (e.g. from people taste-testing a food product) — use the results to inform a revised design idea

Hinge

Without analytical observation of existing products to generate design ideas, Node 2's labelled drawings have no design rationale — students who copy rather than adapt cannot produce drawings that explain design decisions.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkGenerating ideas from existing products requires students to reason analytically before generating — identifying which features matter and why. Without this scaffold, students copy rather than adapt, or generate ideas unconnected to what they observed. A sequence (observe → identify what works → identify what could change → generate new idea) builds design thinking rather than copying.
