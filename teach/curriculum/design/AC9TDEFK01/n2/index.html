Exploring design decisions and purpose

AC9TDEFK01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Foundation Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explore how a product, service or environment is designed for a purpose — identifying at least one design decision and why it was made

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one design decision in a product or environment
  • Connects that decision to a purpose — explains why it was designed that way

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Recognising that products and environments are designed by people.

Width

Minimum
For one familiar product or environment, identify one design decision and explain what purpose it serves
Typical
Explore design decisions across multiple products, services and environments; explain how design decisions (materials, shape, features) serve the purpose; ask questions about why things were designed the way they were
Wider
  • Ask questions about the design of products from the local store — why was particular packaging chosen? how do the text and images attract people's attention? connect design decisions to purpose
  • Explore how community gardens, public swimming pools and parks are designed to help people stay healthy — identify specific design decisions and the purposes they serve
  • Explore how an online or delivery service is designed to meet people's needs — describe how gift packages are sent between locations and how design decisions make this possible

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkConnecting a design decision to its purpose requires students to reason from feature to intent — 'the shade structure is there because...' rather than just noticing the shade structure exists. Scaffolding this with a question structure (what is it? who uses it? what does this part do? why?) builds the reasoning habit before the Y1-2 descriptor asks students to connect design to community need and sustainability.
