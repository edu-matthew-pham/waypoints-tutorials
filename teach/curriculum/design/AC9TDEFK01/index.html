Designed products and environments

AC9TDEFK01 · Foundation Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

explore how familiar products, services and environments are designed by people

Prior knowledge

This is the first Design and Technologies descriptor in the curriculum. Students have no prior DT knowledge assumed. They bring general familiarity with everyday products and environments. They have not yet connected objects or places to the idea that people deliberately designed and made them for a purpose.

By the end

Students explore how familiar products, services and environments are designed by people, identifying that design decisions are made for a purpose.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • identifying how First Nations Australians have long designed and produced domestic items including clothing, tools and shelter, for example the Lamalama Peoples of the eastern Cape York Peninsula weave the reddish coloured fibres from Acacia latifolia alternately with white coloured fibres from Brachychiton diversifolium to produce a striped woven bag
  • exploring how local delivery services meet different needs of people, for example describing how gift packages can be sent to and from people who live in different locations and how online shopping items arrive at a person's home
  • exploring how an environment such as a local playground may have shade structures to protect users and be designed to allow access for all
  • describing how community gardens, public swimming pools and parks are designed to help people stay healthy
  • asking questions about the design of products from the local store, for example why certain packaging materials might have been selected, and how people design the text and images on the packaging to attract people's attention

Waypoints

  • 1. Recognising that products and environments are designed by peopleHingeStudents can identify familiar products and environments and recognise that they were designed and made by people
  • 2. Exploring design decisions and purposeStudents can explore how a product, service or environment is designed for a purpose — identifying at least one design decision and why it was made
