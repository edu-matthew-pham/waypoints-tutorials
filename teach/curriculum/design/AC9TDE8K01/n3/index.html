Analysing ethics and sustainability together in design occupations

AC9TDE8K01 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse how people in design occupations weigh ethical and sustainability factors together when designing and producing products, services or environments

Success criteria

  • Analyses how at least one ethical and one sustainability factor together shape a design decision
  • Does not treat ethics and sustainability as separate checklists — explains how they interact

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Sustainability as a design constraint.

Width

Minimum
Given a design scenario, analyse how at least one ethical and one sustainability factor together influence the design decisions made
Typical
Compare how ethical and sustainability factors are balanced in design across Australia and a country in Asia; identify where factors compete and how designers navigate the tension
Wider
  • Compare design and production across additional industries or country pairs beyond the core Australia-Asia examples
  • Investigate enterprise opportunities that arise when ethical and sustainable design creates a competitive advantage

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to bring ethical factors (Node 1) and sustainability constraints (Node 2) together into a single analysis. Students who have treated them as separate checklists produce analyses where the two factors coexist but don't interact. The integration task reveals whether students understand that ethical and sustainability considerations can reinforce, trade off against, or constrain each other.
