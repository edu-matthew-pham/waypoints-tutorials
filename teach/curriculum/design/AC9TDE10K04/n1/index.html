Emerging production technologies and methods

AC9TDE10K04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse emerging production technologies in terms of productivity, profitability and sustainability, including their ethical implications.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific production technology and correctly assesses at least one sustainability or ethical consideration
  • Goes beyond description — makes a comparative or evaluative judgement about the technology's implications

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse how food and fibre are produced in managed environments and how food and fibre production systems are affected by innovation and sustainability requirements (AC9TDE8K04). They can identify production methods and sustainability considerations. They have not yet made judgements on ethics and security in production and marketing, nor analysed food and water security, digital production tools, or marketing chain impacts.

Width

Minimum
Describe one emerging production technology or method (e.g. protected cropping, hydroponics, aquaculture) and identify one productivity benefit and one sustainability or ethical consideration.
Typical
Analyse emerging production technologies and methods — protected cropping, hydroponics, aquaculture, precision livestock systems — evaluating productivity, profitability and sustainability for each; include animal welfare considerations where relevant; explain how different production systems involve different trade-offs.
Wider
  • Analyse grain sources used by First Nations Australians (e.g. acacia) for their nutrient content and suitability as sustainable food sources in drought-prone regions — compare with conventional cereal crops for productivity and sustainability
  • Investigate precision agriculture tools (GPS tracking, crop sensors, automated feeding, drones for weed management) and evaluate how digital technologies change the ethics and sustainability profile of production systems
  • Compare intensive and extensive production systems for a specific animal product — evaluate environmental impact, animal welfare and productivity trade-offs

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepProduction technology trade-offs look different across production types — crop-based systems have different ethical and sustainability tensions than livestock systems, and digital tools introduce different considerations again. Multiple contexts build a more complete analytical picture than a single production type.
