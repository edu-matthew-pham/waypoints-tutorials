Matching materials and techniques to the design intention

AC9TDE6P03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select materials and techniques that match the design intention and explain why each choice is appropriate

Success criteria

  • Selects materials and techniques appropriate for the design intention
  • Explains the selection by referencing at least one relevant material property or technique characteristic

Prior knowledge

Students can select and use materials, components, tools, equipment and techniques to safely make designed solutions (AC9TDE4P03). They have selected and used tools and materials with guidance. They have not yet matched selection decisions to design intention with explicit justification, or applied the full range of safety, hygiene and environmental considerations across material types.

Width

Minimum
Select a material and technique for a specific task and explain why each matches the design intention — referencing at least one material property
Typical
Choose appropriate materials, tools, equipment and techniques for a specific design purpose; explain how the selection matches the design intention — considering function, aesthetics and sustainability; demonstrate that the selection produces the intended result
Wider
  • Match material and joining techniques to the design intention for a textile project (e.g. a community banner, a fabric bag) — accurately cutting and joining fabric pieces as specified by the design
  • Select and sequence materials, tools and equipment for an electronic product — explaining how each component choice supports the circuit's intended function
  • Select and justify materials for a water-resistant, floating craft — explaining which material properties (density, water resistance, structural strength) determine the selection

Hinge

Material and technique selection in N1 determines what safety practices apply in N2 — students who cannot match materials to design intention will select inappropriate tools or techniques, making N2's safety and hygiene requirements impossible to apply correctly.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents typically select familiar materials by habit rather than matching properties to design intention. Multiple production contexts — textiles, electronics, food, structural — each requiring different property reasoning help students build the habit of justifying selection against design requirements rather than defaulting to familiarity.
