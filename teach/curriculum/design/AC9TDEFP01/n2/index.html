Communicating design ideas

AC9TDEFP01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Foundation Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can communicate a design idea through drawing or modelling

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Produces a drawing or model that represents their design idea
  • Can explain at least one feature of the drawing or model

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Identifying a purpose and generating ideas.

Width

Minimum
Represent a design idea through at least one drawing or model
Typical
Communicate a design idea using drawing or modelling; include basic features or labels; explain the design idea to others
Wider
  • Draw or model a ramp design for rolling a ball or toy car — show key features and explain how the design serves the purpose
  • Model a design for a greenhouse or trellis using available materials — describe what each part is for

Hinge

The communicated design idea is the raw material for Node 3 making — without an externalised representation, making is undirected and cannot be checked against a design intent. Node 3 requires something to make toward.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsDesign communication at Foundation requires students to externalise a mental idea into a physical representation. This is a challenging early skill — the gap between what students imagine and what they can draw or model is significant. Repeated practice across different design contexts builds fluency in using drawing/modelling as a thinking and communication tool.
