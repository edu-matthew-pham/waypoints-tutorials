Identifying a purpose and generating ideas

AC9TDEFP01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Foundation Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify a purpose for designing and making, and generate at least one idea for a solution

Success criteria

  • States a purpose for their design (what it is for or what problem it solves)
  • Generates at least one idea for a solution to that purpose

Prior knowledge

This is the first Design and Technologies process descriptor. Students have no prior design process experience assumed. They bring general play and construction experience. They have not yet framed design as purposeful — generating ideas for a specific purpose, communicating those ideas in a design form, making a solution, or evaluating against a purpose.

Width

Minimum
Identify a purpose for a design task (what problem or need is being addressed); generate at least one idea for a solution
Typical
Identify a purpose for designing and making; generate and explore multiple ideas through drawing, modelling or describing; choose the most suitable idea with a reason
Wider
  • Identify a real purpose from the school environment (e.g. sand blowing out of sandpit, birds getting into waste bins, accessibility needs at school) — generate ideas that directly address the identified purpose
  • Draw or model designs for bee hotels to attract native bees to the school garden — generate multiple ideas and choose one, changing perspectives from front view to plan view

Hinge

Without a purpose-grounded design idea, Node 2 (communicating a design idea) and Node 3 (making) have no design intent to express or realise — purposeless communication and making is construction play, not design.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepFoundation students readily generate ideas but often skip connecting them to a purpose — the idea exists without a 'what is it for'. Multiple design contexts where the purpose is clearly defined before generating ideas builds the habit of purpose-grounded design thinking rather than open-ended construction play.
