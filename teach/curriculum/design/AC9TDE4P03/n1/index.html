Selecting materials with reasons

AC9TDE4P03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 3–4 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select materials and components for a design purpose and give reasons for their choices that reference material properties or sustainability

Success criteria

  • Names at least two materials chosen for the design
  • Gives at least one reason for each choice that references a property or sustainability factor

Prior knowledge

Students can use materials, components, tools, equipment and techniques to safely make designed solutions (AC9TDE2P02). They have used materials and tools in a supervised context. They have not yet selected materials with justified reasons, considered environmental impact across the production process, or used appropriate technologies terminology to describe procedures.

Width

Minimum
Select at least two materials for a design task and give one reason for each choice (referencing a property or sustainability consideration)
Typical
Select materials, components and joining techniques for design tasks with reasons; consider environmental impact in selections — for example whether packaging and offcuts can be recycled; explain selections using technologies terms
Wider
  • Select materials with explicit sustainability consideration — compare materials by compostability, recyclability and durability before choosing; document the selection reasoning
  • Explore material choices from products made in an Asian country (e.g. bamboo, different timber types, natural fibres) — compare to materials typically available locally and discuss how material availability shapes design decisions

Hinge

Students who select materials without reasoning about properties make production decisions that can compromise function and safety — arbitrary selection cannot be remedied in N2 and N3 because material choices are already locked in.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents often select materials based on appearance or familiarity rather than properties relevant to function and sustainability. Scaffolding the selection reasoning (identify the design requirement → identify which property matters → check the material has that property) builds deliberate material selection practice.
