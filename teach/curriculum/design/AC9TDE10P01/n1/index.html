Analysing needs and design opportunities

AC9TDE10P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse a need or opportunity for designing, including considering the needs of diverse users and communities.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Identifies a specific need grounded in user or context analysis — not just a product category
  • Describes at least two distinct user needs that the designed solution must address

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse needs or opportunities for designing, and investigate and select materials, systems, components, tools and equipment (AC9TDE8P01). They can develop basic design briefs. They have not yet developed design briefs independently that incorporate universal design or Safety by Design principles, nor analysed intellectual property rights in design contexts, nor interviewed community members to identify design needs.

Width

Minimum
Identify a genuine need or design opportunity from a given scenario; describe at least two user needs that a designed solution should address.
Typical
Analyse a need or opportunity by investigating users, context and constraints; identify how different users (including diverse or community groups) have different needs; consider how sustainability, accessibility and Safety by Design principles shape what the designed solution must achieve.
Wider
  • Analyse First Nations Australians' traditional grains (e.g. acacia) for their commercial and nutritional potential — develop a design opportunity statement that responds to this as a design brief starting point
  • Interview community members about accessibility requirements to identify an unmet need — document what the interviews revealed that desk research would not have found
  • Analyse new products designed to address sustainability issues (e.g. pool sanitisation, UV disinfection, disposable alternatives) — evaluate how well each responds to the identified need

Hinge

Shallow needs analysis produces a design brief at N2 that does not capture real user and sustainability requirements — a brief built on assumed needs drives design criteria and material selection at N3 that cannot be validated against actual stakeholder needs.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepNeed analysis looks different for different user groups and contexts — a community accessibility need is identified differently from a sustainability opportunity or a commercial product gap. Multiple contexts build analytical flexibility in need identification.
