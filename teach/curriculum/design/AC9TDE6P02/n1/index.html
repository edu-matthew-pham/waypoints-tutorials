Generating design ideas using prior knowledge and research

AC9TDE6P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can generate a range of design ideas for a product, service or environment drawing on prior knowledge, skills and research

Success criteria

  • Generates at least two meaningfully distinct design ideas
  • Ideas are grounded in at least one source of prior knowledge or research

Prior knowledge

Students can generate and communicate design ideas and decisions using appropriate attributions, technical terms and graphical representation techniques (AC9TDE4P02). They have generated and communicated ideas. They have not yet iterated — systematically refining designs based on evaluation — or used the full range of representation techniques including scale, symbols, digital mapping and modelling tools.

Width

Minimum
Generate at least two distinct design ideas for the same need, drawing on prior knowledge and at least one research source
Typical
Generate a range of design ideas for products, services or environments using prior knowledge, skills and research; represent ideas through sketches, diagrams or models; consider functional, aesthetic and sustainability dimensions across ideas
Wider
  • Generate design ideas for an eco-friendly alternative to a non-recyclable product used in a community event — researching what sustainable alternatives exist before generating original ideas
  • Generate design ideas for an animal enclosure using modelling applications — iterating the layout and features based on the animal's environmental and behavioural needs
  • Consider the social values and ethics of clients when designing an environment — interviewing users of a space or seeking permission for designs/images from others, including respect for cultural and intellectual property

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyStudents who only ever design the first thing they think of have a narrow ideation repertoire. Accumulated experience generating ideas across different product, service and environment types — each with different constraints — builds the range students can draw on when a novel design problem is encountered.
