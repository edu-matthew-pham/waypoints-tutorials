← AC9TDE10K03 Materials, force, motion and energy in engineered systems

Force, motion and energy interactions in systems

AC9TDE10K03 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how force, motion and energy interact in a specific engineered system, identifying how each component contributes to system control.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two components in a system and explains how each contributes to system function
  • Explains how force, motion and energy interact — not just lists components

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Material properties in engineered system design.

Width

Minimum
Describe how force, motion and energy interact in one engineered system (e.g. a crane, a car safety system, a 3D printer); explain the function of at least two components.
Typical
Analyse how a complex engineered system combines force, motion and energy — explaining the function of multiple components, how they interact, and how the system achieves its purpose efficiently; examine at least one system with safety-critical requirements (e.g. vehicle safety features).
Wider
  • Analyse the force, motion and energy interactions in vehicle safety features (seatbelts, airbags, crumple zones) — explain how each feature controls force transfer to the occupant and why the combination is more effective than any single feature
  • Investigate how wind turbine placement in a wind farm affects performance — explain the force and energy relationships that determine optimal layout within a given space

Hinge

Students who cannot explain how force, motion and energy interact in a system cannot calculate forces correctly at N3 (they don't know what they're calculating) or critique system effectiveness at N4 (they cannot reason about what makes the combination efficient or not).

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepForce, motion and energy interactions are best understood through contrasting system types. Safety-critical systems (crumple zones), energy harvesting (wind turbines) and mechanical production (3D printers) each foreground different aspects of the interaction and together build a more complete analytical framework.
