Material properties in engineered system design

AC9TDE10K03 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how specific material characteristics and properties are selected to perform roles in engineered systems.

Success criteria

  • Correctly names at least two material properties and connects each to a specific functional role
  • Explains why the combination of properties is needed — not just listing each separately

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse how force, motion and energy are used to manipulate and control engineered systems (AC9TDE8K03). They can identify material properties in functional contexts. They have not yet made judgements on combinations of material properties with force, motion and energy, nor calculated structural loads or critiqued system effectiveness.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two material properties (e.g. rigidity, flexibility, hardness, tensile strength) and explain why each is needed for a specific function in an engineered system.
Typical
Analyse how a range of material properties — rigidity, flexibility, hardness, tensile/compressive strength, weight — are combined in a specific engineered system to meet functional requirements; explain why no single material property is sufficient and how combinations address competing demands.
Wider
  • Investigate the engineering of First Nations Australian tools such as spearthrowers — analyse how material properties (rigidity, flexibility, hardness) are exploited in their design and explain the biomechanical advantage achieved
  • Compare how the same functional requirement (e.g. lightweight strength) is met differently in aerospace, civil engineering and sports equipment — explain what different constraints drive different material selections

Hinge

Material property identification at N1 is prerequisite for N2 — analysing how force, motion and energy interact with materials requires correctly knowing which properties are relevant. Misidentified properties produce incorrect system analysis.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyMaterial property recognition needs to be built across multiple material-system pairs before students can reliably select relevant properties for a novel engineered system. Accumulation across contexts (structural, mechanical, thermal) builds the property recognition schema.
