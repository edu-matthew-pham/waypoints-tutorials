← AC9TDE10K03 Materials, force, motion and energy in engineered systems

Calculating forces and making structural judgements

AC9TDE10K03 n3 · waypoint 3 of 4 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can calculate forces, reactions and loads in structures and make judgements about the relationship between material properties, forces and safety.

Success criteria

  • Correctly calculates at least one force, reaction or load with appropriate units
  • Uses the calculation to make a judgement about material requirements or structural safety

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Force, motion and energy interactions in systems.

Width

Minimum
Calculate at least one force, reaction or load in a simple structure; explain what the calculation tells you about safety or material requirements.
Typical
Calculate forces, reactions and loads in a structure (e.g. a simple bridge or truss); analyse the relationship between material properties, applied forces and safety margins; make judgements about whether a design is fit for purpose given the calculated loads.
Wider
  • Calculate forces in a bridge structure under load and make a judgement about whether a proposed material meets the safety requirements — explain what would happen if the material were substituted
  • Investigate how the placement and sizing of structural elements is optimised in a real engineered structure — explain how engineers balance material cost, safety factor and structural performance
  • Investigate how EV charger selection involves calculating electrical load requirements against vehicle specifications — apply quantitative reasoning to make a recommendation

Hinge

Force calculation errors at N3 directly produce incorrect structural judgements at N4 — a student who calculates a load incorrectly will judge an adequate structure as inadequate or vice versa.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsForce calculation requires procedural accuracy — sign conventions, free body diagrams, reaction forces. Students need sufficient practice with calculation procedures before they can apply them to make analytical judgements. Errors in calculation cascade into incorrect safety conclusions.
