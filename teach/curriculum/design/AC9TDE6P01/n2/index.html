Determining required materials, components and processes

AC9TDE6P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can determine what materials, components, tools, equipment and processes are needed to create a designed solution — and compare options

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two options for a material or component
  • Compares options using at least one criterion and justifies the selection

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Investigating a need or opportunity.

Width

Minimum
Identify at least two options for a key material or component and explain which is more appropriate for the design intention, considering at least one sustainability factor
Typical
Test or research materials, components, tools and equipment to determine which are appropriate for the design; compare options on multiple criteria including function, cost, availability, safety and environmental impact; explain how minimising material use and managing waste affects production decisions
Wider
  • Investigate how to minimise material use and manage waste by comparing the environmental and social impacts of alternative approaches — e.g. repurposing an old item of clothing to create a carry bag versus buying a new one
  • Deconstruct a product (e.g. a robotic weeder, a vacuum cleaner) to identify its components and explain the function of each — connecting component design to the product's overall purpose
  • Investigate complementary parts of a working system by identifying how each component's function depends on the others — explaining why changing one component would affect the whole system

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkStudents typically select materials based on familiarity rather than systematic comparison. The reasoning scaffold — identify options → compare on criteria → justify selection — makes material selection a design decision rather than a default choice.
