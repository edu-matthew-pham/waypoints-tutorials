Investigating a need or opportunity

AC9TDE6P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 2 · Years 5–6 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate a need or opportunity for designing by gathering information from multiple sources and identifying what a solution must achieve

Success criteria

  • Identifies a need or opportunity with evidence from at least two sources
  • States what a solution must achieve — not just what is wrong

Prior knowledge

Students can explore needs or opportunities for designing, and test materials, components, tools, equipment and processes to make designed solutions (AC9TDE4P01). They have explored and tested. They have not yet investigated systematically — the shift is from exploring/testing to purposeful investigation with a view to making design decisions.

Width

Minimum
Identify a need or opportunity and investigate it using at least two sources (e.g. surveying users, examining existing solutions, researching online) — summarising what a solution must achieve
Typical
Survey users in a school or community context to identify a need; investigate existing solutions from around the world to assess suitability; identify the key requirements a designed solution must meet — including functional, social and sustainability requirements
Wider
  • Investigate First Nations Australians' traditional fibre sources as potential commercial solutions for biodegradable string or rope — researching the materials, systems, components and processes needed to develop them commercially
  • Investigate small-space gardening solutions from different countries and judge their suitability for the local environment — explaining what factors determine suitability
  • Survey school community members about a specific need (e.g. more shade, a security system for the community garden, a community meal) — documenting needs and constraints that a designed solution must address

Hinge

Thin investigation in N1 cascades into every downstream phase — design ideas (P02), material selection (N2, P03), and evaluation criteria (P04) all depend on having accurately characterised what the solution must achieve. An underspecified need produces solutions that solve the wrong problem.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents often investigate a single, self-identified need without testing it against actual users. Multiple investigation contexts — each with different stakeholders and information sources — build the habit of grounding needs in evidence rather than assumption.
