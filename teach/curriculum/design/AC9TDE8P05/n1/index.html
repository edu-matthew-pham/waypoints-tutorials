Planning resources and steps from drawings

AC9TDE8P05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 6 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can interpret design drawings to identify the resources and production steps needed to produce a designed solution

Success criteria

  • Identifies materials, tools and steps from a drawing or specification
  • Lists steps in a logical production sequence

Prior knowledge

Students can develop project plans that include consideration of resources to individually and collaboratively make designed solutions (AC9TDE6P05). They have planned with resources in mind but have not yet managed time, cost and production as explicit planning variables, or used planning tools to track and evaluate progress.

Width

Minimum
Given a labelled drawing or specification, identify the materials, tools and production steps needed; list them in a logical sequence
Typical
Interpret drawings to collaboratively develop a detailed procedure; identify resource requirements from specifications including quantities, tools and equipment needed for each step
Wider
  • Limited width — interpreting drawings for production planning is a consistent skill. The breadth comes from the variety of products being planned, not from the interpretation process itself.

Hinge

Errors in interpreting drawings propagate into every subsequent node — incorrect resource lists at Node 1 produce inaccurate time and cost estimates at Node 2, incomplete risk registers at Node 3, and production steps that cannot be executed at Node 4.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsInterpreting technical drawings accurately requires practice — students need repeated experience reading specifications, identifying resource requirements and translating drawings into step sequences. Errors in interpretation propagate into production.
