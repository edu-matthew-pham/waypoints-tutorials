Making production more sustainable

AC9TDE8K04 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can evaluate how food and fibre production methods can become more sustainable and propose improvements based on analysis

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one way a production method could become more sustainable
  • Explains why the proposed change improves sustainability

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: Ethical and sustainable techniques.

Width

Minimum
Given a production method, identify at least one way it could become more sustainable; explain why the change would improve sustainability
Typical
Evaluate sustainability across the production lifecycle — sourcing, growing, harvesting, processing, transport; propose and justify improvements that balance sustainability with economic viability
Wider
  • Investigate traditional First Nations Australian food sources for potential species that offer sustainability benefits in contemporary production
  • Compare how different countries approach sustainable production of the same commodity — what works in one context and why it may not transfer directly

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkEvaluating how production can become more sustainable requires students to reason from evidence: identify current practice → identify the sustainability problem → propose a change → explain why the change improves sustainability → consider trade-offs with productivity. Students who propose changes without this reasoning chain produce aspirational statements rather than evaluations.
Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node integrates managed environment knowledge (Node 1) and ethical/sustainable technique analysis (Node 2) into a forward-looking evaluation. Students must apply both to propose improvements that are both technically feasible and genuinely more sustainable.
