← AC9TDE8P02 Test and refine design ideas

Generating ideas with multiple strategies

AC9TDE8P02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can use a variety of strategies to generate design ideas rather than committing to a first idea

Success criteria

  • Generates more than one design idea for the same brief
  • Communicates each idea clearly enough for someone else to give feedback

Prior knowledge

Students can generate, iterate and communicate design ideas, decisions and processes using technical terms and graphical representation techniques, including using digital tools (AC9TDE6P02). They have generated and communicated ideas but have not yet systematically tested ideas through prototyping, used multiple generation strategies, or produced formal production drawings with scale and symbols.

Width

Minimum
Use at least one strategy (e.g. brainstorming, sketching, experimenting) to generate multiple design ideas; communicate each idea clearly enough for feedback
Typical
Use multiple strategies (brainstorming, sketching, 3D modelling, experimenting) to generate innovative ideas; investigate benefits and drawbacks of ideas including factors that may help or hinder the project
Wider
  • Use digital polling or community consultation to capture diverse views and inform which ideas to develop further
  • Limited width — idea generation strategies are a finite set. The breadth comes from the variety of design challenges they are applied to.

Hinge

Students who only sketch produce variants of the same idea — without multiple generation strategies they arrive at Node 2 with a single design direction that cannot be meaningfully tested against alternatives, and at Node 3 with nothing substantive to communicate as a design process.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyIdea generation requires a repertoire of strategies before students can select the right one for a design challenge. Students who only sketch will produce variants of the same idea. Building familiarity with multiple generation strategies (brainstorming, sketching, experimenting, 3D modelling) is the prior condition for genuine alternative generation.
