Testing through models and prototypes

AC9TDE8P02 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can develop models, prototypes or samples to test the functionality of design ideas before committing to production

Success criteria

  • Builds a model or prototype of a design idea
  • Tests it for at least one functional requirement and records the result

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Generating ideas with multiple strategies.

Width

Minimum
Build a simple model or prototype of a design idea; test it for at least one functional requirement; record the result
Typical
Develop models or prototypes using a range of materials and tools (including rapid prototyping tools like 3D printers); test functionality against design criteria; explain what the test revealed
Wider
  • Develop prototypes at different fidelity levels — low-fidelity (cardboard, paper) vs high-fidelity (3D printed, digital) — and explain when each is appropriate in the design process
  • Test a prototype with potential users and incorporate their feedback into the iteration

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepStudents who skip testing and jump from idea to final production make design decisions based on assumption rather than evidence. Testing through prototyping builds the habit of evidence-based iteration. Seeing how different prototyping approaches (low vs high fidelity, different materials) reveal different kinds of information helps students understand that testing is a deliberate design strategy, not just a formality.
