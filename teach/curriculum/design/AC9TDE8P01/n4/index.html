← AC9TDE8P01 Analyse opportunities and select resources

Integrating analysis into design decisions

AC9TDE8P01 n4 · waypoint 4 of 4 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can demonstrate how their analysis of needs, investigation of resources, and justified selections come together to inform a coherent design direction.

Success criteria

  • The design direction shows a traceable link from the analysed need through investigation to resource selection
  • Can explain at least one decision that was shaped or changed by evidence gathered during investigation

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 3: Selecting and justifying resources.

Width

Minimum
Present a design direction that traces the path from analysed need through investigated options to selected resources, showing how each decision connects to the one before it.
Typical
Document the full analysis-to-selection process for a design task, including how sustainability considerations and community needs shaped resource decisions; identify one decision that changed after investigation and explain why.
Wider
  • Create a survey or consultation process to determine user needs (e.g. students' food choices for a school canteen) and use the findings to drive material and process selection for a designed solution.
  • Compare two viable design directions for the same need, each using different resource selections, and analyse the trade-offs between them.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierThis node requires students to assemble the full analysis-to-selection chain from Nodes 1–3 into a coherent design direction. Students who have understood each component separately often struggle to show how each decision flows from the previous one. The integration task surfaces whether analysis and selection are genuinely connected or held as separate activities.
