Investigating materials and processes

AC9TDE8P01 n2 · waypoint 2 of 4 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can investigate a range of materials, components, tools, equipment and processes by examining and testing their properties and characteristics.

Success criteria

  • Names specific properties of at least two materials or processes and connects each property to the design task
  • Describes an advantage and a disadvantage of at least one material, component or process investigated

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Analysing needs and opportunities.

Width

Minimum
Examine and test at least two materials or components relevant to a design task; describe the properties that make each suitable or unsuitable.
Typical
Examine and test a variety of materials, components, tools and equipment; compare traditional and contemporary technologies, discovering advantages and disadvantages of each approach (e.g. hand-sewn vs. machine-sewn product).
Wider
  • Investigate emerging technologies such as self-healing materials, flame-retardant fabrics, virtual reality or aquaponics and evaluate how they expand the range of available design options.
  • Research how material properties differ across contexts — for example, how the durability differences between natural hardwood and plantation softwood determine suitability for interior vs. exterior use.

Hinge

Students who have investigated only two materials or processes cannot make justified selections at Node 3 — justification requires having compared enough options to explain why the chosen option is the most appropriate, not just that it works.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyMaterial investigation is inherently accumulative — students need to examine and test a range of materials, components and processes before they can make justified selections at Node 3. More materials tested = richer repertoire to draw from. The breadth here is the investigation range, not the testing process.
