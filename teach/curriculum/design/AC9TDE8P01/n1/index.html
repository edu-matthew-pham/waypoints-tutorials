Analysing needs and opportunities

AC9TDE8P01 n1 · waypoint 1 of 4 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can analyse a need or opportunity for designing by identifying who it is for, what the constraints are, and why the opportunity exists.

Success criteria

  • Names the intended user(s) and explains what makes the need or opportunity specific to them
  • Identifies at least two constraints and explains how each shapes what the designed solution must do
  • Distinguishes between a surface-level want and an analysed need grounded in context

Prior knowledge

Students can investigate needs or opportunities for designing and identify materials, components, tools, equipment and processes needed to create designed solutions (AC9TDE6P01). They have not yet analysed needs to justify design opportunities, nor selected and justified specific resources based on evidence such as testing, sustainability or fitness for purpose.

Width

Minimum
Given a design brief, identify the need or opportunity, name the intended user(s), and state at least two constraints (e.g. cost, available materials, context of use).
Typical
Analyse a need or opportunity by considering community context, examining who benefits and who is affected, and identifying constraints including sustainability considerations.
Wider
  • Analyse design opportunities for First Nations Australian communities in specific contexts such as remote energy supply or community housing, identifying how place and culture shape the design need.
  • Compare two design opportunities from different community contexts (e.g. urban school canteen vs. rural sports club) and analyse how context changes the constraints and priorities.

Hinge

The quality of the analysis at this node determines every downstream decision — material selection at Node 2, justified selections at Node 3, and the coherent design direction at Node 4 all depend on a well-understood need. Students who list a surface-level want rather than analysing context and constraints cannot make justified selections or demonstrate a traceable design direction.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepThe quality of the analysis at this node determines every downstream decision — material selection, process choice and evaluation criteria all flow from how well the need is understood. Students who treat the need superficially (listing a want rather than analysing context and constraints) will make unjustified selections at Nodes 3 and 4.
