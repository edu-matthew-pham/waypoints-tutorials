Designing for healthy eating

AC9TDE8K05 n3 · waypoint 3 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can select and justify preparation and presentation techniques based on food properties when designing solutions for healthy eating

Success criteria

  • Selects a preparation technique appropriate for the food and the health objective
  • Justifies the choice by referencing specific food properties

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 2: How preparation techniques affect properties.

Width

Minimum
Given a healthy eating goal, select a preparation technique and explain why it suits the food's properties and the health objective
Typical
Design a food solution that balances sensory appeal with nutritional goals; justify technique and presentation choices by referencing specific food properties; modify an existing recipe to improve its health benefits while maintaining appeal
Wider
  • Compare food design solutions across cultures — how different traditions balance health, flavour and presentation using different techniques and different foods
  • Design a food solution for a specific dietary requirement and evaluate how preparation techniques support both the nutritional need and sensory experience

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierDesigning for healthy eating requires students to draw on food property knowledge (Node 1) and preparation technique analysis (Node 2) simultaneously — selecting a technique requires knowing both what it does to sensory properties and how it affects nutrient retention. Students who have learned these separately often design without considering both dimensions.
