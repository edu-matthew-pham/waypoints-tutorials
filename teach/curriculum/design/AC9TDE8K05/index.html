Food properties and presentation

AC9TDE8K05 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Descriptor

analyse how properties of foods determine preparation and presentation techniques when designing solutions for healthy eating

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how the characteristics of foods influence selection and preparation for healthy eating (AC9TDE6K04). They understand that food characteristics matter for healthy eating but have not yet analysed how specific food properties (sensory properties, nutrient retention) determine preparation and presentation techniques.

By the end

Students analyse how the properties of foods — including sensory properties (flavour, texture, appearance, aroma) and nutrient value — determine the selection of preparation and presentation techniques when designing solutions for healthy eating.

ACARA elaborations

Examples from ACARA that illustrate this standard. They are examples, not a checklist, and belong to the standard as a whole.

  • analysing how First Nations Australians prepare foods for healthy eating, for example using cooking methods that improve edibility, such as removing bitterness to make yams more palatable, roasting bunya nuts to improve texture and flavour, and on many occasions, carefully selecting wood for roasting and smoking to complement the flavour of foods
  • explaining how food preparation techniques impact on the sensory properties, such as flavour, appearance, texture and aroma of food, for example the browning of cut fruit, the absorption of water when cooking rice, and the selection of timbers when smoking foods
  • investigating the relationship between food preparation techniques and the impact on nutrient value including how a recipe can be modified to enhance health benefits, for example stir-frying, steaming vegetables, leaving skin on vegetables or removing skin from chicken
  • analysing food preparation techniques used in different cultures including those from countries across Asia and the impact of these on nutrient retention, aesthetics, taste and palatability, for example stir-frying, steaming, poaching and using a wide variety of vegetables

Waypoints

  • 1. Food properties that matterHingeStudents can identify the sensory properties and nutritional properties of foods that influence how they should be prepared
  • 2. How preparation techniques affect propertiesHingeStudents can analyse how different preparation techniques impact sensory properties and nutrient retention
  • 3. Designing for healthy eatingStudents can select and justify preparation and presentation techniques based on food properties when designing solutions for healthy eating
