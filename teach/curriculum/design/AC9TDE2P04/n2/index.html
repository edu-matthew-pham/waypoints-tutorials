Working cooperatively with identified roles

AC9TDE2P04 n2 · waypoint 2 of 2 · Years 1–2 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can identify roles for group members and work cooperatively toward a shared making outcome

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Carries out an identified role in a cooperative making task
  • The group's shared output is produced — the cooperative process functioned

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sequencing making steps.

Width

Minimum
Participate in a cooperative making task where roles are identified; carry out at least one assigned role
Typical
Identify roles for each group member appropriate to the task; work cooperatively toward a shared goal; adjust roles or steps as needed when problems arise
Wider
  • Make a number of items for a school event with a group — assign roles (cutting, joining, decorating, checking), carry out the assigned role and explain how each role contributes to the shared outcome
  • Write instructions for making a container or bag — include who does each step (role) and in what order; test whether another group can follow the instructions

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Integration: a combining task at the wider tierCooperative making requires students to coordinate sequenced steps (node 1) with distributed roles — two understandings that must work together. Integration failure looks like: students who can sequence steps individually but cannot coordinate with others (duplicating work, waiting, doing steps in the wrong order because communication broke down).
