Environmental consequences of material selection

AC9TDE10K06 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can critique designed solutions to identify the environmental consequences of material selection and compare with sustainable alternatives.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least one environmental consequence of a specific material selection
  • Evaluates an alternative material against the original — not just describes it

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse how characteristics and properties of materials, systems, components, tools and equipment can be combined to create designed solutions (AC9TDE8K06). They can identify material properties and justify selection decisions. They have not yet made judgements on combinations, nor critiqued environmental consequences of material selection, nor investigated emerging and innovative materials at this level.

Width

Minimum
Identify one environmental consequence of a material used in an existing product; describe one alternative material that would reduce that consequence.
Typical
Critique an existing product's material selection for environmental consequences — considering carbon footprint, toxicity, biodegradability, and end-of-life options; research at least one emerging or sustainable alternative and evaluate the trade-offs of switching.
Wider
  • Critique the design of common plastic bags and research innovative sustainable alternatives (bioplastics, seaweed-based materials) — make a judgement on which alternative best balances performance, cost and environmental benefit
  • Analyse how First Nations Australians' use of possum fur (thermal properties, biodegradability, source management) represents sophisticated material knowledge — examine how this is being applied in contemporary high-performance thermal clothing
  • Investigate how locally sourced materials (e.g. bamboo cross-laminated timber) compare to conventional equivalents on carbon footprint, performance and cost — justify a selection decision for a specific application

Hinge

Critiquing environmental consequences of material selection at N1 directly informs N2 judgements about emerging materials — students who haven't developed this evaluative lens cannot make defensible sustainability judgements about new materials.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Context breadth: more contexts in the vary or modify stepEnvironmental consequences of material selection are highly context-dependent — plastics, textiles, structural materials and fibres each have different environmental profiles and different alternative options. Multiple material contexts build analytical flexibility rather than a single-material conclusion.
