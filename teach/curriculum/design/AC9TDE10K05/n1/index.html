Sensory and nutritional assessment of food

AC9TDE10K05 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can conduct or interpret sensory and nutritional assessments of foods and explain how these characteristics inform food solution design.

Success criteria

  • Identifies at least two sensory characteristics and explains how each affects food quality or acceptability
  • Connects assessment findings to a design decision — not just describes the food

Prior knowledge

Students can analyse how the properties of foods determine their preparation and presentation techniques, and how health, food safety and sustainability affect food choices (AC9TDE8K05). They can identify sensory and nutritional properties of food. They have not yet made judgements on how these properties interact in designing sustainable food solutions, nor examined food preservation, trend-driven food innovation, or emerging food technologies.

Width

Minimum
Describe at least two sensory characteristics (e.g. texture, flavour, colour, aroma) and explain how they influence a food design decision.
Typical
Conduct or interpret sensory and nutritional assessments across a range of foods; explain how sensory characteristics (flavour, texture, colour, aroma) and nutritional properties interact; use assessment findings to inform decisions about food solution design.
Wider
  • Compare freshly squeezed juice with commercial alternatives through sensory and nutritional assessment — make a judgement on which better serves a healthy eating solution and explain the trade-offs
  • Analyse how First Nations Australians' understanding of detoxification processes (e.g. cycad nuts) reflects sophisticated knowledge of the functional properties of food
  • Compare locally grown with imported produce through sensory assessment — evaluate how freshness, seasonality and food miles interact in sustainable food solution design

Hinge

Sensory and nutritional assessment skills established at N1 are applied at N3 when evaluating food trends — students who cannot conduct or interpret assessments cannot judge whether innovations improve nutritional value or sensory quality.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsSensory assessment requires precision and consistent application of criteria — colour, texture, flavour and aroma each need to be identified and described reliably. Sufficient practice with varied foods builds the assessment fluency that N3 evaluation requires.
