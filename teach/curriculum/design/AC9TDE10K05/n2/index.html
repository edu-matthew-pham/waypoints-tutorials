Food preservation and spoilage

AC9TDE10K05 n2 · waypoint 2 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain how food spoilage occurs and how preservation methods affect food structure, sensory properties and nutritional value.

Lessons on this site

None yet.

Success criteria

  • Correctly identifies at least one spoilage mechanism and the preservation method that addresses it
  • Explains how the preservation method changes at least one food property (sensory or nutritional)

Prior knowledge

Waypoint 1: Sensory and nutritional assessment of food.

Width

Minimum
Describe one cause of food spoilage and one preservation method; explain how the preservation method changes the food's properties.
Typical
Explain how different causes of food spoilage (microbial, enzymatic, oxidative) are addressed by different preservation methods; analyse how methods such as freezing, dehydrating, or heat treatment affect food structure, sensory properties and nutritional value; apply safe food handling and storage principles including danger-zone temperatures.
Wider
  • Experiment with dehydration and compare the sensory and nutritional properties of the fresh and dehydrated product — make a judgement on when dehydration is a suitable preservation method
  • Evaluate multiple preservation methods for the same food against criteria of safety, nutritional retention, sensory quality and sustainability — make a judgement on the best approach for a specific food solution context
  • Develop a comprehensive food safety checklist for a food service context — explain how each element addresses a specific spoilage or contamination risk

Hinge

Students who cannot identify spoilage causes and preservation responses cannot design food solutions at N3 that are both safe and aligned with food trends — the food safety requirement runs through all food solution design.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Consolidation: more practise steps, short feedback loopsFood safety knowledge requires precision — danger-zone temperatures, contamination vectors, storage requirements. Students need sufficient practice applying these accurately before they can make judgements about preservation method trade-offs. Errors in food safety knowledge have real consequences.
