Independently developing design criteria

AC9TDE10P04 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 9–10 Design and Technologies · Processes and production skills · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can independently develop specific, assessable design criteria that include sustainability considerations.

Success criteria

  • At least two criteria are specific and assessable — a person could check whether a solution meets them
  • At least one sustainability criterion is included

Prior knowledge

Students can develop design criteria collaboratively including sustainability to evaluate design ideas, processes and solutions (AC9TDE8P04). They have experience using criteria to guide evaluation. They have not yet developed criteria independently, reflected substantively on process choices, or iterated based on evaluation feedback.

Width

Minimum
Write at least two design criteria independently (without a provided template); criteria must be assessable against a designed solution and include at least one sustainability criterion.
Typical
Develop a set of design criteria independently from the design brief — including functional, aesthetic, safety, sustainability and user experience criteria; ensure each criterion is specific and assessable; explain the reasoning behind key criteria choices.
Wider
  • Develop criteria that explicitly specify acceptable performance thresholds (e.g. maximum load for a structure, acceptable stress test result) — not just what is required but how it will be measured
  • Develop criteria that address the full product life cycle, not just the use phase — including criteria for material sourcing, production impact and end-of-life
  • Compare criteria developed independently with criteria developed through user consultation — analyse what consultation adds

Hinge

The descriptor specifies criteria developed independently — if students cannot do this at N1, N2 evaluation and N3 reflection are conducted against criteria that are not their own, and the descriptor's core demand is not met.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Structured reasoning: a worked chain of reasons, then a write-and-compare (reasoning) checkIndependent criteria development requires students to reason from the design brief to testable statements. Students who develop criteria without this process produce vague or circular criteria (e.g. 'the product should look good'). The reasoning path (what must this solution do? → how would we know if it did? → what sustainability considerations apply?) produces actionable criteria.
