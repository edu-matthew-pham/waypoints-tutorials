What innovation means in design

AC9TDE8K02 n1 · waypoint 1 of 3 · Years 7–8 Design and Technologies · Knowledge and understanding · Hinge

Draft, from Waypoints Kit v0.2. Generated from Kit's node data and not yet reviewed by a specialist in Design and Technologies: treat it as a starting suggestion and check it before use.

Checkpoint

Students can explain what innovation means in a design context and identify factors that drive or enable innovation

Success criteria

  • Defines innovation in a design context (not just 'something new')
  • Identifies at least two factors that drive innovation

Prior knowledge

Students can explain how people in design and technologies occupations consider competing factors, including sustainability, in the design of products, services and environments (AC9TDE6K01). They have identified factors but have not yet analysed the impact of innovation or emerging technologies on designed solutions, or considered how design contributes to global preferred futures.

Width

Minimum
Define innovation in design terms; identify at least two factors that drive innovation (e.g. new materials, societal change, environmental need, technology development)
Typical
Explain how multiple factors interact to drive innovation — developing novel ideas, responding quickly to change, creating a point of differentiation, adding value for society, reducing costs or improving efficiency
Wider
  • Limited width — the concept of innovation is a single insight. The genuine breadth comes from seeing innovation applied across different technology types and contexts at later nodes.

Hinge

Students who define innovation only as 'something new and digital' will not recognise traditional or adaptive technologies as innovation at Node 2, producing incomplete comparisons and a distorted analysis of impact at Node 3.

Response modes

Where extra effort goes at this waypoint (width), not what kind of lesson it is.

Repertoire building: more cases to sort or classifyUnderstanding what drives innovation requires accumulating a range of examples — students who only see technology-driven innovation will miss social, environmental and economic drivers. Building a repertoire of innovation examples across driver types is the prior condition for analysing technology types at Node 2.
