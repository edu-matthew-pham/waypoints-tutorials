# One country, several governments

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** One country can have national laws and state laws. How can both matter?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Federation shares constitutional power between the Commonwealth and the states. State and territory governments run many everyday services, while responsibilities can overlap.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what federation means. I can give examples of Commonwealth and state or territory responsibilities. I can distinguish state powers, territory self-government and local council responsibilities.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a division, not a ladder

The Constitution kept the states and their powers. It gave the Commonwealth a list of powers of its own. **Territories are different**: the Commonwealth Parliament can make laws for a territory, and a territory's own government gets its powers from a Commonwealth law.

Local councils are set up by state or territory laws. The Australian Constitution does not set them up. The ACT has no local councils; its Legislative Assembly handles territory and local responsibilities.

:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: federal does not mean boss. The Constitution splits powers between the Commonwealth and the states, and the states run their own public schools. The Commonwealth can only act where it has power.

**What is different about a territory compared with a state?**

☐ The Commonwealth Parliament can make laws for a territory

☐ A territory has exactly the same position in the Constitution as a state

☐ A territory has no government of its own at all

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The Commonwealth Parliament can make laws for a territory

The Constitution lets the Commonwealth Parliament make laws for territories. States keep their own powers in a different way.

## Explain the division

These statements and situations are made up.

:::keep-together

### Case 1

**The Commonwealth gives money to a state's public school program. What can you conclude?**

☐ The schools now become Commonwealth schools

☐ The state still runs the schools; the funding is shared

☐ The state must now do whatever the Commonwealth says about schools

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The state still runs the schools; the funding is shared

Paying for a service and running it are different jobs. More than one government can help pay.

:::


:::keep-together

### Case 2

**Which statement best describes federation?**

☐ The Commonwealth, the states and councils have equal powers under the Constitution

☐ The states make every law, and the Commonwealth only looks after defence

☐ The states handed all their powers to the Commonwealth

☐ The Constitution splits law-making powers between the Commonwealth and the states

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The Constitution splits law-making powers between the Commonwealth and the states

The states kept their own governments and powers, and the Commonwealth got powers of its own.
