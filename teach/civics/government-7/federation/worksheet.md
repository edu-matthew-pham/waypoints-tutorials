# One country, several governments

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** One country can have national laws and state laws. How can both matter?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Federation shares constitutional power between the Commonwealth and the states. State and territory governments run many everyday services, while responsibilities can overlap.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what federation means. I can give examples of Commonwealth and state or territory responsibilities. I can distinguish state powers, territory self-government and local council responsibilities.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a division, not a ladder

The Constitution preserves states and their powers while giving the Commonwealth specified powers. **Territories are different**: the Commonwealth can make laws for territories, and their self-government arrangements come from Commonwealth laws.

Local councils are created under state or territory laws, not established as a separate level by the Australian Constitution. The ACT has no local councils; its Legislative Assembly handles territory and local responsibilities.

:::keep-together

### Case 1

**The Commonwealth contributes money to a public school program. What can you conclude?**

☐ State governments no longer matter

☐ All schools become Commonwealth departments

☐ Funding can be shared while the state runs schools

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Funding can be shared while the state runs schools

Funding a service and operating it are different jobs.

:::


:::keep-together

### Case 2

**Which statement best describes federation?**

☐ Local councils wrote the Constitution

☐ National and state governments have a constitutional division of powers

☐ The states give up every power

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** National and state governments have a constitutional division of powers

States retain their own government and powers alongside the Commonwealth.
