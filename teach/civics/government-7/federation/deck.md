## Today

**We are learning to** explain how the Constitution divides powers between the Commonwealth and the states, and give examples of each.

- I can explain what federation means.
- I can give examples of Commonwealth and state or territory responsibilities.
- I can distinguish state powers, territory self-government and local council responsibilities.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Usually Year 7. Do not imply states and territories have identical constitutional positions: s122 permits Commonwealth territory laws. ## Why it matters

> One country can have national laws and state laws. How can both matter?

<!-- Speaker note: At Federation in 1901, the six colonies joined together as states in one country. The Constitution gave some powers to the new **Commonwealth** (federal) Parliament and left the rest with the states. That is a **federation**: the states are not departments of the national government. They keep powers of their own. Two of the reasons people gave for joining were easier trade between the colonies and a shared defence force. -->

## Case 1

**The Commonwealth gives money to a state's public school program. What can you conclude?**

- The schools now become Commonwealth schools
- The state still runs the schools; the funding is shared

* Answer: The state still runs the schools; the funding is shared

**What makes Australia a federation? Give one responsibility at each level of the Commonwealth–state division.**
