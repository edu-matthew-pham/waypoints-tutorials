## Today

- I can explain what federation means.
- I can give examples of Commonwealth and state or territory responsibilities.
- I can distinguish state powers, territory self-government and local council responsibilities.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Usually Year 7. Do not imply states and territories have identical constitutional positions: s122 permits Commonwealth territory laws. Councils are created by state or territory law; the ACT has no local councils. The responsibility sort deliberately says running public schools/hospitals and ordinary state/territory police, rather than treating education, health or all policing as exclusive state subjects. Section 109 assumes a valid Commonwealth law and applies only to inconsistency; it does not grant a new federal power. ## Why it matters

> One country can have national laws and state laws. How can both matter?

<!-- Speaker note: At Federation the colonies united as states in one country. The Constitution gave some powers to the new **Commonwealth**, or federal, Parliament and left others with the states. This is a **federation**, not a national government with states as its departments. Two arguments for joining were easier trade between colonies and a united defence. The states also kept governments of their own. -->

## Exit ticket

**What makes Australia a federation? Give one responsibility at each level of the Commonwealth–state division.**

* The Constitution divides powers between national and state governments. Defence is Commonwealth; running public schools is state or territory, with funding also coming from the Commonwealth.
