# We elect them, they answer to us

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** If we elect a local member, how does anyone become Prime Minister?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Voters choose representatives. The government is drawn from Parliament and must answer to it, with the support of the House of Representatives.

**Students should be able to say:** I can trace a House vote through to the formation of government. I can explain how ministers come from and answer to Parliament. I can describe the roles of the House, Senate and Governor-General.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Worked example: who answers to whom?


:::keep-together

![Voters elect the House. Majority support in the House allows a government to form. The Governor-General appoints its leader as Prime Minister. Ministers come from Parliament and answer to Parliament.](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/_pictures/diagrams/civics/government-7/representative-and-responsible/step-3.svg?v=81f0d2aaa9b0d690)

*From a vote to a government*

:::

The **executive** is the part of government that puts laws into action. It includes the Prime Minister and ministers. Cabinet is the Prime Minister and senior ministers, who make the big decisions together. Ministers can come from either house of Parliament. By custom, the Prime Minister comes from the House of Representatives.

The Governor-General represents the monarch and formally appoints the Prime Minister and ministers. The Prime Minister must lead a side that has majority support in the House. The Senate checks proposed laws and questions the government, but it does not decide who forms government.

:::keep-together

### Explain the connection

**Why does electing the House matter for forming government?**

☐ The party with the most votes across Australia forms government

☐ Whoever has majority support in the House can form government

☐ The Governor-General picks whoever they like as Prime Minister

☐ The Senate picks the Prime Minister after the election

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Whoever has majority support in the House can form government

Voters elect the House, and the House decides which side can govern. That is the link between voters and the government.

:::

## Make: Typical: how one seat is won

Each seat in the House is won by **preferential voting**. Voters number every candidate in order of choice. If no one has more than half the first choices, the candidate with the fewest votes is out, and their ballots go to each of those voters' next choice. This repeats until someone has more than half.

## Make: Wider: how scrutiny works


**Match the tool.** 

**Support is lost.** A government needs the support of the House (its "confidence") to stay in office. If it loses that support, it normally resigns or asks for an election; what happens next depends on the situation. A hard question in Question Time does not remove a government on its own.
