# The Constitution - rules for the rule-makers

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Parliament makes the laws for the whole country. But who makes the rules for Parliament?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see through a made-up club that a constitution sets up decision-makers, limits them, and says how the rules themselves change. They then meet Australia's Constitution as the same idea at national size: it came into effect on 1 January 1901, sets up Parliament, the Governor-General and the High Court, limits what Parliament can make laws about, and can be changed only by the voters at a referendum. This is the first of two hinge lessons: if students do not see the Constitution as the source of the institutions' authority, those institutions will look like arbitrary arrangements in the lessons that follow.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what Australia's Constitution is and when it came into effect. I can name three bodies it sets up and say what each one does. I can explain why Parliament cannot change the Constitution on its own.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: Australia's rulebook


:::keep-together

### The real rulebook

1. $\text{Who is it for?}$ — The Constitution sets rules for the people who make the country's laws.
2. $\text{What does it set up?}$ — Parliament (makes laws), the Governor-General (formally appoints ministers and signs laws), the High Court (decides what the rules mean).
3. $\text{What does it limit?}$ — Parliament can only make laws about the subjects the Constitution lists, such as defence and the post.
4. $\text{Who can change it?}$ — Not Parliament alone. The voters decide, at a referendum.

Now: what is the Constitution's main job?

**Australia's Constitution came into effect on 1 January 1901. What is its main job?**

☐ It says which team of politicians runs the country

☐ It sets up the main parts of the national government and says what they can do

☐ It lists every law Australians must follow

☐ It sets how much tax each person pays

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It sets up the main parts of the national government and says what they can do

Like the club's rulebook, it sets up the decision-makers and says what they can and cannot do.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining Mei's class notes. Click the line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mei's class is learning about Australia's Constitution. It came into effect on 1 January 1901.
 2  It sets up Parliament, the Governor-General and the High Court, and it lists what Parliament can make laws about.
 3  Nib says: "Parliament makes the laws, so Parliament can change the Constitution whenever its members vote to."
 4  Nib says: "So the Constitution is really just another law."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Parliament makes ordinary laws, but it sits under the Constitution. Parliament can propose a change, but only the voters can approve it, at a referendum. Just like the club committee, which cannot rewrite its own limits alone.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which bodies does it set up?

**Which three bodies does the Constitution set up?**

☐ The Prime Minister, the police and the army

☐ Parliament, the states and the councils

☐ Parliament, the Governor-General and the High Court

☐ The Senate, the voters and the newspapers

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Parliament, the Governor-General and the High Court

Parliament makes laws, the Governor-General is the monarch's representative who formally appoints ministers and signs laws into force, and the High Court decides what the Constitution means and whether a law is allowed.

### Sam's idea

**Sam says: 'The Constitution is just an old law, and Parliament can update it like any other law.' What is the best reply?**

☐ That is right, Parliament can change it by voting

☐ No. It sits above ordinary laws, and only the voters can approve a change to it

☐ No. Only the Governor-General can change it

☐ No. Only the High Court can change it

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No. It sits above ordinary laws, and only the voters can approve a change to it

A change needs the voters at a referendum. That is what makes the Constitution different from an ordinary law.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**Why can't Parliament change the Constitution on its own?**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The Constitution limits Parliament. If Parliament could change it alone, it would not limit anything. Only the voters, at a referendum, can approve a change.

:::
