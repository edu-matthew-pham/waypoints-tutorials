# The Constitution - rules for the rule-makers

How Australia is governed · How Australia is governed

<!-- Speaker note: Students see through a made-up club that a constitution sets up decision-makers, limits them, and says how the rules themselves change. - The committee changes the rule at its next meeting, then spends the money
- The committee asks the members to vote, because the rule limits what the committee can decide alone

## The real rulebook

Now: what is the Constitution's main job?

$$\text{Who is it for?}$$

1) $\text{What does it set up?}$ — Parliament (makes laws), the Governor-General (formally appoints ministers and signs laws), the High Court (decides what the rules mean).
2) $\text{What does it limit?}$ — Parliament can only make laws about the subjects the Constitution lists, such as defence and the post.
3) $\text{Who can change it?}$ — Not Parliament alone. The voters decide, at a referendum.

## The real rulebook

**Australia's Constitution came into effect on 1 January 1901. What is its main job?**

- It says which team of politicians runs the country
- It sets up the main parts of the national government and says what they can do
- It lists every law Australians must follow
- It sets how much tax each person pays

* Answer: It sets up the main parts of the national government and says what they can do

<!-- Speaker note: Like the club's rulebook, it sets up the decision-makers and says what they can and cannot do. -->

**Which three bodies does the Constitution set up?**

- The Prime Minister, the police and the army

## Exit ticket

**Why can't Parliament change the Constitution on its own?**

* The Constitution limits Parliament. If Parliament could change it alone, it would not limit anything. Only the voters, at a referendum, can approve a change.
