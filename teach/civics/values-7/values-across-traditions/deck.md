## Today

**We are learning to** identify a value shared across religious traditions in Australia and explain how religious freedom lets different beliefs be practised.

- I can name a value many traditions share, with two traditions' own words for it.
- I can explain that a shared value doesn't mean shared beliefs.
- I can explain religious freedom and why it has limits.

## Why it matters

> Different religions believe different things. Can they still share any values?

<!-- Speaker note: In *A diverse democracy* you met Christian heritage: traditions such as Parliament's prayers and Christmas holidays, and the idea that a tradition isn't an official religion. Many other religions are practised in Australia too, including **Judaism**, **Buddhism**, **Islam** and **Hinduism**. Each has its own beliefs, and within each there are many different groups and views. This lesson doesn't try to explain them. It looks at one value many traditions share: **giving and helping others**. Each tradition has its own word for it: - **Christianity**: *charity*, from the teaching to love your neighbour. - **Judaism**: *tzedakah*, helping people in need, seen as a duty and linked to justice. - **Islam**: *zakat*, a required share of wealth given to those in need; one of the Five Pillars of Islam. - **Buddhism**: *dana*, generosity: giving without expecting anything back. Hindu and Jain traditions use this word too. - **Hinduism**: *seva*, selfless service to others. Sikhs use this word too. Shared values don't mean shared beliefs. Two traditions can both value giving and still believe very different things about God, life and what giving means. -->

**What does religious freedom mean in Australia?**

**Two traditions both value giving. Does that mean they believe the same things?**
