# Values across traditions

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Different religions believe different things. Can they still share any values?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students recall Christian heritage from A diverse democracy and see one value many traditions practised in Australia share, giving and helping others, in each tradition's own word (tzedakah, zakat, dana, seva, charity), while recognising that shared values don't mean shared beliefs. Religious freedom, within the law, connects it to civic life.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name a value many traditions share, with two traditions' own words for it. I can explain that a shared value doesn't mean shared beliefs. I can explain religious freedom and why it has limits.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a shared value, different beliefs

1. $\text{Shared value}$ — Giving and helping others.
2. $\text{Different words}$ — Tzedakah, zakat, dana, seva, charity.
3. $\text{Different beliefs}$ — Each tradition has its own reasons and its own way of giving.
4. $\text{Civic link}$ — Compassion is also a value often named in Australian public life.

:::keep-together

### What does the food bank show?

**A made-up Wattle Creek food bank has volunteers from a mosque, a church, a temple and a synagogue. What does that show?**

☐ People from different traditions can act on a shared value

☐ Religion has nothing to do with helping others

☐ Only one of the groups is really helping

☐ All four groups now believe the same things

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** People from different traditions can act on a shared value

Different traditions share the value of giving, so their members can work together on it, while keeping their own beliefs.

:::


:::keep-together

## Religious freedom


:::keep-together

### Religious freedom

**What does religious freedom mean in Australia?**

☐ Religion can only be practised at home

☐ People can do anything at all if it's part of their religion

☐ Everyone must follow the most common religion

☐ People can follow any religion, or none, within the law

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** People can follow any religion, or none, within the law

Religious freedom lets people follow any religion, or none, within the law that protects everyone.

:::


:::keep-together

### Shared value, different beliefs

**Two traditions both value giving. Does that mean they believe the same things?**

☐ No, they can share a value and believe differently

☐ Yes, otherwise they couldn't work together

☐ No, so they can't work together on anything

☐ Yes, sharing one value means sharing every belief

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, they can share a value and believe differently

A shared value is common ground. Beliefs can still differ, and that's part of a pluralist society.

:::
