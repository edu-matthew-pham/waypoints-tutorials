## Today

**We are learning to** explain why migrants and refugees come to Australia and connect this to values and freedoms in Australian society.

- I can explain the difference between a migrant and a refugee.
- I can give reasons people come to Australia.
- I can connect a value in the citizenship pledge to why people come.

## Why it matters

> Why might someone leave the country they grew up in to start again in Australia?

<!-- Speaker note: People come to live in Australia for many reasons. Some reasons **push** people to leave where they are; others **pull** them towards Australia: - **Pushed to leave:** war, being persecuted (treated cruelly or unfairly because of who they are or what they believe), disasters, or no safe way to make a living. - **Drawn to Australia:** work, family who already live here, study, safety, and freedoms such as free speech and religious freedom. Two words matter here: - A **migrant** is someone who chooses to move to another country, and could usually go back. - A **refugee** is someone who is outside their own country and can't return because of a well-founded fear of persecution because of their race, religion, nationality, political opinion or the social group they belong to. Refugees have to leave for their own safety. Many migrants and refugees later become **Australian citizens**. At the ceremony they make the citizenship pledge you met in the first lesson. In this lesson, Wattle Creek and the people in its examples are made up. The definitions and the pledge are real. -->

## Which part of the pledge?

**Someone who fled persecution for their political opinions becomes a citizen at a made-up Wattle Creek ceremony. Which part of the pledge might matter most to them, and why?**
