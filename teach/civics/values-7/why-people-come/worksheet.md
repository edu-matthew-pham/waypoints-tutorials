# Why people come to Australia

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Why might someone leave the country they grew up in to start again in Australia?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain why people come to Australia (pushes such as war and persecution, pulls such as work, family, study, safety and freedom), distinguish migrants from refugees, and connect becoming a citizen to the values named in the citizenship pledge.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain the difference between a migrant and a refugee. I can give reasons people come to Australia. I can connect a value in the citizenship pledge to why people come.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Are a migrant and a refugee the same thing?**

☐ No, a refugee had to flee; a migrant chose to move

☐ Yes, both are people who moved to a new country

☐ Yes, but refugees can never become citizens

☐ No, a refugee is someone who moved for a better job

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** No. Both moved countries, but a refugee had to flee because of a well-founded fear of persecution and can't safely go back, while a migrant chose to move. Many refugees do go on to become Australian citizens.

:::

## Worked example: the pledge again

1. $\text{Migrant}$ — Chose to move, for example for work, family or study.
2. $\text{Refugee}$ — Had to flee because of a well-founded fear of persecution; can't safely return.
3. $\text{Citizen}$ — Many migrants and former refugees become citizens and make the pledge.
4. $\text{The link}$ — The pledge names democratic beliefs, rights and liberties, and the law.

:::keep-together

### Which part of the pledge?

**Someone who fled persecution for their political opinions becomes a citizen at a made-up Wattle Creek ceremony. Which part of the pledge might matter most to them, and why?**

☐ Rights and liberties, because they lacked freedom where they came from

☐ The words 'under God', because every new citizen must say them

☐ None of it, because refugees don't make the pledge

☐ Only the loyalty part, because they must forget their old country

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Rights and liberties, because they lacked freedom where they came from

Someone who was persecuted for their opinions might especially value the rights and liberties named in the pledge, such as freedom of speech.

:::

## Reasons and values


:::keep-together

### Why people come

**Which of these is the most accurate statement about why people come to Australia?**

☐ Everyone who comes to Australia is a refugee

☐ People come for many reasons: work, family, study, safety

☐ Most people come to join family, so other reasons hardly matter

☐ People only ever come to Australia to earn more money

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** People come for many reasons: work, family, study, safety

People move for many reasons at once. Safety and freedom matter as well as work and family.

:::


:::keep-together

### Keeping your culture

**Does becoming an Australian citizen mean giving up your own culture?**

☐ Yes, citizens must stop speaking other languages

☐ No, people keep their own cultures

☐ Yes, the pledge asks people to give up their culture

☐ Yes, citizens must speak only English in public

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, people keep their own cultures

Citizens share democratic beliefs and obey the law, and keep their own cultures. That's part of being pluralist (A diverse democracy).

:::
