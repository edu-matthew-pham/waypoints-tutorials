# Values we name

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** If you had to name three values that hold Australia together, what would they be? Would people in other countries name the same ones?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify values often named as Australian (freedom, respect, fairness, equality of opportunity, a fair go, compassion, inclusion, religious freedom), see how they are expressed through customs and protocols (the anthem, flag and citizenship ceremonies) and institutions, and recognise that many democracies share them.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name values often called Australian and say what they mean. I can give an example of how a value shows up in public life. I can explain why these values aren't Australia's alone.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a citizenship ceremony

People who become Australian citizens make a promise at a ceremony, called the **citizenship pledge**. There are two versions; one includes the words "under God" and the other doesn't. The rest is the same:

> From this time forward, I pledge my loyalty to Australia and its people, whose democratic beliefs I share, whose rights and liberties I respect, and whose laws I will uphold and obey.

1. $\text{The value}$ — Respect for rights and liberties.
2. $\text{Where it shows}$ — In the citizenship pledge, said at every ceremony.
3. $\text{Also shared}$ — Many democracies name the same value in their own words.

:::keep-together

### Which values does it name?

**At a made-up Wattle Creek citizenship ceremony, new citizens say the pledge. Which values does it name?**

☐ Agreeing with the government on every decision

☐ A fair go for everyone and compassion for people in need

☐ Democratic beliefs, rights and liberties, and the law

☐ Loyalty to one religion and one political party

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Democratic beliefs, rights and liberties, and the law

The pledge names democratic beliefs, respect for rights and liberties, and upholding and obeying the law.

:::


:::keep-together

## What a value is and isn't


:::keep-together

### What a fair go means

**What does a 'fair go' mean?**

☐ Whoever works hardest always wins

☐ Everyone deserves a fair chance

☐ Australians get a better deal than others

☐ Everyone must end up with the same amount

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Everyone deserves a fair chance

A fair go means everyone deserves a fair chance, whoever they are.

:::


:::keep-together

### A value or a law?

**Is a value the same as a law?**

☐ No, a value is an idea; laws can reflect values

☐ Yes, you can be fined for not holding a value

☐ No, values and laws have nothing to do with each other

☐ Yes, every value is written in the Constitution

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, a value is an idea; laws can reflect values

A value is an idea about what matters. Laws often reflect values, but a value isn't itself a law.

:::
