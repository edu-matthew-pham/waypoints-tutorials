# Do we live our values?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Australia says everyone gets a fair go. How could you find out whether that's true?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn what social cohesion means and how shared values can support it, then evaluate whether values are lived using real, dated figures from the Scanlon Institute's Mapping Social Cohesion 2025 survey: evidence of wide support and evidence of gaps.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what social cohesion means. I can tell evidence from a claim. I can make a careful judgement using evidence on both sides, with its year.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Australia often names a fair go as a value. Does that mean everyone in Australia gets one?**

☐ Yes, because it's in the citizenship pledge

☐ Not necessarily: you'd need evidence to know

☐ Yes, if it's an Australian value, everyone gets it

☐ No, naming a value means it isn't true

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Not necessarily. Naming a value tells you what people aim for. To know whether it's lived, you look at evidence, such as whether people from different backgrounds feel they belong and are treated fairly.

:::

## Worked example: reading the evidence

1. $\text{The value}$ — Inclusion: people from every background feel part of things.
2. $\text{Evidence it's widely held}$ — 83% agreed multiculturalism has been good for Australia (2025).
3. $\text{Evidence of a gap}$ — 16% said they'd been discriminated against (2025); belonging fell from 63% (2020) to 46% (2025).
4. $\text{A careful judgement}$ — Partly lived: widely supported, but not everyone experiences it.

:::keep-together

### What does 83% show?

**In 2025, 83% agreed that multiculturalism has been good for Australia. What does that show?**

☐ Every Australian feels included and treated fairly

☐ Most adults surveyed agreed with it in 2025

☐ Multiculturalism will always be popular

☐ No one in Australia experiences discrimination

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Most adults surveyed agreed with it in 2025

The figure shows what most people surveyed thought in 2025. It doesn't show that everyone is included: other rows show gaps.

:::


:::keep-together

## Careful judgements


:::keep-together

### A careful judgement

**Which judgement fits the survey evidence best?**

☐ Inclusion is widely supported but not lived by everyone

☐ Surveys can't tell us anything about values

☐ Australia doesn't live any of its values at all

☐ Australia lives all its values fully, with no gaps

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Inclusion is widely supported but not lived by everyone

A careful judgement uses evidence on both sides: wide support, and real gaps.

:::


:::keep-together

### Read exactly what was asked

**Belonging 'to a great extent' fell from 63% to 46%. Does that mean 54% feel they don't belong at all?**

☐ Yes, everyone else feels they don't belong at all

☐ Yes, because 100 minus 46 is 54

☐ No, they may belong, just not to a great extent

☐ No, it means 46% feel they don't belong

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, they may belong, just not to a great extent

Read exactly what was asked. People not in the 46% might still belong a moderate amount or a little.

:::
