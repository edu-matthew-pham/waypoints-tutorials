## Why it matters

> The law is the same for everyone. Does that mean everyone has the same chance in court?

<!-- Speaker note: In the last three lessons you met the rule of law, fair trials and the courts. This lesson puts them together with democracy. A fair legal system supports democracy in three ways: - it **protects rights**, such as the freedoms you met in *Freedoms and taking part*; - it **makes the government obey the law**, because independent courts can rule that a government broke it; - it **settles disputes peacefully**, by rules everyone knows, instead of by force. Equal laws only help if people can actually use them. That is called **access to justice**. Help includes: - **Legal aid**: free or cheaper legal help from legal aid commissions, which governments fund. Whether you get a lawyer depends on your income and your case. - **Community legal centres**: free advice, often for people who can't afford a lawyer. - **Interpreters**, so people who don't speak English well can follow their case. Most people agree access to justice matters. How well Australia provides it, and how much should be spent on it, is debated. In this lesson, Wattle Creek and its people are made up. The kinds of help are real. -->

## How do courts support democracy?

**How do independent courts support democracy?**

- They can rule that the government broke the law
- They agree with whatever the elected government decides to do
- They choose which laws Parliament is allowed to debate
- They replace elections when people disagree about a law

* Answer: They can rule that the government broke the law

<!-- Speaker note: Independent courts make sure the government stays under the law, which protects the rights democracy depends on. -->

## Explain the link

**Explain one way a fair legal system supports democracy. Use an example from Tarn's case or one of your own made-up cases.**

* Answer: A strong answer: Democracy depends on everyone having the same rights. If Tarn can't afford a lawyer, Tarn's rights only exist on paper. Free advice from a community legal centre helps Tarn actually use the law, so equality before the law is real, not just written.; A different answer that also works: In a democracy the government has to follow the law. If a made-up council closed a park without the notice the law requires, an independent court could rule that it broke the law. That keeps the people in power under the same law as everyone else.
