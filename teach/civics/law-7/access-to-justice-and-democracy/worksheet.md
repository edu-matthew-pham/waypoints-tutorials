# Fair courts and democracy

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The law is the same for everyone. Does that mean everyone has the same chance in court?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students connect the course to democracy: a fair legal system protects rights, keeps government under the law and settles disputes peacefully, and access to justice (legal aid, community legal centres, interpreters, plain processes) is what makes equal laws real in practice.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain one way a fair legal system supports democracy. I can explain why equal laws don't always mean equal chances. I can name kinds of help that give people access to justice.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**In a made-up case, a big company with a team of lawyers is in a dispute with Tarn, who can't afford any legal advice. Do they have the same chance in court?**

☐ No, companies always win against people in court

☐ No, Tarn may struggle without help, even though the law is the same

☐ Yes, the judge will act as Tarn's lawyer

☐ Yes, the law is the same for both of them

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The law is the same, but Tarn may not know the rules, the forms or the best arguments. The judge must stay neutral and can't act as Tarn's lawyer. Companies don't always win, but help such as legal aid or a community legal centre can make the contest fairer.

:::

## Worked example: Tarn's dispute


:::keep-together

### Tarn's first step

**Tarn, an apprentice in Wattle Creek, says a landlord kept Tarn's bond without a good reason. Tarn can't afford a lawyer. What is a sensible first step?**

☐ Wait for the police to take the landlord to court

☐ Give up, because only people with money can use the law

☐ Ask a community legal centre or legal aid for free advice

☐ Ask the local member of Parliament to decide the case

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Ask a community legal centre or legal aid for free advice

Free advice from a community legal centre or legal aid can help Tarn understand the rules and decide what to do next.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is talking about Tarn's dispute. Click the first line where Nib goes wrong.**

```
 1  Tarn is in a dispute with a landlord about a bond in Wattle Creek.
 2  Tarn can't afford a lawyer, but the landlord has one.
 3  Nib says: "The law is the same for everyone, so Tarn has the same chance as the landlord."
 4  Nib says: "So there's no need for legal aid or community legal centres."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the law being the same for everyone doesn't give everyone the same chance. Without money or know-how, Tarn may not be able to use the law well. That is why access to justice matters.

## Why it matters for democracy


:::keep-together

### How do courts support democracy?

**How do independent courts support democracy?**

☐ They can rule that the government broke the law

☐ They agree with whatever the elected government decides to do

☐ They choose which laws Parliament is allowed to debate

☐ They replace elections when people disagree about a law

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They can rule that the government broke the law

Independent courts make sure the government stays under the law, which protects the rights democracy depends on.

:::


:::keep-together

### Explain the link

**Explain one way a fair legal system supports democracy. Use an example from Tarn's case or one of your own made-up cases.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A strong answer: Democracy depends on everyone having the same rights. If Tarn can't afford a lawyer, Tarn's rights only exist on paper. Free advice from a community legal centre helps Tarn actually use the law, so equality before the law is real, not just written.; A different answer that also works: In a democracy the government has to follow the law. If a made-up council closed a park without the notice the law requires, an independent court could rule that it broke the law. That keeps the people in power under the same law as everyone else.

:::
