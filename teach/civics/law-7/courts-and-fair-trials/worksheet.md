# Courts and a fair trial

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Why doesn't every case go straight to the High Court?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn Australia's court ladder (lower courts, higher courts, the High Court; state and federal) and what an appeal is, and the elements of a fair trial: an impartial judge, a jury in most serious criminal trials, witnesses, a lawyer for each side and due process.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe Australia's court ladder from a local court to the High Court. I can explain what an appeal is. I can explain who does what in a jury trial, and why it is fair.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Appeals and juries


:::keep-together

### What can Jess do?

**Jess is found guilty in the local court and thinks the magistrate got the law wrong. What can Jess do?**

☐ Take the case straight to the High Court

☐ Ask the jury to meet again and vote again

☐ Appeal to a higher court to check the decision

☐ Ask the Prime Minister to change the verdict

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Appeal to a higher court to check the decision

An appeal asks a higher court to check whether a lower court got the law or the decision wrong.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why a jury?

**Why are ordinary citizens asked to be jurors in serious criminal trials?**

☐ So the judge doesn't have to explain the law to anyone

☐ So the government can pick who decides each case

☐ So jurors can choose the punishment they think is fair

☐ So people from the community decide whether guilt is proved

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** So people from the community decide whether guilt is proved

A jury means the community, not the government or one judge alone, decides whether guilt has been proved.

**What is an appeal, and who decides the verdict and the sentence in a jury trial?**

{{lines-3}}

**Answer:** An appeal asks a higher court to check a lower court's decision. The jury decides guilty or not guilty; the judge decides the sentence.
