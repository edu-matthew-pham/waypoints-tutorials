# Where our law comes from

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A ladder breaks and someone is hurt. No law says exactly what should happen in a case like this one. Who decides, and what do they go on?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that Australia's law has two sources, Acts of Parliament and common law built up from judges' decisions (precedent), both inherited from Britain, and that judges must be independent. Magna Carta is taught as the charter later read as meaning no one is above the law.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the two sources of Australia's law and give an example of each. I can explain what precedent is and why lower courts follow higher ones. I can explain why judges must be independent of the government.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The judge looks to earlier decisions in cases like it, especially decisions of higher courts. That body of past decisions is the common law. Judges don't just decide by feel, cases don't wait for Parliament, and the government can't tell a judge what to decide.

## Worked example: Lin's ladder

1. $\text{Lin's ladder breaks}$ — Lin takes Wattle Creek Hardware to the local court.
2. $\text{An Act of Parliament}$ — The magistrate checks whether a product safety Act covers the case.
3. $\text{Common law}$ — The magistrate follows what higher courts decided in earlier cases like it.
4. $\text{Independent judge}$ — No minister or Prime Minister can tell the magistrate what to decide.

:::keep-together

### What will the magistrate use?

**Lin buys a ladder at Wattle Creek Hardware and it breaks on its first use. Lin takes the shop to the local court. What will the magistrate use to decide the case?**

☐ Only earlier decisions, because Acts don't apply in court

☐ Only the Act, because judges must ignore earlier decisions

☐ Whatever the shop and Lin agree is fair on the day

☐ Any Act on product safety, plus higher courts' earlier decisions

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Any Act on product safety, plus higher courts' earlier decisions

The magistrate applies any Act that covers the case and follows what higher courts decided in similar cases. Law from Parliament and law from the courts work together.

:::


:::keep-together

## Two sources, one system


:::keep-together

### Where did it come from?

**What does it mean to say Australia's law came from Britain?**

☐ Britain still passes the laws that Australians have to obey

☐ Australia has the same written constitution as Britain

☐ Our courts use common law and precedent, which began in England

☐ Australian courts must check every decision with a British court

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Our courts use common law and precedent, which began in England

The common law, precedent and the idea of independent judges all came to Australia from England with the British colonies.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why independent judges?

**Why does it matter that judges are independent of the government?**

☐ So the government can pick which cases go to court

☐ So judges can make any new law they like in each case

☐ So the government has to obey the law like everyone else

☐ So judges never have to follow earlier decisions

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** So the government has to obey the law like everyone else

If the government could tell judges what to decide, it could put itself above the law. Independent judges can rule against the government when it breaks the law.

:::
