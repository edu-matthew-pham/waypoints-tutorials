## Why it matters

> A ladder breaks and someone is hurt. No law says exactly what should happen in a case like this one. Who decides, and what do they go on?

<!-- Speaker note: Australia's legal system came from Britain. It has two main sources of law: - **Acts of Parliament**: laws that Parliament debates and passes. - **Common law**: law that judges have built up over hundreds of years from their decisions in real cases. When a higher court decides a legal question, lower courts follow that decision in later cases like it. A past decision that later courts follow is called a **precedent**. In this lesson, the town of Wattle Creek, its people and its businesses are made up. The courts and the ideas are real. -->

## What happened?

* The judge looks to earlier decisions in cases like it, especially decisions of higher courts. That body of past decisions is the common law. Judges don't just decide by feel, cases don't wait for Parliament, and the government can't tell a judge what to decide.

## What will the magistrate use?

**Lin buys a ladder at Wattle Creek Hardware and it breaks on its first use. Lin takes the shop to the local court. What will the magistrate use to decide the case?**

- Only earlier decisions, because Acts don't apply in court
- Only the Act, because judges must ignore earlier decisions
- Whatever the shop and Lin agree is fair on the day
- Any Act on product safety, plus higher courts' earlier decisions

* Answer: Any Act on product safety, plus higher courts' earlier decisions

<!-- Speaker note: The magistrate applies any Act that covers the case and follows what higher courts decided in similar cases. Law from Parliament and law from the courts work together. -->

## Where did it come from?

**What does it mean to say Australia's law came from Britain?**

- Britain still passes the laws that Australians have to obey
- Australia has the same written constitution as Britain
- Our courts use common law and precedent, which began in England
- Australian courts must check every decision with a British court

* Answer: Our courts use common law and precedent, which began in England

<!-- Speaker note: The common law, precedent and the idea of independent judges all came to Australia from England with the British colonies. -->

## Why independent judges?

**Why does it matter that judges are independent of the government?**

- So the government can pick which cases go to court
- So judges can make any new law they like in each case
- So the government has to obey the law like everyone else
- So judges never have to follow earlier decisions

* Answer: So the government has to obey the law like everyone else

<!-- Speaker note: If the government could tell judges what to decide, it could put itself above the law. Independent judges can rule against the government when it breaks the law. -->

## Exit ticket

**Name the two sources of Australia's law, and say why judges must be independent.**
