# The same law for everyone

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Someone is charged with stealing. Should they have to prove they didn't do it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn the rule of law (public laws that apply to everyone, the government included) and the principles of a fair criminal trial: the presumption of innocence, the burden of proof on the prosecution, proof beyond reasonable doubt, equality before the law and the right to a lawyer.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain the rule of law in my own words. I can explain who has to prove what in a criminal trial, and why. I can tell when a made-up case fits or breaks the principles of justice.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The prosecution has to prove Morgan did it. Morgan doesn't have to prove anything. Until guilt is proved, Morgan is treated as innocent. The judge runs a fair trial but doesn't investigate the case.

## Worked example: Morgan's trial

1. $\text{Charged}$ — Morgan is charged, but is still treated as innocent.
2. $\text{Burden of proof}$ — The prosecution has to prove Morgan took the watch.
3. $\text{The standard}$ — Beyond reasonable doubt: a blurry video isn't enough.
4. $\text{Morgan's side}$ — Morgan can have a lawyer and doesn't have to prove anything.

:::keep-together

### Is the video enough?

**At Morgan's trial, the prosecution shows a blurry video of someone in a grey jacket near the stall. Is that enough to find Morgan guilty?**

☐ Yes, because the police would only charge someone guilty

☐ Yes, unless Morgan can show where they were that day

☐ No, because videos can never be used as evidence in court

☐ No, it leaves reasonable doubt that the person was Morgan

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, it leaves reasonable doubt that the person was Morgan

The prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt. A blurry video of someone in a grey jacket leaves room to doubt it was Morgan.

:::


:::keep-together

## What the principles mean


:::keep-together

### Presumption of innocence

**What does the presumption of innocence mean?**

☐ A person can't be found guilty if they say they didn't do it

☐ Police aren't allowed to charge anyone without a confession first

☐ A person charged is treated as innocent until guilt is proved

☐ A person charged must show their alibi to be treated as innocent

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A person charged is treated as innocent until guilt is proved

The presumption of innocence means a person charged is treated as innocent until the prosecution proves guilt.

:::


:::keep-together

### Rule of law or not?

**A made-up country has very strict laws, but its ministers don't have to obey them. Does it have the rule of law?**

☐ Yes, because it has many laws and enforces them strictly

☐ No, because strict laws are never allowed under the rule of law

☐ Yes, because the ministers wrote the laws in the first place

☐ No, because the law has to apply to the government too

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, because the law has to apply to the government too

The rule of law means the same law applies to everyone, including those in power. Strict laws that the rulers can ignore are not the rule of law.

:::
