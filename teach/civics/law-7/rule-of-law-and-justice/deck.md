## Today

**We are learning to** explain the rule of law and how the presumption of innocence and the burden of proof protect a person who is charged.

- I can explain the rule of law in my own words.
- I can explain who has to prove what in a criminal trial, and why.
- I can tell when a made-up case fits or breaks the principles of justice.

## Why it matters

> Someone is charged with stealing. Should they have to prove they didn't do it?

<!-- Speaker note: The **rule of law** is the principle that: - laws are public and known in advance, so people can follow them; - the same laws apply to everyone, including the police, ministers and the Prime Minister; - people can only be punished for breaking a law, after a fair process. The rule of law is something Australia aims for. In practice it can fall short, which is why courts, lawyers and fair procedures matter. In this lesson, Wattle Creek and its people are made up. The principles are real. -->

## Is the video enough?

**At Morgan's trial, the prosecution shows a blurry video of someone in a grey jacket near the stall. Is that enough to find Morgan guilty?**

- Yes, because the police would only charge someone guilty
- Yes, unless Morgan can show where they were that day
- No, because videos can never be used as evidence in court
- No, it leaves reasonable doubt that the person was Morgan

* Answer: No, it leaves reasonable doubt that the person was Morgan

<!-- Speaker note: The prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt. A blurry video of someone in a grey jacket leaves room to doubt it was Morgan. -->

## Burden of proof

**In a criminal trial, who carries the burden of proof?**

- The prosecution, which brought the charge
- The person charged, who must show they're innocent
- The judge, who must work out what happened
- The jury, which must find the evidence itself

* Answer: The prosecution, which brought the charge

## Presumption of innocence

**What does the presumption of innocence mean?**

- A person can't be found guilty if they say they didn't do it
- Police aren't allowed to charge anyone without a confession first
- A person charged is treated as innocent until guilt is proved
- A person charged must show their alibi to be treated as innocent

* Answer: A person charged is treated as innocent until guilt is proved

<!-- Speaker note: The presumption of innocence means a person charged is treated as innocent until the prosecution proves guilt. -->

## Rule of law or not?

**A made-up country has very strict laws, but its ministers don't have to obey them. Does it have the rule of law?**

- Yes, because it has many laws and enforces them strictly
- No, because strict laws are never allowed under the rule of law
- Yes, because the ministers wrote the laws in the first place
- No, because the law has to apply to the government too

* Answer: No, because the law has to apply to the government too

<!-- Speaker note: The rule of law means the same law applies to everyone, including those in power. Strict laws that the rulers can ignore are not the rule of law. -->

## Exit ticket
