# Why it's hard to decide

## Today

**We are learning to** identify a challenge arising from competing perspectives on a civic decision and explain what makes it hard to resolve.

- I can explain why a civic decision is hard, using values, interests or missing evidence.
- I can explain why majority support doesn't settle a decision on its own.
- I can suggest a response and say who might still be unhappy.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Integration node (n3): it pulls the course together with what students know about councils and democratic decisions. ## Why it matters

> Most people in the survey support the market. So why can't the council just say yes?

<!-- Speaker note: Wattle Creek council (made up, like everyone in these sources) now has to decide about the Saturday market. You've read the sources and worked out why views differ. This lesson asks why the decision is still hard. A civic decision is hard to settle when: - **Values compete**: good things pull in different directions, such as a lively street and easy access for people who need to park. - **Interests compete**: some people gain and others lose, such as growers who get new customers and traders who lose drive-in sales. - **Evidence is missing**: no one knows yet what will happen, such as whether sales go up or down. A quick reminder of the sources: the **council** proposes a three-month trial; the **Gazette** wants it made permanent; the **Main Street Traders** want a loading zone and their sales checked; **Ines**, who uses a wheelchair, likes the idea but needs parking near the chemist; the **survey** found 110 of 200 residents in support; the **bus** stop moves to River Road. -->

* No. Support matters, but it doesn't make Ines's parking problem, the traders' deliveries or the bus stop go away. The council still has to weigh competing values and interests, and some evidence (like what happens to sales) isn't in yet.

## Why is it hard?

**Why is Ines's parking problem hard for the council, even though most people support the market?**

- Ines doesn't really like the idea of a market
- Councils aren't allowed to think about parking
- A car-free street and nearby parking pull different ways
- The survey shows that parking doesn't matter to anyone

* Answer: A car-free street and nearby parking pull different ways

**The council keeps an accessible parking zone and allows early deliveries. Is everyone now happy?**

- Yes, because the council made the decision
- No, so the council should give up on the market
- Probably not: some may still lose out

## Why a trial?

**Why might a three-month trial help the council?**

- It means the council never has to decide
- It stops anyone disagreeing during the trial
- It gives evidence about what actually happens
- It proves the Gazette was right all along

* Answer: It gives evidence about what actually happens
