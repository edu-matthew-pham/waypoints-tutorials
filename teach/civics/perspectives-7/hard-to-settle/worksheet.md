# Why it's hard to decide

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Most people in the survey support the market. So why can't the council just say yes?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain why the Wattle Creek market decision is hard to settle: competing values (a lively street versus access), competing interests (growers versus some traders) and missing evidence (what happens to sales). They see that majority support doesn't settle it alone, match council responses to the challenges they address, and recognise that some people may still lose out.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why a civic decision is hard, using values, interests or missing evidence. I can explain why majority support doesn't settle a decision on its own. I can suggest a response and say who might still be unhappy.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: Ines and the market

1. $\text{The challenge}$ — A lively, car-free street versus nearby accessible parking.
2. $\text{Why it's hard}$ — Both are good values, and they pull in different directions.
3. $\text{A possible response}$ — Keep an accessible parking zone at one end of the market.
4. $\text{Who might still be unhappy}$ — Anyone who wanted the whole street car-free, or who still can't park nearby.

:::keep-together

### Why is it hard?

**Why is Ines's parking problem hard for the council, even though most people support the market?**

☐ Ines doesn't really like the idea of a market

☐ Councils aren't allowed to think about parking

☐ A car-free street and nearby parking pull different ways

☐ The survey shows that parking doesn't matter to anyone

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A car-free street and nearby parking pull different ways

Closing the street and keeping nearby accessible parking pull in different directions. Majority support doesn't solve that; a change to the plan might.

:::


:::keep-together

## No perfect answer


:::keep-together

### Is everyone happy?

**The council keeps an accessible parking zone and allows early deliveries. Is everyone now happy?**

☐ Yes, a compromise always makes everyone happy

☐ Yes, because the council made the decision

☐ No, so the council should give up on the market

☐ Probably not: some may still lose out

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Probably not: some may still lose out

A compromise can reduce the problems without removing them. Some people may still lose out, which is part of why civic decisions are hard.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why a trial?

**Why might a three-month trial help the council?**

☐ It means the council never has to decide

☐ It stops anyone disagreeing during the trial

☐ It gives evidence about what actually happens

☐ It proves the Gazette was right all along

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It gives evidence about what actually happens

A trial fills in missing evidence, such as what happens to sales and how people get there.
