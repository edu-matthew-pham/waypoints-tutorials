# Whose view is it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The council, the newspaper and the shop owners are all talking about the same street. Can any of them be completely neutral?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read four made-up sources on one local decision (closing Main Street for a Saturday market) and work out each one's perspective: who made it, what they want, and what they put in or leave out. Official and factual-looking sources have a perspective too.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say who made a source and what they want to happen. I can find something a source leaves out. I can explain why an official source still has a view.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Which of the four sources has no perspective at all?**

☐ The bus notice, because it only gives the facts

☐ The council notice, because it comes from the government

☐ The editorial, because newspapers report the news

☐ None of them: each comes from someone with a view

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** None of them. The council notice says what the council hopes the market will do. The bus notice is mostly facts, but it's written for the bus company and leaves out who will find the new stop harder to reach. The editorial openly argues for the market. Every source comes from somewhere.

:::

Try it on the board in the tutorial. Was your prediction right? What was different?

## Worked example: reading the council notice

1. $\text{Who made it?}$ — Wattle Creek Council.
2. $\text{What do they want?}$ — To run the trial and hear people's views.
3. $\text{What's in?}$ — Times, dates, and the council's hope that the market will bring the town together.
4. $\text{What's left out?}$ — Who might find Main Street harder to reach on a Saturday.

:::keep-together

### A fact or a hope?

**The council notice says the market 'will bring the town together'. What is that?**

☐ A mistake, because councils aren't allowed to have hopes

☐ A fact, because markets always bring towns together

☐ What the council hopes, not something it can know yet

☐ A fact, because the council wrote it in a notice

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** What the council hopes, not something it can know yet

The trial hasn't happened, so the council can't know yet. It is the council's hope, which tells you its perspective.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is reading the council notice. Click the first line where Nib goes wrong.**

```
 1  The council notice gives the times, the dates and the trial period.
 2  It says the market will bring the town together and support local growers.
 3  Nib says: "The notice comes from the council, so it's just facts, not a view."
 4  Nib says: "So I don't need to ask what the council left out."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: a council notice is official, but it still comes from the council's point of view. It includes the council's hopes and leaves out who might lose out.

## Whose view is missing?


:::keep-together

### Whose view is missing?

**The Gazette editorial says Main Street is 'half empty by ten o'clock'. Whose view is missing from the editorial?**

☐ People who think the street is too quiet

☐ Shops that would gain from more people walking past

☐ People who need to drive in or park nearby

☐ People who like going to markets in other towns

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** People who need to drive in or park nearby

The editorial argues for the market and leaves out people who might lose out, such as those who drive in or need to park nearby.

:::


:::keep-together

### A source that looks neutral

**The bus notice is mostly plain facts. What perspective does it still have?**

☐ None, because a timetable change is only facts

☐ It's the council's view, because buses are public

☐ It doesn't say who will find the new stop hard to reach

☐ It's quietly arguing against the market

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It doesn't say who will find the new stop hard to reach

Facts can still come from a perspective. The bus company tells passengers where to go, but not who will find River Road harder to reach.

:::
