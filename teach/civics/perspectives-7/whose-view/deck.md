# Whose view is it?

## Today

**We are learning to** identify the perspective of a civic source by asking who made it, what they want and what they leave out.

- I can say who made a source and what they want to happen.
- I can find something a source leaves out.
- I can explain why an official source still has a view.

## Why it matters

> The council, the newspaper and the shop owners are all talking about the same street. Can any of them be completely neutral?

<!-- Speaker note: Wattle Creek council is deciding whether to close Main Street to cars on Saturday mornings for a market. Wattle Creek, its council and everyone in these sources are made up. When you read a source about an issue like this, ask three questions: 1. **Who made it?** A council, a newspaper, a group, a person? 2. **What do they want to happen?** 3. **What did they put in, and what did they leave out?** The answers tell you the source's **perspective**: the view it comes from. Here are the sources for this lesson. > **Council notice (Wattle Creek Council).** From March, Main Street will close to cars from 7 am to 1 pm on Saturdays for a three-month trial market. The market will bring the town together and support local growers. Have your say by 28 February. > **Editorial (*Wattle Creek Gazette*).** A Saturday market is exactly what Main Street needs. Markets in other towns draw crowds, and our street is half empty by ten o'clock. A lively street on a Saturday is what holds a town together. The council should make the trial permanent. > **Post (Main Street Traders).** Some of our members welcome the crowds. Others rely on customers who drive in for a quick stop, and on deliveries before noon. We ask the council to keep a loading zone open and to check our sales after the trial. > **Bus notice (Wattle Creek Buses).** During the trial, Route 2 will not stop on Main Street on Saturday mornings. The nearest stop will be on River Road. -->

## Predict

**Which of the four sources has no perspective at all?**

- The bus notice, because it only gives the facts
- The council notice, because it comes from the government
- The editorial, because newspapers report the news
- None of them: each comes from someone with a view

## A fact or a hope?

**The council notice says the market 'will bring the town together'. What is that?**

- A mistake, because councils aren't allowed to have hopes
- A fact, because markets always bring towns together
- What the council hopes, not something it can know yet
- A fact, because the council wrote it in a notice

* Answer: What the council hopes, not something it can know yet

<!-- Speaker note: The trial hasn't happened, so the council can't know yet. It is the council's hope, which tells you its perspective. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is reading the council notice. Click the first line where Nib goes wrong.**

```
 1  The council notice gives the times, the dates and the trial period.
 2  It says the market will bring the town together and support local growers.
 3  Nib says: "The notice comes from the council, so it's just facts, not a view."
 4  Nib says: "So I don't need to ask what the council left out."
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: a council notice is official, but it still comes from the council's point of view. It includes the council's hopes and leaves out who might lose out. -->

## Official but not neutral

**Why does an official council notice still have a perspective?**

- It's written by the council, which has its own aims
- Because councils always get the facts wrong
- Because it's printed rather than spoken
- It doesn't; official notices are always neutral

* Answer: It's written by the council, which has its own aims

## Whose view is missing?

**The Gazette editorial says Main Street is 'half empty by ten o'clock'. Whose view is missing from the editorial?**

- People who think the street is too quiet
- Shops that would gain from more people walking past
- People who need to drive in or park nearby
- People who like going to markets in other towns

* Answer: People who need to drive in or park nearby

<!-- Speaker note: The editorial argues for the market and leaves out people who might lose out, such as those who drive in or need to park nearby. -->

## A source that looks neutral

**The bus notice is mostly plain facts. What perspective does it still have?**

- None, because a timetable change is only facts
- It's the council's view, because buses are public
- It doesn't say who will find the new stop hard to reach
- It's quietly arguing against the market

* Answer: It doesn't say who will find the new stop hard to reach

<!-- Speaker note: Facts can still come from a perspective. The bus company tells passengers where to go, but not who will find River Road harder to reach. -->
