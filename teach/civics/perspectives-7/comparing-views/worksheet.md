# Why views differ

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The newspaper and the shop owners disagree about the market. Does one of them have to be wrong?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare made-up sources on the Wattle Creek market (an editorial, a traders' post, a resident's letter and a youth council survey): where they agree, where they disagree, and why, using four reasons views differ: values, interests, information and role.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find a point where two sources agree and a point where they disagree. I can explain why their views differ, using values, interests, information or role. I can explain why a disagreement doesn't mean someone is lying.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**The Gazette wants the market made permanent. Some traders want a loading zone and their sales checked first. What best explains the difference?**

☐ One of them must be lying about the facts

☐ They don't really disagree; they want the same thing

☐ They care about different things and have different things at stake

☐ The newspaper knows more, so the traders are wrong

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** They care about different things (a lively street, or customers who drive in) and have different things at stake (the paper's town, the traders' sales). Neither has to be lying. They even agree on some points: both expect the market to bring crowds.

:::

## Worked example: agree, disagree, why

1. $\text{Agree}$ — Ines and the Gazette both like the idea of a market.
2. $\text{Disagree}$ — The Gazette wants it as planned; Ines needs nearby parking first.
3. $\text{Why}$ — Different needs and values: getting to the shops, or a lively street.

:::keep-together

### Where do they agree?

**Ines likes the idea of a market but is worried about parking. The Gazette wants the market made permanent. Where do they agree?**

☐ They don't agree on anything

☐ Both think parking doesn't matter

☐ Both like the idea of a market

☐ Both want the street open to cars

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Both like the idea of a market

Both like the idea of a market. They disagree on whether it can go ahead as planned, because Ines needs to be able to park nearby.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is comparing Ines's letter and the Gazette editorial. Click the first line where Nib goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ines likes the idea of a market but needs to park near the chemist.
 2  The Gazette wants the market made permanent.
 3  Nib says: "They disagree, so one of them must be lying."
 4  Nib says: "I just need to work out which one is telling the truth."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: they disagree because they have different needs and care about different things, not because one is lying. Ines needs nearby parking; the Gazette wants a lively street.

## Comparing with the survey


:::keep-together

### Who was against?

**In the survey, which age group had more people against the market than for it?**

☐ 18 to 64

☐ 12 to 17

☐ 65 and over

☐ None of them

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 65 and over

In the 65 and over group, 30 were against and 20 supported it. Every other group had more support.

:::


:::keep-together

### A letter and a survey

**Most people in the survey support the market. Does that make Ines's letter less important?**

☐ Yes, she's only one person out of 200

☐ No, because one letter outweighs any survey

☐ Yes, letters don't count once there's a survey

☐ No, it shows a need the numbers don't explain

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, it shows a need the numbers don't explain

The survey shows how many people support the idea. Ines's letter shows a specific problem the council would need to solve.
