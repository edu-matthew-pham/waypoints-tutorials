# Why views differ

Whose view is it? · Whose view is it?

## Today

**We are learning to** compare two civic sources and explain why their perspectives differ.

- I can find a point where two sources agree and a point where they disagree.
- I can explain why their views differ, using values, interests, information or role.
- I can explain why a disagreement doesn't mean someone is lying.

## Why it matters

> The newspaper and the shop owners disagree about the market. Does one of them have to be wrong?

## Think, pair, share

**If two people disagree about the market, does one of them have to be wrong?**

- What does each source care about, or stand to gain or lose?
- What does each one know that the other might not?

## Predict

**The Gazette wants the market made permanent. Some traders want a loading zone and their sales checked first. What best explains the difference?**

- One of them must be lying about the facts
- They don't really disagree; they want the same thing
- They care about different things and have different things at stake
- The newspaper knows more, so the traders are wrong

**Ines likes the idea of a market but is worried about parking. The Gazette wants the market made permanent. Where do they agree?**

- Both think parking doesn't matter
- Both like the idea of a market
- Both want the street open to cars

* Answer: Both like the idea of a market

## Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is comparing Ines's letter and the Gazette editorial. Click the first line where Nib goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ines likes the idea of a market but needs to park near the chemist.
 2  The Gazette wants the market made permanent.
 3  Nib says: "They disagree, so one of them must be lying."
 4  Nib says: "I just need to work out which one is telling the truth."
```

* Answer: line 3

## A letter and a survey

**Most people in the survey support the market. Does that make Ines's letter less important?**

- Yes, she's only one person out of 200
- No, because one letter outweighs any survey
- Yes, letters don't count once there's a survey
- No, it shows a need the numbers don't explain

* Answer: No, it shows a need the numbers don't explain
