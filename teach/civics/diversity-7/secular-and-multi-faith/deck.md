## Today

**We are learning to** explain what secular, multi-faith and pluralist mean and why Australia can be secular and multi-faith at once.

- I can explain secular, multi-faith and pluralist in my own words.
- I can explain that secular describes the government, not the people.
- I can say what section 116 of the Constitution stops the Commonwealth doing.

## Why it matters

> Australia is called secular. Does that mean religion has no place here?

<!-- Speaker note: Three words describe how Australia handles religion and culture: - **Secular**: the government is separate from religion. It doesn't set up an official religion, and it doesn't make anyone follow one. - **Multi-faith**: many religions are practised here, and many people have no religion. - **Pluralist**: people with different beliefs and cultures live side by side, and that variety is accepted and respected. Secular is about the **government**. It says nothing about what people believe. A secular country can have lots of religious people in it. In this lesson, Wattle Creek and its people are made up. The Constitution is real. -->

## Which law isn't allowed?

**Section 116 of the Constitution limits the Commonwealth. Which of these would it NOT allow?**

- A town festival where people of different faiths share food
- A federal law making one religion Australia's official religion
- A family choosing to follow no religion at all
- A Member of Parliament who goes to a place of worship each week

* Answer: A federal law making one religion Australia's official religion

<!-- Speaker note: Section 116 stops the Commonwealth from setting up a religion, forcing or stopping religious practice, or using a religious test for a Commonwealth job. -->

## Secular and multi-faith

**How can Australia be secular and multi-faith at the same time?**

- Secular means most people have no religion, so both fit
- The government sets up no religion, while people follow many or none
- The government chooses which religions people are allowed to follow
- Secular and multi-faith mean the same thing, so there's no conflict

* Answer: The government sets up no religion, while people follow many or none

<!-- Speaker note: Secular describes the government. Multi-faith describes society. One doesn't cancel the other. -->
