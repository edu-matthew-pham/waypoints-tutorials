# Secular, pluralist, multi-faith

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Australia is called secular. Does that mean religion has no place here?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that Australia has a secular government in a multi-faith, pluralist society: section 116 limits the Commonwealth, not people's beliefs, and religious people take part in public life. They define secular, multi-faith, pluralist and culturally diverse (a diverse society is used in the text, not matched as a separate term: it can't be told apart from culturally diverse in a one-line match).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain secular, multi-faith and pluralist in my own words. I can explain that secular describes the government, not the people. I can say what section 116 of the Constitution stops the Commonwealth doing.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Yes. None of the rules for standing for federal Parliament is about religion, and the Constitution bans religious tests for Commonwealth jobs. A secular government doesn't mean religious people stay out of public life.

## Worked example: what section 116 says

1. $\text{Section 116}$ — The Commonwealth can't set up a religion or force anyone to practise one.
2. $\text{It also}$ — can't stop people practising a religion, or use a religious test for a Commonwealth job.
3. $\text{So}$ — the government is secular, while the people can be religious or not.

:::keep-together

### Which law isn't allowed?

**Section 116 of the Constitution limits the Commonwealth. Which of these would it NOT allow?**

☐ A town festival where people of different faiths share food

☐ A federal law making one religion Australia's official religion

☐ A family choosing to follow no religion at all

☐ A Member of Parliament who goes to a place of worship each week

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A federal law making one religion Australia's official religion

Section 116 stops the Commonwealth from setting up a religion, forcing or stopping religious practice, or using a religious test for a Commonwealth job.

:::


:::keep-together

## Secular and multi-faith together


:::keep-together

### Secular and multi-faith

**How can Australia be secular and multi-faith at the same time?**

☐ Secular means most people have no religion, so both fit

☐ The government sets up no religion, while people follow many or none

☐ The government chooses which religions people are allowed to follow

☐ Secular and multi-faith mean the same thing, so there's no conflict

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The government sets up no religion, while people follow many or none

Secular describes the government. Multi-faith describes society. One doesn't cancel the other.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which word fits?

**A made-up Wattle Creek club says, 'We welcome people of every faith and none, and we all respect each other's views.' Which word fits best?**

☐ Culturally diverse

☐ Pluralist

☐ Multi-faith

☐ Secular

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Pluralist

Pluralist means different beliefs and cultures are accepted and respected side by side.
