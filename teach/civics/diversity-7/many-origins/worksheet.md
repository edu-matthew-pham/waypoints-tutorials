# Many origins, one democracy

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Is Australia's religious diversity something new?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students bring the course together: Australia's pluralist, multi-faith society has several origins (First Nations peoples, Christian and Western heritage, migrant communities), and real, dated ABS Census data shows religious affiliation changing from 2011 to 2021. They work out the changes in a small spreadsheet and learn what a share can and can't show.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name three origins of Australia's pluralist, multi-faith society. I can work out and describe a change in Census data, with its years. I can say what Census religion data can and can't tell us.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Between the 2011 and 2021 Censuses, which answer to the religion question went up the most as a share of Australians?**

☐ Islam

☐ Christianity

☐ No religion

☐ Buddhism

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** No religion: from about 22% of Australians in 2011 to about 39% in 2021. Christianity went down as a share, though it was still the largest group. Islam and Hinduism went up a little, and Buddhism stayed about the same.

:::

## Worked example: reading the data carefully

1. $\text{Several origins}$ — First Nations peoples, Christian and Western heritage, and migrant communities.
2. $\text{Over a long time}$ — The 2011 Census already shows many religions.
3. $\text{Read with care}$ — A share is part of a whole, the year matters, and the question is optional.

:::keep-together

### Shares and numbers

**Australia's population grew between 2011 and 2021. Buddhism's share stayed about the same (2.5% to 2.4%). What happened to the number of people who answered Buddhism?**

☐ It went down, because the share dropped a little

☐ You can't say anything at all from shares

☐ It went up, since it's the same share of more people

☐ It stayed exactly the same, because the share hardly moved

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It went up, since it's the same share of more people

A share is part of the whole. When the whole grows, the same share means more people.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is reading the Census table. Click the first line where Nib goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib looks at the Census table for 2011, 2016 and 2021.
 2  The table shows several religions, and people with no religion, in every year.
 3  Nib says: "Australia only became multi-faith in the last few years, and it all came from one group."
 4  Nib says: "So before 2021 there was only one religion here."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the table shows Islam, Hinduism and Buddhism already there in 2011, and migrant communities have been coming for a long time. Australia's diversity has several origins, not one, and it isn't new.

## Many origins


:::keep-together

### Story time in five languages

**A made-up Wattle Creek library runs story time in five languages because families in the town speak them at home. Which origin of Australia's diversity does this mostly show?**

☐ Christian heritage

☐ Migrant communities

☐ Western law

☐ The secular government

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Migrant communities

Many languages spoken at home come from migrant communities who settled in Australia over many years.

:::


:::keep-together

### Pluralist or diverse?

**Why is Australia called pluralist, not just diverse?**

☐ One culture is official and the others join it

☐ There are lots of groups, whether or not they get along

☐ Different groups are accepted and respected side by side

☐ Every group lives separately and keeps to itself

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Different groups are accepted and respected side by side

Diverse means many groups. Pluralist means that variety is accepted and respected side by side.
