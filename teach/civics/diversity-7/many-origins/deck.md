## Today

**We are learning to** explain how migrant communities, with First Nations peoples and Christian and Western heritage, make up Australia's pluralist society, and describe a Census trend with its years.

- I can name three origins of Australia's pluralist, multi-faith society.
- I can work out and describe a change in Census data, with its years.
- I can say what Census religion data can and can't tell us.

## Why it matters

> Is Australia's religious diversity something new?

<!-- Speaker note: Australia's diverse, multi-faith society has several origins: - **First Nations peoples**, with many distinct communities, histories and cultures. Your teacher will take these up with local material. - **Christian and Western heritage**, which came with the British colonies (last lesson). - **Migrant communities**, who have come from many countries over a long time, bringing their languages, cultures and religions. The **Census** is a count of everyone in Australia, run by the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) every five years. One question asks about religion. It is the only question you don't have to answer. In this lesson the Census figures are real, and each one has its year. Wattle Creek and Nib's ideas are made up. -->

## Shares and numbers

**Australia's population grew between 2011 and 2021. Buddhism's share stayed about the same (2.5% to 2.4%). What happened to the number of people who answered Buddhism?**

- It went down, because the share dropped a little
- You can't say anything at all from shares
- It went up, since it's the same share of more people
- It stayed exactly the same, because the share hardly moved

* Answer: It went up, since it's the same share of more people

<!-- Speaker note: A share is part of the whole. When the whole grows, the same share means more people. -->
