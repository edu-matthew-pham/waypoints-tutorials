# Christian and Western heritage

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Federal Parliament starts each sitting day with a prayer. Does that make Australia's government religious?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify features of Australia's political and legal systems that come from Christian heritage (Parliament's prayers, Christmas and Easter holidays, the oath) and Western (British) traditions (common law, Westminster, juries), and explain that a tradition is not an official religion. Weighing how much heritage should shape public life is discussion only.

**Students should be able to say:** I can give examples of Christian and Western heritage in Australia's political and legal systems. I can explain why a tradition isn't the same as an official religion. I can describe different views about heritage without saying who is right.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** No. The prayers are a tradition in each chamber's own rules, and members don't have to be there or take part. The Commonwealth still can't set up a religion or make anyone practise one (section 116). The prayers do still happen.

## Worked example: a tradition, not a rule

1. $\text{Prayers}$ — A tradition from Christian heritage; no one has to take part.
2. $\text{Oath or affirmation}$ — Both are allowed, so every member can make the same promise.
3. $\text{Section 116}$ — The Commonwealth still can't set up a religion or force anyone to practise one.

:::keep-together

### Oath or affirmation?

**New members of federal Parliament can take an oath (a promise that names God) or an affirmation (the same promise without naming God). Why does the Constitution offer both?**

☐ So only Christian members can name God when they make the promise

☐ So members of any belief, or none, can make the same promise

☐ Because the oath is for senators and the affirmation is for the House

☐ Because the affirmation is a weaker promise for less important members

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** So members of any belief, or none, can make the same promise

The oath comes from Christian tradition. The affirmation lets every member make the same promise, whatever they believe.

:::

## Heritage and the law today


:::keep-together

### A public holiday

**Christmas Day is a public holiday everywhere in Australia. What does that show?**

☐ A Christian festival became a holiday everyone gets

☐ Everyone in Australia has to celebrate Christmas

☐ Only Christians are allowed the day off work

☐ The government chose Christianity as the official religion

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A Christian festival became a holiday everyone gets

The holiday comes from Christian heritage, but it is a day off for everyone, and no one has to celebrate it.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which tradition?

**Which of these comes from Western (British) traditions of law, not from a religion?**

☐ Christmas Day as a public holiday

☐ The words of the oath that name God

☐ Parliament's prayers at the start of the day

☐ Courts following earlier decisions (precedent)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Courts following earlier decisions (precedent)

Precedent and the common law came from English law, which is part of Western heritage.

:::
