## Today

**We are learning to** identify features of Australia's political and legal systems that come from Christian and Western heritage and explain why a tradition is not an official religion.

- I can give examples of Christian and Western heritage in Australia's political and legal systems.
- I can explain why a tradition isn't the same as an official religion.
- I can describe different views about heritage without saying who is right.

## Why it matters

> Federal Parliament starts each sitting day with a prayer. Does that make Australia's government religious?

<!-- Speaker note: When the British colonies became Australia, they brought ideas and traditions with them. Some came from **Christian heritage**, the Christian religion that most of the colonists followed. Others came from **Western heritage**, the wider British and European traditions of government and law. Some of that heritage is still part of Australia's political and legal systems. A tradition that comes from a religion is different from an official religion, though. Australia's government is secular, as you saw in the last lesson. People weigh this heritage differently. Some see it as a main foundation of Australia's laws and values; others put more weight on the other origins you'll meet in this course. This heritage has also been experienced differently by different communities, including First Nations communities. Your teacher may take this up with local material. In this lesson, the class and its table are made up. The traditions are real. -->

## Oath or affirmation?

**New members of federal Parliament can take an oath (a promise that names God) or an affirmation (the same promise without naming God). Why does the Constitution offer both?**

- So only Christian members can name God when they make the promise
- So members of any belief, or none, can make the same promise
- Because the oath is for senators and the affirmation is for the House
- Because the affirmation is a weaker promise for less important members

* Answer: So members of any belief, or none, can make the same promise

<!-- Speaker note: The oath comes from Christian tradition. The affirmation lets every member make the same promise, whatever they believe. -->
