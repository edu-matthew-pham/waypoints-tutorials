# Spending choices

Before Year 7: HASS · Economics and Business

<!-- Speaker note: Students name the influences on a spending choice (money, people, advertising, and what they care about) and see that every choice gives something up. ## Today

**We are learning to** name influences on a spending choice and say what the choice gives up.

- I can name four kinds of influence on what people buy.
- I can spot the influences behind a choice.
- I can say what a choice gives up.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Year 6 catch-up for AC9HS6K08 n1 (influences on consumer choices). ## Why it matters

> Mia picked a new drink bottle in under a minute. Was it really just because she liked it?

<!-- Speaker note: When we buy something, lots of things push and pull on the choice. These are **influences**: - **Money:** how much it costs, and how much you have. - **People:** what friends and family have, use or say. - **Advertising:** ads, packaging and displays made to catch your eye. - **What you care about:** the environment, fairness, quality, or what your family or culture values. You still choose. Noticing the influences helps you choose **on purpose**. And every choice gives something up: the money you spend on one thing can't buy another. Mia and the shop are made up. Riverbend is a made-up town. -->

## Think first

**Mia chose a $20 metal drink bottle over a $6 plastic one. Her best friend has the same metal one, she saw an ad for it last week, and she wants to stop buying plastic. Why did she choose it?**

- Nothing influenced her
- Only because it was the cheapest
- Several influences together: a friend, an ad and what she cares about
- Only because she liked it

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Several things pushed the same way: her friend has one, she'd seen an ad, and she cares about using less plastic. It wasn't the cheapest, so money pulled the other way. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

**Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Half of Nib's class wears the same brand of shoes.
 2  Nib has seen ads for them at the bus stop all month, and buys a pair.
 3  Nib says: "Nobody influenced me. I just picked what I liked."
 4  Nib says: "So ads and friends don't affect what I buy."
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: half the class having the same shoes, and the ads everywhere, are influences. Nib still chose, but not from nowhere. -->

## Advertising

**Which of these is an influence from advertising?**

- A friend's new shoes
- Having $30 to spend
- Wanting things that last
- A bright display at the shop door

* Answer: A bright display at the shop door

<!-- Speaker note: Displays are made to catch your eye. The others are money, people and what you care about. -->

## What she gives up

**Mia spends her $20 on the bottle. What does she give up?**

- Her friend
- Nothing, because she wanted it
- The next best thing she could have bought with that $20
- The plastic bottle she didn't buy, and everything else in the shop

* Answer: The next best thing she could have bought with that $20

<!-- Speaker note: Every choice gives something up: the next best thing that money could have bought. -->
