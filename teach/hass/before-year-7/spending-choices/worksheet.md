# Spending choices

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mia picked a new drink bottle in under a minute. Was it really just because she liked it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students name the influences on a spending choice (money, people, advertising, and what they care about) and see that every choice gives something up. The common mistake is thinking nothing influences what we buy.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name four kinds of influence on what people buy. I can spot the influences behind a choice. I can say what a choice gives up.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Think first


:::keep-together

**Mia chose a $20 metal drink bottle over a $6 plastic one. Her best friend has the same metal one, she saw an ad for it last week, and she wants to stop buying plastic. Why did she choose it?**

☐ Nothing influenced her

☐ Only because it was the cheapest

☐ Several influences together: a friend, an ad and what she cares about

☐ Only because she liked it

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Several things pushed the same way: her friend has one, she'd seen an ad, and she cares about using less plastic. It wasn't the cheapest, so money pulled the other way.

:::

## The idea: noticing the influences


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Half of Nib's class wears the same brand of shoes.
 2  Nib has seen ads for them at the bus stop all month, and buys a pair.
 3  Nib says: "Nobody influenced me. I just picked what I liked."
 4  Nib says: "So ads and friends don't affect what I buy."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: half the class having the same shoes, and the ads everywhere, are influences. Nib still chose, but not from nowhere.

:::


:::keep-together

### Advertising

**Which of these is an influence from advertising?**

☐ A friend's new shoes

☐ Having $30 to spend

☐ Wanting things that last

☐ A bright display at the shop door

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A bright display at the shop door

Displays are made to catch your eye. The others are money, people and what you care about.

:::


:::keep-together

### What she gives up

**Mia spends her $20 on the bottle. What does she give up?**

☐ Her friend

☐ Nothing, because she wanted it

☐ The next best thing she could have bought with that $20

☐ The plastic bottle she didn't buy, and everything else in the shop

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The next best thing she could have bought with that $20

Every choice gives something up: the next best thing that money could have bought.

:::
