# Questions that guide an inquiry

Before Year 7: HASS · Inquiry skills

<!-- Speaker note: Students see that an inquiry needs a big question and several smaller guiding questions (what, where, who, why). About 20 minutes (Think first and Try 6 min · The idea 6 min · Check 3 min · Make 5 min.). -->

---

## Today

- I can break a big question into smaller ones.
- I can ask what, where, who and why questions about a topic.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a Year 6 catch-up for AC9HS6S01. Year 7 Investigate like a humanities expert lesson 1 builds on it. Any topic works; the garden is made up. Teacher: Try: ask which small questions would help. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Your class wants to find out about the school garden. Is one question enough?

<!-- Speaker note: A big question like "How does our school garden help us?" is hard to answer in one go. **Smaller questions** break it into parts, such as *What grows there? Who looks after it? Why was it started?* Together they guide your inquiry. The garden here is made up. -->

## Which one asks why

**Which small question asks WHY?**

- What grows in it?
- Who looks after it?
- Why was the garden started?
- Where is it?

* Answer: Why was the garden started?

## Check: before you move on

**Big question: How do people in Riverbend get around? Which small question starts with the word that fits?**

Groups: Who · Where · What

___ do most people use buses? · ___ are the bus stops? · ___ kinds of transport are there?

* Answer: ___ do most people use buses? → Who; ___ are the bus stops? → Where; ___ kinds of transport are there? → What
