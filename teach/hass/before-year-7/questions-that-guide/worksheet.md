# Questions that guide an inquiry

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Your class wants to find out about the school garden. Is one question enough?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that an inquiry needs a big question and several smaller guiding questions (what, where, who, why).

**Students should be able to say:** I can break a big question into smaller ones. I can ask what, where, who and why questions about a topic.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

Line: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** line 3

**Answer:** Why was the garden started?

Why asks for a reason.
