## Why it matters

> A pothole on your street, a new school and the money in your pocket. Is one government in charge of all three?

<!-- Speaker note: Australia has **three levels of government**. Each one looks after different things, and each has its own elected representatives. - **Local councils** look after things in your local area: rubbish and recycling, parks and sports fields, local roads, libraries and pet control. - **State and territory governments** look after things across a state or territory: schools, hospitals, police and public transport. - **The federal government** (the Australian Government) looks after things that affect the whole country: money, defence, immigration, and post and the internet. Each level has people you elect, and each can make laws for its own jobs. A council makes local laws, such as rules for dogs in parks. A state parliament makes laws for the state, such as road rules. The federal Parliament makes laws for the whole country. Three kinds of institution run through the system. **Parliaments** make laws. **Courts** decide what the law means when people disagree. And the **King**, represented by the Governor-General (and a Governor in each state), signs laws passed by Parliament. **If you live in Canberra:** the ACT has no local councils. The ACT Government does both the state jobs and the local jobs. Wattle Creek, where these examples happen, is a made-up town. -->

## Why three levels?

**Why does Australia have three levels of government?**

- So one level makes all the laws for the other two
- So the federal government can run every council's work
- So a state can overrule the federal government
- So town, state and country matters each get looked after

* Answer: So town, state and country matters each get looked after
