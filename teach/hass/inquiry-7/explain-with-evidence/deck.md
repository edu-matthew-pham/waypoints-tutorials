# Explain with evidence

Investigate like a humanities expert · Sharing what you found

## Today

**We are learning to** explain why or how something happened by linking named evidence to a claim.

- I can make a claim and name evidence for it.
- I can explain how evidence supports a claim.
- I can keep an explanation within the evidence.

## Why it matters

> A fact beside a claim does not explain it. The reader needs the connection.

## The idea: claim, evidence, explain

**Match each part of a Kilby River explanation to its job.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Less rain helped cause less water to flow into the dam.` | A. Evidence: named findings from records |
| `Record G1 shows 20 mm of rain this month and 60 mm in the same month last year. Record G2 shows about 3 ML a day flowing in this month, and 8 ML a day last year.` | B. Reasoning: the connection and its limits |
| `Less rain could mean less water running off the land into the dam. The lower flow fits this, but other causes need checking.` | C. Claim: the answer being offered |

* Answer: Less rain helped cause less water to flow into the dam. → C. Claim: the answer being offered; Record G1 shows 20 mm of rain this month and 60 mm in the same month last year. Record G2 shows about 3 ML a day flowing in this month, and 8 ML a day last year. → A. Evidence: named findings from records; Less rain could mean less water running off the land into the dam. The lower flow fits this, but other causes need checking. → B. Reasoning: the connection and its limits

## Check: connect both findings

**Using G1 and G2 above, which conclusion fits the evidence best?**

- The records prove that less rain was the only reason less water flowed in.
- Less water flowed in, so the town must have used more water this year.
- Less rain probably meant less runoff, which fits the lower flow into the dam.
- Rain and flow both went down, but there's no way to link the two.

* Answer: Less rain probably meant less runoff, which fits the lower flow into the dam.

<!-- Speaker note: The best conclusion links both records through how water moves, and says "probably" because other causes could also play a part. What this step is for: A geography explanation links both findings to how water moves, without claiming they prove the only cause. -->

## Apply: write and compare

Choose **your current subject’s** task below, using the supplied records or real sources approved by your teacher. Meet its evidence requirements; another case is optional practice on the same move.

- **Economics:** explain why Parkside cannot buy both. Include the cost table, refer to E1 and E2 and use *scarcity* and *opportunity cost*. Compare with the worked example.
- **Civics:** explain how C1 enables participation. Model: *Notice C1 invites comments before a decision, so people can participate by contributing views during consultation.*
- **Geography:** explain how less rain could lead to less water flowing into the dam. Use **both G1 and G2**. Link each one to how water moves through the catchment, and say what else might have played a part. Compare with the matching example.
- **History:** explain why the mirror might look different now. Use **both M1 and M2**, name *cause* or *change*, and explain rather than list. Model: *M1 records a polished face. M2 says wear may have dulled it, so wear could explain the change. We can't be sure exactly how it first looked.*

Real History work needs verified sources and a historical content check. Write in your own book or document; the site does not collect or mark it. For paragraph organisation, try English's [Inside a paragraph](../../../english/cohesion/inside-a-paragraph/).
