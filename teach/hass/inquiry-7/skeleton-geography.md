## 1. Ask and plan a geographical inquiry

**You will:** Write at least two focused geographical questions about the same phenomenon or challenge. Plan at least two types of evidence and a collection method for each, explaining how each will help.

### Good looks like

**Geographical questions vs general questions**

☐ Identifies at least two features that make a question geographical and applies these to classify example questions.

☐ Transforms at least one general question into a geographical inquiry question, explaining what geographical dimension has been added.

**Developing questions for a specific phenomenon or challenge**

☐ Develops at least two geographical inquiry questions about a specific phenomenon or challenge that are specific and investigable with real evidence.

☐ Explains what geographical phenomenon, process or concept each question targets.

**Planning a geographical investigation**

☐ Produces an investigation plan that specifies the inquiry question, at least two types of evidence needed, and a collection method for each.

☐ Explains how the planned evidence would allow the inquiry question to be answered.

**Need help? Try…**

- Can this be investigated?
- Build a question from a big idea
- Right-sized, and what would answer it

:::teacher-note

Supported on the site by the three Asking questions lessons. Check that students' questions name a specific place, spatial distribution or environmental/human connection.

:::


## 2. Collect and represent data (fieldwork and secondary)

**You will:** Collect primary fieldwork observations and secondary data (such as maps, climate tables or ABS statistics) and represent them in an appropriate geographical format.

### Good looks like

**Secondary research: finding and selecting relevant materials**

☐ Locates at least two secondary sources relevant to a geographical inquiry question and explains the criteria used to assess each source's relevance.

☐ Uses at least one advanced search method to find sources and reflects on how it improved search results.

**Representing data in appropriate geographical formats**

☐ Represents geographical data in at least one format (map, table, graph) that is appropriate to the data type and inquiry question.

☐ Applies cartographic conventions (title, scale, north arrow, legend) to any map produced.

**Integration: collect, organise and represent in an inquiry context**

☐ Produces an evidence base using at least one primary and one secondary source, represented in appropriate formats for the inquiry question.

☐ Reflects on one limitation or gap in the evidence base and explains what additional evidence would address it.

**Need help? Try…**

- Where to look, and how to search
- Keep what answers your question
- Organise what you found

## 3. Analyse patterns, trends and relationships

**You will:** Compare your data and maps to identify similarities and differences, describe spatial patterns and trends, and infer geographical relationships.

### Good looks like

**Explaining geographical patterns and trends**

☐ Identifies at least one geographical pattern or trend in data and explains it by reference to a geographical process.

☐ Distinguishes between describing a pattern (what is observed) and explaining it (why it occurs).

**Inferring geographical relationships**

☐ Infers at least one relationship from geographical data and identifies the evidence that supports the inference.

☐ Notes at least one limitation of the inference or an alternative explanation that the data cannot rule out.

**Integration: interpret and analyse geographical data**

☐ Produces a data analysis that demonstrates at least two of: identifying similarities/differences, explaining patterns/trends, inferring relationships.

☐ Grounds each analytical claim in specific data evidence.

**Need help? Try…**

- Geography skills: Interpreting geographical data and patterns *(Coming later)*

:::teacher-note

The student sheet prints `n2`–`n4` (patterns, relationships and integrated analysis); check basic comparison of similarities and differences (`AC9HG7S03 n1`) as students read their tables and maps. Model naming where a pattern holds and any exception (anomaly).

:::


## 4. Draw geographical conclusions

**You will:** Use geographical concepts (such as place, interconnection, environment or sustainability) to organise your findings and explain impacts on people, places and environments.

### Good looks like

**Geographical concepts as conclusion organisers**

☐ Names the geographical concept used to organise the conclusion and explains how it frames the analytical finding.

☐ Produces a conclusion that is structured by a geographical concept, not just a summary of data findings.

**Explaining impacts on people, places and environments**

☐ Explains at least one impact on people and one impact on places or environments, connecting each to the geographical phenomenon studied.

☐ Uses analysis evidence to support each impact claim.

**Conclusions as answers to the inquiry question**

☐ Produces a conclusion that directly responds to the inquiry question.

☐ Supports the conclusion with at least two analytical findings, using geographical terminology.

**Need help? Try…**

- Geography › Water as a resource: Putting it together: classify, connect, change *(joins resource classification, spatial connection and environmental/human effects)*
- Geography skills: Drawing conclusions about impacts *(Coming later)*

:::teacher-note

Lesson 5 of Water as a resource (`geography/water-7/classify-connect-change`) models joining classification, connection and change into a conclusion. Check that students' conclusions directly answer their Stage 1 question.

:::


## 5. Propose strategies for action

**You will:** Propose an individual or collective strategy to respond to the geographical challenge, explain its environmental, economic or social impacts, and note how values shape the choice.

### Good looks like

**Collective action and geographical change**

☐ Proposes at least one collective action and explains how it would address a geographical challenge.

☐ Identifies the scale at which the collective action operates and explains why that scale is appropriate.

**Personal values and explanation of potential impacts**

☐ Identifies at least one personal value or attitude that influences their explanation of a potential impact.

☐ Explains how a different value perspective would produce a different assessment of the same impact.

**Integration: strategy, impact and values**

☐ Produces a strategy proposal with at least two potential impacts explained.

☐ Reflects on at least one value dimension that shapes the impact explanation.

**Need help? Try…**

- Geography skills: Strategies for action and weighing values *(Coming later)*

:::teacher-note

The student sheet prints `n2`–`n4` (collective action, values/impacts and integrated strategy); check individual action options (`AC9HG7S05 n1`) alongside them. Remind students that environmental, economic and social priorities can pull in different directions, so choosing a 'best' strategy depends on how a community weights those values.

:::


## 6. Communicate your geographical explanation

**You will:** Write a geographical description and explanation that uses geographical concepts, terms, research evidence and representations to answer your question.

### Good looks like

**Geographical explanations supported by research findings**

☐ Produces a geographical explanation that integrates at least two research findings as evidence within the explanation (not just appended).

☐ Explains how each piece of evidence supports the geographical idea being argued.

**Geographical representations as communication tools**

☐ Includes at least one geographical representation in a response and explains how it supports the argument.

☐ Integrates the representation into the text of the response rather than presenting it as a separate appendix.

**Integration: create complete geographical responses**

☐ Produces a geographical response that includes description, explanation supported by evidence, and at least one geographical representation.

☐ Uses geographical terminology accurately and consistently throughout.

**Need help? Try…**

- Use the subject's words
- Explain with evidence
- Geography skills: Communicating with maps and representations *(Coming later)*

:::teacher-note

The student sheet prints `n2`–`n4` (evidence-backed explanations, representations and complete response); check accurate geographical terminology and concepts (`AC9HG7S06 n1`, taught in `use-the-subjects-words`) throughout.

:::
