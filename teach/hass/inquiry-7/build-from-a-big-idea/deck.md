# Build a question from a big idea

Investigate like a humanities expert · Asking questions

<!-- Speaker note: Students see that a subject's big ideas (scarcity, power, change, cause) are lenses for investigating topics, and practise building a question by naming a case and asking something about the idea that evidence can answer. Geography's and History's hinge nodes (using their concepts as question frames) are covered here in general; each subject's own lesson goes further. About 40 minutes (Think first and Try 10 min · The idea 10 min · Vary 6 min · Check 5 min · Make 6 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

## Today

- I can name a big idea from my subject.
- I can tell a topic from a question.
- I can build an investigable question from a big idea and a case.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The nodes this serves are marked as hinges in Geography and History (concepts as question frames) and not in Economics and Civics, but the move is the same, so the lesson is the same for all four. ## Why it matters

> Nib's class is told to write a question using a big idea. Nib writes one word: 'Scarcity.' Is that a question?

<!-- Speaker note: Every subject has a few **big ideas**: words that show up again and again because they help you ask good questions. A big idea is a **concept** that helps you look at a topic in a particular way. The mirror is a topic; change and significance are different lenses for investigating it. A concept word on its own is not yet a question. The cases are the same made-up ones as in the last lesson: the Parkside tuckshop, Mill Road, the Kilby River and the Hartley Museum's bronze mirror. -->

**Is one big idea enough to make a good question, or do you need a case as well?**

## In each subject

The move is the same: take a big idea, name a case, ask something about it. The big ideas are what differ.

| | Ask about | A starter that works |
|---|---|---|
| **Economics** | what is given up, who gets what | "What did [group] give up when they chose [this]?" |
| **Civics** | who decides, and by what rules | "Who decides [this], and how?" |
| **Geography** | places, links and changes | "How has [this place] changed, and what changed with it?" |
| **History** | why, what changed, what mattered | "Why did [this] happen, and what changed?" |

History's and Geography's own lessons come back to how to use their ideas well.

## Exit ticket

**How is a question different from a big idea?**

* A big idea is a lens for investigating a topic. A question names a case and asks something about the idea that evidence can answer.
