## Why it matters

> You have five numbers, one for each day. In a paragraph, which day was busiest? In a table?

<!-- Speaker note: After you keep what answers your question, **organise it** so you can see the answer. The shape depends on what you have: numbers over time suit a table or a graph. Views from different sources can be grouped under shared sub-questions or themes, keeping who said what beside each note. Everything here is invented. Parkside has daily buyer counts; Riverbend Council is considering closing Mill Road; Wattle Flat is on the Kilby River in a dry year; Hartley Museum labels a bronze mirror as Han. For Mill Road, compare **ideas across sources**, rather than making separate piles headed “council” and “news”. These invented extracts can be organised like this: | Theme | Council notice C1 | Local news report N1 | |---|---|---| | Access | A timed closure is proposed at school arrival time. | A shop owner says deliveries need road access. | | Participation | Comments are invited before a decision. | Parents say they plan to send comments. | Each row brings related ideas together; the headings keep the source of each note visible. The extracts do not establish whether a closure is safe or what everyone thinks. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

**Click the line where Nib's plan goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib has notes: Monday 212, Tuesday 198, Wednesday 230, Thursday 205, Friday 190.
 2  Nib says: "I will keep my notes in the order I found them, written as sentences."
 3  Nib writes each fact in a paragraph and tries to see which day was busiest.
 4  Nib cannot tell at a glance. Nib decides the notes are fine.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: keeping notes in the order found, as sentences, hides the pattern. Put like with like in a table so the busiest day shows. -->

## Check: before you move on

**You need to compare what C1 and N1 say about access and participation. Which layout helps most?**

- A graph counting words in each source
- Only notes that agree with the closure
- All of C1, then all of N1, in the order copied
- One row per theme, with each source's notes alongside it

* Answer: One row per theme, with each source's notes alongside it
