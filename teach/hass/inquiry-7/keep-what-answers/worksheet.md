# Keep what answers your question

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You find a page full of facts about school canteens. Some are fascinating. How many of them go in your notes?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students keep only what helps answer their question, give a reason that points back to the question, and set the rest aside. Interesting is not relevant.

**Students should be able to say:** I can decide what to keep from a source by asking whether it helps answer my question. I can give a reason for keeping a fact that points back to the question. I can set aside facts that are interesting but not relevant.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: interesting is not the same as relevant. A history of canteens does not tell Nib how many students buy each day. Keep only what answers the question.
