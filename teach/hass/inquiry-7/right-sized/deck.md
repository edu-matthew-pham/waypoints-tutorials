## Think first

**Which question about Mill Road is the right size for a week?**

- Is the council good?
- How many cars use Mill Road at 8:30 on Monday?
- What might closing Mill Road change for students, drivers and shops?
- How has every democracy changed throughout history?

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: The question about effects on students, drivers and shops. It names the case and you could gather evidence (a survey, traffic counts, council papers) and weigh it. 'How has every democracy changed throughout history?' covers too many places and periods. One traffic count is a useful supporting measurement, but does not by itself explain the effects. 'Is the council good?' needs criteria and a clearer focus. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## The right size

**How can you tell a question is the right size?**

- Everyone agrees it is a good question
- It has a one-word answer
- It names a case, and you can say what evidence would answer it and where you would get it
- It is about something important

* Answer: It names a case, and you can say what evidence would answer it and where you would get it

## Evidence for each

**What would answer each question, and where could you get it?**

| | |
|---|---|
| `How does the Parkside tuckshop decide what to stock?` | A. Council rules and meeting records |
| `Who decides whether Mill Road closes, and why?` | B. The stock list, sales records and a talk with the manager |
| `Why did the Kilby River run low last summer?` | C. River height records, rainfall figures and a map of the dam |
| `What does the bronze mirror show about life in Han times?` | D. The mirror, its museum label and what is known about where it was found |

## Check: before you move on

**Which is the best one-line plan for the question 'Why did the Kilby River run low last summer?'**

- Ask one student what they think
- Look at river height records and rainfall figures for last summer, and a map of what draws water from the river
- Read everything about rivers
- Look up the river's length

* Answer: Look at river height records and rainfall figures for last summer, and a map of what draws water from the river

<!-- Speaker note: These records could test possible causes. Include a comparison period and check other influences before concluding why it ran low. What this step is for: A last check on size and evidence across a new case before moving on. -->

## In each subject

Every subject sizes a question the same way. What each one asks you to plan is a little different:

| | Plan to name | Starts well from |
|---|---|---|
| **Economics** | the data and views you would need (prices, sales, choices) | a news story about a choice or a price |
| **Civics** | the rules, records and views you would need | a news story about a decision or a law |
| **Geography** | the evidence and **how you would collect it** (fieldwork or secondary sources, and the tool you would use) | a map, a place or a change |
| **History** | the kinds of evidence you would need (what was made, what was written) | an object or a source |
