## Evidence or not?

**Is it evidence for evolution?**

Groups: Evidence · Not evidence

Fossils in layers, with simpler forms in older rock · Similar bones in a human arm, a bat wing and a whale flipper · DNA more similar between closely related species · Related animals living in the same region, such as Australian marsupials · Two animals that are the same colour

* Answer: Fossils in layers, with simpler forms in older rock → Evidence; Similar bones in a human arm, a bat wing and a whale flipper → Evidence; DNA more similar between closely related species → Evidence; Related animals living in the same region, such as Australian marsupials → Evidence; Two animals that are the same colour → Not evidence

## Why several?

**Why do scientists place so much weight on several lines of evidence?**

- Independent kinds of evidence all point to the same conclusion, which makes it much more reliable
- Each is a different theory
- More evidence is always better, whatever it shows
- One line is enough

* Answer: Independent kinds of evidence all point to the same conclusion, which makes it much more reliable

<!-- Speaker note: Fossils, anatomy, DNA and distribution come from different methods, so agreement is strong support. -->

## What each shows

**Match each line of evidence to what it shows.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Fossils in rock layers` | A. Those animals share a common ancestor |
| `Similar bones in an arm, a wing and a flipper` | B. Living things have changed over time, in order |
| `How similar species' DNA is` | C. Species evolved where their ancestors lived, cut off from elsewhere |
| `Marsupials found mostly in Australia` | D. How closely related they are |

* Answer: Fossils in rock layers → B. Living things have changed over time, in order; Similar bones in an arm, a wing and a flipper → A. ## New species

**A population is split by a new mountain range. Over a long time the two groups face different conditions. What might happen?**

- Natural selection acts differently on each, and they may become separate species
- Each group changes because its members try to suit their new home
- Nothing changes
- The groups merge

* Answer: Natural selection acts differently on each, and they may become separate species

<!-- Speaker note: Isolated groups cannot share genes, and different pressures favour different traits, so they can diverge until they no longer interbreed. -->

## Extinction

**Why do species go extinct?**

- They never go extinct naturally
- They choose to stop reproducing
- They are always the weakest species
- The environment changes faster than the population can adapt, or it has no helpful variation

* Answer: The environment changes faster than the population can adapt, or it has no helpful variation

<!-- Speaker note: Selection can only act on variation that already exists. If no individuals can survive the change, the species dies out. -->

## Fossil timeline

**What does the fossil record show about species over time?**

- Fossils show only species that are now extinct
- All the species alive today are found in the oldest layers
- Species appear and disappear at different times in the rock layers
- The deeper the layer, the bigger the animals

* Answer: Species appear and disappear at different times in the rock layers

<!-- Speaker note: Layers show the order in which groups appear and vanish, which fits change over time. -->

## Resistant bacteria

**Antibiotics kill most bacteria in an infection, but a few resist. What happens next?**

- Nothing: resistance cannot spread
- The bacteria decide to resist
- The resistant bacteria survive and reproduce, so the population becomes more resistant
- The antibiotic makes the bacteria resistant

* Answer: The resistant bacteria survive and reproduce, so the population becomes more resistant

<!-- Speaker note: The antibiotic is the selection pressure. Resistant bacteria already existed by chance, and the treatment leaves them to multiply. -->

## Pesticide

**A farmer sprays the same pesticide for years and it works less well. Why?**

- The insects moved away
- The insects learned to ignore it
- The pesticide wore out
- Insects that happened to resist survived and bred, so resistance became common

* Answer: Insects that happened to resist survived and bred, so resistance became common

<!-- Speaker note: Selection pressure over many generations raises the share of resistant individuals. -->

## Individuals or populations?

**Which statement about evolution is correct?**

- Evolution only happened in the past
- Populations change over generations; individuals do not evolve in their lifetime
- Evolution has a goal
- Individuals change to suit their surroundings

* Answer: Populations change over generations; individuals do not evolve in their lifetime

<!-- Speaker note: Individuals do not change their genes to fit; the proportions of traits in the population change as some survive and reproduce more. -->

## Why so many species?

**Why are there millions of different species today?**

- All of today's species have existed unchanged since life began
- New species stopped forming once humans appeared
- Every species has been evolving towards being human
- Over billions of years, natural selection in different environments has split populations into many species, and it still goes on

* Answer: Over billions of years, natural selection in different environments has split populations into many species, and it still goes on

<!-- Speaker note: Today's biodiversity is the result of the same processes still at work: variation, selection and isolation. -->
