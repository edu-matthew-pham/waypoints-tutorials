# Where are the instructions?

Year 10 Biology · Genetics

## Today

**We are learning to** dNA carries the instructions for an organism. It is coiled into chromosomes in the nucleus; a gene is a section of DNA that codes for a protein. Alleles are different versions of the same gene.

- I can put the cell, nucleus, chromosome, DNA and gene in order of size.
- I can define gene and allele.
- I can explain why chromosomes come in pairs.

## Alleles

**One gene decides your blood group, yet people have blood group A, B, AB or O. How?**

- People have different alleles (versions) of the same gene
- Only some people have the gene
- The environment decides
- There is a separate gene for each blood group

* Answer: People have different alleles (versions) of the same gene

## Pairs

**Your body cells have 46 chromosomes. Why are they described as 23 pairs?**

- One of each pair came from each parent, and the two carry the same genes (perhaps different alleles)
- Boys and girls have different chromosomes
- Pairs are easier to count
- Each chromosome copies itself

* Answer: One of each pair came from each parent, and the two carry the same genes (perhaps different alleles)

## Genotype and phenotype

**Someone has the blood group alleles A and O, and their blood group is A. Which is their genotype and which their phenotype?**

- Genotype: the chromosome the gene is on. Phenotype: the gene
- Both are 'blood group A': the words mean the same thing
- Genotype: the alleles A and O. Phenotype: blood group A, the characteristic that shows
- Genotype: blood group A. Phenotype: the alleles A and O

* Answer: Genotype: the alleles A and O. Phenotype: blood group A, the characteristic that shows

## Elaborate: from code to characteristic

**Put the steps in order.**

Groups: 1st · 2nd · 3rd · 4th

A gene's sequence of bases (A, T, C, G) is read · The cell builds a protein from that code · The protein does a job (a pigment, an enzyme, a structure) · A characteristic appears (such as eye colour)

* Answer: A gene's sequence of bases (A, T, C, G) is read → 1st; The cell builds a protein from that code → 2nd; The protein does a job (a pigment, an enzyme, a structure) → 3rd; A characteristic appears (such as eye colour) → 4th

## Elaborate: the same code everywhere

**A nerve cell and a skin cell from the same person have the same DNA. Why do they look and work so differently?**

- Nerve cells have extra genes
- Skin cells lose most of their DNA
- Each cell uses (switches on) different genes
- They have different chromosomes

* Answer: Each cell uses (switches on) different genes

**Revision summary**

- **DNA** carries coded instructions as a sequence of bases (A, T, C, G).
- A **chromosome** is one long DNA molecule, coiled; humans have 46, in 23 pairs (one of each pair from each parent).
- A **gene** is a section of DNA coding for a protein; **alleles** are different versions of one gene.
- **Genotype:** the alleles you have (such as A and O). **Phenotype:** the characteristic that shows (blood group A).

## Exit ticket

**What's the difference between a gene and an allele?**

* A gene is a section of DNA coding for a protein; an allele is one version of that gene.
