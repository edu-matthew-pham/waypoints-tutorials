# Why is every sperm and egg different?

Year 10 Biology · Genetics

<!-- Speaker note: Students model meiosis, explain how it halves the chromosome number and produces genetically different gametes, and contrast it with mitosis (AC9S10U01, node 3, a hinge node). About 20 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 8 min · Meiosis and mitosis 2 min · Evaluate 3 min · Wider adds about 2 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** meiosis makes gametes. The chromosome pairs separate and each gamete gets one chromosome from each pair, chosen at random, so it has half the number and a different combination. With 23 pairs there are over 8 million combinations.

- I can explain that each gamete gets one chromosome from each pair.
- I can calculate the number of possible gametes for a small number of pairs.
- I can distinguish meiosis from mitosis.

## Why it matters

> You and your brother or sister came from the same two parents. The chance that two of their sperm or eggs carry exactly the same chromosomes is tiny.

<!-- Speaker note: Chromosomes come in pairs, one from each parent. This lesson: how gametes get half, and why each is different. -->

## One of each pair

**On the board, every gamete had three chromosomes. Which is true?**

- Each gamete gets the whole pair from one chromosome
- Each gamete gets exactly one chromosome from each pair
- Each gamete gets all the red ones or all the blue ones
- Each gamete gets any three chromosomes

* Answer: Each gamete gets exactly one chromosome from each pair

## Three pairs

**With 3 pairs, each with 2 choices, how many different gametes are possible?**

* Answer: 8

## 23 pairs

**Humans have 23 pairs. About how many different combinations are possible?**

- 2 × 23 = 46: two choices for each of 23 pairs
- 23: one new combination for each pair
- 23 × 23 = 529
- 2 to the power 23: over 8 million

* Answer: 2 to the power 23: over 8 million

## Elaborate: meiosis and mitosis

**Meiosis (making gametes) or mitosis (growth and repair)?**

Groups: Meiosis · Mitosis

Makes sperm and egg cells · New cells have 46 chromosomes · New cells have 23 chromosomes · New cells are genetically identical to the parent cell · New cells are all genetically different · Healing a cut

* Answer: Makes sperm and egg cells → Meiosis; New cells have 46 chromosomes → Mitosis; New cells have 23 chromosomes → Meiosis; New cells are genetically identical to the parent cell → Mitosis; New cells are all genetically different → Meiosis; Healing a cut → Mitosis

## Elaborate: even more variety

**Each parent can make over 8 million different gametes. Ignoring crossing over, roughly how many different children could two parents have?**

- About 8 million × 8 million: around 70 trillion
- About 16 million
- 46
- About 8 million

* Answer: About 8 million × 8 million: around 70 trillion

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Meiosis** makes gametes: chromosome pairs separate; each gamete gets **one from each pair**, so it has **half** the number.
- Which of each pair goes into a gamete is **random**: 2 × 2 × 2 … for 23 pairs, over **8 million** combinations.
- Random fertilisation (and crossing over) multiply the variety.
