# How different are individuals?

Year 10 Biology · Evolution

## Today

**We are learning to** individuals in a population vary. Measuring one characteristic across a population gives a spread of values, often most near the middle. Some variation is inherited (genes) and some comes from the environment; inherited variation is what natural selection acts on.

- I can describe the variation in a measured population.
- I can classify variation as continuous or discontinuous, inherited or environmental.
- I can explain why only inherited variation is passed on.

## Why it matters

> Look closely at a patch of grass beetles, a school of fish or a class of Year 10s. No two are quite the same. That spread is the raw material of evolution.

## Continuous or not

**Continuous (any value in a range) or discontinuous (a few distinct groups)?**

Groups: Continuous · Discontinuous

Height · Blood group (A, B, AB, O) · The beetles' colour · Pea flower colour (purple or white) · Hand span

* Answer: Height → Continuous; Blood group (A, B, AB, O) → Discontinuous; The beetles' colour → Continuous; Pea flower colour (purple or white) → Discontinuous; Hand span → Continuous

## What gets passed on

**Why does only inherited variation matter for evolution?**

- Environmental variation isn't real
- Environmental variation only happens in humans
- Only inherited characteristics are passed to offspring through genes
- Inherited variation is bigger

* Answer: Only inherited characteristics are passed to offspring through genes

## Elaborate: where variation comes from

**Which of these makes new inherited variation in a population?**

- The environment changing an animal's genes to suit it
- Animals trying hard to change
- Eating different food
- Mutations, plus new combinations of alleles from meiosis and fertilisation

* Answer: Mutations, plus new combinations of alleles from meiosis and fertilisation

## Elaborate: sampling

**Someone measures only the 5 easiest-to-spot beetles and says the population is mostly dark. What's wrong?**

- Beetles can't be measured
- They should have measured only pale beetles
- Nothing: 5 is enough
- The sample is small and biased: the easiest beetles to spot aren't typical

* Answer: The sample is small and biased: the easiest beetles to spot aren't typical

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Individuals in a population **vary**; measuring a characteristic gives a **spread**.
- **Continuous** variation takes any value (height); **discontinuous** falls into groups (blood group).
- Variation can be **inherited**, **environmental** or both; only inherited variation is passed on, so only it matters for evolution.
- New inherited variation comes from **mutations** and from **meiosis and fertilisation**.
