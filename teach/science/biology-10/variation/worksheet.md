# How different are individuals?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Look closely at a patch of grass beetles, a school of fish or a class of Year 10s. No two are quite the same. That spread is the raw material of evolution.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students measure variation in a population, distinguish continuous from discontinuous and inherited from environmental variation, and explain why inherited variation matters for evolution (AC9S10U02, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe the variation in a measured population. I can classify variation as continuous or discontinuous, inherited or environmental. I can explain why only inherited variation is passed on.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Individuals in a population **vary**; measuring a characteristic gives a **spread**.
- **Continuous** variation takes any value (height); **discontinuous** falls into groups (blood group).
- Variation can be **inherited**, **environmental** or both; only inherited variation is passed on, so only it matters for evolution.
- New inherited variation comes from **mutations** and from **meiosis and fertilisation**.
