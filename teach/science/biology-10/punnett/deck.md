# Can you predict the offspring?

Year 10 Biology · Genetics

## Today

**We are learning to** a Punnett square shows every way two parents' gametes can combine for one gene. It gives the probability of each genotype and phenotype (such as 3 in 4 showing the dominant trait for Pp × Pp), not a guaranteed count.

- I can complete a Punnett square for one gene.
- I can use the words genotype, phenotype, dominant, recessive, homozygous and heterozygous.
- I can explain a ratio as a probability, not a fixed count.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: A concept tutorial for introducing or revising the idea. Set the width with `?width=minimum`, `typical` (default) or `wider`. ## Why it matters

> Two purple-flowered pea plants can have white-flowered offspring. Gregor Mendel counted thousands of them in the 1860s and found the same ratio every time.

## Words

**Match each word to its meaning.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Genotype` | A. Two identical alleles (PP or pp) |
| `Phenotype` | B. The characteristic you see, such as purple flowers |
| `Homozygous` | C. Shows only when there is no dominant allele |
| `Heterozygous` | D. Two different alleles (Pp) |
| `Recessive` | E. The pair of alleles, such as Pp |

* Answer: Genotype → E. The pair of alleles, such as Pp; Phenotype → B. The characteristic you see, such as purple flowers; Homozygous → A. Two identical alleles (PP or pp); Heterozygous → D. Two different alleles (Pp); Recessive → C. Shows only when there is no dominant allele

## Probability

**From Pp × Pp, a gardener grows 4 seeds. Are exactly 3 purple and 1 white?**

- No, it's always half and half
- Yes, always exactly 3 and 1
- No: the square only works for animals
- Not necessarily: each seed has a 3 in 4 chance of purple, so small numbers vary; large numbers get close to 75%

* Answer: Not necessarily: each seed has a 3 in 4 chance of purple, so small numbers vary; large numbers get close to 75%

- White came from a mutation every time

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Genotype:** the alleles (PP, Pp, pp). **Phenotype:** the characteristic seen.
- A **dominant** allele shows when present; a **recessive** one only in **homozygous** form (pp).
- A **Punnett square** gives the **probability** of each offspring: Pp × Pp gives 1 PP : 2 Pp : 1 pp, so 3 : 1 purple to white; Bb × bb gives 1 : 1.
- Real numbers vary around the prediction, especially when there are few offspring.
