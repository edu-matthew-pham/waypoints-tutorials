# Why does the moon change shape?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The moon looks like a thin smile one week and a bright full disc two weeks later. Nothing about the moon itself has changed.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sequence the eight moon phases and explain them by the moon's position relative to the Earth and sun, rejecting the Earth's-shadow explanation (AC9S7U03, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can put the eight phases of the moon in order. I can explain the phases by the moon's position, not Earth's shadow. I can predict the phase seen from Earth for a given position.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

{{lines-3}}

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** How much of the sunlit half we can see. The sun always lights half the moon; as the moon goes round the Earth, we see more or less of that half. Earth's shadow is only involved in a lunar eclipse.

**In a lunar eclipse, a full moon goes dark and reddish for an hour or two. How is that different from the moon's phases?**

☐ A lunar eclipse happens at new moon, when the moon is dark

☐ An eclipse is the moon going through all its phases in an hour

☐ An eclipse is Earth's shadow falling on the moon, only at some full moons; the phases come from how much of the lit half we see, every month

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The sun always lights **half** the moon. The **phase** is how much of that lit half we can see, which depends on where the moon is in its orbit.
- New moon → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → full moon → waning gibbous → last quarter → waning crescent (about 29.5 days).
- Phases are **not** caused by Earth's shadow. From Australia a waxing moon is lit on the left.
