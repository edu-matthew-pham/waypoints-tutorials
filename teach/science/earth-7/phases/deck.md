# Why does the moon change shape?

Year 7 Earth and space · The moon

## Today

- I can put the eight phases of the moon in order.
- I can explain the phases by the moon's position, not Earth's shadow.
- I can predict the phase seen from Earth for a given position.

## Why it matters

> The moon looks like a thin smile one week and a bright full disc two weeks later. Nothing about the moon itself has changed.

<!-- Speaker note: You've probably noticed the moon looks different from one night to the next, and by the end of this you'll be able to say exactly why. -->

- Covering half the moon
- In front of the sun
- Covering the whole moon

## Phase or eclipse

**In a lunar eclipse, a full moon goes dark and reddish for an hour or two. How is that different from the moon's phases?**

- A lunar eclipse happens at new moon, when the moon is dark
- An eclipse is the moon going through all its phases in an hour
- An eclipse is Earth's shadow falling on the moon, only at some full moons; the phases come from how much of the lit half we see, every month
- There is no difference: a lunar eclipse is just a very dark phase

* Answer: An eclipse is Earth's shadow falling on the moon, only at some full moons; the phases come from how much of the lit half we see, every month

## Elaborate: the view from Japan

**From Australia a waxing crescent is lit on the left. From Japan it is lit on the right. Why?**

- People in the Northern Hemisphere see the moon 'upside down' compared with us, so left and right swap
- The moon's phase is different in Japan
- Japan sees a different moon
- It's lit on the left everywhere

* Answer: People in the Northern Hemisphere see the moon 'upside down' compared with us, so left and right swap

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The sun always lights **half** the moon. The **phase** is how much of that lit half we can see, which depends on where the moon is in its orbit.
- New moon → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → full moon → waning gibbous → last quarter → waning crescent (about 29.5 days).
- Phases are **not** caused by Earth's shadow. From Australia a waxing moon is lit on the left.

## Exit ticket

**Why do we see a half-lit moon at first quarter?**

* The moon is a quarter of the way round its orbit, at right angles to the sun; we see half of its sunlit half. It isn't Earth's shadow.
