# Why is summer hot?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** On 4 January the Earth is at its closest to the sun all year, five million kilometres closer than in July. That day it's summer in Canberra and winter in London.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain seasons by the Earth's axial tilt, link tilt to the height of the noon sun and day length, and use opposite hemispheres and the distance data to reject the distance explanation (AC9S7U03, node 4, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain that seasons are caused by the tilt, not distance from the sun. I can link the tilt to the height of the noon sun and the length of the day. I can explain why the two hemispheres have opposite seasons.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

☐ The Earth's tilt makes the Southern Hemisphere lean towards the sun in December

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The tilt. In December the Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun: the noon sun is high and the days are long. The Earth is in fact closest to the sun in early January, but that changes the sunlight by only about 7%.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Seasons are caused by the Earth's **tilt**, not its distance from the sun (we are closest in January).
- Leaning **towards** the sun: the noon sun is **higher** (light concentrated on a smaller area) and days are **longer**: summer.
- The two hemispheres have **opposite** seasons. Near the equator the changes are small.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**Explain why Canberra is hotter in December than in June.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** In December the Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun (the tilt), so the noon sun is higher, concentrating the light, and days are longer. It isn't distance: the Earth is actually slightly closer to the sun in January.

:::
