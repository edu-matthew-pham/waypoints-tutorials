# Why is summer hot?

Year 7 Earth and space · The Earth and the sun

## Today

**We are learning to** seasons are caused by the Earth's tilted axis, not its distance from the sun. Leaning towards the sun gives a higher noon sun (concentrated light) and longer days: summer. The two hemispheres have opposite seasons.

- I can explain that seasons are caused by the tilt, not distance from the sun.
- I can link the tilt to the height of the noon sun and the length of the day.
- I can explain why the two hemispheres have opposite seasons.

## Why it matters

> On 4 January the Earth is at its closest to the sun all year, five million kilometres closer than in July. That day it's summer in Canberra and winter in London.

## What happened?

* The tilt. In December the Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun: the noon sun is high and the days are long. The Earth is in fact closest to the sun in early January, but that changes the sunlight by only about 7%.

## Summer and winter

**Which go with Canberra's summer, and which with its winter?**

Groups: Summer · Winter

Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun · Noon sun high in the sky · About 9½ hours of daylight · Sunlight hits the ground at a low angle, spread over a bigger area · About 14½ hours of daylight

* Answer: Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun → Summer; Noon sun high in the sky → Summer; About 9½ hours of daylight → Winter; Sunlight hits the ground at a low angle, spread over a bigger area → Winter; About 14½ hours of daylight → Summer

## Six seasons

**In Kakadu, Bininj people recognise six seasons, marked by changes in rain, wind, plants and animals, rather than four. Why can both systems be useful?**

- The six seasons are caused by a different tilt
- The tilt model explains why the year changes; a six-season calendar describes the changes people actually observe in that place
- Kakadu has no seasons
- Only the four-season model is science

* Answer: The tilt model explains why the year changes; a six-season calendar describes the changes people actually observe in that place

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Seasons are caused by the Earth's **tilt**, not its distance from the sun (we are closest in January).
- Leaning **towards** the sun: the noon sun is **higher** (light concentrated on a smaller area) and days are **longer**: summer.
- The two hemispheres have **opposite** seasons. Near the equator the changes are small.

## Exit ticket

**Explain why Canberra is hotter in December than in June.**

* In December the Southern Hemisphere leans towards the sun (the tilt), so the noon sun is higher, concentrating the light, and days are longer. It isn't distance: the Earth is actually slightly closer to the sun in January.
