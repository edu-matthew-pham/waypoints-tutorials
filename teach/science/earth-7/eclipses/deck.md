# Why isn't there an eclipse every month?

Year 7 Earth and space · The moon

<!-- Speaker note: Students state the conditions for solar and lunar eclipses, distinguish the umbra and penumbra, and explain why eclipses do not occur every month (AC9S7U03, node 3). ## Today

**We are learning to** a solar eclipse happens at new moon when the moon's shadow falls on Earth; a lunar eclipse at full moon when the moon passes through Earth's shadow. The moon's orbit is tilted about 5°, so most months the shadows miss: eclipses come in seasons about six months apart.

- I can state the conditions for a solar and a lunar eclipse.
- I can describe the umbra and penumbra.
- I can explain why eclipses don't happen every month.

## Why it matters

> There's a new moon every month, with the moon between us and the sun. Yet the next total solar eclipse seen from Sydney, on 22 July 2028, will be the first there in over 150 years.

## The tilt

**Why isn't there a solar eclipse at every new moon?**

- The moon moves too fast
- The sun is sometimes too bright
- The moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to Earth's, so the moon usually passes above or below the line from the sun to the Earth
- The moon is too small

* Answer: The moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to Earth's, so the moon usually passes above or below the line from the sun to the Earth

## Elaborate: predicting eclipses

**Many ancient peoples recorded eclipses carefully but found them hard to predict. Babylonian astronomers eventually noticed eclipses repeat in a cycle of about 18 years. Which missing idea makes eclipses predictable from first principles?**

- The speed of light
- The exact date of every new and full moon
- The size of the moon
- The moon's tilted orbit, and where it crosses Earth's orbit plane: eclipses happen only when that crossing line points at the sun

* Answer: The moon's tilted orbit, and where it crosses Earth's orbit plane: eclipses happen only when that crossing line points at the sun

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Solar eclipse:** new moon, moon between sun and Earth, the moon's shadow falls on part of Earth.
- **Lunar eclipse:** full moon, Earth between sun and moon, the moon passes through Earth's shadow (it often looks red).
- **Umbra:** full shadow (total eclipse). **Penumbra:** partial shadow (partial eclipse).
- The moon's orbit is **tilted about 5°**, so most new and full moons miss: eclipses come in **eclipse seasons** about every six months.

## Exit ticket

**Why doesn't every full moon give a lunar eclipse?**

* The moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to Earth's, so at most full moons the moon passes above or below Earth's shadow.
