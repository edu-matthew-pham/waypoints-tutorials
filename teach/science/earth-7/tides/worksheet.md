# Why are there two high tides a day?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** At a beach the water creeps up the sand and back down about every 12 and a half hours, and the biggest tides of the month always arrive near a new or full moon.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain tides as caused by the moon's gravity, account for two high tides a day, and link spring and neap tides to the moon's position and phase (AC9S7U03, node 5).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain that the moon's gravity causes the tides. I can explain why most coasts have two high tides a day. I can link spring and neap tides to the moon's phases.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Two, about 12½ hours apart. The moon pulls the oceans into two bulges (one towards it and one on the far side) and the Earth spins through both each day.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Between the Earth and the sun, so the sun, moon and Earth are in a line

At new moon the moon is between the Earth and the sun. The sun and moon pull in line, which gives a spring tide.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The moon's **gravity** pulls the oceans into **two bulges** (towards the moon and on the far side); the Earth spins through both: **two high tides a day** on most coasts.
- **Spring tides** (biggest range): new and full moon, sun and moon **in line**. **Neap tides** (smallest range): quarter moons, sun at **right angles**.
- Spring and neap tides each come about every two weeks, all year.
