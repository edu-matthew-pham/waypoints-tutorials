# Spinning and orbiting

Year 7 Earth and space · The Earth and the sun

<!-- Speaker note: Students distinguish the Earth's rotation from its revolution, link each to day and night and the year, and describe the tilted axis that keeps its direction (AC9S7U03, node 1, a hinge node). ## Today

**We are learning to** the Earth rotates (spins on its axis) once a day, causing day and night, and revolves (goes round the sun) once a year. Its axis is tilted and always points the same way in space.

- I can explain the difference between rotation and revolution.
- I can link rotation to day and night, and revolution to the year.
- I can describe the Earth's tilted axis and how it points.

## Elaborate: what a person notices across a year

**A student in Canberra records sunset time every week for a year. What pattern will they find?**

- Sunset changes randomly
- Sunset is latest in June and earliest in December
- Sunset gets later until early January, then earlier until mid-June, then later again: a yearly cycle
- Sunset is at the same time every day

* Answer: Sunset gets later until early January, then earlier until mid-June, then later again: a yearly cycle

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Rotation:** the Earth spins on its axis once a day: day and night.
- **Revolution:** the Earth goes round the sun once a year (about 365¼ days, about 365 spins).
- The **axis is tilted 23.4°** and always points the same way in space, so each hemisphere leans towards the sun for half the year.

## Exit ticket

**What is the difference between rotation and revolution?**

* Rotation is the Earth spinning on its axis (once a day, causing day and night); revolution is the Earth travelling round the sun (once a year).
