# What makes a sex cell special?

Year 9 Biology · Reproduction

<!-- Speaker note: Students explain that gametes have half the chromosome number and relate the features of sperm and egg cells to their jobs (AC9S9U02, node 1, a hinge node). About 20 minutes (Engage and Explore 6 min · Explain 9 min · Plant gametes 3 min · Evaluate 3 min · Wider adds about 1 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** gametes (sperm and egg, or the nuclei in pollen and ovules) carry half the usual number of chromosomes, so fertilisation restores the full set. Each is specialised: sperm to swim and get in, the egg to feed the embryo and let only one sperm in.

- I can explain why gametes have half the usual number of chromosomes.
- I can link three features of sperm and egg cells to their jobs.
- I can describe how a plant's male nucleus reaches the egg.

## Why it matters

> A human egg is just big enough to see without a microscope. A sperm is one of the smallest cells in the body. Together they make one cell that grows into a person.

## Half

**Why must a gamete have half the number of chromosomes?**

- So that when two gametes join, the new cell has the full number again
- Because gametes are small
- So the offspring has half as many genes
- To save energy

* Answer: So that when two gametes join, the new cell has the full number again

## Numbers

**Why are millions of sperm released, but only one egg?**

- Most sperm never reach the egg; the egg is large and costly to make because it holds food
- Many sperm join the egg, each adding some chromosomes
- Sperm have no nucleus
- Eggs are smaller

* Answer: Most sperm never reach the egg; the egg is large and costly to make because it holds food

## The ovule

**A flowering plant's egg sits inside an ovule, deep in the flower's ovary. How does that suit its job?**

- It keeps the egg protected, and after fertilisation the ovule grows into a seed with a food store
- It stops pollen ever reaching it
- It lets the egg swim out to meet the pollen
- It is where the pollen is made

* Answer: It keeps the egg protected, and after fertilisation the ovule grows into a seed with a food store

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Gametes** are sex cells: sperm and egg in animals; the male nucleus in pollen and the egg in the ovule in flowering plants.
- Gametes have **half** the chromosomes (23 in humans), so **fertilisation** restores the full number (46).
- **Sperm:** tail, midpiece of mitochondria, acrosome. **Egg:** large, food store, a coat that lets in only one sperm.

## Exit ticket

**Why do sperm and egg cells have 23 chromosomes, not 46?**

* So that when they fuse at fertilisation, the new cell has 46, the full number, again.
