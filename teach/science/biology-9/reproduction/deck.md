# Why don't brothers and sisters look the same?

Year 9 Biology · Reproduction

<!-- Speaker note: Students compare sexual and asexual reproduction and explain why sexual reproduction produces varied offspring (AC9S9U02, node 3, a hinge node). About 20 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 8 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 2 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** in sexual reproduction each offspring gets a random half of each parent's genes, so offspring vary. In asexual reproduction one parent makes genetically identical clones. Variation helps a population survive change; asexual reproduction is fast and needs no partner.

- I can explain why offspring of sexual reproduction vary.
- I can explain why asexual reproduction makes clones.
- I can give an advantage of each.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: A concept tutorial for introducing or revising the idea. Set the width with `?width=minimum`, `typical` (default) or `wider`. ## Why it matters

> Two kittens from the same litter can be black and ginger. A strawberry plant's runners grow into plants identical to it. Same question, two answers.

## Why they vary

**Why are the offspring from sexual reproduction different from each other?**

- Each gets a random one of each parent's two copies of every gene, so each gets a different combination
- The environment changes their genes
- One parent's genes are stronger
- Some offspring get more genes

* Answer: Each gets a random one of each parent's two copies of every gene, so each gets a different combination

## Survival

**A new disease kills plants with one particular set of genes. Which population is more likely to survive?**

- A population from sexual reproduction: some individuals are likely to have genes that resist it
- Both the same
- A population of clones: they are all strong

* Answer: A population from sexual reproduction: some individuals are likely to have genes that resist it

## Many or few

**A cod releases millions of eggs into the sea and leaves them. A kangaroo raises one joey at a time in her pouch for months. Why do both strategies work?**

- The kangaroo has one joey because there isn't enough food in Australia
- A parent has limited energy: the cod spreads it over millions of eggs that mostly die, the kangaroo puts it into one joey likely to survive
- More offspring is always better, so the cod's way is best
- The cod's eggs are clones, so they don't need care

* Answer: A parent has limited energy: the cod spreads it over millions of eggs that mostly die, the kangaroo puts it into one joey likely to survive

## Elaborate: which suits?

**Aphids reproduce asexually all summer, then sexually in autumn before winter. Why might that be useful?**

- It makes no difference
- Cloning only works in warm weather
- Fast cloning while conditions are good; variation before the harsh, changing winter
- They run out of partners in summer

* Answer: Fast cloning while conditions are good; variation before the harsh, changing winter

## Elaborate: bananas

**Nearly all supermarket bananas (Cavendish) are clones grown from cuttings. Panama disease is spreading through banana farms. Why are growers worried?**

- Clones grow too slowly
- Disease only affects clones
- The clones all have the same genes, so if one is vulnerable, every plant is
- Clones can't make bananas

* Answer: The clones all have the same genes, so if one is vulnerable, every plant is

## The need

**Why did a Cavendish banana that resists Tropical Race 4 become a research focus in Australia?**

- Society needed it: a fruit most Australians eat, and many growers' farms and jobs, were at risk from a disease with no cure
- Scientists research only what interests them, whatever anyone needs
- It was the quickest, easiest banana research to do
- A law says every crop must be gene-modified

* Answer: Society needed it: a fruit most Australians eat, and many growers' farms and jobs, were at risk from a disease with no cure

<!-- Speaker note: Research costs money and time. Needs that matter to many people, such as food and jobs, attract the funding and the effort. -->

**Revision summary**

- **Sexual reproduction:** two parents, gametes fuse; each offspring gets a random half of each parent's genes, so offspring **vary**.
- **Asexual reproduction:** one parent, no gametes; offspring are **clones**.
- Variation helps a population survive a changing environment; asexual reproduction is fast and needs no partner.
