# Why don't brothers and sisters look the same?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two kittens from the same litter can be black and ginger. A strawberry plant's runners grow into plants identical to it. Same question, two answers.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare sexual and asexual reproduction and explain why sexual reproduction produces varied offspring (AC9S9U02, node 3, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why offspring of sexual reproduction vary. I can explain why asexual reproduction makes clones. I can give an advantage of each.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Two parents have several offspring. What do you predict?**

☐ Each one is an exact half-way blend of the parents

☐ All the offspring look the same, because they have the same parents

☐ They all look like one parent

☐ Each offspring is a different mix of the parents' features

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Each offspring is a different mix. Each gets a random one of each parent's two copies of every gene, so brothers and sisters differ (unless they're identical twins).

:::

## Explain: two ways to reproduce


:::keep-together

### Which kind

**Sexual or asexual reproduction?**

Groups: Sexual, Asexual

- Two parents: {{field-15}}
- Offspring are clones of the parent: {{field-15}}
- Gametes fuse at fertilisation: {{field-15}}
- No partner needed: {{field-15}}
- Offspring vary: {{field-15}}
- A strawberry plant's runners: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Two parents → Sexual; Offspring are clones of the parent → Asexual; Gametes fuse at fertilisation → Sexual; No partner needed → Asexual; Offspring vary → Sexual; A strawberry plant's runners → Asexual

:::


:::keep-together

### Why they vary

**Why are the offspring from sexual reproduction different from each other?**

☐ Some offspring get more genes than others

☐ Each gets a random mix of its parents' gene copies

☐ The environment changes their genes

☐ One parent's genes are stronger

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Each gets a random mix of its parents' gene copies

Each offspring gets a random one of each parent's two copies of every gene, so each gets a different combination.

:::


:::keep-together

### Survival

**A new disease kills plants with one particular set of genes. Which population is more likely to survive?**

☐ Both the same: a disease kills either kind

☐ The sexual population: some may have resistant genes

☐ The clones: they all come from one strong parent

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The sexual population: some may have resistant genes

Sexual reproduction makes varied offspring, so some individuals are likely to have genes that resist the disease. Clones share one set of genes.

:::


:::keep-together

### Many or few

**A cod releases millions of eggs into the sea and leaves them. A kangaroo raises one joey at a time in her pouch for months. Why do both strategies work?**

☐ The kangaroo has one joey because there isn't enough food in Australia

☐ More offspring is always better, so the cod's way is best

☐ The cod's eggs are clones, so they don't need care

☐ A parent's energy goes into many eggs or into care for one

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A parent's energy goes into many eggs or into care for one

A parent has limited energy: the cod spreads it over millions of eggs that mostly die, and the kangaroo puts it into one joey that is likely to survive.

:::

## Elaborate: which suits?


:::keep-together

**Aphids reproduce asexually all summer, then sexually in autumn before winter. Why might that be useful?**

☐ It makes no difference which way they breed

☐ They run out of partners in summer

☐ Cloning only works in warm weather

☐ Fast cloning in good times; variation before winter

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Fast cloning in good times; variation before winter

Cloning is fast while conditions are good. Before the harsh, changing winter, sexual reproduction makes varied offspring, some of which may cope better.

## Elaborate: bananas


:::keep-together

**Nearly all supermarket bananas (Cavendish) are clones grown from cuttings. Panama disease is spreading through banana farms. Why are growers worried?**

☐ Clones can't make bananas

☐ Clones grow too slowly to replace sick plants

☐ The clones share genes, so if one is vulnerable, all are

☐ Disease only affects clones

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The clones share genes, so if one is vulnerable, all are

This happened before: the Gros Michel banana was wiped out of commercial farming in the 1950s by an earlier strain of Panama disease.

## Elaborate: who decides what gets researched?


:::keep-together

### The need

**Why did a Cavendish banana that resists Tropical Race 4 become a research focus in Australia?**

☐ A law says every crop must be gene-modified

☐ It was the quickest, easiest banana research to do

☐ Scientists research only what interests them, whatever anyone needs

☐ Society needed it: a popular fruit and many jobs were at risk

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Society needed it: a popular fruit and many jobs were at risk

A fruit most Australians eat, and many growers' farms and jobs, were at risk from a disease with no cure. Research costs money and time. Needs that matter to many people, such as food and jobs, attract the funding and the effort.

:::


:::keep-together

### Values

**Some people don't want to eat gene-modified food. How can a value like that influence the research?**

☐ It can steer research towards other methods, such as breeding

☐ It can't: people's values never affect research

☐ It makes the resistant banana unsafe to eat

☐ It changes how fast the fungus spreads

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It can steer research towards other methods, such as breeding

Values can steer researchers towards other methods as well, such as breeding resistant bananas, and affect whether a GM banana is ever grown. Society's values shape which questions get asked, which methods are used and whether a result is taken up.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Sexual reproduction:** two parents, gametes fuse; each offspring gets a random half of each parent's genes, so offspring **vary**.
- **Asexual reproduction:** one parent, no gametes; offspring are **clones**.
- Variation helps a population survive a changing environment; asexual reproduction is fast and needs no partner.
