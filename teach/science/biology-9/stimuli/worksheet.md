# What counts as a stimulus?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You pull your hand off a hot pan before you feel the pain. After lunch, your blood sugar is brought back down without you noticing at all. Both are responses to a stimulus.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify stimuli as internal or external changes and trace them through the stimulus–response pathway (AC9S9U01, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain that a stimulus is a change inside or outside the body. I can name the parts of the stimulus–response pathway and what each does. I can trace an internal and an external stimulus through the pathway.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Elaborate: plants respond too


:::keep-together

**A seedling on a windowsill bends towards the light. Which part of the pathway is missing compared with your body?**

☐ Nothing is different

☐ There are no nerves: the command is a plant hormone (auxin) carried in the plant, not an impulse

☐ There is no stimulus

☐ There is no response

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** There are no nerves: the command is a plant hormone (auxin) carried in the plant, not an impulse

Plants still detect a stimulus and respond, but they coordinate with hormones only.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **stimulus** is any change the body detects, **external** (light, heat, sound) or **internal** (blood sugar, temperature, water).
- **Stimulus → receptor → command centre → effector → response.**
- Receptors detect; the brain, spinal cord or a gland decides; muscles and glands are the effectors.
