# What counts as a stimulus?

Year 9 Biology · Coordination and control

<!-- Speaker note: Students identify stimuli as internal or external changes and trace them through the stimulus–response pathway (AC9S9U01, node 1, a hinge node). About 20 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 8 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 2 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** a stimulus is any change the body detects, outside it or inside it. Every response follows the same pathway: stimulus, receptor, command centre, effector, response.

- I can explain that a stimulus is a change inside or outside the body.
- I can name the parts of the stimulus–response pathway and what each does.
- I can trace an internal and an external stimulus through the pathway.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: A concept tutorial for introducing or revising the idea; it does not replace a reaction-time practical (the ruler drop) or the knee-jerk reflex. Set the width with `?width=minimum`, `typical` (default) or `wider`. The hinge misconception is that stimuli are only external. Three of the five board stimuli are internal on purpose. Board notes: each pathway is simplified to one receptor, one command centre and one effector; the blood sugar and water pathways mix glands and hormones into the boxes, which the next lessons separate. -->

---

## Why it matters

> You pull your hand off a hot pan before you feel the pain. After lunch, your blood sugar is brought back down without you noticing at all. Both are responses to a stimulus.

## Inside or outside

**Internal or external stimulus?**

Groups: Internal · External

A loud bang · Blood sugar rising · The smell of toast · Body temperature falling · Too little water in the blood · Cold wind on your skin

* Answer: A loud bang → External; Blood sugar rising → Internal; The smell of toast → External; Body temperature falling → Internal; Too little water in the blood → Internal; Cold wind on your skin → External

## The parts

**Match each part of the pathway to what it does.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Stimulus` | A. A muscle or gland that carries out the response |
| `Receptor` | B. Processes the information and decides |
| `Command centre` | C. A change inside or outside the body |
| `Effector` | D. What the body does |
| `Response` | E. Detects the change |

* Answer: Stimulus → C. A change inside or outside the body; Receptor → E. Detects the change; Command centre → B. Processes the information and decides; Effector → A. A muscle or gland that carries out the response; Response → D. What the body does

## Smell receptors

**The smell of toast is the stimulus. Which is the receptor, and what does it do?**

- Smell receptors in the nose: they detect chemicals from the toast and send impulses to the brain
- The brain: it smells the toast
- The toast: it gives off the smell
- The salivary glands: they make your mouth water

* Answer: Smell receptors in the nose: they detect chemicals from the toast and send impulses to the brain

## Elaborate: a new pathway

**A runner's muscles use oxygen fast, so carbon dioxide in the blood rises. Breathing speeds up. Sort each into its part of the pathway.**

Groups: Stimulus · Receptor · Command centre · Effector · Response

Carbon dioxide in the blood rises · Chemical receptors in blood vessels and the brain · Brain stem · Diaphragm and rib muscles · Faster, deeper breathing

* Answer: Carbon dioxide in the blood rises → Stimulus; Chemical receptors in blood vessels and the brain → Receptor; Brain stem → Command centre; Diaphragm and rib muscles → Effector; Faster, deeper breathing → Response

## Elaborate: plants respond too

**A seedling on a windowsill bends towards the light. Which part of the pathway is missing compared with your body?**

- Nothing is different
- There are no nerves: the command is a plant hormone (auxin) carried in the plant, not an impulse
- There is no stimulus
- There is no response

* Answer: There are no nerves: the command is a plant hormone (auxin) carried in the plant, not an impulse

<!-- Speaker note: Plants still detect a stimulus and respond, but they coordinate with hormones only. What this step is for: Plants detect and respond to stimuli without nerves, which separates the pathway idea from the nervous system. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **stimulus** is any change the body detects, **external** (light, heat, sound) or **internal** (blood sugar, temperature, water).
- **Stimulus → receptor → command centre → effector → response.**
- Receptors detect; the brain, spinal cord or a gland decides; muscles and glands are the effectors.
