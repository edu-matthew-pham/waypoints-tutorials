# Who decides?

Year 9 Biology · Coordination and control

<!-- Speaker note: Students build stimulus–response pathways, name the CNS as the command centre with sensory and motor neurons, and explain why reflexes are fast (AC9S9U01, node 2, a hinge node). About 20 minutes (Engage and Explore 8 min · Explain 7 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 2 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) is the command centre: sensory neurons carry impulses in from receptors, motor neurons carry them out to effectors. Reflexes go through the spinal cord without waiting for the brain.

- I can build a stimulus–response pathway, choosing the receptor, command centre and effector.
- I can name sensory and motor neurons and the CNS.
- I can explain why a reflex is faster than a decided response.

## Why it matters

> Step on a pin and your foot is off the floor before you've said 'ouch'. Your brain wasn't the one who decided.

## Neurons

**Match each part of the nervous system to its place in the pathway.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Sensory neuron` | A. The command centre |
| `Brain and spinal cord (CNS)` | B. Turns a stimulus into an impulse |
| `Motor neuron` | C. Carries impulses from the CNS to an effector |
| `Receptor` | D. Carries impulses from a receptor to the CNS |

* Answer: Sensory neuron → D. Carries impulses from a receptor to the CNS; Brain and spinal cord (CNS) → A. The command centre; Motor neuron → C. Carries impulses from the CNS to an effector; Receptor → B. Turns a stimulus into an impulse

## Reflexes

**Why is a reflex faster than catching a ball?**

- Pain impulses travel faster than light impulses
- Leg muscles are faster than arm muscles
- The impulse goes through the spinal cord only, with no time spent deciding in the brain
- Reflexes use hormones

* Answer: The impulse goes through the spinal cord only, with no time spent deciding in the brain

## Elaborate: damage to the pathway

**A person's spinal cord is damaged in their upper back. A doctor taps below their knee. What would you expect?**

- Both work normally
- The knee can't jerk, but they feel the tap
- Nothing changes
- The knee-jerk may still work if the reflex arc below the damage is intact, but the person may not feel the tap

* Answer: The knee-jerk may still work if the reflex arc below the damage is intact, but the person may not feel the tap

**Revision summary**

- The **central nervous system** (brain and spinal cord) is the command centre.
- **Sensory neurons** carry impulses from receptors to the CNS; **motor neurons** carry them to effectors (**muscles or glands**).
- A **reflex** is automatic and fast: the impulse goes through the spinal cord without waiting for the brain.

**Name the command centre in a reflex and say why reflexes are fast.**
