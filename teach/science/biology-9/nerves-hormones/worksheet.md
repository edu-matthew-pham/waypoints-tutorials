# Nerve or hormone?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A fright makes you jump in a fraction of a second. Your heart is still pounding a few minutes later. Two different systems are at work.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare the nervous and endocrine systems by message, route, speed, duration and target (AC9S9U01, node 4, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain that hormones travel in the blood, not along nerves. I can compare nervous and hormonal responses by speed and duration. I can explain why only target cells respond to a hormone.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Adrenaline is a hormone released when you get a fright. How does it reach your heart?**

☐ It is made in the heart

☐ In the blood, carried all round the body

☐ Through the air you breathe

☐ Along a nerve, like an impulse

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** In the blood. The adrenal glands release it into the bloodstream, which carries it everywhere; cells with the right receptors (heart, liver, muscles) respond.

:::

## Explain: comparing the two


:::keep-together

### Which system

**Nervous system or endocrine system?**

Groups: Nervous system, Endocrine system

- Electrical impulses: {{field-15}}
- Chemical messages in the blood: {{field-15}}
- Very fast: {{field-15}}
- Effects last minutes to years: {{field-15}}
- Goes to one exact place: {{field-15}}
- Reaches the whole body; target cells respond: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Electrical impulses → Nervous system; Chemical messages in the blood → Endocrine system; Very fast → Nervous system; Effects last minutes to years → Endocrine system; Goes to one exact place → Nervous system; Reaches the whole body; target cells respond → Endocrine system

:::


:::keep-together

### Target cells

**Every cell in the body gets the same adrenaline in the blood. Why do only some cells respond?**

☐ Other cells destroy it

☐ The brain tells the other cells to ignore it

☐ Adrenaline only flows to the heart

☐ Only target cells have receptors that fit adrenaline

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Only target cells have receptors that fit adrenaline

:::


:::keep-together

### The right tool

**Growth over years is controlled by growth hormone, not nerves. Why is a hormone better for this job?**

☐ The effect needs to last a long time and reach cells all over the body

☐ Hormones are faster

☐ Growth is a reflex

☐ Nerves can't reach bones

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The effect needs to last a long time and reach cells all over the body

:::


:::keep-together

### Which one?

**A sprinter reacts to the starting gun. Which system carries the message to the leg muscles, and why?**

☐ Endocrine: a hormone in the blood reaches every leg muscle at once

☐ Both equally: every response uses both systems

☐ Endocrine: the race lasts a long time, so the effect must last

☐ Nervous: the response must be very fast and go to particular muscles

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nervous: the response must be very fast and go to particular muscles

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Nervous system:** electrical impulses along neurons; very fast; short-lived; to one exact place.
- **Endocrine system:** **hormones** released by **glands** into the **blood**; slower; longer-lasting; reach every cell but act only on **target cells** with matching receptors.
- Many responses use both.

## Exit ticket

{{lines-3}}

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nerves: electrical, fast, short-lived, one place. Hormones: chemical in the blood, slower, longer-lasting, target cells everywhere.
