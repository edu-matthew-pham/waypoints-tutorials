# Which system runs it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A fright makes you jump in a fraction of a second. Your heart is still pounding a few minutes later. Two different systems are at work.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students choose nervous or hormonal control for a job and justify it by speed, duration and reach, including why only target cells respond (AC9S9U01, node 4, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can choose nerves or hormones for a job and say why. I can compare nervous and hormonal responses by speed and duration. I can explain why only target cells respond to a hormone.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Dust flies at your eye and you blink. Over the next few years you grow taller. Which system runs each?**

☐ Both are run by nerves

☐ Both are run by hormones

☐ Blinking is run by nerves and growing by hormones

☐ Blinking is run by hormones and growing by nerves

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Blinking by nerves and growing by hormones. A blink has to happen in a fraction of a second and then stop, which suits a nerve signal to the eyelid muscles. Growth needs a message that lasts for years and reaches bones all over the body, which suits a hormone carried in the blood.

:::

## Explain: choosing and justifying


:::keep-together

### Target cells

**Every cell in the body gets the same adrenaline in the blood. Why do only some cells respond?**

☐ Other cells destroy it

☐ The brain tells the other cells to ignore it

☐ Adrenaline only flows to the heart

☐ Only target cells have receptors that fit adrenaline

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Only target cells have receptors that fit adrenaline

:::


:::keep-together

### The right tool

**Growth over years is controlled by growth hormone, not nerves. Why is a hormone better for this job?**

☐ The effect needs to last a long time and reach cells all over the body

☐ Hormones are faster

☐ Growth is a reflex

☐ Nerves can't reach bones

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The effect needs to last a long time and reach cells all over the body

:::


:::keep-together

### The sprinter

**A sprinter reacts to the starting gun. Which system carries the message to the leg muscles, and why?**

☐ Endocrine: a hormone in the blood reaches every leg muscle at once

☐ Both equally: every response uses both systems

☐ Endocrine: the race lasts a long time, so the effect must last

☐ Nervous: the response must be very fast and go to particular muscles

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nervous: the response must be very fast and go to particular muscles

:::


:::keep-together

### Hormones are better?

**A friend says hormones are better than nerves because their effects last longer. What's the best reply?**

☐ You're right, because lasting longer always means a stronger response

☐ You're right, because nerves only work for reflexes

☐ Hormones last longer, but nerves are better for every job because they're faster

☐ Neither is better: a long-lasting effect suits growth, but it would be no use for pulling your hand off a hot pan

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Neither is better: a long-lasting effect suits growth, but it would be no use for pulling your hand off a hot pan

Each system suits different jobs: nerves for fast, short jobs aimed at one place, hormones for slower, lasting jobs that reach many cells.

## Elaborate: when a receptor is faulty


:::keep-together

**A person's liver cells have faulty insulin receptors, but their pancreas still releases normal insulin. What happens to their blood glucose after a meal?**

☐ Nothing changes, because glucose is controlled by nerves

☐ It stays high, because the insulin reaches the liver but the liver cells can't respond to it

☐ It falls faster, because there's insulin left over

☐ It falls normally, because the insulin is still in the blood

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It stays high, because the insulin reaches the liver but the liver cells can't respond to it

A hormone only works where there is a receptor to take its message in. With faulty receptors, a normal amount of insulin reaches the liver but the liver can't act on it, so the effect is much like having no insulin.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- To pick a system for a job, ask how fast it has to be, how long the effect should last and how many places it has to reach.
- **Nerves** suit jobs that are fast, short and aimed at one place, like a blink or pulling your hand off a hot pan.
- **Hormones** suit jobs that can take minutes or years, need to last, or reach many places at once, like growth. Every cell gets the hormone, but only **target cells** with matching receptors respond.
- Many responses need both, like a swerve followed by a pounding heart.
