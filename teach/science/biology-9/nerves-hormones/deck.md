## Why it matters

> A fright makes you jump in a fraction of a second. Your heart is still pounding a few minutes later. Two different systems are at work.

**Every cell in the body gets the same adrenaline in the blood. Why do only some cells respond?**

- Other cells destroy it
- The brain tells the other cells to ignore it
- Adrenaline only flows to the heart
- Only target cells have receptors that fit adrenaline

* Answer: Only target cells have receptors that fit adrenaline

## The right tool

**Growth over years is controlled by growth hormone, not nerves. Why is a hormone better for this job?**

- The effect needs to last a long time and reach cells all over the body
- Hormones are faster
- Growth is a reflex
- Nerves can't reach bones

* Answer: The effect needs to last a long time and reach cells all over the body

## The sprinter

**A sprinter reacts to the starting gun. Which system carries the message to the leg muscles, and why?**

- Endocrine: a hormone in the blood reaches every leg muscle at once
- Both equally: every response uses both systems
- Endocrine: the race lasts a long time, so the effect must last
- Nervous: the response must be very fast and go to particular muscles

* Answer: Nervous: the response must be very fast and go to particular muscles

## Elaborate: when a receptor is faulty

**A person's liver cells have faulty insulin receptors, but their pancreas still releases normal insulin. What happens to their blood glucose after a meal?**

- Nothing changes, because glucose is controlled by nerves
- It stays high, because the insulin reaches the liver but the liver cells can't respond to it
- It falls faster, because there's insulin left over
- It falls normally, because the insulin is still in the blood

* Answer: It stays high, because the insulin reaches the liver but the liver cells can't respond to it

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- To pick a system for a job, ask how fast it has to be, how long the effect should last and how many places it has to reach.
- **Nerves** suit jobs that are fast, short and aimed at one place, like a blink or pulling your hand off a hot pan.
- **Hormones** suit jobs that can take minutes or years, need to last, or reach many places at once, like growth. Every cell gets the hormone, but only **target cells** with matching receptors respond.
- Many responses need both, like a swerve followed by a pounding heart.

## Exit ticket

**Which system would you give blinking to, and which growing, and why?**

* Blinking to nerves, because it has to be fast, short and in one place; growing to hormones, because it has to last for years and reach cells all over the body.
