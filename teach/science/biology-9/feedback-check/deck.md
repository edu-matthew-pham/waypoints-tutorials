## Parts of the loop

**Which part of the temperature loop is it?**

Groups: Receptor · Control centre · Effector

Temperature sensors in the skin and blood · The hypothalamus in the brain · Sweat glands · Muscles that shiver · Nerve endings that detect cold

* Answer: Temperature sensors in the skin and blood → Receptor; The hypothalamus in the brain → Control centre; Sweat glands → Effector; Muscles that shiver → Effector; Nerve endings that detect cold → Receptor

## Blood glucose

**Blood glucose rises after a meal. What reverses the change?**

- Insulin makes blood glucose rise further
- More glucose is released from the liver
- Nothing: it stays high until the next meal
- Insulin is released, cells take up glucose, and blood glucose falls back towards its set point

* Answer: Insulin is released, cells take up glucose, and blood glucose falls back towards its set point

<!-- Speaker note: Negative feedback: the change is detected, and the response reverses it. As glucose returns to the set point, less insulin is released. -->

## Set points

**Which pair names a feedback example and its set point?**

- Insulin, about 37 °C
- Body temperature, about 37 °C
- Sweating, about 37 °C
- Shivering, about 5 mmol/L

* Answer: Body temperature, about 37 °C

<!-- Speaker note: A set point is the value being held: body temperature near 37 °C, or blood glucose near 5 mmol/L. Sweating, shivering and insulin are responses. -->

## The whole loop

**On a hot day, match each part of the temperature loop.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Body temperature held near 37 °C` | A. Corrective response |
| `Exercise raises body temperature to 38 °C` | B. The return is detected |
| `Sweating and more blood flow to the skin` | C. A change away from the set point |
| `The hypothalamus detects 37 °C again, so sweating slows` | D. Set point |

* Answer: Body temperature held near 37 °C → D. Set point; Exercise raises body temperature to 38 °C → C. A change away from the set point; Sweating and more blood flow to the skin → A. Corrective response; The hypothalamus detects 37 °C again, so sweating slows → B. The return is detected

## Type 1 diabetes

**In type 1 diabetes the pancreas stops making insulin. What happens to blood glucose after a meal?**

- It stops being measured
- It falls too low
- It stays too high, because cells are not told to take up glucose
- It stays at the set point

* Answer: It stays too high, because cells are not told to take up glucose

<!-- Speaker note: The signal that triggers the response is missing, so the loop cannot bring glucose back down. Homeostasis is disrupted. -->

## Sweat glands

**A person's sweat glands do not work. Which part of the temperature loop has failed, and what is the consequence?**

- Nothing: sweating is not needed
- The receptor: they cannot feel heat
- The set point: it is lower than normal
- The effector: they cannot cool by sweating and can overheat in hot weather

* Answer: The effector: they cannot cool by sweating and can overheat in hot weather

<!-- Speaker note: Receptors and the control centre still work, but one effector cannot carry out the response, so the body cannot cool as well. -->

## Where is the fault?

**Which part of the loop has failed?**

Groups: Receptor · Control centre · Effector

Cold sensors in the skin send no signals · The brain does not process the signal · Muscles do not shiver when told to

* Answer: Cold sensors in the skin send no signals → Receptor; The brain does not process the signal → Control centre; Muscles do not shiver when told to → Effector
