## Tracing a loop


:::keep-together

### Parts of the loop

**Which part of the temperature loop is it?**

Groups: Receptor, Control centre, Effector

- Sweat glands: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Temperature sensors in the skin and blood → Receptor; The hypothalamus in the brain → Control centre; Sweat glands → Effector; Muscles that shiver → Effector; Nerve endings that detect cold → Receptor

:::


:::keep-together

### Blood glucose

**Blood glucose rises after a meal. What reverses the change?**

☐ Insulin makes blood glucose rise further

☐ More glucose is released from the liver

☐ Nothing: it stays high until the next meal

☐ Insulin is released, cells take up glucose, and blood glucose falls back towards its set point

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Insulin is released, cells take up glucose, and blood glucose falls back towards its set point

Negative feedback: the change is detected, and the response reverses it. As glucose returns to the set point, less insulin is released.

:::


:::keep-together

### Set points

**Which pair names a feedback example and its set point?**

☐ Insulin, about 37 °C

☐ Body temperature, about 37 °C

☐ Sweating, about 37 °C

☐ Shivering, about 5 mmol/L

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Body temperature, about 37 °C

A set point is the value being held: body temperature near 37 °C, or blood glucose near 5 mmol/L. Sweating, shivering and insulin are responses.

:::


:::keep-together

## When the loop fails


:::keep-together

### Type 1 diabetes

**In type 1 diabetes the pancreas stops making insulin. What happens to blood glucose after a meal?**

☐ It stops being measured

☐ It falls too low

☐ It stays too high, because cells are not told to take up glucose

☐ It stays at the set point

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It stays too high, because cells are not told to take up glucose

The signal that triggers the response is missing, so the loop cannot bring glucose back down. Homeostasis is disrupted.

:::


:::keep-together

### Sweat glands

**A person's sweat glands do not work. Which part of the temperature loop has failed, and what is the consequence?**

☐ Nothing: sweating is not needed

☐ The receptor: they cannot feel heat

☐ The set point: it is lower than normal

☐ The effector: they cannot cool by sweating and can overheat in hot weather

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The effector: they cannot cool by sweating and can overheat in hot weather

Receptors and the control centre still work, but one effector cannot carry out the response, so the body cannot cool as well.

:::


:::keep-together

### Where is the fault?
