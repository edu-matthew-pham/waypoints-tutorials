### Pollination

**What is pollination?**

☐ The growing of a seed into a plant

☐ The joining of a male and a female gamete

☐ The release of eggs from an ovary

☐ The transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma

Pollination is transfer. Fertilisation comes later, when the pollen's male gamete reaches the ovule and joins the female gamete.

:::


:::keep-together

### Working together

**How do the male and female reproductive structures work together?**

☐ The male structures make the embryo and the female structures only feed it

☐ Either set can make offspring on its own; the other is a backup

☐ The gametes join inside the testis or the anther

☐ Each makes gametes, and the structures carry them to meet so fertilisation can happen

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Each makes gametes, and the structures carry them to meet so fertilisation can happen

Gametes are made in separate structures and brought together, which is what allows fertilisation.

:::

## Asexual reproduction


:::keep-together

### Asexual or sexual?

**Is it asexual or sexual reproduction?**

Groups: Asexual, Sexual

- A strawberry plant growing new plants on runners: {{field-15}}

- A bacterium splitting in two: {{field-15}}
- A flower pollinated and making seeds: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A strawberry plant growing new plants on runners → Asexual; A hydra growing a bud that breaks away → Asexual; A bacterium splitting in two → Asexual; A flower pollinated and making seeds → Sexual; An egg fertilised by a sperm → Sexual

:::


:::keep-together

### When it helps

**When is asexual reproduction an advantage?**

☐ When the species needs variety

☐ When the environment changes suddenly

☐ In a stable environment or when spreading quickly, because one parent can make many offspring fast

☐ Never

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** In a stable environment or when spreading quickly, because one parent can make many offspring fast

There is no need to find a mate, so a successful organism can quickly fill a suitable place with copies of itself.

:::


:::keep-together

### The limit

**What is the main limit of asexual reproduction?**

☐ Offspring are genetically identical, so one disease or change can harm them all

☐ It can only happen in plants

☐ It is too slow

☐ It needs two parents

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Offspring are genetically identical, so one disease or change can harm them all

No variation means no individuals with different traits that might survive a new threat.

:::

### A new disease

**A new disease spreads through a population. Which population is more likely to have survivors?**

☐ One that reproduces sexually, because its variation may include resistant individuals

☐ One that reproduces asexually, because the clones are identical

☐ Neither can have survivors

☐ Both are equally likely

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** One that reproduces sexually, because its variation may include resistant individuals

Variation gives a chance that some individuals can resist. In a population of clones, if one cannot resist the disease, none can.

:::


:::keep-together

### Using both

**Aphids reproduce asexually in summer and sexually in autumn. Why might it help to use both?**

☐ Asexual is fast when conditions are good; sexual makes variation for harder times

☐ Sexual is faster in summer

☐ Asexual makes more variation

☐ It makes no difference

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Asexual is fast when conditions are good; sexual makes variation for harder times

Each strategy suits different conditions, so species that use both can do well in changing environments.

:::
