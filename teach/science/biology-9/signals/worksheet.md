# Nerves or hormones?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Pull your hand off a hot pan and you've done it in a fraction of a second. After lunch, your body brings your blood sugar back down too, but that takes an hour. Same job, different messengers.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe how the nervous and endocrine systems each run the stimulus–receptor–command centre–effector pathway, and compare their signals, speed, duration and reach (AC9S9U01, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a nerve signal and a hormone each carry a message. I can compare how fast each is and how long it lasts. I can name glands as command centres and target organs as effectors.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: comparing the two systems


:::keep-together

### Nervous or endocrine

**Nervous system or endocrine (hormone) system?**

Groups: Nervous, Endocrine

- Electrical signals along nerves: {{field-15}}
- Chemical messages carried in the blood: {{field-15}}
- Very fast: a fraction of a second: {{field-15}}
- Slower: seconds to hours: {{field-15}}
- Short-lived: stops when the signal stops: {{field-15}}
- Lasts longer: until the hormone is broken down: {{field-15}}
- Goes to one precise place: {{field-15}}
- Reaches the whole body; only target organs respond: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Electrical signals along nerves → Nervous; Chemical messages carried in the blood → Endocrine; Very fast: a fraction of a second → Nervous; Slower: seconds to hours → Endocrine; Short-lived: stops when the signal stops → Nervous; Lasts longer: until the hormone is broken down → Endocrine; Goes to one precise place → Nervous; Reaches the whole body; only target organs respond → Endocrine

:::


:::keep-together

### Glands

**In a hormonal pathway, what is the command centre?**

☐ The blood

☐ A gland, such as the pancreas, which releases the hormone

☐ The target organ

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A gland, such as the pancreas, which releases the hormone

Glands (pancreas, adrenal glands, pituitary) act as command centres: they detect or are told of a change and release a hormone.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why faster

**Why do electrical signals reach their effector faster than hormones?**

☐ Hormones have further to go because glands are far away

☐ They travel along nerves directly to one place; hormones drift around the body in the blood

☐ Electricity is stronger than chemicals

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They travel along nerves directly to one place; hormones drift around the body in the blood

Nerve signals travel at up to about 100 m/s along a wired route. Hormones go wherever the blood goes, which takes about a minute for one lap of the body, and then must build up.

:::


:::keep-together

### Both at once

**A fright makes you jump (nerves) and your heart keep pounding for minutes (adrenaline). Why use both?**

☐ Nerves give the instant reaction; the hormone keeps the whole body ready for longer

☐ The nerves stop working after a fright

☐ Adrenaline is a nerve signal

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nerves give the instant reaction; the hormone keeps the whole body ready for longer

Fast and short, plus slow and lasting: many responses use both systems.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Both systems follow stimulus → receptor → command centre → effector → response.
- **Nervous system**: electrical signals along nerves, from the brain or spinal cord. Very fast, short-lived, to one place.
- **Endocrine system**: hormones released by **glands** (pancreas, adrenal glands, pituitary) into the blood. Slower, longer-lasting, reaching target organs anywhere.
- Many responses use both (a fright: a jump from nerves, a pounding heart from adrenaline).
