# Nerves or hormones?

Year 9 Biology · Coordination and control

<!-- Speaker note: Students describe how the nervous and endocrine systems each run the stimulus–receptor–command centre–effector pathway, and compare their signals, speed, duration and reach (AC9S9U01, node 3). About 25 minutes (Engage and Explore 9 min · Explain 10 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 3 min, Wider about 3 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** the body runs the same stimulus–receptor–command centre–effector pathway in two ways. The nervous system sends electrical signals along nerves: fast, short and to one place. The endocrine system sends hormones from glands in the blood: slower, longer-lasting and to target organs anywhere.

- I can explain how a nerve signal and a hormone each carry a message.
- I can compare how fast each is and how long it lasts.
- I can name glands as command centres and target organs as effectors.

## Why it matters

> Pull your hand off a hot pan and you've done it in a fraction of a second. After lunch, your body brings your blood sugar back down too, but that takes an hour. Same job, different messengers.

## Nervous or endocrine

**Nervous system or endocrine (hormone) system?**

Groups: Nervous · Endocrine

Electrical signals along nerves · Chemical messages carried in the blood · Very fast: a fraction of a second · Slower: seconds to hours · Short-lived: stops when the signal stops · Lasts longer: until the hormone is broken down · Goes to one precise place · Reaches the whole body; only target organs respond

* Answer: Electrical signals along nerves → Nervous; Chemical messages carried in the blood → Endocrine; Very fast: a fraction of a second → Nervous; Slower: seconds to hours → Endocrine; Short-lived: stops when the signal stops → Nervous; Lasts longer: until the hormone is broken down → Endocrine; Goes to one precise place → Nervous; Reaches the whole body; only target organs respond → Endocrine

## Glands

**In a hormonal pathway, what is the command centre?**

- The blood
- A gland, such as the pancreas, which releases the hormone
- The target organ

* Answer: A gland, such as the pancreas, which releases the hormone

## Why faster

**Why do electrical signals reach their effector faster than hormones?**

- Hormones have further to go because glands are far away
- They travel along nerves directly to one place; hormones drift around the body in the blood
- Electricity is stronger than chemicals

* Answer: They travel along nerves directly to one place; hormones drift around the body in the blood

<!-- Speaker note: Nerve signals travel at up to about 100 m/s along a wired route. Hormones go wherever the blood goes, which takes about a minute for one lap of the body, and then must build up. -->

## Both at once

**A fright makes you jump (nerves) and your heart keep pounding for minutes (adrenaline). Why use both?**

- Nerves give the instant reaction; the hormone keeps the whole body ready for longer
- The nerves stop working after a fright
- Adrenaline is a nerve signal

* Answer: Nerves give the instant reaction; the hormone keeps the whole body ready for longer

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Both systems follow stimulus → receptor → command centre → effector → response.
- **Nervous system**: electrical signals along nerves, from the brain or spinal cord. Very fast, short-lived, to one place.
- **Endocrine system**: hormones released by **glands** (pancreas, adrenal glands, pituitary) into the blood. Slower, longer-lasting, reaching target organs anywhere.
- Many responses use both (a fright: a jump from nerves, a pounding heart from adrenaline).

## Exit ticket

**Give one difference between how a nerve signal and a hormone travel, and one difference in how long their effects last.**

* Nerve signals are electrical, along nerves; hormones are chemicals in the blood. Nerve effects are brief; hormone effects last longer.
