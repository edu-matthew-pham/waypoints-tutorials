# What moves the plates?

Year 8 Earth and space · Plate tectonics

## Today

**We are learning to** the lithosphere is broken into rigid plates that move a few centimetres a year. Slow convection currents in the hot mantle carry them, driven by Earth's inner heat: from the core below and from radioactive elements in the mantle rock itself.

- I can explain that the lithosphere is divided into plates that move.
- I can describe mantle convection as the mechanism that moves the plates.
- I can state that plates move a few centimetres a year.

## Why rock rises

**Why does hot rock above the core rise?**

- Hot things always go up because heat rises
- It is less dense than the cooler rock around it
- The core pushes it out
- It has melted into liquid magma, which floats up

* Answer: It is less dense than the cooler rock around it

<!-- Speaker note: Heated rock expands a little, so it is less dense and rises. Cooled rock is denser and sinks. -->

## Three forces

**Match each force to how it moves a plate.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Slab pull` | A. A plate slides away, downhill, from a high mid-ocean ridge |
| `Ridge push` | B. Flowing mantle carries the plate above it along |
| `Mantle drag` | C. Old, cold, dense plate sinks into the mantle and drags the rest of the plate behind it |

* Answer: Slab pull → C. Old, cold, dense plate sinks into the mantle and drags the rest of the plate behind it; Ridge push → A. A plate slides away, downhill, from a high mid-ocean ridge; Mantle drag → B. Flowing mantle carries the plate above it along

## Which matters most?

**The fastest plates (like the Pacific plate) are the ones with long edges sinking into the mantle. Which force does that suggest matters most?**

- Neither: plate speed is random
- Slab pull
- Ridge push

* Answer: Slab pull

<!-- Speaker note: Most geologists now think slab pull is the biggest force. All three are parts of the same convection system. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The **lithosphere** (crust and top of the mantle) is broken into rigid **tectonic plates**, moving a few centimetres a year.
- Earth's inner heat (from the **core** below, and from radioactive elements in the mantle rock) drives **convection currents** in the **mantle**: hot rock is less dense and rises, spreads, cools and sinks.
- The currents carry the plates: apart above rising rock, together above sinking rock.
- The mantle is solid rock that flows extremely slowly, not liquid magma.
