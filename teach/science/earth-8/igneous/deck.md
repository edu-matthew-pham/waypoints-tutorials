# Why do some rocks have big crystals?

Year 8 Earth and space · The rock cycle

## Today

**We are learning to** igneous rock forms when magma or lava cools. Slow cooling underground gives large crystals (granite); fast cooling at the surface gives small crystals (basalt) or glass (obsidian). Crystal size is a record of cooling rate.

- I can tell intrusive (slow cooling underground) from extrusive (fast cooling at the surface) igneous rock.
- I can explain why slower cooling gives larger crystals.
- I can infer where an igneous rock formed from its crystal size.

> Granite kitchen benches have crystals you can see from across the room. Basalt has crystals too small to see. Both crystallised from molten rock: what was different?

## Slow cooling

**Why did slow cooling give bigger crystals?**

- Slow cooling melts the small crystals
- There was more magma
- Only a few crystals started, and the particles had time to join them
- Slow cooling makes each particle bigger

* Answer: Only a few crystals started, and the particles had time to join them

<!-- Speaker note: With little time below the melting point, few crystals start. Each one has time to collect particles from the melt and grow large. -->

## Fast cooling

**Why did fast cooling give small crystals?**

- The particles shrank as they cooled
- Many crystals started at once, and the melt set before any could grow large
- Fast cooling breaks crystals apart

* Answer: Many crystals started at once, and the melt set before any could grow large

<!-- Speaker note: Cooling fast, the melt is soon far below its melting point and crystals start everywhere. They share out the particles and the rock sets before any gets big. -->

## Intrusive or extrusive

**Where did each rock form?**

Groups: Intrusive (underground, slow cooling) · Extrusive (at the surface, fast cooling)

Granite: large crystals you can see · Basalt: crystals too small to see · Obsidian: glass, no crystals · Gabbro: large crystals, dark

* Answer: Granite: large crystals you can see → Intrusive (underground, slow cooling); Basalt: crystals too small to see → Extrusive (at the surface, fast cooling); Obsidian: glass, no crystals → Extrusive (at the surface, fast cooling); Gabbro: large crystals, dark → Intrusive (underground, slow cooling)

## Glass

**With instant cooling, most of the rock was glass. What does glass mean at the particle level?**

- The particles evaporated
- The particles froze where they were, with no pattern
- The particles made one giant crystal

* Answer: The particles froze where they were, with no pattern

## Big and small together

**A rock has large crystals surrounded by tiny ones (geologists call it porphyry). What is the most likely history?**

- It cooled slowly underground for a while, then erupted and cooled quickly
- It cooled quickly and then slowly
- Two different rocks were glued together

* Answer: It cooled slowly underground for a while, then erupted and cooled quickly

<!-- Speaker note: Two stages: slow cooling grew the big crystals, then fast cooling set the rest as tiny crystals around them. -->

## Pumice

**Pumice is a glassy rock full of holes, so light it can float. What can you infer about how it formed?**

- It cooled very fast, while gas bubbles were still escaping
- It was worn into shape by water
- It cooled slowly deep underground

* Answer: It cooled very fast, while gas bubbles were still escaping

<!-- Speaker note: Frothy lava blasted out of a volcano: cooled almost instantly, trapping gas bubbles in glass. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Igneous** rock forms when magma (underground) or lava (at the surface) cools and sets.
- **Slow cooling** (intrusive, underground): few crystals start and grow large. Granite, gabbro.
- **Fast cooling** (extrusive, at the surface): many crystals start, all stay small. Basalt.
- **Instant cooling**: no time for a pattern, so glass. Obsidian, pumice.
- **Crystal size is a record of cooling rate.**

## Exit ticket

**A rock has crystals too small to see. Did it form underground or at the surface? Why?**

* At the surface: it cooled quickly, so many crystals started and none had time to grow large.
