# Filtration and evaporation

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** How do you get clean salt back out of sandy salt water? Two techniques, in the right order: filtration takes out what didn't dissolve, and evaporation takes the water away from what did.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe filtration and evaporation accurately, name the residue and filtrate, and choose the right technique with a property reason (AC9S7U06, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe how filtration and evaporation are done, in the right order. I can name the residue and the filtrate. I can choose filtration, evaporation or both for a mixture, and say why.

## Explain: get the steps in order

### Filtration

**Put the filtration steps in order.**

A. `Pour the mixture in slowly`

B. `Put the cone in a funnel over a beaker`

C. `Leave the solid (the residue) in the paper`

D. `Let the liquid (the filtrate) drip into the beaker`

E. `Fold the filter paper into a cone`

Correct order (letters): {{field-24}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** see the program below

Pouring slowly stops the mixture overflowing the paper.

```science
Fold the filter paper into a cone
Put the cone in a funnel over a beaker
Pour the mixture in slowly
Let the liquid (the filtrate) drip into the beaker
Leave the solid (the residue) in the paper
```

:::


:::keep-together

### Evaporation

**Put the evaporation steps in order.**

A. `Stop heating when most of the water has gone`

B. `Let the rest evaporate to leave crystals`

C. `Heat it gently`

D. `Pour the filtrate into an evaporating dish`

Correct order (letters): {{field-24}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** see the program below

Stopping before the dish is completely dry stops the salt spitting.

```science
Pour the filtrate into an evaporating dish
Heat it gently
Stop heating when most of the water has gone
Let the rest evaporate to leave crystals
```

:::


:::keep-together

### Why each works

**Filtration can't get the salt out of salt water, but evaporation can. Why?**

☐ Heating destroys the water and leaves the salt

☐ Dissolved salt has disappeared, and heating makes it come back

☐ Dissolved salt particles are small enough to pass through the paper, but when heated only the water particles escape, because water boils far below salt

☐ Salt is heavier than water, so it sinks while the lighter water rises away

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Dissolved salt particles are small enough to pass through the paper, but when heated only the water particles escape, because water boils far below salt

Filtration sorts by particle size, and dissolved particles are tiny. Evaporation uses boiling point: the water leaves, the salt stays.

:::

### The procedure

**You have a dry mixture of sand and salt. Order the steps to end up with dry sand and salt crystals, separately.**

A. `Collect the salt crystals`

B. `Rinse the sand in the paper with a little clean water, then leave it to dry`

C. `Filter the mixture`

D. `Gently evaporate the filtrate, rinse water included`

E. `Add water to the mixture and stir until the salt dissolves`

Correct order (letters): {{field-24}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** see the program below

Dissolving first is the key move: the salt has to be in solution before the filter can separate it from the sand.

```science
Add water to the mixture and stir until the salt dissolves
Filter the mixture
Rinse the sand in the paper with a little clean water, then leave it to dry
Gently evaporate the filtrate, rinse water included
Collect the salt crystals
```

## Evaluate: look back

What did you think would stay in the filter paper? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Filtration separates an undissolved solid from a liquid. The solid left in the paper is the **residue**; the liquid that passes through is the **filtrate**.
- It works because the solid's particles are too big for the holes. Dissolved particles pass straight through.
- Evaporation separates a dissolved solid from the liquid: the liquid's particles escape as a gas; the solid's particles stay and form crystals.
- For a solid that dissolves plus one that doesn't: filter first, then evaporate the filtrate.
