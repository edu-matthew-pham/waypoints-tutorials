## Why it matters

> How do you get clean salt back out of sandy salt water? Two techniques, in the right order: filtration takes out what didn't dissolve, and evaporation takes the water away from what did.

<!-- Speaker note: Both techniques use a property difference from the last lesson: **particle size** for filtration, **boiling point** for evaporation. This lesson is about doing them correctly. -->

## Filtration

**Put the filtration steps in order.**

```science
Pour the mixture in slowly
Put the cone in a funnel over a beaker
Leave the solid (the residue) in the paper
Let the liquid (the filtrate) drip into the beaker
Fold the filter paper into a cone
```

## Why each works

**Filtration can't get the salt out of salt water, but evaporation can. Why?**

- Heating destroys the water and leaves the salt
- Dissolved salt has disappeared, and heating makes it come back
- Dissolved salt particles are small enough to pass through the paper, but when heated only the water particles escape, because water boils far below salt
- Salt is heavier than water, so it sinks while the lighter water rises away

* Answer: Dissolved salt particles are small enough to pass through the paper, but when heated only the water particles escape, because water boils far below salt

<!-- Speaker note: Filtration sorts by particle size, and dissolved particles are tiny. Evaporation uses boiling point: the water leaves, the salt stays. -->

**Looking back at pure substances and mixtures: before it is filtered, is sandy salt water a pure substance?**

- No, because it looks cloudy

## The procedure

**You have a dry mixture of sand and salt. Order the steps to end up with dry sand and salt crystals, separately.**

```science
Collect the salt crystals
Rinse the sand in the paper with a little clean water, then leave it to dry
Filter the mixture
Gently evaporate the filtrate, rinse water included
Add water to the mixture and stir until the salt dissolves
```

<!-- Speaker note: Dissolving first is the key move: the salt has to be in solution before the filter can separate it from the sand. ## Evaluate: look back

What did you think would stay in the filter paper? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Filtration separates an undissolved solid from a liquid. The solid left in the paper is the **residue**; the liquid that passes through is the **filtrate**.
- It works because the solid's particles are too big for the holes. Dissolved particles pass straight through.
- Evaporation separates a dissolved solid from the liquid: the liquid's particles escape as a gas; the solid's particles stay and form crystals.
- For a solid that dissolves plus one that doesn't: filter first, then evaporate the filtrate.
