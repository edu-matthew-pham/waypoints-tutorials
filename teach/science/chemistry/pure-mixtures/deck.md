# Pure substance or mixture?

Year 7 Chemistry · Pure substances and mixtures

<!-- Speaker note: Students distinguish pure substances from mixtures at the particle level and classify familiar materials with a particle reason (AC9S7U06, node 1, a hinge node). About 25 minutes (Engage and Explore 8 min · Explain 10 min · Even and uneven mixtures 5 min · Evaluate 3 min · Wider adds about 15 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** a pure substance contains only one kind of particle; a mixture contains two or more. Classify familiar materials by their particles, not by how they look.

- I can tell a pure substance from a mixture using a particle diagram.
- I can classify familiar materials and give a particle reason.
- I can explain why a solution like salt water is a mixture even though it looks the same all through.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: A concept tutorial for introducing or revising the idea. Set the width with `?width=minimum`, `typical` (default) or `wider`. Hinge: technique selection in nodes 3 to 5 needs students to know what kind of mixture they have. The success criterion to watch is "does not confuse homogeneous mixtures with pure substances". -->

---

## Why it matters

> In the supermarket, "100% pure" orange juice means nothing has been added. In science, pure means something much stricter, and you can only check it by looking at the particles.

<!-- Speaker note: Before you can separate a mixture, you need to know whether you have one. That is a question about **particles**, not appearance. -->

## Salt water

**Salt water looks exactly the same all the way through. Why is it still a mixture?**

- It contains two kinds of particles, even though they are evenly spread
- Because it is slightly cloudy
- Because people added the salt
- It isn't: the salt has disappeared, so it is pure water

* Answer: It contains two kinds of particles, even though they are evenly spread

<!-- Speaker note: Evenly spread or not, two kinds of particles make a mixture. -->

**Seawater can contain more or less salt depending on where it is collected. Is seawater with extra salt still the same mixture?**

## The definition

**Which statement is true of every pure substance?**

- It looks shiny and even
- It always has the same make-up, with one kind of particle
- It occurs naturally

* Answer: It always has the same make-up, with one kind of particle

<!-- Speaker note: That is what makes the definition useful: a pure substance's make-up can't vary. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Which did you think was pure, and what made you think so? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A pure substance contains only one kind of particle. A mixture contains two or more.
- You can't tell by looking: salt water and air look the same all the way through, but both are mixtures.
- "Pure" on a food label means nothing added. It does not mean a pure substance.
