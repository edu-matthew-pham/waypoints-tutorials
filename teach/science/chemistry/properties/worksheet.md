# Why can you squash air but not water?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Seal the end of a syringe full of air and you can push the plunger a long way in. Do the same with water and it barely moves. Particle theory explains why, and the same explanation works for density, runny honey and smells crossing a room.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use particle theory to explain physical properties (compressibility, density, viscosity), naming the arrangement, motion or attraction behind each (AC9S7U05, node 3, a hinge node for all of AC9S7U06).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why gases can be squashed and liquids and solids can't. I can explain why a gas is less dense than a liquid using particle spacing. I can name the particle feature (arrangement, motion or attraction) behind a property.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Explain: use the particles


:::keep-together

### The water syringe

**Why does the plunger stop in the water syringe?**

☐ The water particles are too hard to squash

☐ The water particles are already close together, so there is almost no space to push them into

☐ The water particles stop moving when pushed

☐ Water is heavier than air

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The water particles are already close together, so there is almost no space to push them into

The particles stay the same size; there is just no empty space left.

:::


:::keep-together

### Density

**Why is air much less dense than water?**

☐ Air particles are smaller

☐ Air particles are far apart, so there are far fewer in each cm³

☐ Each air particle is lighter than a water particle

☐ Air has no mass

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Air particles are far apart, so there are far fewer in each cm³

Fewer particles in each cm³ means less mass in each cm³: lower density.

:::


## Mix it up


:::keep-together

**Looking back at the particle model: a drop of water is heated until it becomes steam. What happens to the water particles?**

☐ They disappear

☐ They get bigger

☐ They break into smaller pieces

☐ They stay the same size, but move faster and spread far apart

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They stay the same size, but move faster and spread far apart

Ice, water and steam are the same particles. Only their arrangement and motion change, so any squashing comes from the space between them.

## Elaborate: three substances


:::keep-together

**Honey, water and air. Which particle feature best explains each observation?**

Groups: Arrangement: how close and ordered the particles are, Motion: how fast the particles move, Attraction: how strongly particles pull on each other

- Honey pours much more slowly than water.: {{field-15}}
- Air can be squashed but water can't.: {{field-15}}
- Water is about 800 times denser than air.: {{field-15}}
- The smell of honey spreads through a whole room.: {{field-15}}
- Warm honey pours faster than cold honey.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Honey pours much more slowly than water. → Attraction: how strongly particles pull on each other; Air can be squashed but water can't. → Arrangement: how close and ordered the particles are; Water is about 800 times denser than air. → Arrangement: how close and ordered the particles are; The smell of honey spreads through a whole room. → Motion: how fast the particles move; Warm honey pours faster than cold honey. → Motion: how fast the particles move

Honey and water have the same arrangement but behave differently: attraction makes the difference.

Two colours of the same substance start on opposite sides of a barrier. When it lifts, they mix: this is **diffusion**. Predict first: will the gas or the liquid mix faster? Then time both.

**Why did the gas mix faster?**

☐ Gas particles are smaller

## Evaluate: look back

Which syringe did you predict would move furthest, and why? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Gases can be squashed because their particles are far apart, with lots of empty space between them.
- Solids and liquids hardly squash at all: their particles are already close together.
- Gases are much less dense because there are far fewer particles in each cm³. The same substance as a solid or a liquid has a similar density: its particles are close together in both.
- The particles themselves never shrink or squash. Only the spaces between them change.
