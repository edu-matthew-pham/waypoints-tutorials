## Today

**We are learning to** all matter is made of tiny particles with empty space between them, always moving and attracting each other. Match particle diagrams to solids, liquids and gases.

- I can state the four key ideas of particle theory.
- I can match a particle diagram to a solid, a liquid or a gas.
- I can describe how arrangement and motion differ between the three states.

## Why it matters

> Water looks smooth and continuous. Zoom in about a billion times and the picture changes completely: that picture is the particle model, and the rest of chemistry is built on it.

<!-- Speaker note: Scientists explain how substances behave with a **model**: a simplified picture that is good at explaining and predicting. This lesson builds the particle model and its four key ideas. -->

## The four key ideas

**Which statements are part of particle theory?**

Groups: Part of particle theory · Not true

All matter is made of tiny particles. · There is empty space between particles. · Particles are always moving. · Particles attract each other. · Particles expand when they are heated. · There is air between the particles. · Particles in a solid do not move.

* Answer: All matter is made of tiny particles. → Part of particle theory; There is empty space between particles. → Part of particle theory; Particles are always moving. → Part of particle theory; Particles attract each other. → Part of particle theory; Particles expand when they are heated. → Not true; There is air between the particles. → Not true; Particles in a solid do not move. → Not true

## Across the states

**Match each state to its particles: arrangement, motion and attraction.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Solid` | A. Far apart, moving fast in all directions, very weak attraction |
| `Liquid` | B. Close together in a fixed pattern, vibrating in place, strong attraction |
| `Gas` | C. Close together with no pattern, sliding past each other, weaker attraction |

* Answer: Solid → B. Close together in a fixed pattern, vibrating in place, strong attraction; Liquid → C. Close together with no pattern, sliding past each other, weaker attraction; Gas → A. Far apart, moving fast in all directions, very weak attraction

<!-- Speaker note: The three states differ in the same three ways: arrangement, motion and attraction. -->

## The evidence

**In 1827 the botanist Robert Brown saw tiny grains from pollen jiggling in water under a microscope, in random directions, with nothing stirring the water. In 1905 Albert Einstein explained it with particles. What causes the jiggling?**

- The grains are alive and swimming
- The water is flowing slowly
- Water particles are always moving and keep bumping into the grains

* Answer: Water particles are always moving and keep bumping into the grains

<!-- Speaker note: This is Brownian motion: millions of tiny bumps from moving water particles. It was early strong evidence that particles are real and always moving. -->

## Elaborate: designing with materials

**Match each design choice to the particle reason.**

Groups: A gas can be squashed, so it cushions bumps · A liquid hardly squashes, so a push at one end moves the other end straight away · Solid particles are held in fixed positions, so the shape stays under a load · Its particles are lighter

Bike tyres are filled with air, not water. · Car brakes use a liquid in the pipes to push the brake pads. · Bridge beams are made of solid steel.

* Answer: Bike tyres are filled with air, not water. → A gas can be squashed, so it cushions bumps; Car brakes use a liquid in the pipes to push the brake pads. → A liquid hardly squashes, so a push at one end moves the other end straight away; Bridge beams are made of solid steel. → Solid particles are held in fixed positions, so the shape stays under a load
