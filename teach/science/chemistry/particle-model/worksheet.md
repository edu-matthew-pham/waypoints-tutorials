# What is everything made of?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Water looks smooth and continuous. Zoom in about a billion times and the picture changes completely: that picture is the particle model, and the rest of chemistry is built on it.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build the particle model's four key ideas (particles, space, motion, attraction) and match particle diagrams to solids, liquids and gases (AC9S7U05, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can state the four key ideas of particle theory. I can match a particle diagram to a solid, a liquid or a gas. I can describe how arrangement and motion differ between the three states.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### The evidence

**In 1827 the botanist Robert Brown saw tiny grains from pollen jiggling in water under a microscope, in random directions, with nothing stirring the water. In 1905 Albert Einstein explained it with particles. What causes the jiggling?**

☐ The grains are alive and swimming

☐ The water is flowing slowly

☐ Water particles are always moving and keep bumping into the grains

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Water particles are always moving and keep bumping into the grains

This is Brownian motion: millions of tiny bumps from moving water particles. It was early strong evidence that particles are real and always moving.
