## Engage


:::keep-together

**A crash dummy goes from 15 m/s to a stop either against a hard dashboard in 0.02 s or against an airbag in 0.2 s. Compared with the dashboard, the force from the airbag is:**

☐ About ten times smaller, because the acceleration is ten times smaller

☐ About ten times bigger

☐ Zero: the airbag absorbs it all

☐ The same, because the speed change is the same

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** About ten times smaller. The change in speed is the same, but taking ten times as long makes the acceleration ten times smaller, and F = ma.

:::

### The woomera

**A spearthrower (woomera) is a long, light lever that holds the back of the spear. Why does it make a spear go faster than throwing by hand?**

☐ It makes the arm effectively longer, so the force acts on the spear for a longer time and distance, giving a bigger acceleration and a higher launch speed

☐ It removes air resistance

☐ It makes the spear heavier

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It makes the arm effectively longer, so the force acts on the spear for a longer time and distance, giving a bigger acceleration and a higher launch speed

A longer push means more time to accelerate the spear, so it leaves at a higher speed. The same idea explains why a longer bow draw or a golf club swing launches faster.

:::


:::keep-together

### Crumple zone

**A 1000 kg car going 20 m/s is stopped by crumple zones in 0.5 s. What is its acceleration (m/s², size only)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40

a = 20 ÷ 0.5 = 40 m/s².

### Crumple zone force

**What average force is needed to stop it (N, size only)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40000

F = ma = 1000 × 40 = 40 000 N.

:::keep-together

### A better crumple zone

**A better crumple zone stops the same car in 1.0 s instead of 0.5 s. What happens to the force on the people inside?**

☐ It doubles

☐ It stays the same

☐ It halves, so injuries are likely to be less serious

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It halves, so injuries are likely to be less serious

Twice the stopping time gives half the acceleration and so half the force: 20 000 N.

## Elaborate: sport


:::keep-together

**A footballer follows through when kicking a ball. In terms of force and time, why does the ball go further?**

☐ The foot stays in contact and pushes for longer, so the ball gains more speed

☐ The follow-through keeps pushing the ball after it has left the foot

☐ A follow-through reduces air resistance

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The foot stays in contact and pushes for longer, so the ball gains more speed

A longer contact time means the force acts for longer, which gives the ball a higher launch speed.

## Elaborate: braking by itself


:::keep-together

**An automatic emergency braking system detects an obstacle 25 m ahead of a 1500 kg car at 20 m/s. What does it need to work out?**

☐ Whether the braking force the car can apply gives a stopping distance shorter than 25 m

☐ The colour of the obstacle

☐ Whether the car is under the speed limit

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Whether the braking force the car can apply gives a stopping distance shorter than 25 m

It must compare the stopping distance from the car's speed and best possible braking force with the distance to the obstacle, quickly enough to act.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **First law** (inertia): seat belts and head restraints stop passengers carrying on moving when the car stops.
- **Second law**: airbags and crumple zones lengthen the stopping time, so the acceleration and the **force** are smaller. A spearthrower extends the push, so the spear gets a higher launch speed.
- **Third law**: a rocket pushes gas back and the gas pushes the rocket forward.
- Numbers show how much a design helps: twice the stopping time halves the force.
