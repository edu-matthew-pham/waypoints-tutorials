## Today

**We are learning to** newton's laws explain why safety features and techniques work. Seat belts and head restraints deal with inertia (first law); airbags and crumple zones lengthen the stopping time to cut the force (F = ma); rockets push gases back and are pushed forward (third law); and a spearthrower (woomera) extends the thrower's arm so the force acts for longer and over a greater distance, so the spear leaves faster.

- I can use two of Newton's laws to explain a real design or technique.
- I can explain how a spearthrower works in terms of force, time and acceleration.
- I can compare two safety designs with F = ma.

## Which law

**Match each feature to the law that explains it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Seat belt keeps you in your seat when the car stops` | A. Second law: a smaller acceleration means a smaller force |
| `Airbag spreads the stop over more time` | B. Second law: more mass, less acceleration |
| `Rocket gases go down, rocket goes up` | C. First law: your body keeps moving unless a force acts |
| `A heavier ball is harder to accelerate` | D. Third law: action and reaction forces |

* Answer: Seat belt keeps you in your seat when the car stops → C. First law: your body keeps moving unless a force acts; Airbag spreads the stop over more time → A. Second law: a smaller acceleration means a smaller force; Rocket gases go down, rocket goes up → D. Third law: action and reaction forces; A heavier ball is harder to accelerate → B. Second law: more mass, less acceleration

## The woomera

**A spearthrower (woomera) is a long, light lever that holds the back of the spear. Why does it make a spear go faster than throwing by hand?**

- It makes the arm effectively longer, so the force acts on the spear for a longer time and distance, giving a bigger acceleration and a higher launch speed
- It removes air resistance
- It makes the spear heavier

* Answer: It makes the arm effectively longer, so the force acts on the spear for a longer time and distance, giving a bigger acceleration and a higher launch speed

<!-- Speaker note: A longer push means more time to accelerate the spear, so it leaves at a higher speed. The same idea explains why a longer bow draw or a golf club swing launches faster. -->

## Crumple zone

**A 1000 kg car going 20 m/s is stopped by crumple zones in 0.5 s. What is its acceleration (m/s², size only)?**

* Answer: 40

## A better crumple zone

**A better crumple zone stops the same car in 1.0 s instead of 0.5 s. What happens to the force on the people inside?**

- It doubles
- It stays the same
- It halves, so injuries are likely to be less serious

* Answer: It halves, so injuries are likely to be less serious

<!-- Speaker note: Twice the stopping time gives half the acceleration and so half the force: 20 000 N. -->

## Elaborate: sport

**A footballer follows through when kicking a ball. In terms of force and time, why does the ball go further?**

- The foot stays in contact and pushes for longer, so the ball gains more speed
- The follow-through keeps pushing the ball after it has left the foot
- A follow-through reduces air resistance

* Answer: The foot stays in contact and pushes for longer, so the ball gains more speed

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **First law** (inertia): seat belts and head restraints stop passengers carrying on moving when the car stops.
- **Second law**: airbags and crumple zones lengthen the stopping time, so the acceleration and the **force** are smaller. A spearthrower extends the push, so the spear gets a higher launch speed.
- **Third law**: a rocket pushes gas back and the gas pushes the rocket forward.
- Numbers show how much a design helps: twice the stopping time halves the force.

**A car stops from 20 m/s in 0.5 s with one crumple zone, and in 1.0 s with a better one. By what factor does the force on the occupants change?**
