# What is the net force?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A car's engine pushes it forward with 2000 newtons. So why doesn't it keep speeding up for ever?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students represent forces as vectors and add or subtract forces along one line to find the net force, then say what the net force does to motion (AC9S10U05, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why force is a vector. I can find the net force from several forces along one line. I can say whether a net force will change an object's motion.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**A car's driving force is 2000 N forward. Air resistance is 500 N and rolling resistance 300 N, both backward. What is the net force on the car?**

☐ 2800 N forward

☐ 1200 N forward

☐ Zero: the forces cancel

☐ 2000 N forward

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 1200 N forward. The two backward forces add to 800 N; opposite to the 2000 N forward, so they subtract: 2000 − 800 = 1200 N, in the direction of the bigger side (forward).

:::

## Explain: forces as vectors


:::keep-together

### Size and direction

**Why is force called a vector?**

☐ It can only act in a straight line

☐ It has a size and a direction, and both matter

☐ It is measured in newtons

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It has a size and a direction, and both matter

A vector has size and direction. 10 N left and 10 N right are different forces, and they cancel.

:::


:::keep-together

### A push and friction

**Two people push a stalled car forward with 300 N and 250 N. Friction acts backward with 400 N. What is the net force, in newtons? (forward is positive)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 150

Forward: 300 + 250 = 550 N. Backward: 400 N. Net: 550 − 400 = 150 N forward.

:::


:::keep-together

### Tug of war

**In a tug of war, Team A pulls left with 1800 N and Team B pulls right with 2100 N. What is the size of the net force, in newtons?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 300

Opposite directions: subtract. 2100 − 1800 = 300 N, to the right (Team B's way).

:::


:::keep-together

### Balanced

**A skydiver weighs 700 N. Air resistance on her is 700 N up. What is happening to her motion?**

☐ She must be stopped in the air

☐ Nothing is changing: net force zero, so she falls at a steady speed

☐ She is speeding up, because weight is pulling her down

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nothing is changing: net force zero, so she falls at a steady speed

Balanced forces give a net force of zero: the motion doesn't change. She keeps falling at the same speed (terminal velocity).

:::


:::keep-together

## Elaborate: a four-person tug of war


:::keep-together

### Dee's pull

**Ana pulls 400 N and Ben 300 N to the left. Cai pulls 450 N to the right. How hard must Dee pull right for the net force to be zero?**

☐ 150 N

☐ 250 N

☐ 700 N

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 250 N

Left: 400 + 300 = 700 N. Right must also be 700 N: 450 + 250 = 700.

## Elaborate: driving at a steady speed


:::keep-together

### Why no speeding up

**The car's engine is still pushing with 1500 N, but it isn't speeding up. Why?**

☐ The engine's force is used up

☐ Moving things always slow down unless pushed harder

☐ Air and rolling resistance add up to 1500 N backward, so the net force is zero

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Air and rolling resistance add up to 1500 N backward, so the net force is zero

Net force zero: no change in motion. The engine only needs to cancel the resistance to keep a steady speed.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A force is a **vector**: it has a size (in newtons) and a direction.
- Along one line, forces in the same direction **add**; forces in opposite directions **subtract**. Choose one direction as positive.
- What is left is the **net force** (resultant force), in the direction of the bigger side.
- A net force changes motion (speeds up, slows down, turns). A net force of zero leaves the motion unchanged: still, or at a steady speed.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**A cyclist pedals with a forward force of 120 N. Air resistance is 70 N and friction 20 N. Find the net force and say what happens to her speed.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 120 − (70 + 20) = 30 N forward: she speeds up.

:::
