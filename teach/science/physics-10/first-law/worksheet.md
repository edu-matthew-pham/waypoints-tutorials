# Does a moving thing need a push?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Kick a ball on grass and it rolls to a stop. Slide a puck on ice and it goes much further. In space, a probe launched decades ago is still coasting out of the solar system with its engines off.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students state Newton's first law and explain why a moving object with no net force continues at constant velocity, confronting the Aristotelian idea that motion needs a continuous force (AC9S10U05, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can state Newton's first law. I can explain why a moving object with no net force keeps a constant velocity. I can identify friction as the hidden force that slows everyday objects.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**A hockey stick hits a puck, then stops touching it. The puck slides across perfectly smooth ice (no friction). What do you predict happens?**

☐ It stops straight away

☐ It speeds up

☐ It slows down as the force of the hit runs out

☐ It keeps sliding at the same speed

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** It keeps sliding at the same speed. With no net force, nothing changes its motion. The 'force of the hit' isn't stored inside it: the force only acted while the stick touched it.

:::

## Explain: the first law


:::keep-together

### Coasting

**With no push and no friction, what did the puck do?**

☐ Slowed down gradually

☐ Stopped

☐ Kept moving at a constant speed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Kept moving at a constant speed

No net force, no change in motion: Newton's first law.

:::


:::keep-together

### The law

**Which statement is Newton's first law?**

☐ Heavier objects are harder to keep moving

☐ Every object eventually comes to rest

☐ A moving object needs a force to keep it moving

☐ An object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant velocity unless a net force acts on it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** An object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant velocity unless a net force acts on it

:::


:::keep-together

### Why things slow down

**So why does a ball rolling on grass slow down and stop?**

☐ Balls naturally stop

☐ Friction and air resistance act against it: a net force backwards

☐ Gravity pulls it backwards

☐ Its force runs out

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Friction and air resistance act against it: a net force backwards

The 'hidden' forces, friction and air resistance, are the net force that slows it.

:::


:::keep-together

### Inertia

**What is inertia?**

☐ The friction on a moving object

☐ An object's tendency to resist changes in its motion (more mass, more inertia)

☐ A force that keeps objects moving

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** An object's tendency to resist changes in its motion (more mass, more inertia)

:::

## Elaborate: friction is the hidden force


:::keep-together

### What slows it

**Friction is 2 N and the puck's mass is 1 kg. While it slides, what is happening?**

☐ A net force of 2 N backwards makes it slow down steadily

☐ Its push slowly runs out

☐ Friction is balanced by its motion

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A net force of 2 N backwards makes it slow down steadily

The only horizontal force is friction, so it is the net force. The graph slopes down in a straight line: steady slowing.

## Elaborate: seatbelts


:::keep-together

**A car hits a barrier and stops suddenly. A passenger isn't wearing a seatbelt. Using the first law, what happens to them?**

☐ They stop with the car

☐ They keep moving forward at the car's old speed until something (the dashboard, windscreen) exerts a force on them

☐ They are thrown forward by a force from the crash

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They keep moving forward at the car's old speed until something (the dashboard, windscreen) exerts a force on them

A seatbelt applies the force that stops the passenger along with the car, spread over a little more time and a wide area.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Newton's first law:** an object stays at rest, or keeps moving at a constant velocity, unless a **net force** acts on it.
- A moving object does **not** need a force to keep moving. A force is needed to **change** motion.
- Everyday objects slow down because of **friction and air resistance**: hidden forces that are the net force.
- **Inertia** is an object's resistance to changes in its motion; more mass, more inertia.
