## Today

**We are learning to** a free body diagram shows one object and every force acting on it, as arrows from the object: size by length, direction by the way it points. Add the forces along each direction to find the net force, then use Newton's laws to say what the object's motion will do.

- I can draw a free body diagram with every force on an object.
- I can find the net force in each direction.
- I can say what the object's motion will do from its free body diagram.

## Engage: predict

**A plane cruises at a steady speed and height. What can you say about the forces on it?**

- Thrust is bigger than drag, or it would slow down
- There are no forces on it
- They balance both ways: lift equals weight, thrust equals drag
- Lift is bigger than weight, or it would fall

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: They balance in both directions. Steady speed in a straight line means no change in motion, so no net force (Newton's first law): lift balances weight, and thrust balances drag. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What goes on it

**What goes on a free body diagram?**

- Every object in the scene and all the forces between them
- One object, and every force acting on that object
- Only the forces that make the object move

* Answer: One object, and every force acting on that object

## The skydiver

**A 600 N skydiver has 450 N of air resistance acting up on her. What is the net force on her, in newtons, downwards?**

* Answer: 150

<!-- Speaker note: 600 − 450 = 150 N downwards: she is still speeding up. -->

## The box

**A 2 kg box is pushed right with 14 N against 6 N of friction. What is its acceleration, in m/s²?**

* Answer: 4

<!-- Speaker note: Net force 14 − 6 = 8 N right. a = F ÷ m = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 m/s². -->

## The lift

**A lift's cable pulls up with exactly its weight. The lift is moving up. What is happening?**

- It must be stopped
- It is speeding up, because it is moving up
- It moves up at a steady speed: balanced forces, no change in motion

* Answer: It moves up at a steady speed: balanced forces, no change in motion

<!-- Speaker note: First law: balanced forces keep the motion the same, here steady upward motion. -->

## More thrust

**The pilot now increases thrust a little and leaves everything else the same. What happens?**

- The plane climbs straight up
- The plane speeds up: a net force forward
- Nothing changes

* Answer: The plane speeds up: a net force forward

<!-- Speaker note: Thrust bigger than drag gives a net force forward, so the plane accelerates (until the extra speed raises the drag to match). -->

## Elaborate: the skydiver's fall

**As a skydiver falls faster, air resistance grows. What happens to her free body diagram over time?**

- The arrows stay the same throughout
- The upward arrow grows until it equals her weight; then she falls at a steady speed (terminal velocity)
- Her weight arrow shrinks

* Answer: The upward arrow grows until it equals her weight; then she falls at a steady speed (terminal velocity)

<!-- Speaker note: Net force downward shrinks to zero, so the acceleration does too. Opening the parachute makes air resistance jump, and she slows to a new, lower steady speed. What this step is for: A changing force (air resistance growing with speed) shows why free body diagrams change during a motion. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **free body diagram** shows one object and every force acting on it, as labelled arrows from the object.
- Work out the net force in each direction separately (up–down, left–right).
- Net force zero in every direction: no change in motion (still, or steady speed in a straight line).
- A net force: the object accelerates in that direction, with a = F ÷ m.

## Exit ticket

**A 1000 kg car has 3000 N of driving force and 1000 N of drag. Find the net force and the acceleration.**

* 2000 N forward; a = 2000 ÷ 1000 = 2 m/s².
