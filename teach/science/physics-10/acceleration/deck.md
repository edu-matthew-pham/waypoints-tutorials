# What is acceleration, exactly?

Year 10 Physics · Newton's laws

<!-- Speaker note: Students define acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, calculate it from data and graphs in m/s², and treat it as a vector in the direction of the net force (AC9S10U05, node 3, a hinge node). ## Today

**We are learning to** acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, a = Δv ÷ Δt, in m/s². It has a direction, the same as the net force. Slowing down is acceleration too (opposite to the motion), and on a speed–time graph acceleration is the gradient.

- I can define acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, in m/s².
- I can calculate acceleration from velocity and time data, and read it from a speed–time graph.
- I can explain that acceleration has a direction, the same as the net force; slowing down is acceleration too.

## Why it matters

> A Formula 1 car reaches 100 km/h in under 3 seconds. A train takes about 40. Both end up fast; one accelerates far more. Fast and accelerating are not the same thing.

<!-- Speaker note: In everyday talk "accelerate" means speed up. In physics it means something more exact. -->

## From the data

**The trolley went from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s. What was its acceleration, in m/s²?**

* Answer: 3

<!-- Speaker note: a = change in velocity ÷ time = (12 − 0) ÷ 4 = 3 m/s². Its speed went up by 3 m/s every second. -->

**Is it accelerating?**

Groups: Accelerating · Not accelerating

## Which way

**A trolley is moving to the right, and the net force on it points to the left. Which way does its acceleration point?**

- Nowhere: it has no acceleration while it still moves right
- Right, the way it is moving
- Left, the same way as the net force

* Answer: Left, the same way as the net force

<!-- Speaker note: Acceleration always points the same way as the net force. Here that is backwards, so the trolley slows down. -->

**It went from 8 m/s to 0 in 4 s. What was its acceleration, in m/s²? (Use a minus sign for slowing down.)**

* Answer: -2

**Which way did the net force point while it slowed down?**

## Elaborate: turning

**A car drives around a roundabout at a steady 30 km/h. Is it accelerating?**

- Only if it speeds up
- No: its speed is constant
- Yes: its direction is changing, so its velocity is changing

* Answer: Yes: its direction is changing, so its velocity is changing

<!-- Speaker note: There must be a net force towards the centre of the roundabout: the friction of the tyres on the road. What this step is for: A constant-speed turn is acceleration with no change of speed, the hardest case for 'acceleration = speeding up'. -->

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity: **a = Δv ÷ Δt**, in **m/s²**.
- It has a **direction**, the same as the net force. Slowing down is acceleration opposite to the motion (negative).
- A fast object at a steady speed has **zero** acceleration.
- On a speed–time graph, acceleration is the **gradient**.

## Exit ticket

**A sprinter goes from 0 to 9 m/s in 3 s. What is her acceleration?**

* a = (9 − 0) ÷ 3 = 3 m/s², forwards.
