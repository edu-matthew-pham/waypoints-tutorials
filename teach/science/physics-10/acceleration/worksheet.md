# What is acceleration, exactly?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A Formula 1 car reaches 100 km/h in under 3 seconds. A train takes about 40. Both end up fast; one accelerates far more. Fast and accelerating are not the same thing.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students define acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, calculate it from data and graphs in m/s², and treat it as a vector in the direction of the net force (AC9S10U05, node 3, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can define acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, in m/s². I can calculate acceleration from velocity and time data, and read it from a speed–time graph. I can explain that acceleration has a direction, the same as the net force; slowing down is acceleration too.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: a = Δv ÷ Δt


:::keep-together

### From the data

**The trolley went from 0 to 12 m/s in 4 s. What was its acceleration, in m/s²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

a = change in velocity ÷ time = (12 − 0) ÷ 4 = 3 m/s². Its speed went up by 3 m/s every second.

:::


:::keep-together

### On the graph

**On the speed–time graph, where can you see the acceleration?**

☐ Where the line ends

☐ How high the line is

☐ The gradient (steepness) of the line

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The gradient (steepness) of the line

A steeper line means the speed changes faster: bigger acceleration. A flat line means zero acceleration.

:::


:::keep-together

### Units

**What are the units of acceleration?**

☐ N

☐ m/s² (metres per second, per second)

☐ m/s

☐ km/h

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** m/s² (metres per second, per second)

:::


:::keep-together

### Another one

**A cyclist goes from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Acceleration, in m/s²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2

(10 − 2) ÷ 4 = 2 m/s².

:::


:::keep-together

### Accelerating or not

**Is it accelerating?**

Groups: Accelerating, Not accelerating

- A car cruising at a steady 100 km/h on a straight road: {{field-15}}
- A bike slowing down for traffic lights: {{field-15}}
- A runner rounding a bend at a steady speed: {{field-15}}
- A trolley speeding up from rest: {{field-15}}
- A parked car: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A car cruising at a steady 100 km/h on a straight road → Not accelerating; A bike slowing down for traffic lights → Accelerating; A runner rounding a bend at a steady speed → Accelerating; A trolley speeding up from rest → Accelerating; A parked car → Not accelerating

:::


:::keep-together

### Which way

**A trolley is moving to the right, and the net force on it points to the left. Which way does its acceleration point?**

☐ Nowhere: it has no acceleration while it still moves right

☐ Right, the way it is moving

☐ Left, the same way as the net force

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Left, the same way as the net force

Acceleration always points the same way as the net force. Here that is backwards, so the trolley slows down.

:::

## Elaborate: slowing down


:::keep-together

### Negative acceleration

**It went from 8 m/s to 0 in 4 s. What was its acceleration, in m/s²? (Use a minus sign for slowing down.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -2

(0 − 8) ÷ 4 = −2 m/s². The minus sign means the acceleration points backwards, opposite to the motion, the same way as the net force (friction).

:::


:::keep-together

### Direction

**Which way did the net force point while it slowed down?**

☐ Backwards, the same way as the acceleration

☐ There was no net force

☐ Forwards, the way it was moving

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Backwards, the same way as the acceleration

Acceleration always points the same way as the net force.

:::

## Elaborate: turning


:::keep-together

**A car drives around a roundabout at a steady 30 km/h. Is it accelerating?**

☐ Only if it speeds up

☐ No: its speed is constant

☐ Yes: its direction is changing, so its velocity is changing

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Yes: its direction is changing, so its velocity is changing

There must be a net force towards the centre of the roundabout: the friction of the tyres on the road.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity: **a = Δv ÷ Δt**, in **m/s²**.
- It has a **direction**, the same as the net force. Slowing down is acceleration opposite to the motion (negative).
- A fast object at a steady speed has **zero** acceleration.
- On a speed–time graph, acceleration is the **gradient**.
