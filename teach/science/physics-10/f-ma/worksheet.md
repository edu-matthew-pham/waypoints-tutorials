# How hard do you need to push?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Push an empty shopping trolley and it shoots off. Push a full one just as hard and it barely gets going. Same push, different result: by exactly how much?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students apply F = ma to find force, mass or acceleration with units, use the net force, and explain how changing force or mass changes acceleration (AC9S10U05, node 4, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can use F = ma to find force, mass or acceleration, with correct units. I can explain that F in F = ma is the net force. I can explain what doubling the force or the mass does to the acceleration.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**A push gives an empty trolley an acceleration of 4 m/s². You load it until its mass is doubled and push exactly as hard. Predict its acceleration.**

☐ 4 m/s²: the push is the same

☐ 8 m/s²

☐ A little less, maybe 3.5 m/s²

☐ 2 m/s²: half as much

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 2 m/s². Doubling the mass halves the acceleration for the same net force: a = F ÷ m.

:::

## Explain: F = ma


:::keep-together

### The rule

**For 4 kg you needed 8 N; for 8 kg, 16 N. What rule links force, mass and acceleration?**

☐ Force = mass + acceleration

☐ Force = mass ÷ acceleration

☐ Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma)

8 = 4 × 2 and 16 = 8 × 2. F is the net force in N, m the mass in kg, a the acceleration in m/s².

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the force

**What net force gives a 1200 kg car an acceleration of 3 m/s², in N?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3600

F = ma = 1200 × 3 = 3600 N.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the acceleration

**A net force of 50 N acts on a 10 kg box. Its acceleration, in m/s²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

a = F ÷ m = 50 ÷ 10 = 5 m/s².

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mass

**A net force of 600 N gives a sprinter an acceleration of 10 m/s². Her mass, in kg?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

m = F ÷ a = 600 ÷ 10 = 60 kg.

:::


:::keep-together

### Net force

**A 2 kg box is pushed with 10 N against 4 N of friction. What force goes into F = ma?**

☐ The friction, 4 N

☐ The net force, 6 N

☐ The push, 10 N

☐ 14 N

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The net force, 6 N

Net force 10 − 4 = 6 N, so a = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 m/s².

:::


:::keep-together

### Units

**Match each quantity to its unit.**

A. newtons (N)

B. metres per second (m/s)

C. kilograms (kg)

D. metres per second squared (m/s²)

|---|---|

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Force (F) → A. newtons (N); Mass (m) → C. kilograms (kg); Acceleration (a) → D. metres per second squared (m/s²); Velocity (v) → B. metres per second (m/s)

**What happens to the acceleration?**

Groups: Doubles, Halves, Stays the same

### Triple the force

**A trolley accelerates at 6 m/s². The net force is tripled and the mass stays the same. New acceleration, in m/s²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 18

Three times the force, three times the acceleration: 18 m/s².

## Elaborate: stopping a car


:::keep-together

### Deceleration

**A 1500 kg car brakes from 20 m/s to rest in 4 s. What is the size of its deceleration, in m/s²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

Δv ÷ Δt = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 m/s² (backwards).

:::


:::keep-together

### Braking force

**What braking force does that need, in N?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7500

F = ma = 1500 × 5 = 7500 N, backwards.

:::


:::keep-together

### A loaded car

**The same car is loaded with people and luggage (more mass). To stop in the same time, the braking force must be…**

☐ the same

☐ smaller

☐ bigger

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** bigger

F = ma: the same deceleration with more mass needs more force. With the same braking force, the loaded car would decelerate less and take longer to stop.

## Elaborate: is it really a straight line?


:::keep-together

**You measure a trolley's acceleration for several pulling forces, keeping the mass the same. What graph of acceleration against force does F = ma predict?**

☐ A curve that levels off

☐ A straight line through the origin (gradient 1 ÷ mass)

☐ A horizontal line

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A straight line through the origin (gradient 1 ÷ mass)

a = F ÷ m, so a is proportional to F: a straight line through the origin with gradient 1/m. Real data that misses the origin usually points to friction.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Newton's second law:** **F = ma**. F is the **net** force (N), m the mass (kg), a the acceleration (m/s²).
- Rearranged: a = F ÷ m and m = F ÷ a.
- Double the net force, double the acceleration. Double the mass, half the acceleration.
- The acceleration points the same way as the net force.
