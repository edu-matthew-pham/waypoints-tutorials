# How hard do you need to push?

Year 10 Physics · Newton's laws

<!-- Speaker note: Students apply F = ma to find force, mass or acceleration with units, use the net force, and explain how changing force or mass changes acceleration (AC9S10U05, node 4, a hinge node). ## Today

**We are learning to** newton's second law: the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration, F = ma. Double the net force and the acceleration doubles; double the mass and the acceleration halves.

- I can use F = ma to find force, mass or acceleration, with correct units.
- I can explain that F in F = ma is the net force.
- I can explain what doubling the force or the mass does to the acceleration.

## Why it matters

> Push an empty shopping trolley and it shoots off. Push a full one just as hard and it barely gets going. Same push, different result: by exactly how much?

<!-- Speaker note: You know a net force makes things accelerate. This lesson finds the exact rule. -->

## Engage: predict

**A push gives an empty trolley an acceleration of 4 m/s². You load it until its mass is doubled and push exactly as hard. Predict its acceleration.**

- 4 m/s²: the push is the same
- 8 m/s²
- A little less, maybe 3.5 m/s²
- 2 m/s²: half as much

## The rule

**For 4 kg you needed 8 N; for 8 kg, 16 N. What rule links force, mass and acceleration?**

- Force = mass + acceleration
- Force = mass ÷ acceleration
- Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma)

* Answer: Force = mass × acceleration (F = ma)

<!-- Speaker note: 8 = 4 × 2 and 16 = 8 × 2. F is the net force in N, m the mass in kg, a the acceleration in m/s². -->

**What net force gives a 1200 kg car an acceleration of 3 m/s², in N?**

**A net force of 50 N acts on a 10 kg box. Its acceleration, in m/s²?**

## Find the mass

**A net force of 600 N gives a sprinter an acceleration of 10 m/s². Her mass, in kg?**

* Answer: 60

## Net force

**A 2 kg box is pushed with 10 N against 4 N of friction. What force goes into F = ma?**

- The friction, 4 N
- The net force, 6 N
- The push, 10 N
- 14 N

* Answer: The net force, 6 N

<!-- Speaker note: Net force 10 − 4 = 6 N, so a = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 m/s². -->

## Units

**Match each quantity to its unit.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Force (F)` | A. newtons (N) |
| `Mass (m)` | B. metres per second (m/s) |
| `Acceleration (a)` | C. kilograms (kg) |
| `Velocity (v)` | D. metres per second squared (m/s²) |

* Answer: Force (F) → A. newtons (N); Mass (m) → C. kilograms (kg); Acceleration (a) → D. metres per second squared (m/s²); Velocity (v) → B. metres per second (m/s)

<!-- Speaker note: F = ma only works with these units: mass in kg (not g), acceleration in m/s² (not m/s). -->

## Double, half or same

**What happens to the acceleration?**

Groups: Doubles · Halves · Stays the same

The net force doubles, same mass · The mass doubles, same net force · Both force and mass double · The net force halves, same mass

* Answer: The net force doubles, same mass → Doubles; The mass doubles, same net force → Halves; Both force and mass double → Stays the same; The net force halves, same mass → Halves

## A loaded car

**The same car is loaded with people and luggage (more mass). To stop in the same time, the braking force must be…**

- the same
- smaller
- bigger

* Answer: bigger

<!-- Speaker note: F = ma: the same deceleration with more mass needs more force. With the same braking force, the loaded car would decelerate less and take longer to stop. -->

## Elaborate: is it really a straight line?

**You measure a trolley's acceleration for several pulling forces, keeping the mass the same. What graph of acceleration against force does F = ma predict?**

- A curve that levels off
- A straight line through the origin (gradient 1 ÷ mass)
- A horizontal line

* Answer: A straight line through the origin (gradient 1 ÷ mass)

<!-- Speaker note: a = F ÷ m, so a is proportional to F: a straight line through the origin with gradient 1/m. Real data that misses the origin usually points to friction. ## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Newton's second law:** **F = ma**. F is the **net** force (N), m the mass (kg), a the acceleration (m/s²).
- Rearranged: a = F ÷ m and m = F ÷ a.
- Double the net force, double the acceleration. Double the mass, half the acceleration.
- The acceleration points the same way as the net force.

## Exit ticket

**What acceleration does a net force of 30 N give a 6 kg trolley?**

* a = F ÷ m = 30 ÷ 6 = 5 m/s², in the direction of the net force.
