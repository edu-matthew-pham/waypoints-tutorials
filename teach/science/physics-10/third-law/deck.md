# Push the wall, and it pushes you?

Year 10 Physics · Newton's laws

## Today

**We are learning to** forces come in pairs. When object A pushes or pulls object B, B pushes or pulls A back with a force of the same size in the opposite direction. The two forces act on different objects, so they never cancel each other out.

- I can state Newton's third law.
- I can name both forces in an action–reaction pair.
- I can tell a third-law pair from two balanced forces on one object.

## The law

**What does Newton's third law say?**

- Bigger objects push harder
- When A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A
- Every force is balanced by another force on the same object

* Answer: When A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A

<!-- Speaker note: Forces come in pairs: same size, same type, opposite directions, on different objects. -->

## Partners

**Match each force to its partner in the pair.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Your foot pushes back on the ground` | A. You pull the Earth up |
| `A rocket pushes gas down` | B. The water pushes the swimmer forward |
| `The Earth pulls you down` | C. The gas pushes the rocket up |
| `A swimmer pushes water back` | D. The ground pushes forward on your foot |

* Answer: Your foot pushes back on the ground → D. The ground pushes forward on your foot; A rocket pushes gas down → C. The gas pushes the rocket up; The Earth pulls you down → A. You pull the Earth up; A swimmer pushes water back → B. The water pushes the swimmer forward

## The book on the table

**A pile of books rests on a table. Its weight is 49 N down; the table pushes it up with 49 N. Are these a third-law pair?**

- No: both act on the book. The partner of the book's weight is the book pulling the Earth up
- Yes: they are equal and opposite
- Yes: they cancel out

* Answer: No: both act on the book. The partner of the book's weight is the book pulling the Earth up

<!-- Speaker note: Balanced forces act on one object. A third-law pair always acts on two objects: Earth pulls book, book pulls Earth; table pushes book, book pushes table. -->

## Truck and car

**A truck and a small car collide. Which feels the bigger force?**

- Neither: the forces on each are the same size
- The truck, because it is heavier
- The car, because it gets damaged more

* Answer: Neither: the forces on each are the same size

<!-- Speaker note: Third law: the forces are equal. The car, with less mass, accelerates more (F = ma), which is why it fares worse. -->

## Horse and cart

**A horse pulls a cart forward with 500 N. By the third law, the cart pulls back on the horse with 500 N. So how can the cart start moving?**

- It can't: equal and opposite forces cancel, so nothing moves
- The horse pulls a little harder than the cart pulls back
- The two forces act on different objects, so they don't cancel: the cart speeds up if the horse's pull is bigger than the friction on the cart

* Answer: The two forces act on different objects, so they don't cancel: the cart speeds up if the horse's pull is bigger than the friction on the cart

## Elaborate: walking

**Why is it hard to walk on very slippery ice?**

- Ice is too cold for your muscles
- You can't push back on the ice hard enough, so it can't push you forward hard enough
- You push on the ice hard, but it pushes back on you less hard

* Answer: You can't push back on the ice hard enough, so it can't push you forward hard enough

<!-- Speaker note: To walk forward you push back on the ground and it pushes you forward. With little friction, your backward push slips, and so does the forward push you get. What this step is for: Walking and rockets show the third law is how anything starts moving: by pushing on something else. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Newton's third law:** when object A pushes or pulls object B, B pushes or pulls A with a force of the same size in the opposite direction.
- The two forces in a pair act on **different objects**, so they never cancel each other.
- Balanced forces act on the **same** object (weight and support on a book); they are not a third-law pair.
- You move by pushing on something else: it pushes you (walking, swimming, rockets).
