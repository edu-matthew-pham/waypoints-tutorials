# How does light bend?

Year 9 Physics · Wave and particle models

## Today

**We are learning to** the wave model explains two things you can see. In reflection, wave fronts bounce off a surface and the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence. In refraction, light slows down in a denser material such as glass or water, so the wave fronts bunch up and the direction bends towards the normal; light going straight in at 90° does not bend.

- I can say why light bends when it goes into glass.
- I can say what happens to a ray that goes straight in.
- I can use the law of reflection.

## Why it bends

**Why does light bend as it goes from air into glass at an angle?**

- It slows down, so the wave fronts change direction
- It speeds up, so the wave fronts change direction
- The glass pushes it sideways

* Answer: It slows down, so the wave fronts change direction

<!-- Speaker note: One end of each wave front enters the glass first and slows down while the other end is still travelling faster in the air. The front swings round, like a line of marchers when one end slows. -->

## Straight in

**A ray goes straight into glass along the normal (angle of incidence 0°). What happens?**

- It bends away from the normal
- It slows down but doesn't change direction
- It bends towards the normal

* Answer: It slows down but doesn't change direction

<!-- Speaker note: The whole wave front reaches the glass at the same moment and slows together, so it doesn't turn. -->

## Mirror

**A ray hits a mirror at 35° to the normal. At what angle does it reflect?**

- 35° on the other side of the normal
- 55°
- It depends on the mirror

* Answer: 35° on the other side of the normal

## The straw

**Why does a straw in water look bent at the surface?**

- Light from the part under water bends as it leaves the water, so it seems to come from a different place
- Water makes light go faster
- The water bends the straw

* Answer: Light from the part under water bends as it leaves the water, so it seems to come from a different place

<!-- Speaker note: Light leaving the water speeds up and bends away from the normal. Your brain assumes it travelled in a straight line, so the underwater part seems shifted. -->

## Why more

**Diamond bends light more than glass. What does that tell you about the speed of light in diamond?**

- It is the same: only the angle changes
- Light is slower in diamond than in glass
- Light is faster in diamond

* Answer: Light is slower in diamond than in glass

<!-- Speaker note: The more the light slows down, the more the wave fronts bunch up and the more the direction changes. -->

## Elaborate: fibre optics

**If light inside glass hits the surface at a very shallow angle, it can't get out: it reflects back in. This is why light can run along a glass fibre for kilometres. What makes fibre optic cables useful?**

- They carry signals as light, which has a high frequency and so can carry a lot of information
- They are cheaper than copper wire
- Light moves faster in glass than in air

* Answer: They carry signals as light, which has a high frequency and so can carry a lot of information

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Reflection**: the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, both measured from the normal.
- **Refraction**: light slows down in a denser material, so the wave fronts bunch up and the direction bends **towards the normal**. Going out, it bends away.
- Light going straight in (along the normal) slows down but doesn't bend.
- The more the light slows, the more it bends.

## Exit ticket

**A ray of light goes from air into water and bends towards the normal. What does that tell you about the speed of light in water?**

* It is slower in water than in air. The change of speed turns the wave fronts and so the ray.
