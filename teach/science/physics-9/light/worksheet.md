# What is the electromagnetic spectrum?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A sunburn, a microwave oven, a TV remote and a hospital X-ray all use the same kind of wave. What makes them so different?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe light and the electromagnetic spectrum as transverse waves differing in frequency and wavelength, and link frequency to energy and use (AC9S9U04, node 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what makes light and X-rays the same kind of wave. I can say how wavelength, frequency and energy change from radio to gamma. I can match a wave to a use and a risk.

## Explain: the wave model of light


:::keep-together

### Transverse

**Light is a transverse wave. What does that mean?**

☐ The wave needs particles to travel through

☐ The vibration is along the direction of travel, like sound

☐ The vibration is at right angles to the direction the wave travels

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The vibration is at right angles to the direction the wave travels

Transverse: like a rope shaken sideways, the vibration is across the direction of travel. Electromagnetic waves are vibrating electric and magnetic fields, so they need no particles.

:::


:::keep-together

### Same speed

**Radio waves and gamma rays travel through a vacuum. How do their speeds compare?**

☐ Gamma rays are much faster

☐ Radio waves are faster because they are longer

☐ They are the same: 300 000 km/s

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They are the same: 300 000 km/s

All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum, the speed of light. They differ in frequency and wavelength, not speed.

:::


:::keep-together

### Wavelength and energy

**Going from radio to gamma, what happens to the wavelength and the energy?**

☐ The wavelength gets shorter and the energy smaller

☐ The wavelength gets longer and the energy greater

☐ The wavelength gets shorter and the energy greater

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The wavelength gets shorter and the energy greater

Speed is fixed, so a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength, and a higher frequency means more energy in each wave.

:::


:::keep-together

### Danger

**Why are UV, X-rays and gamma rays more dangerous to living cells than radio waves?**

☐ Each wave carries a lot more energy, enough to damage cells

☐ They are brighter

☐ They travel faster

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Each wave carries a lot more energy, enough to damage cells

High-frequency waves carry much more energy in each wave, enough to damage the molecules in cells. Low-frequency waves such as radio don't.

## Elaborate: choosing a wave


:::keep-together

### Microwave or X-ray

**Why does a mobile phone use microwaves and not X-rays?**

☐ X-rays travel too slowly

☐ Microwaves are visible

☐ Microwaves carry information well and are safe at low power, but X-rays would damage cells

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Microwaves carry information well and are safe at low power, but X-rays would damage cells

A communication technology needs waves that carry signals and are safe to use all around us. High-energy X-rays would be dangerous.

## Elaborate: sun safety


:::keep-together

**Why does Australian sun safety advice focus on UV, even on cool, cloudy days?**

☐ UV has enough energy per wave to damage skin cells, and you can't feel it as heat

☐ UV is the hottest part of sunlight

☐ Clouds make UV stronger

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** UV has enough energy per wave to damage skin cells, and you can't feel it as heat

UV is invisible and not felt as heat, but each wave carries enough energy to damage skin cells. That's why shade, clothing, a hat and sunscreen matter on any bright day.

### Evidence is not enough

**Scientists knew UV causes skin cancer well before 1981, but sun-safe habits spread only after the campaigns began. What does that show?**

☐ Campaigns only work when the science is brand new

☐ The science changed in 1981

☐ Sunscreen wasn't on sale in Australia before 1981

☐ Evidence alone isn't enough: science is taken up when the message is clear, the actions are easy and people keep hearing it from sources they trust

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Evidence alone isn't enough: science is taken up when the message is clear, the actions are easy and people keep hearing it from sources they trust

Adoption depends on more than evidence: cost, convenience, trust, repetition and rules all matter. Australia still has one of the highest skin cancer rates in the world, so the campaigns continue.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Light is a **transverse wave** and one part of the **electromagnetic spectrum**: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
- All travel at the **same speed** in a vacuum (300 000 km/s), with no medium needed.
- Radio to gamma: **shorter wavelength, higher frequency, more energy per wave**.
- Frequency decides use and risk: low-energy waves carry signals; high-energy waves can damage cells.
