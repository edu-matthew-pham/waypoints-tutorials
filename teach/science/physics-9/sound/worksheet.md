# How does sound travel?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** In space, an explosion is completely silent. Yet on Earth you can hear a thunderclap from kilometres away. What is sound actually made of?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe sound as a longitudinal wave that needs a medium, use compressions and rarefactions to explain how energy is transferred, and link frequency to pitch and amplitude to loudness (AC9S9U04, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe a sound wave with compressions and rarefactions. I can say what changes when a sound gets higher or louder. I can explain why sound cannot travel through a vacuum.

## Explain: the wave model of sound


:::keep-together

### Compression

**What is a compression in a sound wave?**

☐ The sound getting quieter

☐ A place where the particles are spread further apart

☐ A place where the particles are squashed closer together

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A place where the particles are squashed closer together

A compression is a squashed patch; a rarefaction is a stretched, spread-out patch. A sound wave is a line of them, one after the other.

:::


:::keep-together

### Longitudinal

**In sound, the particles vibrate backwards and forwards along the direction the wave travels. What do we call this kind of wave?**

☐ Transverse

☐ Stationary

☐ Longitudinal

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Longitudinal

Longitudinal: the vibration is along the direction of travel. That is why sound is squashes and stretches.

:::


:::keep-together

### Pitch

**You raise the pitch. What has increased?**

☐ The frequency: more vibrations each second, so shorter waves

☐ The speed of every particle

☐ The amplitude: bigger vibrations

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The frequency: more vibrations each second, so shorter waves

Higher pitch means higher frequency (more compressions pass each second). Loudness depends on amplitude, the size of the vibration.

:::


:::keep-together

:::keep-together

### Vacuum

**Why can't sound travel through a vacuum?**

☐ Sound is too slow in a vacuum

☐ A vacuum absorbs the sound

☐ There are no particles to vibrate and pass the energy on

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** There are no particles to vibrate and pass the energy on

Sound is energy passed from particle to particle. With no particles, there is nothing to pass it along.

:::

## Elaborate: different media


:::keep-together

### Why faster

**Sound travels at about 340 m/s in air, 1500 m/s in water and 5000 m/s in steel. Why is it fastest in steel?**

☐ Steel is warmer

☐ The particles are closer together and tightly bonded, so each passes the vibration on sooner

☐ Steel particles are heavier

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The particles are closer together and tightly bonded, so each passes the vibration on sooner

In a solid the particles are packed close together and joined to their neighbours, so a vibration is passed on very quickly. In a gas they are far apart, so it takes longer.

## Elaborate: an instrument


:::keep-together

**A yidaki (didjeridu) is a hollow wooden tube. The player's lips vibrate the air inside it. A longer tube makes a lower note. Using the wave model, which is the best explanation?**

☐ A longer tube makes the sound louder, so it sounds lower

☐ Sound travels slower in longer tubes

☐ The longer column of air vibrates more slowly, so the frequency is lower and the pitch is lower

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The longer column of air vibrates more slowly, so the frequency is lower and the pitch is lower

A longer air column vibrates at a lower frequency, so the pitch is lower. The material and shape of the tube also affect how the sound carries.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Sound is a **longitudinal wave**: particles vibrate to and fro along the direction the wave travels.
- A wave is a line of **compressions** (squashed) and **rarefactions** (spread out). The pattern travels; the particles stay in place.
- **Frequency** (vibrations per second) sets the **pitch**; **amplitude** (size of the vibration) sets the **loudness**.
- Sound needs a **medium**: it cannot travel through a vacuum. It is faster in solids than in gases because the particles are closer together and more tightly bonded.
