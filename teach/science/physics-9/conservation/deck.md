# Where does the energy go?

Year 9 Physics · Conservation of energy and efficiency

## Today

**We are learning to** energy is never made or destroyed: it only moves and changes type. This is the law of conservation of energy. When energy seems to disappear (a ball bounces lower, a car slows down), it has gone into heat and sound in the surroundings. The total is always the same.

- I can state the law of conservation of energy.
- I can account for all the energy in a system, including heat and sound.
- I can explain where energy goes when it seems to be lost.

> Drop a ball and it bounces back up, but never quite as high. Each bounce is lower until it stops. Has its energy been used up?

## The law

**What does the law of conservation of energy say?**

- Energy is used up when things move
- You should save energy by switching things off
- Energy can't be made or destroyed, only transferred or transformed; the total stays the same

* Answer: Energy can't be made or destroyed, only transferred or transformed; the total stays the same

<!-- Speaker note: In any system, total energy in = total energy out. -->

## Count every kJ

**A coaster car has 150 kJ in total. At one point it has 90 kJ of potential energy and 40 kJ of kinetic energy. How many kJ have become heat and sound?**

* Answer: 20

<!-- Speaker note: 150 − 90 − 40 = 20 kJ. Every kJ is accounted for. -->

## Braking

**People say a car 'loses' energy when it brakes. What really happens?**

- The energy is destroyed by the brakes
- Its kinetic energy becomes heat in the brakes, which spreads into the air
- The energy goes back into the fuel

* Answer: Its kinetic energy becomes heat in the brakes, which spreads into the air

<!-- Speaker note: 'Lost' means 'transferred somewhere we can't use it'. Feel a bike's brakes after a long downhill: they're hot. -->

## The pendulum

**Why can a pendulum never swing higher than where it started?**

- It would need more energy than it had: energy can't be made
- Pendulums only swing lower
- Gravity gets stronger

* Answer: It would need more energy than it had: energy can't be made

<!-- Speaker note: With no extra energy put in, the most it can do is reach its starting height, and friction makes it a little lower each swing. -->

## Where it went

**The car ended up stopped in a dip. Where is its starting energy now?**

- It is kinetic energy, waiting to be used
- It has all gone
- Most is heat and sound in the surroundings; the rest is potential energy where it rests

* Answer: Most is heat and sound in the surroundings; the rest is potential energy where it rests

## Elaborate: perpetual motion

**An inventor claims a wheel that keeps spinning forever and also powers a light. Why is this impossible?**

- It would spin too fast
- The light's energy and the friction heat must come from somewhere; with no energy in, the wheel must slow down
- Wheels can't power lights

* Answer: The light's energy and the friction heat must come from somewhere; with no energy in, the wheel must slow down

<!-- Speaker note: Energy out (light, heat) with nothing in would mean making energy. Every machine needs an input. What this step is for: Perpetual motion machines are a classic test of the law: any that claim to run forever while doing work break it. -->

**Revision summary**

- **Conservation of energy:** energy is never made or destroyed; it is only transferred or transformed. The total stays the same.
- Energy that seems "lost" or "used up" has been transferred to the surroundings, usually as heat and sound.
- Total energy in = total energy out, so you can account for every joule.

## Exit ticket

**A skateboarder has 2000 J at the top of a ramp and 1700 J of kinetic energy at the bottom. Where are the other 300 J?**

* Transferred as heat and sound because of friction and air resistance. The total is still 2000 J.
