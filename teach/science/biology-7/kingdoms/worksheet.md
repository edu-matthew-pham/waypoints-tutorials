# Plant, animal or something else?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A mushroom pops up on the lawn overnight. It grows from the ground and doesn't walk away, so it must be a plant. Right?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place organisms into kingdoms using observable features, justify each placement, recognise a hard-to-classify case, and see that kingdoms sort by features rather than relatedness, a model revised as evidence improved (AC9S7U01, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the five kingdoms. I can place an organism in its kingdom and give the observable feature that decides it. I can explain why some organisms are hard to classify. I can explain why kingdoms don't always show which organisms are related, and why classification changes with new evidence.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Why features

**Why do scientists use observable features to classify?**

☐ Anyone can check them, so everyone sorts the same organism the same way

☐ Because organisms choose their own group

☐ They always show which organisms are most closely related

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Anyone can check them, so everyone sorts the same organism the same way

:::


:::keep-together

### Closer relatives

**DNA evidence shows which living things share a recent common ancestor. Which is a mushroom's closer relative?**

☐ A sea star (an animal)

☐ Neither: fungi aren't related to anything

☐ A fern (a plant)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A sea star (an animal)

Fungi and animals share a more recent common ancestor than fungi and plants do. Kingdoms sort by features; they don't always show who is related.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why it changed

**Scientists once used two kingdoms (plants and animals), then five, and now group life into three domains. Why did it keep changing?**

☐ New evidence (microscopes, then DNA) showed groups that didn't fit the old system

☐ The earlier scientists were careless

☐ Living things have evolved into new kingdoms since then

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** New evidence (microscopes, then DNA) showed groups that didn't fit the old system

Microscopes revealed single-celled life that was neither plant nor animal; DNA showed that 'bacteria' are really two very different groups (bacteria and archaea), and that protists aren't one family. Classification is a model, revised when the evidence demands it.

:::

## Elaborate: the full address of a kangaroo


:::keep-together

**Put the red kangaroo's classification in order, from the biggest group to the smallest.**

A. `Family: Kangaroos and wallabies (Macropodidae)`

B. `Phylum: Chordates (animals with a backbone)`

C. `Order: Diprotodonts (Diprotodontia: kangaroos, wombats, koalas and possums)`

D. `Species: Osphranter rufus (red kangaroo)`

E. `Class: Mammals`

F. `Genus: Osphranter`

G. `Kingdom: Animals`

Correct order (letters): {{field-24}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** see the program below

Kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species: each level is a smaller group that shares more features. The two-part scientific name is genus + species: Osphranter rufus.

```science
Kingdom: Animals
Phylum: Chordates (animals with a backbone)
Class: Mammals
Order: Diprotodonts (Diprotodontia: kangaroos, wombats, koalas and possums)
Family: Kangaroos and wallabies (Macropodidae)
Genus: Osphranter
Species: Osphranter rufus (red kangaroo)
```

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Living things are grouped by **observable features** into **kingdoms**: animals, plants, fungi, protists and bacteria.
- **Plants** make their own food from sunlight; **fungi** absorb food from other material; **animals** eat other living things.
- **Protists** are mostly single cells with a nucleus; **bacteria** are single tiny cells with no nucleus.
- Some organisms (Euglena, algae) are hard to place: classification is a tool that changes with evidence.
- Kingdoms sort by **features**; they don't always show who is **related**. Fungi are closer relatives of animals than of plants, and protists aren't one family.
- Scientists now group life by evolutionary relationships, using DNA: **three domains** (bacteria, archaea and eukaryotes). The five kingdoms are still a useful way to sort what you can see.
