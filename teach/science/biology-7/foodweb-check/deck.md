## Abiotic or biotic?

**Is it abiotic (non-living) or biotic (living)?**

Groups: Abiotic · Biotic

Sunlight · Rainfall · Soil pH · Temperature · Grass · Bacteria in the soil · A hawk

* Answer: Sunlight → Abiotic; Rainfall → Abiotic; Soil pH → Abiotic; Temperature → Abiotic; Grass → Biotic; Bacteria in the soil → Biotic; A hawk → Biotic

## A dry year

**In a web, grass is eaten by grasshoppers, which are eaten by lizards. A drought cuts grass growth. What happens to the grasshoppers?**

- They increase, because lizards have less to eat
- They decrease, because they have less food
- They increase, because the grass is drier
- Nothing, because grasshoppers are not plants

* Answer: They decrease, because they have less food

<!-- Speaker note: A change in an abiotic factor such as water hits the producer first, and then the animals that depend on it. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Who else?

**Besides the grasshoppers, which population is also affected by the drought?**

- The lizards, as their food falls
- Only the grass
- The sun
- No other population

* Answer: The lizards, as their food falls

<!-- Speaker note: The effect cascades up the web: fewer grasshoppers means less food for the lizards, and then for whatever eats them. -->

## Abiotic means

**What is an abiotic factor?**

- A non-living part of the environment
- Anything that harms living things
- An animal that eats only plants
- A living thing that affects other living things

* Answer: A non-living part of the environment

<!-- Speaker note: Abiotic means non-living, so it covers physical and chemical conditions such as temperature, water, light and soil. -->

## What it shows

**Does a food web diagram show this?**

Groups: Shows it · Does not show it

Who eats whom · The direction energy flows · How many of each organism there are · What a drought does · How the web changes with the seasons

* Answer: Who eats whom → Shows it; The direction energy flows → Shows it; How many of each organism there are → Does not show it; What a drought does → Does not show it; How the web changes with the seasons → Does not show it

## A weak prediction

**A web shows a hawk eats lizards. Someone predicts that if lizards disappear the hawks will die out. Why might that be wrong?**

- It is right: if lizards disappear, the hawks must die out
- Hawks would switch to eating grass
- Food webs can't be used to make predictions
- Hawks may eat other animals the web leaves out

* Answer: Hawks may eat other animals the web leaves out

<!-- Speaker note: Hawks may also eat other animals that the diagram leaves out, so they could switch food. Real animals often eat several foods and a diagram simplifies, so a prediction is only as good as the web it comes from. -->

## Model limits

**Which is a limitation of a food web model?**

- It only works for plants
- It leaves out some organisms and links
- It shows how many organisms there are
- It is always completely accurate

* Answer: It leaves out some organisms and links

<!-- Speaker note: Because it shows only some organisms and links, predictions from it are less certain. A model simplifies, so knowing what it leaves out tells you how far to trust its predictions. -->

## Exit ticket

**Give one abiotic factor and say how changing it would affect one population.**

* For example less rainfall means less grass, so fewer grasshoppers. Any reasoned chain is fine.
