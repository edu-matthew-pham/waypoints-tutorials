# Where does the energy go?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A field of grass can feed many grasshoppers, which feed fewer lizards, which feed a couple of kookaburras, which feed perhaps one eagle. Why the shrinking numbers?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students trace energy through at least three trophic levels, explain that energy is lost at each level (about 90%), and distinguish one-way energy flow from matter cycling (AC9S7U02, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can trace the direction of energy through at least three levels of a food web. I can explain that energy is lost at each level. I can explain the difference between energy flow and matter cycling.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Grass captures 10 000 kJ of energy from sunlight. A grasshopper eats grass, a lizard eats grasshoppers, a kookaburra eats lizards. About how much of that energy reaches the kookaburra?**

☐ Almost all of it, 10 000 kJ

☐ About 10 kJ

☐ It goes round and comes back to the grass

☐ About half

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** About 10 kJ. Roughly 90% is used or lost as heat at each step, so only about 10% passes on: 10 000 → 1 000 → 100 → 10.

:::

## Explain: flow, loss and cycling


:::keep-together

### Down the levels

**A lizard gets 100 kJ from the grasshoppers it eats. About how much reaches a kookaburra that eats the lizard, in kJ?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10

About 10% passes on: 100 × 0.1 = 10 kJ.

:::


:::keep-together

### Where it goes

**Where does the other 90% go at each step?**

Groups: Not passed to the next level, Passed to the next level

- Heat given off by the animal's body: {{field-15}}
- Energy used for moving, breathing and keeping warm: {{field-15}}
- Parts not eaten (bones, fur, roots): {{field-15}}
- Energy stored in the eaten animal's body: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Heat given off by the animal's body → Not passed to the next level; Energy used for moving, breathing and keeping warm → Not passed to the next level; Parts not eaten (bones, fur, roots) → Not passed to the next level; Energy stored in the eaten animal's body → Passed to the next level

:::


:::keep-together

### Energy and matter

**Which statement is true?**

☐ Energy is recycled by decomposers; matter is lost

☐ Energy and matter are both recycled

☐ Energy flows one way through a web and is lost as heat; matter (the atoms) is recycled by decomposers

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Energy flows one way through a web and is lost as heat; matter (the atoms) is recycled by decomposers

That's why every food web needs a constant supply of sunlight, but no new atoms.

:::


## Mix it up


:::keep-together

**Looking back at food webs: in the chain grass → grasshopper → lizard → kookaburra, which is the secondary consumer?**

☐ The grasshopper

☐ The grass

☐ The kookaburra

☐ The lizard

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The lizard

A secondary consumer eats a primary consumer. The lizard eats the grasshopper, which eats the grass.

## Elaborate: why so few eagles?


:::keep-together

**Why are there far fewer wedge-tailed eagles than grasshoppers in an ecosystem?**

☐ Eagles have fewer babies

☐ So little energy reaches the top of the food chain that it can only support a few large predators

☐ Grasshoppers are easier to see

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** So little energy reaches the top of the food chain that it can only support a few large predators

Each level up gets about a tenth of the energy, so it can support far fewer organisms.

:::

## Elaborate: a desert and a rainforest


:::keep-together

**A rainforest's plants capture far more energy each year than a desert's. What would you predict about their food webs?**

☐ They would be the same size

☐ The rainforest can support more organisms and more levels of consumers

☐ The desert would have more top predators

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The rainforest can support more organisms and more levels of consumers

Energy captured by producers sets the size of everything above them.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Energy** enters as sunlight captured by producers and **flows one way** along the arrows.
- At each step only about **10%** is passed on; about **90%** is used for living or lost as **heat**.
- That's why there are **few top predators**.
- **Matter cycles**: decomposers return atoms to the soil and air, and producers use them again. Energy does not cycle.
