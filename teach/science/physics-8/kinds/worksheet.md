# Moving or stored?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A roller coaster has no engine after the first climb. Yet it races down, swoops up the next hill and keeps going for a minute or more. Where does its energy come from?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students classify energy as kinetic (activity: movement, heat, sound, light, electrical) or potential (stored: chemical, gravitational, elastic), with examples, and see the two change into each other (AC9S8U05, nodes 1 and 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can sort energy into kinetic and potential. I can give examples of chemical, gravitational and elastic potential energy. I can say what decides how much energy is stored.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**A heavy book sits still on a high shelf. Does it have any energy?**

☐ Yes: stored energy, because it is high up and could fall

☐ No: energy is only in living things

☐ Only if someone pushes it

☐ No: it isn't moving

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Yes. It has gravitational potential energy, stored because it is high up. Knock it off and that stored energy becomes kinetic energy as it falls.

:::

### Fastest point

**On the coaster, where is the kinetic energy greatest?**

☐ At the bottom of the deepest dip, where the car is fastest

☐ It stays the same everywhere

☐ At the top of the first hill

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** At the bottom of the deepest dip, where the car is fastest

Lowest point, least potential energy, so the most kinetic energy: the car is fastest there.

:::


:::keep-together

### How much is stored

**What decides how much gravitational potential energy something has?**

☐ How fast it is moving

☐ How heavy it is and how high it is

☐ What colour it is

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** How heavy it is and how high it is

Heavier and higher means more stored. Likewise, stretching a spring further stores more elastic energy, and different foods store different amounts of chemical energy.

:::


:::keep-together

### Active or stored

**What do all kinds of kinetic energy have in common, and how is potential energy different?**

☐ Kinetic energy is active now (something moving, vibrating or flowing); potential energy is stored, ready to be released

☐ Kinetic energy is useful; potential energy is wasted

☐ Potential energy is energy that might exist one day but doesn't yet

☐ Kinetic energy is only in moving objects; heat, light and sound aren't kinetic

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Kinetic energy is active now (something moving, vibrating or flowing); potential energy is stored, ready to be released

Ask 'is it active now, or stored?' Active (moving, vibrating, flowing) is kinetic; stored and ready to be released is potential.

## Elaborate: trampolines and bows


:::keep-together

**On a trampoline you land, sink down, and spring back up. Which energy is stored at the lowest point?**

☐ Elastic potential energy, in the stretched trampoline

☐ Kinetic energy, because you're moving fast

☐ No energy: you've stopped for an instant

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Elastic potential energy, in the stretched trampoline

Kinetic → elastic potential as the mat stretches, then back to kinetic and up to gravitational potential as you rise.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Kinetic energy** is energy of activity: movement, heat (thermal), sound, light, electrical.
- **Potential energy** is stored: chemical (food, fuel, batteries), gravitational (height), elastic (stretched or squashed).
- How much is stored depends on height and mass, how far something is stretched, or what a substance is made of.
- Energy changes between kinetic and potential, as on a roller coaster. The total stays the same.
