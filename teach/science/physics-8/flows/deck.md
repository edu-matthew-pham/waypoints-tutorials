## Today

**We are learning to** real systems pass energy through many steps. A flow diagram follows it from start to end: a box for each stage, an arrow for each transfer or transformation, and side arrows for every by-product. Tracing all the outputs, useful and not, is how you find out where energy goes.

- I can trace energy through a system with several steps.
- I can draw a flow diagram with every by-product.
- I can spot what is missing from someone else's flow diagram.

## Engage: predict

**A real coaster car starts at the top of a 30 m hill with no engine. Could the next hill also be 30 m tall?**

- No: energy is used up by going downhill
- Yes, if it goes fast enough
- Yes: it has the same energy
- No: some energy becomes heat and sound along the track, so it can't climb as high

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: No. Friction and air resistance turn some of the car's energy into heat and sound on the way, so it has less to turn back into height. The energy isn't used up: it's in the surroundings. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Elaborate: the coaster that stops

**If a coaster had no brakes and no engine and just kept rolling back and forth, where would all its energy end up?**

- As heat (and sound) in the track, the wheels and the air: the car stops in a dip
- Back as potential energy at the top
- Destroyed by friction

* Answer: As heat (and sound) in the track, the wheels and the air: the car stops in a dip

<!-- Speaker note: Each pass turns more into heat and sound until the car rests in the lowest dip. The energy is all still there, spread out. What this step is for: Following a system to the end shows where all the energy finally goes: heat in the surroundings. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Real systems pass energy through many stages: trace it one step at a time.
- A **flow diagram** has a box for each stage, an arrow for each transfer or transformation, and side arrows for **by-products**.
- Count every output, useful and by-product. By-products (mostly heat and sound) go to the surroundings; they are never destroyed.
