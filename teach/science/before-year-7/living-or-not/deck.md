# Living or not?

Before Year 7 science · Living things

<!-- Speaker note: Students decide whether things are living, once living or non-living by checking a short list of what living things do, and drop the idea that moving means alive. About 18 minutes (Think first 3 min · The idea 6 min · Check 5 min · Apply 4 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a living thing does.
- I can sort things into living, once living and non-living.
- I can say why a flame or a car isn't alive.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: living things need energy, grow, have young and react to their surroundings, and do all of these together. The common mistake is that moving means alive (and, the other way round, that a still seed or a bare tree is not). The car and the seed questions meet each half. Plain wording is on purpose for students who are not confident readers. The Year 7 lessons *Why sort living things?* and *Plant, animal or something else?* use a longer list (cells, breathing, getting rid of waste); here we stay with four things a Year 3 student can check. If you need more, add "breathes" or "gets rid of waste" yourself. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask for a show of hands for each option before anyone reveals the answer. Teacher: The idea: take the three questions as a class. For the seed, ask what it would do if you gave it water. Teacher: Check: students sort alone, then compare the ones that made them stop (the flame, the T-shirt). Teacher: Apply: take two or three answers and ask the class to find the list items each one used. -->

## Why it matters

> A flame moves, grows and uses up fuel. So why isn't it alive?

<!-- Speaker note: Is a flame alive? Is a seed sitting on a shelf? You'll make up your mind with a short list of things that living things do. -->

## Think first

**A candle flame moves, grows bigger when it gets more air, and uses up the wax. Is it alive?**

- No, because it doesn't do everything that living things do
- Yes, because it moves and grows
- Yes, because it needs air
- You can't tell

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: No. A flame moves, grows and needs air, but it doesn't have young or react to its surroundings the way a living thing does. To be alive, a thing needs to do all of them together. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What happened?

* No. A flame moves, grows and needs air, but it doesn't have young or react to its surroundings the way a living thing does. To be alive, a thing needs to do all of them together.

<!-- Speaker note: Reveal after running. Ask who predicted it, and what made them think so. -->

## A car

**A car moves along, uses fuel and makes noise. Is it alive?**

- Yes: it uses fuel like food
- Yes: it moves by itself
- Only when the engine is running
- No: it doesn't grow or have young

* Answer: No: it doesn't grow or have young

<!-- Speaker note: A living thing needs food or energy, grows, has young and reacts to what is around it. A car does only one of those. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## A seed

**A dry seed sits still on a shelf. It doesn't seem to be doing anything. Is it alive?**

- Yes, but only after it has grown
- No: it isn't moving
- No: it doesn't eat
- Yes: it can grow when it gets water and warmth

* Answer: Yes: it can grow when it gets water and warmth

<!-- Speaker note: A seed is alive but waiting. It is a living thing that can grow when it gets water and warmth. -->

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## A wooden spoon

**A wooden spoon was made from a tree. Which group is it in?**

- Living: it's made of tree
- Once living: it came from a living thing but is not alive now
- Non-living: it never had anything to do with living things

* Answer: Once living: it came from a living thing but is not alive now

<!-- Speaker note: Things like wood, paper and dry leaves are called once living. They come from living things but are not alive now. -->

## Check

**Which group is it in?**

Groups: Living · Once living · Non-living

A kangaroo · A flame · A fallen gum leaf, dry and brown · A tree in winter with no leaves · A rock · A cotton T-shirt

* Answer: A kangaroo → Living; A flame → Non-living; A fallen gum leaf, dry and brown → Once living; A tree in winter with no leaves → Living; A rock → Non-living; A cotton T-shirt → Once living

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Sorting into three groups applies the whole list, and the 'why' text names the trait that rules each one in or out. -->

## Apply

Write your answer, then compare it with the examples.

## Exit ticket

**A student says: 'A robot vacuum moves around by itself, so it's alive.' What would you say?**

* Moving isn't enough. A robot vacuum doesn't grow, have young or need food. A living thing does all of these together.
