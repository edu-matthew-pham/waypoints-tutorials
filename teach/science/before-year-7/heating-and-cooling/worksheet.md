# Heating, cooling and changing state

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You can melt ice and freeze the water again. Is it still the same stuff?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that heating melts and cooling freezes, that it is the same stuff each time, and that heat moves from warmer to cooler.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what makes a solid melt and a liquid freeze. I can say why melting and freezing can be undone. I can say which way heat moves.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

When you **add heat** to a solid, it can **melt** into a liquid. When you **take heat away** from a liquid, it can **freeze**. It is the **same stuff** in a different state, so you can change it back. Heat always moves from the **warmer** thing to the **cooler** thing.

:::keep-together

### Making it melt

**What do you do to melt a solid?**

☐ Put it in the shade

☐ Add heat

☐ Squash it

☐ Take heat away

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Add heat

A solid melts when it gets enough heat. Taking heat away makes a liquid freeze.

:::


:::keep-together

### Chocolate in the fridge

**A chocolate bar melts in the sun. You put it in the fridge and it goes hard again. What does this show?**

☐ It has turned into something new

☐ It was the same chocolate all along

☐ The fridge made new chocolate

☐ It is a different kind of chocolate

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It was the same chocolate all along

Melting and freezing are changes of state. The stuff stays the same, so you can undo the change.

:::


:::keep-together

### Ice in your hand

**You hold an ice cube in your warm hand and it melts. Which way does the heat move?**

☐ It doesn't move

☐ Both ways equally

☐ From the ice to your hand

☐ From your hand to the ice

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** From your hand to the ice

Heat moves from your warm hand into the cooler ice, which is why the ice melts and your hand feels cold.

:::

### Puddle

**Water in a puddle freezes on a cold night and melts in the morning sun. What is true?**

☐ It is the same water each time

☐ The morning sun makes new water

☐ Frozen water can't melt again

☐ The ice is a new substance

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is the same water each time

Freezing and melting are changes of state. It is the same water in a different state.

:::


:::keep-together

### Hot tea

**A hot cup of tea stands on a table. What happens to its heat?**

☐ It disappears

☐ It stays in the tea forever

☐ It moves from the air into the tea

☐ It moves into the cooler air and the table

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It moves into the cooler air and the table

Heat moves from the warmer tea into the cooler air and table, so the tea cools.
