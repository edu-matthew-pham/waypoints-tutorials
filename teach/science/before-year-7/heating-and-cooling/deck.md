# Heating, cooling and changing state

Before Year 7 science · Solids

<!-- Speaker note: Students see that heating melts and cooling freezes, that it is the same stuff each time, and that heat moves from warmer to cooler. About 18 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 3 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what makes a solid melt and a liquid freeze.
- I can say why melting and freezing can be undone.
- I can say which way heat moves.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: heating and cooling change the state of the same stuff, so the change can be undone. The common mistake is that melting makes something new, so it can't be changed back. The freezer question and the chocolate bar meet it. Heat moving from warm to cool is a short addition because the Year 7 heating curve asks 'where does the heat go?'. The board is the one used in the Year 7 lesson *Where does the heat go?*. Keep to the temperature slider here: the energy at the changes of state is Year 7. At exactly 0 °C and 100 °C real water can be partly one state and partly the other. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: take a show of hands, then ask for an example of something they've seen melt and set again. Teacher: Try: ask what they see when the temperature goes up, then when it comes down. Teacher: Apply: ask for examples that are not water. -->

---

## Why it matters

> You can melt ice and freeze the water again. Is it still the same stuff?

<!-- Speaker note: Ice, water and steam look very different. This lesson is about what heating and cooling do to them, and what stays the same. -->

## Think first

**An ice cube melts into water. You put the water back in the freezer. What do you get?**

- Nothing, because it is gone
- Ice again, because it was the same stuff all along
- A kind of water that can't freeze
- Something new that is not ice

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Ice again. Melting is a change you can undo: it is the same stuff, only in a different state. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Making it melt

**What do you do to melt a solid?**

- Put it in the shade
- Add heat
- Squash it
- Take heat away

* Answer: Add heat

<!-- Speaker note: A solid melts when it gets enough heat. Taking heat away makes a liquid freeze. -->

## Chocolate in the fridge

**A chocolate bar melts in the sun. You put it in the fridge and it goes hard again. What does this show?**

- It has turned into something new
- It was the same chocolate all along
- The fridge made new chocolate
- It is a different kind of chocolate

* Answer: It was the same chocolate all along

<!-- Speaker note: Melting and freezing are changes of state. The stuff stays the same, so you can undo the change. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Ice in your hand

**You hold an ice cube in your warm hand and it melts. Which way does the heat move?**

- It doesn't move
- Both ways equally
- From the ice to your hand
- From your hand to the ice

* Answer: From your hand to the ice

<!-- Speaker note: Heat moves from your warm hand into the cooler ice, which is why the ice melts and your hand feels cold. -->

## Puddle

**Water in a puddle freezes on a cold night and melts in the morning sun. What is true?**

- It is the same water each time
- The morning sun makes new water
- Frozen water can't melt again
- The ice is a new substance

* Answer: It is the same water each time

<!-- Speaker note: Freezing and melting are changes of state. It is the same water in a different state. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Hot tea

**A hot cup of tea stands on a table. What happens to its heat?**

- It disappears
- It stays in the tea forever
- It moves from the air into the tea
- It moves into the cooler air and the table

* Answer: It moves into the cooler air and the table

<!-- Speaker note: Heat moves from the warmer tea into the cooler air and table, so the tea cools. -->

## Exit ticket

**How can you tell that melted ice is the same stuff as the ice?**

* You can freeze it again and get ice back.
