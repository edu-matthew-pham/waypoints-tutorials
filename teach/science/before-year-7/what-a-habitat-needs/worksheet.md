# What a habitat needs

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Plants need water. So why does a pot plant die when you water it every day?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students name the physical conditions of a habitat and see that a living thing can only cope with a range of each, so too much can be as harmful as too little.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the physical conditions of a habitat. I can say why too much of a condition can harm a living thing. I can say what a change in one condition could do to living things.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Think first

Lock in a guess before you read on.

:::keep-together

**A pot plant is watered a little more every day. What happens to it?**

☐ It grows better and better the more water it gets

☐ It grows well with some water, but too much harms it

☐ It only needs water if it is in the sun

☐ Water makes no difference to it

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** It grows well with some water, but too much harms it. Roots need air as well as water, and a soggy pot drowns them. Most living things can only cope with a range of any condition.

:::


## The idea

A habitat's physical conditions include **temperature**, **light**, **water** and **how salty** the water is. Each living thing has a **range** it can cope with. Below the range or above it, it struggles to grow and survive.

:::keep-together

### A hot pond

**A pond fish can survive when the water is between 5 °C and 30 °C. In a heatwave the pond water warms to 34 °C. What is likely to happen?**

☐ Nothing, because fish like warm water

☐ It only matters if the pond freezes

☐ The fish gets stronger

☐ The fish is in danger, because the water is above its range

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The fish is in danger, because the water is above its range

34 °C is above the fish's range of 5 to 30 °C. A condition that is too high can harm a living thing, just like one that is too low.

:::


:::keep-together

### A dry creek

**A dry summer dries up half a creek. Which sentence is best?**

☐ Nothing else changes

☐ Living things that need a lot of water will struggle

☐ Only the water itself is changed

☐ Every living thing there will do better

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Living things that need a lot of water will struggle

When a condition changes, the living things that depend on it are affected.

:::


## Check


:::keep-together

### A fern

**A fern grows best in shade. What happens if you move it into hot, full sun all day?**

☐ It grows twice as fast

☐ Nothing changes

☐ It dries out and may die, because there is too much light and heat

☐ It turns into a different plant

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It dries out and may die, because there is too much light and heat

More of a condition isn't always better. Too much sun and heat is outside this fern's range.

:::


:::keep-together

### Conditions in a habitat

**Which statement is true about the conditions in a habitat?**

☐ Conditions never change

☐ Each living thing can only cope with a range of them

☐ Every living thing needs exactly the same conditions

☐ More is always better

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Each living thing can only cope with a range of them

Different living things have different ranges, and too much or too little is a problem.

:::
