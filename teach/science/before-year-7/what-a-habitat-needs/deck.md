# What a habitat needs

Before Year 7 science · Living things

<!-- Speaker note: Students name the physical conditions of a habitat and see that a living thing can only cope with a range of each, so too much can be as harmful as too little. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · The idea 6 min · Check 4 min · Apply 4 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can name the physical conditions of a habitat.
- I can say why too much of a condition can harm a living thing.
- I can say what a change in one condition could do to living things.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: each living thing survives within a range of each physical condition. The common mistake is thinking more of a good thing is always better (more water, more sun, more warmth). The pot plant and the fern meet it. This lesson uses Year 6 nodes 1 and 2 (conditions, and how changes affect growth and survival). Human activity (node 3) is left to Year 7, where *What if the dingo disappeared?* looks at what happens when something is lost. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students about a plant they have overwatered, or one that died in the sun. Teacher: The idea: for the pond fish, draw the range on the board (5 to 30) and mark 34. Teacher: Apply: ask for living things from different habitats so the class sees different ranges. -->

## Why it matters

> Plants need water. So why does a pot plant die when you water it every day?

<!-- Speaker note: A **habitat** is the place where a living thing lives. It has physical conditions, like how hot it is, how much light there is and how much water. Living things can only cope with some amounts of each. -->

## Think first

**A pot plant is watered a little more every day. What happens to it?**

- It grows better and better the more water it gets
- It grows well with some water, but too much harms it
- It only needs water if it is in the sun
- Water makes no difference to it

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: It grows well with some water, but too much harms it. Roots need air as well as water, and a soggy pot drowns them. Most living things can only cope with a range of any condition. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What happened?

* It grows well with some water, but too much harms it. Roots need air as well as water, and a soggy pot drowns them. Most living things can only cope with a range of any condition.

## Conditions

**Is it a physical condition of a habitat?**

Groups: A physical condition · Not a physical condition

How hot or cold it is · How much light reaches the ground · How salty the water is · A kookaburra · How much rain falls · A snail

* Answer: How hot or cold it is → A physical condition; How much light reaches the ground → A physical condition; How salty the water is → A physical condition; A kookaburra → Not a physical condition; How much rain falls → A physical condition; A snail → Not a physical condition

## A hot pond

**A pond fish can survive when the water is between 5 °C and 30 °C. In a heatwave the pond water warms to 34 °C. What is likely to happen?**

- Nothing, because fish like warm water
- It only matters if the pond freezes
- The fish gets stronger
- The fish is in danger, because the water is above its range

* Answer: The fish is in danger, because the water is above its range

<!-- Speaker note: 34 °C is above the fish's range of 5 to 30 °C. A condition that is too high can harm a living thing, just like one that is too low. -->

## A dry creek

**A dry summer dries up half a creek. Which sentence is best?**

- Nothing else changes
- Living things that need a lot of water will struggle
- Only the water itself is changed
- Every living thing there will do better

* Answer: Living things that need a lot of water will struggle

<!-- Speaker note: When a condition changes, the living things that depend on it are affected. -->

## A fern

**A fern grows best in shade. What happens if you move it into hot, full sun all day?**

- It grows twice as fast
- Nothing changes
- It dries out and may die, because there is too much light and heat
- It turns into a different plant

* Answer: It dries out and may die, because there is too much light and heat

<!-- Speaker note: More of a condition isn't always better. Too much sun and heat is outside this fern's range. -->

## Conditions in a habitat

**Which statement is true about the conditions in a habitat?**

- Conditions never change
- Each living thing can only cope with a range of them
- Every living thing needs exactly the same conditions
- More is always better

* Answer: Each living thing can only cope with a range of them

<!-- Speaker note: Different living things have different ranges, and too much or too little is a problem. -->

## Exit ticket

**Why can a plant die from too much water?**

* It has a range it can cope with. A soggy pot leaves its roots without air.
