# Particles: the first model

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You can't see the tiny pieces that ice, water and steam are made of. But picturing them helps to explain why they behave so differently.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use a first particle model (arrangement and movement) to explain solids, liquids and gases, and see that a change of state does not change the particles.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe the particles in a solid, a liquid and a gas. I can say what changes, and what stays the same, when something melts or boils. I can use the particle model to explain something I can see.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

The **particle model** says everything is made of tiny **particles**. In a **solid** they are packed together and shake in place. In a **liquid** they are close but slide past each other. In a **gas** they are far apart and move fast. When something melts or boils, the particles stay the same: only how they are arranged and how they move changes.

:::keep-together

### The three states

**Match each state to its particles.**

A. Far apart, moving fast in every direction

B. Close together, sliding past each other

C. Packed together, shaking in place

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Solid → C. Packed together, shaking in place; Liquid → B. Close together, sliding past each other; Gas → A. Far apart, moving fast in every direction

In a solid the particles stay in place. In a liquid they slide. In a gas they spread out to fill the space.

:::


:::keep-together

### Boiling

**Water boils and becomes steam. What changes?**

☐ The particles turn into a different kind

☐ The particles spread far apart and move fast

☐ The particles stop moving

☐ The particles get bigger

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The particles spread far apart and move fast

The particles are the same water particles. Heat makes them move faster, so they spread out and become a gas.

:::


:::keep-together

### Gas

**Why does a gas fill the whole space it is in?**

☐ Its particles are stuck together

☐ Its particles are packed tightly in place

☐ Gases are lighter than air

☐ Its particles move fast in every direction and spread out

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its particles move fast in every direction and spread out

The particles of a gas move fast in all directions and aren't held in place, so they spread out to fill all the space.

:::
