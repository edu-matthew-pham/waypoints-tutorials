# Particles: the first model

Before Year 7 science · Solids

<!-- Speaker note: Students use a first particle model (arrangement and movement) to explain solids, liquids and gases, and see that a change of state does not change the particles. About 18 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 3 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can describe the particles in a solid, a liquid and a gas.
- I can say what changes, and what stays the same, when something melts or boils.
- I can use the particle model to explain something I can see.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: a state is how particles are arranged and moving, and a change of state changes that, not the particles. The common mistake is that the particles melt, grow or get hot. The Think first question and the sort meet it. The board is the zoom lens used in the Year 7 lesson *What is everything made of?*, with the same three samples. In Year 7 students add attraction between particles and the model's limits. Here we use only arrangement and movement. The model is two-dimensional on the screen and the particles are drawn as dots: say so, because real particles aren't dots. Not yet tried with a class. ## Why it matters

> You can't see the tiny pieces that ice, water and steam are made of. But picturing them helps to explain why they behave so differently.

<!-- Speaker note: A **model** is a picture that helps us explain what we can't see. The particle model says everything is made of very small pieces called **particles**. This lesson uses it to explain solids, liquids and gases. -->

## Think first

**Ice melts into water. What happens to the tiny particles it is made of?**

- They get bigger
- They melt too
- They stay the same but move differently and can slide past each other
- They disappear

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: They stay the same but move differently. In ice the particles are packed together and shake in place. In water they can slide past each other. The particles don't melt or change size. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What happened?

* They stay the same but move differently. In ice the particles are packed together and shake in place. In water they can slide past each other. The particles don't melt or change size.

## The three states

**Match each state to its particles.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Solid` | A. Far apart, moving fast in every direction |
| `Liquid` | B. Close together, sliding past each other |
| `Gas` | C. Packed together, shaking in place |

* Answer: Solid → C. Packed together, shaking in place; Liquid → B. Close together, sliding past each other; Gas → A. Far apart, moving fast in every direction

<!-- Speaker note: In a solid the particles stay in place. In a liquid they slide. In a gas they spread out to fill the space. -->

## Boiling

**Water boils and becomes steam. What changes?**

- The particles turn into a different kind
- The particles spread far apart and move fast
- The particles stop moving
- The particles get bigger

* Answer: The particles spread far apart and move fast

<!-- Speaker note: The particles are the same water particles. Heat makes them move faster, so they spread out and become a gas. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Gas

**Why does a gas fill the whole space it is in?**

- Its particles are stuck together
- Its particles are packed tightly in place
- Gases are lighter than air
- Its particles move fast in every direction and spread out

* Answer: Its particles move fast in every direction and spread out

<!-- Speaker note: The particles of a gas move fast in all directions and aren't held in place, so they spread out to fill all the space. -->

## A solid

**Which picture goes with a solid, such as an ice cube?**

- Particles packed close together in a pattern, shaking in place
- Particles spread far apart, moving fast
- Particles that are bigger than in a liquid
- Particles sliding past each other

* Answer: Particles packed close together in a pattern, shaking in place

<!-- Speaker note: In a solid, particles are packed in a pattern and can only shake in place. -->

## What changes

**Does the particle model say it changes when ice melts?**

Groups: Changes · Stays the same

How the particles move · How close the particles are · The size of each particle · What the particles are made of

* Answer: How the particles move → Changes; How close the particles are → Changes; The size of each particle → Stays the same; What the particles are made of → Stays the same

## Exit ticket

**What changes when a liquid becomes a gas, and what doesn't?**

* The particles spread apart and move faster. The particles themselves stay the same.
