# Who eats whom? A first look

Before Year 7 science · Living things

<!-- Speaker note: Students build a short food chain, name the producer, and say which way the arrows point and why. About 18 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 5 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 3 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a producer and a consumer are.
- I can draw a food chain with the arrows the right way round.
- I can say what an arrow in a food chain means.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: an arrow shows where the energy goes, so it points from the food to the eater. The common mistake is drawing the arrow the way the eating happens ('the lizard eats the grasshopper' → lizard → grasshopper). The Think first question and the board's red arrows both meet it. The Year 7 lesson *Who eats whom?* uses the same board with a bigger web. Decomposers (Year 4 node 3) and what happens if a link goes (node 5) are left to Year 7, where *What if the dingo disappeared?* covers it. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to explain the direction they chose before showing the answer. Teacher: Try: if the board turns arrows red, ask 'Which way does the energy go?' Teacher: Apply: look for chains that start with an animal. Ask what the animal eats. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Grass doesn't eat anything, but a kangaroo eats grass. So where does the grass get its energy?

<!-- Speaker note: Every living thing needs energy. A food chain shows where it comes from. The tricky part is the arrows: which way should they point? -->

## Think first

**A lizard eats a grasshopper. Which way should the arrow point in a food chain?**

- There is no arrow, only a line
- Either way, it doesn't matter
- From the grasshopper to the lizard
- From the lizard to the grasshopper

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: From the grasshopper to the lizard. The arrow shows where the energy goes: the lizard gets energy from the grasshopper. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Where a chain starts

**Which one makes its own food, so it starts a food chain?**

- Lizard
- Kookaburra
- Grasshopper
- Grass

* Answer: Grass

<!-- Speaker note: Grass is a producer. It makes its own food using sunlight, and the rest of the chain gets its energy from it. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Reading an arrow

**In the chain grass → grasshopper → lizard → kookaburra, what does the arrow from lizard to kookaburra mean?**

- The lizard is bigger than the kookaburra
- The lizard eats the kookaburra
- The kookaburra and lizard are friends
- The kookaburra eats the lizard, and energy goes to the kookaburra

* Answer: The kookaburra eats the lizard, and energy goes to the kookaburra

<!-- Speaker note: An arrow goes from the food to the eater: the kookaburra eats the lizard, so the energy moves from the lizard to the kookaburra. -->

## Which way?

**Which food chain has its arrows the right way round?**

- Seaweed → fish → seal
- Seal → fish → seaweed
- Fish → seaweed → seal
- Seaweed → seal → fish

* Answer: Seaweed → fish → seal

<!-- Speaker note: Seaweed is the producer, a fish eats it, and a seal eats the fish. Each arrow goes from the food to the eater. -->

## Producer or consumer

**Is it a producer or a consumer?**

Groups: Producer · Consumer

A gum tree · A kangaroo · Seaweed · A fish that eats seaweed · A lizard · Grass

* Answer: A gum tree → Producer; A kangaroo → Consumer; Seaweed → Producer; A fish that eats seaweed → Consumer; A lizard → Consumer; Grass → Producer

**A fish eats seaweed and a seal eats the fish. Draw the chain with arrows.**

* Seaweed → fish → seal.
