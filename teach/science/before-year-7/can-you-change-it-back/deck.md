# Can you change it back?

Before Year 7 science · Solids

<!-- Speaker note: Students see that dissolving and melting make no new stuff and can be undone, while burning and cooking make new stuff, and that a dissolved solid isn't gone. About 18 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 3 min · The idea 6 min · Check 3 min · Apply 3 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a change you can undo looks like.
- I can say what happens to a solid that dissolves.
- I can say how to tell if a change made new stuff.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: if no new stuff is made, the change can be undone. The common mistake is that when something dissolves it has gone. The sugar question and the board meet it. Burning and cooking are introduced as the contrast. The board is the one used in the Year 7 lesson *Pure substance or mixture?*. The next lesson, *How do you separate a mixture?*, uses the same idea to get a dissolved salt back. The test of 'is something new made?' is deliberately plain. It becomes 'chemical change' in Year 8. ## Why it matters

> Stir sugar into tea and it disappears. Where did it go, and could you get it back?

<!-- Speaker note: Things change all the time. Ice melts, toast burns, sugar vanishes in tea. In some changes you can get the stuff back. In others you can't. This lesson is about how to tell which. -->

## Think first

**You stir a spoon of sugar into a cup of water and it seems to disappear. What has happened to it?**

- It has floated away as a gas
- It has turned into water
- It has been destroyed
- It is still there, spread through the water in tiny pieces

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: It is still there, spread through the water in tiny pieces too small to see. If you let the water dry up, the sugar is left behind. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Getting the salt back

**Salt is dissolved in water. How could you get the salt back?**

- Freeze it and the salt turns into ice
- Let the water dry up, and the salt is left behind
- You can't, because it has gone
- Add more water

* Answer: Let the water dry up, and the salt is left behind

<!-- Speaker note: When the water dries up, the salt is left. Dissolving didn't make anything new. -->

## Toast

**You toast a slice of bread. Can you change it back to plain bread?**

- Yes, if you wet it
- No, because toasting makes new stuff
- Yes, if you let it cool down
- Yes, because all changes can be undone

* Answer: No, because toasting makes new stuff

<!-- Speaker note: Toasting makes new stuff with different properties. You can't turn it back into bread. -->

## Sort the changes

**Can you change it back?**

Groups: Can change back · Can't change back

Melting an ice block · Burning a piece of wood · Dissolving sugar in tea · Baking a cake · Freezing water · Iron rusting

* Answer: Melting an ice block → Can change back; Burning a piece of wood → Can't change back; Dissolving sugar in tea → Can change back; Baking a cake → Can't change back; Freezing water → Can change back; Iron rusting → Can't change back

## New stuff

**Which of these shows that a change made new stuff?**

- The stuff moves to another place
- A gas is given off and a crust is left that can't change back
- The stuff changes shape
- The stuff gets cold

* Answer: A gas is given off and a crust is left that can't change back

<!-- Speaker note: Signs of new stuff are a gas given off, a new colour, a new smell or something that can't be changed back. -->

## Tea

**Tea with sugar stirred in is left in the sun and the water dries up. What is left?**

- Nothing
- A new kind of sugar
- Sugar
- More water

* Answer: Sugar

<!-- Speaker note: Sugar is still there when the water dries up. -->

## Exit ticket

**Give one change that can be undone and one that can't, and say how you can tell.**

* Melting ice can be undone because no new stuff is made. Burning wood can't be undone because ash and smoke are new stuff.
