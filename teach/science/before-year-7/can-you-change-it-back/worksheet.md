# Can you change it back?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Stir sugar into tea and it disappears. Where did it go, and could you get it back?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that dissolving and melting make no new stuff and can be undone, while burning and cooking make new stuff, and that a dissolved solid isn't gone.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a change you can undo looks like. I can say what happens to a solid that dissolves. I can say how to tell if a change made new stuff.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

In a change you can undo, **no new stuff is made**. Dissolving, melting and freezing are like this. In a change you can't undo, **new stuff is made**: burning, cooking and rusting. A dissolved solid hasn't gone. It is still there, spread through the liquid.

:::keep-together

### Getting the salt back

**Salt is dissolved in water. How could you get the salt back?**

☐ Freeze it and the salt turns into ice

☐ Let the water dry up, and the salt is left behind

☐ You can't, because it has gone

☐ Add more water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Let the water dry up, and the salt is left behind

When the water dries up, the salt is left. Dissolving didn't make anything new.

:::


:::keep-together

### Toast

**You toast a slice of bread. Can you change it back to plain bread?**

☐ Yes, if you wet it

☐ No, because toasting makes new stuff

☐ Yes, if you let it cool down

☐ Yes, because all changes can be undone

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, because toasting makes new stuff

Toasting makes new stuff with different properties. You can't turn it back into bread.

:::


:::keep-together

### New stuff

**Which of these shows that a change made new stuff?**

☐ The stuff moves to another place

☐ A gas is given off and a crust is left that can't change back

☐ The stuff changes shape

☐ The stuff gets cold

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A gas is given off and a crust is left that can't change back

Signs of new stuff are a gas given off, a new colour, a new smell or something that can't be changed back.

:::


:::keep-together

### Tea

**Tea with sugar stirred in is left in the sun and the water dries up. What is left?**

☐ Nothing

☐ A new kind of sugar

☐ Sugar

☐ More water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sugar

Sugar is still there when the water dries up.
