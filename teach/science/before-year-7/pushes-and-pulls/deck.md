# Pushes and pulls

Before Year 7 science · Forces and the Earth

<!-- Speaker note: Students describe a force as a push or pull one thing gives another, with a direction and size shown by an arrow, and drop the idea that a force is something an object carries. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a force is.
- I can say what gives a force and what gets it.
- I can read the direction and size from an arrow.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: a force is a push or pull that one thing gives to another. The common mistake is that an object has force inside it, as in 'the ball has force', 'it ran out of force'. The kick question meets it. The board is the force diagram used in the Year 7 lessons *How big, which way?* and *Does balanced mean no forces?*. Here there are only two forces, with no weight or friction, to keep to pushes and pulls. The Year 4 node covers friction, gravity and magnets too. They are Year 7 ideas in *How big, which way?* and *Would you weigh less on the Moon?*. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Apply: look for 'the force is in the ball' and ask where it came from. -->

---

## Why it matters

> When you kick a ball, where does the force come from: your foot, or the ball?

<!-- Speaker note: Every push and pull is a **force**. To use the word well, you need to know two things: what is giving the force, and what is getting it. -->

## Think first

**A girl kicks a football. Where does the force on the ball come from?**

- From the air around the ball
- From the ball, which has force inside it
- From the speed of the ball
- From her foot, which pushes on the ball

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: From her foot, which pushes on the ball. A force is a push or pull that one thing gives to another. It isn't something that lives inside the ball. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Two things

**A magnet picks up a paperclip. What gives the force, and what gets it?**

- The magnet gives it, and the paperclip gets it
- Neither, because they never touched
- The paperclip gives it to the magnet
- The air gives it to the paperclip

* Answer: The magnet gives it, and the paperclip gets it

<!-- Speaker note: The magnet pulls on the paperclip. A force doesn't need touching: magnets and gravity pull from a distance. -->

## Push or pull

**Is it a push or a pull?**

Groups: Push · Pull

Kicking a ball · A dog tugging a rope · Opening a door by its handle towards you · Wind blowing a sail · A magnet drawing a nail towards it · Pressing a button

* Answer: Kicking a ball → Push; A dog tugging a rope → Pull; Opening a door by its handle towards you → Pull; Wind blowing a sail → Push; A magnet drawing a nail towards it → Pull; Pressing a button → Push

## Reading arrows

**On a force diagram, one arrow is twice as long as another. What does that mean?**

- The longer one is a slower force
- The longer one is a pull
- The longer one is a bigger force
- The longer one is further away

* Answer: The longer one is a bigger force

<!-- Speaker note: A longer arrow means a bigger force. The arrow points the way the force acts. -->

## Tug of war

**A rope in a tug of war has a long arrow pointing left and a short arrow pointing right. What does this show?**

- A bigger pull to the left than to the right
- Nobody is pulling
- A bigger pull to the right than to the left
- Both pulls are the same

* Answer: A bigger pull to the left than to the right

<!-- Speaker note: The longer arrow is the bigger force, and it points left. -->

## A swing

**A child pushes a friend on a swing. What gives the force and what gets it?**

- Nobody gives it, because it swings by itself
- The child gives it, and the swing and friend get it
- The friend gives it to the swing
- The swing gives it to the child

* Answer: The child gives it, and the swing and friend get it

<!-- Speaker note: The child pushes, so the child gives the force. The swing and the friend on it get it. -->

## Exit ticket

**A dog pulls a sled. What gives the force, what gets it, and is it a push or a pull?**

* The dog gives it, the sled gets it, and it is a pull.
