# Pushes and pulls

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** When you kick a ball, where does the force come from: your foot, or the ball?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe a force as a push or pull one thing gives another, with a direction and size shown by an arrow, and drop the idea that a force is something an object carries.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a force is. I can say what gives a force and what gets it. I can read the direction and size from an arrow.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**What happens:** From her foot, which pushes on the ball. A force is a push or pull that one thing gives to another. It isn't something that lives inside the ball.

## The idea

A **force** is a **push** or a **pull** that one thing gives to another. It acts in a **direction** and has a **size**. An arrow can show both: it points the way the force acts, and a longer arrow is a bigger force. A force needs two things, one giving it and one getting it.

:::keep-together

### Two things

**A magnet picks up a paperclip. What gives the force, and what gets it?**

☐ The magnet gives it, and the paperclip gets it

☐ Neither, because they never touched

☐ The paperclip gives it to the magnet

☐ The air gives it to the paperclip

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The magnet gives it, and the paperclip gets it

The magnet pulls on the paperclip. A force doesn't need touching: magnets and gravity pull from a distance.

:::


:::keep-together

### Reading arrows

**On a force diagram, one arrow is twice as long as another. What does that mean?**

☐ The longer one is a slower force

☐ The longer one is a pull

☐ The longer one is a bigger force

☐ The longer one is further away

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The longer one is a bigger force

A longer arrow means a bigger force. The arrow points the way the force acts.

:::

### Tug of war

**A rope in a tug of war has a long arrow pointing left and a short arrow pointing right. What does this show?**

☐ A bigger pull to the left than to the right

☐ Nobody is pulling

☐ A bigger pull to the right than to the left

☐ Both pulls are the same

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A bigger pull to the left than to the right

The longer arrow is the bigger force, and it points left.

:::


:::keep-together

### A swing

**A child pushes a friend on a swing. What gives the force and what gets it?**

☐ Nobody gives it, because it swings by itself

☐ The child gives it, and the swing and friend get it

☐ The friend gives it to the swing

☐ The swing gives it to the child

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The child gives it, and the swing and friend get it

The child pushes, so the child gives the force. The swing and the friend on it get it.

:::
