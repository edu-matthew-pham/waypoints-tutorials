# Built to survive

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A penguin has feathers but can't fly. A bat can fly but has no feathers. So which one is a feature?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students tell a feature (a body part) from a behaviour (an action) and say what job a feature does, so a key can later ask about parts you can see.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name a feature of an animal and say what job it does. I can tell a feature from a behaviour. I can say why a key asks about features you can see.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

{{lines-3}}

**What happens:** The strong, hooked beak. A feature is a part of the body. Laughing, swooping and waiting are things the kookaburra does, which are its behaviour.

:::


## The idea

A feature is a part of the body. Each one has a job: fur keeps an animal warm, webbed feet help it swim and sharp claws help it grip. What an animal does, like flying, hiding or migrating, is its behaviour.

:::keep-together

### Features and their jobs

**Match each feature to its job.**

A. Push through water to swim

B. Stop animals eating it

C. Keeps the animal warm

D. Grip and catch food

### Why keys use features

**A key asks 'Does it have feathers?' instead of 'Does it fly?' Why is that the better question?**

☐ Every bird has both of them

☐ You can see feathers by looking, but flying isn't always seen

☐ Feathers matter more to a bird than flying

☐ Flying is a harder word to spell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** You can see feathers by looking, but flying isn't always seen

Features can be seen on any animal at any time. Behaviour has to be watched, and it can be misleading.

:::

### A fish

**A fish has gills and a smooth, narrow body. Which sentence names a feature and its job?**

☐ Its smooth, narrow body helps it slip through the water

☐ It lives in the sea

☐ It lays eggs

☐ It swims away from sharks

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its smooth, narrow body helps it slip through the water

A feature is a part of the body, and the answer says what job it does.

:::


:::keep-together

### A koala

**A koala has thick fur and strong claws. Which is a behaviour?**

☐ Thick fur

☐ Strong claws

☐ Sleeping most of the day

☐ A grey coat

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sleeping most of the day

Sleeping is something the koala does, so it is behaviour. Fur and claws are parts of its body.
