# Built to survive

Before Year 7 science · Living things

<!-- Speaker note: Students tell a feature (a body part) from a behaviour (an action) and say what job a feature does, so a key can later ask about parts you can see. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · The idea 7 min · Check 4 min · Apply 3 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can name a feature of an animal and say what job it does.
- I can tell a feature from a behaviour.
- I can say why a key asks about features you can see.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: a feature is a part of the body with a job; behaviour is what the animal does. The common mistake is mixing the two (a bird 'has flying'). The sorting task and the key question meet it directly. Year 5 describes this as structural features and behaviours; this lesson keeps only the first, because Year 7 keys use features. Behaviour is Year 5 node 2 and is not taught here. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to say what the other three options are. They are things the kookaburra does. Teacher: The idea: run the sort as a quick whole-class vote before anyone locks in. Teacher: Apply: if students pick an action, ask 'Can you point to it on the animal?' -->

---

## Why it matters

> A penguin has feathers but can't fly. A bat can fly but has no feathers. So which one is a feature?

<!-- Speaker note: Look at any animal and you can spot parts of its body that do a job. This lesson is about telling a **feature** (a part you can see) from a **behaviour** (something the animal does). -->

## Think first

**Which of these is a feature of a kookaburra's body?**

- It sits still on a branch and waits
- It laughs in the morning
- It swoops down on lizards
- A strong, hooked beak

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: The strong, hooked beak. A feature is a part of the body. Laughing, swooping and waiting are things the kookaburra does, which are its behaviour. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Features and their jobs

**Match each feature to its job.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Thick fur` | A. Push through water to swim |
| `Webbed feet` | B. Stop animals eating it |
| `Sharp claws` | C. Keeps the animal warm |
| `Spines on a cactus` | D. Grip and catch food |

* Answer: Thick fur → C. Keeps the animal warm; Webbed feet → A. Push through water to swim; Sharp claws → D. Grip and catch food; Spines on a cactus → B. Stop animals eating it

<!-- Speaker note: Each feature suits the way that animal lives, which is why it helps it survive. -->

## Feature or behaviour

**Is it a feature (a body part) or a behaviour (something it does)?**

Groups: Feature · Behaviour

Feathers · Flying · Webbed feet · Hiding in a burrow by day · A long, thin beak · Flying north each winter

* Answer: Feathers → Feature; Flying → Behaviour; Webbed feet → Feature; Hiding in a burrow by day → Behaviour; A long, thin beak → Feature; Flying north each winter → Behaviour

## Why keys use features

**A key asks 'Does it have feathers?' instead of 'Does it fly?' Why is that the better question?**

- Every bird has both of them
- You can see feathers by looking, but flying isn't always seen
- Feathers matter more to a bird than flying
- Flying is a harder word to spell

* Answer: You can see feathers by looking, but flying isn't always seen

<!-- Speaker note: Features can be seen on any animal at any time. Behaviour has to be watched, and it can be misleading. -->

## A fish

**A fish has gills and a smooth, narrow body. Which sentence names a feature and its job?**

- Its smooth, narrow body helps it slip through the water
- It lives in the sea
- It lays eggs
- It swims away from sharks

* Answer: Its smooth, narrow body helps it slip through the water

<!-- Speaker note: A feature is a part of the body, and the answer says what job it does. -->

## A koala

**A koala has thick fur and strong claws. Which is a behaviour?**

- Thick fur
- Strong claws
- Sleeping most of the day
- A grey coat

* Answer: Sleeping most of the day

<!-- Speaker note: Sleeping is something the koala does, so it is behaviour. Fur and claws are parts of its body. -->

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Choosing their own animal makes the feature and job pair stick and shows whether students can name a part rather than an action. When students get stuck: A student says 'flying' is a feature of a bird. Ask: Can you point to flying on a bird that is sitting still? Fix: Point to the wings and feathers. ## Exit ticket

**Is 'swims fast' a feature or a behaviour? Give a feature that helps a fish swim fast.**

* Swimming fast is behaviour. A feature that helps is a smooth, narrow body or strong fins.
