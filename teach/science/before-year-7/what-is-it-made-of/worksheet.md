# What is it made of?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Salt and sand both look like little grains. What could you do to tell them apart?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students name properties of materials, pair each with a test, and use a test to tell apart materials that look alike.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name some properties of materials. I can say a test for a property. I can use a test to tell two materials apart.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Think first

Lock in a guess before you read on.

**A bag of salt and a bag of sand have been mixed up. Both are small grains. How could you tell which is which?**

☐ Look at them: salt is always white

☐ Taste them

☐ Stir a spoonful of each into water and see which disappears

☐ You can't tell without a microscope

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Stir a spoonful of each into water. The salt dissolves and seems to disappear, but the sand sinks to the bottom. Tasting is never a safe way to test a material in a science lesson, and colour alone can be misleading.

:::


## The idea

A **property** is something you can find out about a material by testing it, like **hard**, **flexible**, **waterproof** or **magnetic**. Two materials can look the same but have different properties, so testing is more reliable than looking.

:::keep-together

### Properties and tests

A. Try to bend it

B. Hold a magnet near it

C. Try to scratch it with a coin

D. Drop water on it and see if it soaks in

### Two grey bars

**Two shiny grey metal bars look the same. One is iron, and one is aluminium. Which test could tell them apart?**

☐ Ask which one is shinier

☐ Hold them next to each other

☐ Look at them closely in the light

☐ Hold a magnet near each one

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Hold a magnet near each one

Iron is attracted to a magnet and aluminium isn't. A test of a property can tell two materials apart when they look the same.

:::


:::keep-together

### Property or object

**Which one is a property of a material?**

☐ A bucket

☐ A drinking straw

☐ Flexible

☐ A garden hose

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Flexible

Flexible describes how a material behaves. A hose, bucket and straw are objects made from materials.

:::

### A waterproof cloth

**You need to know if a cloth is waterproof. What should you do?**

☐ Drop some water on it and see if it soaks in

☐ Look at its colour

☐ Feel how soft it is

☐ Smell it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Drop some water on it and see if it soaks in

To test if a cloth is waterproof, check whether water soaks through it.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two cups

**Two clear cups look the same. One is glass and one is plastic. Which test is a good place to start?**

☐ Look at which is clearer

☐ Tap each gently and listen, and check which bends

☐ Ask which one looks more expensive

☐ Weigh each in your hand while guessing

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Tap each gently and listen, and check which bends

Glass and plastic sound different and bend differently. A test you can repeat tells them apart.
