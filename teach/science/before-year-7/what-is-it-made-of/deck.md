# What is it made of?

Before Year 7 science · Solids

<!-- Speaker note: Students name properties of materials, pair each with a test, and use a test to tell apart materials that look alike. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · The idea 6 min · Check 4 min · Apply 4 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can name some properties of materials.
- I can say a test for a property.
- I can use a test to tell two materials apart.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: you tell materials apart by testing their properties, not only by looking. The common mistake is that things that look the same are the same material. The salt and sand question and the grey bars meet it. Safety: never ask students to taste or smell unknown materials. The Think first question lists tasting as a wrong option on purpose. This lesson leads into the Year 7 lessons *Pure substance or mixture?* and *How do you separate a mixture?*, where properties such as dissolving and particle size are used to separate things. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: if you have salt, sand and water, do the test as a demonstration. Teacher: The idea: bring in two objects that look alike, such as two grey spoons, and a magnet. Teacher: Apply: look for tests that only look at colour or shape and ask what would happen if the two looked the same. -->

## Why it matters

> Salt and sand both look like little grains. What could you do to tell them apart?

<!-- Speaker note: Everything around you is made of **materials**, like metal, glass, wood or plastic. Scientists describe a material by its **properties**: how it behaves when you test it. -->

## Think first

**A bag of salt and a bag of sand have been mixed up. Both are small grains. How could you tell which is which?**

- Look at them: salt is always white
- Taste them
- Stir a spoonful of each into water and see which disappears
- You can't tell without a microscope

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Stir a spoonful of each into water. The salt dissolves and seems to disappear, but the sand sinks to the bottom. Tasting is never a safe way to test a material in a science lesson, and colour alone can be misleading. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What happened?

* Stir a spoonful of each into water. The salt dissolves and seems to disappear, but the sand sinks to the bottom. Tasting is never a safe way to test a material in a science lesson, and colour alone can be misleading.

## Properties and tests

**Match each property to a simple test.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Hard` | A. Try to bend it |
| `Flexible` | B. Hold a magnet near it |
| `Waterproof` | C. Try to scratch it with a coin |
| `Magnetic` | D. Drop water on it and see if it soaks in |

* Answer: Hard → C. Try to scratch it with a coin; Flexible → A. Try to bend it; Waterproof → D. Drop water on it and see if it soaks in; Magnetic → B. Hold a magnet near it

<!-- Speaker note: Each property can be tested, so you can find out about a material instead of guessing. -->

## Two grey bars

**Two shiny grey metal bars look the same. One is iron, and one is aluminium. Which test could tell them apart?**

- Ask which one is shinier
- Hold them next to each other
- Look at them closely in the light
- Hold a magnet near each one

* Answer: Hold a magnet near each one

<!-- Speaker note: Iron is attracted to a magnet and aluminium isn't. A test of a property can tell two materials apart when they look the same. -->

## Property or object

**Which one is a property of a material?**

- A bucket
- A drinking straw
- Flexible
- A garden hose

* Answer: Flexible

<!-- Speaker note: Flexible describes how a material behaves. A hose, bucket and straw are objects made from materials. -->

## A waterproof cloth

**You need to know if a cloth is waterproof. What should you do?**

- Drop some water on it and see if it soaks in
- Look at its colour
- Feel how soft it is
- Smell it

* Answer: Drop some water on it and see if it soaks in

<!-- Speaker note: To test if a cloth is waterproof, check whether water soaks through it. -->

## Two cups

**Two clear cups look the same. One is glass and one is plastic. Which test is a good place to start?**

- Look at which is clearer
- Tap each gently and listen, and check which bends
- Ask which one looks more expensive
- Weigh each in your hand while guessing

* Answer: Tap each gently and listen, and check which bends

<!-- Speaker note: Glass and plastic sound different and bend differently. A test you can repeat tells them apart. -->

## Exit ticket

**Name a property of metal and say how you could test it.**

* Hard: try to scratch it. Magnetic (for iron): hold a magnet near it.
