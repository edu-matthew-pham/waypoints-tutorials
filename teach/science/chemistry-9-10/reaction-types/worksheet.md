# What kind of reaction is it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Drop an iron nail into blue copper sulfate solution and it comes out coated in copper, while the blue slowly fades. Iron has pushed copper out of its compound.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions by their patterns, use reactivity to predict whether a displacement happens, and classify unfamiliar reactions and predict products (AC9S10U07, nodes 1 to 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can recognise synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions from their patterns. I can predict the products of a displacement reaction using reactivity. I can classify a reaction I haven't seen before.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**An iron nail is put in copper sulfate solution. Iron is more reactive than copper. What happens?**

☐ The copper sulfate turns into iron

☐ Nothing: metals don't react with solutions

☐ The iron and copper mix to make an alloy

☐ Iron takes copper's place: iron sulfate forms and copper coats the nail

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Iron takes copper's place: iron + copper sulfate → iron sulfate + copper. The more reactive metal displaces the less reactive one from its compound. That is a displacement reaction.

:::

## Explain: spotting the pattern


:::keep-together

### Three patterns

**Match each pattern to its name.**

A. Displacement

B. Decomposition

C. Synthesis

{{columns 60% 40%}}

{{rows 1}}

| Part | Letter |
|---|---|
| `A + B → AB` | |
| `AB → A + B` | |
| `A + BC → AC + B` | |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A + B → AB → C. Synthesis; AB → A + B → B. Decomposition; A + BC → AC + B → A. Displacement

Join, break apart, swap.

:::


:::keep-together

### Classify them

**Which kind of reaction is it?**

Groups: Synthesis, Decomposition, Displacement

- iron + sulfur → iron sulfide: {{field-15}}
- 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: {{field-15}}
- calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide: {{field-15}}
- 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electricity passed through water): {{field-15}}
- magnesium + copper sulfate → magnesium sulfate + copper: {{field-15}}
- chlorine + sodium bromide → sodium chloride + bromine: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** iron + sulfur → iron sulfide → Synthesis; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O → Synthesis; calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide → Decomposition; 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electricity passed through water) → Decomposition; magnesium + copper sulfate → magnesium sulfate + copper → Displacement; chlorine + sodium bromide → sodium chloride + bromine → Displacement

:::

### Predict the products

**Zinc is more reactive than copper. What are the products of zinc + copper sulfate?**

☐ Copper zinc + sulfate

☐ Zinc copper sulfate

☐ No reaction

☐ Zinc sulfate + copper

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Zinc sulfate + copper

The more reactive metal (zinc) takes the less reactive one's place: zinc + copper sulfate → zinc sulfate + copper.

:::


:::keep-together

### The other way round

**Copper is put into zinc sulfate solution. What happens?**

☐ Nothing: copper is less reactive, so it can't displace zinc

☐ The copper dissolves

☐ Copper sulfate and zinc form

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nothing: copper is less reactive, so it can't displace zinc

Reactivity decides whether a displacement happens.

:::


:::keep-together

### Synthesis product

**Aluminium burns in oxygen. It is a synthesis reaction (A + B → AB). What is the product?**

☐ aluminium and oxygen, mixed together

☐ aluminium oxide and water

☐ aluminium oxide

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** aluminium oxide

Two substances join into one: aluminium + oxygen → aluminium oxide.

:::


:::keep-together

### Heating copper carbonate

**Green copper carbonate powder is heated strongly. It turns black (copper oxide) and gives off carbon dioxide. Which kind of reaction is it, and what drives it?**

☐ Synthesis: the copper carbonate joins with the heat

☐ Decomposition, driven by heat: one compound breaks into two simpler substances

☐ Displacement: carbon dioxide takes copper's place

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Decomposition, driven by heat: one compound breaks into two simpler substances

copper carbonate → copper oxide + carbon dioxide. Many decompositions need energy put in: heat, as here and for limestone, or electricity, as for splitting water.

:::


:::keep-together

### Swapping partners

**In some reactions two compounds swap partners (AB + CD → AD + CB): a double displacement. Neutralisation is one. What are the products of hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide?**

☐ sodium hydroxide chloride

☐ sodium chloride + hydrogen

☐ sodium chloride + water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** sodium chloride + water

Sodium and hydrogen swap partners: sodium chloride and water (H + OH). acid + base → salt + water. A precipitation, where two solutions make a solid, swaps partners in the same way.

:::

## Elaborate: the other way round


:::keep-together

**On this board, copper is mixed with zinc oxide. Watch it for a while. Why does no reaction happen?**

☐ There isn't enough copper

☐ Copper is less reactive than zinc, so it can't take the oxygen from zinc

☐ The temperature is too low

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Copper is less reactive than zinc, so it can't take the oxygen from zinc

A displacement goes only from more reactive to less reactive. The reactivity series (potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium, zinc, iron, copper, silver, gold) predicts which way.

## Elaborate: making useful things


:::keep-together

### Iron from rock

**In a blast furnace, carbon takes oxygen from iron oxide: iron oxide + carbon → iron + carbon dioxide. Which kind of reaction is this, and why does it work?**

☐ Displacement: carbon is more reactive than iron, so it takes the oxygen

☐ Synthesis: iron and carbon join

☐ Decomposition: iron oxide breaks apart on its own

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Displacement: carbon is more reactive than iron, so it takes the oxygen

Metals less reactive than carbon (zinc, iron, copper) are extracted from their ores this way.

:::


:::keep-together

### Lime from limestone

**Builders heat limestone (calcium carbonate) to make lime (calcium oxide) for cement. Which kind of reaction is this?**

☐ Synthesis

☐ Decomposition

☐ Displacement

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Decomposition

calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide. Cement making releases a lot of carbon dioxide for this reason.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Synthesis**: substances join into one (A + B → AB), such as magnesium burning.
- **Decomposition**: one substance breaks into simpler ones (AB → A + B), such as hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen, often with heat or electricity.
- **Displacement**: a more reactive element takes a less reactive one's place (A + BC → AC + B). It doesn't happen the other way round. In a **double displacement** two compounds swap partners (AB + CD → AD + CB), as in neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water.
- Spot the pattern to classify a reaction and predict its products.
