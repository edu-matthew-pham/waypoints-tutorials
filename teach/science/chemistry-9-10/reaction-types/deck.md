# What kind of reaction is it?

Years 9 and 10 Chemistry · Reaction rates

<!-- Speaker note: Students identify synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions by their patterns, use reactivity to predict whether a displacement happens, and classify unfamiliar reactions and predict products (AC9S10U07, nodes 1 to 4). ## Today

**We are learning to** many reactions follow one of three patterns. Synthesis: two substances join into one (A + B → AB). Decomposition: one substance breaks into simpler ones (AB → A + B). Displacement: a more reactive element takes the place of a less reactive one (A + BC → AC + B). Spotting the pattern lets you predict the products.

- I can recognise synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions from their patterns.
- I can predict the products of a displacement reaction using reactivity.
- I can classify a reaction I haven't seen before.

## Why it matters

> Drop an iron nail into blue copper sulfate solution and it comes out coated in copper, while the blue slowly fades. Iron has pushed copper out of its compound.

## Engage: predict

**An iron nail is put in copper sulfate solution. Iron is more reactive than copper. What happens?**

- The copper sulfate turns into iron
- Nothing: metals don't react with solutions
- The iron and copper mix to make an alloy
- Iron takes copper's place: iron sulfate forms and copper coats the nail

## Three patterns

**Match each pattern to its name.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `A + B → AB` | A. Displacement |
| `AB → A + B` | B. Decomposition |
| `A + BC → AC + B` | C. Synthesis |

* Answer: A + B → AB → C. Synthesis; AB → A + B → B. Decomposition; A + BC → AC + B → A. Displacement

## Classify them

**Which kind of reaction is it?**

Groups: Synthesis · Decomposition · Displacement

iron + sulfur → iron sulfide · 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O · calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide · 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electricity passed through water) · magnesium + copper sulfate → magnesium sulfate + copper · chlorine + sodium bromide → sodium chloride + bromine

* Answer: iron + sulfur → iron sulfide → Synthesis; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O → Synthesis; calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide → Decomposition; 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electricity passed through water) → Decomposition; magnesium + copper sulfate → magnesium sulfate + copper → Displacement; chlorine + sodium bromide → sodium chloride + bromine → Displacement

**Zinc is more reactive than copper. What are the products of zinc + copper sulfate?**

- Copper zinc + sulfate
- Zinc copper sulfate
- No reaction

## The other way round

**Copper is put into zinc sulfate solution. What happens?**

- Nothing: copper is less reactive, so it can't displace zinc
- The copper dissolves
- Copper sulfate and zinc form

* Answer: Nothing: copper is less reactive, so it can't displace zinc

## Heating copper carbonate

**Green copper carbonate powder is heated strongly. It turns black (copper oxide) and gives off carbon dioxide. Which kind of reaction is it, and what drives it?**

- Synthesis: the copper carbonate joins with the heat
- Decomposition, driven by heat: one compound breaks into two simpler substances
- Displacement: carbon dioxide takes copper's place

* Answer: Decomposition, driven by heat: one compound breaks into two simpler substances

<!-- Speaker note: copper carbonate → copper oxide + carbon dioxide. Many decompositions need energy put in: heat, as here and for limestone, or electricity, as for splitting water. -->

## Swapping partners

**In some reactions two compounds swap partners (AB + CD → AD + CB): a double displacement. Neutralisation is one. What are the products of hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide?**

- sodium hydroxide chloride
- sodium chloride + hydrogen
- sodium chloride + water

* Answer: sodium chloride + water

## Iron from rock

**In a blast furnace, carbon takes oxygen from iron oxide: iron oxide + carbon → iron + carbon dioxide. Which kind of reaction is this, and why does it work?**

- Displacement: carbon is more reactive than iron, so it takes the oxygen
- Synthesis: iron and carbon join
- Decomposition: iron oxide breaks apart on its own

* Answer: Displacement: carbon is more reactive than iron, so it takes the oxygen

<!-- Speaker note: Metals less reactive than carbon (zinc, iron, copper) are extracted from their ores this way. -->

## Lime from limestone

**Builders heat limestone (calcium carbonate) to make lime (calcium oxide) for cement. Which kind of reaction is this?**

- Synthesis
- Decomposition
- Displacement

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Synthesis**: substances join into one (A + B → AB), such as magnesium burning.
- **Decomposition**: one substance breaks into simpler ones (AB → A + B), such as hydrogen peroxide → water + oxygen, often with heat or electricity.
- **Displacement**: a more reactive element takes a less reactive one's place (A + BC → AC + B). It doesn't happen the other way round. In a **double displacement** two compounds swap partners (AB + CD → AD + CB), as in neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water.
- Spot the pattern to classify a reaction and predict its products.
