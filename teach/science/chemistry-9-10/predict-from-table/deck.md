# Use the table to predict

Years 9 and 10 Chemistry · Atoms and the periodic table

## Today

**We are learning to** the periodic table is not a list to look up but a tool for prediction. An element's group gives its number of outer electrons, and so how reactive it is and what it bonds with; its period gives its number of shells. From the position alone you can predict whether an element is a metal, how it reacts and what its atom looks like.

- I can predict outer electrons and shells from group and period.
- I can predict reactivity and the ion an element forms.
- I can explain two properties with atomic structure.

## Why it matters

## Engage: predict

**You are told only that an unfamiliar element sits in group 1, period 4. What can you predict?**

- Nothing: you must look it up
- It is an unreactive gas
- It has 4 outer electrons
- It is a metal with one outer electron, which is very reactive, losing that electron to form a 1+ ion

## Down a group

**Which is more reactive, rubidium (group 1, period 5) or lithium (group 1, period 2)?**

- Lithium: it is smaller
- They are the same, because they are in the same group
- Rubidium: its outer electron is further from the nucleus and easier to lose

* Answer: Rubidium: its outer electron is further from the nucleus and easier to lose

<!-- Speaker note: Down group 1 there are more shells, so the outer electron is further away, held less tightly and lost more easily. -->

## Bonding

**Chlorine (group 17) forms compounds with sodium (group 1). Predict the ions.**

- Na⁺ and Cl⁻: sodium gives up its one outer electron and chlorine gains one to fill its outer shell
- Na²⁺ and Cl²⁻
- Na⁻ and Cl⁺

* Answer: Na⁺ and Cl⁻: sodium gives up its one outer electron and chlorine gains one to fill its outer shell

<!-- Speaker note: Group 1 loses 1 electron, group 17 gains 1, so they combine one to one: NaCl. -->

## Noble gases

**Why are the noble gases (group 18) almost unreactive?**

- They are too light to react
- They are gases, and gases don't react
- Their outer shells are full, so they have no need to gain, lose or share electrons

* Answer: Their outer shells are full, so they have no need to gain, lose or share electrons

<!-- Speaker note: A full outer shell is stable. Atoms of other elements react to reach that arrangement. -->

## Elaborate: a flame test

**Sodium salts give a yellow flame and copper salts a green flame. What does this tell you about the atoms?**

- The colour comes from the flame, not the metal
- Electrons jump between shells with fixed energies and give out light of a colour set by each element
- All metals burn the same

* Answer: Electrons jump between shells with fixed energies and give out light of a colour set by each element

<!-- Speaker note: When an electron falls from a higher to a lower shell it gives out light of one fixed colour. Each element has its own pattern, a fingerprint you can use to identify it. What this step is for: Flame tests are the classic evidence that atoms have electron shells with fixed energies. -->

## Elaborate: gaps in the table

**Mendeleev left gaps and predicted the properties of the missing elements, such as the one later named germanium. Why was this good evidence for his table?**

- The table was copied from another scientist
- He had already found the elements
- The predictions came true when the elements were found, so the pattern in the table was real

* Answer: The predictions came true when the elements were found, so the pattern in the table was real

<!-- Speaker note: A model that predicts something new, and is then confirmed, is more convincing than one that only fits what is already known. What this step is for: Mendeleev's gaps and predictions show the table working as a predictive model before atomic structure was understood. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The **group** gives the number of outer electrons; the **period** gives the number of shells.
- Outer electrons decide reactivity and bonding: group 1 loses one electron, group 17 gains one, group 18 is stable.
- Reactivity grows **down** group 1 and falls **down** group 17.
- The table is a **predictive model**: Mendeleev's gaps predicted elements before anyone found them.

## Exit ticket

**Predict the number of outer electrons and the ion formed by calcium, which is in group 2, period 4.**

* Two outer electrons; it loses them to form a Ca²⁺ ion.
