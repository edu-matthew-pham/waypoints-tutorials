# From uranium to lead

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A grain of uranium doesn't decay once. It sets off a chain of fourteen steps, passing through radon, a gas that seeps into some basements, and ends as lead. How can you follow it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students trace a decay sequence, track atomic and mass numbers at each step, and explain how the sequence ends at a stable nucleus (AC9S9U06, node 5, an integration node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can follow a decay chain from an unstable isotope to a stable one. I can work out the atomic and mass numbers after each alpha or beta decay. I can say why a chain stops.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

My prediction, and why:

**What happens:** Thorium-234 (atomic number 90, mass number 234), which is unstable too. The chain carries on through a series of alpha and beta decays until a stable nucleus, lead-206, is reached.

## Explain: tracking numbers


:::keep-together

### Atomic number

**Uranium-238 has atomic number 92. After one alpha decay, what is the new atomic number?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 90

An alpha particle carries away 2 protons, so 92 − 2 = 90 (thorium).

:::


:::keep-together

### Mass number

**And the new mass number, after the alpha decay from mass number 238?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 234

An alpha particle has 4 particles (2 protons and 2 neutrons), so 238 − 4 = 234.

:::


:::keep-together

### Beta step

**Thorium-234 (atomic number 90) then decays by beta emission. What is the new atomic number?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 91

A neutron becomes a proton, so the atomic number goes up by 1: 91 (protactinium). The mass number stays at 234.

:::


:::keep-together

### Total fall

**Uranium-238 ends as lead-206. By how much does the mass number fall in total?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 32

238 − 206 = 32.

:::


:::keep-together

### Alpha count

**Only alpha decays change the mass number, by 4 each time. How many alpha decays are in the chain?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

32 ÷ 4 = 8 alpha decays. They lower the atomic number by 16, from 92 to 76; beta decays raise it back to 82.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why it stops

**Why does the chain stop at lead-206?**

☐ It has become too small

☐ It has run out of energy

☐ Its nucleus has a stable balance of protons and neutrons, so it doesn't decay

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its nucleus has a stable balance of protons and neutrons, so it doesn't decay

Uranium-238's nucleus is too big, with too many neutrons, to last. Alpha decays make it smaller; beta decays turn spare neutrons into protons. When it reaches a mix of protons and neutrons that lasts, nothing more happens.

:::

## Elaborate: radon in the home


:::keep-together

**Radon-222 is a gas made in the uranium chain, and it seeps up from rock into some buildings. Why is it of more concern than the solid isotopes before it?**

☐ It is hotter

☐ It has a longer half-life than uranium

☐ As a gas it can be breathed in, and it decays to more radioactive solids inside the lungs

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** As a gas it can be breathed in, and it decays to more radioactive solids inside the lungs

The earlier solids stay in the rock. A gas gets out and can be breathed in, so its later decay products are released next to lung tissue. Good ventilation helps.

:::

## Elaborate: missing mass


:::keep-together

**The total mass of the products of a decay is slightly less than the mass of the original nucleus. What does physics say happened to the 'missing' mass?**

☐ It became the energy carried away by the radiation (E = mc²)

☐ It was destroyed

☐ It left as a gas

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It became the energy carried away by the radiation (E = mc²)

A tiny amount of mass becomes a large amount of energy, which is why a small amount of radioactive material releases so much energy.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **decay chain** is a series of decays that ends only at a **stable** nucleus.
- **Alpha** decay: atomic number −2, mass number −4. **Beta** decay: atomic number +1, mass number unchanged.
- Uranium-238 ends as lead-206 after **8 alpha** and **6 beta** decays.
- Alpha decays make the nucleus smaller and beta decays fix its balance of neutrons to protons, until it is stable.
