## Today

**We are learning to** isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) with different numbers of neutrons. They react in the same way but have different masses, and some are unstable: they are radioactive.

- I can define isotopes.
- I can say what is the same and what is different about two isotopes of an element.
- I can name isotopes (carbon-14) and recognise that some are unstable.

## Why it matters

> Every living thing contains a tiny amount of carbon that is slowly turning into nitrogen. That carbon is how scientists date bones and charcoal tens of thousands of years old.

## What happened?

* 2 more neutrons. Both are carbon because both have 6 protons. The mass number counts protons and neutrons, so 14 − 6 = 8 neutrons, against 6 in carbon-12. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon.

## What isotopes are

**What are isotopes?**

- Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons
- Atoms that have gained or lost electrons
- Atoms of different elements with the same mass number

* Answer: Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons

## Same or different

**Carbon-12 and carbon-14 atoms: same or different?**

Groups: Same · Different

Number of protons · Number of electrons (neutral atoms) · Number of neutrons · Mass number · How they react with oxygen · Whether the nucleus is stable

* Answer: Number of protons → Same; Number of electrons (neutral atoms) → Same; Number of neutrons → Different; Mass number → Different; How they react with oxygen → Same; Whether the nucleus is stable → Different

## Chlorine

**Chlorine has two common isotopes, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 (17 protons). How many neutrons are in chlorine-37?**

* Answer: 20

## Same reactions

**Why does carbon-14 burn in oxygen just like carbon-12?**

- The neutrons do the reacting
- Reactions depend on the electrons, and both have 6 arranged the same way
- It doesn't: carbon-14 does not burn

* Answer: Reactions depend on the electrons, and both have 6 arranged the same way

<!-- Speaker note: Isotopes of an element have the same number of electrons, so they react in the same way. Only the nucleus differs. -->

## Unstable isotopes

**Some isotopes are unstable. What does that mean?**

- The nucleus will decay, giving out radiation and often becoming a different element
- The electrons keep leaving the atom
- The atom will react violently with water

* Answer: The nucleus will decay, giving out radiation and often becoming a different element

<!-- Speaker note: An unstable (radioactive) nucleus has a mix of protons and neutrons that does not last. It decays, giving out radiation. Most elements have both stable and unstable isotopes. -->

## Living things

**Plants take in carbon dioxide, including a little made with carbon-14. Animals eat the plants. Why does that matter for dating old bones?**

- Every living thing has the same small share of carbon-14; after death no new carbon-14 comes in, and what is there slowly decays
- Bones make carbon-14 after death
- Carbon-14 turns into carbon-12 as bones age

* Answer: Every living thing has the same small share of carbon-14; after death no new carbon-14 comes in, and what is there slowly decays

## The average

**About 3 in every 4 chlorine atoms are chlorine-35, and 1 in 4 is chlorine-37. What is the average mass number? (to 1 decimal place)**

* Answer: 35.5

<!-- Speaker note: (3 × 35 + 1 × 37) ÷ 4 = 142 ÷ 4 = 35.5. That is why the periodic table gives chlorine a relative atomic mass of 35.5. -->

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Isotopes** are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) with different numbers of neutrons, so different mass numbers. We name them by mass number: carbon-12, carbon-13, carbon-14.
- Isotopes have the same number of electrons, so they react in the same way.
- Some isotopes are **stable**. Others are **unstable** (radioactive): their nuclei decay, giving out radiation.
- Carbon-14 is made in the air from nitrogen-14 and taken in by living things, which is why it can date things that were once alive.

## Exit ticket

**Uranium-235 and uranium-238 both have 92 protons. How many neutrons does each have, and are they the same element?**

* 143 and 146 neutrons. Both are uranium (92 protons): they are isotopes.
