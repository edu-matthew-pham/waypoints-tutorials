# Why do elements in a group behave alike?

Years 9 and 10 Chemistry · Atoms and the periodic table

## Today

**We are learning to** elements in the same group have the same number of outer-shell electrons, so they react in similar ways. Group 1 metals lose their one outer electron easily; group 17 non-metals gain one easily; group 18 has full shells and hardly reacts.

- I can explain that elements in the same group have the same number of outer-shell electrons.
- I can use outer electrons to explain the properties of a group.
- I can contrast a reactive group with the noble gases.

> Lithium, sodium and potassium look nothing alike in a jar, yet drop any of them in water and they fizz across the surface. Why do they behave the same?

## Sort by group

**Which group does each belong to?**

Groups: Group 1 · Group 2 · Group 17 · Group 18

Lithium (2, 1) · Magnesium (2, 8, 2) · Fluorine (2, 7) · Chlorine (2, 8, 7) · Neon (2, 8) · Argon (2, 8, 8) · Potassium (2, 8, 8, 1)

* Answer: Lithium (2, 1) → Group 1; Magnesium (2, 8, 2) → Group 2; Fluorine (2, 7) → Group 17; Chlorine (2, 8, 7) → Group 17; Neon (2, 8) → Group 18; Argon (2, 8, 8) → Group 18; Potassium (2, 8, 8, 1) → Group 1

## Noble gases

**Why do the noble gases (group 18) hardly react?**

- They have no electrons
- They are gases
- Their outer shells are full, so they don't tend to lose, gain or share electrons

* Answer: Their outer shells are full, so they don't tend to lose, gain or share electrons

## Elaborate: down the group

**Potassium reacts with water much more violently than lithium. Why, when both have one outer electron?**

- Potassium's outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is held less tightly and lost more easily
- Potassium has more outer electrons
- Potassium is heavier

* Answer: Potassium's outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is held less tightly and lost more easily

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Elements in the same **group** have the same number of **outer-shell electrons**, so they react in similar ways.
- **Group 1** (alkali metals): one outer electron, easily lost: very reactive metals.
- **Group 17** (halogens): seven outer electrons, one short of full: very reactive non-metals.
- **Group 18** (noble gases): full outer shells: hardly react.
- The **period** (row) tells you how many shells an atom has.
