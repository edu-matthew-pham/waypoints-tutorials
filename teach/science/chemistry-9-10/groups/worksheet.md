# Why do elements in a group behave alike?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Lithium, sodium and potassium look nothing alike in a jar, yet drop any of them in water and they fizz across the surface. Why do they behave the same?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain why elements in the same group have similar chemical properties using outer-shell electrons, contrasting group 1, group 17 and the noble gases (AC9S10U06, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain that elements in the same group have the same number of outer-shell electrons. I can use outer electrons to explain the properties of a group. I can contrast a reactive group with the noble gases.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Sodium and chlorine

**Why do sodium (group 1) and chlorine (group 17) react together so readily?**

☐ Sodium loses one electron easily and chlorine gains one easily

☐ They have the same number of outer electrons

☐ They are both metals

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sodium loses one electron easily and chlorine gains one easily

Each ends up with a full outer shell: sodium by losing one electron, chlorine by gaining one. They form sodium chloride (salt).

## Elaborate: down the group


:::keep-together

**Potassium reacts with water much more violently than lithium. Why, when both have one outer electron?**

☐ Potassium's outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is held less tightly and lost more easily

☐ Potassium has more outer electrons

☐ Potassium is heavier

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Potassium's outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is held less tightly and lost more easily

More shells means the outer electron is further out and more shielded by the inner electrons: easier to lose, so more reactive.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Elements in the same **group** have the same number of **outer-shell electrons**, so they react in similar ways.
- **Group 1** (alkali metals): one outer electron, easily lost: very reactive metals.
- **Group 17** (halogens): seven outer electrons, one short of full: very reactive non-metals.
- **Group 18** (noble gases): full outer shells: hardly react.
- The **period** (row) tells you how many shells an atom has.
