# Where do the atoms go?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A log burns down to a handful of ash. Where did the rest of the log go?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students model a reaction as atoms rearranging, count atoms on both sides, and connect the unchanged atoms to conservation of mass (AC9S9U07, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can use a model to show a reaction with the same atoms before and after. I can count the atoms on each side of an equation and check they match. I can explain that atoms are rearranged, not made or destroyed, so mass is conserved.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Methane (natural gas, CH₄) burns in a gas stove. What happens to the carbon and hydrogen atoms in the methane?**

☐ They turn into heat and light

☐ They float away as smoke, still as methane

☐ They join oxygen atoms to make new particles

☐ They are destroyed by the flame

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** They join oxygen atoms: carbon ends up in carbon dioxide (CO₂) and hydrogen in water (H₂O). Every atom is still there.

:::

Try it on the board in the tutorial. Was your prediction right? What was different?

## Explain: rearranged, not destroyed


:::keep-together

### The atom count

**What happened to the number of each type of atom during the reaction?**

☐ It stayed the same

☐ It went up as new particles formed

☐ It went down as the methane was used up

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It stayed the same

Particles change; atoms don't. Bonds break and new bonds form.

:::


:::keep-together

### Count one side

**In CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, how many oxygen atoms are on the left side?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4

2O₂ is two oxygen particles, each with 2 atoms: 4 oxygen atoms.

:::


:::keep-together

### Count the other side

**How many oxygen atoms are on the right side?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4

CO₂ has 2, and 2H₂O has 2 (one in each water): 4.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why mass is conserved

**Why must the mass of the products equal the mass of the reactants?**

☐ The same atoms are there, just rearranged, and each atom keeps its mass

☐ The products are the same substances as the reactants

☐ Energy is conserved

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The same atoms are there, just rearranged, and each atom keeps its mass

Same atoms, same mass. This is the law of conservation of mass.

:::


## balanced or not?


:::keep-together

### Balanced or not

**Is each equation balanced (the same atoms on both sides)?**

Groups: Balanced, Not balanced

- H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl: {{field-15}}
- H₂ + O₂ → H₂O: {{field-15}}
- 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: {{field-15}}
- C + O₂ → CO₂: {{field-15}}
- N₂ + H₂ → NH₃: {{field-15}}
- CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl → Balanced; H₂ + O₂ → H₂O → Not balanced; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O → Balanced; C + O₂ → CO₂ → Balanced; N₂ + H₂ → NH₃ → Not balanced; CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O → Not balanced

:::


:::keep-together

### Changing the formula

**To balance H₂ + O₂ → H₂O, a student changes it to H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂. What is wrong?**

☐ Nothing: it balances now

☐ You can't put a 2 after O

☐ H₂O₂ is a different substance (hydrogen peroxide), not water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** H₂O₂ is a different substance (hydrogen peroxide), not water

Balance by changing how many particles (the big numbers in front), never the formulas: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

:::

## Elaborate: making water


:::keep-together

### Predict the amount

**In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 12 hydrogen particles react with 6 oxygen particles. How many water particles can form?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12

Every 2 H₂ and 1 O₂ make 2 H₂O. Six times over: 12 H₂O, with 24 H and 12 O atoms, the same as at the start.

## Elaborate: open and closed


:::keep-together

### An open fire

**Wood burned on an open fire leaves ash that weighs much less than the wood. Does that break conservation of mass?**

☐ Yes: the energy given out had mass

☐ Yes: the burnt atoms were destroyed

☐ No: carbon dioxide and water vapour went into the air, carrying atoms away

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: carbon dioxide and water vapour went into the air, carrying atoms away

In an open container, gaseous products escape. In a sealed one, the mass doesn't change.

:::


:::keep-together

### A rusting nail

**An iron nail gets heavier as it rusts. Where does the extra mass come from?**

☐ Water makes it heavier

☐ Rust is a heavier kind of iron

☐ Oxygen atoms from the air join the iron

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Oxygen atoms from the air join the iron

Iron + oxygen → iron oxide. The mass gained is the mass of the oxygen atoms.

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- In a chemical reaction, **atoms are rearranged**: bonds break and new bonds form. No atom is made or destroyed.
- The same atoms means the **same mass**: the law of **conservation of mass**.
- A **balanced equation** has the same number of each type of atom on both sides. Balance it by changing the numbers in front, never the formulas.
- Mass seems to change only when a gas escapes or joins from the air.
