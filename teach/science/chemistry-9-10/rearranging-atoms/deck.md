# Where do the atoms go?

Years 9 and 10 Chemistry · Equations and conservation of mass

<!-- Speaker note: Students model a reaction as atoms rearranging, count atoms on both sides, and connect the unchanged atoms to conservation of mass (AC9S9U07, node 2, a hinge node). ## Today

**We are learning to** in a chemical reaction atoms are rearranged into new particles, never made or destroyed. Counting atoms before and after shows why mass is conserved and why equations must balance.

- I can use a model to show a reaction with the same atoms before and after.
- I can count the atoms on each side of an equation and check they match.
- I can explain that atoms are rearranged, not made or destroyed, so mass is conserved.

## Why mass is conserved

**Why must the mass of the products equal the mass of the reactants?**

- The same atoms are there, just rearranged, and each atom keeps its mass
- The products are the same substances as the reactants
- Energy is conserved

* Answer: The same atoms are there, just rearranged, and each atom keeps its mass

<!-- Speaker note: Same atoms, same mass. This is the law of conservation of mass. -->

## Balanced or not

**Is each equation balanced (the same atoms on both sides)?**

## Changing the formula

**To balance H₂ + O₂ → H₂O, a student changes it to H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂. What is wrong?**

- Nothing: it balances now
- You can't put a 2 after O
- H₂O₂ is a different substance (hydrogen peroxide), not water

* Answer: H₂O₂ is a different substance (hydrogen peroxide), not water

## An open fire

**Wood burned on an open fire leaves ash that weighs much less than the wood. Does that break conservation of mass?**

- Yes: the energy given out had mass
- Yes: the burnt atoms were destroyed
- No: carbon dioxide and water vapour went into the air, carrying atoms away

* Answer: No: carbon dioxide and water vapour went into the air, carrying atoms away

<!-- Speaker note: In an open container, gaseous products escape. In a sealed one, the mass doesn't change. -->

## A rusting nail

**An iron nail gets heavier as it rusts. Where does the extra mass come from?**

- Water makes it heavier
- Rust is a heavier kind of iron
- Oxygen atoms from the air join the iron

* Answer: Oxygen atoms from the air join the iron

<!-- Speaker note: Iron + oxygen → iron oxide. The mass gained is the mass of the oxygen atoms. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- In a chemical reaction, **atoms are rearranged**: bonds break and new bonds form. No atom is made or destroyed.
- The same atoms means the **same mass**: the law of **conservation of mass**.
- A **balanced equation** has the same number of each type of atom on both sides. Balance it by changing the numbers in front, never the formulas.
- Mass seems to change only when a gas escapes or joins from the air.

## Exit ticket

**When charcoal burns in a sealed jar, does the total mass change? Why?**

* No: the carbon and oxygen atoms are rearranged into carbon dioxide, which stays in the jar. Same atoms, same mass.
