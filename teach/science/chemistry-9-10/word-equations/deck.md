# What goes in, what comes out?

Years 9 and 10 Chemistry · Equations and conservation of mass

<!-- Speaker note: Students identify reactants and products, explain that products are new substances with different properties, and write word equations that include every product (AC9S9U07, nodes 1 and 3). ## Today

**We are learning to** in a chemical reaction the reactants are used up and new substances, the products, are made. A word equation names them: reactants → products. It must include every product (burning a fuel makes water as well as carbon dioxide), but it shows no amounts or atoms.

- I can name the reactants and products of a reaction.
- I can write a word equation that includes every product.
- I can say what a word equation shows and does not show.

## What happened?

* methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. Methane contains carbon and hydrogen: the carbon ends up in carbon dioxide and the hydrogen in water (the drops on the cold spoon). Oxygen from the air is a reactant too. Heat is energy given out, not a substance, so it is not in the equation.

## New substances

**Why are carbon dioxide and water counted as new substances, and not just methane and oxygen in a new shape?**

- They have different properties: methane burns and water puts fires out
- They are heavier
- They are gases

* Answer: They have different properties: methane burns and water puts fires out

## Sodium in nature

**Sodium is never found as a pure metal in nature, but there is plenty of sodium chloride in seawater. Why?**

- Sodium dissolves in seawater as a metal
- Sodium is a rare element
- Sodium reacts so readily that any sodium has long since reacted, leaving it in compounds

* Answer: Sodium reacts so readily that any sodium has long since reacted, leaving it in compounds

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- In a chemical reaction the **reactants** are used up and new substances, the **products**, are made. Products have different properties.
- A **word equation** names them: reactants → products. Read the arrow as "react to make".
- Include every reactant and product: burning a fuel that contains hydrogen makes water as well as carbon dioxide; oxygen from the air is a reactant.
- Heat and light are energy, not substances, so they are not in the equation.
- A word equation shows no amounts and no atoms: formulas and balanced equations do.
