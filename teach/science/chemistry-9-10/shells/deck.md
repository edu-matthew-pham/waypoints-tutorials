# How are the electrons arranged?

Years 9 and 10 Chemistry · Atoms and the periodic table

<!-- Speaker note: Students arrange the electrons of elements 1 to 20 in shells (2, 8, 8, 2) and identify the outer-shell electrons, the variable the periodic table depends on (AC9S10U06, node 1, a hinge node). About 25 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 10 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 4 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** electrons fill shells around the nucleus in order: 2 in the first, 8 in the second, 8 in the third (for the first 20 elements). The electrons in the outer shell decide how an element behaves.

- I can arrange the electrons of any element from 1 to 20 in shells.
- I can state the capacities of the first three shells (2, 8, 8).
- I can find the number of outer-shell electrons.

## Why it matters

> Neon glows in signs but never reacts with anything. Sodium, one proton heavier, catches fire in water. The difference is one electron.

## Elaborate: coloured flames

**Heated in a flame, sodium glows yellow and potassium lilac. Each element always gives the same colours. What does that suggest about electrons?**

- They sit at fixed energy levels, different in each element; jumping between them gives out light of particular colours
- The flame changes colour with temperature, not the element
- Electrons are coloured

* Answer: They sit at fixed energy levels, different in each element; jumping between them gives out light of particular colours

<!-- Speaker note: Each element's emission spectrum is a fingerprint of its electron energy levels: evidence for Bohr's shells. What this step is for: Flame colours are evidence for fixed energy levels: the observation that pushed the atomic model to shells. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Electrons fill **shells** in order: **2**, then **8**, then **8** (then 2 more for potassium and calcium).
- The **outer shell** is the outermost occupied one; its electrons decide how the element reacts.
- A full shell is followed by a new shell, which is why rows of the periodic table end and begin.
