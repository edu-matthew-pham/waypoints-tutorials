# How are the electrons arranged?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Neon glows in signs but never reacts with anything. Sodium, one proton heavier, catches fire in water. The difference is one electron.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students arrange the electrons of elements 1 to 20 in shells (2, 8, 8, 2) and identify the outer-shell electrons, the variable the periodic table depends on (AC9S10U06, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can arrange the electrons of any element from 1 to 20 in shells. I can state the capacities of the first three shells (2, 8, 8). I can find the number of outer-shell electrons.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Sodium has 11 electrons. How do you predict they are arranged around the nucleus?**

☐ All 11 in one shell

☐ 2, 8, 1 (three shells)

☐ 8, 3

☐ 2, 9 (two shells)

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 2, 8, 1. The first shell holds 2 and the second 8, so the 11th electron starts a third shell. That single outer electron explains a lot about sodium.

:::

☐ Into the third shell: 2, 8, 9

:::

A full shell, then a new one: that's why potassium starts a new row of the periodic table.

## Elaborate: coloured flames


:::keep-together

**Heated in a flame, sodium glows yellow and potassium lilac. Each element always gives the same colours. What does that suggest about electrons?**

☐ They sit at fixed energy levels, different in each element; jumping between them gives out light of particular colours

☐ The flame changes colour with temperature, not the element

☐ Electrons are coloured

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They sit at fixed energy levels, different in each element; jumping between them gives out light of particular colours

Each element's emission spectrum is a fingerprint of its electron energy levels: evidence for Bohr's shells.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Electrons fill **shells** in order: **2**, then **8**, then **8** (then 2 more for potassium and calcium).
- The **outer shell** is the outermost occupied one; its electrons decide how the element reacts.
- A full shell is followed by a new shell, which is why rows of the periodic table end and begin.
