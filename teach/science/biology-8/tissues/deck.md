# What is a tissue?

Year 8 Biology · From cells to organs

<!-- Speaker note: Students define a tissue as a group of similar specialised cells doing a shared job, give animal and plant examples, and explain how a tissue does what single cells can't (AC9S8U02, node 2). About 23 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 10 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 3 min, Wider about 3 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** a tissue is a group of similar specialised cells working together to do one job. Muscle tissue contracts, epithelial tissue lines and protects, xylem carries water, phloem carries sugar. A tissue can do what a single cell can't do on its own.

- I can define a tissue.
- I can name an animal tissue and a plant tissue and the job of each.
- I can explain how a tissue does a job that one cell can't.

## What a tissue is

**What is a tissue?**

- A group of similar specialised cells working together to do one job
- Any group of cells in the same place
- One very large specialised cell

* Answer: A group of similar specialised cells working together to do one job

## Tissue jobs

**Match each tissue to its job.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Muscle tissue` | A. Carries water up a plant from the roots |
| `Epithelial tissue` | B. Carries electrical signals |
| `Nervous tissue` | C. Contracts to move the body or pump blood |
| `Xylem` | D. Carries sugar around a plant |
| `Phloem` | E. Lines and protects surfaces, inside and out |

* Answer: Muscle tissue → C. Contracts to move the body or pump blood; Epithelial tissue → E. Lines and protects surfaces, inside and out; Nervous tissue → B. Carries electrical signals; Xylem → A. Carries water up a plant from the roots; Phloem → D. Carries sugar around a plant

## Why a tissue

**Why does the body use muscle tissue, not just single muscle cells?**

- Millions of cells pulling together can make a force no single cell could
- Tissues don't need energy
- Single muscle cells can't contract

* Answer: Millions of cells pulling together can make a force no single cell could

## Xylem tubes

**Xylem is made of long, hollow cells joined end to end. How does that suit its job?**

- Each hollow cell stores water for itself
- Hollow cells are lighter, so the plant can grow taller
- Together they form tubes that water can flow through from the roots to the leaves

* Answer: Together they form tubes that water can flow through from the roots to the leaves

<!-- Speaker note: No single cell could carry water up a tree. Joined end to end, xylem cells make a continuous pipe. -->

## Scar tissue

**After a heart attack, part of the cardiac muscle can be replaced by tough scar tissue (connective tissue). Why does the heart pump less well?**

- The nervous tissue stops working everywhere
- Scar tissue's cells can't contract, so that part no longer helps squeeze
- Scar tissue is heavier than muscle

* Answer: Scar tissue's cells can't contract, so that part no longer helps squeeze

<!-- Speaker note: A tissue's job comes from its cells. Replace muscle cells with connective tissue cells and that part of the wall can no longer contract. -->

**Revision summary**

- A **tissue** is a group of similar specialised cells working together to do one job.
- Animal tissues include muscle (contracts), epithelial (lines and protects), nervous (carries signals) and blood. Plant tissues include xylem (water), phloem (sugar) and palisade (photosynthesis).
- A tissue can do what one cell can't do alone.
- Next: **organs** are made of several tissues working together.
