# What is an organ made of?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A leaf looks like one green sheet. Slice it thinly and look through a microscope, and you find at least five different layers, each doing a different job.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe an organ as several tissue types working together and explain each tissue's contribution (AC9S8U02, node 3, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name two tissues in an organ and explain how each helps. I can explain why organs need more than one tissue. I can put cells, tissues, organs, systems and organisms in order.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### A blocked tissue

**A wilt disease blocks the xylem in a leaf. What happens to the leaf?**

☐ Nothing much: the other tissues still do their own jobs

☐ The palisade cells take in water from the air instead

☐ Its cells run short of water, so photosynthesis slows and the leaf wilts

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its cells run short of water, so photosynthesis slows and the leaf wilts

A change in one tissue changes the whole organ, because its tissues depend on each other.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.**
- A **tissue** is a group of similar cells doing one job; an **organ** is several tissues working together.
- A leaf: epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, xylem and phloem. The heart: cardiac muscle, lining, connective tissue, nervous tissue.
- No single tissue can do everything an organ needs.
