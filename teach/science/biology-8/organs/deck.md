# What is an organ made of?

Year 8 Biology · From cells to organs

<!-- Speaker note: Students describe an organ as several tissue types working together and explain each tissue's contribution (AC9S8U02, node 3, a hinge node). About 25 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 9 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 4 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** an organ is made of several different tissues working together. A leaf has epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll and vascular tissue; the heart has cardiac muscle, a lining, connective tissue and nervous tissue.

- I can name two tissues in an organ and explain how each helps.
- I can explain why organs need more than one tissue.
- I can put cells, tissues, organs, systems and organisms in order.

## Why it matters

> A leaf looks like one green sheet. Slice it thinly and look through a microscope, and you find at least five different layers, each doing a different job.

## Leaf tissues

**Match each leaf tissue to its job.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Palisade mesophyll` | A. Brings water from the roots |
| `Spongy mesophyll` | B. Air spaces let gases reach every cell |
| `Xylem` | C. Protects the leaf |
| `Phloem` | D. Carries sugar to the rest of the plant |
| `Epidermis` | E. Most photosynthesis, near the light |

* Answer: Palisade mesophyll → E. Most photosynthesis, near the light; Spongy mesophyll → B. Air spaces let gases reach every cell; Xylem → A. Brings water from the roots; Phloem → D. Carries sugar to the rest of the plant; Epidermis → C. Protects the leaf

## Why several

**Why does a leaf need more than one tissue?**

- It doesn't; the extra tissues are spare
- Photosynthesis needs light, water, gas exchange, protection and transport: no single tissue can do all of them
- More tissues make it greener

* Answer: Photosynthesis needs light, water, gas exchange, protection and transport: no single tissue can do all of them

## A blocked tissue

**A wilt disease blocks the xylem in a leaf. What happens to the leaf?**

- Nothing much: the other tissues still do their own jobs
- The palisade cells take in water from the air instead
- Its cells run short of water, so photosynthesis slows and the leaf wilts

* Answer: Its cells run short of water, so photosynthesis slows and the leaf wilts

<!-- Speaker note: A change in one tissue changes the whole organ, because its tissues depend on each other. -->

## Only muscle?

**If the heart were made only of cardiac muscle, what would go wrong?**

- It would beat too slowly
- Nothing: muscle does it all
- No smooth lining (blood would clot), no valves (blood would flow backwards), no nerves to adjust how fast it beats

* Answer: No smooth lining (blood would clot), no valves (blood would flow backwards), no nerves to adjust how fast it beats

## Elaborate: when a tissue fails

**In atherosclerosis, fatty deposits and thickened tissue build up in artery walls. How does this affect the circulatory system?**

- Arteries narrow, so less blood reaches organs like the heart muscle, which can cause a heart attack
- Arteries get wider and blood flows faster
- It only affects the blood, not the organs

* Answer: Arteries narrow, so less blood reaches organs like the heart muscle, which can cause a heart attack

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organism.**
- A **tissue** is a group of similar cells doing one job; an **organ** is several tissues working together.
- A leaf: epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, xylem and phloem. The heart: cardiac muscle, lining, connective tissue, nervous tissue.
- No single tissue can do everything an organ needs.
