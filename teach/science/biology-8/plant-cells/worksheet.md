# What makes a plant cell different?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A tree can stand 80 metres tall without a single bone. A lettuce leaf goes limp in the fridge, then crisp again in cold water. Both come down to parts that only plant cells have.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify the cell wall, chloroplasts and large vacuole as plant-only structures and explain each, describe mitochondria and ribosomes in both cell types, and link a cell's organelles to its job (AC9S8U01, nodes 4 and 5).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the parts only plant cells have and say what each is for. I can say which parts plant and animal cells share. I can explain why some cells have more of one part than others.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### No chloroplasts

**Why don't animal cells need chloroplasts?**

☐ Animals get their food by eating, so they don't make it from light

☐ Animal cells are too small for chloroplasts

☐ Animals live where there is no light

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Animals get their food by eating, so they don't make it from light

Plants make their own food by photosynthesis; animals eat. Each cell's parts match how the organism lives.

:::


:::keep-together

### A limp lettuce

**A lettuce leaf goes limp when it loses water. Which part explains this?**

☐ The vacuoles shrink, so the cells push less against their walls

☐ The cell walls dissolve

☐ The chloroplasts stop working

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The vacuoles shrink, so the cells push less against their walls

A full vacuole presses the cell against its wall and keeps it firm. Lose water and the cell goes soft: the leaf wilts. Put it in water and it firms up again.

:::


:::keep-together

### Many mitochondria

**A muscle cell has far more mitochondria than a skin cell. Why?**

☐ Mitochondria make muscles strong

☐ Muscle cells are bigger

☐ Contracting needs a lot of energy, and mitochondria release it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Contracting needs a lot of energy, and mitochondria release it

Cells have the parts their job needs, in the numbers it needs: same DNA, different organelles.

## Elaborate: a root hair cell


:::keep-together

### No chloroplasts

**Root hair cells have no chloroplasts. Why not?**

☐ They are not plant cells

☐ Chloroplasts would make them too heavy

☐ They are underground, with no light to photosynthesise; their job is taking in water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They are underground, with no light to photosynthesise; their job is taking in water

A cell has the parts its job needs. Chloroplasts in the dark would be useless.

## Elaborate: energy for movement


:::keep-together

### Protein factories

**Cells in a gland that makes digestive enzymes (proteins) are packed with one organelle. Which?**

☐ Ribosomes, which make proteins

☐ Chloroplasts

☐ Vacuoles

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Ribosomes, which make proteins

Enzymes are proteins, and ribosomes make proteins. Like mitochondria in muscle, the number of an organelle follows the job.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Only plant cells have a **cell wall** (support), **chloroplasts** (photosynthesis) and a **large vacuole** (keeps the cell firm, stores sap).
- Plant and animal cells both have a nucleus, membrane, cytoplasm, **mitochondria** (release energy from food) and **ribosomes** (make proteins).
- How many of each part a cell has depends on its job: muscle cells have many mitochondria; root hair cells have no chloroplasts.
