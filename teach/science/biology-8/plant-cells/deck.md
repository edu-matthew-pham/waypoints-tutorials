# What makes a plant cell different?

Year 8 Biology · Cells

<!-- Speaker note: Students identify the cell wall, chloroplasts and large vacuole as plant-only structures and explain each, describe mitochondria and ribosomes in both cell types, and link a cell's organelles to its job (AC9S8U01, nodes 4 and 5). ## Today

**We are learning to** plant cells have three parts animal cells don't: a cell wall for support, chloroplasts for photosynthesis and a large vacuole that keeps the cell firm. Both kinds have mitochondria (for energy) and ribosomes (for making proteins), and how many of each a cell has depends on its job.

- I can name the parts only plant cells have and say what each is for.
- I can say which parts plant and animal cells share.
- I can explain why some cells have more of one part than others.

## Why it matters

> A tree can stand 80 metres tall without a single bone. A lettuce leaf goes limp in the fridge, then crisp again in cold water. Both come down to parts that only plant cells have.

## Jobs

**Match each part to its job.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Cell wall` | A. Holds cell sap and keeps the cell firm |
| `Chloroplast` | B. Photosynthesis: captures light to make glucose |
| `Large vacuole` | C. Make proteins |
| `Mitochondria` | D. Release energy from food (respiration) |
| `Ribosomes` | E. Rigid support, so the plant holds its shape without a skeleton |

* Answer: Cell wall → E. Rigid support, so the plant holds its shape without a skeleton; Chloroplast → B. Photosynthesis: captures light to make glucose; Large vacuole → A. Holds cell sap and keeps the cell firm; Mitochondria → D. Release energy from food (respiration); Ribosomes → C. Make proteins

## No chloroplasts

**Why don't animal cells need chloroplasts?**

- Animals get their food by eating, so they don't make it from light
- Animal cells are too small for chloroplasts
- Animals live where there is no light

* Answer: Animals get their food by eating, so they don't make it from light

<!-- Speaker note: Plants make their own food by photosynthesis; animals eat. Each cell's parts match how the organism lives. -->

## A limp lettuce

**A lettuce leaf goes limp when it loses water. Which part explains this?**

- The vacuoles shrink, so the cells push less against their walls
- The cell walls dissolve
- The chloroplasts stop working

* Answer: The vacuoles shrink, so the cells push less against their walls

<!-- Speaker note: A full vacuole presses the cell against its wall and keeps it firm. Lose water and the cell goes soft: the leaf wilts. Put it in water and it firms up again. -->

## Many mitochondria

**A muscle cell has far more mitochondria than a skin cell. Why?**

- Mitochondria make muscles strong
- Muscle cells are bigger
- Contracting needs a lot of energy, and mitochondria release it

* Answer: Contracting needs a lot of energy, and mitochondria release it

**Cells in a gland that makes digestive enzymes (proteins) are packed with one organelle. Which?**

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Only plant cells have a **cell wall** (support), **chloroplasts** (photosynthesis) and a **large vacuole** (keeps the cell firm, stores sap).
- Plant and animal cells both have a nucleus, membrane, cytoplasm, **mitochondria** (release energy from food) and **ribosomes** (make proteins).
- How many of each part a cell has depends on its job: muscle cells have many mitochondria; root hair cells have no chloroplasts.
