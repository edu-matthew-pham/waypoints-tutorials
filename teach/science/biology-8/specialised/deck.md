# Why are cells different shapes?

Year 8 Biology · From cells to organs

<!-- Speaker note: Students explain that cells with the same DNA specialise, and describe how structural features of specialised cells serve their functions (AC9S8U02, node 1, a hinge node). About 25 minutes (Engage and Explore 9 min · Explain 8 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 2 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** almost all your cells have the same DNA, but they specialise: each develops a shape and contents that suit its job, like a red blood cell packed with haemoglobin or a nerve cell with a long axon.

- I can describe two features of a specialised cell and explain how each helps.
- I can explain that cells with the same DNA can specialise for different jobs.
- I can link a cell's contents (such as mitochondria) to its job.

## Why it matters

> A nerve cell in your leg can be a metre long. A red blood cell is a tiny squashed disc with no nucleus. Same DNA, same body. Why so different?

## Feature and function

**Match each feature to how it helps.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Red blood cell: no nucleus` | A. Carries signals a long way |
| `Nerve cell: long axon` | B. Large surface for absorbing water |
| `Muscle cell: many mitochondria` | C. Releases lots of energy for contracting |
| `Root hair cell: long extension` | D. More room for haemoglobin to carry oxygen |

* Answer: Red blood cell: no nucleus → D. More room for haemoglobin to carry oxygen; Nerve cell: long axon → A. Carries signals a long way; Muscle cell: many mitochondria → C. Releases lots of energy for contracting; Root hair cell: long extension → B. Large surface for absorbing water

## Root hairs

**Why do root hair cells have no chloroplasts?**

- They are underground, where there is no light for photosynthesis
- Roots take in their food from the soil, so they don't need to make any
- Only leaves are allowed chloroplasts

* Answer: They are underground, where there is no light for photosynthesis

## Specialised

**What does 'specialised' mean for a cell?**

- It has different DNA
- Its shape and parts suit one particular job
- It is bigger than other cells

* Answer: Its shape and parts suit one particular job

## Elaborate: design a cell

**Cells lining your small intestine absorb digested food. Which feature would you expect them to have?**

- A long tail to swim
- Tiny folds (microvilli) on the surface, for a much larger area
- No membrane, so food gets in easily

* Answer: Tiny folds (microvilli) on the surface, for a much larger area

## Elaborate: energy-hungry cells

**Sperm cells and muscle cells both have many mitochondria. What do the two jobs have in common?**

- Both make proteins
- Both store food
- Both need lots of energy for movement

* Answer: Both need lots of energy for movement

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Nearly all your cells have the **same DNA**; each **specialised** cell uses different instructions.
- A specialised cell's **shape and parts suit its job**: red blood cells (no nucleus, haemoglobin), nerve cells (long axon), muscle cells (many mitochondria), root hair cells (long extension, no chloroplasts).
