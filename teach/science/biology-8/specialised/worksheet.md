# Why are cells different shapes?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A nerve cell in your leg can be a metre long. A red blood cell is a tiny squashed disc with no nucleus. Same DNA, same body. Why so different?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain that cells with the same DNA specialise, and describe how structural features of specialised cells serve their functions (AC9S8U02, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe two features of a specialised cell and explain how each helps. I can explain that cells with the same DNA can specialise for different jobs. I can link a cell's contents (such as mitochondria) to its job.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Nearly all your cells have the **same DNA**; each **specialised** cell uses different instructions.
- A specialised cell's **shape and parts suit its job**: red blood cells (no nucleus, haemoglobin), nerve cells (long axon), muscle cells (many mitochondria), root hair cells (long extension, no chloroplasts).
