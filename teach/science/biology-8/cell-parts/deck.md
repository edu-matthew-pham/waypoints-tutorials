# What does every cell have?

Year 8 Biology · Cells

<!-- Speaker note: Students identify the nucleus, cell membrane and cytoplasm in plant and animal cells and describe each one's function (AC9S8U01, node 3, a hinge node). About 20 minutes (Engage and Explore 6 min · Explain 8 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 4 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** plant and animal cells share three parts: the nucleus (the control centre, holding DNA), the cell membrane (controls what gets in and out) and the cytoplasm (where reactions happen).

- I can state the function of the nucleus, cell membrane and cytoplasm.
- I can link a feature of each to its function.
- I can explain that plant and animal cells both have these three parts.

## Why it matters

> A cheek cell and a leaf cell look completely different. Look closer and they share three parts. Every job in a cell depends on them.

<!-- Speaker note: Every cell, from your skin to a gum leaf, is built on the same three parts. -->

## The membrane

**The cell membrane lets oxygen and food in but keeps most proteins in. What is it like?**

- An open door: everything passes
- A selective gate: some things pass, others don't
- A solid wall: nothing passes

* Answer: A selective gate: some things pass, others don't

## Elaborate: no nucleus

**Scientists removed the nucleus from a single-celled organism (an amoeba). It kept moving for a few days, then died and could not divide. What does this show?**

- The membrane is the control centre
- The nucleus holds the instructions the cell needs to keep making what it needs and to reproduce
- The nucleus is needed to move

* Answer: The nucleus holds the instructions the cell needs to keep making what it needs and to reproduce

**Revision summary**

- Plant and animal cells share the **nucleus** (holds DNA, controls the cell), **cell membrane** (controls what gets in and out) and **cytoplasm** (where reactions happen).
- Both also have **mitochondria** (release energy).
- Plant cells also have a **cell wall**, **chloroplasts** (where photosynthesis captures light energy to make food) and a large **vacuole**.
