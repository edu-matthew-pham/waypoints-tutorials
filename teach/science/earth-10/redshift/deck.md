# How do we know the universe is expanding?

Year 10 Earth and space · The big bang

<!-- Speaker note: Students explain redshift as evidence that galaxies are moving away, describe Hubble's distance–speed pattern, and connect an expanding universe to the big bang model (AC9S10U03, node 3). About 23 minutes (Engage and Explore 9 min · Explain 8 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 2 min, Wider about 1 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** light from distant galaxies is redshifted: shifted to longer wavelengths, so they are moving away. Farther galaxies move away faster (Hubble's law), exactly what an expanding universe predicts, from any galaxy. Run backwards, everything was once together: evidence for the big bang.

- I can explain what redshift tells us about a galaxy's motion.
- I can describe how speed depends on distance (Hubble's law).
- I can explain how an expanding universe supports the big bang theory.

## Redshift

**What does a redshift in a galaxy's light tell us?**

- The galaxy is red
- The galaxy is moving away from us: its light is stretched to longer, redder wavelengths
- The galaxy is hot
- The galaxy is moving towards us

* Answer: The galaxy is moving away from us: its light is stretched to longer, redder wavelengths

## Elaborate: a blueshift

**The Andromeda galaxy, our nearest big neighbour, is blueshifted. Does this disprove the expanding universe?**

- It means Andromeda is red
- Yes: the universe is shrinking
- No: nearby galaxies' own motions under gravity can beat the expansion; over large distances, the redshift pattern is clear
- Yes: Hubble was wrong

* Answer: No: nearby galaxies' own motions under gravity can beat the expansion; over large distances, the redshift pattern is clear

**Revision summary**

- **Redshift:** light from distant galaxies is shifted to longer wavelengths: they are moving **away**.
- **Hubble's law:** farther galaxies move away **faster**, in proportion to distance.
- This is what an **expanding universe** looks like from any galaxy (no centre). Run backwards, everything was once together: evidence for the **big bang**.
