# How do we know the universe is expanding?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** In 1929 Edwin Hubble found that almost every galaxy is moving away from us. Does that put us at the centre of the universe?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain redshift as evidence that galaxies are moving away, describe Hubble's distance–speed pattern, and connect an expanding universe to the big bang model (AC9S10U03, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what redshift tells us about a galaxy's motion. I can describe how speed depends on distance (Hubble's law). I can explain how an expanding universe supports the big bang theory.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: redshift and Hubble's law


:::keep-together

### Redshift

**What does a redshift in a galaxy's light tell us?**

☐ The galaxy is very hot

☐ The galaxy's stars are red

☐ The galaxy is moving away from us

☐ The galaxy is moving towards us

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The galaxy is moving away from us

As the galaxy moves away, its light is stretched to longer, redder wavelengths.

:::


:::keep-together

### The pattern

**On your graph, how does speed depend on distance?**

☐ Farther galaxies move away faster, in proportion

☐ There's no pattern

☐ All galaxies move at the same speed

☐ Nearer galaxies move away faster

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Farther galaxies move away faster, in proportion

Double the distance, double the speed: the points lie close to a straight line through the origin.

:::


:::keep-together

### Back in time

**How does an expanding universe support the big bang theory?**

☐ Run backwards, all galaxies were once together, hot and dense

☐ It doesn't: expansion and the big bang are unrelated

☐ It proves the universe is shrinking

☐ It shows we are at the centre of the universe

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Run backwards, all galaxies were once together, hot and dense

:::

## Elaborate: a blueshift


:::keep-together

**The Andromeda galaxy, our nearest big neighbour, is blueshifted. Does this disprove the expanding universe?**

☐ No: a nearby galaxy's own motion under gravity can beat the expansion

☐ Yes: one blueshift shows the universe is shrinking

☐ Yes: it shows Hubble's pattern was wrong

☐ No: a blueshift also means moving away

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: a nearby galaxy's own motion under gravity can beat the expansion

Andromeda and the Milky Way are pulled together by gravity. Over large distances, the redshift pattern is clear.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Redshift:** light from distant galaxies is shifted to longer wavelengths: they are moving **away**. (Strictly, space stretches the light on its way to us; it isn't a Doppler shift from galaxies moving through space.)
- **Hubble's law** (built on Vesto Slipher's redshift measurements): farther galaxies move away **faster**, in proportion to distance.
- This is what an **expanding universe** looks like from any galaxy (no centre). Run backwards, everything was once together: evidence for the **big bang**.
