# How did the universe begin?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The hydrogen nuclei in the water you drink were made in the first minutes of the universe. The carbon in you was made inside stars.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe the big bang (the universe very hot and dense 13.8 billion years ago, expanding since; whether time began then is an open question) and outline the main stages as the universe expanded and cooled (AC9S10U03, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe what the big bang theory says. I can outline at least three stages from the big bang to galaxies. I can explain that the universe has been expanding and cooling.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Atoms

**Why could atoms only form after about 380 000 years?**

☐ Gravity was too weak to pull particles into atoms

☐ It was too hot: radiation knocked electrons off any atom as it formed

☐ There were no electrons yet, only protons and neutrons

☐ Stars hadn't formed yet to build the atoms

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It was too hot: radiation knocked electrons off any atom as it formed

Before then, light and other radiation had enough energy to knock electrons off any atom as soon as it formed. Once the universe cooled enough, atoms could last.

:::


:::keep-together

### Your atoms

**Where did the carbon and oxygen in your body come from?**

☐ Made inside stars, long after the big bang

☐ Made in the first three minutes of the big bang

☐ Made on Earth

☐ They have always existed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Made inside stars, long after the big bang

Stars fuse light elements into heavier ones, and supernovae spread them out into the gas that later formed the sun, Earth and you.

:::


:::keep-together

### Expanding and cooling

**About how long ago did the big bang happen, and what has the universe been doing since?**

☐ About 13.8 billion years ago; it has been expanding and heating up

☐ About 13.8 billion years ago; it expanded at first and has stayed the same size since the galaxies formed

☐ About 4.6 billion years ago; it has been expanding and cooling ever since

☐ About 13.8 billion years ago; it has been expanding and cooling ever since

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** About 13.8 billion years ago; it has been expanding and cooling ever since

:::keep-together

**Measurements show the universe's expansion is speeding up, and galaxies hold far more mass than we can see. How do scientists treat these findings?**

☐ They add dark energy and dark matter to the model and test them

☐ They decide the universe must be shrinking after all

☐ They ignore them, because the theory already works

☐ They throw out the big bang theory and start again

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They add dark energy and dark matter to the model and test them

New observations test these ideas, from telescopes such as ASKAP in Western Australia. This is how a theory grows.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The **big bang theory**: the universe was very hot and dense **13.8 billion years ago**, and has been **expanding and cooling** since; whether time itself began then is still an open question.
- Stages: inflation (a leading idea) → protons and neutrons → hydrogen and helium nuclei (3 min) → atoms and free light (380 000 years) → first stars (about 200 million years) → galaxies → the sun and Earth (9.2 billion years).
- Heavier elements were made **in stars**.
