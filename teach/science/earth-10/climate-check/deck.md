## Adds or not?

**Does it add greenhouse gases to the atmosphere?**

Groups: Adds greenhouse gases · Does not add them

Burning coal for electricity · Clearing forests · Cattle and rice paddies (methane) · Planting forests · Wind turbines making electricity

* Answer: Burning coal for electricity → Adds greenhouse gases; Clearing forests → Adds greenhouse gases; Cattle and rice paddies (methane) → Adds greenhouse gases; Planting forests → Does not add them; Wind turbines making electricity → Does not add them

## The imbalance

**How do more greenhouse gases create an energy imbalance?**

- They block more sunlight from reaching the surface
- They cool the surface
- They make the Sun's light stronger as it passes through
- They trap more outgoing heat, so more energy comes in than goes out

* Answer: They trap more outgoing heat, so more energy comes in than goes out

<!-- Speaker note: Incoming energy stays about the same, but less escapes. With energy in greater than out, Earth warms until it balances again. -->

## Indicator or not?

**Is it an indicator of climate change?**

Groups: Indicator · Not an indicator

Rising global average air temperature · Rising sea level · Shrinking Arctic sea ice · Warming oceans · A single cold day in winter

* Answer: Rising global average air temperature → Indicator; Rising sea level → Indicator; Shrinking Arctic sea ice → Indicator; Warming oceans → Indicator; A single cold day in winter → Not an indicator

## Sea level

**How does the energy imbalance cause sea levels to rise?**

- More rain falls into the oceans from the sky
- The sea floor is sinking
- Warmer water expands, and melting land ice adds water
- Floating sea ice melts

* Answer: Warmer water expands, and melting land ice adds water

<!-- Speaker note: Extra energy warms the oceans and melts glaciers and ice sheets on land, both raising the sea. -->

## Reading a graph

**A graph shows global average temperature rising over 100 years, with ups and downs. What does it show?**

- That temperature does not change
- That every year is warmer than the last
- That one cold year disproves warming
- A warming trend, with natural ups and downs

* Answer: A warming trend, with natural ups and downs

<!-- Speaker note: The trend is the key. Yearly ups and downs are normal weather and climate variability on top of it. -->

## Melting ice

**Why does shrinking Arctic sea ice make the Arctic warm faster than elsewhere?**

- Melting ice gives out heat
- Dark ocean absorbs sunlight the white ice used to reflect
- Ice blocks the Sun's rays from entering the atmosphere
- Less ice means more greenhouse gases

* Answer: Dark ocean absorbs sunlight the white ice used to reflect

<!-- Speaker note: Less ice lowers the albedo, so more sunlight is absorbed, which melts more ice. This is why the Arctic is warming faster than the global average. -->

## Mitigation or adaptation?

**Is it mitigation or adaptation?**

Groups: Mitigation · Adaptation

Replacing coal power with solar and wind · Planting trees to absorb carbon dioxide · Building sea walls · Growing drought-resistant crops · Heatwave warning systems

* Answer: Replacing coal power with solar and wind → Mitigation; Planting trees to absorb carbon dioxide → Mitigation; Building sea walls → Adaptation; Growing drought-resistant crops → Adaptation; Heatwave warning systems → Adaptation

## In terms of the model

**How does mitigation address the energy imbalance?**

- It cools the oceans straight away
- It protects coasts and crops from the effects
- It removes all the heat already in the oceans
- It cuts emissions, so less outgoing heat is trapped

* Answer: It cuts emissions, so less outgoing heat is trapped

<!-- Speaker note: Cutting emissions (or removing carbon dioxide) reduces the extra trapping, so energy in and out come closer to balance. -->

## Why adapt as well?

**Why is adaptation needed even if emissions fall?**

- Emissions cannot be reduced
- Warming already under way continues for some time
- Adaptation replaces the need for mitigation
- Falling emissions cool the Earth straight away

* Answer: Warming already under way continues for some time

<!-- Speaker note: People need to cope with effects that are already coming. Greenhouse gases already in the air, and heat already in the oceans, keep warming and sea level rising for decades. -->

## Exit ticket

**Give one mitigation and one adaptation, and say how they differ.**

* Mitigation reduces the cause (e.g. renewable energy). Adaptation limits the harm (e.g. sea walls).
