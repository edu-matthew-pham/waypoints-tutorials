# Where does the sunlight go?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Fresh snow reflects about 80% of the sunlight that hits it. The open ocean reflects about 6%. When sea ice melts, that difference matters for the whole planet.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify the sun as Earth's energy source and explain why different surfaces absorb or reflect different proportions of sunlight (albedo) (AC9S10U04, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can identify the sun as Earth's main energy source. I can distinguish absorbed from reflected sunlight with examples. I can explain why melting ice increases the energy absorbed.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The **sun** is Earth's main energy source: about 340 W/m² on average at the top of the atmosphere.
- Some sunlight is **reflected** (about 30% on average: the **albedo**); the rest is **absorbed** and warms Earth.
- Bright surfaces (ice, snow, cloud) reflect most; dark ones (ocean, forest) absorb most. Melting ice means more absorbed.
