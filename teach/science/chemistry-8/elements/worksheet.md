# What is an element?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Everything around you, from your phone to your fingernails, is built from fewer than a hundred kinds of atom. Each kind is an element.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students define an element as a pure substance of only one type of atom, tell elements from compounds by their particles, and use chemical symbols (AC9S8U06, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can define an element. I can tell an element from a compound by the atoms in its particles. I can use chemical symbols, including two-letter ones.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**A jar of pure oxygen and a jar of pure water. Both are pure substances. Which is an element?**

☐ Only the oxygen

☐ Neither: oxygen particles have two atoms

☐ Only the water

☐ Both: they are pure

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Only the oxygen. Every oxygen particle (O₂) is two oxygen atoms: one type of atom, so oxygen is an element. Water is pure too, but each particle has hydrogen and oxygen atoms: two types, so water is a compound.

:::

## Explain: elements and symbols


:::keep-together

### What an element is

**What is an element?**

☐ A pure substance made of only one type of atom

☐ Any substance made of single atoms

☐ Any pure substance

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A pure substance made of only one type of atom

One type of atom, and nothing else. The atoms may be single (helium) or joined (oxygen, O₂).

:::


:::keep-together

**Element or compound?**

### Co or CO

**Why is the symbol for cobalt written Co, not CO?**

☐ Co is one element; CO is two symbols, C and O

☐ It doesn't matter how you write it

☐ CO is the symbol for cobalt gas

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Co is one element; CO is two symbols, C and O

A symbol is one capital letter, or a capital and a small letter. Co is one element (cobalt); CO is two symbols, C and O: carbon monoxide.

:::


:::keep-together

### The periodic table

**What does the periodic table show?**

☐ Every element, in order of atomic number

☐ Every substance, including compounds

☐ Only the elements found in living things

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Every element, in order of atomic number

It shows every element, one box each, in order of atomic number, with similar elements in columns. About 118 elements are known; around 90 occur naturally. Everything else is made from them.

:::


:::keep-together

### Reading a box

**A box on the periodic table shows 26, Fe and Iron. What does each part tell you?**

☐ 26 is the temperature iron melts at

☐ 26 is the atomic number, Fe the symbol, Iron the name

☐ 26 is the number of atoms in one particle of iron

☐ Fe is the formula of a compound of iron

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 26 is the atomic number, Fe the symbol, Iron the name

Each box gives an element's atomic number, symbol and name (often its atomic mass too). Find the name and you have the symbol, or the other way round.

:::

## Elaborate: Co or CO?


:::keep-together

### Two capitals

**CO is a poisonous gas from faulty heaters. Cobalt (Co) is a hard, shiny metal. How does the board show CO is not an element?**

☐ It has two letters

☐ The particles are moving

☐ Each particle has two types of atom

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Each particle has two types of atom

Each particle has a carbon atom and an oxygen atom: two types of atom joined, so a compound. Two capital letters in a formula mean two elements.

:::

## Elaborate: two forms of oxygen


:::keep-together

### One element, two substances

**Ozone (O₃) and oxygen (O₂) are both made of only oxygen atoms, but ozone protects us from ultraviolet light high in the atmosphere and is harmful to breathe. What does this show?**

☐ One element can form different substances

☐ O₃ contains a different type of oxygen atom

☐ Ozone must be a compound

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** One element can form different substances

Oxygen atoms joined in pairs (O₂) or threes (O₃) make two different substances. Carbon does the same: diamond and graphite are both pure carbon. How the atoms are joined changes the properties.

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- An **element** is a pure substance made of only one type of atom. Its particles can be single atoms (He) or atoms joined together (O₂, O₃).
- A **compound** has two or more types of atom joined (H₂O, CO₂).
- Each element has a **symbol**: one capital letter, or a capital and a small letter (C, Co, Na). Some come from Latin names (Fe, Na, Cu, Au).
- The **periodic table** lists all the elements, about 118.

{{lines-3}}

:::

**Answer:** Nitrogen: only one symbol (N), so one type of atom. Ammonia has two symbols (N and H): a compound.
