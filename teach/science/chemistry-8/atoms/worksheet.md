# What are particles made of?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Water, oxygen and carbon dioxide are all made of particles. So why is one a drink, one keeps you alive, and one puts out fires?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students extend the particle model so that particles are atoms with a type, and explain that atom type decides the element (AC9S8U06, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain that the particles in the particle model are atoms. I can explain that different elements are made of different types of atom. I can explain that atom type decides what element a substance is.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: atoms and elements


:::keep-together

### Particles are atoms

**In the Year 7 particle model, everything is made of particles. What are those particles made of?**

☐ Air

☐ Cells

☐ Atoms

☐ Smaller bits of the same substance, all the way down

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Atoms

Every particle is an atom, or atoms joined together.

:::


:::keep-together

### Atom type

**What makes oxygen a different element from nitrogen?**

☐ Its atoms are a different type

☐ It has more atoms

☐ It is a different colour

☐ Its atoms are bigger

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its atoms are a different type

There are about 118 types of atom, one for each element. Atom type decides the element.

:::


:::keep-together

### One type, two atoms

**An oxygen particle (O₂) has two atoms. Is oxygen still an element?**

☐ No: two atoms joined make a compound

☐ Yes: both atoms are the same type

:::

**Answer:** Yes: both atoms are the same type

## Elaborate: how the idea of atoms grew


:::keep-together

**Put the ideas about matter in the order they were proposed, earliest first.**

A. `Aristotle: no atoms; everything is earth, water, air and fire, mixed`

B. `Dalton: each element has its own type of atom, and atoms join in fixed ratios`

C. `Boyle: an element is a substance that cannot be broken down into simpler ones`

D. `Democritus: matter is made of tiny pieces that cannot be cut, "atomos"`

Correct order (letters): {{field-24}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** see the program below

Democritus (about 400 BCE): atoms as an idea without evidence. Aristotle (about 350 BCE): his four elements won for 2000 years. Boyle (1661): elements defined by experiment. Dalton (1808): atoms explain why elements combine in fixed ratios. In Year 9 the model changes again: atoms have smaller parts inside.

```science
Democritus: matter is made of tiny pieces that cannot be cut, "atomos"
Aristotle: no atoms; everything is earth, water, air and fire, mixed
Boyle: an element is a substance that cannot be broken down into simpler ones
Dalton: each element has its own type of atom, and atoms join in fixed ratios
```

## Evaluate: look back

What did you think air was made of? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The particles in the particle model are **atoms** (or atoms joined together).
- There are about 118 **types of atom**, one for each **element**.
- All atoms of one element are the same type. **Atom type decides the element.**
- An element can be single atoms (helium) or atoms of one type joined (oxygen, O₂).
