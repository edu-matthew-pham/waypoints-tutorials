# Is it a new substance?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You can melt ice and freeze it again. You can't unburn toast. Is that how to tell a chemical change? Dissolved salt comes back when the water dries up…


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students state the new substance test for chemical change, explain it as atoms rearranging into new particles, and use it in place of the reversibility rule (AC9S8U07, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can state that a chemical change makes a new substance. I can classify changes as physical or chemical with the new substance test. I can explain why "can it be reversed?" is not a reliable test, with a counterexample.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: the new substance test


:::keep-together

### The reaction

**On the reaction board, what happened to the atoms?**

☐ They rearranged into new particles: hydrogen chloride

☐ New atoms were made

☐ Some atoms were destroyed

☐ They melted together

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They rearranged into new particles: hydrogen chloride

The atoms are the same; they are arranged into different particles. A new particle means a new substance.

:::


:::keep-together

### The change of state

**When the ice melted and boiled, what happened to the water particles?**

☐ They stayed water particles; only their spacing and motion changed

☐ They became steam particles, a new substance

☐ They turned into hydrogen and oxygen

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They stayed water particles; only their spacing and motion changed

A change of state is physical: the same particles, arranged and moving differently.

:::


:::keep-together

### When the old rule fails

**Dissolving salt can be reversed, and it is physical. Which of these breaks the old "can it be reversed?" rule the other way?**

☐ Cutting paper

☐ Freezing water

☐ Recharging a battery: a chemical change that can be reversed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Recharging a battery: a chemical change that can be reversed

Charging and using a battery runs chemical reactions forwards and backwards. Reversibility is a clue, not the test.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **chemical change** makes a **new substance**: atoms separate and join into new particles.
- A **physical change** (melting, boiling, dissolving) keeps the same particles.
- **"Is there a new substance?"** is the test. "Can it be reversed?" is only a clue: dissolving can be reversed and is physical.
- In a chemical change no atoms are made or destroyed; they are rearranged.
