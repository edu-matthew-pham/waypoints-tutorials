# Symbols, formulas or models?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** H₂O, a drawing of three balls, and the word 'water' all mean the same thing. Why do chemists need so many ways to say it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare symbols, formulas, structural formulas, models and particle diagrams, say what each shows, read formulas, and choose a representation for a purpose (AC9S8U06, node 5).

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a chemical formula and count the atoms. I can say what symbols, formulas and models each show. I can choose a representation that suits the job.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**What happens:** 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom. The small number belongs to the symbol just before it; no number means 1. A formula tells you which atoms and how many, but not the shape: for that you need a model.

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::

**Answer:** 6

### Choosing one

**You want to show a friend that air is a mixture of nitrogen and oxygen. Which is best?**

☐ The formulas N₂ and O₂

☐ A particle diagram of a box with separate N₂ and O₂ particles

☐ A 3D model of one oxygen particle

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A particle diagram of a box with separate N₂ and O₂ particles

Pick the representation that shows what matters for the job: here, separate particles side by side.

:::


:::keep-together

### One more oxygen

**Hydrogen peroxide is H₂O₂ and water is H₂O. What is the difference?**

☐ H₂O₂ is a mixture of water and oxygen

☐ H₂O₂ has one more oxygen atom in each particle, so it is a different compound

☐ None: both are water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** H₂O₂ has one more oxygen atom in each particle, so it is a different compound

Change one small number and you have a new substance with new properties.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **symbol** (O, Na) names an element.
- A **formula** (H₂O, CO₂) gives the types of atom and how many of each. The small number counts the atom before it; a big number in front counts particles (3CO₂).
- A **structural formula** shows which atoms are joined; a **3D model** shows the shape; a **particle diagram** shows elements, compounds and mixtures.
- No single representation shows everything: choose the one that suits the job.
