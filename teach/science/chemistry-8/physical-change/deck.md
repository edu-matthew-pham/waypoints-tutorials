# Same particles, new look?

Year 8 Chemistry · Chemical change

<!-- Speaker note: Students explain physical change as the same particles rearranged, spread out or packed together, with no new substance formed, and represent it with the particle model (AC9S8U07, node 1). ## Today

**We are learning to** in a physical change (melting, boiling, dissolving) the particles move, spread out or pack together, but every particle stays the same: no new substance is made. Steam is still made of water particles.

- I can explain a physical change at the particle level.
- I can say what changes and what stays the same in a physical change.
- I can draw particle diagrams of a physical change.

- Hydrogen and oxygen, split off from the water

## The particles

**In melting, boiling and dissolving, what happens to the particles?**

- They change how they are arranged and how they move, but stay the same particles
- They turn into new particles
- They get bigger as they warm up

* Answer: They change how they are arranged and how they move, but stay the same particles

<!-- Speaker note: A physical change rearranges the same particles. Nothing new is made. -->

## Where the salt went

**Salt dissolved in water seems to disappear. How could you show it is still there?**

- Filter it out with filter paper
- You can't: dissolved salt is destroyed
- Evaporate the water: the salt is left behind

* Answer: Evaporate the water: the salt is left behind

<!-- Speaker note: The salt particles are spread between the water particles. Evaporating the water leaves them behind as crystals. -->

## Why it can be reversed

**Melting, boiling and dissolving can usually be reversed. Why?**

- Being reversible is what makes a change physical
- Any change can be reversed if you try hard enough
- No energy goes in or out, so nothing is lost
- The same particles are still there, so they can be brought back to how they were

* Answer: The same particles are still there, so they can be brought back to how they were

<!-- Speaker note: No new substance is made, so the original particles are all still there to rearrange back. Being reversible is a result of a physical change, not what defines it. -->

**What is in each steam particle?**

- Air
- Separate hydrogen and oxygen atoms
- Two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom: H₂O, the same as liquid water

* Answer: Two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom: H₂O, the same as liquid water

## The mass

**100 g of ice melts in a sealed bag. What is the mass of the water?**

- 100 g: the same particles, so the same mass
- Less than 100 g: water takes up less space
- More than 100 g: liquids are heavier

* Answer: 100 g: the same particles, so the same mass

<!-- Speaker note: No particles are made or lost in a physical change, so the mass stays the same. (Ice is actually a little less dense than water, which is why it floats.) -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- In a **physical change** (melting, freezing, boiling, condensing, dissolving, cutting) the particles change how they are arranged, how far apart they are and how fast they move.
- The particles themselves stay the same, so **no new substance** is made. Steam is still water; dissolved salt is still salt.
- The number of particles stays the same, so the **mass** stays the same.
- Next lesson: a **chemical change**, where atoms rearrange into new particles.

## Exit ticket

**Wax melts on a candle and drips. Use particles to explain why this is a physical change.**

* The wax particles move from fixed positions to sliding past each other, but each particle is still wax: no new substance, so physical.
