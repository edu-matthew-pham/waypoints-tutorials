# Why isn't salt poisonous?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Sodium is a metal that fizzes and can burst into flame in water. Chlorine is a poisonous gas. Put them together and you can sprinkle the result on your chips.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students define a compound (different atom types chemically joined in a fixed ratio, a new substance with new properties), tell it apart from a mixture, and read formulas (AC9S8U06, node 3, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can define a compound as two or more types of atom chemically joined in a fixed ratio. I can read a formula to find the atom types and how many of each. I can explain that a compound has different properties from the elements it is made from.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: compounds and formulas


:::keep-together

### Joined or mixed

**Both boards had hydrogen and oxygen atoms. What made water a compound and not a mixture?**

☐ It has more atoms

☐ It is a liquid

☐ Its atoms are closer together

☐ Its hydrogen and oxygen atoms are chemically joined in each particle

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its hydrogen and oxygen atoms are chemically joined in each particle

In a compound, different types of atom are chemically joined. In a mixture they are just together, not joined.

:::


:::keep-together

### Fixed ratio

**Water is always H₂O, never H₃O or HO. What does that tell you about compounds?**

☐ Water can be any mix of hydrogen and oxygen

☐ The atoms are joined in a fixed ratio

☐ Hydrogen is rarer than oxygen

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The atoms are joined in a fixed ratio

Every water particle has exactly 2 hydrogen atoms for each oxygen atom. A mixture of hydrogen and oxygen gases can be in any ratio.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why salt is safe

**Sodium fizzes in water and chlorine is poisonous, yet sodium chloride is safe to eat. Why?**

☐ The sodium and chlorine atoms changed into other kinds of atom

☐ There is only a tiny amount of sodium and chlorine in it

☐ The sodium and chlorine are mixed in it, and cancel each other out

☐ It is a new substance: the atoms are joined, so it has new properties

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is a new substance: the atoms are joined, so it has new properties

A compound is a new substance. Its properties belong to the joined particles, not to the elements it was made from.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **compound** is two or more **types** of atom **chemically joined** in a **fixed ratio**.
- A compound is a **new substance** with new properties (sodium chloride is not like sodium or chlorine).
- In a **mixture**, substances are together but not joined, and each keeps its properties.
- A **formula** gives the atoms in one particle: H₂O is 2 hydrogen and 1 oxygen.
