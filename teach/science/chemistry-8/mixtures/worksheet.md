# Mixed, not joined?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You breathe in oxygen and nitrogen with every breath, yet only the oxygen keeps you alive. In air, oxygen still behaves exactly like oxygen.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students define a mixture, explain that its parts keep their properties, vary in amount and can be separated physically, and distinguish mixtures from compounds (AC9S8U06, node 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what a mixture is. I can tell a mixture from a compound. I can sort substances into elements, compounds and mixtures.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: mixtures and compounds

### What a mixture is

**What is a mixture?**

☐ Two or more substances mingled together but not chemically joined

### Getting it back

**How could you get the salt back from salt water?**

☐ You can't: salt and water have joined

☐ Evaporate the water: the salt is left

☐ Filter it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Evaporate the water: the salt is left

Mixtures can be separated by physical means (Year 7: filtering, evaporating, distilling, chromatography).

### Solutions, suspensions and colloids

**Three kinds of mixture. Match each to an example and what it does.**

A. Milk: tiny drops spread through; it looks cloudy but nothing settles

C. Muddy water: bits of solid spread through, then settle if left to stand

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Solution → B. Salt water: the salt dissolves, the liquid stays clear and nothing settles; Suspension → C. Muddy water: bits of solid spread through, then settle if left to stand; Colloid → A. Milk: tiny drops spread through; it looks cloudy but nothing settles

All three are mixtures. In a solution the particles are dissolved and pass through filter paper; a suspension's bits are big enough to settle and be filtered out; a colloid's are in between, so it looks cloudy but doesn't settle. Fog and whipped cream are colloids too.

### Both mixtures?

**Both boxes hold hydrogen and oxygen in very different amounts. Are they both mixtures of hydrogen and oxygen?**

☐ No: only one of them has the right amounts

A mixture's make-up can vary. Water, the compound, is always 2 hydrogen to 1 oxygen.

### Compound or mixture

**Humid air has water vapour in it. Water is a compound. Is humid air a compound?**

☐ It is an element

☐ Yes: it contains a compound

☐ No: it is a mixture of elements and a compound, none joined to each other

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: it is a mixture of elements and a compound, none joined to each other

A mixture can hold elements, compounds or both, as long as the different substances aren't joined.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A **mixture** is two or more substances mingled together but not chemically joined. Each keeps its own particles and properties.
- A mixture's parts can be in any amounts; a **compound** always has a fixed ratio (H₂O).
- Mixtures can be separated by physical means (filtering, evaporating, distilling); compounds only by chemical reactions.
- Air and salt water are mixtures; water and carbon dioxide are compounds.

**Iron and sulfur are mixed, then heated to make iron sulfide. Which is the mixture and which the compound, and how could you tell with a magnet?**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Before heating: a mixture (a magnet pulls out the iron). After: iron sulfide, a compound (the iron is joined to sulfur, so a magnet can't pull it out).
