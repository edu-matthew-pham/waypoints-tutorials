# Mixed, not joined?

Year 8 Chemistry · Elements and compounds

<!-- Speaker note: Students define a mixture, explain that its parts keep their properties, vary in amount and can be separated physically, and distinguish mixtures from compounds (AC9S8U06, node 4). ## Today

**We are learning to** a mixture is two or more substances mingled together but not chemically joined. Each keeps its own properties, the amounts can vary, and the parts can be separated by physical means. A compound is the opposite: atoms joined in a fixed ratio, with new properties.

- I can explain what a mixture is.
- I can tell a mixture from a compound.
- I can sort substances into elements, compounds and mixtures.

## Why it matters

> You breathe in oxygen and nitrogen with every breath, yet only the oxygen keeps you alive. In air, oxygen still behaves exactly like oxygen.

## What a mixture is

**What is a mixture?**

- Two or more substances mingled together but not chemically joined
- Two or more types of atom joined in a fixed ratio
- Any substance with more than one atom in each particle

* Answer: Two or more substances mingled together but not chemically joined

## Mixture or compound

**Mixture or compound?**

Groups: Mixture · Compound

Each part keeps its own properties · New properties, unlike its elements · The amounts can be anything · A fixed ratio of atoms · Separated by filtering, evaporating or distilling · Separated only by a chemical reaction

* Answer: Each part keeps its own properties → Mixture; New properties, unlike its elements → Compound; The amounts can be anything → Mixture; A fixed ratio of atoms → Compound; Separated by filtering, evaporating or distilling → Mixture; Separated only by a chemical reaction → Compound

## Solutions, suspensions and colloids

**Three kinds of mixture. Match each to an example and what it does.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Solution` | A. Milk: tiny drops spread through; it looks cloudy but nothing settles |
| `Suspension` | B. Salt water: the salt dissolves, the liquid stays clear and nothing settles |
| `Colloid` | C. Muddy water: bits of solid spread through, then settle if left to stand |

* Answer: Solution → B. Salt water: the salt dissolves, the liquid stays clear and nothing settles; Suspension → C. Muddy water: bits of solid spread through, then settle if left to stand; Colloid → A. Milk: tiny drops spread through; it looks cloudy but nothing settles

<!-- Speaker note: All three are mixtures. In a solution the particles are dissolved and pass through filter paper; a suspension's bits are big enough to settle and be filtered out; a colloid's are in between, so it looks cloudy but doesn't settle. Fog and whipped cream are colloids too. -->

## Compound or mixture

**Humid air has water vapour in it. Water is a compound. Is humid air a compound?**

- It is an element
- Yes: it contains a compound
- No: it is a mixture of elements and a compound, none joined to each other

* Answer: No: it is a mixture of elements and a compound, none joined to each other

**Revision summary**

- A **mixture** is two or more substances mingled together but not chemically joined. Each keeps its own particles and properties.
- A mixture's parts can be in any amounts; a **compound** always has a fixed ratio (H₂O).
- Mixtures can be separated by physical means (filtering, evaporating, distilling); compounds only by chemical reactions.
- Air and salt water are mixtures; water and carbon dioxide are compounds.

## Exit ticket

**Iron and sulfur are mixed, then heated to make iron sulfide. Which is the mixture and which the compound, and how could you tell with a magnet?**

* Before heating: a mixture (a magnet pulls out the iron). After: iron sulfide, a compound (the iron is joined to sulfur, so a magnet can't pull it out).
