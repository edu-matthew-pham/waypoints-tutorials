# Hotter or colder?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mix citric acid and bicarb soda in water and it fizzes and goes cold. Shake a hand warmer and it gets hot. Neither is plugged in. Where does the heat go, and where does it come from?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain exothermic and endothermic reactions as energy released to or absorbed from the surroundings, and classify everyday reactions (AC9S8U07, nodes 4 and 5).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what exothermic and endothermic mean. I can say which way energy moves in each, between the reaction and its surroundings. I can give an everyday example of each.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Citric acid and bicarb soda react when you mix them in a cup of water. It fizzes and the cup goes cold. What is happening?**

☐ The powders were stored in a freezer

☐ The reaction takes in energy from the water, the cup and your hand

☐ The reaction gives out energy, so the cup cools down

☐ The reaction makes 'cold', which flows into your hand

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The reaction takes in energy from its surroundings (the water, the cup, the air, your hand), so they get colder. A reaction that takes in energy is endothermic. There is no 'cold energy': cold just means less thermal energy.

:::

## Explain: exothermic and endothermic


:::keep-together

### Energy out

**In an exothermic reaction, where does the energy go?**

☐ Into the reaction mixture, so it gets colder

☐ Out to the surroundings, so they get hotter

☐ It is destroyed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Out to the surroundings, so they get hotter

Exo = out. Energy stored in the reactants is released to the surroundings.

:::


:::keep-together

### Sort them

**Exothermic or endothermic?**

Groups: Exothermic (gives energy out), Endothermic (takes energy in)

- Wood burning: {{field-15}}
- Respiration in your cells: {{field-15}}
- A hand warmer (iron rusting quickly): {{field-15}}
- Citric acid and bicarb soda fizzing in water: {{field-15}}
- Photosynthesis (taking in light energy): {{field-15}}
- Bicarb soda reacting with vinegar and fizzing: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Wood burning → Exothermic (gives energy out); Respiration in your cells → Exothermic (gives energy out); A hand warmer (iron rusting quickly) → Exothermic (gives energy out); Citric acid and bicarb soda fizzing in water → Endothermic (takes energy in); Photosynthesis (taking in light energy) → Endothermic (takes energy in); Bicarb soda reacting with vinegar and fizzing → Endothermic (takes energy in)

:::


:::keep-together

### Cold, but is it a reaction?

**An instant ice pack goes cold when you snap it: it takes in heat. Is it a chemical reaction?**

☐ Yes: anything that takes in heat is a chemical reaction

☐ No: a salt dissolves in water, which is a physical change

☐ Yes: a change in temperature proves a new substance was made

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: a salt dissolves in water, which is a physical change

Inside the pack, a salt (usually ammonium nitrate or urea) dissolves in water. The particles spread out but stay the same, so it is a physical change, as in Physical change. Dissolving can still take in energy from the surroundings, which is why the pack gets cold.

:::


:::keep-together

### Stopping on its own

**On the board, the nitrogen and oxygen only reacted at 1800 K, and the temperature fell. Why might an endothermic reaction stop on its own?**

☐ Endothermic reactions are always slow

☐ It runs out of atoms

☐ It cools down, so fewer collisions have enough energy

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It cools down, so fewer collisions have enough energy

It takes in energy and cools its surroundings, so fewer collisions have enough energy to react. Unless energy keeps coming in (a hot engine, sunlight for photosynthesis), an endothermic reaction cools itself down.

:::


:::keep-together

### Where the energy comes from

**Burning gas in a stove gives out a lot of energy. Where was that energy before?**

☐ In the match or spark that lit it

☐ Nowhere: burning makes new energy

☐ Stored in the gas and oxygen as chemical energy

☐ In the air around the flame

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Stored in the gas and oxygen as chemical energy

A fuel and oxygen together store chemical energy. In an exothermic reaction their atoms rearrange into new particles that store less, and the difference is given out to the surroundings.

:::

## Elaborate: hand warmers and ice packs


:::keep-together

### Which product

**A sports trainer needs something to reduce swelling on a sprained ankle, at a game far from a freezer. Which product, and why?**

☐ Either: both change temperature

☐ An instant ice pack: it takes in energy

☐ A hand warmer: its reaction is exothermic

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** An instant ice pack: it takes in energy

As the ice pack's salt dissolves, it takes in energy from the ankle. Taking energy in for cooling, giving it out for warming: the direction of energy transfer is the design choice. (The ice pack does this by dissolving, a physical change; the hand warmer by a reaction.)

## Elaborate: why burning keeps going


:::keep-together

**Wood is exothermic when it burns, yet a log doesn't catch fire on its own. You need a match. Why, and why does it keep burning once lit?**

☐ Wood only turns exothermic once it is hot

☐ It needs energy to start; its own heat then keeps it going

☐ The match adds enough fuel to keep it going

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It needs energy to start; its own heat then keeps it going

Reactions need enough energy to start; once lit, the energy given out heats the next bit enough to react. Collisions need enough energy to react (activation energy, Year 10). An exothermic reaction supplies that energy to its own next steps, so it keeps going.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Exothermic** reactions give out energy to their surroundings, so the surroundings get hotter: burning, respiration, hand warmers.
- **Endothermic** reactions take in energy from their surroundings, so the surroundings get colder: citric acid and bicarb soda, photosynthesis, bicarb and vinegar.
- Energy is never made or destroyed; it moves between the reaction and its surroundings.
- There is no "cold energy": colder means less thermal energy.
