# How can you tell a reaction happened?

Year 8 Chemistry · Chemical change

<!-- Speaker note: Students list observable signs of chemical change and explain that each is a clue to a new substance, not proof, distinguishing misleading signs in physical changes (AC9S8U07, node 3). About 22 minutes (Engage and Explore 7 min · Explain 9 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 3 min, Wider about 2 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** you can't see atoms rearrange, but you can see the signs: a colour change, a gas given off, a solid forming in a liquid, light, a smell, or a temperature change. Each is a clue that a new substance formed, not proof: boiling water bubbles too. The test is still a new substance.

- I can list the signs of a chemical change.
- I can explain why a sign is a clue, not proof.
- I can spot a sign that misleads in a physical change.

## Signs

**Is it a sign of a chemical change?**

Groups: A sign · Not a sign

A colour change when two substances mix · Bubbles of a new gas · A solid forming when two clear liquids mix (a precipitate) · Light or heat given out, or the mixture getting colder · A new smell · The substance changing shape

* Answer: A colour change when two substances mix → A sign; Bubbles of a new gas → A sign; A solid forming when two clear liquids mix (a precipitate) → A sign; Light or heat given out, or the mixture getting colder → A sign; A new smell → A sign; The substance changing shape → Not a sign

## Clue or trap

**Chemical change, or a sign that misleads?**

Groups: Chemical change · Misleading sign: physical change

A cake browning in the oven · Vinegar and bicarb fizzing · Water boiling and bubbling · Blue and yellow paint mixing to green · A nail rusting orange · A hot drink cooling down

* Answer: A cake browning in the oven → Chemical change; Vinegar and bicarb fizzing → Chemical change; Water boiling and bubbling → Misleading sign: physical change; Blue and yellow paint mixing to green → Misleading sign: physical change; A nail rusting orange → Chemical change; A hot drink cooling down → Misleading sign: physical change

## The strongest evidence

**When magnesium burns there is a bright light, and a white powder is left. Which is the strongest evidence of a chemical change?**

- The white powder: a new substance with different properties from the shiny metal
- The magnesium getting hot
- The bright light

* Answer: The white powder: a new substance with different properties from the shiny metal

<!-- Speaker note: A new substance with new properties is the test; the light and heat are signs that go with it. -->

## Why each is a clue

**Why does each sign suggest a new substance has formed?**

| | |
|---|---|
| `A colour change` | A. A new substance has formed that doesn't dissolve in the liquid |
| `Bubbles of gas` | B. Atoms rearranging into new particles gives out or takes in energy |
| `A precipitate` | C. A new substance that is a gas has been made |
| `Heat or light given out, or getting colder` | D. Particles of a new substance are reaching your nose |
| `A new smell` | E. The new substance has a different colour from the starting ones |

* Answer: A colour change → E. The new substance has a different colour from the starting ones; Bubbles of gas → C. A new substance that is a gas has been made; A precipitate → A. A new substance has formed that doesn't dissolve in the liquid; Heat or light given out, or getting colder → B. Atoms rearranging into new particles gives out or takes in energy; A new smell → D. Particles of a new substance are reaching your nose

<!-- Speaker note: Each sign is a property of a new substance (colour, state, whether it dissolves, smell) or the energy of atoms rearranging. A physical change can show the same sign, which is why a sign is a clue, not proof. -->

## Not a reaction

**As the water boils, it bubbles. Why isn't this a chemical change?**

- Because it can be reversed
- The particles are still water: no new substance forms
- Because no light is given out

* Answer: The particles are still water: no new substance forms

<!-- Speaker note: The same particles, more spread out. Bubbles are a clue that misleads here. -->

## Elaborate: a precipitate

**Two clear solutions are mixed and the liquid goes cloudy with a fine yellow solid that settles. What does this suggest, and how could you check?**

- A new insoluble substance (a precipitate) formed; filter it out and test its properties
- One of the solutions has frozen
- Nothing new: the two solutions have just mixed, like two paints

* Answer: A new insoluble substance (a precipitate) formed; filter it out and test its properties

<!-- Speaker note: A solid forming from two clear solutions is a strong sign of a new substance. Filtering it out and testing it is how a chemist would confirm it. ## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Signs of a chemical change: a colour change, a gas given off, a precipitate, light, a smell, a temperature change.
- Each sign is a **clue**, not proof: boiling water bubbles and paint mixing changes colour, with no new substance.
- The **test** is whether a new substance formed, with new properties.

## Exit ticket

**Bicarb and vinegar fizz and the mixture gets colder. Name two signs of a chemical change, and say what would prove it.**

* Gas given off (fizzing) and a temperature change. A new substance would prove it (carbon dioxide, which turns limewater cloudy).
