# How can you tell a reaction happened?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Bake a cake and it rises, browns and smells wonderful. Leave a nail outside and it turns orange. In each case, how do you know something new was made?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students list observable signs of chemical change and explain that each is a clue to a new substance, not proof, distinguishing misleading signs in physical changes (AC9S8U07, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can list the signs of a chemical change. I can explain why a sign is a clue, not proof. I can spot a sign that misleads in a physical change.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**Which of these shows a chemical change has happened for certain?**

☐ It changes colour

☐ Bubbles appear

☐ It gets warmer

☐ None of them on its own: each is a clue, and the test is whether a new substance formed

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** None of them on its own. Bubbles, warmth and colour change are all signs, but boiling water bubbles, a radiator warms things, and mixing paints changes colour, with no new substance. Signs are clues; the test is a new substance.

:::

## Explain: signs and the test


:::keep-together

### Signs

**Is it a sign of a chemical change?**

Groups: A sign, Not a sign

- A colour change when two substances mix: {{field-15}}
- Bubbles of a new gas: {{field-15}}
- A solid forming when two clear liquids mix (a precipitate): {{field-15}}
- Light or heat given out, or the mixture getting colder: {{field-15}}
- A new smell: {{field-15}}
- The substance changing shape: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A colour change when two substances mix → A sign; Bubbles of a new gas → A sign; A solid forming when two clear liquids mix (a precipitate) → A sign; Light or heat given out, or the mixture getting colder → A sign; A new smell → A sign; The substance changing shape → Not a sign

:::


:::keep-together

### Clue or trap

**Chemical change, or a sign that misleads?**

Groups: Chemical change, Misleading sign: physical change

- A cake browning in the oven: {{field-15}}
- Vinegar and bicarb fizzing: {{field-15}}
- Water boiling and bubbling: {{field-15}}
- Blue and yellow paint mixing to green: {{field-15}}
- A nail rusting orange: {{field-15}}
- A hot drink cooling down: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A cake browning in the oven → Chemical change; Vinegar and bicarb fizzing → Chemical change; Water boiling and bubbling → Misleading sign: physical change; Blue and yellow paint mixing to green → Misleading sign: physical change; A nail rusting orange → Chemical change; A hot drink cooling down → Misleading sign: physical change

:::


:::keep-together

### The strongest evidence

**When magnesium burns there is a bright light, and a white powder is left. Which is the strongest evidence of a chemical change?**

☐ The white powder: a new substance with different properties from the shiny metal

☐ The magnesium getting hot

☐ The bright light

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The white powder: a new substance with different properties from the shiny metal

A new substance with new properties is the test; the light and heat are signs that go with it.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why each is a clue

**Why does each sign suggest a new substance has formed?**

A. A new substance has formed that doesn't dissolve in the liquid

B. Atoms rearranging into new particles gives out or takes in energy

C. A new substance that is a gas has been made

D. Particles of a new substance are reaching your nose

E. The new substance has a different colour from the starting ones

A new substance has formed that doesn't dissolve in the liquid; Heat or light given out, or getting colder → B. Atoms rearranging into new particles gives out or takes in energy; A new smell → D. Each sign is a property of a new substance (colour, state, whether it dissolves, smell) or the energy of atoms rearranging. A physical change can show the same sign, which is why a sign is a clue, not proof.

## Elaborate: melting gives signs too


:::keep-together

### Not a reaction

**As the water boils, it bubbles. Why isn't this a chemical change?**

☐ Because it can be reversed

☐ The particles are still water: no new substance forms

☐ Because no light is given out

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The particles are still water: no new substance forms

The same particles, more spread out. Bubbles are a clue that misleads here.

:::

## Elaborate: a precipitate


:::keep-together

**Two clear solutions are mixed and the liquid goes cloudy with a fine yellow solid that settles. What does this suggest, and how could you check?**

☐ A new insoluble substance (a precipitate) formed; filter it out and test its properties

☐ One of the solutions has frozen

☐ Nothing new: the two solutions have just mixed, like two paints

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A new insoluble substance (a precipitate) formed; filter it out and test its properties

A solid forming from two clear solutions is a strong sign of a new substance. Filtering it out and testing it is how a chemist would confirm it.

:::

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Signs of a chemical change: a colour change, a gas given off, a precipitate, light, a smell, a temperature change.
- Each sign is a **clue**, not proof: boiling water bubbles and paint mixing changes colour, with no new substance.
- The **test** is whether a new substance formed, with new properties.
