# Where does the carbon in a tree go?

Year 9 Earth and space · The carbon cycle

<!-- Speaker note: Students explain photosynthesis and respiration (including decomposition) as opposite flows of carbon between the atmosphere and biosphere, and explain balance between them (AC9S9U03, node 3, a hinge node). About 22 minutes (Engage and Explore 9 min · Explain 7 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 2 min, Wider about 1 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** photosynthesis moves carbon from the atmosphere into living things; respiration and decomposition move it back. These huge flows nearly balance, so the air's CO₂ stays steady until something upsets them.

- I can explain that photosynthesis moves carbon from the air into living things.
- I can explain that respiration and decomposition move it back.
- I can explain why the air's CO₂ stays steady when the flows balance.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: A concept tutorial for introducing or revising the idea. Set the width with `?width=minimum`, `typical` (default) or `wider`. The hinge is representing each process as a directional flow between spheres. The misconception that plants are made from soil is raised in Engage. Board notes: stores in billions of tonnes of carbon (GtC); the air starts at a balanced 280 ppm (before 1800). Photosynthesis takes about 120 GtC a year; respiration and decomposition return about the same; the oceans exchange about 80 each way. Switching a process off is a "what if": with respiration and decomposition off, plants draw the air's CO₂ down within years; with photosynthesis off, it climbs. The model's numbers are rounded and its rules simple (plants take up a little more CO₂ when there is more of it). -->

## Balance

**With every process on, why did the CO₂ in the air stay steady?**

- Carbon leaving the air (photosynthesis, oceans) equalled carbon entering it (respiration, decomposition, oceans)
- The ocean stopped working
- Plants stopped photosynthesising
- Nothing was happening

* Answer: Carbon leaving the air (photosynthesis, oceans) equalled carbon entering it (respiration, decomposition, oceans)

## Clearing a forest

**If a large forest is cleared and burned, what happens to the carbon flows?**

- Nothing changes
- Less photosynthesis takes carbon out of the air, and burning and rotting put more in: CO₂ in the air rises
- The carbon disappears
- CO₂ in the air falls

* Answer: Less photosynthesis takes carbon out of the air, and burning and rotting put more in: CO₂ in the air rises

## Elaborate: a carbon atom's journey

**Put a carbon atom's journey in order.**

Groups: 1st · 2nd · 3rd · 4th · 5th

CO₂ in the air · Taken in by a grass leaf in photosynthesis · Eaten by a kangaroo, part of its body · The kangaroo dies and fungi decompose it · Respired by the fungi back into the air as CO₂

* Answer: CO₂ in the air → 1st; Taken in by a grass leaf in photosynthesis → 2nd; Eaten by a kangaroo, part of its body → 3rd; The kangaroo dies and fungi decompose it → 4th; Respired by the fungi back into the air as CO₂ → 5th

## Elaborate: seasons in the air

**Measurements of CO₂ in the air in the Northern Hemisphere dip every northern summer and rise every winter. Why?**

- The sun burns CO₂ in summer
- In summer the huge northern forests photosynthesise more than they respire; in winter respiration and decomposition win
- People burn less fuel in summer
- Trees only respire in winter, when they have lost their leaves

* Answer: In summer the huge northern forests photosynthesise more than they respire; in winter respiration and decomposition win

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Photosynthesis:** carbon moves from the **atmosphere** (CO₂) into the **biosphere** (sugars, then wood, leaves and animals).
- **Respiration** (plants, animals, decomposers) and **decomposition** move carbon back to the atmosphere.
- When the flows balance, CO₂ in the air stays steady; change one and the store changes.

## Exit ticket

**Describe how carbon moves between the air and living things.**

* Photosynthesis takes CO₂ from the air into plants; respiration by plants, animals and decomposers returns it to the air as CO₂.
