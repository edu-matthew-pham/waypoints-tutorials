# What does burning fossil fuels do to the carbon cycle?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Coal is a forest from 300 million years ago. Burning it takes seconds.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain that burning fossil fuels moves carbon from the geosphere to the atmosphere and why this is a net addition to the air's carbon store (AC9S9U03, node 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain where the carbon in fossil fuels comes from. I can explain why burning them raises CO₂ in the air even though natural flows are bigger. I can describe where the extra carbon goes.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Why does more CO₂ in the air matter for Earth's temperature?**

☐ CO₂ thins the ozone layer, letting in more sunlight

☐ CO₂ is a greenhouse gas: it absorbs some of the heat Earth gives off, so more CO₂ slows heat escaping to space and Earth warms

☐ CO₂ comes out of chimneys hot, and that heat warms the air

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Fossil fuels** are carbon stored in the **geosphere** over millions of years.
- **Combustion** moves it to the **atmosphere** in years: a new flow added to a balanced cycle.
- Oceans and plants take up part of it (in the real world a bit over half); the rest builds up, so **CO₂ in the air rises** (280 ppm before 1800, about 420 ppm today).
