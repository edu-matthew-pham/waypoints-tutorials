# Where is all the carbon?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The carbon atoms in your body have been in dinosaurs, in ocean water and in the air, many times over. They are billions of years old and still in use.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify carbon stores in each of Earth's spheres, distinguish stores from flows, and explain that carbon moves between stores without being made or destroyed (AC9S9U03, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name a carbon store in each of Earth's four spheres. I can explain the difference between a carbon store and a carbon flow. I can explain that carbon moves between stores but is never made or destroyed.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: stores in every sphere


:::keep-together

### Which sphere

**Which sphere is each carbon store in?**

Groups: Atmosphere, Hydrosphere, Biosphere, Geosphere

- Carbon dioxide in the air: {{field-15}}
- Carbon dioxide dissolved in sea water: {{field-15}}
- Carbon in trees and animals: {{field-15}}
- Dead leaves and roots in soil: {{field-15}}
- Coal, oil and natural gas: {{field-15}}
- Limestone (calcium carbonate) rock: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Carbon dioxide in the air → Atmosphere; Carbon dioxide dissolved in sea water → Hydrosphere; Carbon in trees and animals → Biosphere; Dead leaves and roots in soil → Biosphere; Coal, oil and natural gas → Geosphere; Limestone (calcium carbonate) rock → Geosphere

:::


:::keep-together

### Store or flow

**What is the difference between a carbon store and a carbon flow?**

☐ They mean the same thing

☐ A store is where carbon is kept; a flow is carbon moving from one store to another each year

☐ Stores are in the air; flows are in the ocean

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A store is where carbon is kept; a flow is carbon moving from one store to another each year

Stores are measured in tonnes of carbon; flows in tonnes a year. Photosynthesis is a flow from air to living things.

:::


:::keep-together

### A burning log

**When a log burns, what happens to its carbon?**

☐ It turns into energy

☐ It is destroyed by the fire

☐ It moves into the air as carbon dioxide: the same atoms, in a different store

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It moves into the air as carbon dioxide: the same atoms, in a different store

Carbon is never made or destroyed. Burning moves it from the biosphere to the atmosphere.

:::


:::keep-together

### Steady

**The air's carbon stayed steady on the board, even though more than a hundred billion tonnes moved in and out each year. Why?**

☐ Nothing was really moving

☐ The board stops the air changing

☐ The flows into the air and out of it were balanced

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The flows into the air and out of it were balanced

A store stays the same when what flows in equals what flows out, like a bath with the tap on and the plug out.

:::


:::keep-together

## Elaborate: shells to stone


:::keep-together

### Shells to stone

**Sea creatures take carbon dissolved in water to build shells of calcium carbonate. When they die, the shells pile up and, over millions of years, become limestone. Which stores has that carbon moved through?**

☐ Hydrosphere → biosphere → geosphere

☐ Atmosphere → atmosphere

☐ Geosphere → biosphere → hydrosphere

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Hydrosphere → biosphere → geosphere

Rocks such as limestone are the largest carbon store of all, but their carbon moves out very slowly, mostly over millions of years.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Carbon is stored in every sphere: **atmosphere** (carbon dioxide), **hydrosphere** (dissolved in the oceans), **biosphere** (living things, dead matter, soil) and **geosphere** (fossil fuels, limestone and other rocks).
- The oceans hold far more than the air; rocks hold the most of all.
- Carbon is never made or destroyed. It **flows** between stores: the **carbon cycle** is the map of those flows.
- A store stays steady when the flows into it balance the flows out.
