# Why does a gas fill its container?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Tip a sugar cube out of its box and it stays a cube. Tip out juice and it spreads across the table. Open perfume and soon the whole room smells. Same particles idea, three behaviours.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe arrangement, motion and attraction for each state and use them to explain why solids keep their shape and gases fill containers (AC9S7U05, node 2, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe the arrangement, motion and attraction of particles in a solid, a liquid and a gas. I can explain why a solid keeps its shape and a gas fills its container. I can use a particle diagram as evidence in an explanation.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Why gases fill

**Why does a gas spread to fill its whole container?**

☐ Gas particles are light and float

☐ Its particles expand to fill the space

☐ Air pushes it around

☐ Its particles move fast in all directions and barely attract each other

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its particles move fast in all directions and barely attract each other

Nothing holds gas particles together, so they spread in every direction until they fill the space.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why solids keep their shape

**Why does a solid keep its shape?**

☐ Its particles don't move

☐ Strong attraction holds its particles in fixed positions, where they vibrate

☐ It is heavier than a liquid

☐ Its particles are bigger

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Strong attraction holds its particles in fixed positions, where they vibrate

Each particle vibrates but stays in its place, so the whole solid keeps its shape.

## Elaborate: comparing attraction

To boil, a substance's particles need enough energy to pull completely away from each other. The stronger the attraction, the hotter it has to get.

:::keep-together

A. `Water (boils at 100 °C)`

B. `Oxygen (boils at −183 °C)`

C. `Table salt (boils at 1413 °C)`

D. `Ethanol (boils at 78 °C)`

Correct order (letters): {{field-24}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** see the program below

A higher boiling point means stronger attraction, so salt, which needs over 1400 °C, is at one end and oxygen, which is already a gas at −183 °C, is at the other.

:::keep-together

**Most solids sink in their own liquid (solid wax sinks in melted wax), but ice floats on water. What does this mean for the particle model?**

☐ Ice floats because of air bubbles trapped inside it

☐ Water is a special case: frozen water particles lock into an open pattern with more space than in the liquid, so the model needs this extra detail

☐ The model is wrong and should be thrown out

☐ Solid particles are always further apart than liquid particles

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Water is a special case: frozen water particles lock into an open pattern with more space than in the liquid, so the model needs this extra detail

The model keeps its core ideas and gains one extra detail for water. (The diagram exaggerates the gap: ice takes up about 9% more space than the same mass of water.)

## Evaluate: look back

Where did you think the gas would be? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Solid:** close together, fixed pattern, vibrating in place, strong attraction. It keeps its shape.
- **Liquid:** close together, no pattern, sliding past each other, weaker attraction. It flows and spreads across the bottom of its container.
- **Gas:** far apart, moving fast in all directions, very weak attraction. It spreads to fill its whole container.
- A gas spreads in every direction, including down. It is not floating up.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**A solid keeps its shape but a gas does not. Use arrangement, motion and attraction to explain the difference.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** In a solid the particles are close, held in fixed places by strong attraction, and only vibrate, so the shape stays. In a gas they are far apart, move fast in all directions and barely attract, so they spread out.
