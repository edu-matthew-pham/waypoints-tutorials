## Describe each state

**Which state does each description belong to?**

Groups: Solid · Liquid · Gas

Particles close together in a fixed pattern · Particles close together with no pattern · Particles far apart · Particles vibrate in place · Particles slide past each other · Particles move fast in all directions · Strong attraction between particles · Weaker attraction: particles stay close but can move · Very weak attraction · Keeps its shape · Spreads across the bottom of its container · Fills its whole container

* Answer: Particles close together in a fixed pattern → Solid; Particles close together with no pattern → Liquid; Particles far apart → Gas; Particles vibrate in place → Solid; Particles slide past each other → Liquid; Particles move fast in all directions → Gas; Strong attraction between particles → Solid; Weaker attraction: particles stay close but can move → Liquid; Very weak attraction → Gas; Keeps its shape → Solid; Spreads across the bottom of its container → Liquid; Fills its whole container → Gas

## Why gases fill

**Why does a gas spread to fill its whole container?**

- Gas particles are light and float
- Its particles expand to fill the space
- Air pushes it around
- Its particles move fast in all directions and barely attract each other

* Answer: Its particles move fast in all directions and barely attract each other

<!-- Speaker note: Nothing holds gas particles together, so they spread in every direction until they fill the space. -->

## Why solids keep their shape

**Why does a solid keep its shape?**

- Its particles don't move
- Strong attraction holds its particles in fixed positions, where they vibrate
- It is heavier than a liquid
- Its particles are bigger

* Answer: Strong attraction holds its particles in fixed positions, where they vibrate

<!-- Speaker note: Each particle vibrates but stays in its place, so the whole solid keeps its shape. -->

## Elaborate: the odd one out, ice

**Most solids sink in their own liquid (solid wax sinks in melted wax), but ice floats on water. What does this mean for the particle model?**

- Ice floats because of air bubbles trapped inside it
- Water is a special case: frozen water particles lock into an open pattern with more space than in the liquid, so the model needs this extra detail
- The model is wrong and should be thrown out
- Solid particles are always further apart than liquid particles

* Answer: Water is a special case: frozen water particles lock into an open pattern with more space than in the liquid, so the model needs this extra detail

## Evaluate: look back

Where did you think the gas would be? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- **Solid:** close together, fixed pattern, vibrating in place, strong attraction. It keeps its shape.
- **Liquid:** close together, no pattern, sliding past each other, weaker attraction. It flows and spreads across the bottom of its container.
- **Gas:** far apart, moving fast in all directions, very weak attraction. It spreads to fill its whole container.
- A gas spreads in every direction, including down. It is not floating up.

## Exit ticket

**A solid keeps its shape but a gas does not. Use arrangement, motion and attraction to explain the difference.**

* In a solid the particles are close, held in fixed places by strong attraction, and only vibrate, so the shape stays. In a gas they are far apart, move fast in all directions and barely attract, so they spread out.
