## Why it matters

> Ships at sea make fresh water by boiling seawater and catching the steam. Forensic scientists find out which pen wrote a note by letting water creep up a strip of paper.

## The dyes

**Why do the dyes in the ink end up at different heights?**

- Each dye dissolves in the water and sticks to the paper differently, so the water carries each one a different distance
- Heavier dyes sink lower
- The water makes new colours as it rises
- The dyes evaporate at different speeds

* Answer: Each dye dissolves in the water and sticks to the paper differently, so the water carries each one a different distance

<!-- Speaker note: Dyes that dissolve well in the water and stick only weakly to the paper get carried the furthest. -->

## Evaluate: look back

What did you think would collect in the second flask? Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- Distillation separates a liquid from what is dissolved in it by boiling the liquid and cooling the vapour back into a liquid somewhere else.
- Evaporation and distillation use the same property (boiling point), but distillation collects the vapour and evaporation lets it escape.
- Chromatography separates a mixture of dissolved substances, such as dyes, because each is carried a different distance.
- Distillation suits seawater (to get water). Chromatography suits inks, food colourings and plant pigments.

## Exit ticket

**Why would a ship use distillation, not evaporation, to get drinking water from seawater?**

* Distillation collects the water vapour as liquid; evaporation lets it escape and keeps only the salt.
