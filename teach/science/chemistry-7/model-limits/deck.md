## Reactions

**Why can't the particle model explain why some substances react?**

- Because reactions are too fast to see
- It treats particles as plain balls with no parts, so it says nothing about how they could join or swap
- Because the model only works for gases

* Answer: It treats particles as plain balls with no parts, so it says nothing about how they could join or swap

<!-- Speaker note: To explain a phenomenon, a model needs the right parts. Reactions need atoms that can join and rearrange (Year 8), and electricity needs electrons (Year 9). -->

## Evidence

**What evidence supports the particle model?**

- It explains everything
- Diffusion, gases being compressible, and tiny specks in water jiggling under a microscope
- We can see particles with our eyes

* Answer: Diffusion, gases being compressible, and tiny specks in water jiggling under a microscope

<!-- Speaker note: Tiny grains of pollen in water jiggle (Brownian motion) because moving water particles keep knocking them about, which Einstein explained in 1905 and which is strong evidence for particles. -->

## Why it explains smells

**The particle model explains a smell spreading across a room. Which part of the model does the explaining?**

- The smell spreads only because a breeze carries it
- Particles are always moving, with space between them, so the smell's particles spread out between the air particles
- Smell particles get bigger until they fill the room
- Smell particles are lighter than air, so they float up and spread

* Answer: Particles are always moving, with space between them, so the smell's particles spread out between the air particles

<!-- Speaker note: The model assumes moving particles with space between them, which is exactly what diffusion needs, so it explains it well. -->

## Mix it up

**Looking back at squashing: a syringe of air can be squashed, but a syringe of water cannot. What does the particle model say is different?**

- Water particles are harder than air particles
- Air has no mass, so nothing pushes back
- Air particles are far apart, with space to move into; water particles are already close together
- Air particles are smaller than water particles

* Answer: Air particles are far apart, with space to move into; water particles are already close together

## Elaborate: a better model

**In Year 8 the particles get parts: they are made of atoms of different types that can join. What can this new model explain that the plain particle model couldn't?**

- Why gases are easy to squash
- Why smells spread
- Why substances react and make new substances

* Answer: Why substances react and make new substances

<!-- Speaker note: A new model keeps what the old one did well and adds what it couldn't do, and scientists choose the simplest model that does the job. What this step is for: Seeing that the atomic model adds what was missing shows why scientists use several models. -->

## Elaborate: how the idea grew

**Ancient Greek thinkers suggested matter is made of tiny particles, but most people didn't accept it for over 2000 years. What changed?**

- It was voted on
- Someone saw a particle
- Evidence: experiments on gases, chemical reactions and Brownian motion that the idea explained and predicted

* Answer: Evidence: experiments on gases, chemical reactions and Brownian motion that the idea explained and predicted

<!-- Speaker note: Models are accepted when they explain and predict evidence better than the alternatives, and changed when new evidence doesn't fit. What this step is for: The long road from an idea to an accepted model shows that evidence, not argument alone, decides. -->

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- The particle model says matter is made of tiny particles that move and attract each other.
- It explains states, diffusion, compressing gases and expansion very well, and evidence such as diffusion and Brownian motion supports it.
- It can't explain colour, reactions or electricity, because its particles are plain balls with no parts.
- Every model has a scope and limits. Scientists keep a model for what it does well and use or build other models for the rest.
