# How big, which way?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Push a shopping trolley gently, then hard. Same direction, different force. Push it from the other side: same size, different force. Two things to get right every time.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students represent forces as arrows with the right size and direction, and name them (AC9S7U04, node 1, a hinge node).

**Students should be able to say:** I can draw a force as an arrow with the right direction and relative size. I can name the forces acting on an object. I can draw a force diagram that fits a situation.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Explain: reading a force diagram


:::keep-together

### Length is size

**When you made the push bigger, what happened to its arrow?**

☐ It got longer, pointing the same way

☐ It stayed the same; only the number changed

☐ It changed direction

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It got longer, pointing the same way

Length shows size. A force twice as big gets an arrow twice as long.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two equal forces

**The trolley's weight is 49 N and the support is 49 N. How should their arrows look?**

☐ Both pointing down

☐ Weight longer, because it is heavier

☐ The same length, pointing in opposite directions

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The same length, pointing in opposite directions

Equal sizes, equal lengths. Weight points down; the support from the ground points up.

## Elaborate: a boomerang


:::keep-together

**First Peoples of Australia shaped boomerangs with a curved upper surface, like an aircraft wing. In flight, as air moves over it, which force does that shape mainly create?**

☐ Thrust: the shape keeps pushing it forward, like an engine

☐ More weight

☐ Lift: air pushing the boomerang up (and sideways, which curves its path)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Lift: air pushing the boomerang up (and sideways, which curves its path)

The aerofoil shape makes the air push on it: lift. Spinning, the lift tilts the boomerang so it curves back. Its force diagram: weight down, lift, and air resistance against its motion.

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A force has a **size** (newtons, N) and a **direction**, so it is drawn as an **arrow**.
- The arrow's **length** shows the size; it **points** the way the force acts.
- A **force diagram** shows every force on one object, each one named.
- Common forces: weight (down), support (up from a surface), friction (against sliding), air resistance (against motion through air), pushes and pulls.
