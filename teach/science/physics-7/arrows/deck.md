# How big, which way?

Year 7 Physics · Forces and motion

<!-- Speaker note: Students represent forces as arrows with the right size and direction, and name them (AC9S7U04, node 1, a hinge node). About 25 minutes (Engage and Explore 6 min · Explain 7 min · Practise 6 min · Evaluate 3 min · Typical adds 3 min, Wider about 3 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** a force has a size and a direction, so we draw it as an arrow. In a force diagram every force on an object gets an arrow of the right length, pointing the right way, with its name.

- I can draw a force as an arrow with the right direction and relative size.
- I can name the forces acting on an object.
- I can draw a force diagram that fits a situation.

## Why it matters

> Push a shopping trolley gently, then hard. Same direction, different force. Push it from the other side: same size, different force. Two things to get right every time.

## Naming forces

**Match each force to what causes it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Weight` | A. Air pushing against a moving object |
| `Support (normal force)` | B. Surfaces rubbing, acting against sliding |
| `Friction` | C. Earth's gravity pulling the object down |
| `Air resistance` | D. Something in contact pushing or pulling |
| `Push or pull` | E. A surface pushing back up on the object |

* Answer: Weight → C. Earth's gravity pulling the object down; Support (normal force) → E. A surface pushing back up on the object; Friction → B. Surfaces rubbing, acting against sliding; Air resistance → A. Air pushing against a moving object; Push or pull → D. Something in contact pushing or pulling

## Elaborate: four situations

**Which diagram fits each situation? (Forward is the way the object moves.)**

Groups: Forward arrow longer than backward arrow · Forward and backward arrows equal · Backward arrow longer than forward arrow

A cyclist speeding up · A car driving at a steady 60 km/h · A sled slowing down on flat snow · A box being pushed but not moving · A runner speeding up out of the blocks

* Answer: A cyclist speeding up → Forward arrow longer than backward arrow; A car driving at a steady 60 km/h → Forward and backward arrows equal; A sled slowing down on flat snow → Backward arrow longer than forward arrow; A box being pushed but not moving → Forward and backward arrows equal; A runner speeding up out of the blocks → Forward arrow longer than backward arrow

## Elaborate: a boomerang

**First Peoples of Australia shaped boomerangs with a curved upper surface, like an aircraft wing. In flight, as air moves over it, which force does that shape mainly create?**

- Thrust: the shape keeps pushing it forward, like an engine
- More weight
- Lift: air pushing the boomerang up (and sideways, which curves its path)

* Answer: Lift: air pushing the boomerang up (and sideways, which curves its path)

## Evaluate: look back

Write one sentence: "At first I thought… Now I think… because…"

**Revision summary**

- A force has a **size** (newtons, N) and a **direction**, so it is drawn as an **arrow**.
- The arrow's **length** shows the size; it **points** the way the force acts.
- A **force diagram** shows every force on one object, each one named.
- Common forces: weight (down), support (up from a surface), friction (against sliding), air resistance (against motion through air), pushes and pulls.
