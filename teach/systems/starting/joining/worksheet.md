## Worked example: three ways to join


:::keep-together

### Three ways to join

1. $\text{Cable}$ — Fastest and steadiest. The computer has to stay where the cable reaches.
2. $\text{Wi-Fi}$ — Radio waves to a router. You can move around, but it slows down further away and through walls.
3. $\text{Mobile data}$ — Radio waves to a phone tower. Works almost anywhere, but it's usually slower.
4. $\text{The internet}$ — Lots of networks (homes, schools, companies) joined together.

Now: why does the lab use cables?

**The school's computer lab uses cables for its 30 computers. Why?**

☐ Computers can't use Wi-Fi

☐ Cables are cheaper than air

☐ Wi-Fi isn't a network

☐ Cables are faster and more reliable, and the computers never move

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Cables are faster and more reliable, and the computers never move

When a computer stays on a desk, a cable is the fastest, steadiest way to join it.

:::


:::keep-together

## Which way?


:::keep-together

### Slow Wi-Fi

**Your Wi-Fi is slow in your bedroom at the far end of the house. What would most likely help?**

☐ Type faster

☐ Turn the screen brighter

☐ Move closer to the router, with fewer walls in the way

☐ Delete some photos

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Move closer to the router, with fewer walls in the way

Distance and walls weaken Wi-Fi.

:::


:::keep-together

☐ The Wi-Fi in your house

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Lots of networks joined together

Homes, schools and companies each have a network, and the internet joins them all.
