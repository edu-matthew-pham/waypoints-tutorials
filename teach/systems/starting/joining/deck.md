Starting out · Joining up

## Today

- I can say what a network is.
- I can compare joining by cable, Wi-Fi and mobile data.
- I can choose how to join a device to a network.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the boards yourself first. Speeds are in words here (very fast, fast, medium, slow); the Year 7–8 board shows the same model in Mb/s and ms. The one idea: a network joins computers so they can send data, by cable or radio waves; each way has strengths. The internet is networks joined together. Simplification: the speeds come from a simple model (in the Year 7–8 lesson's teacher notes): a cable is fastest, Wi-Fi loses speed with distance and walls, mobile data is slower but works anywhere. Year 7–8 catch-up: Networks › Wired or wireless (8K02 n1) names bandwidth, latency and reliability for the same three. Teacher: Worked example: list the devices on the school network together. ## Three ways to join

Now: why does the lab use cables?

$$\text{Cable}$$

1) $\text{Wi-Fi}$ — Radio waves to a router. You can move around, but it slows down further away and through walls.
2) $\text{Mobile data}$ — Radio waves to a phone tower. Works almost anywhere, but it's usually slower.
3) $\text{The internet}$ — Lots of networks (homes, schools, companies) joined together.

- Wi-Fi isn't a network
