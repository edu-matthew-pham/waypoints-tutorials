# Counting dots

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The letter A is stored as 65, and 65 is stored with switches. How do on and off make 65?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students add the dots on face-up cards to read a number, build numbers by taking the biggest card that fits, then store the letters A (65) and H (72) as switches, joining switches to letter codes (AC9TDI6K04 node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a number from dot cards by adding the face-up cards. I can make a number by choosing the cards. I can store a letter as switches using its code.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: make 10


:::keep-together

### 10 on four cards

1. $10$ — The number to make.
2. $8$ — The biggest card that fits in 10. Turn it up: 2 left.
3. $4$ — Too big for 2: leave it down.
4. $2$ — Fits exactly: turn it up. Nothing left.
5. $1010$ — The 8 and the 2 are up, the 4 and the 1 are down.

Now: which cards show 9?

**Which cards show 9?**

☐ The 9 card

☐ The 8, 2 and 1

☐ The 4, 4 and 1

☐ The 8 and the 1

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The 8 and the 1

Take the biggest card that fits: 8. Then 1 more: the 1 card.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam reads the pattern 1000. Click the line where it first goes wrong.**

```
 1  The cards have 8, 4, 2 and 1 dots, from left to right.
 2  Only the left-hand card is face up.
 3  Sam counts it as 1 dot, because it's the first card.
 4  So the pattern 1000 shows 1.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the left-hand card is the 8 card. 1000 shows 8 dots, not 1.

:::


:::keep-together

### All four up

**All four cards are face up. How many dots?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** 15

8 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 15: the most four cards can show.
