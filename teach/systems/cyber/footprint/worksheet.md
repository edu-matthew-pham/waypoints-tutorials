> **Key question:** Ava typed three things into the club app. It knows eight things about her. Where did the other five come from?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sort what an app records into data given and data collected as it is used, judge each sign-up field against the app's purpose, and set an account to its most private while keeping its purpose (AC9TDI8P14 nodes 1 to 3). A 5E mission lesson.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name data an app collects without my typing it. I can judge whether data is needed for an app's purpose. I can set my own privacy settings and explain the trade-off.

## Explain: does it need it?

Your **digital footprint** is all the data about you that systems record: what you give and what they collect as you use them.

1. $\text{purpose}$ — Start from what the app is for: news about club meetings.
2. $\text{Name}$ — Needed: news is addressed to a person.
3. $\text{School email}$ — Needed: that's how the news arrives.
4. $\text{Home address}$ — Not needed: nothing is posted. Collecting it adds risk and nothing else.
5. $\text{test}$ — For each item ask: would the app still do its job without it? If yes, don't collect it.
