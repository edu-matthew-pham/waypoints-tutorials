# The Australian Privacy Principles

Cyber safety and privacy · Secure the system

## Today

- I can say what five of the Australian Privacy Principles require.
- I can judge whether an app meets or breaches a principle, with evidence.
- I can propose a change that would meet it.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Try the board yourself. The club app is made up. The one idea: the principles limit collection, use, sharing and storage, and give people access; a policy doesn't override them. Accuracy notes: the APPs are in Schedule 1 of the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth). They bind agencies and organisations with turnover over $3 million, plus some smaller ones; a school club wouldn't usually be bound itself; independent schools usually are (government schools come under their state's privacy law), and the principles are the standard to design against. The lesson uses five: APP 3, 5, 6, 11 and 12 (13 is correction). Check the OAIC's summary at oaic.gov.au for the current wording before teaching, and check where the Children's Online Privacy Code is up to (the OAIC released an exposure draft in 2026). Teacher: Evaluate: discuss which principle protects the student in the homework app. -->

## Explain: five principles

**Sam reads the club's privacy policy. Click the line where it first goes wrong.**

```
 1  The club app has a privacy policy.
 2  The policy says the club may share members' details with partners.
 3  So the club can give members' emails to a sports shop for advertising.
 4  So it doesn't need to ask members first.
```

* Answer: line 3

## Exit ticket

**Name two Australian Privacy Principles and what each requires.**

* Any two: APP 3 collect only what is needed; APP 5 tell people what and why; APP 6 use and share only for that purpose unless they agree; APP 11 keep it secure; APP 12 let people see it.
