## Today

- I can explain that whoever chose the task, I'm my own learning coach.
- I can say what my coach does before, during and after a task.
- I can say what my learning coach would say when I'm stuck.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **The core idea.** Two questions: who chose the task (you or someone else) and who coaches you through it (always you). Who chose the task is a feature of the task: in the research, self-directed versus externally directed (Loyens, Magda and Rikers 2008; Jossberger and colleagues 2010, after Knowles). Coaching yourself through it, planning, monitoring and reflecting, is self-regulated learning, and it applies at both ends. Self-directed learning isn't the better version, and at Years 6 to 8 it mostly means choosing a topic, a video, a tutorial or who to ask. **The coach analogy** is the site's own. It builds on the teacher shifting into a coaching role and then fading (Barr), self-instructional training, where adult talk-aloud becomes the child's whisper and then silent self-talk (Meichenbaum and Goodman 1971), and cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown and Newman 1989). That's why the coach's voice is a think-aloud: model it yourself today (see the class plan). **Feelings** belong to the coach. ## The coach's three jobs

**Here are things a learner might say to themselves. When would the coach usually say each one?**

Groups: Before: plan · During: check · After: look back

"What is this actually asking me to do?" · "What's my first step?" · "Does this answer make sense?" · "I'm getting fed up. Short break, then one part at a time." · "What worked, and what would I do differently next time?" · "I lost marks the same way twice. What's going on there?"

* Answer: "What is this actually asking me to do?" → Before: plan; "What's my first step?" → Before: plan; "Does this answer make sense?" → During: check; "I'm getting fed up. Short break, then one part at a time." → During: check; "What worked, and what would I do differently next time?" → After: look back; "I lost marks the same way twice. What's going on there?" → After: look back

```
 1  Nib's teacher sets a worksheet of ten area questions.

 3  Nib does all ten questions as fast as it can and hands them in.
 4  Nib used the wrong formula from question 2 onwards.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing the coach's voice for someone else first, before applying it to yourself. For students who need more support: Two questions on a card: Who chose the task? Who coaches me through it? (Always me.) For early finishers: Pick a task you chose outside school (a game, a sport, an instrument). What would your coach plan, check and look back on? The hard part, on purpose: Students may hear 'your own coach' as 'do it without help'.
