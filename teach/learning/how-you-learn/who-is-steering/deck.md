# Who's steering?

How you learn best · Who's steering?

<!-- Speaker note: Students judge a made-up learner who chose her own goal but never checks, sort learners by who chose the goal and whether they check, match three ideas that get mixed up (self-directed learning, self-regulated learning, everyday self-regulation), and find Nib's slip (choosing the goal means you're learning well). Typical width (Years 7 and 8): a long project with frustration in it. About 40 minutes (Engage 6 min · Explore 10 min · Explain 12 min · Elaborate 5 min · Evaluate 7 min). -->

## Today

- I can explain the difference between choosing what to learn and steering how I learn it.
- I can say how managing my feelings helps me keep learning when I'm stuck.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **The distinction.** Self-directed learning (from adult education, Malcolm Knowles) is about the learner choosing goals, resources and approach: the *what* and *why*. Self-regulated learning (Zimmerman, Pintrich, Winne) is about planning, monitoring and adjusting on a task, which can be set by someone else: the *how*. Everyday self-regulation (managing emotions and behaviour) overlaps with both. The research uses these words in slightly different ways; the lesson keeps them simple on purpose, and that's fine to say if a student asks. **Why it matters at school.** Most school tasks are set by teachers, so self-regulated learning is the one students use most. Projects, homework and anything outside school ask for both. **There's no single right answer** in the Engage question or the class debate. Accept reasoned views. Years 6: give the link with `?width=minimum` to hide the bridge project scenario. Teacher: Run the class debate in the class plan before Evaluate. -->

## Three ideas

**Match each idea to what it's mostly about.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Self-directed learning` | A. Managing how you feel and act, like calming down when you're stuck |
| `Self-regulated learning` | B. Planning, checking and adjusting while you learn |
| `Self-regulation (everyday)` | C. Choosing what to learn and why, and how to go about it |

* Answer: Self-directed learning → C. Choosing what to learn and why, and how to go about it; Self-regulated learning → B. Planning, checking and adjusting while you learn; Self-regulation (everyday) → A. Managing how you feel and act, like calming down when you're stuck

## Nib's slip

**Nib is learning to juggle. Click the line where Nib's thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib decides to learn to juggle three balls, all by itself.
 2  Nib thinks: I chose this myself, so I must be learning it well.
 3  Every day Nib throws the balls up for ten minutes without counting the catches.
 4  After a month, Nib still drops the third ball every time.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: choosing the goal doesn't mean the learning is going well. Nib still needs a plan and a check, like counting catches each day. -->

## Which idea?

**Taking three deep breaths before trying a hard question again is mostly…**

- Everyday self-regulation
- Self-directed learning
- Neither

## Elaborate: a bigger project

**Priya chose to build a model bridge for a term-long project. Three weeks in, it keeps collapsing and she's fed up. What would help her most?**

- Decide she's just not good at building
- Start a completely new project she likes more
- Keep building the same design faster
- Take a break, then test one part at a time to find where it fails

* Answer: Take a break, then test one part at a time to find where it fails

<!-- Speaker note: Calming down first (everyday self-regulation), then checking one part at a time (self-regulated learning), keeps her own goal (self-directed) on track. What this step is for: Years 7 and 8: a long, self-chosen project needs both skills, and feelings come into it. -->
