## Why it matters

> A drink bottle and its cap cost $11 together. The bottle costs $10 more than the cap. How much is the cap? Most people answer in two seconds, and most people are wrong.

<!-- Speaker note: Every puzzle and person here is made up. Getting a puzzle wrong is the point: it shows what your head does when it's in a hurry. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Are the people who got the bottle puzzle right smarter than the people who didn't?**

- What did the people who got it right do that the others didn't?
- Could anyone do that?

## Engage: predict

**A drink bottle and its cap cost $11 together. The bottle costs $10 more than the cap. How much is the cap? Go with your first answer.**

- 50 cents
- $10
- $1.50
- $1

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: 50 cents. If the cap were $1, the bottle would be $11 and the pair would cost $12. With a 50-cent cap, the bottle is $10.50 and the pair is $11. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Nib's slip

**Nib tries a puzzle. Click the line where Nib's thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  A pencil and a rubber cost $1.20 together. The pencil costs $1 more than the rubber.
 2  Nib reads it and thinks: what's the rubber?
 3  The answer 20 cents pops into Nib's head straight away, so it must be right.
 4  Nib writes 20 cents and moves on.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: Nib decides the answer must be right because it came quickly. A quick check would catch it: with a 20-cent rubber, the pencil is $1.20 and the pair is $1.40. The rubber is 10 cents. -->

<!-- Speaker note: Teacher: think aloud as you check the bottle answer on the board: 'If the cap is $1… then the bottle is… so together…'. That's the 'during' move made visible. -->

## Before, during, after

**Sort what careful solvers do into before, during and after the task.**

Groups: Before · During · After

Read what the question is really asking · Decide how to start · Notice when something seems too easy or too strange · Test an answer by putting it back into the question · Ask: did that work? What would I do differently? · Notice which kind of question tricked you, for next time

* Answer: Read what the question is really asking → Before; Decide how to start → Before; Notice when something seems too easy or too strange → During; Test an answer by putting it back into the question → During; Ask: did that work? What would I do differently? → After; Notice which kind of question tricked you, for next time → After

## The 'during' move

**What's the quickest way to check the bottle answer?**

- Put it back into the question and see if the total is $11
- Ask a friend
- Do it again faster

* Answer: Put it back into the question and see if the total is $11

<!-- Speaker note: Putting an answer back into the question is a check that works for lots of maths and science tasks. Ask on mini whiteboards or by show of hands before the reveal. -->

## Elaborate: not just puzzles

**Jun has a science report due Friday. Match each thing she does to when she does it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `She reads the task sheet and highlights what the report must include.` | A. Before |
| `Halfway through, she notices she's written lots about the method but nothing about the results yet.` | B. After |
| `After it's marked, she notes that her conclusion didn't use her data, for next time.` | C. During |

* Answer: She reads the task sheet and highlights what the report must include. → A. Before; Halfway through, she notices she's written lots about the method but nothing about the results yet. → C. During; After it's marked, she notes that her conclusion didn't use her data, for next time. → B. After
