## Why it matters

> Every new tool restarts the argument unless you judge it the same way each time: what it's for, what it does to the thinking, and where the data goes.

## Think first

**The tool's website says: "Students using it scored 30% higher on homework." What does that show about learning?**

- Nothing at all
- That it's worth a trial
- Very little on its own
- That it improves learning

## The idea

Judge a proposed tool with three questions, in this order. The guide has a section for each.

1. **What is it for?** The learning purpose, and the thinking it touches (lesson 1's question, for a tool). Would a well-prompted general tool the school already has do the same job? (Guide: *Start with the learning purpose*; *Compare against a well-prompted general LLM*.)
2. **Does it help learning, or just doing?** Does it give answers, or hints that keep the thinking with the student? What does its evidence measure: doing with help, or learning later? (Guide: *Three kinds of tools to evaluate*.)
3. **Can we trust it?** Where student work goes, what's switched on by default, the minimum age, who gains and who loses. (Guide: *Can we trust it?*)

Then **a small, fair trial**, with a question it answers, before anyone adopts it. (Guide: *Choose a small trial*; *Example trial questions*.) And the decision goes to whoever decides on tools at your school, against its AI principles: you recommend; the school decides.

## Check

**A vendor's pitch says each of these. Which question does it bear on?**

Groups: What is it for? · Helps learning? · Can we trust it?

"Gives step-by-step hints instead of full answers" · "Students using it scored 30% higher on homework" · "For students 13 and over" · "Student work may be used to improve our models" · "Built for Year 9 science revision" · "Teachers see every student's chat"

* Answer: "Gives step-by-step hints instead of full answers" → Helps learning?; "Students using it scored 30% higher on homework" → Helps learning?; "For students 13 and over" → Can we trust it?; "Student work may be used to improve our models" → Can we trust it?; "Built for Year 9 science revision" → What is it for?; "Teachers see every student's chat" → Can we trust it?

## Apply



- **Three questions and a trial:** Use the guide's sections for each question.
- **Who decides:** Recommend; don't adopt on your own.

## Exit ticket

**What three questions would you ask about any proposed AI tool, and in what order?**

* What is it for (and would what we have do it)? Does it help learning or just doing? Can we trust it with students' data? Then a small, fair trial.
