# What thinking is this task for?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Every task is a way of getting students to do some thinking. Decide what that thinking is, and most AI questions about the task answer themselves.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Teachers name the thinking a task is for, see a persuasive essay sorted into keep the thinking, AI can help and depends on the goal, then sort their own task's parts.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the thinking a task is for, as a sentence about what students can do. I can sort a task's parts into keep the thinking, AI can help and depends on the goal. I can say where that sort would change for a younger or older class.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**What happens:** Usually the planning and the explaining: those are the thinking a practical report exists to build. The format is how students show it. Teachers who say "all of it" have the hardest time deciding where AI fits, because every part then looks equally important to protect.

## The idea

A task is a vehicle. The essay, the report, the folio are how students do some **thinking** you want them to get better at. Name that thinking in one sentence: *what should students be able to do after this task that they couldn't before?*

Then sort the task's parts into three groups (the same words students use in *AI at our school*):

- **Keep the thinking:** this part *is* the learning. If AI did it, the student would miss the point of the task.
- **AI can help:** not the goal of this task. Help here can free time for the thinking (a spellcheck on a science poster).
- **Depends on the goal:** help for one student or one goal, a crutch for another. A hint when stuck keeps a student going; a worked answer can take the thinking away. Translating a word helps in science; in a language class it may be the whole point.

**Age and stage matter.** A part can move groups between Year 7 and Year 10: once a skill is learned, help with it stops costing learning.

Notice what isn't in this lesson: what AI can or can't do. That's next lesson. Decide what the task is for first, or you'll design around the tool instead of the learning.

## Worked example

The persuasive essay, sorted:

| Part | Group | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a position and the reasons for it | Keep the thinking | That's the argument. |
| Answering the strongest objection | Keep the thinking | The hardest part, and the most valuable. |
| Finding a statistic to support a reason | Depends on the goal | Fine if research isn't the goal; not if it is. |
| Feedback on a draft | Depends on the goal | Helpful if the student decides what to change. |
| Spelling and punctuation | AI can help | Unless conventions are what you're assessing. |

:::keep-together

**The Year 10 persuasive essay: "Should our school start later? About 800 words." Which sentence best names the thinking it's for?**

☐ Writing an 800-word persuasive essay.

☐ Knowing the arguments for and against later school start times.

☐ Using persuasive language features correctly.

☐ Building an argument from reasons and evidence, and answering the strongest objection, in your own voice.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Building an argument from reasons and evidence, and answering the strongest objection, in your own voice.

The first names a skill students can get better at, in a sentence. With it, the parts sort: choosing a position and reasons is the thinking; checking spelling isn't.

:::

## Apply


**Name the thinking and sort the parts.** Write one sentence: *after this task, students are better at …*. Then list the task's main parts (three to six) and sort them.

**Keep it.** Keep what you wrote (on paper, or copy it into your planning). Lessons 2 to 4 use it.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
