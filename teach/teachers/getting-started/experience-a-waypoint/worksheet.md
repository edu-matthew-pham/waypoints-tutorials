# Experience a waypoint

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** What might a wrong answer tell you about how a student is thinking?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Teachers try the actual resource-types interactions and interpret what feedback and support can tell them.

**Students should be able to say:** I can distinguish feedback, support and optional width.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Try: the real lesson

In the [live resource-types lesson](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/geography/water-7/resource-types/):

1. Choose a prediction and press **Lock in my prediction**. Read the explanation under **Try** and compare it with your choice.
2. In **Worked example: two ways of coming back**, choose **Fish are a continuous resource** for the fishing question, then **Check answer**. Read the feedback before correcting it.
3. Open **2 · Find Nib's mistake**. Nib treats renewable and continuous as the same because both “come back”. Look at the reason Nib gives for putting them in the same group.
4. Open **Build up to it**. Answer the question about which resource people can use up, then return to Nib's explanation and find the faulty line.
5. Set **Width** to **Minimum**, then **Typical** to try **Typical: more resources**. The extra examples ask you to use the same idea. **Wider** adds a discussion you could have with your class. Choose which examples to use and how long to spend.

Tick the box when you have tried those steps. It is a reminder for you; this guide cannot see what you did in the other tab.

## The idea: what feedback adds


:::keep-together

**After a student chooses “Fish are a continuous resource”, what makes the feedback useful?**

☐ It explains why fish and sunlight belong in different groups.

☐ It lets the student skip the original task once the smaller question is right.

☐ It confirms understanding as soon as the student switches to the right answer.

☐ It identifies the student’s exact misunderstanding without a follow-up question.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It explains why fish and sunlight belong in different groups.

The feedback explains why fish and sunlight belong in different groups. Ask the student to explain that difference in their own words.

:::

## Apply: ask one follow-up

For example, ask the student to name another resource and explain which group it belongs in. Discuss what their explanation tells you. The site does not mark this response.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
