# Putting it together - scarcity, choice and the future

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A council has $2 million. People want a water recycling plant, a skate park and longer library hours. It can afford one. How should it decide, and who pays the cost?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students link needs and wants, scarcity, allocation, opportunity cost and sustainability in one chain, and apply it to a made-up council decision. Doable alone, with discussion as an optional extra using the class plan.

**Students should be able to say:** I can put the chain in order and say how each idea leads to the next. I can name an opportunity cost in a council or community decision. I can give one point about the future (sustainability).

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**What happens:** Wants. Unlimited wants meet limited resources and that is scarcity. Scarcity forces choices, and each choice has an opportunity cost.

## Worked example: the council's $2 million


:::keep-together

### Why a choice at all

**The council can afford a water recycling plant, a skate park or longer library hours. It picks the plant. What makes this a choice at all?**

☐ Scarcity: there is $2 million and not enough for all three

☐ The council is greedy

☐ The plant is the cheapest

☐ Nobody wants the others

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Scarcity: there is $2 million and not enough for all three

There are three wants and one budget, so the council has to choose.

:::


:::keep-together

### What the council gives up

**The council ranks them: plant, then library hours, then skate park. It picks the plant. What is the opportunity cost?**

☐ All the others

☐ The skate park

☐ Longer library hours

☐ The $2 million

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Longer library hours

Library hours were the next best option, so they are the opportunity cost.

:::


:::keep-together

### Looking ahead

**Which is a sustainability reason to pick the plant?**

☐ It costs the most

☐ It reuses water, so there is more left for the future

☐ More people like it

☐ It is built quickly

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It reuses water, so there is more left for the future

Sustainability asks whether the resource will still be there later. Recycling water helps with that.

:::

## Judge a choice


:::keep-together

**A town can use its last block of land for a clinic or a car park. It picks the clinic. Use the chain: say why it had to choose, what the opportunity cost is, and one sustainability point.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A strong answer: It had to choose because wants are larger than the one block, which is scarcity. The opportunity cost is the car park, the next best use. A sustainability point: a clinic keeps people well for the long term, but building uses up the last land.; A different choice that also works: Scarcity means only one can fit. The opportunity cost is the clinic if they pick the car park, since it was ranked first. A sustainability point: the car park may cause more traffic and pollution for years.

:::

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**A council picks a skate park over a library. In one or two sentences, use scarcity and opportunity cost to explain it.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** There was not enough money for both (scarcity), so it chose. The opportunity cost is the library, the next best use of the money.
