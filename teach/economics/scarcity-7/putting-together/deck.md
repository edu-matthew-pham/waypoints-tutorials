# Putting it together - scarcity, choice and the future

Scarcity and opportunity cost · Choice and opportunity cost

<!-- Speaker note: Students link needs and wants, scarcity, allocation, opportunity cost and sustainability in one chain, and apply it to a made-up council decision. Doable alone, with discussion as an optional extra using the class plan. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 8 min · Make 6 min. ## Today

**We are learning to** explain the chain from unlimited wants and scarcity to allocation, opportunity cost and sustainability.

- I can put the chain in order and say how each idea leads to the next.
- I can name an opportunity cost in a council or community decision.
- I can give one point about the future (sustainability).

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is the integration lesson. ## Why it matters

> A council has $2 million. People want a water recycling plant, a skate park and longer library hours. It can afford one. How should it decide, and who pays the cost?

<!-- Speaker note: You have met needs and wants, scarcity and opportunity cost. This lesson puts them in one chain and uses it on a real-sounding decision. Choosing well also means thinking ahead. A resource used up now is not there later, so we ask whether a choice is **sustainable**: does it leave enough for the future? -->

## Why a choice at all

**The council can afford a water recycling plant, a skate park or longer library hours. It picks the plant. What makes this a choice at all?**

- Scarcity: there is $2 million and not enough for all three
- The council is greedy
- The plant is the cheapest
- Nobody wants the others

* Answer: Scarcity: there is $2 million and not enough for all three

<!-- Speaker note: There are three wants and one budget, so the council has to choose. -->

## What the council gives up

**The council ranks them: plant, then library hours, then skate park. It picks the plant. What is the opportunity cost?**

- All the others
- The skate park
- Longer library hours
- The $2 million

* Answer: Longer library hours

<!-- Speaker note: Library hours were the next best option, so they are the opportunity cost. -->

## Looking ahead

**Which is a sustainability reason to pick the plant?**

- It costs the most
- It reuses water, so there is more left for the future
- More people like it
- It is built quickly

* Answer: It reuses water, so there is more left for the future

<!-- Speaker note: Sustainability asks whether the resource will still be there later. Recycling water helps with that. -->

## Judge a choice

**A town can use its last block of land for a clinic or a car park. It picks the clinic. Use the chain: say why it had to choose, what the opportunity cost is, and one sustainability point.**

* Answer: A strong answer: It had to choose because wants are larger than the one block, which is scarcity. The opportunity cost is the car park, the next best use. A sustainability point: a clinic keeps people well for the long term, but building uses up the last land.; A different choice that also works: Scarcity means only one can fit. The opportunity cost is the clinic if they pick the car park, since it was ranked first. A sustainability point: the car park may cause more traffic and pollution for years.
