# Needs, wants and why wanting never stops

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mia saves up for a new bike and buys it. A month later she wants a helmet to match. What is going on, and will it ever stop?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn the test for a need (you cannot stay safe and well without it) and a want (you would like it but could live without it), and see through Mia's story that meeting a want tends to bring another. This is the first of two hinge lessons: if students think wants could all be met with enough stuff, scarcity in the next lesson will look like a supply problem someone could fix.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether something is a need or a want and give a reason. I can explain why wants keep growing, with an example. I can write two needs and two wants from my own life.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: wanting does not stop


:::keep-together

### Wanting keeps going

1. $\text{Name the need}$ — Mia's need is a way to get around, and her old bike already does that.
2. $\text{Name the want}$ — A new bike, then a matching helmet, a bell and a phone holder.
3. $\text{Ask what happens next}$ — After each want is met, a new one appears.
4. $\text{So}$ — Wants are not a problem that enough stuff would solve. They keep growing.

Now: what does Mia's story show?

**Mia buys a new bike. A month later she wants a helmet in the same colour. What does this show?**

☐ Mia wasted her money

☐ Mia was not really happy

☐ Mia needs a helmet more than a bike

☐ Meeting one want often leads to another

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Meeting one want often leads to another

Wants do not run out. When one is met, a new one often turns up, and people can usually think of something more.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining Mia's story. Click the line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia has an old bike. She wants a new one, so she saves up and buys it.
 2  A month later she wants a helmet in the same colour, then a bell, then a phone holder for it.
 3  Nib says: "Mia got what she wanted, so she should be happy and want nothing else."
 4  Nib says: "So if factories made enough things, everyone would have everything they want, and wanting would stop."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib thinks that if factories made enough, wanting would stop. Mia shows the opposite. Each want she met made a new one, so more stuff does not end wanting.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why wanting never ends

**Which best explains why people's wants never run out?**

☐ Shops keep making new things

☐ There is never enough of anything

☐ Meeting one want usually shows you another

☐ People are greedy

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Meeting one want usually shows you another

Each want met tends to show you the next one, so the list keeps growing.
