# Needs, wants and why wanting never stops

Scarcity and opportunity cost · Needs and wants

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn the test for a need (you cannot stay safe and well without it) and a want (you would like it but could live without it), and see through Mia's story that meeting a want tends to bring another. This is the first of two hinge lessons: if students think wants could all be met with enough stuff, scarcity in the next lesson will look like a supply problem someone could fix. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 6 min · Make 6 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** tell needs from wants and explain why wants never run out.

- I can say whether something is a need or a want and give a reason.
- I can explain why wants keep growing, with an example.
- I can write two needs and two wants from my own life.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a hinge lesson. The next lesson (scarcity) only makes sense if students see that wants are unlimited, and that this is a normal, permanent part of life, not a shortage someone could fix by making more things. If this lesson goes thinly, scarcity will look to students like 'not enough stock'. Spend the time here. Everything is made up (Mia, Dev, Sam, the town). Do not ask students what their families have, spend or lack. The Make step uses 'my own life' but only what they choose to write, in their own book. The first of the wider steps invites students to look at an advert. Choose a suitable one in advance, from a school-appropriate source. Teacher: Try: stop at the trainers. A need can be inside a want. Teacher: Worked example: let Nib's mistake sit before explaining it. Ask students to say what Mia's story shows. Teacher: Vary: ask them to give the reason, not only the answer. -->

## Why it matters

> Mia saves up for a new bike and buys it. A month later she wants a helmet to match. What is going on, and will it ever stop?

<!-- Speaker note: A **need** is something you must have to be safe and well, such as clean water, food, a place to live and clothes to keep you warm. A **want** is something you would like but could live without, such as a games console or the newest trainers. Needs are few and can be met. Wants are different: they keep growing. That difference is where this whole topic starts. -->

## Wanting keeps going

Now: what does Mia's story show?

$$\text{Name the need}$$

1) $\text{Name the want}$ — A new bike, then a matching helmet, a bell and a phone holder.
2) $\text{Ask what happens next}$ — After each want is met, a new one appears.
3) $\text{So}$ — Wants are not a problem that enough stuff would solve. They keep growing.

## Wanting keeps going

**Mia buys a new bike. A month later she wants a helmet in the same colour. What does this show?**

- Mia wasted her money
- Mia was not really happy
- Mia needs a helmet more than a bike
- Meeting one want often leads to another

* Answer: Meeting one want often leads to another

<!-- Speaker note: Wants do not run out. When one is met, a new one often turns up, and people can usually think of something more. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining Mia's story. Click the line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia has an old bike. She wants a new one, so she saves up and buys it.
 2  A month later she wants a helmet in the same colour, then a bell, then a phone holder for it.
 3  Nib says: "Mia got what she wanted, so she should be happy and want nothing else."
 4  Nib says: "So if factories made enough things, everyone would have everything they want, and wanting would stop."
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: Nib thinks that if factories made enough, wanting would stop. Mia shows the opposite. Each want she met made a new one, so more stuff does not end wanting. -->
