# Opportunity cost - what you give up

Scarcity and opportunity cost · Choice and opportunity cost

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up, and not the price paid or everything else. They find it for time, money and land choices. Width is the point here: the same idea in several settings is what makes it transfer. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 6 min · Make 6 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** define opportunity cost as the next-best alternative given up and identify it in a resource choice.

- I can say what opportunity cost is in my own words.
- I can find the next best alternative in a choice.
- I can explain why the price is not the opportunity cost.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Two misconceptions run through this lesson: price as opportunity cost, and the cost as everything given up. The ranking step (first, second, third) is what fixes both. Insist that students rank before they answer. Everything is made up. For the Make step, students choose a choice they are comfortable with; they do not need to share spending. No names read out unless students offer. Teacher: Try: ask pairs to say the ranking aloud first. Teacher: Worked example: let Nib's mistake sit. Ask 'Where did the $8 go? What did Mia lose by going?' Teacher: Vary: stress the time and land cases. Same idea, different resource. -->

## Why it matters

> Mia has a free Saturday and $20. She could go to the pool, buy a book or save the money. She picks the pool. What did that choice cost her, apart from the ticket?

<!-- Speaker note: Scarcity means we cannot have everything, so we have to choose. Every choice leaves something behind. The **opportunity cost** of a choice is the next best alternative you gave up when you made it. It is one thing: the option you would have picked if the one you chose had not been there. -->

## Predict

**Mia picks the pool (ticket $8) over a book ($12) and saving. She likes the book more than saving. What is her opportunity cost?**

- The $8 ticket
- The book and the savings
- The book she gave up
- Nothing, because she got what she chose

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: The book. It was her next best choice, and she gave it up. The $8 is the price, not the opportunity cost, and the savings was a worse option than the book. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Mia's choice

Now: how do you find the opportunity cost of a choice?

$$\text{List the options}$$

1) $\text{Rank them}$ — Pool first (chosen), book second, saving third.
2) $\text{Find the next best}$ — The book.
3) $\text{So}$ — The opportunity cost of the pool is the book, not the $8 and not everything else.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Start: write List the options and say "Pool, book, saving." Line 1: write Rank them and say "Pool first (chosen), book second, saving third." Line 2: write Find the next best and say "The book." Line 3: write So and say "The opportunity cost of the pool is the book, not the $8 and not everything else." -->

**Mia picks the pool. Her ranking was pool, then book, then saving. What did she give up?**

- The book, her next best option

* Answer: The book, her next best option

## Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is working out Mia's opportunity cost. Click the line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia has $20 and a free Saturday. She can buy a book, go to the pool, or save the money.
 2  She picks the pool, which costs $8.
 3  Nib says: "So Mia's opportunity cost is the $8 she paid."
 4  Nib says: "Or maybe it's everything she didn't do: the book, the savings and the sleep-in."
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the $8 is the price Mia paid. Opportunity cost is what she gave up, the book. Line 4 makes a second mistake: it counts everything she did not do, when only the next best option counts. -->

## Only the next best

**Why is the opportunity cost not 'the book, the savings and the sleep-in'?**

- Only the next best option counts, because you could only have chosen one
- Because opportunity cost is always money
- Because those are cheap
- Because Mia did not want them

* Answer: Only the next best option counts, because you could only have chosen one

<!-- Speaker note: You could only have made one different choice, so the cost is the best of the ones you did not pick. -->
