# Explaining why a business succeeds

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two Riverbend delivery businesses started in the same year. Five years later, one is busy and growing and the other has shrunk to one van. How would you explain the difference?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students combine entrepreneurial knowledge, skills and values with evidence from a business's story to explain what helped it succeed, including recognising an opportunity, initiative and innovation. The planted mistake is circular reasoning: explaining success with the result ('it made lots of money'). The lesson presents this as one way to explain success and names luck, timing and starting money as part of the story.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how two characteristics helped a business succeed, with evidence. I can tell an explanation from a description of the result. I can tell initiative from innovation.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: explaining or describing?


:::keep-together

### Two delivery businesses

1. $\text{Same start}$ — Both began the same year, each with a little money saved.
2. $\text{What changed}$ — New bike lanes opened and car traffic in town slowed.
3. $\text{Riverbend Cargo Bikes}$ — Saw the chance, tried a lunchtime trial, added cool boxes, kept to agreed times.
4. $\text{Dan's Deliveries}$ — Kept the same van routes and didn't try anything new.
5. $\text{So}$ — One way to explain the difference: what each did when things changed, plus the change that helped bikes and hurt vans.

Now: what is wrong with Nib's explanation?

**Nib is explaining why Riverbend Cargo Bikes succeeded. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Riverbend Cargo Bikes ran a lunchtime trial for three cafes before anyone asked.
 2  It fitted its bikes with cool boxes so it could carry medicine and fresh food.
 3  Nib says: "It succeeded because it made lots of money and grew."
 4  Nib says: "So the trial and the cool boxes didn't really matter."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: making money and growing is the success itself, so it can't explain the success. An explanation names what the business did, such as the lunchtime trial and the cool boxes.

:::

### Dan's Deliveries

**Dan's Deliveries kept the same van routes when the bike lanes opened and traffic slowed. Which explanation is the strongest?**

☐ He didn't change his routes when needs changed, and slower traffic hurt vans

☐ He wasn't a real entrepreneur, since real ones always succeed

☐ His business shrank because it made less money each year

☐ He was unlucky, so nothing he did made any difference

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** He didn't change his routes when needs changed, and slower traffic hurt vans

The strongest explanation names what Dan did (or didn't do) and also names the circumstance that made things harder.

## What counts as success?


:::keep-together

### Success for a not-for-profit

**Riverbend Pantry is a not-for-profit that keeps groceries affordable. Which is the best sign it is succeeding?**

☐ Its owners took home a big share of the money

☐ It has more customers than any other shop

☐ More families can afford fresh food, and it covers its costs

☐ It made the biggest profit of any shop in town

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** More families can afford fresh food, and it covers its costs

Success is measured against what the business is trying to do. For the pantry, that is affordable food for families.

:::


:::keep-together

### Explanation with evidence

**Which explanation of a business's success uses evidence?**

☐ The bakery succeeded because bakeries in busy towns always do well

☐ The bakery succeeded because its owner is a natural entrepreneur

☐ The bakery is successful because it's a really successful bakery

☐ It got busier after opening at 6 am for tradespeople, a need it spotted

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It got busier after opening at 6 am for tradespeople, a need it spotted

A good explanation links a characteristic (recognising an opportunity) to evidence (opening at 6 am) and an outcome (busier).
