# Explaining why a business succeeds

Entrepreneurs and business success · Values and success

<!-- Speaker note: Students combine entrepreneurial knowledge, skills and values with evidence from a business's story to explain what helped it succeed, including recognising an opportunity, initiative and innovation. The planted mistake is circular reasoning: explaining success with the result ('it made lots of money'). The lesson presents this as one way to explain success and names luck, timing and starting money as part of the story. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 14 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 6 min · Make 9 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** explain how at least two entrepreneurial characteristics contributed to a business's success, using evidence and naming what was outside its control.

- I can explain how two characteristics helped a business succeed, with evidence.
- I can tell an explanation from a description of the result.
- I can tell initiative from innovation.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: ACARA frames this standard as characteristics of entrepreneurs influencing business success. That is one way to explain success, and the lesson says so where students meet it. What it leaves out: luck and timing (the bike lanes opening, road works), money and connections to start with, and survivorship (we hear about the businesses that lasted, not the many that did the same things and closed). Researchers disagree about how much personal characteristics explain business outcomes. A discussion prompt: "If two people do exactly the same things and only one succeeds, what else is going on?" The wider step uses a business that closed despite doing things well, on purpose: the node's wider item "evaluate a failed business venture" can slide into blaming the owner's character. The Make step uses a real business the class has looked at; use its own published words. Teacher: Try: after the match, ask which of the four could Dan's Deliveries have done too. ## Why it matters

> Two Riverbend delivery businesses started in the same year. Five years later, one is busy and growing and the other has shrunk to one van. How would you explain the difference?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its businesses and people. Two more characteristics come into this lesson: - **Initiative:** acting on your own to start something or fix a problem, without waiting to be told. - **Innovation:** finding a new or better way of doing something that adds value. This lesson looks at **one way to explain** why a business succeeds: by what the people running it did. It is a useful way, not the whole story. Luck, timing, and how much money someone had to start with matter too, and the businesses we hear about are mostly the ones that lasted. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Two people open the same kind of business in the same street and work just as hard. One succeeds and one closes. What could explain the difference?**

- What might each of them have done differently?
- What might have happened to them that they couldn't control?

<!-- Speaker note: Collect both kinds of reason on the board. The lesson uses the first kind as one way to explain success, and keeps the second kind in view. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Two delivery businesses

**Nib is explaining why Riverbend Cargo Bikes succeeded. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Riverbend Cargo Bikes ran a lunchtime trial for three cafes before anyone asked.
 2  It fitted its bikes with cool boxes so it could carry medicine and fresh food.
 3  Nib says: "It succeeded because it made lots of money and grew."
 4  Nib says: "So the trial and the cool boxes didn't really matter."
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: making money and growing is the success itself, so it can't explain the success. An explanation names what the business did, such as the lunchtime trial and the cool boxes. -->

## Explaining or describing?

**Sort each statement about Riverbend Cargo Bikes: does it explain why it succeeded, or only describe the result?**

## Dan's Deliveries

**Dan's Deliveries kept the same van routes when the bike lanes opened and traffic slowed. Which explanation is the strongest?**

- He didn't change his routes when needs changed, and slower traffic hurt vans
- He wasn't a real entrepreneur, since real ones always succeed
- His business shrank because it made less money each year
- He was unlucky, so nothing he did made any difference
