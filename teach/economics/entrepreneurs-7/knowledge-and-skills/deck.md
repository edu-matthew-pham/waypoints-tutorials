**We are learning to** identify the knowledge and skills entrepreneurs use, with an example of each from a business.

- I can name the skills entrepreneurs use, with an example of each.
- I can explain why a good idea alone doesn't make a business.
- I can find those skills in a real business we have looked at.

## Why it matters

> Maya noticed that the farm boxes delivered around Riverbend arrived wilted on hot days. Lots of people noticed. Why did Maya end up with a business and they didn't?

<!-- Speaker note: An **entrepreneur** is someone who starts or runs a business, spotting a chance and acting on it. (People use the word in slightly different ways. Here we use the skills the Australian Curriculum names.) Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its businesses and people. Entrepreneurs bring **knowledge** (what they know about their customers, products and town) and **skills**. Three skills matter most here: - **Recognising an opportunity:** seeing a need that nobody is meeting well yet. - **Establishing a shared vision:** getting the people involved (workers, suppliers, partners) to agree on what the business is aiming for. - **Making decisions about how the business is run:** prices, places, times, tools, and what to change when something isn't working. -->

## Maya's Farm Boxes

Now: which skill was Maya using with the growers?

$$\text{Knowledge}$$

1) $\text{Recognising an opportunity}$ — Boxes arrived wilted on hot days, and no one was fixing it.
2) $\text{Shared vision}$ — Growers and drivers agreed: picked this morning, delivered by lunch.
3) $\text{Decisions about running it}$ — Routes, prices, and reusable cool crates instead of cardboard.
4) $\text{So}$ — The idea started it; the vision and the decisions made it work.

## Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining what made Maya an entrepreneur. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Maya noticed that farm boxes arrived wilted on hot days.
 2  She got three growers and two drivers to agree: picked this morning, delivered by lunch.
 3  She chose the routes, set the prices and switched to reusable cool crates.
 4  Nib says: "Spotting the wilting boxes was a real entrepreneurial skill."
 5  Nib says: "Getting the growers on board and choosing routes are just chores, not entrepreneurial skills."
 6  Nib says: "So having the idea was the only skill Maya used."
```

* Answer: line 5

<!-- Speaker note: Line 5: getting the growers on board is establishing a shared vision, and choosing routes and prices is making decisions about how the business runs. Both are entrepreneurial skills, not chores. Line 6 follows from the same mistake. -->

## Why the skills matter

**Why does it matter that the vision and the decisions count as skills, not just the idea?**

- Many people have good ideas; the skills to make them work are what start a business
- Because ideas are worth nothing in business at all
- Because the growers are the real entrepreneurs, not Maya
- It doesn't matter, because only the person with the idea is the entrepreneur

* Answer: Many people have good ideas; the skills to make them work are what start a business

<!-- Speaker note: Spotting the opportunity started it. The vision and the decisions are what turned it into a working business. -->
