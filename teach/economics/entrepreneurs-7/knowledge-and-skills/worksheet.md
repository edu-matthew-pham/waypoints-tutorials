# What entrepreneurs know and do

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Maya noticed that the farm boxes delivered around Riverbend arrived wilted on hot days. Lots of people noticed. Why did Maya end up with a business and they didn't?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify the knowledge and skills entrepreneurs use: recognising opportunities, establishing a shared vision and making decisions about how a business is run. The planted mistake is that the idea is the only skill. Students then find the skills in a real local business the class has looked at.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the skills entrepreneurs use, with an example of each. I can explain why a good idea alone doesn't make a business. I can find those skills in a real business we have looked at.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: Nib and the idea


:::keep-together

### Maya's Farm Boxes

1. $\text{Knowledge}$ — Maya knew which vegetables wilt fastest and how cold they need to stay.
2. $\text{Recognising an opportunity}$ — Boxes arrived wilted on hot days, and no one was fixing it.
3. $\text{Shared vision}$ — Growers and drivers agreed: picked this morning, delivered by lunch.
4. $\text{Decisions about running it}$ — Routes, prices, and reusable cool crates instead of cardboard.
5. $\text{So}$ — The idea started it; the vision and the decisions made it work.

Now: which skill was Maya using with the growers?

**Maya spent a week talking with growers until they all agreed on 'picked this morning, delivered by lunch'. Which skill was she using?**

☐ Designing a logo, so the boxes look good on the doorstep

☐ Recognising an opportunity, because she spotted the wilting problem

☐ Establishing a shared vision, so everyone works towards the same aim

☐ Making a profit, because the growers will now sell her more

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Establishing a shared vision, so everyone works towards the same aim

A shared vision is an aim the people involved agree on, so they pull the same way.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining what made Maya an entrepreneur. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Maya noticed that farm boxes arrived wilted on hot days.
 2  She got three growers and two drivers to agree: picked this morning, delivered by lunch.
 3  She chose the routes, set the prices and switched to reusable cool crates.
 4  Nib says: "Spotting the wilting boxes was a real entrepreneurial skill."
 5  Nib says: "Getting the growers on board and choosing routes are just chores, not entrepreneurial skills."
 6  Nib says: "So having the idea was the only skill Maya used."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 5

Line 5: getting the growers on board is establishing a shared vision, and choosing routes and prices is making decisions about how the business runs. Both are entrepreneurial skills, not chores. Line 6 follows from the same mistake.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why the skills matter

**Why does it matter that the vision and the decisions count as skills, not just the idea?**

☐ Many people have good ideas; the skills to make them work are what start a business

☐ Because ideas are worth nothing in business at all

☐ Because the growers are the real entrepreneurs, not Maya

☐ It doesn't matter, because only the person with the idea is the entrepreneur

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Many people have good ideas; the skills to make them work are what start a business

Spotting the opportunity started it. The vision and the decisions are what turned it into a working business.

## Spot the opportunity


:::keep-together

### Spot the gap

**Which of these is recognising an opportunity?**

☐ Asking the bank for a loan before deciding what to sell

☐ Opening a third pizza shop on a street that already has two, with the same menu

☐ Noticing that the town's only swimming pool has no lessons for adults, and starting them

☐ Choosing a bright colour for the shop's sign

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Noticing that the town's only swimming pool has no lessons for adults, and starting them

An opportunity is a need that no one is meeting well yet. Adult swimming lessons fill a gap.

:::


:::keep-together

### Sam's rule about tech companies

**Sam says: 'Only people who start giant tech companies are entrepreneurs.' What is the best reply?**

☐ Small local business owners and community groups that start something use the same skills

☐ Sam is right, because only big companies need a shared vision

☐ Only people who invent something new can be entrepreneurs

☐ Entrepreneurs have to have employees, so sole traders don't count

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Small local business owners and community groups that start something use the same skills

Entrepreneurs run businesses of every size, from a market stall to a national company.
