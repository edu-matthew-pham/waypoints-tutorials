# Values and the decisions entrepreneurs make

Entrepreneurs and business success · Values and success

## Today

**We are learning to** explain how a value shaped an entrepreneur's decision about an opportunity, stakeholders or compliance, and what it cost or gained.

- I can say what a value is and give an example of one shaping a business decision.
- I can explain who a business's stakeholders are, and how they differ from shareholders.
- I can say what a value might cost or gain a business.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a hinge lesson. The Worked example's stakeholder check, with its Build up to it question, is the check before moving on; give it the class time. ## Why it matters

> Leo could sell cheap new gadgets, or fix up old laptops for students who can't afford new ones. Fixing laptops earns him less on each sale. Why might he choose it anyway?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its businesses and people. Skills are *how* an entrepreneur gets things done. **Values** are what they think matters: fairness, safety, honesty, looking after the environment, helping the community. Values shape decisions in three areas: - **Taking an opportunity:** which chances to go after, and which to leave. - **Negotiating with stakeholders:** how to deal with the people affected. **Stakeholders** are everyone affected by a business's decisions: workers, customers, suppliers, neighbours and owners. - **Complying with the law and regulations:** following the rules every business must follow, and how seriously to take them. -->

## Leo's Laptop Repairs

**Leo buys parts from Sunita's Parts Supply. Is Sunita a stakeholder in Leo's business?**

- No, because she doesn't own any part of Leo's business
- Only if Leo makes a profit that year
- No, because stakeholders are only the customers

## Values that cost something

**Leo wipes every laptop's old files before he sells it, which takes him about an hour each. He does it because he values people's privacy. What does this show?**

- Values are bad for business, because they waste time
- Leo is only doing it because a law forces him to
- Values are personal opinions and don't change what a business does
- A value can cost a business time or money and still shape the decision

* Answer: A value can cost a business time or money and still shape the decision

## The bakery's flour

**Riverbend Bakery buys flour from a local mill that costs more than a big supplier, because the baker values supporting local farms. What is one likely result?**

- The bakery must close, because any extra cost makes a business fail
- The mill becomes a shareholder in the bakery
- Nothing changes, because values don't affect a business's costs
- Her costs go up a little, and the mill keeps a steady customer
