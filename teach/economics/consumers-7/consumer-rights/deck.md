## Today

**We are learning to** explain the consumer guarantees, warranties, cooling-off periods and how to return goods, and apply them to a situation.

- I can explain what the consumer guarantees are and that a sign can't remove them.
- I can tell a warranty from a consumer guarantee.
- I can say when a cooling-off period applies, and that a change of mind usually isn't covered.
- I can explain the first step in returning a faulty product.

## Why it matters

> Riverbend Appliances has a big sign: NO REFUNDS. Mia's new kettle stops working after a week. Is she stuck with it?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its people and shops. The rights in this lesson are real: they come from the Australian Consumer Law. When you buy from a business in Australia, some rights come with every purchase, automatically. They are called the **consumer guarantees**: - Products must be of **acceptable quality** (safe, free from faults, and lasting a reasonable time for what they are) and **match their description**. - Services must be done with **care and skill**. A business can't take these away. A "no refunds" sign doesn't remove them. - A **warranty** is an **extra promise** a business or maker can choose to give, such as "12 months' warranty". It sits on top of the guarantees and doesn't replace them. - **Change of mind** is different: if nothing is wrong and you just don't want it, the business doesn't have to give a refund, though many choose to. - A **cooling-off period** gives you time to change your mind after some agreements you didn't ask for, such as a door-to-door sale or a sales phone call. For those, it is usually 10 business days. - **Returning goods:** start by contacting the business that sold it, and keep your receipt or proof of purchase. -->
