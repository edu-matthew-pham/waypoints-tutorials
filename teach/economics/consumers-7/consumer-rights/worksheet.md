# Your rights when you buy

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Riverbend Appliances has a big sign: NO REFUNDS. Mia's new kettle stops working after a week. Is she stuck with it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain their rights when buying: the consumer guarantees (acceptable quality, matching the description, services with care and skill), what a warranty adds, why a change of mind isn't covered, cooling-off periods after unsolicited sales, and the first step in returning goods. The planted mistake is that rights end when the warranty ends.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what the consumer guarantees are and that a sign can't remove them. I can tell a warranty from a consumer guarantee. I can say when a cooling-off period applies, and that a change of mind usually isn't covered. I can explain the first step in returning a faulty product.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: after the warranty


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Warranty}$ — The maker promised 12 months. That extra promise is over.
2. $\text{Consumer guarantee}$ — Still there: a kettle should last a reasonable time.
3. $\text{First step}$ — Contact the shop that sold it, with the receipt.
4. $\text{So}$ — The warranty ending doesn't end Mia's rights. What she gets depends on the problem.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is working out Mia's rights for an older kettle. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia's kettle came with a 12-month warranty from the maker.
 2  Fourteen months later, it stops heating.
 3  Nib says: "The warranty is over, so Mia has no rights at all."
 4  Nib says: "There's no point asking the shop about it."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the warranty was an extra promise. The consumer guarantees are still there underneath, and a kettle should last a reasonable time. Whether Mia gets a repair, a replacement or a refund depends on the problem, but she does still have rights. So line 4 is wrong too.

:::


:::keep-together

### What should Mia do first?

**Mia's kettle has a fault. What should she do first?**

☐ Contact the shop that sold it, with her receipt or proof of purchase

☐ Throw it away and buy another one

☐ Post about it online and wait for the shop to see it

☐ Write to the government straight away

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Contact the shop that sold it, with her receipt or proof of purchase

The first step is to contact the business that sold it. The receipt shows when and where she bought it.

:::


:::keep-together

### Dev's jumper

**Dev buys a jumper with nothing wrong with it, then decides he doesn't like it. What are his rights?**

☐ He can always get a refund within 30 days

☐ The shop doesn't have to refund him, though it might choose to

☐ He gets a cooling-off period, because he changed his mind

☐ He can claim under the consumer guarantees

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The shop doesn't have to refund him, though it might choose to

A change of mind isn't covered by the guarantees. Some shops offer change-of-mind returns as their own policy.

## What applies?


:::keep-together

### The extended warranty

**Riverbend Appliances says Mia must buy an extended warranty, or she'll have no protection at all if the kettle breaks. Is that true?**

☐ Yes, but only for the first week after she buys it

☐ Yes: without the warranty she has no rights at all

☐ Yes, if she has thrown away the box it came in

☐ No: the consumer guarantees come with the kettle anyway

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: the consumer guarantees come with the kettle anyway

An extended warranty is an extra promise. It can't replace the consumer guarantees.

:::


:::keep-together

### Cooling off

**How long is the cooling-off period after a door-to-door sale?**

☐ 10 business days

☐ As long as the seller says

☐ There isn't one

☐ 24 hours

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10 business days

For a door-to-door sale, the cooling-off period is 10 business days.
