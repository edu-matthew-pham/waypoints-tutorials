# What businesses must do

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Brightbasket Supermarkets sold hundreds of baby rattles last month. Now tests show a small part can come loose. Whose problem is that?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain businesses' responsibilities for consumer and financial products and services: mandatory and voluntary standards, honest information, and product safety recalls. The planted mistake is that safety is the customer's problem once something is sold.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain the difference between a mandatory and a voluntary standard. I can explain what a product safety recall asks a business to do. I can give an example of a business's responsibility to give honest information.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the rattle recall


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Sold}$ — Brightbasket sold hundreds of rattles last month.
2. $\text{Found}$ — A small part can come loose: a choking risk for babies.
3. $\text{Rule}$ — Toys for children up to three must not have small parts that come off (mandatory).
4. $\text{Recall}$ — Warn parents, tell them what to do, stop selling, usually offer a refund.
5. $\text{So}$ — The business's responsibility for safety goes on after the sale.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is thinking about the rattle. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Brightbasket sold hundreds of a baby rattle last month.
 2  Tests now show a small part can come loose, and a baby could choke on it.
 3  Nib says: "Once it's sold, any problem with it is the customer's problem."
 4  Nib says: "So the recall is just Brightbasket being kind."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: a business's responsibility for safety doesn't end at the sale. The rattle still had to meet the safety rules, and when a sold product turns out to be unsafe, the business is expected to act, usually with a recall. So the recall isn't just kindness (line 4).

:::


:::keep-together

### What a recall asks for

**Which of these is part of what a recall asks a business to do?**

☐ Wait to see whether anyone gets hurt first

☐ Warn people, say what to do, and stop selling it

☐ Lower the price so it sells faster, then stop

☐ Keep selling the rattles until the stock runs out

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Warn people, say what to do, and stop selling it

A recall warns people, tells them what to do, and takes the product off sale. Recalls usually offer a repair, a replacement or a refund.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which kind of standard?

**The rattle broke a rule the law sets for toys for young children. What kind of standard is that?**

☐ A mandatory standard, because the law requires it

☐ A warranty, because it's a promise about the toy

☐ Not a standard, just a good idea

☐ A voluntary standard, because the maker chose it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A mandatory standard, because the law requires it

A standard the law requires is mandatory. Breaking it can bring fines, and the product may have to be recalled.

## Name the responsibility


:::keep-together

### The 'unbreakable' case

**A shop's ad says a phone case is 'unbreakable', but it cracks easily. Which responsibility did the shop fail?**

☐ Giving honest information

☐ Meeting a voluntary standard

☐ Setting a fair price

☐ Running a recall

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Giving honest information

Saying something that isn't true about a product misleads customers.

:::


:::keep-together

### Who a recall protects

**Who is a product safety recall mainly meant to protect?**

☐ The government

☐ The business's profits

☐ The people who bought or use the product

☐ Other shops that sell it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The people who bought or use the product

A recall protects the people who bought or use an unsafe product.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**What is the difference between a mandatory and a voluntary standard, and what does a product safety recall ask a business to do?**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A mandatory standard is required by law; a voluntary one is a business's choice. A recall asks the business to warn people about the danger, tell them what to do, and stop selling the product.
