## Why it matters

> Brightbasket Supermarkets sold hundreds of baby rattles last month. Now tests show a small part can come loose. Whose problem is that?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its people and shops. The rules in this lesson are real Australian rules. Businesses have responsibilities to the people who buy from them: - **Mandatory standards:** safety rules set by law that some products must meet. For example, toys for children up to three years old must not have small parts that can come off, because young children can choke on them. - **Voluntary standards:** standards a business can choose to meet, even though the law doesn't require them. - **Honest information:** a business must not mislead customers about what a product is or does. - **Product safety recalls:** if a product turns out to be unsafe, the business is expected to act. Usually it starts a **recall** itself, and the government can also order one. A recall tells people about the danger and what to do. Once a recall starts, selling the product is against the law. Recalls usually offer a repair, a replacement or a refund. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

$$\text{Sold}$$

1) $\text{Found}$ — A small part can come loose: a choking risk for babies.
2) $\text{Rule}$ — Toys for children up to three must not have small parts that come off (mandatory).
3) $\text{Recall}$ — Warn parents, tell them what to do, stop selling, usually offer a refund.
4) $\text{So}$ — The business's responsibility for safety goes on after the sale.

## Which kind of standard?

**The rattle broke a rule the law sets for toys for young children. What kind of standard is that?**

- A mandatory standard, because the law requires it
- A warranty, because it's a promise about the toy
- Not a standard, just a good idea
- A voluntary standard, because the maker chose it

* Answer: A mandatory standard, because the law requires it

<!-- Speaker note: A standard the law requires is mandatory. Breaking it can bring fines, and the product may have to be recalled. -->

## Exit ticket

**What is the difference between a mandatory and a voluntary standard, and what does a product safety recall ask a business to do?**

* A mandatory standard is required by law; a voluntary one is a business's choice. A recall asks the business to warn people about the danger, tell them what to do, and stop selling the product.
