# Rights and responsibilities

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mia buys a kettle at Riverbend Appliances. What can she expect from the shop? And what can the shop expect from her?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students distinguish rights (what you can claim) from responsibilities (what you must do), and see that both customers and businesses have both, often in pairs. This is the hinge lesson for AC9HE7K05: the next three lessons assign rights and responsibilities to each side, and students who think customers only have rights will assign them wrongly.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain the difference between a right and a responsibility. I can list rights and responsibilities for both a customer and a business. I can show how a right on one side pairs with a responsibility on the other.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: does Mia have responsibilities?


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Mia can claim}$ — A kettle that works, is safe, and is as described.
2. $\text{Mia must}$ — Pay for it, and use it the way the instructions say.
3. $\text{The shop can claim}$ — Payment for the kettle.
4. $\text{The shop must}$ — Sell safe kettles and tell the truth about them.
5. $\text{So}$ — Both sides have rights and responsibilities, and many of them pair up.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is working out the rights and responsibilities in Mia's sale. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia buys a kettle at Riverbend Appliances.
 2  Mia can expect the kettle to work and be safe.
 3  Nib says: "Customers only have rights. Businesses only have responsibilities."
 4  Nib says: "So Mia doesn't need to pay on time or read the instructions."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: both sides have rights and both have responsibilities. Mia must pay and use the kettle properly; the shop has a right to be paid. So line 4 is wrong too.

:::

### Rights that pair up

**Rights and responsibilities often pair up. Match each right to the responsibility on the other side.**

A. Mia's responsibility to pay the agreed price

B. The shop's responsibility not to mislead her

C. The shop's responsibility to sell safe kettles

### Right or responsibility?

**Which of these is a right, not a responsibility?**

☐ Getting a product that matches how it was described

☐ Reading the terms before you sign up

☐ Paying the bill on time

☐ Using the product the way it is meant to be used

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Getting a product that matches how it was described

Getting what was described is something you can claim. The others are things you must do.

## Spot the right


:::keep-together

### Same or different?

**Sam says: 'A right and a responsibility are the same thing.' What is the best reply?**

☐ A right is something you can claim; a responsibility is something you must do

☐ A right is for adults and a responsibility is for children

☐ A right is the law, and a responsibility is just good manners

☐ Sam is right: both are rules about shopping

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A right is something you can claim; a responsibility is something you must do

A right is something you can claim. A responsibility is something you must do.

:::


:::keep-together

### Not a right

**Which of these is NOT a consumer right?**

☐ A product that is safe

☐ Getting a discount if you ask nicely

☐ Honest information about what you are buying

☐ A product that matches its description

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Getting a discount if you ask nicely

A shop may choose to give a discount, but you can't claim one.
