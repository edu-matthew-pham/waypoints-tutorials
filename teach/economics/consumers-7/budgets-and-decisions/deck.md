## Today

**We are learning to** explain how a budget or savings plan supports a decision about a purchase, and link it to a consumer right or responsibility.

- I can read a budget and work out what is left over.
- I can write a formula for what is left over, and change the plan to see what happens.
- I can explain why checking a budget before committing helps, including with pay-later deals.
- I can link a purchase decision to one right or responsibility.

## Why it matters

> Sam wants a $240 bike. A sign says he can take it home today and pay later. Should he?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and Sam's budget is made up. A **budget** is a plan for your money: what comes **in**, what goes **out**, and what is **left over**. A **savings plan** uses what is left over to save for something over time. A budget helps you decide **before** you commit: can you afford this, and how long will it take? Some shops let you take something home now and **pay later** in parts. Paying later still has an opportunity cost: each repayment is money you can't use for something else then. Borrowing can be useful or costly, depending on what it's for. You'll look at that later. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

$$\text{In}$$

1) $\text{Out}$ — $25 + $15 + $40 = $80 a week.
2) $\text{Left over}$ — $110 − $80 = $30 a week, or $60 every two weeks.
3) $\text{Pay-later deal}$ — $60 every two weeks: all of it, with nothing spare.
4) $\text{So}$ — The budget shows the deal would use everything Sam has left. He should decide with that in view.

**If Sam buys the bike on the pay-later deal, what is the opportunity cost?**

- Only the bike shop's costs

- What else he could have done with $60 every two weeks

## After the purchase

**Sam saves up and buys the bike. Which is his responsibility now?**

- Using the bike the way it's meant to be used
- Getting a bike that is safe and works properly
- Getting a free warranty from the bike shop
- Getting a refund if he changes his mind later

* Answer: Using the bike the way it's meant to be used

## Exit ticket

**How does a budget help before buying something on a pay-later deal? Name one right or responsibility that also matters.**

* The budget shows what is left over, so you can check the repayments fit and see what else you'd give up. Rights: the product must be of acceptable quality. Responsibilities: read the terms, use the product as it is meant to be used. (Keeping the receipt is a good habit.)
