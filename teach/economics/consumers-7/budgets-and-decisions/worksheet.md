# Plan before you buy

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Sam wants a $240 bike. A sign says he can take it home today and pay later. Should he?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain how a budget and savings plan support better decisions about consumer and financial products, using a made-up weekly budget on a small spreadsheet, and link the decision to a right or responsibility. The planted mistake is committing to a pay-later deal first and budgeting afterwards. Debt is touched, not taught: paying later still has an opportunity cost, and borrowing can be useful or costly depending on what it's for.

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a budget and work out what is left over. I can write a formula for what is left over, and change the plan to see what happens. I can explain why checking a budget before committing helps, including with pay-later deals. I can link a purchase decision to one right or responsibility.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: Nib's pay-later plan


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{In}$ — $90 + $20 = $110 a week.
2. $\text{Out}$ — $25 + $15 + $40 = $80 a week.
3. $\text{Left over}$ — $110 − $80 = $30 a week, or $60 every two weeks.
4. $\text{Pay-later deal}$ — $60 every two weeks: all of it, with nothing spare.
5. $\text{So}$ — The budget shows the deal would use everything Sam has left. He should decide with that in view.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is giving Sam advice. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Sam wants a $240 bike.
 2  A pay-later deal splits it into four payments of $60, one every two weeks.
 3  Nib says: "Buy it today. We can work out the budget later."
 4  Nib says: "Sam will find the $60 somehow."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Sam should check his budget before he commits. He has $30 left over a week, which is $60 every two weeks: exactly the payment, with nothing spare. If a shift is cancelled, he could miss a payment, and missed payments can add fees. Line 4 hopes the money appears.

:::


:::keep-together

### The cost of paying later

**If Sam buys the bike on the pay-later deal, what is the opportunity cost?**

☐ The whole $240, paid on the first day

☐ Only the bike shop's costs

☐ Nothing, because he doesn't pay it all today

☐ What else he could have done with $60 every two weeks

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** What else he could have done with $60 every two weeks

Each repayment is money Sam can't spend or save for something else then. That is the opportunity cost of paying later.

## Budgets and rights together


:::keep-together

### After the purchase

**Sam saves up and buys the bike. Which is his responsibility now?**

☐ Using the bike the way it's meant to be used

☐ Getting a bike that is safe and works properly

☐ Getting a free warranty from the bike shop

☐ Getting a refund if he changes his mind later

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Using the bike the way it's meant to be used

Using a product as it's meant to be used is the buyer's part: damage from misuse isn't covered by the guarantees. Keeping the receipt is a good habit too, though other proof of purchase also works.

:::


:::keep-together

### Mia's view of budgets

**Mia says: 'Budgets are only for people who are short of money.' What is the best reply?**

☐ Anyone can use a budget to see what they can afford and plan ahead

☐ Budgets are only for businesses, not people

☐ Mia is right: if you have enough, you don't need a plan

☐ Budgets are only needed for things over $1000

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Anyone can use a budget to see what they can afford and plan ahead

A budget is a tool for anyone who wants to plan, whatever the amount.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**How does a budget help before buying something on a pay-later deal? Name one right or responsibility that also matters.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The budget shows what is left over, so you can check the repayments fit and see what else you'd give up. Rights: the product must be of acceptable quality. Responsibilities: read the terms, use the product as it is meant to be used. (Keeping the receipt is a good habit.)

:::
