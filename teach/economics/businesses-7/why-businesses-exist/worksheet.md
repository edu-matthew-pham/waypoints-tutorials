# Goods, services and why businesses exist

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** In Mia's town, a bakery sells bread, a bike workshop fixes punctures, and a community pantry sells low-cost groceries with no owner taking a profit. Are all three businesses, and why do they exist?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students distinguish goods from services and learn that businesses exist for several reasons — providing goods and services, making a profit, creating employment and meeting community needs. This is the hinge lesson for AC9HE7K02: if students think profit is the only reason a business exists, cooperatives and not-for-profits in lesson 2 will not make sense.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain the difference between a good and a service, with an example of each. I can give at least two reasons why businesses exist beyond making a profit. I can explain why a community organisation that sells goods or services counts as a business.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: more than profit


:::keep-together

### Three businesses in Mia's town

1. $\text{Corner Bakery}$ — Sells bread and pies (goods). Exists to provide food, earn a profit for the baker, and employ three staff.
2. $\text{Pedal Fix Workshop}$ — Repairs bikes (service) and sells parts (goods). Exists to keep riders moving and earn the mechanics a living.
3. $\text{Riverbend Pantry}$ — Sells low-cost groceries (goods). Exists to meet a community need and employ local workers, not for owner profit.
4. $\text{So}$ — Profit is one reason businesses exist, alongside providing goods and services, creating jobs, and serving a community.

Now: why does Riverbend Pantry exist?

**In Mia's town, Riverbend Pantry sells fresh food at low prices, pays two shop workers, and puts any spare money back into keeping prices down. Which two reasons explain why it exists?**

☐ To provide goods to the community and create employment

☐ To earn dividends for private shareholders

☐ To give away free charity parcels using only unpaid volunteers

☐ To make a large profit for a private owner

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** To provide goods to the community and create employment

Riverbend Pantry provides goods a community needs and employs staff, even though it does not run for an owner's profit.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why the reason matters

**Why does it matter for the rest of this course that businesses can exist for reasons beyond profit?**

☐ Because only charities are allowed to employ local workers

☐ Because some business types, such as cooperatives and not-for-profits, are set up around those other reasons

☐ Because businesses that sell services are not allowed to make a profit

☐ Because once a business employs staff, its owners can no longer keep any profit

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Because some business types, such as cooperatives and not-for-profits, are set up around those other reasons

### Sam's rule about factories

**Sam says: 'The local vet clinic doesn't make anything in a factory, so it isn't a real business.' What is the best reply?**

☐ The vet clinic is only a business on days when it sells pet food

☐ Sam is right: a business must manufacture physical objects

☐ It is a business that provides a service — checking and treating animals — and goods are not the only thing businesses sell

☐ The vet clinic is a charity because treating animals is a service

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is a business that provides a service — checking and treating animals — and goods are not the only thing businesses sell

Businesses provide goods, services, or both. Treating animals is a service.
