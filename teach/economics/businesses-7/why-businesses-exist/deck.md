# Goods, services and why businesses exist

Types of businesses · Why businesses exist

<!-- Speaker note: Students distinguish goods from services and learn that businesses exist for several reasons — providing goods and services, making a profit, creating employment and meeting community needs. ## Today

**We are learning to** tell goods from services and explain why businesses exist beyond making a profit.

- I can explain the difference between a good and a service, with an example of each.
- I can give at least two reasons why businesses exist beyond making a profit.
- I can explain why a community organisation that sells goods or services counts as a business.

## Why it matters

> In Mia's town, a bakery sells bread, a bike workshop fixes punctures, and a community pantry sells low-cost groceries with no owner taking a profit. Are all three businesses, and why do they exist?

<!-- Speaker note: A **business** is an organisation that provides goods or services to people. (All businesses and people in this course — such as Mia, Corner Bakery, Pedal Fix Workshop and Riverbend Pantry — are made up.) A **good** is a physical item you can touch and take away, such as a loaf of bread or a bicycle tyre. A **service** is work someone does for you, such as fixing a flat tyre, cutting hair or teaching a music lesson. Many businesses provide both at once. People often think businesses exist only to make a **profit** (money left over after all costs are paid). Profit is one common reason, but businesses also exist to **provide goods and services** people need, to **create employment** (jobs), and to **meet a community need**. -->

- To earn dividends for private shareholders
- To give away free charity parcels using only unpaid volunteers

## Why the reason matters

**Why does it matter for the rest of this course that businesses can exist for reasons beyond profit?**

- Because only charities are allowed to employ local workers
- Because some business types, such as cooperatives and not-for-profits, are set up around those other reasons
- Because businesses that sell services are not allowed to make a profit
- Because once a business employs staff, its owners can no longer keep any profit

* Answer: Because some business types, such as cooperatives and not-for-profits, are set up around those other reasons
