# Matching the structure to the job

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Fresh apples can reach Mia's family from a Saturday market stall, a national supermarket chain, an online box delivery, or a remote community store. Why don't all those businesses use the same structure?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students connect business purpose and structure to what goods and services are provided and how they reach customers across markets, independent stores, shopping centres, online platforms and remote stores.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a business's purpose and structure shape the way it provides goods or services. I can compare how two different business types reach customers. I can use business terms (sole proprietor, partnership, cooperative, corporation, franchise, not-for-profit, goods, services) accurately.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: two ways to provide food


:::keep-together

### Structure shapes provision

1. $\text{Solo Bike Mechanic}$ — Purpose: earn a living fixing local bikes -> Structure: Sole proprietorship -> Provision: personal repair service from an independent workshop.
2. $\text{Valley Growers}$ — Purpose: help 20 small farms reach buyers -> Structure: Cooperative -> Provision: shared coolroom and weekly market & grocer deliveries.
3. $\text{Made-Up Grocers Corp}$ — Purpose: national grocery sales and shareholder profit -> Structure: Corporation -> Provision: hundreds of supermarkets in shopping centres and online.

Now: why do different business structures provide goods and services in different ways?

**How does Made-Up Grocers Corp's structure (a corporation owned by thousands of shareholders) help it run supermarkets in shopping centres across every state?**

☐ Being a growers' cooperative means only member farmers are allowed to shop there

☐ Having one solo owner lets it make fast decisions without consulting anyone

☐ Being a not-for-profit means all its surplus must go into free community parcels

☐ Shareholder investment lets it fund warehouses, trucks and large stores across the country

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Shareholder investment lets it fund warehouses, trucks and large stores across the country

A corporation can raise large amounts of capital from shareholders, which funds the warehouses and store network needed for national distribution.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is advising a solo piano teacher and a town food pantry on their business structure. Click the line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Lin wants to teach piano from her living room two afternoons a week.
 2  Riverbend Pantry wants to use any spare money from grocery sales to keep food affordable for local families.
 3  Nib says: "A corporation is always the best structure for every business, because it is the biggest."
 4  Nib says: "So Lin and Riverbend Pantry should both set up as shareholder corporations."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib thinks a corporation is always the best structure for every business. A solo piano teacher needs simple, personal control (sole proprietorship), and a community food pantry exists to reinvest surplus into affordable food rather than pay shareholders (not-for-profit).

:::

### Why the growers teamed up

**Why does a cooperative structure suit twenty small orchards that want to supply fresh fruit to city grocers?**

☐ It requires the orchards to stop selling fruit at local markets

☐ It lets outside shareholders outvote the farmers on how the shed is run

☐ Pooling their harvest in a member-owned packing shed lets small growers fill big orders they could not supply alone

☐ It hands ownership of all twenty orchards to a single sole proprietor

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Pooling their harvest in a member-owned packing shed lets small growers fill big orders they could not supply alone

The cooperative structure lets small independent producers share expensive equipment and reach customers they could not reach alone.

## Explain two businesses


:::keep-together

**Choose two different business types (for example, a sole proprietor and a corporation, or a cooperative and a not-for-profit). Explain how each one provides goods or services differently, connecting its structure and purpose to how its goods or services reach customers.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sole proprietor vs corporation: A sole proprietor bike repairer owns the workshop alone to earn a living and provide a local service, so customers bring their bikes straight to the independent shop for personal repairs. A supermarket corporation is owned by shareholders to make a profit at scale, so it uses shared capital to buy goods in bulk and sell them through shopping centres and online delivery.; Cooperative vs not-for-profit: A growers' cooperative is owned by member farms so they can share a packing shed and supply fresh fruit (goods) to local markets and grocers together. A community pantry is a not-for-profit that exists to meet a community need, so it reinvests any surplus to keep groceries affordable at its local store.

:::
