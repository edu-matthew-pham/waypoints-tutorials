## Today

**We are learning to** explain two reasons people work beyond earning an income, using material and non-material living standards.

- I can explain two reasons people work besides earning an income.
- I can tell material from non-material living standards.
- I can back a reason with evidence from what someone says or does.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **What the idea leaves out.** ACARA frames work as contributing to identity, self-esteem and happiness. That is true for many people, and the lesson says "for many people" where students meet it. It leaves out that some work is tiring, unsafe, dull or badly paid, that many people work mainly because they have to, and that people who can't work or can't find work are not worth less. If a student says "my mum hates her job", that fits: work brings different things to different people. Don't ask students about their family's work. A discussion prompt if the class is ready: "If everyone had enough money without working, would people still work? What would they do?" **Living standards.** Material: goods and services money buys (food, housing, a phone, a holiday). Non-material: things money can't buy directly (friendships, health, safety, free time, a sense of purpose). Some things sit across both (health is helped by money but not bought outright); accept a reasoned answer. The wider match is "strongest for them right now". A student who argues Rosa's strongest reason is income has a point; ask for their evidence. All Riverbend people are made up. Teacher: Try: after the match, ask which of the five reasons the coach gets nothing from (income). Teacher: Worked example: let the class find Nib's line, then do the material/non-material question together. Teacher: Vary: Dev's view is a good moment to say work doesn't do all this for everyone. -->

## Why it matters

> Mai could earn more at a bigger shop, but she stays at Riverbend Pantry. The netball coach isn't paid at all. Why would anyone do that?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its people. **Earning an income** is one reason people work. For many people, work gives them other things too: - **Identity:** part of how they see themselves ("I'm a nurse", "I'm the coach"). - **Self-esteem:** feeling good about doing something well. - **A part in the community:** helping others and belonging to something. - **Living standards**, which come in two kinds. **Material** living standards are the goods and services money can buy, such as food, housing and a phone. **Non-material** living standards are things money can't buy directly, such as friendships, health, safety, free time and a sense of purpose. Work doesn't give all of this to everyone. Some jobs are tiring, unsafe or dull, and some people can't work or can't find work. That doesn't make anyone worth less. -->
