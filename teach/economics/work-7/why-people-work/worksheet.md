# Why people work

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mai could earn more at a bigger shop, but she stays at Riverbend Pantry. The netball coach isn't paid at all. Why would anyone do that?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain reasons people work beyond earning an income: identity, self-esteem, a part in the community, and material and non-material living standards. The planted mistake is that people only work for money. The lesson says 'for many people' on purpose: work doesn't do all of this for everyone.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain two reasons people work besides earning an income. I can tell material from non-material living standards. I can back a reason with evidence from what someone says or does.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: only for money?


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Mai}$ — Earns a wage. Stays for the community and the families she knows.
2. $\text{The coach}$ — Earns nothing. Gets pride, friendships and a part in the club.
3. $\text{Rosa}$ — Earns a profit. The yard is also part of who she is.
4. $\text{So}$ — Income is one reason. Identity, self-esteem, community and non-material living standards are others.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is explaining why people in Riverbend work. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mai works part-time at the pantry. She could earn more elsewhere, but she likes knowing the families.
 2  The netball coach isn't paid, but she runs training every week.
 3  Nib says: "People only ever work to get money."
 4  Nib says: "So the coach is wasting her time, and Mai made a mistake."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: income is one reason, not the only one. Mai stays for the community, and the coach volunteers for pride, friendships and the team. So line 4 is wrong too.

:::


:::keep-together

### Material or non-material?

**Which of these is a non-material living standard?**

☐ Buying groceries for the week

☐ Having good friends at work

☐ Being able to afford a new phone

☐ Paying the rent on time

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Having good friends at work

Friendships can't be bought. They are part of non-material living standards.

:::


:::keep-together

### Rosa's yard

**Rosa says, 'This yard is mine. It's who I am.' Which reason for working is she describing?**

☐ Material living standards, because the yard is a building

☐ Earning an income, because she owns a business

☐ Self-esteem, because she earns a high income

☐ Identity: the work is part of how she sees herself

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Identity: the work is part of how she sees herself

When work is part of how someone sees themselves, it adds to their identity.

## Name the reason


:::keep-together

### Leo's laptops

**Leo says he loves seeing a student's face when a laptop he fixed starts up. Which reason for working is that?**

☐ Self-esteem: he feels good about doing a job well

☐ Earning an income: the customer pays for the fix

☐ Material living standards: what his pay can buy

☐ Identity: owning a business is who he is

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Self-esteem: he feels good about doing a job well

Feeling good about doing something well is self-esteem.

:::


:::keep-together

### The volunteer firefighter

**A volunteer firefighter isn't paid, but trains every week. Which is the strongest reason?**

☐ To earn an income

☐ Because volunteers can't find paid work

☐ To raise their material living standards

☐ To help keep the community safe, and be part of the brigade

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** To help keep the community safe, and be part of the brigade

Helping the community and belonging to a group are reasons to work beyond income.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**Give two reasons someone might work besides earning an income, and say whether each is material or non-material.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** For example: friendships at work (non-material), pride in doing a job well (self-esteem, non-material), being part of a club or community (non-material). Money for a holiday or a car is material.
