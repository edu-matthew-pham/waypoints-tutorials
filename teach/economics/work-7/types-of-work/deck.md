**We are learning to** identify and describe types of work, paid and unpaid, with an example of each.

- I can name and describe at least four types of work, with an example of each.
- I can tell casual work from part-time work.
- I can explain why unpaid work is still work.

## Why it matters

> On a Saturday in Riverbend, six people are busy all day. Only four of them get paid. Are the other two working?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its people. **Work** is effort that does something useful for someone. Some work is **paid** and some is **unpaid**. Paid work comes in different arrangements: - **Full-time:** a full working week, usually around 38 hours. - **Part-time:** regular, set hours, but fewer than full-time. - **Casual:** no promise of ongoing hours. You work the shifts you are offered, even if they often fall on the same days. - **At home:** paid work done from home instead of a workplace. **Unpaid** work includes **volunteering** (giving your time to a group or cause) and caring or housework for your family. A lot of unpaid work is **unrecognised**: no one pays for it or counts it, even though people rely on it. -->

## Casual or part-time?

**Sam works at the bakery whenever they ring him. Mai works three set days every week. What is the difference?**

- There's no difference: casual and part-time mean the same thing
- Mai is casual because she only works three days
- Sam is casual (no promised hours) and Mai is part-time (set hours)
- Sam is part-time because he works fewer hours than Mai

* Answer: Sam is casual (no promised hours) and Mai is part-time (set hours)

<!-- Speaker note: Part-time means regular set hours, fewer than full-time. Casual means no promise of ongoing hours. -->

## Why unpaid work counts

**Why does it matter that unpaid work counts as work?**

- Because one day everyone who volunteers will be paid
- Because unpaid work is always more important than paid work
- Communities rely on it, even though it isn't paid or counted
- It doesn't matter, because unpaid work isn't useful

* Answer: Communities rely on it, even though it isn't paid or counted

<!-- Speaker note: Families, clubs and community groups rely on unpaid work. Calling it work helps us see it. -->
