# Types of work

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** On a Saturday in Riverbend, six people are busy all day. Only four of them get paid. Are the other two working?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify and describe types of work (full-time, part-time, casual, at home, paid, unpaid and volunteer) and learn that much unpaid work is unrecognised. This is the hinge lesson for AC9HE7K04: if students think only paid jobs are work, the next two lessons on income and why people work become narrow.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name and describe at least four types of work, with an example of each. I can tell casual work from part-time work. I can explain why unpaid work is still work.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: Nib's rule about work


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Paid}$ — Rosa (full-time), Mai (part-time), Sam (casual), Jo (at home).
2. $\text{Unpaid}$ — Ana (caring for her father), the coach (volunteering).
3. $\text{Test}$ — Is it useful effort for someone? Would someone else have to do it otherwise?
4. $\text{So}$ — All six are working. Pay tells you the type of work, not whether it is work.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is deciding who in Riverbend is working. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Rosa, Sam, Mai and Jo are all paid for the work they do.
 2  Ana cares for her father every day, and the netball coach runs training every week. Neither is paid.
 3  Nib says: "Only paid jobs count as work."
 4  Nib says: "So Ana and the coach aren't working."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: work is useful effort for someone, paid or not. Ana's caring and the coach's volunteering are unpaid work. So line 4 is wrong too.

:::


:::keep-together

### Casual or part-time?

**Sam works at the bakery whenever they ring him. Mai works three set days every week. What is the difference?**

☐ There's no difference: casual and part-time mean the same thing

☐ Mai is casual because she only works three days

☐ Sam is casual (no promised hours) and Mai is part-time (set hours)

☐ Sam is part-time because he works fewer hours than Mai

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sam is casual (no promised hours) and Mai is part-time (set hours)

Part-time means regular set hours, fewer than full-time. Casual means no promise of ongoing hours.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why unpaid work counts

**Why does it matter that unpaid work counts as work?**

☐ Because one day everyone who volunteers will be paid

☐ Because unpaid work is always more important than paid work

☐ Communities rely on it, even though it isn't paid or counted

☐ It doesn't matter, because unpaid work isn't useful

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Communities rely on it, even though it isn't paid or counted

Families, clubs and community groups rely on unpaid work. Calling it work helps us see it.

## Say what kind


:::keep-together

### Jo's work from home

**Jo works from home designing websites for paying clients. Is that real work?**

☐ Yes: it is paid work done from home

☐ Only if she works full-time hours

☐ No: real work happens at a workplace

☐ No: it only counts if she has a boss

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Yes: it is paid work done from home

Working from home is a type of paid work. The place changes; the work is the same.

:::


:::keep-together

### Unrecognised work

**Which of these is unpaid work that is usually unrecognised?**

☐ Designing a website for a paying client

☐ Cooking and cleaning for your household every day

☐ Running a car yard full-time

☐ A shift as a casual at the bakery

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Cooking and cleaning for your household every day

Housework is real work that someone has to do, but no one pays for it or counts it.
