# How people earn an income

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mrs Ng doesn't go out to work, but money comes in every week. Where could it come from?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify and explain the ways people derive an income: wages, salaries, business profit, dividends, rent and social security. The planted mistake is that income only comes from going out to work. Leo's month also separates a business owner's income (profit) from all the money customers pay.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name at least three ways people get an income, with an example of each. I can explain the difference between a wage and a salary. I can explain why a business owner's income is the profit, not all the money customers pay.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: Nib's income rule


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Working for someone}$ — Sam (wage), the principal (salary).
2. $\text{Owning something}$ — Leo (business profit), Theo (dividends), Mrs Ng (rent).
3. $\text{Government support}$ — Ana (social security, as a carer).
4. $\text{So}$ — Income comes from different economic roles. Going to work is one of them.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is working out who in Riverbend has an income. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Sam is paid a wage for each hour he works at the bakery.
 2  Mrs Ng's tenants pay her every week for her flat, and Theo is paid dividends on his shares.
 3  Nib says: "You only have an income if you go out to work."
 4  Nib says: "So Mrs Ng and Theo have no income."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: income can come from owning things (rent, dividends, a business) or from a government payment, not only from going to work. So Mrs Ng and Theo do have an income.

:::


:::keep-together

### Leo's month

**Customers paid Leo's Laptop Repairs $5000 this month. Parts, rent and power cost $3000. How much did Leo earn from his business this month?**

☐ $3000, what the business spent

☐ $5000, all the money customers paid

☐ $8000, the money in and the costs added

☐ $2000, the profit left after costs

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $2000, the profit left after costs

A business owner's income is the profit: $5000 − $3000 = $2000. It goes up and down each month.

:::


:::keep-together

### Theo's dividends

**Theo owns shares in a company. Why is he paid dividends?**

☐ He owns part of the company, so he shares its profit

☐ He works at the company, so it pays him for his work

☐ The government pays people who own shares

☐ He lent the company money, so it pays him back

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** He owns part of the company, so he shares its profit

Dividends are a share of profit paid to the company's owners, the shareholders.

:::

## What kind of income?


:::keep-together

### Rosa's income

**Rosa owns Rosa's Car Yard. How does she get her income from it?**

☐ From the profit left after the yard pays its costs

☐ From dividends, because she works there

☐ From all the money customers pay for cars

☐ From a wage the government pays business owners

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** From the profit left after the yard pays its costs

A business owner's income is the profit after costs.

:::


:::keep-together

### More than one source

**Which person has more than one source of income?**

☐ The principal, who is paid a salary monthly

☐ Sam, who works lots of shifts at the bakery

☐ Leo: business profit, plus a wage for an evening class

☐ Theo, who gets dividends twice a year

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Leo: business profit, plus a wage for an evening class

Leo has two: profit from his business and a wage from teaching.
