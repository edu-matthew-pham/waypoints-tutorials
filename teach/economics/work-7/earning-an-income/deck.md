## Today

**We are learning to** identify ways people earn or receive an income and explain the difference between two of them.

- I can name at least three ways people get an income, with an example of each.
- I can explain the difference between a wage and a salary.
- I can explain why a business owner's income is the profit, not all the money customers pay.

## Why it matters

> Mrs Ng doesn't go out to work, but money comes in every week. Where could it come from?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its people. **Income** is money a person receives, usually regularly. People earn or receive it in different ways: - A **wage**: paid for each hour (or week) you work for someone. - A **salary**: a set amount for the year, paid in regular instalments, however the hours fall. - **Profit** from owning a business: what is left after the business pays its costs. - **Dividends**: a share of a company's profit, paid to people who own **shares** in it. - **Rent**: money for letting someone use a house, flat or other property you own. - **Social security**: a government payment for people who need support, such as people who are older, caring for someone, living with a disability or looking for work. -->

## Leo's month

**Customers paid Leo's Laptop Repairs $5000 this month. Parts, rent and power cost $3000. How much did Leo earn from his business this month?**

- $3000, what the business spent
- $5000, all the money customers paid
- $8000, the money in and the costs added
- $2000, the profit left after costs

* Answer: $2000, the profit left after costs

<!-- Speaker note: A business owner's income is the profit: $5000 − $3000 = $2000. It goes up and down each month. -->
