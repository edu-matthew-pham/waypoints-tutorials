# Reading a trend

Responding to an economic issue · Reading economic data

<!-- Speaker note: Students use an interactive graph to read made-up data on casual and full-time workers in Riverbend over ten years, and describe each trend with its direction, how much it changed, and the period. ## Today

**We are learning to** identify and describe a trend in data over time, using an interactive graph.

- I can read values off a graph with a cursor.
- I can describe a trend with its direction, how much, and the period.
- I can tell a trend from one year's change.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The graph's legend says "Fulltime" (one word) because board series names can't contain a hyphen. All data is made up, shaped to show a clear rise in casual work with one dip, and a slow fall in full-time work. The years are numbered 1 to 10 on purpose, so the data isn't mistaken for real years. The elaboration's question ("For a 10-year period, to what extent has the number of people in casual work changed?") is the lesson's question. If you want real data, the ABS Labour Force releases include full-time and part-time employment; check what each table counts (casual is not the same as part-time) before using it. ## Why it matters

> Riverbend counted its casual and full-time workers every year for ten years. In one year, casual work dropped. Is casual work falling?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and this is made-up data. A **trend** is the overall direction of change over a period of time. One year's jump or dip isn't the trend: look at the whole period. Describe a trend in three parts: - **Direction:** up, down, or about the same. - **How much:** from what to what, or by how much. - **Over what period:** which years. For example: "Over the ten years, casual work rose from 420 to 650 workers." **Casual** and **full-time** work are the types from Work, income and identity: casual has no promise of ongoing hours; full-time is a full working week. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

$$\text{One year}$$

1) $\text{Whole period}$ — Year 1 to year 10: 420 up to 650.
2) $\text{Trend}$ — Over the ten years, casual work rose by 230 workers.
3) $\text{So}$ — Describe the whole period: direction, how much, over what period.
