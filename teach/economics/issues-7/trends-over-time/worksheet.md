# Reading a trend

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Riverbend counted its casual and full-time workers every year for ten years. In one year, casual work dropped. Is casual work falling?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use an interactive graph to read made-up data on casual and full-time workers in Riverbend over ten years, and describe each trend with its direction, how much it changed, and the period. The planted mistake is reading one year's dip as the trend.

**Students should be able to say:** I can read values off a graph with a cursor. I can describe a trend with its direction, how much, and the period. I can tell a trend from one year's change.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: one dip isn't a trend


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{One year}$ — Year 4 to year 5: 510 down to 440. A dip.
2. $\text{Whole period}$ — Year 1 to year 10: 420 up to 650.
3. $\text{Trend}$ — Over the ten years, casual work rose by 230 workers.
4. $\text{So}$ — Describe the whole period: direction, how much, over what period.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is describing the trend in casual work. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  In year 4 there were 510 casual workers in Riverbend.
 2  In year 5 there were 440.
 3  Nib says: "Casual work went down, so casual work in Riverbend is falling."
 4  Nib says: "Over the ten years, there are fewer casual jobs."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: one year's drop isn't the trend. Over all ten years, casual work rose from 420 to 650. So line 4 is wrong too.

:::


:::keep-together

### How much it grew

**By how many workers did casual work grow from year 1 to year 10?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 230

650 − 420 = 230 more casual workers.

:::


:::keep-together

### Describe the trend

**Which sentence describes the trend in casual work best?**

☐ Over ten years it rose from 420 to 650 workers

☐ Casual work went up and down over the years

☐ There were 650 casual workers in year 10

☐ It fell from 510 to 440 workers, in year 5

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Over ten years it rose from 420 to 650 workers

Direction (rose), how much (420 to 650), over what period (ten years).

## Compare the two


:::keep-together

### Which changed more?

**Over the ten years, which changed more?**

☐ They changed by the same amount

☐ Casual work: up 230, while full-time fell 90

☐ Neither changed

☐ Full-time work: down 230, while casual rose 90

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Casual work: up 230, while full-time fell 90

Casual rose by 230; full-time fell by 90.

:::


:::keep-together

### Both lines together

**What do the two trends together suggest about work in Riverbend?**

☐ More of the town's work may now be casual, and less full-time

☐ Most people in Riverbend now work as casuals

☐ Full-time work in Riverbend will disappear next year

☐ The two lines have nothing to do with each other

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** More of the town's work may now be casual, and less full-time

A reasonable inference from the two trends, one step beyond the data.
