# From information to an issue

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Fewer shoppers walk down Riverbend's main street every Saturday. More parcels arrive in town every week. What problem do these two facts point to?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students interpret information to identify an economic or business issue, making a reasonable inference beyond the data. The three-part move (the data shows, this suggests, so the issue is) is the scaffold. This is the hinge lesson for AC9HE7S03: students who repeat the data and call it the issue can't use data to say anything in the next two lessons.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a piece of information shows. I can make a reasonable inference one step beyond it. I can name the economic issue it points to.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Which of these is the best statement of the issue?**

☐ Every shop on the main street will close next year

☐ Shopper numbers fell from 900 to 600

☐ People in Riverbend don't like shopping any more

☐ More people may be shopping online, so main-street shops could be losing customers

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The data shows fewer shoppers and more parcels. That suggests people are shopping online more, so the issue is that main-street shops may be losing customers. 'Fell from 900 to 600' only repeats the data. 'Every shop will close' goes much further than the data does.

:::

## Worked example: the three-part move


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{The data shows}$ — Fewer shoppers on the main street, and more parcels delivered.
2. $\text{This suggests}$ — More people may be shopping online.
3. $\text{So the issue is}$ — Main-street shops may be losing customers.
4. $\text{Not}$ — "Shoppers fell from 900 to 600" (only the data) or "every shop will close" (too far).

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is working out the issue on the main street. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Saturday shoppers on the main street: 900, then 750, then 600.
 2  Parcels delivered each week: 1200, then 1650, then 2100.
 3  Nib says: "So the issue is that shopper numbers fell from 900 to 600."
 4  Nib says: "That's all the data tells us."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: that only repeats the data. An issue says what the data points to, such as main-street shops losing customers to online shopping.

:::


:::keep-together

### Data, inference, issue

**Which sentence follows the three-part move?**

☐ The data shows more parcels and fewer shoppers, and the numbers are 2100 and 600

☐ The data shows the shops will all close, so the issue is they will close

☐ The issue is that online shopping is bad and should be banned

☐ The data shows more parcels, which suggests more online shopping, so the issue is main-street shops losing customers

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The data shows more parcels, which suggests more online shopping, so the issue is main-street shops losing customers

Data, then a reasonable inference, then the issue it points to.

:::


:::keep-together

### Too far

**Why is 'every shop will close next year' a weak inference?**

☐ Because inferences should never go beyond the data at all

☐ Because shops never close

☐ The data shows a fall, not closures, so it goes much further than the evidence

☐ Because it uses the word 'year'

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The data shows a fall, not closures, so it goes much further than the evidence

A good inference goes one reasonable step beyond the data.

## Spot the inference


:::keep-together

### Rosa's sales

**Rosa's Car Yard sold 40 cars last year and 25 this year. Which is a reasonable inference, not just the data?**

☐ Rosa's Car Yard will have to close down next year

☐ Nobody in Riverbend wants to buy a car any more

☐ Fewer people may be buying cars, or buying elsewhere

☐ Rosa sold 15 fewer cars this year than last year

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Fewer people may be buying cars, or buying elsewhere

An inference says what the data might mean, one step beyond it.

:::


:::keep-together

### The order of the move

**Which is the best order for the three-part move?**

☐ Data shows, this suggests, so the issue is

☐ The issue is, this suggests, data shows

☐ Data shows, the issue is, so this suggests

☐ This suggests, the issue is, data shows

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Data shows, this suggests, so the issue is

Start from the data, step to an inference, then name the issue.
