# From information to an issue

Responding to an economic issue · Reading economic data

<!-- Speaker note: Students interpret information to identify an economic or business issue, making a reasonable inference beyond the data. ## Today

**We are learning to** interpret information to identify an economic issue, using an inference one step beyond the data.

- I can say what a piece of information shows.
- I can make a reasonable inference one step beyond it.
- I can name the economic issue it points to.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a hinge lesson. The Worked example's bug task, with its Build up to it question, is the check before moving on. ## Why it matters

> Fewer shoppers walk down Riverbend's main street every Saturday. More parcels arrive in town every week. What problem do these two facts point to?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and this is made-up data. **Information** tells you what happened. An **issue** is the problem or question it points to: something people or businesses need to think about or decide. To get from one to the other, make an **inference**: a reasonable step beyond what the data says. One way to write it: > The data shows ___. This suggests ___. So the issue is ___. | | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | |---|---|---|---| | Shoppers counted on the main street on a Saturday | 900 | 750 | 600 | | Parcels delivered in Riverbend each week | 1200 | 1650 | 2100 | -->

## Find Nib's mistake

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

$$\text{The data shows}$$

1) $\text{This suggests}$ — More people may be shopping online.
2) $\text{So the issue is}$ — Main-street shops may be losing customers.
3) $\text{Not}$ — "Shoppers fell from 900 to 600" (only the data) or "every shop will close" (too far).

## Data, inference, issue

**Which sentence follows the three-part move?**

- The data shows more parcels and fewer shoppers, and the numbers are 2100 and 600
- The data shows the shops will all close, so the issue is they will close
- The issue is that online shopping is bad and should be banned
- The data shows more parcels, which suggests more online shopping, so the issue is main-street shops losing customers

* Answer: The data shows more parcels, which suggests more online shopping, so the issue is main-street shops losing customers

<!-- Speaker note: Data, then a reasonable inference, then the issue it points to. -->

## Too far

**Why is 'every shop will close next year' a weak inference?**

- Because inferences should never go beyond the data at all
- Because shops never close
- The data shows a fall, not closures, so it goes much further than the evidence
- Because it uses the word 'year'

* Answer: The data shows a fall, not closures, so it goes much further than the evidence

<!-- Speaker note: A good inference goes one reasonable step beyond the data. -->

## The order of the move

**Which is the best order for the three-part move?**

- Data shows, this suggests, so the issue is
- The issue is, this suggests, data shows
- Data shows, the issue is, so this suggests

* Answer: Data shows, this suggests, so the issue is
