# Cause and effect in data

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** In Riverbend, households that earn more also tend to save more. Does earning more cause the saving, or does saving cause the earning?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read a made-up scatter of Riverbend households' weekly income and saving, place a new household on the pattern, and explain the relationship with a cause, an effect and an economic because. The planted mistake is reversing cause and effect and saying the pattern proves it. The lesson says that two things going together doesn't prove a cause, and that this habit transfers beyond economics.

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a pattern in a graph and use it to predict. I can name the cause, the effect and the because. I can explain why two things going together doesn't prove one causes the other.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

☐ Saving more makes people earn more

☐ It's a coincidence, so there's nothing to explain

☐ Earning more leaves more money after needs, so they can save more

☐ The pattern proves exactly how much anyone will save

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Income is the likely cause and saving the effect: more income leaves more after needs, so saving can rise. The pattern supports this but doesn't prove it on its own, and it doesn't tell you what any one household will do.

:::

## Worked example: cause, effect and because


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Pattern}$ — Households with more income tend to save more.
2. $\text{Cause}$ — Income rises.
3. $\text{Effect}$ — Saving can rise.
4. $\text{Because}$ — More is left over after paying for needs.
5. $\text{Careful}$ — The pattern supports this but doesn't prove it on its own.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is explaining the graph. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  In the graph, households with more income also save more each week.
 2  The two go up together.
 3  Nib says: "So the graph proves that saving more makes you earn more."
 4  Nib says: "If you want a pay rise, just save more."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: two things going up together doesn't prove a cause, and Nib mostly has it the wrong way round. The economic reasoning says more income makes saving possible. So line 4 doesn't follow.

:::


:::keep-together

### Cause, effect and because

**Which sentence explains the relationship with a cause, an effect and a because?**

☐ Income and saving both go up together across the graph

☐ More income can lead to more saving, because more is left after needs

☐ Saving goes up, so income goes up, because the dots go up

☐ Some households save more than others, so income varies

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** More income can lead to more saving, because more is left after needs

Cause (income), effect (saving), and an economic because (more left after needs).

:::


:::keep-together

### Why it isn't proof

**Why can't the graph alone prove that income causes saving?**

☐ Because saving and income have nothing to do with each other

☐ Because there are only twelve households, so the data is useless

☐ Because graphs are always wrong

☐ Two things going together doesn't prove one causes the other; something else could be involved

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Two things going together doesn't prove one causes the other; something else could be involved

The pattern supports an explanation. The economic because makes it convincing, but other things could also play a part.

## Name the parts


:::keep-together

### The bus

**After Riverbend's bus started running every two hours, fewer people shopped at the shopping centre. What is a reasonable because?**

☐ It became harder to get there without a car

☐ People stopped liking the shopping centre

☐ The bus made the shops more expensive

☐ Fewer shoppers caused the bus to change

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It became harder to get there without a car

Fewer buses made the trip harder, so some people shopped elsewhere.

:::


:::keep-together

### Sam's rule

**Sam says: 'The two lines go up together, so one definitely causes the other.' What is the best reply?**

☐ They might be linked, but going up together doesn't prove a cause

☐ Sam is right, but only for money

☐ Lines can never show a link

☐ Sam is right: going up together always means a cause

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They might be linked, but going up together doesn't prove a cause

A pattern is evidence for a cause, not proof. Look for the because, and for anything else that could cause both.
