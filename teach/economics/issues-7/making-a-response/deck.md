# Developing a response

Responding to an economic issue · Choosing a response

## Today

**We are learning to** develop a response to an economic issue that chooses one alternative and justifies it by weighing costs and benefits.

- I can work out and compare the costs of two alternatives.
- I can write a response with the issue, alternatives, costs and benefits, a choice and a because.
- I can explain why two people can choose differently and both be well reasoned.

## Why it matters

> Mia needs a phone plan. Plan A has more data; Plan B is cheaper each month but locks her in for two years. Which should she choose, and how would she decide?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and these are made-up phone plans. A **response** to an economic issue is a choice, with reasons. It chooses one **alternative** (option) over another, using the costs and benefits. A response has five parts: 1. **The issue:** what needs deciding. 2. **The alternatives:** the options. 3. **The costs and benefits** of each, financial and non-financial. 4. **The choice.** 5. **Because:** which costs and benefits matter most, and why. Listing costs and benefits isn't a response on its own. You **weigh** them: one big cost can matter more than three small benefits. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Plan A has three good points; Plan B has one. Is Plan A the better choice?**

- Does every good point matter the same amount?
- Could someone sensible choose Plan B?

<!-- Speaker note: Let the class argue it out before naming 'weighing, not counting'. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Find Nib's mistake

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

$$\text{The issue}$$

1) $\text{The alternatives}$ — Plan A ($35 a month, 30 GB, 12 months) or Plan B ($25 a month, 10 GB, 24 months).
2) $\text{Costs and benefits}$ — A: more data, short contract, costs $120 more a year. B: cheaper, less data, locked in for two years.
3) $\text{The choice}$ — Plan B, for Mia.
4) $\text{Because}$ — She uses little data, and saving $120 a year matters more to her than being free to switch.

## Two different choices

**Ari picks Plan A and Mia picks Plan B, using the same list. Can both be good responses?**

- No, the cheaper plan is always the right answer
- Yes, if each weighs the points for their own needs
- No, only one of the two plans can be the right one
- Only if they both end up choosing the same plan

* Answer: Yes, if each weighs the points for their own needs

<!-- Speaker note: A response is judged by its reasoning, not by matching one right answer. -->

## A non-financial reason

**Which reason uses a non-financial cost?**

- Plan A costs $420 a year, which is more than Plan B
- Plan B saves me $120 a year compared with Plan A
- Plan B costs $25 a month, paid on the first of each month
- Plan B locks me in for two years, so I'm stuck if my needs change

* Answer: Plan B locks me in for two years, so I'm stuck if my needs change

<!-- Speaker note: Being locked in isn't money, but it is something given up: the freedom to switch. -->
