# Developing a response

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Mia needs a phone plan. Plan A has more data; Plan B is cheaper each month but locks her in for two years. Which should she choose, and how would she decide?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students develop a response to an economic issue: choosing one alternative over another and justifying it by weighing costs and benefits. The case is two made-up phone plans (the elaboration's phone-and-budget example), with 12-month costs worked out on the sheet board. The planted mistake is counting the list instead of weighing it. The Make step is the student's own response, unmarked, with two model answers that choose differently.

**Students should be able to say:** I can work out and compare the costs of two alternatives. I can write a response with the issue, alternatives, costs and benefits, a choice and a because. I can explain why two people can choose differently and both be well reasoned.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: weighing, not counting


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{The issue}$ — Mia needs a phone plan.
2. $\text{The alternatives}$ — Plan A ($35 a month, 30 GB, 12 months) or Plan B ($25 a month, 10 GB, 24 months).
3. $\text{Costs and benefits}$ — A: more data, short contract, costs $120 more a year. B: cheaper, less data, locked in for two years.
4. $\text{The choice}$ — Plan B, for Mia.
5. $\text{Because}$ — She uses little data, and saving $120 a year matters more to her than being free to switch.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is helping Mia choose. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Plan A benefits: lots of data, a short 12-month contract, good coverage at the skate park.
 2  Plan B benefit: it costs less each month.
 3  Nib says: "Plan A has three benefits on the list, and Plan B has one."
 4  Nib says: "So Plan A is the answer, because it has more things on the list."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: counting the list isn't weighing it. If Mia doesn't use much data and wants to save, Plan B's lower cost might matter more than Plan A's three smaller benefits. A response says which points matter most, and why.

:::


:::keep-together

### Response or list?

**Which of these is a response, not just a list?**

☐ Plan A has more data each month, and Plan B is cheaper

☐ Both plans have good and bad points, so either will do

☐ I choose Plan B: saving $120 matters more than data I won't use

☐ Plan A costs $420 a year and Plan B costs $300 a year

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** I choose Plan B: saving $120 matters more than data I won't use

A response makes a choice and says which costs and benefits matter most, and why.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two different choices

**Ari picks Plan A and Mia picks Plan B, using the same list. Can both be good responses?**

☐ No, the cheaper plan is always the right answer

☐ Yes, if each weighs the points for their own needs

☐ No, only one of the two plans can be the right one

☐ Only if they both end up choosing the same plan

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Yes, if each weighs the points for their own needs

A response is judged by its reasoning, not by matching one right answer.

## Parts of a response


:::keep-together

### Dev's response

**Dev writes: 'I choose Plan A.' What is missing?**

☐ Nothing: he made a choice, and that's the response

☐ A longer list of Plan A's benefits

☐ What each plan costs over the whole year

☐ The because: which points mattered most, and why

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The because: which points mattered most, and why

A response needs the reason, linked to the costs and benefits.

:::


:::keep-together

### A non-financial reason

**Which reason uses a non-financial cost?**

☐ Plan A costs $420 a year, which is more than Plan B

☐ Plan B saves me $120 a year compared with Plan A

☐ Plan B costs $25 a month, paid on the first of each month

☐ Plan B locks me in for two years, so I'm stuck if my needs change

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Plan B locks me in for two years, so I'm stuck if my needs change

Being locked in isn't money, but it is something given up: the freedom to switch.

:::
