## Today

**We are learning to** identify financial and non-financial costs and benefits of a decision, including the opportunity cost.

- I can list the costs and benefits of a decision.
- I can tell financial from non-financial costs and benefits.
- I can name the opportunity cost as one of the costs.

## Why it matters

> Sam has been offered Saturday morning shifts at Riverbend Bakery. The pay is good and the bus is cheap. Is that the whole story?

<!-- Speaker note: Riverbend is a made-up town, and so are its people. Every economic decision has **costs** and **benefits**. - **Costs** are what you give up: money, time, and the other things you could have done instead. - **Benefits** are what you gain: money, time saved, skills, enjoyment, friendships, or good for the community. Both come in two kinds: - **Financial:** counted in money (pay, fares, prices). - **Non-financial:** not counted in money, but still real (time, tiredness, skills, enjoyment). The **opportunity cost** of a choice (the next best thing you give up) is always one of its costs, and it is often non-financial. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Sam's offered Saturday shifts. The pay is good and the bus is cheap. Should he take them?**

- What would he gain?
- What would he give up, including things that aren't money?

## Predict

**Sam would earn $90 each Saturday, and the bus costs $8. What are the costs of taking the shifts?**

- Nothing: he earns more than the fare, so there's no cost
- The bus fare, and his Saturday mornings he can't spend on anything else
- Only the $90 he earns
- Only the $8 bus fare

## Find Nib's mistake

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

$$\text{Financial benefit}$$

1) $\text{Financial cost}$ — $8 bus fare.
2) $\text{Non-financial costs}$ — Netball (his next best choice, so his opportunity cost), and less time with friends.

4) $\text{So}$ — Money is part of the picture, not all of it.

## Sam's opportunity cost

**What is Sam's opportunity cost of taking the shifts?**

- The $90 he earns at the bakery each Saturday
- Nothing, because he is earning money for it
- The $8 bus fare he pays to get there and back
- His Saturday netball game, his next best choice

* Answer: His Saturday netball game, his next best choice

<!-- Speaker note: Opportunity cost is the one next best alternative given up: here, his Saturday netball game. Less time with friends is another non-financial cost. -->

## Free isn't costless

**Mia is deciding whether to join the school band. Lessons are free. Dev says, 'It's free, so there are no costs.' What is the best reply?**

- Still costs: the time she gives up to practise
- Dev is right: no money means no costs
- The only cost is buying an instrument
- There's no cost, since she'll enjoy it

* Answer: Still costs: the time she gives up to practise

<!-- Speaker note: Non-financial costs, like time, count even when something is free. -->
