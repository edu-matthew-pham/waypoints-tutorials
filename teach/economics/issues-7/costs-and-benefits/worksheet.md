# Costs and benefits

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Sam has been offered Saturday morning shifts at Riverbend Bakery. The pay is good and the bus is cheap. Is that the whole story?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students identify the potential costs and benefits of a decision, financial and non-financial, and see the opportunity cost as one of the costs. The case is Sam deciding whether to take Saturday shifts (the elaboration's part-time work example). This is the hinge lesson for AC9HE7S04: students who count only money will weigh every response in the next lesson wrongly.

**Students should be able to say:** I can list the costs and benefits of a decision. I can tell financial from non-financial costs and benefits. I can name the opportunity cost as one of the costs.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Sam would earn $90 each Saturday, and the bus costs $8. What are the costs of taking the shifts?**

☐ Nothing: he earns more than the fare, so there's no cost

☐ The bus fare, and his Saturday mornings he can't spend on anything else

☐ Only the $90 he earns

☐ Only the $8 bus fare

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The bus fare is a financial cost. Giving up his Saturday mornings is a non-financial cost. His next best choice for that time, his Saturday netball game, is his opportunity cost.

:::

## Worked example: more than money


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

1. $\text{Financial benefit}$ — $90 pay each Saturday.
2. $\text{Financial cost}$ — $8 bus fare.
3. $\text{Non-financial costs}$ — Netball (his next best choice, so his opportunity cost), and less time with friends.
4. $\text{Non-financial benefits}$ — Skills, a reference, new friends.
5. $\text{So}$ — Money is part of the picture, not all of it.

Now: where does Nib go wrong?

**Nib is weighing up Sam's Saturday shifts. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Sam could work Saturday mornings at the bakery for $90.
 2  The bus there and back costs $8.
 3  Nib says: "The only cost is the bus fare."
 4  Nib says: "So the shifts are $82 of pure benefit."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the bus fare is a cost, but not the only one. Sam also gives up his Saturday mornings, his netball game and time with friends. So line 4 leaves out real costs.

:::


:::keep-together

### Sam's opportunity cost

**What is Sam's opportunity cost of taking the shifts?**

☐ The $90 he earns at the bakery each Saturday

☐ Nothing, because he is earning money for it

☐ The $8 bus fare he pays to get there and back

☐ His Saturday netball game, his next best choice

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** His Saturday netball game, his next best choice

Opportunity cost is the one next best alternative given up: here, his Saturday netball game. Less time with friends is another non-financial cost.

:::


:::keep-together

### A non-financial benefit

**Which of these is a non-financial benefit?**

☐ Missing his Saturday netball game

☐ The $90 pay he takes home each Saturday

☐ The bus fare he pays to get to work

☐ Making new friends among the bakery staff

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Making new friends among the bakery staff

Friendships aren't counted in money, but they are something gained.

:::

## Costs and benefits


:::keep-together

### Free isn't costless

**Mia is deciding whether to join the school band. Lessons are free. Dev says, 'It's free, so there are no costs.' What is the best reply?**

☐ Still costs: the time she gives up to practise

☐ Dev is right: no money means no costs

☐ The only cost is buying an instrument

☐ There's no cost, since she'll enjoy it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Still costs: the time she gives up to practise

Non-financial costs, like time, count even when something is free.

:::


:::keep-together

### Benefit or cost?

**Which is a benefit, not a cost?**

☐ Getting better at an instrument

☐ The bus fare to rehearsals

☐ Giving up Tuesday afternoons

☐ Missing a friend's birthday party for a concert

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Getting better at an instrument

A skill gained is a non-financial benefit.
