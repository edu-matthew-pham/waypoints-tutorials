## Today

**We are learning to** identify attitudes and values of the past in a source, and explain how its words, pictures or assumptions show them, before judging them.

- I can name an attitude or value a source shows.
- I can explain how the source shows it, using its words, a picture or what it takes for granted.
- I can describe a past value in its time before I judge it.

## Why it matters

> A saying of Confucius, studied for centuries, says women and servants are hard to handle. What does that tell us about the world it came from?

<!-- Speaker note: A source can tell you about more than its maker. It can show the **attitudes and values** of their whole society: what people thought was right, important or normal. Historians call this reading **the world behind the source**. Values show up in three places: - **the words** a source uses, - **the pictures and symbols** it uses, - **what it takes for granted**: things it doesn't bother to explain, because everyone then accepted them. **Confucius** (551 to 479 BCE) was a teacher. After he died, his followers collected his sayings in a book, the *Analects*, and officials in Han China studied it closely. One saying, in the words the Australian Curriculum uses, is: > "Women and underlings are especially difficult to handle." *Underlings* means servants and people of low rank. We are reading this saying as evidence of past attitudes, not because it is right. -->

## The silk painting

**In the middle of the Mawangdui painting, the lady has attendants and her family make offerings for her. Which values does that show?**

- Her high rank mattered even after death, and family had a duty to the dead
- Servants and noblewomen were treated as equals in Han China
- Han people did not care about what happened after someone died
- Every person in Han China was buried with a painting like this one

* Answer: Her high rank mattered even after death, and family had a duty to the dead

<!-- Speaker note: The picture takes for granted that rank and family duty carry on after death. -->

## What each part shows

**Match each part of the Mawangdui painting to the attitude or value it suggests.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Family members making offerings` | A. Rank still mattered after death |
| `Attendants around the lady` | B. Care to keep the dead person's body safe |
| `Heaven at the top, with the sun and moon` | C. Belief in a world beyond this one |
