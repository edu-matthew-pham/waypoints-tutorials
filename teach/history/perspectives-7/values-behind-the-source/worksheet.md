# The world behind the source

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A saying of Confucius, studied for centuries, says women and servants are hard to handle. What does that tell us about the world it came from?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Identify the attitudes and values of the society that produced a source, explain how the source shows them (words, images and symbols, what it takes for granted), and try to understand them in their time rather than only by today's standards.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name an attitude or value a source shows. I can explain how the source shows it, using its words, a picture or what it takes for granted. I can describe a past value in its time before I judge it.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: words, pictures, what it takes for granted

| Where to look | In the Confucius saying |
|---|---|
| The words | "Handle": women and servants are to be managed |
| Who is speaking, to whom | A respected teacher, to male students |
| What it takes for granted | A household where a man is in charge and everyone has a rank |
| So the value it shows | Order by rank, with men of rank at the top |

Pictures work the same way. In 1972, archaeologists opened a Han tomb at **Mawangdui**, in Changsha. It held a T-shaped painting on silk, about 2 metres long, made about 2,200 years ago for a noblewoman, Lady Xin Zhui. From top to bottom it shows:

- **Heaven**: the sun (with a crow in it) and the moon (with a toad and a hare)
- **The lady**: with attendants around her; below, her family make offerings for her
- **The world below**: strange creatures that were believed to guard the body

You can see the painting on the [Hunan Museum's page](https://web.hnmuseum.com/en/node/7370).

### The silk painting

**In the middle of the Mawangdui painting, the lady has attendants and her family make offerings for her. Which values does that show?**

☐ Her high rank mattered even after death, and family had a duty to the dead

☐ Servants and noblewomen were treated as equals in Han China

☐ Han people did not care about what happened after someone died

☐ Every person in Han China was buried with a painting like this one

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Her high rank mattered even after death, and family had a duty to the dead

The picture takes for granted that rank and family duty carry on after death.

## What each part shows


:::keep-together

**Match each part of the Mawangdui painting to the attitude or value it suggests.**

A. Rank still mattered after death

B. Care to keep the dead person's body safe

C. Belief in a world beyond this one

D. A duty to care for the dead
