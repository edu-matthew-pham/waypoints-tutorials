## Today

**We are learning to** tell apart what a source describes and the perspective of the person who made it, and say how the maker's position shaped it.

- I can say what a source describes and, separately, whose perspective it shows.
- I can use the four questions to work out a perspective.
- I can explain how a maker's position shaped what they noticed.

## Why it matters

> A Han court historian wrote about the Xiongnu, the herding people to the north. He tells us how they lived. What else does he tell us without meaning to?

<!-- Speaker note: A source tells you about **what it describes**: that is its **content**. It also tells you about **the person who made it**: what they noticed, what they thought was normal, what they liked and disliked. That second thing is the source's **perspective**, its point of view. A perspective is not the same as a lie. Everyone sees from somewhere. A historian's job is to notice where. These four questions get you there: 1. **Who made it?** 2. **What was their position?** (their job, their group, where and when they lived) 3. **What would someone in that position notice, and care about?** 4. **So what perspective does the source show?** Here is a source retold in our own words (not a translation). **Sima Qian** was a historian at the Han emperor's court, writing around 100 BCE. In his history he described the **Xiongnu**, a herding people to the north. Han writers say Xiongnu horsemen often raided the Han border. No writing of the Xiongnu's own has been found. > The Xiongnu live in the north. They move with their herds, following water and grass. They have no walled cities and do not farm. Their boys learn to ride and shoot when they are small. The strong eat the best food and the old get what is left: they look down on the old and weak. They do not know proper behaviour. -->

## Predict

**'They do not know proper behaviour.' What does this sentence tell a historian most about?**

- Nothing at all, because it is only an opinion
- How the Xiongnu actually behaved every day
- What the Xiongnu thought about themselves
- What Sima Qian thought counted as proper behaviour

## Where he stood

**Which fact about Sima Qian best explains his perspective on the Xiongnu?**

- He worked for the Han court, whose border the Xiongnu raided
- He lived more than two thousand years before us
- He wrote his history on bamboo strips in ink, by hand
- He wrote about many other peoples as well as the Xiongnu

## Vary: the emperor's own words

You may have met this source before, as Source C. It is words cut into stone, set up on a mountain by order of the First Emperor on one of his tours, around 219 BCE. In our words: it praises the emperor as a ruler without equal. It says he brought peace and order, that everyone now lives in peace, and that everyone lives under one set of laws, weights and measures.

## Make: Typical: Greek writers and 'barbarians'

Ancient Greek writers called people who did not speak Greek *barbaroi*. Historians think the word first imitated how other languages sounded to Greek ears ("bar-bar").

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: A word one group used for everyone else shows perspective in a single word, in another society. -->

## Make: Wider: two sides of one battle

Around 1274 BCE, Egypt and the Hittite empire fought at **Kadesh**, in what is now Syria. Pharaoh Ramesses II had the battle carved on temple walls across Egypt as his great victory. Hittite records tell it differently, and about fifteen years later the two powers made a peace treaty.
