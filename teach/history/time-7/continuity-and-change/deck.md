## Today

**We are learning to** explain what changed and what stayed the same across a turning point, using dated evidence for each.

- I can name something that changed and something that stayed the same across a turning point.
- I can back each one up with a dated source or event.
- I can explain why both can be true of the same period.

## Why it matters

> By 206 BCE the Qin dynasty had fallen, and soon a new family took the throne. Did everything start again?

<!-- Speaker note: When historians look at a turning point, they ask two questions: - **Change:** what was different afterwards? - **Continuity:** what stayed the same? The answer is nearly always *both*. A new ruler can change some things while most people's lives, and much of how the country is run, carry on. By 206 BCE the Qin dynasty had fallen. A commoner called Liu Bang won the war that followed, in 202 BCE, and became the first emperor of the **Han** dynasty. The Han count their rule from 206 BCE, and they ruled, with one short break, for about 400 years. -->

## What happened?

* Some things changed (the ruling family, and later the ideas used to choose officials), but much stayed: officials running commanderies, the written script, standard measures, the empire's borders, and most people's lives as farmers.

## Sources in order

**Match each dated source to what it shows.**

## Both at once

**Which sentence best describes the change from Qin to Han?**

- The Han threw out everything the Qin had built and began again
- A new family ruled, but it kept much of the Qin way of running the empire
- Nothing changed, because the Han and Qin were the same dynasty
- Everything changed at once in 206 BCE and then stayed the same

* Answer: A new family ruled, but it kept much of the Qin way of running the empire

<!-- Speaker note: Historians describe periods as a mix: some things continue while others change. What this step is for: Choosing the statement that holds both together checks the 'coexist' part of the checkpoint. -->
