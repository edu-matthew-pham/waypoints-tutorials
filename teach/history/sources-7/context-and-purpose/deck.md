# Why was it made, and what was going on?

Source origin and purpose · Why was it made?

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn the next two questions to ask of any source: context (what was going on when it was made, not just the place) and purpose (why it was made). The second hinge lesson on this skill: students who answer 'ancient China' for context and 'it shows the emperor' for purpose cannot combine the four questions in the next lesson. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 6 min · Make 6 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

## Today

- I can say what was going on when a source was made, beyond its place and period.
- I can say why a source was made, without mixing that up with what it shows.
- I can write a context and a purpose for a source in my own words.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a hinge lesson. Lesson 4 (what can it tell us?) needs students to treat all four questions as distinct. If this lesson goes thinly, the combined conclusion will blur context with setting and purpose with content. Spend the time here. Context and purpose are sometimes opinions or inferences. For ancient sources, historians reason from what they know of the time. The cards give one defensible reading. If your class's depth study has a different view of why an inscription or a tomb was made, discuss it. Judge the reasoning, not whether it matches the card. The worked example is ancient China in the Qin and Han period, and it is a model: the same questions work on any society. For the tomb (Source A), the usual reading is that the army was made to protect the emperor after his death. Keep modern links factual: the site is a World Heritage site; avoid claims about China today. Everything beyond the described sources is made up (Ravi, Dev). Do not ask students about their families or beliefs. Teacher: Try: the match has four different purposes for four sources. Ask students to say why the label is different from the other three. ## Why it matters

> The First Emperor had words cut into stone on mountains. Why would a ruler do that? What was going on that made it worth the trouble?

<!-- Speaker note: Once you know **who** made a source and **what** it shows, two more questions help you read it. **Context** is what was going on around the source when it was made. It is the particular events and conditions of the time, not just the place or the period. "Ancient China" is a place and a time, but it is not a context. **Purpose** is **why** it was made. What was it for? What did the maker want people to do, think or remember? Here is a source from the last lesson, with its context filled in. > **Source C: an inscription.** Words cut into stone, set up on a mountain by order of the First Emperor on one of his tours, around 219 BCE. It says the emperor brought peace and order, and that everyone now lives under one set of laws, weights and measures. *Context:* a few years earlier the Qin had defeated six rival kingdoms and joined them into one empire. Many people in the new empire had been enemies of the Qin. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Why would a ruler cut words into stone on a mountain? What was going on that made it worth the trouble?**

- Who was meant to read it?
- What would the ruler want them to think?

<!-- Speaker note: Accept a range of answers if the reasoning is sound. Push for something specific that was happening, not a general reason. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Find the context

**Which context explains why the First Emperor might want an inscription like Source C?**

- He liked carving stone
- China is a big country
- Stone lasts a long time
- He had just joined rival kingdoms into one empire and wanted people to accept his rule

* Answer: He had just joined rival kingdoms into one empire and wanted people to accept his rule

<!-- Speaker note: He had just united rival kingdoms. Many people in them had been Qin's enemies, so he had reason to tell them the new empire was good and here to stay. -->

## Say the purpose

**Which is the best statement of the purpose of Source C?**

- To show the emperor
- To show the emperor's power and win people's support for his rule
- To be made of stone
- To say the emperor brought peace

* Answer: To show the emperor's power and win people's support for his rule

<!-- Speaker note: Purpose is what the maker wanted it to do. Showing power and winning support is a reason. 'It shows the emperor' only says what is in it. -->
