# Why was it made, and what was going on?

Source origin and purpose · Why was it made?

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn the next two questions to ask of any source: context (what was going on when it was made, not just the place) and purpose (why it was made). ## Today

**We are learning to** say what was going on when a source was made (context) and why it was probably made (purpose).

- I can say what was going on when a source was made, beyond its place and period.
- I can say why a source was made, without mixing that up with what it shows.
- I can write a context and a purpose for a source in my own words.

## Why it matters

> The First Emperor had words cut into stone on mountains. Why would a ruler do that? What was going on that made it worth the trouble?

<!-- Speaker note: Once you know **who** made a source and **what** it shows, two more questions help you read it. **Context** is what was going on around the source when it was made. It is the particular events and conditions of the time, not just the place or the period. "Ancient China" is a place and a time, but it is not a context. **Purpose** is **why** it was made. What was it for? What did the maker want people to do, think or remember? For ancient sources, the maker rarely tells us the purpose. No Qin text says why the clay army was made, for example. Historians work out a likely purpose from the source, its context and other evidence, so purposes are often said with "probably" or "historians think". Here is a source from the last lesson, with its context filled in. > **Source C: an inscription.** Words cut into stone, set up on a mountain by order of the First Emperor on one of his tours, around 219 BCE. It praises the emperor as a ruler without equal. It says he brought peace and order, that everyone now lives in peace, and that everyone lives under one set of laws, weights and measures. *Context:* a few years earlier the Qin had defeated six rival kingdoms and joined them into one empire. Many people in the new empire had been enemies of the Qin. -->

## Find the context

**Which context explains why the First Emperor might want an inscription like Source C?**

- He was travelling on a tour of the mountains when it was carved

* Answer: He had just joined six rival kingdoms, many of them old enemies, into one empire
