# Who made it, and what does it show?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A clay warrior stands in a tomb in China. It is dressed as a soldier. So was it made by a soldier? Let's look at what the figure tells us, and what it doesn't.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn the first two questions to ask of any source: origin (who made it, when and where) and content (what it shows or says). The first of two hinge lessons on this skill: students who read the maker off the content (a soldier made it because it shows a soldier) carry the mistake into every later question about context and purpose.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say who made a source, when and where. I can say what a source shows or says, without mixing that up with who made it. I can keep origin and content apart in my own words.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a different source

> **Source B: a bamboo slip.** A thin strip of bamboo with writing in ink. Many strips were tied together to make a page. These ones were buried with a Qin official in 217 BCE in a tomb at Shuihudi, in what is now Hubei province. They were written by officials of the Qin government. They list laws and rules, including rules for looking after grain stores and the punishments for people who break the rules.

:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is looking at Source A, the clay warrior. Click the line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib is looking at a clay warrior from the First Emperor's tomb (Source A).
 2  Nib sees that the figure is dressed as a soldier, in armour.
 3  Nib asks "Who made it?" and writes: "Soldiers, because it shows a soldier."
 4  Nib asks "What does it show?" and writes: "A soldier in armour."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib has answered 'who made it?' by looking at what the figure shows. The warrior is dressed as a soldier, but that is its content. The origin is the craftsmen who made it in workshops.

:::

### Why keep them apart

**Why is it useful to ask origin and content as two separate questions?**

☐ Because content is always wrong

☐ Because origin is more important

☐ Because what a source shows is not always who made it

☐ Because there are two kinds of source

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Because what a source shows is not always who made it

A source can show one thing and be made by someone else. Asking both questions keeps them apart.
