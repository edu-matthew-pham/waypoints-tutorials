# What can this source tell us?

Source origin and purpose · Putting it together

<!-- Speaker note: Students use all four questions (origin, content, context, purpose) together to decide what a source can and cannot tell us about a particular question. A source is rarely useful or useless on its own: it depends on the question. This gives students a way to say what a source is good for, and prepares the next course, which looks at accuracy and usefulness. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 6 min · Make 6 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

## Today

- I can use origin, content, context and purpose on one source.
- I can say what a source can tell us about a question, and what it cannot.
- I can write a sentence that names the limit of a source.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This lesson puts the four questions together. It has a hinge-friendly check in the exit ticket, but the two earlier lessons are the hinges. If students are unsure of origin versus content or context versus purpose, go back to those lessons before this one. The next course in the pathway, Source usefulness, takes up accuracy (fact and opinion, reliability) and usefulness (value for a specific question). This lesson names the idea, 'what it can and cannot tell us', but does not ask students to judge accuracy. Keep them to describing. The worked example is ancient China in the Qin and Han period. It is a model: swap in your own depth study's sources, keeping the questions the same. Everything else is plain-words descriptions, and the made-up students, Ravi and Dev, are the only people invented. Teacher: Try: the order is the same for every source. Ask why the conclusion comes last. Teacher: Worked example: the same source answers two questions differently. Ask students what changed between them. Teacher: Vary: ask students to name the question first, then the source. -->

## Why it matters

> The First Emperor's inscription says everyone now lives in peace. Can we use it to find out whether people really did? Or can it tell us something else?

<!-- Speaker note: You now have four questions to ask of any source. **Origin**: who made it, when and where. **Content**: what it shows or says. **Context**: what was going on. **Purpose**: why it was made. On their own they just describe the source. Together, they help you decide **what it can tell you**, and what it can't. That depends on what you want to find out. -->

## Predict

**The First Emperor had an inscription cut into stone saying everyone lived in peace under his rule. Can it tell us whether everyone really did?**

- Yes: it is carved in stone so it must be true
- No: it can tell us nothing at all
- Yes: it was made at the time
- Not reliably: it was made to praise him, so it shows how he wanted to be seen

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Not reliably. It was made to win support, so it is better evidence of how he wanted to be seen than of what everyone thought. That is still useful. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Pick the conclusion

**You want to know: What did the First Emperor want people to think of him? Which conclusion about Source C is best?**

- It shows he wanted to be seen as a ruler who brought peace and order, because it was made to win support
- It proves everyone loved him
- It is a secondary source, so it is not useful
- It tells us nothing, because it praises him

* Answer: It shows he wanted to be seen as a ruler who brought peace and order, because it was made to win support

<!-- Speaker note: A source made to win support is good evidence of how its maker wanted to be seen. -->

## A different question

**Now you want to know: Did ordinary people in the conquered kingdoms like the new empire? What can Source C tell you about that?**

- Not much: it was made to win their support, so it won't show people who disagreed
- Nothing, because it is old
- Everything, because it is carved in stone
- A lot: it says everyone lives in peace

* Answer: Not much: it was made to win their support, so it won't show people who disagreed

## Say it in a sentence

**Which of these is the best way to finish a sentence about what a source can tell us?**

- 'Source C is a stone.'
- 'Source C is true.'
- 'Source C shows how the emperor wanted to be seen, but it cannot show what people really thought.'
- 'Source C is old.'

* Answer: 'Source C shows how the emperor wanted to be seen, but it cannot show what people really thought.'

<!-- Speaker note: A good conclusion names what the source can tell us and what it cannot. -->

## Which is the limit?

**A stone inscription was made to win support for the emperor. What is its limit as evidence?**

- It is unlikely to show people who disagreed with him
- It is too old to use
- It is not primary
- It cannot be read

* Answer: It is unlikely to show people who disagreed with him

## The warriors

**A clay warrior was made for the First Emperor's tomb. Which is the best conclusion about what it can tell us?**

- It shows what Qin soldiers' armour and weapons looked like, and how much the emperor spent on his tomb
- It tells us what every Qin person believed
- It tells us nothing, because it is made of clay
- It is true because it is a statue

* Answer: It shows what Qin soldiers' armour and weapons looked like, and how much the emperor spent on his tomb

<!-- Speaker note: Its content shows armour and weapons. Its purpose and context show how much the emperor invested in his tomb. -->
