# What can this source tell us?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The First Emperor's inscription says everyone now lives in peace. Can we use it to find out whether people really did? Or can it tell us something else?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use all four questions (origin, content, context, purpose) together to decide what a source can and cannot tell us about a particular question. A source is rarely useful or useless on its own: it depends on the question. This gives students a way to say what a source is good for, and prepares the next course, which looks at accuracy and usefulness.

**Students should be able to say:** I can use origin, content, context and purpose on one source. I can say what a source can tell us about a question, and what it cannot. I can write a sentence that names the limit of a source.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**The First Emperor had an inscription cut into stone saying everyone lived in peace under his rule. Can it tell us whether everyone really did?**

☐ Not reliably: it praises him, so it mostly shows how he wanted to be seen

☐ Yes: it was made at the time, so it shows what really happened

☐ Yes: it was carved in stone for everyone to read, so it must be true

☐ No: a source that praises a ruler can tell us nothing at all

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Not reliably. Historians think it was made to win support, so it is better evidence of how he wanted to be seen than of what everyone thought. That is still useful.

## Worked example: Source C


:::keep-together

### Pick the conclusion

**You want to know: What did the First Emperor want people to think of him? Which conclusion about Source C is best?**

☐ It shows that people across the empire really were living in peace and order

☐ It shows he wanted to be seen as a ruler who brought peace and order, since it was probably made to win support

☐ It shows how he wanted to be seen, but only because it is a secondary source

☐ It tells us nothing about him, because a source that praises him is biased

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It shows he wanted to be seen as a ruler who brought peace and order, since it was probably made to win support

A source made to win support is good evidence of how its maker wanted to be seen.

:::


:::keep-together

### A different question

**Now you want to know: Did ordinary people in the conquered kingdoms like the new empire? What can Source C tell you about that?**

☐ Not much: it was probably made to win their support, so it won't show people who disagreed

☐ A lot, because the emperor would have known what his people felt

☐ Not much, because it is over two thousand years old

☐ A lot: it says everyone lives in peace, and it was made at the time

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Not much: it was probably made to win their support, so it won't show people who disagreed

The same source is good for one question and weak for another. Its purpose helps you see which.

:::


:::keep-together

### Say it in a sentence

**Which of these is the best way to finish a sentence about what a source can tell us?**

☐ 'Source C is false, because it praises the emperor and leaves things out.'

☐ 'Source C shows how the emperor wanted to be seen, but it cannot show what people really thought.'

☐ 'Source C is primary, because it was made on the emperor's orders.'

☐ 'Source C is true, because it was made at the time the emperor ruled.'

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 'Source C shows how the emperor wanted to be seen, but it cannot show what people really thought.'

A good conclusion names what the source can tell us and what it cannot.

:::

## Say what a source can tell us


:::keep-together

### The warriors

**A clay warrior was made for the First Emperor's tomb. Which is the best conclusion about what it can tell us?**

☐ It shows how Qin soldiers fought in battle and how they won their wars

☐ It shows what Qin people believed about life after death, all across the empire

☐ It shows exactly what each soldier looked like, because each face is different

☐ It shows what Qin armour and weapons looked like, and how much effort went into the emperor's tomb

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It shows what Qin armour and weapons looked like, and how much effort went into the emperor's tomb

Its content shows armour and weapons. Its purpose and context show how much effort went into the emperor's tomb.

:::
