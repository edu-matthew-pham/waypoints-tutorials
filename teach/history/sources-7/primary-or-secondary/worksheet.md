# Primary or secondary? It is about when, not how old

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Sima Qian's history of China is over two thousand years old. Is it a primary source about the Qin? It feels like it should be. Let's check.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a source is primary if it was made at the time of the event being investigated, and secondary if it was made later by someone looking back. The test is the time of the event, not age. It is an optional catch-up for classes that need the idea: later lessons use the words primary and secondary, and students who sort by age will misread what a source can tell them.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether a source is primary or secondary for a given event, and give a reason. I can explain why a very old source can still be secondary. I can name a primary and a secondary source for an event from my own life.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**You are investigating how the First Emperor of Qin ruled, around 220 BCE. A history book written about 100 years later describes his rule. Is it primary or secondary?**

☐ Secondary: it is only one writer's view

☐ Primary: it is a history of his rule

☐ Secondary: it was written 100 years later

☐ Primary: it is over 2000 years old

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Secondary. It was written about a hundred years after the events, by someone looking back. Being very old does not make a source primary, being about his rule does not either, and one writer's view is not the test.

:::

## Worked example: how old it is does not decide


:::keep-together

### Same test, new source

**A student keeps a diary on the day of the school fair last term. She is investigating that fair. Is the diary primary or secondary?**

☐ Secondary: it is only a few months old

☐ Secondary: a diary is one person's view, not the facts

☐ Primary, but only once it is many years old

☐ Primary: it was written on the day of the event

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Primary: it was written on the day of the event

Primary. It was made on the day of the event by someone there. A recent source can be primary when the event is recent.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is sorting a source for the First Emperor's rule. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib is deciding what kind of source Sima Qian's history of the Qin is.
 2  Nib knows it was written by a Han court historian about 100 years after the Qin.
 3  Nib knows the First Emperor ruled over 2000 years ago.
 4  Nib says: "It is over 2000 years old, so it must be a primary source about the Qin."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: Nib thinks a source is primary because it is old. Sima Qian wrote about a hundred years after the Qin. Old is not the test. It was made long after the events, so it is secondary.

:::


:::keep-together

### The one question to ask

**Which question best tells you whether a source is primary or secondary?**

☐ Can I trust what it says?

☐ Is it more than a few hundred years old?

☐ Was it written by someone famous?

☐ Was it made at the time of the event I am investigating?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Was it made at the time of the event I am investigating?

Primary or secondary depends on when it was made compared with the event. It does not depend on age, where it is kept or whether it is right.

:::

### Ravi's book

**Ravi says: 'This book is 400 years old, so it must be a primary source about the Qin.' What is the best reply?**

☐ It is primary only if it quotes Qin sources

☐ It is old, but it was written long after the Qin, so it is secondary

☐ No: books are always secondary, whenever they were written

☐ You are right: anything that old counts as primary

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is old, but it was written long after the Qin, so it is secondary

Old is not the test. The Qin ruled over 2000 years ago, so a 400-year-old book is still a later retelling.
