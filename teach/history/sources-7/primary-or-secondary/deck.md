# Primary or secondary? It is about when, not how old

Source origin and purpose · Which kind of source?

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a source is primary if it was made at the time of the event being investigated, and secondary if it was made later by someone looking back. The test is the time of the event, not age. This is a hinge: every later source question assumes students sort sources correctly, and students who sort by age will misread what a source can tell them. About 35 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 7 min · Practise 5 min · Make 5 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). -->

## Today

- I can say whether a source is primary or secondary for a given event, and give a reason.
- I can explain why a very old source can still be secondary.
- I can name a primary and a secondary source for an event from my own life.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a hinge lesson and an optional warm-up. ## Why it matters

> Sima Qian's history of China is over two thousand years old. Is it a primary source about the Qin? It feels like it should be. Let's check.

<!-- Speaker note: Historians sort sources into two kinds, and the sorting depends on the question they are asking. A **primary source** comes from the time of the event you are investigating. It was made then, by people who were there or had to deal with it. A **secondary source** was made later, by someone looking back at the event. The test is not how old the source is. The test is: **was it made at the time of the event?** -->

## Think, pair, share

**Two students are investigating the same school fair. One uses a photo taken on the day. The other uses an article written a year later. Whose source is better?**

- Better for what question?
- What would the article writer know that the photo cannot show?

<!-- Speaker note: Steer away from 'primary is better'. The point is that they are different kinds, made at different times, for different questions. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Predict

**You are investigating how the First Emperor of Qin ruled, around 220 BCE. A history book written about 100 years later describes his rule. Is it primary or secondary?**

- Primary: it is over 2000 years old
- Secondary: it was written long after, by someone looking back
- It cannot be either
- Primary: it is a book of facts

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Secondary. It was written about a hundred years after the events. Being very old does not make a source primary. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## The one question to ask

**Which question best tells you whether a source is primary or secondary?**

- Is it true?
- Was it made at the time of the event I am investigating?
- Is it in a museum?
- Is it old?

* Answer: Was it made at the time of the event I am investigating?

<!-- Speaker note: Primary or secondary depends on when it was made compared with the event. It does not depend on age, where it is kept or whether it is right. -->

## Which is secondary?

**Which of these is a secondary source about the First Emperor's rule?**

- A history written 100 years later
- A law on a bamboo slip from his time
- A clay warrior from his tomb
- A stone inscription he ordered

* Answer: A history written 100 years later

<!-- Speaker note: A history written a century later was made by someone looking back, so it is secondary. Ask on mini whiteboards or by show of hands before the reveal. -->

Qin laws on bamboo slips found in an official's tomb · A textbook chapter on the Qin · A Qin official's note about a case he judged · A film about the First Emperor

## Exit ticket

**What is the test for whether a source is primary or secondary, and why is it not how old it is?**

* Whether it was made at the time of the event being investigated. An old source written long after the event is still secondary.
