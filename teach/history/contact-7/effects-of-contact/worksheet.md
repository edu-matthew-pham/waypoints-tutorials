# What did contact and conflict lead to?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** For about sixty years, a Han emperor sent silk, food and a bride to the ruler of his enemies every so often. What did each side get out of that?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe the heqin peace agreements, the Han conquest of the Hexi Corridor and the trade that followed, and explain what each changed for the Han, the Xiongnu and others, not only the winner.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe effects of contact and conflict of more than one kind. I can explain what an effect changed, not just that something changed. I can show that contact changed more than one society, not only the winner.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**After the Han conquered the Hexi Corridor and drove the Xiongnu out, which society do you think changed?**

☐ Both: the Han gained land, the Xiongnu lost grazing land

☐ The Xiongnu, because the losing side is the one that changes

☐ The Han, because they won and gained new land

☐ Neither: a conquest moves a border, but nothing else changes

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Both. The Han gained land, towns and a safer route west; the Xiongnu lost grazing land and a route they had controlled.

:::

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing about the effects of the Han conquest of the Hexi Corridor (Nib's notes are made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  In 121 BCE a Han army drove the Xiongnu out of the Hexi Corridor.
 2  The Han set up commanderies there and built towns for soldiers and settlers.
 3  Nib says: "The Han won, so the Han are the only ones whose lives changed."
 4  Nib says: "For the Xiongnu, it was just one lost battle."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib says only the winner changed. The Xiongnu lost grazing land and a route, and the Han changed too: new towns, new crops and trade, and wars so costly that Emperor Wu brought in new taxes. Contact changes every society involved, not only the winner.

:::


:::keep-together

### For the Han

**Emperor Wu's wars against the Xiongnu were very expensive. What did that change inside Han China?**

☐ The government ran short of money and brought in new taxes

☐ The emperor stopped collecting taxes so farmers would join the army

☐ Nothing, because the Han won, and winners do not pay for wars

☐ The Xiongnu paid for the wars, so Han people paid less tax

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The government ran short of money and brought in new taxes

Even the winner changed: costly wars led to new taxes and state control of salt and iron (the monopolies you may have met in People and power).

## Effects and change


:::keep-together

### The best description

**Which statement best describes the effects of Han contact with the lands to the west?**

☐ There were no real effects, because the lands were so far away

☐ Conquest, trade and peace agreements each changed the Han and their neighbours

☐ The Han won, so the effects were all good for the Han and bad for everyone else

☐ Trade mattered most; the wars left things much as they were

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Conquest, trade and peace agreements each changed the Han and their neighbours

Several kinds of effect, and every society involved changed.

:::

### Which side changed?

**Did each change happen to the Han, or to the Xiongnu?**

- They lost grazing land in the Hexi Corridor: {{field-15}}
