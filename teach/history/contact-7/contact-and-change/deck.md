- I can connect a cause, a contact and its effects into one chain of change.
- I can explain how two societies affected each other without meeting.
- I can use real examples to show contact changing a society.

## Why it matters

> Rich Romans wore Chinese silk, but no Roman we know of ever met a Han merchant. How did the silk get there?

<!-- Speaker note: In the last two lessons you looked at causes, then at effects. Historians join them up into a **chain**: a cause leads to a contact, the contact has effects, and the effects change the societies involved, sometimes for centuries. That is what it means to call contact a **driver of change**. Two chains from the Han period: > **Silk to Rome.** Silk came from China, and people in the Roman Empire wanted it. It was light, soft and very expensive, a sign of wealth. But Rome and Han China never traded face to face. Silk was passed from trader to trader across Central Asia, Persia and India, and each one added to the price. Roman writers had only a hazy idea of where it came from: they called the silk-makers the *Seres*, "the silk people". One Roman writer, Pliny the Elder, complained that Rome was sending huge sums east to pay for luxuries such as silk. Historians call this **indirect contact**: two societies affected each other without meeting. > **Buddhism to China.** Buddhism began in India, centuries before the Han. Merchants and monks travelled the same routes as silk and horses, and they brought their beliefs with them. By 65 CE a Chinese record mentions a Han prince who followed Buddhism. From about 148 CE a Buddhist teacher from Parthia (in what is now Iran), called An Shigao, was working at the Han capital, Luoyang, putting Buddhist texts into Chinese. Over the next centuries Buddhism became one of the main religions of China. Look at how each chain runs: a want (silk, or spreading a belief) leads to contact along the trade routes, and the contact changes what people wear, spend and believe. -->

## Indirect contact

**Why do historians call the link between Rome and Han China 'indirect contact'?**

- Because Roman and Han armies fought each other far from home
- Because they sent letters to each other instead of meeting
- Because they had no effect on each other at all

* Answer: They affected each other through traders in between, without meeting face to face
