# How did contact change the ancient world?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Rich Romans wore Chinese silk, but no Roman we know of ever met a Han merchant. How did the silk get there?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain contact as a driver of change through two connected chains, Roman demand for silk met through traders in between, and Buddhism reaching Han China along the trade routes, and recognise that ancient societies did not develop alone.

**Students should be able to say:** I can connect a cause, a contact and its effects into one chain of change. I can explain how two societies affected each other without meeting. I can use real examples to show contact changing a society.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is comparing Rome and Han China (Nib's notes are made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Rome was in the west and Han China was in the east.
 2  No Roman we know of ever met a Han merchant face to face.
 3  Nib says: "So Rome and Han China each developed on their own, without the other."
 4  Nib says: "Contact only changes a society when people meet."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib says the two societies developed on their own because they never met. They never met face to face, but Roman demand for silk changed what Romans wore and spent, and the same routes carried Buddhism into China. Contact can drive change even at a distance.

### Indirect contact

**Why do historians call the link between Rome and Han China 'indirect contact'?**

☐ Because Roman and Han armies fought each other far from home

☐ Because they sent letters to each other instead of meeting

☐ Because they had no effect on each other at all

☐ They affected each other through traders in between, without meeting face to face

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They affected each other through traders in between, without meeting face to face

Indirect means through others: goods passed from hand to hand across Asia.

## Contact as a driver


:::keep-together

### The best explanation

**Which statement best explains contact as a driver of change in the ancient world?**

☐ Contact through trade, war and travel brought new goods, beliefs and rulers that changed societies

☐ Contact changed rulers, but ordinary people's lives stayed the same

☐ Contact mattered most when one society conquered another

☐ Each ancient society changed only because of things that happened inside it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Contact through trade, war and travel brought new goods, beliefs and rulers that changed societies

Trade, war and travel all carried change between societies.

:::


:::keep-together
