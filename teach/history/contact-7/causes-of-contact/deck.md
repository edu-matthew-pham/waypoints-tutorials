## Today

**We are learning to** describe several causes of contact and conflict between Han China and other societies, sort them into kinds, and explain how one cause shaped what happened.

- I can describe at least two causes of a contact or conflict.
- I can sort causes into kinds, such as power, land, security and trade.
- I can explain how one cause shaped what happened next.

## Why it matters

> In 138 BCE an emperor sent about a hundred men walking west into lands no one at his court had mapped. Why would he do that?

<!-- Speaker note: A **contact** is any time two societies meet: through trade, travel, talks between rulers, or war. A **conflict** is when the meeting turns into a fight. Historians ask what caused each one, and the answer is almost never a single thing. Causes come in different kinds: | Kind of cause | What it means | |---|---| | **Power** | A ruler wants to be stronger, more respected, or in charge of more people | | **Land** | Wanting more farmland or territory, and the people on it to tax | | **Security** | Protecting your people from attack | | **Trade and resources** | Wanting goods, animals or materials you cannot get at home | If you have done *Time, cause and change*, you may have met several causes behind Qin's unification of China. This lesson asks the same kind of question about contact between societies: why did Han China meet, trade with and fight the peoples around it? Here are three cases from Han China. > **The Han and the Xiongnu.** North of China lived the **Xiongnu**, herders who moved with their animals across the grasslands and fought on horseback. Han writers say Xiongnu horsemen raided Han farms and wanted Han goods such as silk. (The Xiongnu left no writing of their own, so we have only the Han side.) The Han wanted protection from the raids, and they wanted horses, because their own were smaller. For about sixty years Han emperors tried to keep the peace with gifts. From 133 BCE, Emperor Wu stopped and sent armies against the Xiongnu. > **Zhang Qian's mission west (138 BCE).** According to Sima Qian, Emperor Wu sent Zhang Qian west to find the Yuezhi, a people the Xiongnu had driven out, and ask them to join the Han against the Xiongnu. The Xiongnu caught him and held him for about ten years. He escaped and reached the Yuezhi, but they did not want a war. He came home about thirteen years after he left, with news of rich lands to the west, including Ferghana (in Central Asia), where people bred big, strong horses. > **The war for Ferghana's horses (104 to 101 BCE).** Zhang Qian's news led to a new contact. Emperor Wu wanted Ferghana's horses for his army, and Han writers say he also prized them as a sign of his power. According to Sima Qian, Ferghana's ruler refused to hand them over, so Emperor Wu sent armies west. The first failed in 104 BCE; the second came back in 101 BCE with the horses. -->

## How a cause shapes events

**The Han wanted protection from Xiongnu horsemen. How did that cause shape what Emperor Wu did next?**

- It changed nothing, because the Han already had enough horses
- He gave up on the north and moved the capital far to the south
- He looked for allies against the Xiongnu, which is why he sent Zhang Qian west
- He asked the Xiongnu to protect the Han from other raiders

* Answer: He looked for allies against the Xiongnu, which is why he sent Zhang Qian west

<!-- Speaker note: A security cause led to a search for allies. The news about Ferghana's horses came only when Zhang Qian returned, and it led to a new contact. Causes do not just sit in a list: they shape the contact that follows. -->

## Vary: take one cause away



<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Removing one cause and seeing what is left shows that causes are separate and can each keep a contact going. For students who need more support: Give a two-column frame (cause; kind of cause) and fill the Han–Xiongnu row together before students choose their own case. For early finishers: Emperor Wu wanted Ferghana's horses partly as a sign of Heaven's favour. Is that a power cause, a resources cause, or something else? What would you need to know? The hard part, on purpose: Students give one cause ('they wanted land') and stop. Keep asking: what else made them want this, and what kind of reason is that? When students get stuck: A student gives only one cause. Ask: Why did the Han want horses? Is that the same reason as wanting land? Fix: Different kinds: security, resources and power sit beside land. / A student lists causes but cannot say what kind each is. Ask: Is this about being stronger, getting land, staying safe, or getting goods? Fix: Use the four-kind table as a sorting frame. / A student mixes up causes and results. Ask: Did this happen before the contact, or because of it? Fix: Causes come first; Zhang Qian's news and the horses were results. -->

## The strongest explanation

**Which explanation of the war for Ferghana's horses is strongest?**
