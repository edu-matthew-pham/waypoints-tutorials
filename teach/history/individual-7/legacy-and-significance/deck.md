## Why it matters

> His dynasty lasted about fifteen years. The way of running China that he set up lasted about two thousand. How can both be true?

<!-- Speaker note: A person's **legacy** is what they leave behind: what carried on, and what changed, because of them, during their life and after it. **What did not last.** The First Emperor died in 210 BCE, on a tour of his empire. Within a year, men called up for frontier service rebelled (you may have met their revolt, according to Sima Qian, in *People and power*), and fighting spread. The Qin dynasty had fallen by 206 BCE, about fifteen years after it united the kingdoms. Historians think the heavy demands for labour, tax and harsh punishment helped turn many people against the Qin. **What did last.** The Han dynasty, which replaced the Qin and ruled for about four hundred years, kept much of what he had set up: - appointed officials running commanderies and counties (the Han also gave kingdoms to relatives for a time); - one shared written script, which shaped Chinese writing from then on; - standard weights, measures and coins; - the idea of one emperor ruling one empire. Later dynasties kept the same basic way of running China for about two thousand years. **How people saw him.** Opinions about him have never agreed: - **At the time:** his own stone inscriptions praise him as a ruler without equal, who brought peace and order and one set of laws, weights and measures. They were made for him, so historians read them as his message, not as a fair judgement. - **Han writers:** Sima Qian and others writing for the Han described him as harsh and cruel. The writer Jia Yi argued that the Qin fell because it ruled by force and did not care for its people. These writers worked for the dynasty that replaced his, so they had reasons to make the Qin look bad. - **Since then:** some writers have praised him for uniting China; others have condemned his cruelty. Historians today look at both: what he built, and what it cost. That disagreement is part of why he is **significant**. Significant means *important to history*: changed many lives, effects lasted, still argued about. It does not mean *good*. A person can be very significant and still have done terrible things. -->

## Why so harsh?

**Why might Han writers have described the First Emperor so harshly?**

- Because they had all met him and disliked him in person at court
- They worked for the Han, so a cruel Qin made the Han look better
- There was no reason at all; they were simply recording the facts

* Answer: They worked for the Han, so a cruel Qin made the Han look better
