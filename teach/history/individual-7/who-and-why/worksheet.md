# Who was he, and why does he matter?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Millions of people have seen photos of the clay army made for his tomb. Is that why he matters to historians, or is there a better reason?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Identify the First Emperor (when he lived, his position, the Warring States context) and use three tests of significance (changed many lives, effects lasted, remembered and argued about) to explain why he matters.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say who the First Emperor was, when he lived and what position he held. I can describe the world he grew up in. I can use a test of significance, with evidence, instead of saying someone is famous.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining why the First Emperor is significant (Nib's notes are made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  The First Emperor was King of Qin and then emperor, until he died in 210 BCE.
 2  His clay army is visited by millions of people every year.
 3  Nib says: "He is so famous today, so that makes him significant."
 4  Nib says: "So I don't need to say what changed because of him."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib says he is significant because he is famous. Fame tells you he is well known today, not what changed because of him. A historian asks whose lives he changed, and whether those changes lasted.

### Why the context?

**Why does the lesson describe the Warring States before the First Emperor's achievements?**

☐ Because he was born long before the Warring States period began

☐ To show that Qin had always been the strongest kingdom

☐ Because he started the wars of the Warring States himself

☐ His achievements make sense against a world of rival kingdoms

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** His achievements make sense against a world of rival kingdoms

Context is the world a person lived in: here, rival kingdoms that had fought for centuries. It shows what was possible and what they changed.

## Who, when and why


:::keep-together

### Who was he?

**Which is the best short description of who the First Emperor was?**

☐ The man who built the Great Wall that tourists visit today, all on his own, in about 1500 CE

☐ Ying Zheng, King of Qin from 246 BCE, who united the rival kingdoms and ruled as emperor from 221 to 210 BCE

☐ An army general who defeated Qin and took its land in about 300 BCE

☐ A Han emperor who wrote the first history of China in about 100 BCE

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Ying Zheng, King of Qin from 246 BCE, who united the rival kingdoms and ruled as emperor from 221 to 210 BCE

Name, position, dates and what he did: that identifies him.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which test?

**Which test of significance does each piece of evidence fit?**

Groups: Changed many lives, Effects lasted, Remembered and argued about

- Farmers across the empire paid tax to one government: {{field-15}}
- The Han dynasty kept his commanderies and script: {{field-15}}
- Han writers called him a cruel ruler: {{field-15}}
- People across China kept writing one shared script for centuries: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Farmers across the empire paid tax to one government → Changed many lives; The Han dynasty kept his commanderies and script → Effects lasted; Han writers called him a cruel ruler → Remembered and argued about; People across China kept writing one shared script for centuries → Effects lasted
