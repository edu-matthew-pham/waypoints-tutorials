## Why it matters

> Millions of people have seen photos of the clay army made for his tomb. Is that why he matters to historians, or is there a better reason?

<!-- Speaker note: You have met the **First Emperor** many times in these courses: his clay army, his stone inscriptions, his officials and commanderies. This lesson asks who he was, and why historians think he matters. | Question | Answer | |---|---| | Who? | Ying Zheng, later called **Qin Shi Huang**, which means "First Emperor of Qin" | | When? | Born about 259 BCE, died 210 BCE | | Where? | The state of Qin, in the Wei River valley in the north-west of what is now China | | What position? | King of Qin from 246 BCE, when he was about 13; Emperor from 221 BCE until he died | **The world he grew up in.** For about 250 years before him, the land we now call China was split into rival kingdoms that fought each other. Historians call this the **Warring States** period. Qin was one of these kingdoms, and over many years it had made its army and government strong. Between 230 and 221 BCE, Qin's armies defeated the last six rival kingdoms. Ying Zheng then took a new title, never used before: *huangdi*, emperor. **What makes a person significant?** Historians use the word **significant** for a person, event or change that matters to history. Many people are famous, but that is not the test. Historians ask questions like these: 1. **Many lives:** did they change the lives of many people, at the time? 2. **Lasting effects:** did those changes last after they died? 3. **Remembered and argued about:** do people still remember them, and disagree about them? These three tests are one way historians judge significance (there are others, and historians argue about which matter most). A person can be famous for something that changed very little, and a person most of us have never heard of can be very significant. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Think of something very famous today: a song, a game, a building. In 500 years, will historians think it was significant? How would they decide?**

- What would have to change because of it?
- Who would remember it, and why?
