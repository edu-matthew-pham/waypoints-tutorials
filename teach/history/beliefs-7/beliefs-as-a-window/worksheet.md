# What do beliefs show about a society?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You have learned what many people in Han China believed and what they did. Now turn it round: if beliefs were all you knew, what could you work out about the society?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain what the beliefs and practices of Han China reveal about its values (family duty, a ruler who rules well, provision for the dead) and organisation, and identify a distinctive feature by comparing with Egypt.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what a belief or practice reveals about what a society valued. I can explain how a belief shaped the way a society was organised. I can name one thing that set a society's beliefs or practices apart.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**A historian knows only that families in a society made offerings to their ancestors for years. What could she work out?**

☐ Exactly how rich every single family in that society was

☐ That family, and duty between generations, mattered a lot there

☐ That the people there must have had no rulers or laws at all

☐ Nothing useful, because those beliefs were just superstition

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** That family mattered, and that the living were thought to owe the dead. Beliefs are evidence of what a society valued.

:::

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing about beliefs in Han China. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Many people in Han China made offerings to their ancestors.
 2  Advisers read floods as warnings from Heaven to the emperor.
 3  Nib says: "They were just superstitious, so their beliefs tell us nothing."
 4  Nib says: "Historians should stick to wars and emperors instead."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib calls the beliefs superstition and throws them away as evidence. Whatever we think of a belief, it shaped what people did. Offerings show how much family mattered; the Mandate of Heaven shows that rulers needed Heaven's approval. Beliefs are some of the best evidence of what a society valued.

:::


:::keep-together

### The Mandate and power

**What does the Mandate of Heaven reveal about how Han China was organised?**

☐ Farmers chose the emperor in a vote held every few years

☐ Priests ran the government and the emperor obeyed them

☐ Rulers held power, but were believed to need Heaven's approval to keep it

☐ The emperor could do anything and no idea could ever limit him

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Rulers held power, but were believed to need Heaven's approval to keep it

The belief shows power at the top that was thought to depend on ruling well.

## A window on the society


:::keep-together

### Explain, not describe

**Which sentence explains, rather than only describes?**

☐ Families in Han China made offerings because it was their custom

☐ Families in Han China were just superstitious about their ancestors

☐ Families kept offerings going, which shows family duty did not end at death

☐ Families in Han China made offerings to their ancestors at home

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Families kept offerings going, which shows family duty did not end at death

Explaining means saying what a practice reveals about values or organisation.

:::
