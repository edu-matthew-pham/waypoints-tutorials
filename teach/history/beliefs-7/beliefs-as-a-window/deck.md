## Why it matters

> You have learned what many people in Han China believed and what they did. Now turn it round: if beliefs were all you knew, what could you work out about the society?

<!-- Speaker note: In the last two lessons you went from beliefs to practices, and from tombs to beliefs. This lesson asks a bigger question: what do a society's beliefs and practices **reveal** about it? Historians use them like a window. They show what people **valued** (what they thought mattered most) and how the society was **organised** (who owed what to whom). Here is what Han China's beliefs and practices reveal: | Belief or practice | What it reveals | |---|---| | Duty to parents, offerings to ancestors | Family mattered greatly, and families had a clear order: children owed parents, and the living owed the dead | | The Mandate of Heaven | A ruler's right to rule was believed to depend on ruling well, so rulers needed to look as though Heaven approved of them | | Official posts for Confucian scholars from 136 BCE, and an academy to train officials from 124 BCE | More and more officials were educated in Confucian writings, so those values shaped how the country was run. Later Han writers described this as Confucian teaching winning out; many historians think the change was slower. | | Tombs stocked like homes, with models of granaries and farms | Providing for the dead mattered, and so did food, stored grain and a household that ran well | Comparing helps you see what was **distinctive**, meaning what set a society apart. In ancient Egypt, many people believed that a person's spirit could not survive without the body, so wealthy families had bodies preserved by **mummification**. In Han China, wealthy families also cared for the body (Lady Dai was buried in four nested coffins), but put simply, the evidence puts more weight on stocking the tomb like a home and on what the living kept doing for the dead. Both societies believed in an afterlife, and both put great effort into burials. The difference shows what each one stressed. -->

## The Mandate and power

**What does the Mandate of Heaven reveal about how Han China was organised?**

- Farmers chose the emperor in a vote held every few years
- Priests ran the government and the emperor obeyed them
- Rulers held power, but were believed to need Heaven's approval to keep it
- The emperor could do anything and no idea could ever limit him

* Answer: Rulers held power, but were believed to need Heaven's approval to keep it

<!-- Speaker note: The belief shows power at the top that was thought to depend on ruling well. -->
