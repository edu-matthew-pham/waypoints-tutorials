# What do the tombs tell us?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** One Han tomb held so much that, fifty years after it was opened, the museum was still counting. What would you want to know before using it as evidence?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe death and funerary customs in Qin and Han China from tomb evidence (Mawangdui, tomb models, the terracotta army), connect each to a belief or value, and say whose burials the evidence shows.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe three funerary customs of an ancient society, with evidence. I can explain the belief or value behind a custom. I can say whose burials a piece of evidence shows.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Answer:** Silk clothes folded in a tomb → Providing for the dead; Clay soldiers in pits beside a tomb → Protecting the dead; A bamboo slip naming a dish and how many → Recording what was given; A pottery model of a pig pen → Providing for the dead

:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing about burials in Han China. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Lady Dai's tomb at Mawangdui held thousands of objects.
 2  They included food, silk clothes, lacquer dishes and wooden servants.
 3  Nib says: "So everyone in Han China was buried with thousands of things."
 4  Nib says: "Farmers must have had rooms full of grave goods too."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib takes one rich family's tomb as how everyone was buried. Mawangdui belonged to a marquis's family. Most people were farmers, and their graves were much simpler. The tomb is strong evidence of what wealthy people did and believed, not of every burial.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why 'probably'?

**Historians think the terracotta army was probably meant to guard the First Emperor. Why do they say 'probably'?**

☐ The soldiers were made long after the emperor had died

☐ Historians are not sure the clay soldiers really exist at all

☐ Saying 'probably' is just a polite way of saying it is true

☐ No written source from the time says why the soldiers were made

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No written source from the time says why the soldiers were made

When no source states a purpose, historians reason from the evidence and say how sure they are.

### Choosing evidence

**Which piece of evidence would help most to find out how ordinary farmers in Han China were buried?**

☐ Simple graves dug up in a village cemetery

☐ A model house from a noble's tomb

☐ The First Emperor's terracotta army

☐ Lady Dai's tomb at Mawangdui

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Simple graves dug up in a village cemetery

To learn about a group, look for evidence from that group.
