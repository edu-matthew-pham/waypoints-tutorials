## Why it matters

> Why would a family put dishes of food, a set of clothes and a little clay model of a granary into a grave?

<!-- Speaker note: A **belief** is something people hold to be true about the world: what happens after death, who gives a ruler the right to rule, what you owe your family. A **practice** is something people do. Historians look for how the two connect, because a belief usually explains a practice. Many people in Han China held beliefs that fitted together: | What many people believed | What they did because of it | |---|---| | The spirits of ancestors lived on, and could help their descendants | Families made regular offerings of food and drink to their ancestors | | Children owe their parents care and respect, while they live and after they die (Confucian teaching on **filial piety**, which means duty to parents) | Careful funerals, and offerings that kept going for years | | Heaven gave a ruler the right to rule only while he ruled well: the **Mandate of Heaven** | Advisers read floods and droughts as Heaven's warnings, and told the emperor to change how he ruled | | Life after death was much like life before it | Tombs were filled with food, clothes, dishes and models of houses and granaries | When Confucius was asked about duty to parents, the *Analects* (a collection of his sayings) record him answering that parents should be served properly while they are alive, buried properly when they die, and given offerings properly after that. In 136 BCE Emperor Wu gave official posts to scholars of the Confucian classics, and in 124 BCE he founded an academy to train officials in them. Later Han writers described this as Confucian teaching winning out; many historians think the change was slower. Dong Zhongshu, a Confucian adviser to Emperor Wu, taught that floods and droughts were Heaven's way of warning the emperor. See how the beliefs link up. If you believe duty to your parents does not stop when they die, then funerals and offerings matter. If the dead live on much as before, they need what the living need, so the tomb is stocked. Together the beliefs work like a system: each one helps explain the others, and together they explain what people did. This is a simplified picture. Beliefs varied between places and families and changed over the 400 years of the Han, and people also followed other teachings, such as Daoism. -->
