# What did they believe, and what did they do about it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Why would a family put dishes of food, a set of clothes and a little clay model of a granary into a grave?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe key beliefs held by many people in Han China (duty to ancestors and parents, the Mandate of Heaven, an afterlife much like this life) and explain how each led to practices such as offerings, funerals and furnished tombs.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe two beliefs held by many people in an ancient society. I can explain how a belief led to a practice. I can explain why a practice that looks odd makes sense once you know the belief behind it.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Archaeologists open a Han tomb and find dishes of food, clothes and a model house. What best explains why they were put there?**

☐ It was just a custom people followed without any reason behind it

☐ Robbers broke in later and left the things they did not want

☐ The family was getting rid of things nobody in the house used any more

☐ Many people believed the dead lived on and would need what the living need

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** A belief about the afterlife. Many people in Han China believed life after death was much like life before it, so the dead would need food, clothes and a home.

:::

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing about Han tombs. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Han tombs often held model houses, granaries and farm animals.
 2  Many families also made offerings to their ancestors.
 3  Nib says: "They put model houses in tombs because they liked decorating."
 4  Nib says: "So the tombs don't tell us anything about what they believed."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib treats the model houses as decoration, a custom with no reason. Many people believed the dead lived on and needed a home, food and help, so the family gave them those things. The practice makes sense once you know the belief.

:::


:::keep-together

### Duty after death

**Why did a family that followed Confucian teaching keep making offerings after a parent died?**

☐ They wanted to show the neighbours how rich they were

☐ They thought their parent had not really died at all

☐ They believed their duty to a parent carried on after death

☐ It was a habit they kept up without really meaning it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They believed their duty to a parent carried on after death

Filial piety, duty to parents, did not stop at death, so offerings continued.

## Beliefs as a system


:::keep-together

### The best statement

**Which statement about beliefs in Han China is best?**

☐ Everyone in Han China believed exactly the same things in the same way

☐ Many people held beliefs, but each one stood on its own

☐ Beliefs linked up, and they varied by place and family

☐ People had no beliefs of their own, just rules from the emperor

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Beliefs linked up, and they varied by place and family

Linked beliefs, held by many but not identically by all.

:::


:::keep-together
