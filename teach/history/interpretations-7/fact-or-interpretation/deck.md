## Today

**We are learning to** tell a historical fact from a historical interpretation, and explain that an interpretation is an argument built from evidence.

- I can say whether a statement is a fact or an interpretation, and why.
- I can explain why an interpretation is more than an opinion.
- I can give an example of each about the same event.

## Why it matters

> The Qin unified China in 221 BCE. By 206 BCE the dynasty was gone. That much is fact. Why it fell is another kind of statement.

<!-- Speaker note: Historians make two kinds of statement: - A **fact** is something the evidence directly shows, and the sources agree on. *The First Emperor died in 210 BCE.* If you have done Source usefulness, you checked factual claims there; a fact is a claim that has passed those checks. - An **interpretation** is an **argument** about what the facts mean: why something happened, what caused it, or how much it mattered. *The Qin fell because its rule was too harsh.* An interpretation is not just an opinion. A historian has to back it with evidence and reasons. But two careful historians can look at the same facts and build **different** interpretations, which is why you need to know which kind of statement you are reading. Here are some facts about the end of the Qin: | Date | What happened | |---|---| | 221 BCE | The Qin unify China. King Zheng becomes the First Emperor. | | 210 BCE | The First Emperor dies while touring his empire. | | 209 BCE | A revolt begins and spreads across the old states. | | 207 BCE | The last Qin ruler surrenders. | | 206 BCE | The Qin dynasty is over. Years of war follow: Liu Bang wins in 202 BCE, though the Han count their rule from 206 BCE. | -->

## Predict

**'The Qin fell because it ruled too harshly.' Is that a fact or an interpretation?**

- A fact: Han writers who lived just after said so
- Neither: it is just an opinion, so it is made up
- A fact: it is written in lots of history books
- An interpretation: it argues why the Qin fell

## More than an opinion

**What makes an interpretation more than just an opinion?**

- It is written by someone famous or important
- It gives reasons and uses evidence that others can check
- It is the oldest explanation anyone wrote
- Most people agree with it

* Answer: It gives reasons and uses evidence that others can check

<!-- Speaker note: An interpretation is an argument from evidence. Others can check the evidence and the reasoning. -->

**Match each statement to the best description of it.**

## Make: Typical: Hatshepsut's broken statues

Hatshepsut ruled Egypt as pharaoh about 3,500 years ago. In the 1920s, archaeologists found many of her statues smashed. For a long time, historians argued that her successor, Thutmose III, had them broken out of hatred for her. In 2025, an archaeologist re-read the old excavation notes and argued that much of the damage was a ritual done to many pharaohs' statues, plus later reuse of the stone.

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: A real interpretation changing with re-examined evidence shows interpretations are arguments, in another society. -->
