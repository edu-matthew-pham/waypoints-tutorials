# Fact or interpretation?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The Qin unified China in 221 BCE. By 206 BCE the dynasty was gone. That much is fact. Why it fell is another kind of statement.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain what a historical interpretation is (an argument about the past built from evidence, not a simple statement of fact or an opinion) and distinguish facts from interpretations, with an example of each.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether a statement is a fact or an interpretation, and why. I can explain why an interpretation is more than an opinion. I can give an example of each about the same event.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: same facts, built into an argument

The **Han writers** who came after the Qin, such as **Jia Yi** (who lived about 200 to 169 BCE) and the court historian **Sima Qian**, built this interpretation, in our words:

> The Qin won its empire by force, then kept ruling by force and harsh laws, and did not care for its people. So when the revolt came, the people turned against it, and it fell fast.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The facts it uses** | The Qin conquered its rivals; it had strict laws; a revolt began in 209 BCE and spread fast; the dynasty ended by 206 BCE. |
| **The argument it makes** | Ruling by force with no care for the people *caused* the fall. |
| **Is it a fact?** | No. It is an interpretation: an argument from the facts. Historians still discuss it. |

### More than an opinion

**What makes an interpretation more than just an opinion?**

☐ It is written by someone famous or important

☐ It gives reasons and uses evidence that others can check

☐ It is the oldest explanation anyone wrote

☐ Most people agree with it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It gives reasons and uses evidence that others can check

An interpretation is an argument from evidence. Others can check the evidence and the reasoning.

## Facts and interpretations


:::keep-together

**Match each statement to the best description of it.**

A. An interpretation: an argument about why

B. A fact: an event the sources agree on

C. An interpretation: an argument about importance

D. A fact: a date the sources agree on

{{columns 60% 40%}}

{{rows 1}}

| Part | Letter |
|---|---|
| `The Qin unified China in 221 BCE` | |
| `The Qin fell because it ruled by force` | |
| `The Qin was the most important dynasty` | |
| `A revolt began in 209 BCE` | |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The Qin unified China in 221 BCE → D. A fact: a date the sources agree on; The Qin fell because it ruled by force → A. An interpretation: an argument about why; The Qin was the most important dynasty → C. An interpretation: an argument about importance; A revolt began in 209 BCE → B. A fact: an event the sources agree on

Facts say what happened and when; interpretations argue why or how much it mattered.

## Make: Typical: Hatshepsut's broken statues

Hatshepsut ruled Egypt as pharaoh about 3,500 years ago. In the 1920s, archaeologists found many of her statues smashed. For a long time, historians argued that her successor, Thutmose III, had them broken out of hatred for her. In 2025, an archaeologist re-read the old excavation notes and argued that much of the damage was a ritual done to many pharaohs' statues, plus later reuse of the stone.
