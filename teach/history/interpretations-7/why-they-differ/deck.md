## Today

**We are learning to** describe two interpretations of the same event and explain why they differ: different evidence, different questions, different values.

- I can describe two interpretations of the same event.
- I can give at least two reasons they differ (evidence, questions, values).
- I can explain why both can stand, without calling one simply right.

## Why it matters

> Writers of the Han dynasty said the Qin fell because it was cruel. Some historians today, reading Qin documents dug up from the ground, aren't so sure. Is one side wrong?

<!-- Speaker note: Historians have explained the fall of the Qin in different ways. Here are three, in our own words. None of them is *the* answer. **A. Han writers** (Jia Yi and Sima Qian, writing within about a century of the Qin's fall) The Qin ruled by force and harsh laws and did not care for its people, so when the revolt came, nobody defended it. **B. Some historians today** Qin law texts dug up at Shuihudi (found in 1975) and Liye (found in 2002) show a detailed set of laws that officials applied with some care. These historians think Han writers may have made the Qin sound crueller than it was. They look harder at other parts of the story: a sudden crisis at court after the First Emperor died, when his advisers plotted over who would rule, and the old states rising up again. **C. One archaeologist's question** In 2014 the archaeologist Gideon Shelach asked whether the Qin really "collapsed" at all, or **changed into** the Han, since the Han kept so much of what the Qin built. -->

## Make: Typical: the end of the Western Roman Empire

In 476 CE the last emperor in the west of the Roman Empire was removed. Historians have explained the end of the Western empire in different ways: some stress a **failing economy**, some stress **political upheaval** (emperors quickly replacing each other), and some stress **invasion** by outside groups. Many combine them.
