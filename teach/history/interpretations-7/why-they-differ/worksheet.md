# Why historians tell it differently

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Writers of the Han dynasty said the Qin fell because it was cruel. Some historians today, reading Qin documents dug up from the ground, aren't so sure. Is one side wrong?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe how the same event can be interpreted in different ways, and explain what causes the differences (different evidence, questions and values), without treating one interpretation as simply right.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe two interpretations of the same event. I can give at least two reasons they differ (evidence, questions, values). I can explain why both can stand, without calling one simply right.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Usually all three: they can see different evidence (law texts dug up since 1975), they ask different questions (what lesson for rulers? how did the state work? was it a collapse at all?), and they hold different values. Several interpretations can stand at once.

:::

## Worked example: where the difference comes from

| | A. Han writers | B. Some historians today |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence they could use** | Accounts and stories passed down at the Han court | The same accounts, **plus** Qin law texts dug up since 1975 |
| **Question they asked** | What lesson should rulers learn from the Qin? | How did the Qin state actually work, day to day? |
| **Values** | Confucian: a good ruler cares for the people. They also wrote for the Han, who had replaced the Qin, so historians think they had reasons to make the Qin look bad. | Historians today aim to test old accounts against every kind of evidence. |

So the difference is not that one side is lying. They had **different evidence**, asked **different questions**, and held **different values**.

☐ Nobody knows anything about the Qin, so all views are equal

Different interpretations can stand at once. Explaining why they differ is the historian's job.

## Make: Typical: the end of the Western Roman Empire

In 476 CE the last emperor in the west of the Roman Empire was removed. Historians have explained the end of the Western empire in different ways: some stress a **failing economy**, some stress **political upheaval** (emperors quickly replacing each other), and some stress **invasion** by outside groups. Many combine them.
