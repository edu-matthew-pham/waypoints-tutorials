## Today

**We are learning to** build an argument that explains why an event is interpreted in different ways, using the evidence, questions and values behind each interpretation.

- I can plan an argument with a claim, the interpretations, reasons and a conclusion.
- I can explain why interpretations differ, not just say that they do.
- I can write the argument in my own book for a partner or my teacher to read.

## Why it matters

> Two interpretations of the Qin's fall stand side by side. Your job isn't to pick one. It's to explain why they're different.

<!-- Speaker note: You have met two interpretations of why the Qin fell: - **Han writers** such as Jia Yi: the Qin ruled by force and did not care for its people. - **Some historians today**, who have also read Qin law texts dug up since 1975: the Qin may not have been as cruel as Han writers said, so they look harder at a crisis at court and the old states rising again. Now the question is: **why** are they different? A good answer is a short **argument** with four parts: 1. **Claim**: answer the question in one sentence. 2. **The interpretations**: say what each one argues, and what evidence it uses. 3. **Reasons**: explain why they differ: different **evidence**, different **questions**, different **values**. 4. **Conclusion**: sum up. -->

## Fix the argument

**Which sentence, put in place of Nib's line 3, would make the argument strongest?**

- Everyone is entitled to their own opinion about the past
- Historians today can read Qin law texts that Han writers never saw
- History is all just stories, so it doesn't really matter
- The Han writers were clearly right, so the others are wrong

* Answer: Historians today can read Qin law texts that Han writers never saw

## What each part does

**Match each part of the argument to its job.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Claim` | A. Say what each side argues and its evidence |
| `The interpretations` | B. Sums up why the interpretations differ |
| `Reasons` | C. Name the evidence, questions or values behind the difference |
| `Conclusion` | D. Answers the question in one sentence |

* Answer: Claim → D. Answers the question in one sentence; The interpretations → A. Say what each side argues and its evidence; Reasons → C. Name the evidence, questions or values behind the difference; Conclusion → B. Sums up why the interpretations differ

## Make: Typical: Hatshepsut's statues again

For a long time, historians argued that Thutmose III smashed Hatshepsut's statues out of hatred for her. In 2025, an archaeologist re-read the notes, drawings and letters from the 1920s excavations and argued that much of the damage was a ritual done to many pharaohs' statues, plus later reuse of the stone.
