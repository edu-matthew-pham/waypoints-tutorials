# Where did it grow, and why there?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Look at a map of China today and most people live in the east, on the plains and along the rivers. More than 2,000 years ago, it was much the same. Why there, and not out in the desert?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe the landforms, rivers, climate and resources of the region where ancient China grew, and explain why the river valleys and plains could support many people while deserts and mountains could not.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the main landforms, rivers and climate of the place an ancient society grew. I can say what each feature gave people, or how it got in their way. I can explain why people could settle in a place, using its geography.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Early farming villages in northern China grew up along the Yellow River and its branches, not out in the Gobi Desert. What is the most likely reason?**

☐ The emperor ordered people to live near the rivers

☐ Villages near rivers were safe from floods

☐ The rivers gave water and soft, rich soil for crops

☐ The desert was too cold for anyone to live in

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Water and soft, rich soil. Farming needs both, and the desert has neither. (Floods were a danger, not a safety, and farming villages there are thousands of years older than any emperor.)

:::

## Worked example: why the Wei River valley?


:::keep-together

### Why there?

**Historians often point out that the Wei River valley was a strong base for the state of Qin. Which reason uses the geography best?**

☐ It was close to the sea, so the Qin could trade by ship

☐ It was the warmest and wettest part of China, so rice grew easily

☐ It was in the middle of the Gobi Desert, far from any enemies

☐ It had river water, good farmland and mountains that slowed attackers

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It had river water, good farmland and mountains that slowed attackers

Farmland and water fed the people, and the mountains around the valley helped protect them. Both are reasons the place worked.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing a map note (made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  The Qin capital was in the Wei River valley, a branch of the Yellow River.
 2  The valley had river water, good soil, and mountains and passes around it.
 3  Nib says: "The rivers and mountains just happened to be nearby. The Qin could have built the same cities anywhere."
 4  Nib says: "So the land doesn't help explain where people lived."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib treats the rivers and mountains as scenery that 'just happened' to be there. The water, soil and mountains are the reasons people could farm and defend the valley. Somewhere without them could not support the same cities.

:::


:::keep-together

### Farming or defence?

**Which feature mainly helped with defence rather than farming?**

☐ The yellow soil carried by the Yellow River

☐ The warm, wet weather along the Yangtze

☐ The soft loess soil of the plateau

☐ The Qinling Mountains south of the Wei valley

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The Qinling Mountains south of the Wei valley

Mountains made a barrier that attackers had to cross. Soil, silt and rain help crops grow.
