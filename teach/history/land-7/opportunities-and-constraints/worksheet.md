# Help and limits

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The Yellow River fed millions of people. It also drowned whole villages when it burst its banks. So was it a gift or a curse?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain how the physical environment shaped settlement, trade and development in ancient China by weighing opportunities and constraints together, including how people changed their land in response.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say how one feature of the land can be both an opportunity and a constraint. I can tell a natural feature from a change people made to the land. I can write a balanced explanation that includes what people did, not only what the land did.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the land and what people did


:::keep-together

### Natural or human-made?

**Is the Lingqu Canal a natural feature of the land?**

☐ Yes: it was there before the Qin, and they just made use of it

☐ No: it is a road over the hills, not a waterway

☐ No: people dug it to join two rivers through a barrier of hills

☐ Yes: it carries water like a river, so it is part of the landscape

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: people dug it to join two rivers through a barrier of hills

The canal is a human response to a constraint: hills that blocked river travel between north and south.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing a draft paragraph (made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  The Yellow River's silt fed millions of farmers.
 2  Its floods forced people to build dykes again and again.
 3  Nib says: "So geography decided everything. The Qin and Han could not change anything about their land."
 4  Nib says: "Their history was set by the rivers and mountains before anyone was born."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib says geography decided everything. The land set opportunities and limits, but people responded: they built dykes against floods and canals across hills. Their choices changed what the land made possible.

:::


:::keep-together

### Cut off?

**Did the mountains and deserts of the west cut ancient China off from other societies?**

☐ No: they were easy to cross, so they made no real difference

☐ Yes: deserts and mountains always stop trade, wherever they are

☐ No: they made contact harder, but people still crossed by narrow routes

☐ Yes: no one ever crossed them, so China had no contact with the west

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: they made contact harder, but people still crossed by narrow routes

Barriers shape where and how contact happens, through narrow routes such as the Hexi Corridor. They rarely stop it altogether.

:::


:::typical

## Vary: a route to the west

In the Han period, in the 2nd century BCE, an official named **Zhang Qian** was sent west on a mission to the lands of Central Asia. Routes through the Hexi Corridor became part of the long trade routes later called the **Silk Roads**, which UNESCO lists as World Heritage.
