# From farming to cities

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A bronze-maker spends all day at a hot furnace. A tax official spends all day counting. Neither grows a single grain of millet. So who feeds them?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain how fertile land and water control let farmers grow a surplus, and how that surplus fed people who did not farm, such as craftworkers, officials and soldiers.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a surplus is and work it out from a record. I can explain how a surplus fed people who did not farm. I can explain why farming had to come before cities and governments.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

```
 1  People were growing millet along the Yellow River about 7,800 years ago.

 4  Nib says: "So the cities came first, and the farming came after."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib has the order backwards. People were farming along the Yellow River thousands of years before any emperor. Cities, armies and officials needed a farming surplus to feed them, so the farming had to come first.

### What surplus means

**What does the 200-sack surplus in the village record mean?**

☐ The whole harvest that the village grew and stored that year

☐ Every farmer in the village was now rich, with grain to spare

☐ Spare grain that nobody needed, so the village threw it away

☐ Food beyond what the families needed, to store or tax to feed others

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Food beyond what the families needed, to store or tax to feed others

A surplus is extra food. Its value is what it lets happen: storage for bad years, and food for people who do not farm.

:::

## Vary: taming a river

The state of Qin built the **Dujiangyan** water system around 256 BCE, on the Min River in what is now Sichuan. It split the river to control floods and sent water out to the fields of the Chengdu Plain. It is still in use today, and UNESCO lists it as World Heritage.

### When the river floods

**The Yellow River's floods sometimes wiped out a whole year's crops. What does that tell you about fertile land?**

☐ Good soil helped, but harvests could fail, so people stored grain

☐ Good soil meant every harvest was large, whatever the weather

☐ Floods only ever helped, because they brought fresh soil

☐ Floods meant nobody could farm near the river at all

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Good soil helped, but harvests could fail, so people stored grain

Rich land came with risks. Granaries and water works were how people dealt with bad years.
