## Why it matters

> A bronze-maker spends all day at a hot furnace. A tax official spends all day counting. Neither grows a single grain of millet. So who feeds them?

<!-- Speaker note: Farming in northern China is very old. Archaeologists have found millet grown along the middle Yellow River about 7,800 years ago. The Qin did not unite China until 221 BCE, more than 5,000 years later. A **surplus** is food left over after the farming families have eaten what they need. Historians often explain how ancient cities grew like this: 1. Good soil and water let farmers grow crops most years. 2. Some years they grow **more than their own families need**: a surplus. 3. The surplus can be stored, traded, or paid to the government as **grain tax**. 4. Stored grain feeds people who do **not** farm: craftworkers, officials, soldiers and scholars. 5. With more of those people, towns grow into cities, and governments grow too. It is one way of explaining it, not the only one, and each link needs evidence. But it helps explain why farming land mattered so much. Here is a grain record for **a made-up Qin farming village** (the village and the numbers are made up for this lesson): | This year | Sacks of grain | |---|---| | Harvested by all the village's farms | 600 | | Eaten by the farming families | 400 | | Paid to the government as grain tax | 100 | | Stored in the village granary | 100 | -->

## The chain

**Match each step in one way historians explain the growth of cities to what it leads to.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Fertile soil and river water` | A. Harvests are more reliable, with less damage from floods and drought |
| `Dykes and canals to control water` | B. Food for craftworkers, officials and soldiers who do not farm |
| `More grain than farm families need` | C. A surplus to store, trade or pay as tax |
| `Stored and taxed grain` | D. Crops grow well in most years |

* Answer: Fertile soil and river water → D. Crops grow well in most years; Dykes and canals to control water → A. Harvests are more reliable, with less damage from floods and drought; More grain than farm families need → C. A surplus to store, trade or pay as tax; Stored and taxed grain → B. Food for craftworkers, officials and soldiers who do not farm

## Vary: taming a river

The state of Qin built the **Dujiangyan** water system around 256 BCE, on the Min River in what is now Sichuan. It split the river to control floods and sent water out to the fields of the Chengdu Plain. It is still in use today, and UNESCO lists it as World Heritage.

## When the river floods

**The Yellow River's floods sometimes wiped out a whole year's crops. What does that tell you about fertile land?**

- Good soil helped, but harvests could fail, so people stored grain
- Good soil meant every harvest was large, whatever the weather
- Floods only ever helped, because they brought fresh soil
- Floods meant nobody could farm near the river at all

* Answer: Good soil helped, but harvests could fail, so people stored grain
