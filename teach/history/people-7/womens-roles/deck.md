## Why it matters

> Most of what we read about Han China was written by men who worked for the court. What does that leave out?

<!-- Speaker note: Historians want to know about everyone in a society, not only the people in charge. For women in Qin and Han China, that is harder, because of **who did the writing**. Most writing that survives was by educated men working for the court, and they mostly wrote about government, war and other men. So historians piece women's lives together from several kinds of evidence: > **Ban Zhao** (born about 45 CE, died about 117 CE) was a scholar at the Han court. After her brother died, she helped finish the *Book of Han*, the official history of the earlier Han. She taught the empress and other women at court, and wrote *Lessons for Women*, a book for the young women of her family about how they should behave as wives and daughters: modest, hard-working and obedient. Readers today disagree about it. > **Empress Lü** was the wife of the first Han emperor. After he died in 195 BCE, she held power for about fifteen years as empress dowager and regent, putting her own relatives in high positions. Most of what we know about her comes from Sima Qian, a Han court historian writing later, who disapproved of her. > **Tomb objects.** Han tombs hold pottery figures and pictures of women working: spinning and weaving, cooking, serving and entertaining. They show work that the written histories rarely mention. Each kind tells you something different, and each has limits. A book telling women how to behave tells you what an educated woman at court thought they *should* do. It does not tell you what every woman actually did. -->

## What can the book tell us?

**What can Lessons for Women best tell historians?**

- That women in Han China had no say in anything
- Nothing, because it was written by a woman
- What one educated woman at court thought women should do
- How every woman in Han China actually lived

* Answer: What one educated woman at court thought women should do

## Empress Lü

**How did Empress Lü influence Han society?**

- She wrote the official history of the Han
- She had no influence because women could not hold power
- She ruled with the title of emperor
- She held power at court as regent for about fifteen years

* Answer: She held power at court as regent for about fifteen years

<!-- Speaker note: She ruled as regent and put her relatives in high positions. Some women held great power, usually through family: as mothers, wives and widows of rulers. -->

## What each kind can show

**Match each kind of evidence to what it can best show about women in Han China.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Ban Zhao's Lessons for Women` | A. What a court historian thought of a powerful woman |
| `Sima Qian's account of Empress Lü` | B. A gap in the evidence, not proof they did nothing |
| `Pottery figures of women at work in tombs` | C. Kinds of work women did, at least in households that could afford such tombs |
| `The lack of writing about farm women` | D. What an educated woman thought women should do |

* Answer: Ban Zhao's Lessons for Women → D. What an educated woman thought women should do; Sima Qian's account of Empress Lü → A.
