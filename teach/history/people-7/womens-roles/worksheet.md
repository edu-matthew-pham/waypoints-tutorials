# What did women do, and how do we know?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Most of what we read about Han China was written by men who worked for the court. What does that leave out?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe some roles of women in Qin and Han China, identify kinds of evidence about their lives and its limits, and explain how women influenced the society despite constraints.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe two roles women had in an ancient society. I can explain why evidence about most women is limited. I can give a specific example of a woman's influence.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Why do historians know less about the lives of most women in Han China than about officials?**

☐ Most surviving writing was by men at court

☐ Women in Han China were not allowed to write

☐ All the sources about women were deliberately destroyed

☐ Women did nothing important, so there was nothing to write

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Who did the writing. Educated men at court wrote most of what survives, and they wrote mostly about government. Some women did write (Ban Zhao, below). The gap is a limit of the evidence, not a sign that women did nothing.

:::

## Worked example: reading the evidence


:::keep-together

### What can the book tell us?

**What can Lessons for Women best tell historians?**

☐ That women in Han China had no say in anything

☐ Nothing, because it was written by a woman

☐ What one educated woman at court thought women should do

☐ How every woman in Han China actually lived

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** What one educated woman at court thought women should do

A book about how people should behave is evidence of expectations, from one person's point of view.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing about women in Han China (Nib's notes are made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Most writing that survives from Han China was by men at court.
 2  Very few written sources describe the lives of ordinary women.
 3  Nib says: "So women in Han China had no influence on their society."
 4  Nib says: "That's why historians don't need to study them."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib treats the lack of written sources as proof that women had no influence. The sources are thin because of who did the writing. Ban Zhao and Empress Lü both shaped the Han court, and tomb objects show women's work everywhere.

:::


:::keep-together

### Empress Lü

**How did Empress Lü influence Han society?**

☐ She wrote the official history of the Han

☐ She had no influence because women could not hold power

☐ She ruled with the title of emperor

☐ She held power at court as regent for about fifteen years

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** She held power at court as regent for about fifteen years

She ruled as regent and put her relatives in high positions. Some women held great power, usually through family: as mothers, wives and widows of rulers.

## What each kind can show


:::keep-together

**Match each kind of evidence to what it can best show about women in Han China.**

A. What a court historian thought of a powerful woman

B. A gap in the evidence, not proof they did nothing

C. Kinds of work women did, at least in households that could afford such tombs

D. What an educated woman thought women should do
