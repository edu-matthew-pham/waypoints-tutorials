**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The First Emperor's tomb, the walls and the roads were not built by the emperor. Who built them, and did that give those people any power?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe the roles of slaves, convict and conscripted labourers and other groups in Qin and Han China, explain how the society depended on them, and describe changes in relationships between groups over time.

## Worked example: did they matter?


:::keep-together

### The rebellion

**How did the rebellion of Chen Sheng and Wu Guang in 209 BCE influence Qin society?**

☐ Their revolt spread and helped bring down the Qin dynasty

☐ It made the Qin rulers go easier on conscripts

☐ It was a revolt of officials against the emperor

☐ It changed nothing, because conscripts had no power

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Their revolt spread and helped bring down the Qin dynasty

People with little power could still change history, especially when many acted together.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing about slaves and labourers in Qin and Han China (Nib's notes are made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Conscripted labourers, convicts and slaves did much of the heavy work in Qin and Han China.
 2  They had very little power over their own lives.
 3  Nib says: "So they didn't matter to the history of the Qin and Han."
 4  Nib says: "Only the emperor and his officials changed anything."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib says these groups did not matter because they had little power. The society depended on their work, and conscripts' revolt helped end the Qin. Having little power is not the same as having no influence.

:::


:::keep-together

### According to whom?

**Why does the lesson say 'according to Sima Qian' about the rebellion?**

☐ Because he was the only person in China who could write

☐ Because historians think the revolt never happened

☐ He wrote a century later, for the Han, so he had a view

☐ Because Sima Qian saw the revolt with his own eyes

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** He wrote a century later, for the Han, so he had a view

Naming the source keeps the account honest: this is what Sima Qian tells us, from where he stood.

## Change or no change?


:::keep-together

### Lasting change?

**In 9 CE Wang Mang banned buying and selling slaves. Powerful families opposed it, and he cancelled the ban in 12 CE. What does that show about change over time?**

☐ A change in the rules could be undone

☐ Nothing ever changed in Han China

☐ Slavery in China ended for good in 9 CE

☐ Once a law is made, it lasts for good

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A change in the rules could be undone

Change over time is not always in one direction; reforms can be reversed.

:::


:::keep-together

### Role or change?

**Is each statement about the role of a group, or about a change over time?**

**Answer:** Farmers paid grain tax → Role of a group; The Han taxed merchants more heavily under Emperor Wu → Change over time; Convicts did hard labour → Role of a group; Selling slaves was banned in 9 CE and allowed again in 12 CE → Change over time
