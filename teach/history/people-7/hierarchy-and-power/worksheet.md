# Who ran the society, and how?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The emperor lived in the capital. A village paying grain tax could be weeks of travel away. So who made sure the grain arrived?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe the layers of society in Qin and Han China and the roles of the nobility and officials, and explain how they used their power through registers, tax, law and organised labour.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe the layers of an ancient society. I can explain the difference between a noble and an official. I can explain one way the people at the top used their power.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining how the Qin emperor ruled (Nib's notes are made up for this lesson). Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  The First Emperor ruled all of China from his capital.
 2  Below him were nobles, officials and the farmers who were most of the people.
 3  Nib says: "The emperor was at the top, so he just made everything happen."
 4  Nib says: "So we don't need to know what the officials did."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib says the emperor just made things happen because he was at the top. Being at the top is who had power, not how it worked. The emperor's orders were carried out by officials who counted people, collected tax, ran the courts and called up labour.

:::

### Nobles or officials?

**What was the main difference between a noble and an official in Qin China?**

☐ A noble's power came from family; an official's from the emperor

☐ Nobles did all the paperwork and officials fought in the army

☐ Officials were richer, so they had more power than nobles

☐ There was no difference: both inherited their jobs

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A noble's power came from family; an official's from the emperor

An official was appointed by the emperor and could be dismissed, so he depended on the emperor. That is one reason the Qin used officials instead of noble families.

:::

## Layers and power


:::keep-together

### The hierarchy

**Which statement describes the hierarchy best?**

☐ A few at the top held most power; most people were farmers

☐ The emperor held all the power, and officials had none

☐ Merchants were at the top because they had the most money

☐ Power was shared out fairly evenly between the layers

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A few at the top held most power; most people were farmers

Layers, with power concentrated at the top, and most people farming near the bottom, paying tax and owing labour.

:::
