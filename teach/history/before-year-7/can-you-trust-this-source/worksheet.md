# Can you trust this source?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A web page says the school pond is the best place to see frogs. Do you believe it? What would you want to know first?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students ask two questions before trusting a source: where does it come from, and is it fact or opinion? They drop the idea that anything on a page can be trusted.

**Students should be able to say:** I can ask who made a source and when. I can tell a fact I could check from an opinion. I can say what I would check before relying on a source.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

Look for two things in every source: where it comes from, and whether it is saying facts or opinions.

:::keep-together

### Where from

**Two pages say how many frogs live in the pond. One is by the science teacher, with the date she counted them. The other has no name or date. Which do you trust more?**

☐ They are the same

☐ Neither, because pages can be wrong

☐ The other one, because it is shorter

☐ The teacher's page: you know who made it and when

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The teacher's page: you know who made it and when

A source that tells you who made it and when is easier to check.

:::


:::keep-together

### Fact or opinion

**A page says 'This is the best pond in the world.' What is it?**

☐ A fact, because it is on a page

☐ Nothing: it has no meaning

☐ A fact, because it says 'world'

☐ An opinion: someone's view, not a fact you can check

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** An opinion: someone's view, not a fact you can check

'Best' is a view. You cannot check it like a measurement.

:::


## Check


:::keep-together

### The canteen page

**A page about school lunches is written by the canteen manager, who makes the lunches. It says 'Our lunches are the tastiest in town.' What should you think?**

☐ It is the best source because it is by an expert

☐ It is an opinion from someone who made them, so check it against other views

☐ It is false because the manager made it

☐ It is a fact because the manager knows

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is an opinion from someone who made them, so check it against other views

It is useful for what the canteen thinks, but 'tastiest' is an opinion, so look for other views too.

:::


:::keep-together
