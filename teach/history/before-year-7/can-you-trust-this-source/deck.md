# Can you trust this source?

Before Year 7 History · Sources

<!-- Speaker note: Students ask two questions before trusting a source: where does it come from, and is it fact or opinion? They drop the idea that anything on a page can be trusted. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 4 min · Check 4 min · Apply 2 min). -->

## Today

- I can ask who made a source and when.
- I can tell a fact I could check from an opinion.
- I can say what I would check before relying on a source.

## Why it matters

> A web page says the school pond is the best place to see frogs. Do you believe it? What would you want to know first?

<!-- Speaker note: When you read something to find out about a topic, it helps to ask two questions before you trust it. **Where does it come from?** Who made it, and when? **What does it say?** Are they facts you could check, or is it someone's opinion? -->

## Think first

**A page about the school pond says 'The pond is the best place to see frogs.' It has no name and no date. What would you want to know first?**

- Who wrote it, and when
- If it has a picture
- What colour the page is
- How many words it has

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Who wrote it, and when. Without that, you cannot tell if they know the pond, or if it is out of date. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Where from

**Two pages say how many frogs live in the pond. One is by the science teacher, with the date she counted them. The other has no name or date. Which do you trust more?**

- They are the same
- Neither, because pages can be wrong
- The other one, because it is shorter
- The teacher's page: you know who made it and when

* Answer: The teacher's page: you know who made it and when

<!-- Speaker note: A source that tells you who made it and when is easier to check. -->

## Fact or opinion

**A page says 'This is the best pond in the world.' What is it?**

- A fact, because it is on a page
- Nothing: it has no meaning
- A fact, because it says 'world'
- An opinion: someone's view, not a fact you can check

* Answer: An opinion: someone's view, not a fact you can check

<!-- Speaker note: 'Best' is a view. You cannot check it like a measurement. -->

## The canteen page

**A page about school lunches is written by the canteen manager, who makes the lunches. It says 'Our lunches are the tastiest in town.' What should you think?**

- It is the best source because it is by an expert
- It is an opinion from someone who made them, so check it against other views
- It is false because the manager made it
- It is a fact because the manager knows

* Answer: It is an opinion from someone who made them, so check it against other views

<!-- Speaker note: It is useful for what the canteen thinks, but 'tastiest' is an opinion, so look for other views too. -->

## Fact or opinion

**Is each statement about school lunches a fact or an opinion?**

Groups: Fact · Opinion

Lunch costs $4 · The pasta is delicious · There are three hot choices each day · Nobody likes the salad

* Answer: Lunch costs $4 → Fact; The pasta is delicious → Opinion; There are three hot choices each day → Fact; Nobody likes the salad → Opinion
