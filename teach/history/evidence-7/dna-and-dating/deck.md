## Why it matters

> One report says two ancient people were close biological relatives. Another gives an estimated age range for charcoal. Have the labs answered the same question?

<!-- Speaker note: **DNA** carries inherited biological information. Scientists can compare surviving DNA from ancient human remains to investigate biological relationships. DNA does not tell us a person's name or beliefs by itself. The comparison and the question matter. **Radiocarbon dating** estimates when once-living material stopped exchanging carbon with its surroundings. It can help date charcoal or other suitable organic samples. It does not directly date a stone wall. Reports give uncertainty, often as a range. A date for a sample is evidence to compare with its archaeological context. These two reports are **invented teaching examples** about an ancient settlement. No actual people or laboratory results are described. - **Report A:** DNA from two ancient individuals is consistent with a close biological relationship. - **Report B:** a charcoal sample has a modelled calendar range of 300 to 200 BCE at the stated confidence level. -->

## Exit ticket

**What could DNA comparison tell us about two ancient individuals, and what could it not tell us by itself?**

* It could support an inference about biological relationship. It would not by itself give their names, beliefs or an exact event date.
