# DNA and dating answer different questions

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** One report says two ancient people were close biological relatives. Another gives an estimated age range for charcoal. Have the labs answered the same question?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe how ancient DNA can reveal biological relationships. Distinguish that question from dating organic material, and read a date range as an estimate rather than an exact year.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe one thing ancient DNA comparison can reveal. I can distinguish a biological relationship from a dating estimate.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: putting the reports together

Report A tells you something layers cannot: that two people were probably related. Report B gives a time estimate. Read the whole range, not just the middle year. Neither report tells you why people lived together, or whether the charcoal belongs to the event you are investigating.

:::keep-together

### Use both reports

**Which conclusion uses both reports carefully?**

☐ The DNA gives an exact date for the charcoal and the people

☐ Two people were probably related; the charcoal has a date range

☐ The two were probably royals, since they were related

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Two people were probably related; the charcoal has a date range

Each report adds a different piece of evidence. How they connect to the settlement still depends on where the bones and charcoal were found.

:::


:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib writes down Report B as “the settlement was built in exactly 250 BCE”. What is the problem?**

☐ The range should be read the other way round, 200 to 300 BCE

☐ It is an estimate for the charcoal, not the year of building

☐ Charcoal cannot be dated at all, so the report is wrong

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is an estimate for the charcoal, not the year of building

250 BCE is inside the range, but the report does not pick that year. And it dates the charcoal, not the building.

:::

## Which evidence?


:::keep-together

**You want to find out whether two ancient people were biological relatives. Which is the best evidence to start with?**

☐ How deep each was buried, compared with the other

☐ Similar pots buried with each of them in their graves

☐ A comparison of their DNA, plus the dig records

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A comparison of their DNA, plus the dig records

Comparing DNA can test whether they were related. The dig records help you make sense of the result.

:::
