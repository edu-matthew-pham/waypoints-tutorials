# Reading the layers

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two pieces of pottery look alike. One is in the bottom layer and one is near the surface. What would you need to know before calling one older?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Read an excavation as a sequence of layers. Use the position of a find and the evidence beside it to explain what may have changed, while keeping relative age separate from an exact date.

**Students should be able to say:** I can use undisturbed layers to put finds in relative order. I can use finds to suggest a change and name a limit.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

The earlier floor lies below the burnt material. A later floor lies above it. A fire between two periods of use is one interpretation. The burnt material alone does not show who started a fire or whether there was an attack.

:::keep-together

### What follows from the evidence?

**Which account fits the sequence best?**

☐ An invading army certainly caused the fire

☐ The new floor was laid before the earlier floor

☐ A fire may have happened between two periods of use

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A fire may have happened between two periods of use

A, B and C support a sequence of use, burning and later use. The cause of the burning is still open.

:::


:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib writes: “B is deeper than C, so B is exactly 500 years older.” What needs changing?**

☐ Keep the order but remove the exact time gap

☐ Reverse the order because deeper means newer

☐ Keep the time gap because three layers prove it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Keep the order but remove the exact time gap

Depth can establish relative order here. It does not measure a number of years.

:::

## Vary: change one condition

Change one condition: a later pit cuts through C and B into A. A recent coin falls into its filling. The coin is deep, but the pit is later than the layers it cuts.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?

## A fresh case


:::keep-together

**At a second made-up, undisturbed site, a floor lies below flood mud and a rebuilt wall lies above it. Which account is supported?**

☐ The wall proves why the flood happened

☐ A period of use was followed by flooding and rebuilding

☐ Flooding came before any floor existed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A period of use was followed by flooding and rebuilding

The positions give a sequence. They do not give the cause or an exact year.

:::
