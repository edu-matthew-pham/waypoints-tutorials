# Reading the layers

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two pieces of pottery look alike. One is in the bottom layer and one is near the surface. What would you need to know before calling one older?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Read an excavation as a sequence of layers. Use the position of a find and the evidence beside it to explain what may have changed, while keeping relative age separate from an exact date.

**Students should be able to say:** I can use undisturbed layers to put finds in relative order. I can use finds to suggest a change and name a limit.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Dig it yourself

Now dig the same made-up site from the top down. You reach the newest layer first.

## Worked example: what happened here?

The earlier floor is below the burnt material, and a later floor is above it. A fire between two periods of use is one way to read that. The burnt material on its own does not show who started the fire or whether there was an attack.

:::keep-together

### What can you say from the layers?

**Which story fits the layers best?**

☐ The new floor was laid before the earlier floor

☐ A fire may have happened between two periods of use

☐ An invading army probably burned the place down

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A fire may have happened between two periods of use

An earlier floor, then burnt material, then a new floor. That fits people using the place, a fire, then people using it again. What caused the fire is still an open question.

:::


:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib writes: “B is deeper than C, so B is exactly 500 years older.” What needs changing?**

☐ Keep the 500 years, because three layers prove it

☐ Swap the order, because deeper layers are newer

☐ Keep the order but take out the 500 years

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Keep the order but take out the 500 years

Being deeper tells you B is older here. It does not tell you by how many years.

## A new site


:::keep-together

**At a second made-up site, nothing has been disturbed. A floor lies under a layer of flood mud, and a rebuilt wall sits on top of the mud. Which story do the layers support?**

☐ A flood came first, and then people laid the floor on the mud

☐ People used the floor, then a flood came, then they rebuilt

☐ The rebuilt wall shows what caused the flood in the first place

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** People used the floor, then a flood came, then they rebuilt

The positions give you an order. They do not tell you the cause or the year.

:::
