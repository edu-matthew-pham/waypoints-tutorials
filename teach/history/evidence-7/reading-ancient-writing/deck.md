## Why it matters

> A ruler’s inscription says a town prospered. A dig finds burnt roofs. Does one source have to be thrown away?

<!-- Speaker note: An **inscription** is writing cut or marked onto a surface such as stone. Laws, letters and accounts can also be written sources. Reading an ancient text can involve damaged words, unfamiliar meanings and translation. Even when the words can be read, we still ask who made the text, why and in what circumstances. These source cards are **made up**, set in an invented ancient town. They are not translations of a real Qin inscription. - **Written card:** a ruler's stone inscription praises a new storehouse and calls the town prosperous. One damaged word could mean “gift” or “payment”. - **Physical card:** an excavation records a storehouse floor below burnt roof wood. Written, physical and oral sources need questions appropriate to them. Writing is not automatically the most reliable kind. Comparing different sources on a question is sometimes called **triangulation**. -->
