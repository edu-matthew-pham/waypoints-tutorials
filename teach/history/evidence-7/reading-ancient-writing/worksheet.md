# Reading ancient writing alongside other evidence

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A ruler’s inscription says a town prospered. A dig finds burnt roofs. Does one source have to be thrown away?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Identify inscriptions as ancient written sources and explain translation and context problems. Compare a written claim with physical evidence instead of ranking writing as automatically more reliable.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name a written source and a difficulty in interpreting it. I can compare source types without assuming writing is always best.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

For “What did the ruler want readers to think?”, the inscription helps. For “Was there a fire after the floor was used?”, the layer record helps. The evidence can answer different parts of the story. A boast of prosperity does not prove everyone was prosperous; burnt wood does not prove the inscription was false when it was made.

:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib says: “It was carved in stone, so the town must have been prosperous for everyone.” Which reply is strongest?**

☐ Stone writing can never be used

☐ The inscription shows a claim; its purpose and other evidence need checking

☐ A physical find always tells the whole story

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The inscription shows a claim; its purpose and other evidence need checking

An inscription is evidence of what was recorded. Assessing the wider claim needs more evidence.

## A fresh case


:::keep-together

**A second made-up edict orders a road to be repaired. What can it establish most directly?**

☐ Someone issued a repair order; completion needs other evidence

☐ Every section was repaired at once

☐ No one used the road before the order

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Someone issued a repair order; completion needs other evidence

An instruction is not proof that it was carried out. Road remains or later records could help.

:::
