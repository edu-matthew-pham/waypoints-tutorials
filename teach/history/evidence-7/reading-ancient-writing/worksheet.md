# Reading ancient writing alongside other evidence

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A ruler’s inscription says a town prospered. A dig finds burnt roofs. Does one source have to be thrown away?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Identify inscriptions as ancient written sources and explain translation and context problems. Compare a written claim with physical evidence instead of ranking writing as automatically more reliable.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name a written source and a difficulty in interpreting it. I can compare source types without assuming writing is always best.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the storehouse

If your question is “What did the ruler want people to think?”, the inscription helps. If your question is “Was there a fire after the floor was used?”, the dig helps. Each answers a different part of the story. A boast about prosperity does not prove everyone was well off. Burnt wood does not prove the inscription was false when it was carved.

:::keep-together

### Plan the comparison

**What would help you most to compare the two cards?**

☐ Check the dates, purpose and dig records of both cards

☐ Go with whichever card gives more detail about the town

☐ Believe the inscription, since a ruler would know best

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Check the dates, purpose and dig records of both cards

Find out what each source supports, and whether they are even about the same time.

:::


:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib says: “It was carved in stone, so the town must have been prosperous for everyone.” Which reply is best?**

☐ The burnt floor tells the whole story, so use that instead

☐ Writing in stone can never be used as evidence, so ignore it

☐ It shows what the ruler claimed; check it against other evidence

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It shows what the ruler claimed; check it against other evidence

An inscription is good evidence of what someone chose to record. Whether it was true for everyone needs more evidence.

:::

## An order to repair a road


:::keep-together

**A second made-up inscription orders a road to be repaired. What does it show most directly?**

☐ That the road was broken by a war or a flood

☐ That the road was repaired, since the order was carved

☐ That a repair was ordered, not that it was done

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** That a repair was ordered, not that it was done

An order is not proof that it was carried out. The remains of the road, or later records, could help.

:::
