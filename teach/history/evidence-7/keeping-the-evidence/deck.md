## Why it matters

> A road could be built quickly across an excavation. The objects can be moved to a museum. What might still be lost?

<!-- Speaker note: **Conservation** means caring for remains and sites so their evidence survives. A find's position, its layer and its relation to other finds can be evidence too. Once those relationships are destroyed without records, putting the objects in a museum cannot recreate them. Careful excavation also changes a site, which is why recording and conservation planning matter. Governments can establish protected areas, control building work and support site management. UNESCO coordinates the World Heritage system and international cooperation; listing does not itself replace local protection or daily care. **Real example:** UNESCO's account of the Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor describes the terracotta figures and a conservation plan that sets protection boundaries and construction controls. See [UNESCO's site account](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/441). The road cases below are **made up**, not proposals for that site. -->

## What follows from the evidence?

**Which recommendation uses the evidence criterion most clearly?**

- Ignore the excavation because some objects look plain
- Investigate B because it avoids the layers; compare costs before deciding
- Choose A because speed is the only issue

* Answer: Investigate B because it avoids the layers; compare costs before deciding
