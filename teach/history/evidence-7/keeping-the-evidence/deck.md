## Today

**We are learning to** explain why a site's context must be kept as well as its objects, and who helps protect sites.

- I can name a protection role for a government or international body.

## Why it matters

> A road could be built quickly across an excavation. The objects can be moved to a museum. What might still be lost?

<!-- Speaker note: **Conservation** means looking after old remains and sites so the evidence in them lasts. The evidence is not only the objects. Where something was found, which layer it was in, and what was next to it are evidence too. If a site is destroyed before anyone records those things, putting the objects in a museum cannot bring that information back. Even a careful dig changes a site, which is why recording and planning matter. Governments can protect sites by law, control building work nearby and pay for their care. UNESCO, an international organisation, runs the World Heritage list and helps countries work together. Being on the list does not replace the local protection and daily care a site needs. **A real example:** UNESCO's [page on the Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/441) describes the clay figures and a conservation plan that sets protection boundaries and limits on building. The road cases below are **made up**. They are not plans for that site. -->

## Choose a route

**Which recommendation uses the evidence most clearly?**

- Look at Route B, which avoids the layers, then compare costs

## A lost record

**At a made-up site, the finds are kept but every record of where they were found is lost. Which question becomes harder to answer?**

- What was this tool used for, judging by its edge?
- What happened first at this site, and what came later?
- What is this pot made of, and how was it fired?

* Answer: What happened first at this site, and what came later?

<!-- Speaker note: Without the layers and positions, you lose the order that would answer the question about time. What this step is for: Students apply the same reasoning to a new case on their own. -->
