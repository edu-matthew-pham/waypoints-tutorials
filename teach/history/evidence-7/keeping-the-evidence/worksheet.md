# Keeping the evidence for the next question

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A road could be built quickly across an excavation. The objects can be moved to a museum. What might still be lost?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain why destroying archaeological evidence closes off future questions. Identify how governments and international bodies help conserve sites, and weigh a made-up development proposal against clear evidence criteria.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why objects and their context need conserving. I can name a protection role for a government or international body.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

A made-up council compares two routes. Route A is shorter but cuts through unrecorded layers. Route B avoids those layers but costs more. A useful recommendation names the evidence at risk, a protection action and the remaining trade-off. It does not pretend every cost or consequence is known.

:::keep-together

### What follows from the evidence?

**Which recommendation uses the evidence criterion most clearly?**

☐ Ignore the excavation because some objects look plain

☐ Investigate B because it avoids the layers; compare costs before deciding

☐ Choose A because speed is the only issue

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Investigate B because it avoids the layers; compare costs before deciding

Avoiding irreplaceable layers protects evidence. Costs and practical effects still need consideration.

:::


:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib says: “We kept the pots, so nothing important was lost.” What is the best correction?**

☐ The original positions and layers may have been lost

☐ A museum can work out every original position

☐ Every object should always stay buried

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The original positions and layers may have been lost

The pots survive, but their relationships may not. Evidence includes context.

:::

## A fresh case


:::keep-together

**A made-up site can keep its finds but loses all records of their locations. Which future question becomes harder?**

☐ Which activity came before another at this site?

☐ What colour is this surviving pot?

☐ How heavy is this surviving tool?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Which activity came before another at this site?

The lost locations and layers weaken the sequence that could answer the timing question.

:::
