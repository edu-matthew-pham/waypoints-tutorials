# Keeping the evidence for the next question

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A road could be built quickly across an excavation. The objects can be moved to a museum. What might still be lost?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain why destroying archaeological evidence closes off future questions. Identify how governments and international bodies help conserve sites, and weigh a made-up development proposal against clear evidence criteria.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why objects and their context need conserving. I can name a protection role for a government or international body.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

☐ Yes, a photo of the objects replaces the site

My prediction, and why:

**What happens:** No. A saved object, or a photo of it, cannot tell you which layer it came from or what lay next to it.

## Worked example: two routes for a road

A made-up council is choosing between two routes for a road. Route A is shorter, but it cuts through layers nobody has recorded. Route B goes around those layers, but it costs more. A good recommendation names the evidence at risk, an action to protect it, and the trade-off that is left. It does not pretend every cost is known.

:::keep-together

### Choose a route

**Which recommendation uses the evidence most clearly?**

☐ Ignore the layers, because the objects in them look plain

☐ Look at Route B, which avoids the layers, then compare costs

☐ Choose Route A, because the only thing that matters is speed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Look at Route B, which avoids the layers, then compare costs

Going around layers that cannot be replaced protects the evidence. The cost and other effects still need weighing up.

:::


:::keep-together

### Check Nib’s claim

**Nib says: “We kept the pots, so nothing important was lost.” What is the best correction?**

☐ Where the pots were, and their layers, may have been lost

☐ Every object should always stay buried, so digging was wrong

☐ A museum can work out later where every pot was found

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Where the pots were, and their layers, may have been lost

The pots survive, but the information about where they were may not. That information is evidence too.

:::

## A lost record


:::keep-together

**At a made-up site, the finds are kept but every record of where they were found is lost. Which question becomes harder to answer?**

☐ What was this tool used for, judging by its edge?

☐ What happened first at this site, and what came later?

☐ What is this pot made of, and how was it fired?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** What happened first at this site, and what came later?

Without the layers and positions, you lose the order that would answer the question about time.

:::
