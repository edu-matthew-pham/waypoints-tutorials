# Why did people move?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Picture people leaving Africa. Did you imagine a group packing up and walking off towards a new continent? Scientists think it happened very differently.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Describe how environmental changes, such as shifts in rainfall, spreading deserts and changing sea levels, are thought to have moved early humans out of Africa and into other parts of the world over many generations.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe one environmental change and how it could move people. I can explain why migration happened over many generations, not as a planned trip. I can tell a push from a pull.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the valley that dried up


:::keep-together

### Read the model

**In the made-up valley model, why did the group end up in the next valley?**

☐ The group decided one day to walk to a better valley they had heard about

☐ The lake flooded over many years and pushed them north

☐ The rain lessened, the lake dried and the animals moved, so they followed

☐ The group was forced out by another group, and the climate had nothing to do with it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The rain lessened, the lake dried and the animals moved, so they followed

Less rain changed the lake and the animals, and the group followed. That is the chain.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is explaining how people left Africa. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Our species began in Africa and later spread across the world.
 2  Nib says: "One day a group decided to leave Africa and walk to a new continent they had heard about."
 3  Nib says: "They packed up and made the trip in a few months."
 4  Nib says: "So migration was a planned journey to a place they knew."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Nib pictures a planned trip to a place they knew about. Nobody at the time knew about other continents. Groups shifted their range a little at a time, over many generations, as water and food moved.

:::


:::keep-together

### Push or pull?

**Which of these is a pull (something drawing people to a place) rather than a push (something driving them away)?**

☐ A desert spreading slowly towards their camp

☐ New grassland and a lake in a corridor that just turned green

☐ The home valley's lake drying up over many years

☐ The animals they hunt leaving the area for good

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** New grassland and a lake in a corridor that just turned green

Pushes drive people away from where they are; pulls draw them to somewhere new. Environmental change can do both.

:::

## Vary: a green Arabia

In 2018, scientists reported a fossil finger bone of our species found at Al Wusta, in what is now desert in Saudi Arabia. It is about 85,000 years old or more. The same layers held signs of a lake and grassland, from a time when the region was much wetter than it is today (Groucutt and others, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*).

## Push, pull or barrier?


:::keep-together

**Sort each environmental change by what it did to people's movement.**

Groups: Push away, Pull towards, Barrier

- Desert spreading into a group's home range: {{field-15}}
- A new lake and grassland to the north: {{field-15}}
- A wide, dry desert with no water stops: {{field-15}}
- Herds leaving the area as the grass dies: {{field-15}}
- A coastline rich in shellfish, newly exposed as the sea falls: {{field-15}}
- Deep sea with no land in sight: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Desert spreading into a group's home range → Push away; A new lake and grassland to the north → Pull towards; A wide, dry desert with no water stops → Barrier; Herds leaving the area as the grass dies → Push away; A coastline rich in shellfish, newly exposed as the sea falls → Pull towards; Deep sea with no land in sight → Barrier

:::
