# How do we know?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Someone finds one stone tool in a cave. Does it tell you who made it, when, and where they came from?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Identify the kinds of evidence historians and archaeologists use for early human migration, describe what each adds, and explain that interpretations rest on several kinds together and can change.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name kinds of evidence and say what each can tell us. I can explain why one find is not proof of a whole story. I can write an interpretation that uses two kinds of evidence and names what is still uncertain.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

**A single stone tool is found lying on the surface of a cave floor, not in any layer. What can it tell you on its own?**

☐ Nothing at all, so it does not count as evidence

☐ Which kind of human made it and where they travelled from

☐ That someone made a tool, but not when, who or where from

☐ Exactly when the cave was first used by people

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** That someone made a tool. With no layer, no date and no bones, it cannot say when, who or from where. It is still evidence, just a small piece.

:::

## Worked example: reading the dig report


:::keep-together

### Choosing a dating method

**Why did the archaeologists use luminescence, not radiocarbon, for layer 5?**

☐ There was no charcoal or bone, and it may be too old for radiocarbon

☐ Radiocarbon dating only works on stone tools, not on sand

☐ Luminescence is always more exact than radiocarbon, so it is best

☐ Layer 5 is above layer 4, so it needed a different method

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** There was no charcoal or bone, and it may be too old for radiocarbon

Each method dates different material. With no charcoal, and an age that may be past radiocarbon's limit, the sand itself was dated.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's mistake

**Nib is writing up the made-up dig. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Layer 4 has stone tools and a hearth.
 2  The charcoal from the hearth is radiocarbon dated to about 45,000 years ago.
 3  Nib says: "So now we know exactly who lit the fire and the route they took to get here."
 4  Nib says: "This one hearth proves the whole story of how people reached this region."
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib treats one dated hearth as proof of a whole story: who the people were and the exact route they took. The charcoal dates a fire. Who lit it, and where they came from, need other evidence, such as fossils or DNA, and could change with new finds.

:::


:::keep-together

### Putting it together

**The archaeologists say people used the shelter at least twice, about 15,000 years apart. Which evidence supports that?**

☐ The five layers, since each layer means one more visit

☐ The hearth and its charcoal, dated to about 45,000 years ago

☐ Tools in two dated layers, about 60,000 and 45,000 years old

☐ No human bones were found, so the visits must be far apart

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Tools in two dated layers, about 60,000 and 45,000 years old

Two dated layers, both with tools, in the right order, show two times of use. Whether people came back in between is still open. That is an interpretation built from several pieces.

:::

## Evidence or proof?


:::keep-together

**Sort each statement: does it stay within the evidence, or does it claim more than the evidence can show?**

Groups: Stays within the evidence, Claims too much

- Charcoal dated to about 45,000 years ago shows a fire burned around then: {{field-15}}
- One stone tool proves which language its maker spoke: {{field-15}}
- Tools in two dated layers suggest people used the shelter more than once: {{field-15}}
- Radiocarbon shows a stone axe is 200,000 years old: {{field-15}}
- DNA suggests two groups are closely related: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Charcoal dated to about 45,000 years ago shows a fire burned around then → Stays within the evidence; One stone tool proves which language its maker spoke → Claims too much; Tools in two dated layers suggest people used the shelter more than once → Stays within the evidence; Radiocarbon shows a stone axe is 200,000 years old → Claims too much; DNA suggests two groups are closely related → Stays within the evidence

:::
