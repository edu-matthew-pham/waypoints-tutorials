## Why it matters

> Someone finds one stone tool in a cave. Does it tell you who made it, when, and where they came from?

<!-- Speaker note: There is no writing from the time people first spread out of Africa. So historians and archaeologists build **interpretations** (explanations of what probably happened) from many kinds of evidence, each answering a different part of the puzzle: | Kind of evidence | What it can tell you | A limit | |---|---|---| | **Excavation and the layers of a site** (stratigraphy) | The order things were left in. Lower, undisturbed layers are older. | Layers give an order, not a date in years. | | **Radiocarbon dating** | An age for things that were once alive (charcoal, bone, shell), up to about 50,000 years old. | It cannot date stone, and it stops working beyond about 50,000 years. | | **Luminescence dating** | When sand or soil was last exposed to sunlight or heat, which can reach much further back. | It dates the layer, not an object directly. | | **Fossils and stone tools** | Who was there (which kind of human) and what they made and did. | One find is one moment in one place. | | **DNA** | How groups of people are related, and roughly when they split apart. | Ancient DNA does not always survive. | | **Oral history** | Accounts passed down by speaking, across generations. | Like any source, it is understood through its community and its purpose. | If you have done the course *How we know*, you met layers, dating and DNA there. In short: layers give an order, dating methods give an age, and DNA shows how people are related. Here the question is how they work **together**. > **A made-up dig report (made up for this lesson).** At a made-up rock shelter somewhere in western Asia (not in Australia, and not a real site), archaeologists dig through five layers. In layer 4 they find stone tools and a hearth with charcoal. The charcoal is radiocarbon dated to about 45,000 years ago. Layer 5, below it, has more tools but no charcoal; luminescence dating of its sand gives about 60,000 years ago. No human bones are found. -->

## Choosing a dating method

**Why did the archaeologists use luminescence, not radiocarbon, for layer 5?**

- There was no charcoal or bone, and it may be too old for radiocarbon
- Radiocarbon dating only works on stone tools, not on sand
- Luminescence is always more exact than radiocarbon, so it is best
- Layer 5 is above layer 4, so it needed a different method

* Answer: There was no charcoal or bone, and it may be too old for radiocarbon

<!-- Speaker note: Each method dates different material. With no charcoal, and an age that may be past radiocarbon's limit, the sand itself was dated. -->

## Putting it together

**The archaeologists say people used the shelter at least twice, about 15,000 years apart. Which evidence supports that?**

- The five layers, since each layer means one more visit
- The hearth and its charcoal, dated to about 45,000 years ago
- Tools in two dated layers, about 60,000 and 45,000 years old
- No human bones were found, so the visits must be far apart

* Answer: Tools in two dated layers, about 60,000 and 45,000 years old

<!-- Speaker note: Two dated layers, both with tools, in the right order, show two times of use. Whether people came back in between is still open. That is an interpretation built from several pieces. -->

## Evidence or proof?

**Sort each statement: does it stay within the evidence, or does it claim more than the evidence can show?**

Groups: Stays within the evidence · Claims too much

* Answer: Charcoal dated to about 45,000 years ago shows a fire burned around then → Stays within the evidence; One stone tool proves which language its maker spoke → Claims too much; Tools in two dated layers suggest people used the shelter more than once → Stays within the evidence; Radiocarbon shows a stone axe is 200,000 years old → Claims too much; DNA suggests two groups are closely related → Stays within the evidence

## Exit ticket

**Why can one stone tool not tell us the whole story of how people reached a place?**

* It shows someone made a tool there. Without layers, dates, fossils or DNA, it cannot say when, who or where they came from; an interpretation needs several kinds of evidence.
