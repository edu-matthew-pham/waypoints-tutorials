# Prism problems

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A farmer needs a water trough that holds 500 L, a builder orders concrete in cubic metres, a pool needs filling. Each is a prism problem, often run backwards.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use V = Ah for rectangular and triangular prisms, work backwards to missing lengths, and solve capacity problems in context (AC9M8M02, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the volume of any right prism from the area of its end. I can find a missing length from a volume or a capacity. I can answer in the units the problem asks for.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Change the litres to cubic metres first.

:::keep-together

**A tank has a base 4 m by 3 m and holds 36 000 L when full. How deep is it?**

☐ 0.3 m

☐ 3 m

☐ 12 m

☐ 3000 m

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 3 m. 36 000 L = 36 m³. The base is 4 × 3 = 12 m², so the depth is 36 ÷ 12 = 3 m.

## The end, then the length


:::keep-together

### Any prism

Here is the routine.

1. $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12\ \text{cm}^2$ — Find the area of the end with its own formula.
2. $V = A \times h = 12 \times 10 = 120\ \text{cm}^3$ — Times the length of the prism.
3. $h = V \div A$ — To find a missing length, divide the volume by the end's area.

Now try one.

**A triangular prism's end has legs 5 cm and 4 cm (a right angle between them). The prism is 9 cm long. What is its volume in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 90

A = ½ × 5 × 4 = 10 cm², so V = 10 × 9 = 90 cm³.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia finds the volume of a triangular prism. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A prism's end is a right triangle with legs 3 cm and 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long.
 2  Mia uses volume = length × width × height.
 3  V = 3 × 4 × 10 = 120 cm³.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: l × w × h is for a rectangular box. Any prism is the area of its end times its length: ½ × 3 × 4 × 10 = 60 cm³.

:::


:::keep-together

### What you need

**The end of a triangular prism is a right triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. Which do you need for its area?**

☐ All three sides

☐ The 3 cm and 4 cm sides (they meet at the right angle)

☐ Only the 5 cm side

☐ The 5 cm side and the length

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The 3 cm and 4 cm sides (they meet at the right angle)

The two legs are a base and its perpendicular height: ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 cm².

:::

## Prism problems


:::keep-together

### End area times length

**A prism's end has an area of 15 cm² and it is 8 cm long. What is its volume in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 120

15 × 8 = 120 cm³.

:::


:::keep-together

### A fish tank

**A fish tank is 50 cm by 40 cm by 30 cm. How many litres does it hold?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

50 × 40 × 30 = 60 000 cm³ = 60 L.

:::


:::keep-together

### Buckets

**A trough is 2 m long, 1 m wide and 0.5 m deep. How many 10 L buckets fill it?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 100

2 × 1 × 0.5 = 1 m³ = 1000 L, and 1000 ÷ 10 = 100 buckets.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: litres

**Review: how many mL does 1.25 L hold?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1250

1.25 × 1000 = 1250 mL.
