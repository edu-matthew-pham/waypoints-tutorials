# Prism problems

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Volume

<!-- Speaker note: Students use V = Ah for rectangular and triangular prisms, work backwards to missing lengths, and solve capacity problems in context (AC9M8M02, node 2). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** the volume of any right prism is the area of its end (the cross-section) times its length: V = Ah. Work backwards to find a missing length: divide the volume by the area of the end. Give the answer in the units the problem needs.

- I can find the volume of any right prism from the area of its end.
- I can find a missing length from a volume or a capacity.
- I can answer in the units the problem asks for.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need volume and capacity conversions (previous lesson) and composite areas. The board's prism is built in layers of the end's area; its triangular prism has a right-triangle end. Wider: packaging (the box with the least card for a given volume), and finding dimensions for a required capacity. -->

## Why it matters

> A farmer needs a water trough that holds 500 L, a builder orders concrete in cubic metres, a pool needs filling. Each is a prism problem, often run backwards.

<!-- Speaker note: A **right prism** has the same cross-section (its end, or base) all the way along. For every right prism, $$V = A \times h$$ where $A$ is the area of the end and $h$ is the length (or height) of the prism. Find $A$ with the right area formula for the end: a rectangle, a triangle, or a composite shape. To find a missing length, work backwards: $h = V \div A$. Convert units before you start, so every length is in the same unit. -->

## Any prism

Here is the routine.

Now try one.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12\ \text{cm}^2$$

1) $V = A \times h = 12 \times 10 = 120\ \text{cm}^3$ — Times the length of the prism.
2) $h = V \div A$ — To find a missing length, divide the volume by the end's area.

## Any prism

**A triangular prism's end has legs 5 cm and 4 cm (a right angle between them). The prism is 9 cm long. What is its volume in cm³?**

* Answer: 90

<!-- Speaker note: A = ½ × 5 × 4 = 10 cm², so V = 10 × 9 = 90 cm³. -->

**Mia finds the volume of a triangular prism. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A prism's end is a right triangle with legs 3 cm and 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long.
 2  Mia uses volume = length × width × height.
 3  V = 3 × 4 × 10 = 120 cm³.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: l × w × h is for a rectangular box. Any prism is the area of its end times its length: ½ × 3 × 4 × 10 = 60 cm³. -->

## What you need

**The end of a triangular prism is a right triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. Which do you need for its area?**

- All three sides
- The 3 cm and 4 cm sides (they meet at the right angle)
- Only the 5 cm side
- The 5 cm side and the length

* Answer: The 3 cm and 4 cm sides (they meet at the right angle)

<!-- Speaker note: The two legs are a base and its perpendicular height: ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 cm². -->
