## Why it matters

> How much rain does it take to fill a water tank?

<!-- Speaker note: Rain falling on a roof runs into a tank. This tank is a cylinder with a cone-shaped bottom, and a roof of 100 m² feeds it (shape and sizes made up for the task). How many millimetres of rain fill it from empty? ## Find the mistake

**Tom finds the volume of concrete in a block with a hole through it. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A concrete block is 40 cm by 20 cm by 20 cm. A round hole with a radius of 5 cm goes straight through the 20 cm height.
 2  Block: 40 × 20 × 20 = 16 000 cm³.
 3  Hole: 3.14 × 5² × 20 = 1570 cm³.
 4  Concrete: 16 000 + 1570 = 17 570 cm³.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: a hole has no concrete in it. Tom added the hole's 1570 cm³ instead of taking it away. -->

Now try a bigger pool on paper: work it both ways, one step per line, then type the volume.

$$V = 6 \times 5 \times 1 + 4 \times 5 \times 2$$

1) $V = 30 + 40 = 70 \text{ m}^3$ — The total.
2) $V = 10 \times 5 \times 2 - 6 \times 5 \times 1$ — Take away: a big box as deep as the deep end, minus the box of missing water.
3) $V = 100 - 30 = 70 \text{ m}^3$ — The same answer both ways.

## Two ways to see a pool

**A pool is 12 m long and 6 m wide. The first 8 m is 1 m deep and the last 4 m is 2.5 m deep (made up for the task). What is its volume? (m³) Find it by adding two boxes, then check it by taking one box away from a bigger one.**

* Answer: $V = 8 \times 6 \times 1 + 4 \times 6 \times 2.5$ (add: a shallow box and a deep box); $V = 48 + 60 = 108$ (the total); $12 \times 6 \times 2.5 - 8 \times 6 \times 1.5 = 180 - 72 = 108$ (check: a big box as deep as the deep end, minus the missing water)

<!-- Speaker note: Adding: 48 + 60 = 108 m³. Taking away: 180 − 72 = 108 m³. The same, so I can trust it. -->
