## Build the model


:::keep-together

### Two rates

1. $\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance (km)}}{5} + \dfrac{\text{climb (m)}}{600}$ — Naismith's rule: two parts, two units.
2. $\dfrac{8}{5} = 1.6$ — Hours for the distance.
3. $\dfrac{450}{600} = 0.75$ — Hours for the climb.
4. $1.6 + 0.75 = 2.35$ — Total hours: about 2 hours 21 minutes.

**For the lookout walk, how many hours does the 8 km take at 5 km/h? (hours)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1.6

8 ÷ 5 = 1.6 hours.

:::


:::keep-together

### Add the parts

**How many hours does the 450 m of climb add? (hours, 2 decimal places)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.75

450 ÷ 600 = 0.75 hours.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Kai works out the lookout walk's time. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The track is 8 km with a 450 m climb. The rule is 5 km/h plus 600 m per hour of climb.
 2  450 m is 0.45 km, so the distance is 8 + 0.45 = 8.45 km.
 3  Time = 8.45 ÷ 5 = 1.69 hours.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the climb is metres of height, not kilometres of walking. It has its own rate: 600 m per hour.

## Set up and use the model


:::keep-together

### No climb

**A walk is 12 km with no climb. How many hours? (hours)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2.4

12 ÷ 5 = 2.4.

:::


:::keep-together

### Steep

**A walk is 5 km with 1200 m of climb. How many hours?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

5 ÷ 5 = 1 and 1200 ÷ 600 = 2, so 3 hours.

:::


:::keep-together
