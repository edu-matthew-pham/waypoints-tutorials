## Systematic or random

A **systematic** error pushes every reading the same way (the stretched tape, the scale that is not zeroed). A **random** error goes high or low from reading to reading.

Now try one on paper: a tape reads 1.52 m for a table that a laser says is 1.60 m. Work out by how much the tape is out, then say what kind of error it is and where it comes from.

$$1.60 - 1.52 = 0.08$$

1) $\text{all low, by about the same} \Rightarrow \text{systematic}$ — Same direction, same size.
2) $\text{averaging will not fix it}$ — The average is low by 0.08 too.
3) $\text{tape stretched?} \to \text{instrument}$ — Name where it comes from.
4) $\text{test the guess with a different tape}$ — A fix that checks the diagnosis.

## Systematic or random

**A cloth tape reads 1.52 m for a table that a laser says is 1.60 m. By how much is the tape out? (m)**

* Answer: $1.60 - 1.52$ (compare with the trusted value); $= 0.08$ (work it out); every reading is low, so the error is systematic, from the instrument (same direction every time, and it comes from the tape)

## Find the mistake

**Dev's group measures a table six times and wants to know if their tape is accurate. Click the line where the reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Dev's group measures a table six times with a cloth tape: 1.52, 1.53, 1.51, 1.52, 1.53, 1.52 m.
 2  The teacher's laser says the table is 1.60 m long.
 3  The readings are all within 0.02 m of each other.
 4  So the readings agree, which means the tape must be accurate.
 5  We will report the table as 1.52 m.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: the readings agree with each other, but they are all about 0.08 m below the laser's 1.60 m. They are close together and still wrong in the same way, so the tape (or the way it was used) has a systematic error. -->

* Answer: line 3

## Averaging

**A group measures a pencil ten times and the readings scatter: some a little high, some a little low. What does averaging the ten do?**

- It removes the error, because the average is exact
- It makes the error bigger
- It reduces the error, because the highs and lows partly cancel
- It does nothing, because every reading is wrong

* Answer: It reduces the error, because the highs and lows partly cancel

<!-- Speaker note: A random error goes both ways, so the average is better than one reading. A systematic error goes one way and survives the average. -->

## A falling ball

**A group measures how long a ball takes to fall by starting the stopwatch when they let go and stopping it when they hear it land, from the top of a tall building. What is the main source of error?**

- None: the time is exact
- The person: they are not careful
- The instrument: the stopwatch is broken
- The method: sound takes time to travel, so the time is too long

* Answer: The method: sound takes time to travel, so the time is too long

<!-- Speaker note: Waiting for the sound adds a delay to every drop, a systematic error from the method. Watching the ball land instead would remove it. -->

## Which direction

**Five readings of a length are $12.3$, $12.1$, $12.4$, $12.2$ and $12.0$ cm; the true length is $11.0$ cm. Which kind of error is the main one?**

- Both are equal
- None: the readings are close together
- Random: the readings differ
- Systematic: every reading is too big

* Answer: Systematic: every reading is too big

<!-- Speaker note: All five are over the true value by 1 to 1.4 cm, so the main error is systematic. The 0.4 cm scatter is the smaller, random part. -->
