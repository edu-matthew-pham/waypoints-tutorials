# Break it into solids

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A silo, a drain pipe and a pool with a deep end are each built from simpler solids. Which ones, and how do they fit together?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students break a composite object into cylinders, cones and boxes, decide whether each volume is added or taken away, and say which faces are hidden where solids join (AC9M10M01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the solids a composite object is made of. I can say whether each solid is added or taken away. I can say which faces are hidden where two solids join.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Added or taken away


:::keep-together

### Which solids?

Here is a small seed silo (made up): a cylinder 1 m in radius and 4 m tall, with a cone roof 1.2 m tall. Name the solids first, then decide: add or take away?

1. $V = V_{\text{cylinder}} + V_{\text{cone}}$ — Two solids, joined: add their volumes.
2. $V_{\text{cylinder}} = 3.14 \times 1^2 \times 4 = 12.56$ — Base area times height, in m³.
3. $V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{1}{3} \times 3.14 \times 1^2 \times 1.2 = 1.256$ — A third of the cylinder that fits round the roof.
4. $V = 12.56 + 1.256 = 13.816 \approx 13.8 \text{ m}^3$ — The seed silo holds about 13.8 m³.

Now the big silo: add the two volumes the same way. Does the grain fill the roof too?

**The silo is a cylinder with a cone on top. How do you find the volume of grain it holds?**

☐ Use the cylinder's volume only, because the roof is just a lid

☐ Multiply the two volumes

☐ Add the cylinder's volume and the cone's volume

☐ Take the cone's volume away from the cylinder's volume

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Add the cylinder's volume and the cone's volume

The two solids sit one on top of the other and the grain fills both: add the volumes.

:::


:::keep-together

### Faces that join

**The cone's flat base sits exactly on the cylinder's top circle. Which faces are no longer on the outside of the silo?**

☐ The cylinder's top circle and the cone's base circle

☐ The cylinder's curved wall

☐ The cone's curved face

☐ None of them: every face is still on the outside

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The cylinder's top circle and the cone's base circle

The two circles are pressed together, so both are hidden: neither can be seen or painted.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Dev works out how much grain the silo holds. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The silo is a cylinder (radius 2 m, height 6 m) with a cone roof (radius 2 m, height 1.5 m).
 2  Cylinder: V = 3.14 × 2² × 6 = 75.36 m³.
 3  Cone: V = 3.14 × 2² × 1.5 = 18.84 m³.
 4  Total: 75.36 + 18.84 = 94.2 m³.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: a cone holds a third of the cylinder that fits round it. Dev used the whole cylinder, 3.14 × 2² × 1.5 = 18.8, and forgot the one third.

:::

### A concrete pipe

**A concrete pipe is 2 m long. Its outer radius is 0.5 m and the hole through it has a radius of 0.4 m (made up for the task). What is the volume of concrete? (m³, 2 decimal places, $\pi \approx 3.14$)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $V_{\text{outer}} = 3.14 \times 0.5^2 \times 2 = 1.57$ (the whole cylinder, as if it were solid); $V_{\text{hole}} = 3.14 \times 0.4^2 \times 2 = 1.0048$ (the hole is a cylinder too); $V = 1.57 - 1.0048 = 0.5652$ (taken away, not added)

1.57 − 1.0048 = 0.5652, so about 0.57 m³ of concrete.

:::

## Composite volumes


:::keep-together

### The shed

**A shed is a box $4$ m by $3$ m and $2.5$ m tall, with a roof that is a triangular prism on top (triangle base $4$ m, triangle height $1$ m, $3$ m long). What is its total volume? (m³; made up for the task)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 36

Box 4 × 3 × 2.5 = 30. Roof prism: base area ½ × 4 × 1 = 2, times 3 = 6. Total 36 m³.

:::


:::keep-together

### Added or taken away

☐ A hollow concrete pipe

☐ A silo with a cone roof

☐ Two boxes stacked in a tower

☐ A shed with a prism roof

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A hollow concrete pipe

The pipe is a solid cylinder with the hole taken out of it.
