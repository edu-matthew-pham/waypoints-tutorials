# Irregular shapes

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** How big is a lake? Councils, farmers and park rangers estimate areas like this from maps all the time, with a grid and some careful counting.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students estimate the area of irregular shapes by counting full and part squares on finer and finer grids, and the perimeter with straight pieces round the edge (AC9M8M01, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can estimate an irregular area by counting full squares and half the part squares. I can explain why a finer grid gives a better estimate. I can estimate a curved perimeter and say how precise my answers are.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Why half?


:::keep-together

### Half a square

**Why is each part square counted as half a square?**

☐ So the answer is smaller

☐ Because the grid is half a kilometre

☐ Some are mostly in and some mostly out, so on average about half is inside

☐ Because half of them are inside

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Some are mostly in and some mostly out, so on average about half is inside

The edge cuts each part square somewhere: on average, half of it is inside.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam estimates his lake's area. Click the line where his method goes wrong.**

```
 1  Sam's lake covers 20 squares completely and part of 14 more.
 2  Each square is 1 km by 1 km, so 1 km².
 3  He counts all 34 squares as 1 km² each.
 4  The lake's area is 34 km².
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: a square the edge passes through is only partly inside. Count each as half: 20 + 7 = 27 km².

:::


:::keep-together

☐ Exactly the same

☐ Twice as long

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A little shorter: a straight piece is the shortest way between its two points

Each straight piece cuts the corner of the curve, so the total is a little short; more pieces get closer.

## Estimating


:::keep-together

### Count

**A shape covers 30 full squares and part of 12 more. Each square is 1 m². Estimate its area in m².**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 36

30 + 12 ÷ 2 = 36 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### Half-metre squares

**On a grid of 0.5 m squares, a shape covers 40 full squares and part of 20 more. Estimate its area in m².**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12.5

Each square is 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m². (40 + 10) × 0.25 = 12.5 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### Perimeter

**Which gives the best estimate of a pond's perimeter?**

☐ Counting the squares inside it

☐ 24 short straight pieces round its edge

☐ 4 straight pieces round its edge

☐ Multiplying its length by its width

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 24 short straight pieces round its edge

More, shorter pieces follow the curve more closely.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: composite shapes

**Review: a rectangle 8 m by 5 m has a 2 m by 2 m square cut from one corner. What is its area in m²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 36

8 × 5 − 2 × 2 = 40 − 4 = 36 m².

:::
