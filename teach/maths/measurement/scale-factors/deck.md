## A wall at 1 : 50

$$1 : 100 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{real} = \text{plan} \times 100$$

1) $2.5 \times 100 = 250 \text{ cm}$ — The answer is in the same unit as the plan.
2) $250 \text{ cm} = 2.5 \text{ m}$ — Change to a sensible unit last.
3) $\text{real } 3.6 \text{ m} = 360 \text{ cm} \to 360 \div 100 = 3.6 \text{ cm}$ — Real to plan: divide.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ## A wall at 1 : 50

**A plan is drawn at 1 : 50. A wall is 5 cm long on the plan. How long is the real wall? (m)**

* Answer: $5 \times 50$ (plan to real: multiply by the scale factor); $= 250$ cm (the answer is in the plan's unit, centimetres); $250 \text{ cm} = 2.5$ m (change to metres last)

<!-- Speaker note: 5 × 50 = 250 cm = 2.5 m. -->

## Find the mistake

**Ivy finds the real length of a wall drawn 5 cm long at 1 : 50. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ivy's plan is drawn at a scale of 1 : 50.
 2  A wall measures 5 cm on the plan.
 3  Real length = 5 ÷ 50 = 0.1 cm.
 4  So the real wall is 0.1 cm long.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the real wall is bigger than the plan, so multiply by 50. 5 × 50 = 250 cm, which is 2.5 m. -->

## Which way round

**What does a scale of 1 : 100 mean?**

- 100 cm on the plan is 1 cm in real life
- 1 cm on the plan is 100 cm in real life
- The plan is 100 times bigger than the real thing
- 1 cm in real life is 100 cm on the plan

* Answer: 1 cm on the plan is 100 cm in real life

<!-- Speaker note: The first number is the plan, the second is real life: 1 cm on the plan stands for 100 cm. -->
