# Pythagoras' theorem

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Pythagoras

## Today

**We are learning to** draw a square on each side of a right-angled triangle. The two smaller squares together have exactly the same area as the biggest: a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle.

- I can find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.
- I can explain Pythagoras' theorem with squares on the sides.
- I can check that an answer is sensible (longer than each short side, shorter than both together).

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need squares and square roots (Number Foundations) and right-angled triangles (Classifying triangles). The one idea: the areas of the squares on the two shorter sides add to the area of the square on the hypotenuse. The common mistake is adding the sides (Leah's mistake). The sense check (between the longer short side and the sum) catches most slips. This is the curriculum's Year 8 content; it is placed here, after circles, because it needs Year 7 squares and roots and triangle names. -->

## Why it matters

> Builders check a corner is square with a 3-4-5 triangle: 3 m along one wall, 4 m along the other, and if the diagonal is exactly 5 m, the corner is a right angle. People have used this for over three thousand years.

<!-- Speaker note: In a right-angled triangle, the longest side is opposite the right angle. It is called the **hypotenuse**, $c$. The two sides that meet at the right angle are $a$ and $b$. **Pythagoras' theorem** says that the squares on the two shorter sides add up to the square on the hypotenuse: $$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$ To find the hypotenuse: square the two shorter sides, add, then take the square root. -->

## Square, add, root

Here is the method.

Now try one: sides 12 cm and 16 cm.

$$c \text{ is opposite the right angle}$$

1) $a^2 + b^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$ — Square the two shorter sides and add.
2) $c = \sqrt{100} = 10 \text{ cm}$ — Take the square root.
3) $6 < 10 < 6 + 8$ — Check: longer than each shorter side, shorter than both together.

## The hypotenuse

**Which side of a right-angled triangle is the hypotenuse?**

- The side along the bottom
- The side opposite the right angle, the longest
- Either side at the right angle
- The shortest side

* Answer: The side opposite the right angle, the longest

<!-- Speaker note: The hypotenuse faces the right angle and is always the longest side. -->
