# Pythagoras' theorem

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Builders check a corner is square with a 3-4-5 triangle: 3 m along one wall, 4 m along the other, and if the diagonal is exactly 5 m, the corner is a right angle. People have used this for over three thousand years.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build right-angled triangles with squares on each side, see that the two smaller squares exactly make the biggest, and use a² + b² = c² to find the hypotenuse (AC9M8M06, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle. I can explain Pythagoras' theorem with squares on the sides. I can check that an answer is sensible (longer than each short side, shorter than both together).

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Square, add, root


:::keep-together

### Square, add, root

Here is the method.

1. $c \text{ is opposite the right angle}$ — Find the hypotenuse first.
2. $a^2 + b^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$ — Square the two shorter sides and add.
3. $c = \sqrt{100} = 10 \text{ cm}$ — Take the square root.
4. $6 < 10 < 6 + 8$ — Check: longer than each shorter side, shorter than both together.

Now try one: sides 12 cm and 16 cm.

**A right-angled triangle has sides 12 cm and 16 cm at the right angle. How long is the hypotenuse, in cm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20

12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400, so c = √400 = 20 cm.

:::


:::keep-together

### The hypotenuse

**Which side of a right-angled triangle is the hypotenuse?**

☐ The side along the bottom

☐ The side opposite the right angle, the longest

☐ Either side at the right angle

☐ The shortest side

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The side opposite the right angle, the longest

The hypotenuse faces the right angle and is always the longest side.
