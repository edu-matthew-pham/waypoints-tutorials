# Reading log scales

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A sound meter creeping from 85 to 88 hardly looks like a change. Is it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read a decibel scale as steps that multiply (every 3 dB halves the safe time, so 100 dB allows 15 minutes against 8 hours), then use one ruler of powers of 10 to put a blood cell, a hair and the distance to the Sun side by side, and compare two sizes by counting steps (AC9M10M02, nodes 2 and 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a level on the decibel scale as a number of 3 dB steps. I can explain why a log ruler is used for sizes that run from tiny to huge. I can compare two quantities on a log ruler by counting the steps between them.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Halving, and a ruler for sizes


:::keep-together

### Halving steps

Here's one the other way up. 94 dB is the same as 85 dB plus three steps.

1. $94 - 85 = 9$ — How far above 85 dB.
2. $9 \div 3 = 3$ — Each 3 dB is one step, so 3 steps.
3. $8 \div 2 \div 2 \div 2$ — Each step halves the safe time, so halve 3 times.
4. $8 \div 8 = 1$ — Halving 3 times is dividing by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8: 1 hour.

Now try one on paper: 97 dB. Work out the steps first, then type the hours.

**How many hours is it safe to stay at 97 dB? Use 8 hours at 85 dB, and halve the time for every 3 dB more.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $97 - 85 = 12$ dB more (how far above 85); $12 \div 3 = 4$ steps (each 3 dB is one step); $8 \div 2 \div 2 \div 2 \div 2 = 8 \div 16$ (halve once for each step, four times); $8 \div 16 = 0.5$ hours (half an hour, or 30 minutes)

12 dB is 4 steps: 8 ÷ 16 = 0.5 hours, which is 30 minutes.

:::


:::keep-together

### One ruler for sizes

**The ruler shows a red blood cell, a hair and the distance to the Sun. Why are its marks 10 times apart each time instead of 1 metre apart?**

☐ It's only a style: either ruler shows the same picture

☐ The sizes cover so many powers of 10 that 1 metre apart would squash the cell and the hair together, and the Sun off the page

☐ Because 10 is an easier number than 1

☐ Because the Sun is exactly 10 times bigger than a hair

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The sizes cover so many powers of 10 that 1 metre apart would squash the cell and the hair together, and the Sun off the page

A log ruler gives every power of 10 the same space, so tiny and enormous sizes both fit on one page.

:::

### Find the mistake

**Priya finds the number halfway between 10 m and 1000 m on a ruler like this. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Priya's ruler has marks at 10 m and 1000 m. She finds the number halfway between them.
 2  The ruler has equal steps of ×10 between its marks.
 3  Halfway between 10 and 1000 is the average of the two.
 4  (10 + 1000) ÷ 2 = 505.
 5  So the halfway mark is 505 m.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: on a ruler with equal steps of ×10 the halfway mark is one ×10 step from each end, 100 m, not the average, 505 m.

## Steps and ruler


:::keep-together

### Hours

**How many hours is it safe to stay at 91 dB?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2

91 dB is 2 steps above 85: 8 ÷ 4 = 2 hours.

### The middle

**A ruler has marks at 100 and 10 000, with equal steps of ×10 between them. What number is halfway along?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1000

From 100, one step is 1000 and the next step is 10 000. Halfway is 1000.

:::


:::keep-together

### The gap

**On the sizes ruler a hair is near $10^{-4}$ m and the Earth to the Sun is near $10^{11}$ m. The Sun's distance is about $10^{k}$ times a hair. What's $k$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

From -4 to 11 is 15 steps along the ruler, each ×10, so 10¹⁵.

:::

**Answer:** -6

The point moves 6 places right to reach 8: k = −6.

## Make: back to the concert


**Standing at the concert.** Back to the concert. Work this out on paper first, then type your answer.

**How many times shorter?.** 85 dB(A) averaged over 8 hours is the workplace exposure standard, and every 3 dB(A) increase doubles the risk of hearing damage: [Workplace exposure standards for noise, SafeWork SA](https://safework.sa.gov.au/workers/your-health-and-safety-at-work/hazardous-noise/workplace-exposure-standards). Halving the time for each step is the lesson's reading of "doubles the risk" (the same total risk in half the time). Sizes: a red blood cell 6 to 8 micrometres and a hair 20 to 200 (we use 70), from [Micrometre, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometre). The Earth's average distance from the Sun, 150 million km: [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/).

**Also used in**

- **Earthquakes.** The last lesson: each step up the magnitude scale multiplies the energy by about 32.
- **Computing.** Kilo, mega and giga are each 1000 times the one before, so each is an equal step on a ruler of data sizes.
