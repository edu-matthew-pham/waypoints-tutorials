# Circle problems

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Circles

<!-- Speaker note: Students choose between circumference and area, find a radius from an area, and solve problems with semicircles, quarter circles, rings and tracks (AC9M8M03, node 2). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** choose the formula by what you need: C = 2πr for a length round the edge, A = πr² for the space inside. Work backwards from an area with r = √(A ÷ π). Half and quarter circles, rings and tracks are parts of circles added to or taken from other shapes.

- I can choose the circumference or the area formula for a problem.
- I can find the radius from an area.
- I can solve problems with parts of circles.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the area of a circle (previous lesson) and square roots. The one idea: ask whether the answer is a length (C = 2πr) or a space (A = πr²); to go back from an area, divide by π and take the square root. The common mistake is stopping at r² (Leo's mistake). Answers accept π from the calculator or 3.14; ask students to say which they used. Wider: composite shapes with circles (a stadium, a keyhole), and circles in First Nations Australian weaving designs, where the context is shared respectfully with local knowledge. -->

---

## Why it matters

> A running track, a round garden bed, a pizza deal: is a 30 cm pizza more than twice a 20 cm one? Circle problems need the right formula, sometimes run backwards.

<!-- Speaker note: Ask first: is the answer a **length** round the edge (circumference, $C = 2\pi r$) or the **space** inside (area, $A = \pi r^2$)? To find the radius from an area, undo each step: divide by $\pi$, then take the square root. $$r = \sqrt{A \div \pi}$$ For part of a circle, take the fraction: a semicircle is half the area $\frac{1}{2}\pi r^2$, a quarter circle a quarter. Its perimeter is the curved part plus the straight edges. Say which value of π you used (the π button, or 3.14). -->

## Backwards

Here is the routine.

$$78.5 \div \pi \approx 25$$

1) $r = \sqrt{25} = 5\ \text{cm}$ — Undo the square with a square root.
2) $\pi \times 5^2 \approx 78.5$ — Check by going forwards.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ## Find the mistake

**Leo finds the radius of a circle with area 50 cm². Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A circle has an area of 50 cm².
 2  A = πr², so r = 50 ÷ 3.14.
 3  r = 15.9 cm.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: 50 ÷ 3.14 is r², not r. Take the square root: r = √15.9 ≈ 4.0 cm. -->
