# Prisms and capacity

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A tent, a chocolate bar in a triangular box, a drink carton: how much do they hold? If the shape is the same all the way through, one rule finds them all.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build triangular prisms in layers, find volume as cross-section area × length for any right prism, and link volume to capacity (1 cm³ = 1 mL, 1000 cm³ = 1 L) (AC9M7M02, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the cross-section of a prism. I can find the volume of a triangular prism. I can change between cm³, mL and litres.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Cross-section times length


:::keep-together

### Any prism

Here is the rule for any prism, and capacity.

1. $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12 \text{ cm}^2$ — Find the area of the cross-section (a triangle: halve).
2. $V = 12 \times 10 = 120 \text{ cm}^3$ — Times the length: one layer of 12 for each centimetre.
3. $120 \text{ cm}^3 = 120 \text{ mL}$ — Capacity: 1 cm³ holds 1 mL.
4. $1000 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ L}$ — 1000 mL is a litre: a 10 cm cube holds 1 L.

Now try one: a triangle end with base 6 cm and height 4 cm, 10 cm long.

**A prism's cross-section is a triangle with base 6 cm and height 4 cm. The prism is 10 cm long. What is its volume, in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 120

½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm², × 10 = 120 cm³.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Lena finds how much air is inside a tent. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A tent is a triangular prism 3 m long. Its triangle end has base 2 m and height 1.5 m.
 2  Area of the triangle end = 2 × 1.5 = 3 m²
 3  Volume = 3 × 3
 4  = 9 m³
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the end is a triangle, so halve: ½ × 2 × 1.5 = 1.5 m². Volume = 1.5 × 3 = 4.5 m³.

:::
