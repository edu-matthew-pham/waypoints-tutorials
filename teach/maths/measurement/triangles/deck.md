## Today

**We are learning to** two copies of any triangle, one turned round, make a parallelogram with the same base and height. So a triangle's area is half of base × height: A = ½bh.

- I can explain why a triangle's area is half of base × height.
- I can find the area of any triangle, including one whose height falls outside it.
- I can find a missing base or height from an area.

## Why it matters

> Sails, roof gables and the slices of a pizza cut into wedges are triangles. How much fabric does a triangular sail need?

<!-- Speaker note: A triangle also has a **base** (any side) and a **perpendicular height**: the distance from the opposite corner (the apex) straight down to the base, at a right angle. Two copies of a triangle, one turned round, fit together into a parallelogram with the same base and height. So the triangle is **half** of it: $$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times b \times h$$ -->

## Halve it

Here is the reason, then the routine.

Now try one: base 10 cm, height 7 cm.

$$6 \times 4 = 24$$

1) $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12 \text{ cm}^2$ — One triangle is half of it.
2) $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 7 = 35 \text{ cm}^2$ — Multiply the base by the height, then halve (or halve first: 5 × 7).
