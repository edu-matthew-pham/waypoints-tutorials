> **Key question:** Orienteers don't say north-east. They say 040°. What does the number tell you?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read three-figure bearings, draw the triangle with the bearing's angle at the start, and find the north and east parts of a leg, or the bearing from the parts (AC9M10M03, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a three-figure bearing and say which way it points. I can find the north and east parts of a leg from its bearing and length. I can find a bearing from the north and east parts.

## The triangle at the start


:::keep-together

### Past due east

Here is one that stays in the first quarter, between north and east.

1. $\text{angle from north} = 70°$ — A bearing is measured clockwise from north, so the angle at the start is the bearing, 70°.
2. $\text{north} = 300 \cos 70° = 102.6$ — North is next to the 70° angle, so cosine.
3. $\text{east} = 300 \sin 70° = 281.9$ — East is opposite the 70° angle, so sine.

A walker goes 300 m on a bearing of 070°: about 103 m north and 282 m east.

Now try one on paper that goes past east: 5 km on a bearing of 130°. Sketch the path first, then find the angle from the north–south line.

**A hiker walks 5 km on a bearing of 130°. How far east of the start is she? (1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 130° is past due east (090°), so she's heading south of east. (sketch the compass cross and the path first); The angle from the north–south line is $180° - 130° = 50°$, measured from south. (use the line she's closest to); East is opposite the 50° angle, so east $= 5 \sin 50°$ (sine, because east is across from the angle); $5 \sin 50° = 3.8$ km (work it out)

The angle from south is 180° − 130° = 50°. East is opposite it: 5 × sin 50° = 3.8 km. (The south part is 5 × cos 50° = 3.2 km.)

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mina finds how far east a boat has sailed. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The boat sails 800 m on a bearing of 025°, so it heads a little east of north.
 2  That makes 25° the angle between its path and the east line.
 3  East is next to that angle, so east = 800 × cos 25° = 725 m.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: a bearing is measured from north, so the 25° is between the north line and the path. East is opposite that angle, so east = 800 × sin 25° = 338 m, not 800 × cos 25° = 725 m.

:::

## North, east and bearings


:::keep-together

### North part

**A ship sails $120$ km on a bearing of $030°$. How far north has it gone? (nearest km)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 104

North is next to the 30° angle: 120 × cos 30° = 104 km.

:::


:::keep-together

### The bearing back

**A walker goes $300$ m north and then $400$ m east. What is her bearing from the start? (nearest degree)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 53

East is opposite the bearing's angle and north is next to it: tan t = 400 ÷ 300, so t = 53°. The bearing is 053°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: a helicopter flies from its base to a farm that is $12$ km east and $5$ km north. How far is the flight, in km?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 13

5² + 12² = 169, and √169 = 13 km.

:::
