> **Key question:** Two people stand at different corners of the same triangle. Is a side the 'opposite' for both?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students name the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent sides from a marked angle and see the names swap when the angle moves (AC9M9SP01, node 1).

## Names depend on the angle


:::keep-together

### Hypotenuse, opposite, adjacent

1. $\text{hypotenuse}$ — Across from the right angle: the longest side.
2. $\text{opposite}$ — Across from the marked angle: it does not touch it.
3. $\text{adjacent}$ — Next to the marked angle: it touches it, and is not the hypotenuse.
4. $\text{move the angle}$ — Opposite and adjacent swap. The hypotenuse stays.

**Which side is always the hypotenuse?**

☐ The bottom side

☐ The upright side

☐ The side touching the marked angle

☐ The side across from the right angle (the longest)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The side across from the right angle (the longest)

The hypotenuse is across from the right angle. It is the longest side and does not change with the marked angle.

:::


:::keep-together

### Move the angle

**The marked angle moves to the top corner. The upright side was opposite. What is it now?**

☐ It has no name

☐ The hypotenuse

☐ Adjacent: it touches the new angle

☐ Opposite: it was opposite before

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Adjacent: it touches the new angle

From the top corner, the upright side touches the angle, so it is adjacent.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mo names the sides from the top angle. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The triangle has its right angle at the bottom right.
 2  The marked angle is the one at the top.
 3  The upright side is opposite, because it is upright.
 4  The bottom side is adjacent.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: from the top angle the upright side touches it, so it is adjacent, not opposite. The names follow the angle.

### Hypotenuse

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 13

The longest side, across from the right angle.

### Opposite

**The marked angle is between the 5 cm side and the 13 cm side. Which side is opposite?**

☐ The 13 cm side

☐ The 5 cm side

☐ The 12 cm side

☐ None of them

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The 12 cm side

The 12 cm side doesn't touch the marked angle.

### Review

**Review: a ladder is 5 m long and its foot is 3 m out. How high does it reach? (Pythagoras)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4
