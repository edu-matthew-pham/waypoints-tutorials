Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Trigonometry

## Today

- I can name the hypotenuse.
- I can name the opposite and adjacent sides from a marked angle.

## Why it matters

> Two people stand at different corners of the same triangle. Is a side the 'opposite' for both?

<!-- Speaker note: Every trig problem starts the same way: pick the angle you are working from, then name the three sides. Get the names wrong and everything after it goes wrong. -->

## Hypotenuse, opposite, adjacent

$$\text{hypotenuse}$$

1) $\text{opposite}$ — Across from the marked angle: it does not touch it.
2) $\text{adjacent}$ — Next to the marked angle: it touches it, and is not the hypotenuse.
3) $\text{move the angle}$ — Opposite and adjacent swap. The hypotenuse stays.

## Hypotenuse, opposite, adjacent

**Which side is always the hypotenuse?**

- The bottom side
- The upright side
- The side touching the marked angle
- The side across from the right angle (the longest)

* Answer: The side across from the right angle (the longest)

<!-- Speaker note: The hypotenuse is across from the right angle. It is the longest side and does not change with the marked angle. -->

## Move the angle

**The marked angle moves to the top corner. The upright side was opposite. What is it now?**

- It has no name
- The hypotenuse
- Adjacent: it touches the new angle
- Opposite: it was opposite before

* Answer: Adjacent: it touches the new angle

<!-- Speaker note: From the top corner, the upright side touches the angle, so it is adjacent. -->

## Find the mistake

**Mo names the sides from the top angle. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The triangle has its right angle at the bottom right.
 2  The marked angle is the one at the top.
 3  The upright side is opposite, because it is upright.
 4  The bottom side is adjacent.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: from the top angle the upright side touches it, so it is adjacent, not opposite. The names follow the angle. -->
