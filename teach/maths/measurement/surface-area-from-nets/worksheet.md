# Surface area from nets

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** How big is the paper label on a can of tomatoes? Unroll it and see.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students unfold prisms and cylinders into nets and add the area of every piece (AC9M9M01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can draw or describe the net of a prism or cylinder. I can find the area of each piece, including the curved side. I can add them without missing a face.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**A can has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. The label wraps once all the way round. About how wide is the label when unrolled flat?**

☐ About 19 cm

☐ About 10 cm

☐ About 28 cm

☐ About 6 cm

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** About 19 cm. The label goes once round the can, so its width is the circumference: 2 × π × 3 ≈ 18.8 cm. 6 cm is the diameter, which only goes across.

:::

## Every face


:::keep-together

### The net

1. $\text{curved side} = 2\pi r \times h$ — A rectangle as wide as the circumference.
2. $2 \times 3.14 \times 3 \times 10 = 188.4$ — The label.
3. $\text{two ends} = 2 \times \pi r^2$ — Two circles.
4. $2 \times 3.14 \times 3^2 = 56.5$ — The lid and the base.
5. $\text{total} = 188.4 + 56.5 = 244.9 \text{ cm}^2$ — All three pieces.

**The label is a rectangle 18.8 cm by 10 cm. What is its area? (cm²)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 188

18.8 × 10 = 188 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Add every piece

**Each end is a circle of radius 3 cm. What is the area of one circle? (use $\pi \approx 3.14$; cm², 1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 28.3

3.14 × 3 × 3 = 28.26, about 28.3 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Dee finds the total surface area of the can. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Can: radius 3 cm, height 10 cm.
 2  The curved side is 2 × 3.14 × 3 × 10 = 188.4 cm².
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the can has two circular ends as well as the curved side. She forgot to add them.

## Total surface area


:::keep-together

### Curved side

**A cylinder has radius 2 cm and height 5 cm. What is the area of its curved side? (use $\pi \approx 3.14$; cm², 1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 62.8

2 × 3.14 × 2 × 5 = 62.8.

:::


:::keep-together

### Both ends

**What is the total surface area of that cylinder, with both ends? (cm², 1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 87.9

62.8 + 2 × 3.14 × 4 = 62.8 + 25.1 = 87.9.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: scale factor 3 on a shape with area 4 cm². What is the new area? (cm²)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 36

4 × 3² = 36.
