# Surface area of composite objects

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Composite solids

<!-- Speaker note: Students find the surface area of a composite object by counting only the faces on the outside: joined faces are hidden and a hole adds an inside surface (AC9M10M01, node 2). ## Today

- I can say which faces of a composite object are on the outside.
- I can leave out the faces that are joined.
- I can add the inside surface of a hole.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Students need Break it into solids and Surface area from nets (Year 9). This is a hinge lesson: the folded easier questions let students count the flat circles first. The curved face of a cone, πrl, is given as a fact, like the cone's volume in the last lesson. ## Only what you can see

A small seed silo (made up): a cylinder wall 5 m tall with a radius of 1.5 m, and a cone roof 2 m tall. Its slant length isn't given, so you have to find it first. Pythagoras works on the right-angled triangle inside the cone.

Now look at the big silo and decide which faces are on the outside.

$$l = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 2^2} = 2.5$$

1) $A_{\text{wall}} = 2 \times 3.14 \times 1.5 \times 5 = 47.1$ — Curved wall, 2πrh, in m².
2) $A_{\text{roof}} = 3.14 \times 1.5 \times 2.5 = 11.775$ — Curved roof, πrl.
3) $A = 47.1 + 11.775 = 58.875 \approx 58.9 \text{ m}^2$ — Add the two outside faces. No circles: they are hidden.

## Find the mistake

**Mei works out the paint for the silo. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mei paints the silo: a wall 6 m tall with a radius of 2 m, and a cone roof with a slant length of 2.5 m.
 2  Curved wall: 2 × 3.14 × 2 × 6 = 75.4 m².
 3  Cone roof: 3.14 × 2 × 2.5 = 15.7 m².
 4  The cone and the cylinder join along a circle, so she adds both circles: 2 × 3.14 × 2² = 25.1 m².
 5  Total: 75.4 + 15.7 + 25.1 = 116.2 m².
```

* Answer: line 4

## A pipe, inside and out

**The concrete pipe from last lesson is 2 m long, with an outer radius of 0.5 m and a hole radius of 0.4 m (made up for the task). It is open at both ends. A painter coats every surface: the outside, the inside and the two ring-shaped ends. What is the total area? (m², 1 decimal place, $\pi \approx 3.14$)**

* Answer: $A_{\text{outside}} = 2 \times 3.14 \times 0.5 \times 2 = 6.28$ (the outer curved surface); $A_{\text{inside}} = 2 \times 3.14 \times 0.4 \times 2 = 5.024$ (the hole adds an inside surface); $A_{\text{ends}} = 2 \times (3.14 \times 0.5^2 - 3.14 \times 0.4^2) = 0.5652$ (two rings: the big circle minus the hole); $A = 6.28 + 5.024 + 0.5652 = 11.87$ (add all four surfaces)

<!-- Speaker note: 6.28 + 5.024 + 0.5652 = 11.8692, about 11.9 m². -->

## The stack

**A box $4$ m by $3$ m by $2$ m has a smaller box, $2$ m by $3$ m by $1$ m, standing on its top. The small box's $2$ by $3$ face sits flat on the top, wholly on it. What is the surface area of the whole stack, including the bottom? (m²)**

* Answer: 62

## A hole

**A cube with sides of $4$ cm has a square hole $1$ cm by $1$ cm drilled straight through the middle, from one face to the opposite one. What is its surface area? (cm²)**

* Answer: 110

<!-- Speaker note: A plain cube is 6 × 16 = 96. The hole removes two 1 cm² squares: 94. It adds four inside walls of 4 × 1 = 4 cm² each, which is 16: 110 cm². -->

## Exit ticket

**A box stands on top of a bigger box. Why is the surface area of the stack less than the two boxes' surface areas added together?**

* The face where they touch is hidden on both, so it counts for neither.
