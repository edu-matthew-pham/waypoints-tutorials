# Composite shapes

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** An L-shaped bedroom, a house front with a pointed roof, a photo frame: none is a single rectangle, but each is easy once you cut it up.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students cut composite shapes into rectangles and triangles, or take pieces away from a rectangle, and find the area and the perimeter (AC9M8M01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can cut a composite shape into pieces I know and add their areas. I can find an area by taking pieces away from a big rectangle. I can work out a missing side and the perimeter.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Lengths and pieces


:::keep-together

### Missing lengths

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{an L } 10 \text{ m by } 7 \text{ m, a } 6 \times 4 \text{ corner missing}$ — A different L, as an example.
2. $\text{pieces: } 4 \times 7 \text{ and } 6 \times 3$ — Cut into shapes you know, with each piece's own lengths.
3. $28 + 18 = 46\ \text{m}^2$ — Add the pieces.
4. $10 \times 7 - 6 \times 4 = 70 - 24 = 46\ \text{m}^2$ — Or: the big rectangle, take away the missing corner.
5. $10 + 3 + 6 + 4 + 4 + 7 = 34\ \text{m}$ — The perimeter adds every side, in metres.

Now find the step.

**The L-shape is 8 m along the bottom and 3 m along the top. How long is the step across in the middle?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

The bottom (8 m) = the top (3 m) + the step: 8 − 3 = 5 m.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ella finds the area of the L-shape. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The L-shape is 8 m wide at the bottom and 6 m tall on the left.
 2  Ella cuts it up and down into a piece 3 m wide and a piece 5 m wide.
 3  Left piece: 3 × 6 = 18 m².
 4  Right piece: 5 × 6 = 30 m².
 5  Total: 18 + 30 = 48 m².
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: the right piece is only 3 m tall, not 6 m. 5 × 3 = 15, so the total is 18 + 15 = 33 m².

:::


:::keep-together

## Composite shapes


:::keep-together

### Rectangle and triangle

**A shape is a rectangle 10 cm by 4 cm with a triangle on top (base 10 cm, height 3 cm). What is its area in cm²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 55

10 × 4 + ½ × 10 × 3 = 40 + 15 = 55 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### A frame

**A photo frame is 30 cm by 20 cm on the outside, with a 24 cm by 14 cm hole for the photo. What is the frame's area in cm²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 264

30 × 20 − 24 × 14 = 600 − 336 = 264 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Perimeter shortcut

**An L-shape fits exactly inside an 8 m by 6 m rectangle (one corner cut out). What is its perimeter in metres?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 28

The steps in the L add up to the missing sides of the rectangle, so the perimeter is 8 + 6 + 8 + 6 = 28 m.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: triangles

**Review: what is the area of a triangle with base 6 cm and height 5 cm, in cm²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

½ × 6 × 5 = 15 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Another L

**An L-shape is 10 m wide along the bottom and 7 m tall on the left. Its left part is 4 m wide; its right part is 6 m wide and only 3 m tall. What is its area in m²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 46

4 × 7 + 6 × 3 = 28 + 18 = 46 m².

:::
