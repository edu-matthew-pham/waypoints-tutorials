# Finding a side

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A 5 m ladder leans against a wall with its foot 3 m out. How high up the wall does it reach? Screens, ramps, roofs and ladders all hide a right-angled triangle.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use Pythagoras to find the hypotenuse or a shorter side (subtracting the squares), with non-whole answers to two decimal places and a sense check, in contexts such as ladders, screens and ramps (AC9M8M06, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find a shorter side of a right-angled triangle. I can decide whether to add or subtract the squares. I can solve problems with ladders, screens and ramps, and check my answer makes sense.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Which side of the triangle is the ladder?

**A 5 m ladder leans against a wall. Its foot is 3 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?**

☐ 8 m

☐ 4 m

☐ About 5.83 m

☐ 2 m

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 4 m. The ladder is the hypotenuse: 5² − 3² = 25 − 9 = 16, so the height is 4 m. 5.83 m adds the squares, which would make the wall taller than the ladder.

## Add or subtract?


:::keep-together

### Add for c, subtract for a side

Here are both kinds.

1. $c^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 100,\ c = 10$ — Missing the hypotenuse: add the squares.
2. $b^2 = 10^2 - 6^2 = 100 - 36 = 64,\ b = 8$ — Missing a shorter side: subtract from the hypotenuse's square.
3. $b^2 = 7^2 - 4^2 = 33,\ b = \sqrt{33} \approx 5.74$ — Not a perfect square: between 5 and 6 (25 and 36), about 5.74.
4. $5.74 < 7$ — Check: the hypotenuse must still be the longest.

Now try one: hypotenuse 13 cm, one side 5 cm.

**A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm and one shorter side 5 cm. How long is the other side, in cm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12

13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, √144 = 12 cm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ken finds a shorter side. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm and one shorter side 6 cm. Find the other side.
 2  b² = 10² + 6²
 3  = 100 + 36 = 136
 4  b = √136 ≈ 11.66 cm
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: 10 cm is the hypotenuse, so subtract: b² = 100 − 36 = 64, b = 8 cm. His 11.66 cm is longer than the hypotenuse, which is impossible.

:::


:::keep-together

### A screen

**A rectangular screen is 48 cm wide and 36 cm high. How long is its diagonal? Write your steps.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with sides 48 and 36 (the corners of a rectangle are right angles); $48^2 + 36^2 = 2304 + 1296 = 3600$ (for the hypotenuse, add the squares); $\sqrt{3600} = 60$ cm (take the square root)

48² + 36² = 3600, √3600 = 60 cm.

:::
