# Scientific notation

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The speed of light has nine digits. Scientists write it in a few characters instead.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students write numbers in scientific notation with a number part from 1 to 10, see the decimal point move left or right on a board, and avoid the sign slip on small numbers (AC9M9M02, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can write a big number in scientific notation. I can write a small number in scientific notation with a negative power. I can turn scientific notation back into a decimal.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Light travels at about 300 000 000 metres per second. Which is that in scientific notation?**

☐ $3 \times 10^{9}$

☐ $3 \times 10^{7}$

☐ $3 \times 10^{-8}$

☐ $3 \times 10^{8}$

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 3 × 10⁸. The decimal point moves 8 places from 3 to 300 000 000. There are 8 zeros. A big number has a positive power; 3 × 10⁻⁸ would be 0.00000003.

## Big and small


:::keep-together

### How far

Here's converting both ways.

1. $45\,000$ — A big number.
2. $4.5 \times 10^{4}$ — The point moves 4 places left to sit after the 4: a positive power.
3. $0.00072$ — A small number.
4. $7.2 \times 10^{-4}$ — The point moves 4 places right, to sit after the 7: a negative power.
5. $3.6 \times 10^{5} = 360\,000$ — Back again: move the point 5 places right.

Now try one on paper: $0.000\,038$ in scientific notation. Then type the power.

**Write $45\,000$ as $4.5 \times 10^{k}$. What's $k$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4

The point moves 4 places, from 4.5 to 45 000. So k = 4.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which is scientific notation?

**Which one is in scientific notation?**

☐ $1.2 \times 10^{4}$

☐ $12 \times 10^{3}$

☐ $120 \times 10^{2}$

☐ $0.12 \times 10^{5}$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $1.2 \times 10^{4}$

All four are 12 000. Only 1.2 × 10⁴ has a number from 1 up to 10.
