## What happened?

* 6. The hypotenuse doubled (5 to 10), so every side doubles: 3 becomes 6. Adding 5 to both sides (8) would change the shape.

## Doubling

$$3 \text{ and } 5$$

1) $\times 2 \Rightarrow 6 \text{ and } 10$ — The bigger copy: every side doubles.
2) $3 \div 5 = 0.6$ — The small triangle's ratio.
3) $6 \div 10 = 0.6$ — The big triangle's ratio.
4) $\text{same angle} \Rightarrow \text{same ratio}$ — The angle decides the ratio, not the size.

## Find the mistake

**Ana compares a triangle with its bigger copy. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Small triangle: opposite 3, hypotenuse 5.
 2  Big copy: opposite 6, hypotenuse 10.
 3  Small ratio: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6. Big ratio: 6 ÷ 10 = 0.6.
 4  So the bigger triangle has a bigger ratio.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: both sides doubled, so the ratio stayed the same. A bigger triangle doesn't mean a bigger ratio. -->
