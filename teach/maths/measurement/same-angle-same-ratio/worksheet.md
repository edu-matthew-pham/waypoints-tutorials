# Same angle, same ratio

> **Key question:** A tree and a person cast shadows at the same moment. Why can you find the tree's height from the two shadows?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that scaling a right-angled triangle keeps the ratio of any two sides, so the angle alone sets the ratio (AC9M9SP01, node 2).

## Why the ratio is fixed


:::keep-together

### Doubling

1. $3 \text{ and } 5$ — The small triangle's opposite and hypotenuse.
2. $\times 2 \Rightarrow 6 \text{ and } 10$ — The bigger copy: every side doubles.
3. $3 \div 5 = 0.6$ — The small triangle's ratio.
4. $6 \div 10 = 0.6$ — The big triangle's ratio.
5. $\text{same angle} \Rightarrow \text{same ratio}$ — The angle decides the ratio, not the size.

**A triangle has opposite 3 and hypotenuse 5. What is opposite ÷ hypotenuse?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.6

3 ÷ 5 = 0.6.

:::


:::keep-together

### The bigger copy

**A bigger copy has opposite 6 and hypotenuse 10. What is opposite ÷ hypotenuse?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.6

6 ÷ 10 = 0.6. The same ratio: both sides doubled.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ana compares a triangle with its bigger copy. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Small triangle: opposite 3, hypotenuse 5.
 2  Big copy: opposite 6, hypotenuse 10.
 3  Small ratio: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6. Big ratio: 6 ÷ 10 = 0.6.
 4  So the bigger triangle has a bigger ratio.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: both sides doubled, so the ratio stayed the same. A bigger triangle doesn't mean a bigger ratio.
