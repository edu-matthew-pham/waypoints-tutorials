## The parts


:::keep-together

### Two radii make a diameter

Here are the relationships.

1. $d = 2r$ — The diameter is two radii, end to end through the centre.
2. $r = 5 \Rightarrow d = 2 \times 5 = 10$ — Radius to diameter: double.
3. $d = 120 \Rightarrow r = 120 \div 2 = 60$ — Diameter to radius: halve.
4. $C$ — The circumference is the distance round; the next lessons find it from d.

Now match the parts.

**Match each part of a circle to what it is.**

A. right across, through the centre

B. the distance all the way round

C. a straight line joining two points on the circle

D. from the centre to the edge

**Answer:** radius → D. from the centre to the edge; diameter → A. right across, through the centre; chord → C. a straight line joining two points on the circle; circumference → B. the distance all the way round

The radius is half the diameter; the diameter is the longest chord; the circumference is the perimeter.

**Jay works out how far a chair at the edge of a round table is from its centre. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  A round table is 120 cm across, measured through the centre.
 2  The distance across through the centre is the radius.
 3  So the radius is 120 cm.
 4  A chair at the edge is 120 cm from the centre.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: across through the centre is the diameter. The radius is half of it: 120 ÷ 2 = 60 cm.
