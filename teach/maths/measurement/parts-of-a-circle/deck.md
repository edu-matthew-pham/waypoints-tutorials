# Parts of a circle

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Circles

<!-- Speaker note: Students name the parts of a circle (centre, radius, diameter, chord, circumference), use d = 2r in both directions, and reason about the biggest circle that fits a rectangle (AC9M7M03, node 1). ## Today

**We are learning to** every point of a circle is the same distance from its centre: the radius. The diameter goes right across through the centre, two radii long (d = 2r). A chord joins any two points; the circumference is the distance all the way round.

- I can name the parts of a circle.
- I can find the diameter from the radius, and the radius from the diameter.
- I can explain why the diameter is the longest chord.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students meet these words informally in primary school; this lesson makes them exact. The one idea: the radius is centre to edge; the diameter is two radii right across; d = 2r. The common mistake is mixing them up in context (Jay's mistake). Circumference is named here and found in the next two lessons. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Wheels, plates, clocks and pizza: why does a wheel roll smoothly? Because every point on its edge is the same distance from the axle.

<!-- Speaker note: A **circle** is every point the same distance from one point, its **centre**. That distance is the **radius** ($r$). - The **diameter** ($d$) goes right across through the centre. It is two radii: $d = 2r$, so $r = d \div 2$. - A **chord** is a straight line joining any two points on the circle. The diameter is the longest chord. - The **circumference** ($C$) is the distance all the way round: the circle's perimeter. -->

## Two radii make a diameter

Here are the relationships.

Now match the parts.

$$d = 2r$$

1) $r = 5 \Rightarrow d = 2 \times 5 = 10$ — Radius to diameter: double.
2) $d = 120 \Rightarrow r = 120 \div 2 = 60$ — Diameter to radius: halve.
3) $C$ — The circumference is the distance round; the next lessons find it from d.

## Two radii make a diameter

**Match each part of a circle to what it is.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `radius` | A. right across, through the centre |
| `diameter` | B. the distance all the way round |
| `chord` | C. a straight line joining two points on the circle |
| `circumference` | D. from the centre to the edge |

* Answer: radius → D. from the centre to the edge; diameter → A. right across, through the centre; chord → C. a straight line joining two points on the circle; circumference → B. the distance all the way round

## Find the mistake

**Jay works out how far a chair at the edge of a round table is from its centre. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  A round table is 120 cm across, measured through the centre.
 2  The distance across through the centre is the radius.
 3  So the radius is 120 cm.
 4  A chair at the edge is 120 cm from the centre.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: across through the centre is the diameter. The radius is half of it: 120 ÷ 2 = 60 cm. -->

## Chords

**Is every chord a diameter?**

- Yes: they both go across the circle
- No: only a chord that passes through the centre is a diameter
- No: a chord is always curved

* Answer: No: only a chord that passes through the centre is a diameter

<!-- Speaker note: Every diameter is a chord (the longest one), but most chords miss the centre. -->
