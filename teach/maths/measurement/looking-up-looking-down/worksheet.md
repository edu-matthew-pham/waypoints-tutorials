# Looking up, looking down

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The lifeguard's eyes are 6 m above the sand. The swimmer is somewhere out there. How far?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students draw the triangle for an angle of elevation or depression, see that the two angles at either end of a line of sight are equal, and use tangent or sine to find a distance or an angle (AC9M10M03, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can say which angle is the angle of elevation and which is the angle of depression. I can draw the triangle and put the angle in the right corner. I can name the sides from that angle and find a distance or an angle.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**What happens:** 8°. The lifeguard's horizontal line and the sand are parallel, so the angle down at her end and the angle up at the swimmer's end are alternate angles. They're equal.

## Draw it, then name the sides


:::keep-together

### From the other end

Here is one that looks down from a cliff.

1. $\text{angle at the boat} = 22°$ — The angle of depression from the clifftop is the same size as the angle of elevation from the boat.
2. $\tan 22° = \dfrac{45}{d}$ — The cliff, 45 m, is opposite the angle. The distance along the water is next to it.
3. $d = \dfrac{45}{\tan 22°} = 111.4$ — Divide, because the distance is the bottom of the fraction.

A walker on a 45 m clifftop sees a boat at an angle of depression of 22°. The boat is about 111 m out.

Now try one on paper, looking up this time: a hiker, a 30 m tower and an angle of elevation of 25°. Draw the triangle first.

**A hiker stands on level ground and looks up at the top of a 30 m lookout tower. The angle of elevation is 25°. How far is she from the foot of the tower? (1 decimal place, ignoring her eye height)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The 25° is at the hiker's end. The tower is opposite it and the ground distance is next to it. (draw the triangle and mark the angle first); $\tan 25° = \dfrac{30}{d}$ (opposite over adjacent); $d = \dfrac{30}{\tan 25°}$ (the unknown is the bottom of the fraction, so divide); $d = 64.3$ m (work it out)

tan 25° = 30 ÷ d, so d = 30 ÷ tan 25° = 64.3 m.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Dee finds how far out the swimmer is. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The lifeguard's eyes are 6 m up, and she looks down at the swimmer at 8°.
 2  I'll put the 8° at her eyes, inside the triangle, between the tower and her line of sight.
 3  So the sand is opposite the 8° and the tower is next to it.
 4  tan 8° = d ÷ 6.
 5  d = 6 × tan 8° = 6 × 0.1405 = 0.84 m.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the angle of depression is measured down from the horizontal, not from the tower. The 8° is between the dashed line and the line of sight, so the triangle's angle at the swimmer is 8°, and the tower is opposite it.

:::


:::keep-together

### The line of sight

**How far is the lifeguard's eye from the swimmer, along the line of sight? Use $\sin 8° = 0.1392$. (1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 43.1

The tower, 6 m, is opposite the 8° angle and the line of sight is the hypotenuse, so sin 8° = 6 ÷ line. Line = 6 ÷ 0.1392 = 43.1 m.

## Elevation and depression


:::keep-together

### The boat

**A cliff is $50$ m high. From the top, a boat is at an angle of depression of $20°$. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff? ($\tan 20° = 0.364$; 1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 137.4

The angle at the boat is 20°. tan 20° = 50 ÷ d, so d = 50 ÷ 0.364 = 137.4 m.

:::


:::keep-together

### The ball

**From a window $12$ m up, a ball lies on level ground $30$ m from the wall. What is the angle of depression of the ball from the window? (1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 21.8

The angle at the ball is the same as the angle of depression. tan t = 12 ÷ 30 = 0.4, so t = tan⁻¹(0.4) = 21.8°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: the two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are $9$ and $12$. How long is the hypotenuse?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, and √225 = 15.
