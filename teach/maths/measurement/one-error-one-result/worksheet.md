# One error, one result

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A room is 4.0 m by 3.0 m, to the nearest 10 cm. How much carpet do you order?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that a calculated result carries the errors of every measurement in it, find the lowest and highest a result could be from the lowest and highest of each measurement, and see why the area's error is bigger than a length's (AC9M10M04, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the lowest and highest a result could be from the lowest and highest of each measurement. I can say how far a result could be out. I can explain why an area is out by more than a length.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Why the area is out by more


:::keep-together

### Highest and lowest

1. $l: 3.95 \text{ to } 4.05,\; w: 2.95 \text{ to } 3.05$ — Each reading is out by up to 0.05 m, so write its lowest and highest.
2. $\text{lowest } A = 3.95 \times 2.95 = 11.6525$ — Both at their lowest.
3. $\text{highest } A = 4.05 \times 3.05 = 12.3525$ — Both at their highest.
4. $12.3525 - 12 = 0.3525$ — How far above the measured 12 m² the area could be.

Now try one on paper after the worked example: a courtyard 8.0 m by 5.0 m, each to the nearest 0.1 m. Work out the highest the area could be, then how far above the measured area that is.

**A courtyard is measured as 8.0 m by 5.0 m, each to the nearest 0.1 m. How far above the measured area could the real area be, at most? (m²)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $8.0 + 0.05 = 8.05$ and $5.0 + 0.05 = 5.05$ (the highest each length could be); $8.05 \times 5.05 = 40.6525$ (the highest the area could be); $40.6525 - 40 = 0.6525$ (take away the measured area, $8.0 \times 5.0 = 40$)

8.05 × 5.05 = 40.6525 and 40.6525 − 40 = 0.6525, about 0.65 m²: more than ten times the 0.05 m each length could be out by.

:::


:::keep-together

### Lowest

**The lengths are $4.0$ m and $3.0$ m, each to the nearest $0.1$ m. What is the lowest the area could be? (m², 2 decimal places)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 11.65

3.95 × 2.95 = 11.6525, about 11.65 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### How much bigger

**The measured area is $4.0 \times 3.0 = 12$ m². How much bigger than that could the area be, at most? (m², 2 decimal places)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.35

4.05 × 3.05 = 12.3525, and 12.3525 − 12 = 0.3525, about 0.35 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Noor works out how much carpet to order. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Noor measures a room as 4.0 m by 3.0 m, each to the nearest 0.1 m.
 2  Each length could be out by 0.05 m.
 3  The area is 4.0 × 3.0 = 12.0 m².
 4  So the area is out by 0.05 m², at most 12.05 m².
 5  She orders 12.05 m² of carpet.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: each length can be 0.05 m out, but the area is length times width, and both are out at once. The area could be out by about 0.35 m², not 0.05 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### Why bigger

**The area could be out by about 0.35 m², much more than the 0.05 m each length could be out by. Why?**

☐ Both lengths are out, and each error is multiplied by the other length: about 4 × 0.05 + 3 × 0.05

☐ The tape is worse at measuring areas

☐ The error is always multiplied by 7 for any room

☐ Because the area is in square metres

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Both lengths are out, and each error is multiplied by the other length: about 4 × 0.05 + 3 × 0.05

A strip 4 m long and 0.05 m wide plus a strip 3 m long and 0.05 m wide is about 0.2 + 0.15 = 0.35 m² of extra floor.

## Lowest and highest


:::keep-together

### A rectangle

**A rectangle is measured as $6.0$ m by $2.0$ m, each to the nearest $0.1$ m. What is the highest its area could be? (m², 2 decimal places)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12.4

6.05 × 2.05 = 12.4025, about 12.40 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### A tile

**A square tile is measured as $0.30$ m by $0.30$ m, each to the nearest $0.01$ m. What is the lowest its area could be? (m², 4 decimal places)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.087

Each side could be 0.295 m: 0.295 × 0.295 = 0.087025, about 0.0870 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: the room's area is $12$ m² and could be out by $0.35$ m². What is the percentage error? (%, 1 decimal place)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2.9

0.35 ÷ 12 = 0.0292, × 100 = 2.9%. Each length had a percentage error of about 1.3% and 1.7%: the area's percentage error is close to their sum.
