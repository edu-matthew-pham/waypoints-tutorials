# Volume in layers

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Volume

## Today

**We are learning to** fill a box with unit cubes layer by layer. Each layer has as many cubes as the base has squares, so the volume is the area of the base times the height: V = A × h. Volume is in cubic units, cm³.

- I can explain why the volume of a box is the area of its base times its height.
- I can find the volume of a box in cm³ or m³.
- I can find a missing length from a volume.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the area of a rectangle (Area unit) and multiplying decimals. The one idea: a prism fills up in layers, each a copy of the base, so V = A × h. The common mistake is counting only the outside (Finn's mistake), or adding the faces (a surface area). Cubic units (cm³) are new. -->

---

## Why it matters

> How many sugar cubes fit in a box, how much water fills a fish tank, how much concrete for a path? All are volumes: how much space a solid takes up.

<!-- Speaker note: **Volume** is how much space a solid takes up, counted in unit cubes. A cube 1 cm along each edge is one **cubic centimetre**, 1 cm³. A box (a **rectangular prism**; a **prism** is any solid with the same shape all the way through) fills up in layers. The bottom layer covers the base: a 4 cm by 3 cm base holds $4 \times 3 = 12$ cubes. Every layer above is the same, so the number of layers is the height: $$V = \text{area of the base} \times \text{height} = A \times h$$ -->

## Layers

Here is the idea, then the routine.

Now try one: a 6 by 2 by 3 box.

$$4 \times 3 = 12$$

1) $12 \times 5 = 60 \text{ cm}^3$ — 5 layers of 12.
2) $V = A \times h = l \times w \times h$ — For a box, the base area is length × width.
3) $V = 6 \times 2 \times 3 = 36 \text{ cm}^3$ — Volume is in cubic units: cm³.

## Why it works

**Why is the volume of a box the area of its base times its height?**

- Because the base is the biggest face
- Because volume is always three numbers multiplied
- Each layer has one cube for each square of the base, and there are as many layers as the height

* Answer: Each layer has one cube for each square of the base, and there are as many layers as the height

<!-- Speaker note: Each layer is the base (A cubes), and there are h layers: V = A × h. -->
