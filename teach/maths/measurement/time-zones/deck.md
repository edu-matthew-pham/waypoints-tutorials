## Today

**We are learning to** a city with a bigger UTC offset is ahead; a smaller offset is behind. Find the difference between the two offsets, then add or take it away; the day changes when you cross midnight. A flight's arrival time is the departure time plus the flight, changed to the local time where it lands.

- I can change a time from one time zone to another using UTC offsets.
- I can tell when the day changes.
- I can work out when a flight lands, in local time.

## Why it matters

> You call your cousin in London after school and wake her up at 5 am. A flight from Sydney lands in Los Angeles before it took off. Time zones explain both.

<!-- Speaker note: Each place keeps its own clock, set by its **time zone**. A zone is written as its **offset** from UTC (Coordinated Universal Time). This lesson uses the offsets for most of October: Sydney UTC+11 (daylight saving, from early October to early April; UTC+10 the rest of the year), Perth UTC+8, Tokyo UTC+9, London UTC+1 (until late October) and Los Angeles UTC−7 (until early November). To change a time from one zone to another, find the **difference** between the offsets. A place with a bigger offset is **ahead** (later); a smaller offset is **behind** (earlier). Go by the offsets, not the map: Los Angeles is east of Sydney across the Pacific, but across the International Date Line, so it is 18 hours behind. Add or take away the difference, and change the **day** if you cross midnight. **24-hour time** (21:30, not 9:30 pm) avoids mixing up morning and night. For a flight: add the flight time to the departure time (in the departure zone), then change to the arrival zone. -->

## Perth

Here is the routine.

Now find Perth's time.

$$+11 - (+1) = 10$$

1) $\text{London's offset is smaller}$ — So London is behind Sydney.
2) $09{:}00 - 10\ \text{h} = 23{:}00\ \text{the day before}$ — Take it away, crossing midnight back to Sunday.
