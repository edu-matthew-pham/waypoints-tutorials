# Area problems

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** How much paint for the end wall of a house, with its triangle of roof? Real shapes mix rectangles and triangles, and real measurements mix metres and centimetres.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students solve area problems by choosing the base and perpendicular height, converting to one unit, and splitting composite shapes into rectangles and triangles (adding or subtracting parts), with reasoning they own (AC9M7M01, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can pick the right base and height when a shape has extra lengths. I can change lengths to the same unit before finding an area. I can find the area of a shape made of rectangles and triangles.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Choose, convert, split


:::keep-together

### Three moves

Here are the three moves.

1. $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 4 = 20 \text{ cm}^2$ — Choose: the base and the height at a right angle to it. Ignore the slanted 5 cm side.
2. $250 \text{ cm} = 2.5 \text{ m}, \quad 4 \times 2.5 = 10 \text{ m}^2$ — Convert: both lengths in metres, then multiply.
3. $24 + 9 = 33 \text{ m}^2$ — Split: a rectangle (6 × 4) plus a triangle (½ × 6 × 3).
4. $8 \times 5 - 3 \times 2 = 34 \text{ m}^2$ — Or subtract: a rectangle with a corner missing is the big rectangle minus the corner.

Your working can be short notes. Then choose the reason for the key step.

**The end wall of a house is a rectangle 6 m wide and 4 m high, with a triangle of roof on top: base 6 m, height 3 m. Find the area of the whole wall. Write your steps.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Rectangle: $6 \times 4 = 24$ m² (a rectangle's area is length × width); Triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3 = 9$ m² (a triangle is half of base × height); Wall: $24 + 9 = 33$ m² (the whole is the sum of its parts)

24 + 9 = 33 m².

:::


:::keep-together
