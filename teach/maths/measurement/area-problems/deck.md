# Area problems

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Area

<!-- Speaker note: Students solve area problems by choosing the base and perpendicular height, converting to one unit, and splitting composite shapes into rectangles and triangles (adding or subtracting parts), with reasoning they own (AC9M7M01, node 2). ## Today

**We are learning to** pick the base and the height at a right angle to it, put both lengths in the same unit, and split a shape into rectangles and triangles. Area is in square units: m², cm².

- I can pick the right base and height when a shape has extra lengths.
- I can change lengths to the same unit before finding an area.
- I can find the area of a shape made of rectangles and triangles.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the parallelogram and triangle formulas (previous lessons) and converting cm and m. ## Why it matters

> How much paint for the end wall of a house, with its triangle of roof? Real shapes mix rectangles and triangles, and real measurements mix metres and centimetres.

<!-- Speaker note: Area problems come with extra information. Three moves sort them out: - **Choose the base and its height.** The height is at a right angle to the base; a slanted side is not needed. - **Use one unit.** Change every length to the same unit (100 cm = 1 m) before you multiply. Area comes out in square units: m² or cm². - **Split the shape.** A shape made of rectangles and triangles has area equal to the sum of the parts (or a big shape minus a missing part). -->

## Three moves

Here are the three moves.

Your working can be short notes. Then choose the reason for the key step.

$$\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 4 = 20 \text{ cm}^2$$

1) $250 \text{ cm} = 2.5 \text{ m}, \quad 4 \times 2.5 = 10 \text{ m}^2$ — Convert: both lengths in metres, then multiply.
2) $24 + 9 = 33 \text{ m}^2$ — Split: a rectangle (6 × 4) plus a triangle (½ × 6 × 3).
3) $8 \times 5 - 3 \times 2 = 34 \text{ m}^2$ — Or subtract: a rectangle with a corner missing is the big rectangle minus the corner.

## Three moves

**The end wall of a house is a rectangle 6 m wide and 4 m high, with a triangle of roof on top: base 6 m, height 3 m. Find the area of the whole wall. Write your steps.**

* Answer: Rectangle: $6 \times 4 = 24$ m² (a rectangle's area is length × width); Triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3 = 9$ m² (a triangle is half of base × height); Wall: $24 + 9 = 33$ m² (the whole is the sum of its parts)

- The base 10 cm and the slanted side 5 cm

* Answer: The base 10 cm and the height 4 cm
