# Using a plan

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A plan, a ruler and a new sofa. Will it fit?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read lengths off a scaled plan, convert them with the scale, and use them to decide whether things fit, including how a reading error on the plan is multiplied by the scale (AC9M10M05, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can read a length off a plan and find the real length. I can use real lengths to decide whether something fits. I can say how far out a plan reading could be.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Reading with care


:::keep-together

### Wall left

1. $\text{plan: wall } 8.4 \text{ cm},\; 1 : 50$ — Read the plan with a ruler, then use the scale.
2. $8.4 \times 50 = 420 \text{ cm} = 4.2 \text{ m}$ — Plan to real, then to metres.
3. $4.2 - 0.9 = 3.3 \text{ m}$ — Take away the door gap.
4. $3.3 \ge 2.2 \Rightarrow \text{fits}$ — Compare with what you want to fit.

**The long wall is 8.4 cm on the 1 : 50 plan, and a door gap takes 0.9 m of it. How much wall is left for furniture? (m)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $8.4 \times 50 = 420$ cm $= 4.2$ m (plan to real, then to metres); $4.2 - 0.9$ (take away the door gap); $= 3.3$ (work it out)

8.4 × 50 = 420 cm = 4.2 m, and 4.2 − 0.9 = 3.3 m.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Leo checks whether a 2.2 m sofa fits along the long wall, drawn 8.4 cm at 1 : 50. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Leo's plan is drawn at a scale of 1 : 50.
 2  The long wall measures 8.4 cm on the plan.
 3  Real length = 8.4 × 50 = 420.
 4  So the long wall is 420 m long, plenty of room for a 2.2 m sofa.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: 420 is in centimetres, because the plan was in centimetres. The wall is 4.2 m, which still fits a 2.2 m sofa but not by the amount Leo thinks.

:::


:::keep-together

## Using plans


:::keep-together

### A corridor

**A corridor is $1.9$ cm wide on a $1 : 50$ plan. You want to carry a $1.0$ m wide bed frame through it. Does it fit?**

☐ Only just: the corridor is 1.0 m wide

☐ Yes: the corridor is 1.9 m wide

☐ No: the corridor is 0.95 m wide

☐ Yes: the corridor is 95 m wide

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: the corridor is 0.95 m wide

1.9 × 50 = 95 cm = 0.95 m, narrower than the 1.0 m bed frame.

:::


:::keep-together

### A table

**A table is $3.5$ m long. At $1 : 50$, how long is it drawn on the plan? (cm)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

3.5 m = 350 cm, and 350 ÷ 50 = 7 cm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: at $1 : 50$ a room is $6$ cm by $4$ cm on the plan. What is its real area? (m²)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

The real room is 3 m by 2 m, so 6 m².
