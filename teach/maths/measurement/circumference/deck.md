## Today

**We are learning to** because C ÷ d = π, the circumference is π × diameter: C = πd, or C = 2πr from the radius. Going backwards, d = C ÷ π.

- I can find a circle's circumference from its diameter or radius.
- I can find a diameter or radius from a circumference.
- I can give an exact answer in terms of π.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need π as C ÷ d (previous lesson), d = 2r, and dividing decimals (a calculator is fine). The one idea: C = πd, because that is what π means. The common mistake is using the radius in place of the diameter (Mei's mistake, a spiral of Parts of a circle). Rearranging to d = C ÷ π uses the inverse-operations idea from Algebra. -->

## Why it matters

> A bike wheel 70 cm across rolls forward one circumference with every turn. How many turns to school? A trundle wheel measures a sports field the same way.

<!-- Speaker note: The last lesson found that $C \div d = \pi$ for every circle. Turned round, that is a formula for the circumference: $$C = \pi d \qquad \text{or, since } d = 2r, \qquad C = 2\pi r$$ Use $\pi \approx 3.14$, as the questions say. Going backwards, from a circumference to the diameter, divide by π: $d = C \div \pi$. -->

## Use the diameter

Here is the formula forwards and backwards.

Now try one: diameter 8 cm.

$$C = \pi d = 3.14 \times 8 = 25.12 \text{ cm}$$

1) $C = 2\pi r = 2 \times 3.14 \times 30 = 188.4 \text{ cm}$ — From the radius: double it first (2r is the diameter).
2) $d = C \div \pi = 18.84 \div 3.14 = 6 \text{ m}$ — Backwards: divide by π.
3) $C = 10\pi \text{ cm}$ — Exact: leave π in the answer (for d = 10). 10π means 10 × π, about 31.4.

## Backwards

**A circular garden bed has circumference 18.84 m. Using π ≈ 3.14, find its diameter. Write your steps.**

* Answer: $C = \pi d$, so $18.84 = 3.14 \times d$ (the circumference is π times the diameter); $d = 18.84 \div 3.14 = 6$ m (divide by π to undo multiplying by π)
