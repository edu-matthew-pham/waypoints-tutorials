## Compare surface areas


:::keep-together

### Same volume, different shapes

1. $V = \pi r^2 h = 375$ — The volume is fixed.
2. $h = 375 \div (\pi r^2)$ — Height from the radius.
3. $r = 3 \Rightarrow h = 13.3, \; SA = 306$ — A tall can.
4. $r = 4 \Rightarrow h = 7.5, \; SA = 289$ — A medium can.
5. $r = 5 \Rightarrow h = 4.8, \; SA = 307$ — A wide can.
6. $\text{least metal near } r \approx 3.9, \; h \approx 7.8$ — Height about the width.

**A can of radius 4 cm and height 7.5 cm has total surface area $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$. What is it? (cm², nearest whole number, $\pi \approx 3.14$)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 289

2 × 3.14 × 16 + 2 × 3.14 × 4 × 7.5 = 100.5 + 188.4 = 288.9, about 289 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Which can

**A can with radius 3 cm and height 13.3 cm has surface area 306 cm². A can with radius 4 cm and height 7.5 cm has 289 cm². Both hold 375 mL. Which uses less metal?**

☐ They use the same

☐ The radius 3 cm can

☐ The radius 4 cm can

☐ Can't tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The radius 4 cm can

Less surface area means less metal: 289 &lt; 306.

:::


:::keep-together


Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: surface area is in cm², not cm³. Cubic units are for volume.
