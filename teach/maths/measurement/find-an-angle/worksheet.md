# Find an angle

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A ramp rises 1 m over 14 m of ground. How steep is that, in degrees?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use tan⁻¹, sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹ to find an unknown angle from two sides (AC9M9M03, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can pick the ratio from the two sides I know. I can use the inverse key to find the angle. I can check the angle makes sense.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The inverse ratio


:::keep-together

### Ratio to angle

1. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{14}$ — Rise is opposite, run is adjacent.
2. $\tan\theta = 0.0714$ — Divide.
3. $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.0714)$ — Use the inverse key.
4. $\theta \approx 4.1°$ — The ramp is about 4°.

**The ramp has opposite $= 1$ and adjacent $= 14$. What is the ratio $1 \div 14$ to 3 decimal places?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.071

1 ÷ 14 = 0.0714.

:::


:::keep-together

### The inverse key

**Use $\tan^{-1}$ on $0.5$ (opposite $=$ 1, adjacent $= 2$). What is the angle, to the nearest degree?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 27

tan⁻¹ 0.5 = 26.6°, about 27°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam finds the angle of the ramp. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The ramp rises 1 m over a run of 14 m. I want the angle.
 2  tan(angle) = 1 ÷ 14 = 0.071.
 3  So the angle = tan 0.071 = 0.07°.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: tan 0.071 would be an angle's tangent, not an angle. Use tan⁻¹ 0.071 to turn the ratio into an angle.
