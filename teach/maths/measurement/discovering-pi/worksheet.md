# Discovering π

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A bike wheel, a coin and the Earth's equator: wildly different sizes, but for each one the distance round is the same number of times the distance across. People have known this for four thousand years.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students roll circles of different sizes out flat, divide the circumference by the diameter each time, and discover the constant π ≈ 3.14, with its approximations and a quick estimate C ≈ 3d (AC9M7M03, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what π means: how many diameters fit round a circle. I can estimate a circumference from a diameter. I can say why 3.14 and 22/7 are close to π but not exact.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The same number every time


:::keep-together

### What π means

Here is what π is, and what it is not.

1. $\pi = C \div d$ — π is how many diameters fit round the circumference.
2. $\pi = 3.14159265\ldots$ — Its digits never end and never settle into a repeating pattern.
3. $\pi \approx 3.14 \quad \text{or} \quad \pi \approx \tfrac{22}{7}$ — Two handy approximations (22/7 = 3.142…).
4. $C \approx 3 \times d$ — A quick estimate: a bit more than 3 diameters.

Now: what does the table show?

**What does the table show about C ÷ d?**

☐ It is about 3.14 for every circle

☐ It gets bigger for bigger circles

☐ It changes every time

☐ It is 2, because the diameter is two radii

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is about 3.14 for every circle

C ÷ d is the same number for every circle: π ≈ 3.14.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Kai measures a plate to find π. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  I measured a plate: circumference 78.5 cm, diameter 25 cm.
 2  78.5 ÷ 25 = 3.14
 3  So π is exactly 3.14.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: a measurement gives π only to a few places. π = 3.14159… has digits that never end or settle into a pattern; 3.14 is an approximation.

:::
