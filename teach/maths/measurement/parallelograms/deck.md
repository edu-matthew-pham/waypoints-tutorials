Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Area

## Today

**We are learning to** cut a triangle off one end of a parallelogram and move it to the other end: it becomes a rectangle with the same base and height. So the area of a parallelogram is base × perpendicular height.

- I can explain why a parallelogram has the same area as a rectangle with the same base and height.
- I can find the area of a parallelogram with base × perpendicular height.
- I can tell the perpendicular height from a slanted side.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need area of rectangles by counting rows of squares (Year 6) and the meaning of cm². ## Why it matters

> Tiles, bricks in a wall, and the stripes of a pedestrian crossing seen from an angle are parallelograms. How much area does a slanted tile cover?

<!-- Speaker note: The **area** of a shape is how many unit squares it covers. A rectangle 6 cm long and 4 cm high has 4 rows of 6 squares: $6 \times 4 = 24$ square centimetres, written 24 cm². A **parallelogram** has two pairs of parallel sides, like a rectangle pushed over. Its **base** is one side, and its **perpendicular height** is the distance straight up from the base to the opposite side, at a right angle (marked ∟). The slanted side is longer than the height. -->

## Cut, move, multiply

Here is why the formula works, then how to use it.

Now try one: base 8 cm, height 5 cm.

$$A = 6 \times 4 = 24 \text{ cm}^2$$

1) $\text{parallelogram} \to \text{rectangle}$ — Cut along the height and move the triangle: the same base and height.
2) $A = b \times h$ — So a parallelogram's area is base × perpendicular height.
3) $A = 8 \times 5 = 40 \text{ cm}^2$ — Use the height at a right angle to the base, never the slanted side.

## Why it works

**Why does a parallelogram have the same area as a rectangle with the same base and height?**

- The triangle cut off one end fits exactly into the gap at the other end
- Both shapes have four sides
- Its slanted sides are the same length as the rectangle's sides

* Answer: The triangle cut off one end fits exactly into the gap at the other end
