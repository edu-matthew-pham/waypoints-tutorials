# Area of parallelograms

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Tiles, bricks in a wall, and the stripes of a pedestrian crossing seen from an angle are parallelograms. How much area does a slanted tile cover?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students cut a parallelogram along its height and move the triangle to make a rectangle, see that sliding the top keeps the area, and use A = bh with the perpendicular height (AC9M7M01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why a parallelogram has the same area as a rectangle with the same base and height. I can find the area of a parallelogram with base × perpendicular height. I can tell the perpendicular height from a slanted side.

## Base times height


:::keep-together

### Cut, move, multiply

Here is why the formula works, then how to use it.

1. $A = 6 \times 4 = 24 \text{ cm}^2$ — A rectangle: 4 rows of 6 unit squares.
2. $\text{parallelogram} \to \text{rectangle}$ — Cut along the height and move the triangle: the same base and height.
3. $A = b \times h$ — So a parallelogram's area is base × perpendicular height.
4. $A = 8 \times 5 = 40 \text{ cm}^2$ — Use the height at a right angle to the base, never the slanted side.

Now try one: base 8 cm, height 5 cm.

**A parallelogram has base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. What is its area, in cm²?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40

8 × 5 = 40 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: 5 cm is the slanted side, not the height. The height is the perpendicular distance, 4 cm: 7 × 4 = 28 cm².

:::keep-together

### Why it works

**Why does a parallelogram have the same area as a rectangle with the same base and height?**

☐ The triangle cut off one end fits exactly into the gap at the other end

☐ Both shapes have four sides

☐ Its slanted sides are the same length as the rectangle's sides

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The triangle cut off one end fits exactly into the gap at the other end

Cut along the height and move the triangle: the parallelogram becomes the rectangle, with nothing added or lost.
