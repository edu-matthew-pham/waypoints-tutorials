# Calculating in scientific notation

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Light takes about 8 minutes to reach us from the Sun. How far is that, without a calculator?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students multiply and divide numbers in scientific notation using the index laws, fix the number part when it reaches 10 or more, and use the speed of light and a time to find the distance to the Sun (AC9M9M02, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can multiply and divide numbers in scientific notation. I can fix the number part so it's from 1 to 10. I can use scientific notation to find a distance from a speed and a time.

**Light travels at $3 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. It takes about 500 seconds ($5 \times 10^{2}$) to reach us from the Sun. How far is the Sun?**

☐ $1.5 \times 10^{12}$ m

☐ $8 \times 10^{10}$ m

☐ $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m

☐ $1.5 \times 10^{10}$ m

My prediction, and why:

**What happens:** 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. Distance = speed × time = (3 × 10⁸) × (5 × 10²) = 15 × 10¹⁰ = 1.5 × 10¹¹ m: 150 million kilometres.

## Number parts, then powers


:::keep-together

### Multiply

Here's the whole routine, with a number part that grows.

1. $(3 \times 10^{8}) \times (5 \times 10^{2})$ — Speed times time.
2. $3 \times 5 = 15$ — Multiply the number parts.
3. $10^{8} \times 10^{2} = 10^{10}$ — Add the powers.
4. $15 \times 10^{10}$ — Too big: the number part is 10 or more.
5. $1.5 \times 10 \times 10^{10}$ — 15 = 1.5 × 10.
6. $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ — Add the extra 10 to the power.

Now try one on paper: $(2.5 \times 10^{4}) \times (4 \times 10^{3})$. Then type the power in your answer.

**Work out $(2 \times 10^{3}) \times (3 \times 10^{4})$ as $6 \times 10^{k}$. What's $k$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

2 × 3 = 6 and 3 + 4 = 7: 6 × 10⁷.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia works out $(4 \times 10^{3}) \times (5 \times 10^{4})$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Work out (4 × 10³) × (5 × 10⁴).
 2  Multiply the numbers: 4 × 5 = 20.
 3  Add the powers: 3 + 4 = 7.
 4  So the answer is 20 × 10⁷.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: 20 × 10⁷ isn't in scientific notation, because 20 is more than 10. 20 = 2 × 10, so it's 2 × 10⁸.

:::


:::keep-together

### Divide

**What is $(6 \times 10^{5}) \div (2 \times 10^{2})$?**

☐ $3 \times 10^{7}$

☐ $3 \times 10^{2.5}$

☐ $3 \times 10^{3}$

☐ $4 \times 10^{3}$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $3 \times 10^{3}$

6 ÷ 2 = 3 and 10⁵ ÷ 10² = 10³.

## Calculate


:::keep-together

### Multiply

**Work out $(2 \times 10^{5}) \times (3 \times 10^{2})$ as $6 \times 10^{k}$. What's $k$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

2 × 3 = 6 and 5 + 2 = 7.

:::


:::keep-together

### A bigger number part

**Work out $(9 \times 10^{6}) \times (6 \times 10^{3})$ as $5.4 \times 10^{k}$. What's $k$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10

9 × 6 = 54 and 6 + 3 = 9: 54 × 10⁹ = 5.4 × 10¹⁰.

:::


:::keep-together

### Divide

**Work out $(6 \times 10^{-2}) \div (3 \times 10^{4})$ as $2 \times 10^{k}$. What's $k$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -6

6 ÷ 3 = 2 and −2 − 4 = −6.

:::
