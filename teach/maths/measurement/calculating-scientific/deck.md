## Multiply

Here's the whole routine, with a number part that grows.

Now try one on paper: $(2.5 \times 10^{4}) \times (4 \times 10^{3})$. Then type the power in your answer.

$$(3 \times 10^{8}) \times (5 \times 10^{2})$$

1) $3 \times 5 = 15$ — Multiply the number parts.
2) $10^{8} \times 10^{2} = 10^{10}$ — Add the powers.
3) $15 \times 10^{10}$ — Too big: the number part is 10 or more.
4) $1.5 \times 10 \times 10^{10}$ — 15 = 1.5 × 10.
5) $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ — Add the extra 10 to the power.

## Find the mistake

**Mia works out $(4 \times 10^{3}) \times (5 \times 10^{4})$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Work out (4 × 10³) × (5 × 10⁴).
 2  Multiply the numbers: 4 × 5 = 20.
 3  Add the powers: 3 + 4 = 7.
 4  So the answer is 20 × 10⁷.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: 20 × 10⁷ isn't in scientific notation, because 20 is more than 10. 20 = 2 × 10, so it's 2 × 10⁸. -->
