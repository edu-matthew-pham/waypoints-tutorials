# Volume and capacity

Area, volume, circles and Pythagoras · Volume

<!-- Speaker note: Students build boxes and tanks, read their volume and capacity together, and convert between cm³, mL, L and m³ (AC9M8M02, node 1). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 8 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** volume measures space in cubic units; capacity measures how much a container holds. 1 cm³ holds 1 mL, 1000 cm³ holds 1 L, and 1 m³ holds 1000 L. A cubic metre is 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm³, not 100.

- I can convert between cm³, mL, L and m³.
- I can explain why 1 m³ is 1 000 000 cm³.
- I can choose a sensible unit for a container.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Bring a 1 L carton and a 10 cm cube (or a drawing) if you can. The one idea: 1 cm³ holds 1 mL, so 1000 cm³ is 1 L, and 1 m³ (100 × 100 × 100 cm³) is 1000 L. The common mistake is converting a cubic unit with the length factor once (Ravi's mistake). The board gives capacity in mL or L for cm and in L for m. Wider: why a cubic metre of water has a mass of about a tonne, and water storage in kilolitres or megalitres (dams, town water). -->

---

## Why it matters

> A 1 L milk carton and a 10 cm cube hold exactly the same amount. A cubic metre of water is a tonne. Why do the units fit together so neatly?

<!-- Speaker note: **Volume** is the space a solid takes up, in cubic units (cm³, m³). **Capacity** is how much a container holds, in millilitres (mL), litres (L) or kilolitres (kL). They link exactly: | Volume | Capacity | |---|---| | 1 cm³ | 1 mL | | 1000 cm³ (a 10 cm cube) | 1 L | | 1 m³ | 1000 L = 1 kL | A cubic metre is 100 cm × 100 cm × 100 cm = **1 000 000 cm³**: a length conversion is used three times, once for each direction. -->

## Find the mistake

**Ravi works out how much a 2 m³ tank holds. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A water tank holds 2 m³.
 2  1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m³ = 100 cm³.
 3  So the tank holds 2 × 100 = 200 cm³.
 4  That is 200 mL.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: a cubic metre is 100 cm in each of three directions: 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm³. The tank holds 2000 L. -->
