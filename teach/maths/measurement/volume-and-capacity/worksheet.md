# Volume and capacity

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** How much water does a box hold, and how do you get it from the box's size? A cubic metre of water weighs about a tonne, but how do litres and cubic centimetres fit together?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build boxes and tanks, read their volume and capacity together, and convert between cm³, mL, L and m³ (AC9M8M02, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can convert between cm³, mL, L and m³. I can explain why 1 m³ is 1 000 000 cm³. I can choose a sensible unit for a container.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Converting units


:::keep-together

### Cubic metres

**How many litres does 1 m³ hold?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1000

1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm³ = 1 000 000 mL = 1000 L.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ravi works out how much a 2 m³ tank holds. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A water tank holds 2 m³.
 2  1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m³ = 100 cm³.
 3  So the tank holds 2 × 100 = 200 cm³.
 4  That is 200 mL.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: a cubic metre is 100 cm in each of three directions: 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm³. The tank holds 2000 L.

:::


:::keep-together

## Volume and capacity


:::keep-together

### mL to L

**Write 4500 mL in litres.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4.5

4500 ÷ 1000 = 4.5 L.

:::


:::keep-together

### m³ to L

**A container holds 0.6 m³. How many litres is that?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 600

0.6 × 1000 = 600 L.

:::


:::keep-together

### A box

**A box is 20 cm by 15 cm by 10 cm. How many litres does it hold?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

20 × 15 × 10 = 3000 cm³ = 3000 mL = 3 L.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: triangular prisms

**Review: a triangular prism has a right-triangle end with legs 3 cm and 4 cm, and is 10 cm long. What is its volume in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

Base area ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 cm², times 10 = 60 cm³.

:::

## Make: a tank for the roof, on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. The roof, the storm and the tank are made up for the task. Write the rain and the tank in litres, then type the overflow.

**Also used in**

- **Rainwater tanks.** The Bureau of Meteorology says 1 mm of rain on 1 m² gives about 1 L, an approximation ([Bureau of Meteorology](https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/how/faq-data.shtml)). Real tanks collect less, because some water is lost.
- **Pool dosing.** The label is per 10 000 L, so the pool's volume in m³ is changed to litres first.
- **Cartons.** A 1 L carton holds 1000 cm³, about a 10 cm cube.
