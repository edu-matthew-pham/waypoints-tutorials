## Divide the powers

Here's a conversion the other way, from micrograms to milligrams.

Now try one on paper: a tablet is 0.5 mg. How many micrograms is that? Write it as $5 \times 10^{k}$ µg and type $k$.

$$250 \text{ µg}$$

1) $2.5 \times 10^{2} \text{ µg}$ — In scientific notation.
2) $1 \text{ µg} = 10^{-3} \text{ mg}$ — A microgram is a thousandth of a milligram.
3) $2.5 \times 10^{2} \times 10^{-3}$ — Multiply by the power of 10.
4) $2.5 \times 10^{-1} \text{ mg} = 0.25 \text{ mg}$ — Add the powers: 2 + (−3) = −1.

## Divide the powers

**1 mg is $10^{-3}$ g and 1 µg is $10^{-6}$ g. So 1 mg = $10^{k}$ µg. What's $k$?**

* Answer: 3

<!-- Speaker note: 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻⁶ = 10^(−3 − (−6)) = 10³: a milligram is 1000 micrograms. -->

## Find the mistake

**Dev converts 0.25 mg to micrograms. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Convert 0.25 mg to micrograms.
 2  1 mg = 10⁻³ g and 1 µg = 10⁻⁶ g.
 3  So 1 mg = 10⁻³ × 10⁻⁶ µg = 10⁻⁹ µg.
 4  So 0.25 mg = 0.25 × 10⁻⁹ µg.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: to find how many micrograms are in a milligram, divide: 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻⁶ = 10³. So 0.25 mg = 0.25 × 10³ = 250 µg. -->
