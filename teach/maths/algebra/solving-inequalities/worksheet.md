# Solving inequalities

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A fair has an entry fee and a price for each ride, and your money is fixed. How do you work out the most rides you can go on?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students solve 12 + 4.5r ≤ 40 for a day at the fair, read r ≤ 6.2 as six whole rides, reverse the sign when dividing by a negative, solve with the unknown on both sides and solve a double inequality; they finish by comparing two parks (AC9M10A02, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can solve a linear inequality from a budget and show it on a number line. I can reverse the sign when I divide by a negative, and say why. I can read the answer in the context: whole rides, not 6.2.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Solve, then read it


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Lena solves $40 - 4.5r \ge 12$, the money left after the rides. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Solve 40 − 4.5r ≥ 12.
 2  Take 40 from both sides: −4.5r ≥ −28.
 3  Divide both sides by −4.5: r ≥ 6.2.
 4  Check r = 0: 40 − 0 = 40 ≥ 12 is true, but 0 ≥ 6.2 is false.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: dividing by −4.5 reverses the sign, so r ≤ 6.2. Her check on line 4 is the clue: r = 0 is allowed, and 0 ≥ 6.2 is false.

:::


:::keep-together

### Read the answer

**$12 + 4.5r \le 40$ gives $r \le 6.2$. How many rides can you go on?**

☐ 6

☐ 6.2

☐ 7

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

r ≤ 6.2 includes 6 but not 7, and rides are whole numbers, so 6 is the most. The solution of the inequality is a range, and the context picks the values that make sense.

## Solve and read

### In a context

**A skate park charges $8 entry and $2.50 a game. You have $30. What is the most whole games?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

8 + 2.5g ≤ 30, so 2.5g ≤ 22 and g ≤ 8.8. The most whole games is 8.

:::


:::keep-together

### Reverse or not

**Solve $5 - 2x \ge 11$.**

☐ $x \le 3$

☐ $x \le -3$

☐ $x \ge 3$

☐ $x \ge -3$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $x \le -3$

−2x ≥ 6, then divide by −2 and reverse: x ≤ −3. Check x = −4: 5 + 8 = 13 ≥ 11. ✓

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: solve $x^2 + x = 20$. Give the positive solution.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4

x² + x − 20 = 0, so (x + 5)(x − 4) = 0: x = −5 or x = 4. The positive one is 4.

:::
