# The balance method

Algebra Foundations · Equations

<!-- Speaker note: Students solve linear equations by doing the same to both sides (the balance method), including x on both sides, and verify every answer in the first equation (AC9M7A03, node 3). About 50 minutes (Predict and Run 10 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 12 min · Practise 10 min · Make 6 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** do the same to both sides to get x on its own, then check your answer in the first equation.

- I can solve an equation by doing the same thing to both sides, and show each step.
- I can solve an equation with x on both sides.
- I can check my answer by putting it back into the first equation, every time.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: This is a **hinge** lesson: later modelling work (ratios, rates, money problems) depends on fluent solving *and* the checking habit. A student who skips the check will not notice a wrong answer. Do the "x on both sides" scale yourself. The key move is taking a **bag of x** from both sides, which students rarely think of until they see the scale. Teacher: Leo's mistake (one side only) is the misconception this lesson exists to fix. Ask: what happened to the right side? -->

---

## Why it matters

> Engineers, nurses working out doses and anyone splitting a bill rearrange equations this way: keep it balanced, and every step stays true.

<!-- Speaker note: Backtracking stops working when $x$ is on **both** sides, as in $x + 7 = 3x + 1$. The **balance method** works for every linear equation: whatever you do to one side, do to the other, so the scale stays level. -->

## In order

Each line keeps the equation balanced.

$$3x + 5 = 20$$

1) $3x + 5 - 5 = 20 - 5$ — Take 5 from **both** sides.
2) $3x = 15$ — Now three lots of $x$ are 15.
3) $\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{15}{3}$ — Divide **both** sides by 3.
4) $x = 5$ — $x$ is on its own.
5) $3(5) + 5 = 20 \checkmark$ — Check in the **first** equation, not the last line.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ```

## Which step first?

**To solve $3x + 5 = 20$, which first step keeps whole numbers?**

- Divide both sides by 3
- Take 5 from both sides

* Answer: Take 5 from both sides

<!-- Speaker note: Both are allowed, but dividing first gives x + 5/3 = 20/3. Taking 5 first gives 3x = 15, then x = 5. -->

## Harder scales



- **Bigger numbers:** Each task loads a new scale. Same moves, bigger numbers.
- **Bags on both sides:** Now there are bags of $x$ on both sides. Backtracking cannot solve this; the balance method can.
- **Bags and blocks on both sides:** Bags and blocks on both sides. Get the bags on one side first.

## Exit ticket

**Solve 4x + 3 = 2x + 11. Show each step and check your answer in the first equation.**

* Take 2x from both sides: 2x + 3 = 11. Take 3: 2x = 8. Divide by 2: x = 4. Check: 4 × 4 + 3 = 19 and 2 × 4 + 11 = 19.
