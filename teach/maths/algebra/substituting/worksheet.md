# Substituting into formulas

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A formula is a recipe for a number. Put in the values you know, and it gives you the one you want: a perimeter, a cost, a speed.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students substitute values into formulas, writing in the hidden × signs and using the order of operations, including decimals, division and brackets (AC9M7A01, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can replace each letter in a formula with its value, writing in the × signs. I can work out the result in the right order, including brackets and division. I can show my substitution as a line of working, with units.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Three steps


:::keep-together

### Area

Here is how to substitute, step by step.

1. $P = 2l + 2w$ — Write the formula first.
2. $l = 5, \; w = 3$ — Write down the values you know.
3. $P = 2 \times 5 + 2 \times 3$ — Replace each letter with its value. Write in the × signs that were hidden in $2l$ and $2w$.
4. $P = 10 + 6$ — Multiply before adding.
5. $P = 16 \text{ cm}$ — Add, and give the units.

**The area of a rectangle is $A = lw$. Find $A$ when $l = 8$ and $w = 3$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 24

A = 8 × 3 = 24.

:::


:::keep-together

### A plumber

**A plumber charges $C = 5h + 20$ dollars. Find $C$ when $h = 3$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 35

C = 5 × 3 + 20 = 15 + 20 = 35 dollars.

:::

## Substitute


:::keep-together

### Tickets

**Find $T = 12n + 6$ when $n = 4$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 54

T = 12 × 4 + 6 = 48 + 6 = 54.

:::


:::keep-together

### Brackets again

**Find $y = 3(x - 2)$ when $x = 7$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

y = 3 × (7 − 2) = 3 × 5 = 15.

:::


:::keep-together

### A triangle

**Find $A = \frac{bh}{2}$ (the area of a triangle) when $b = 6$ and $h = 5$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

A = 6 × 5 ÷ 2 = 30 ÷ 2 = 15.

:::


:::keep-together

**Review: in $V = 3t + 10$, which are the constants?**

☐ Only 10

☐ 3 and 10

☐ $V$ and $t$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3 and 10

3 and 10 stay the same; V and t are variables that change.

## Make: °F to °C, on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. Write the formula, the line with 86 in place of $F$, then the arithmetic one step at a time, and type the temperature.

**Also used in**

- **Weather abroad.** US forecasts and ovens use °F. The conversion formula turns them into °C, once you put the value in.
- **Ladders and scoreboards.** A rule like points $= 3W + D$ gives every team's points when you put in their wins and draws.
- **Checking answers.** Every equation you solve later is checked by substituting the answer back in.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
