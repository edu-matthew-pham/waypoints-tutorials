# Substituting into formulas

Algebra Foundations · Expressions

<!-- Speaker note: Students substitute values into formulas, writing in the hidden × signs and using the order of operations, including decimals, division and brackets (AC9M7A01, node 2). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** to use a formula, replace each letter with its value, write in the × signs, then work it out in the right order.

- I can replace each letter in a formula with its value, writing in the × signs.
- I can work out the result in the right order, including brackets and division.
- I can show my substitution as a line of working, with units.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Modify boards yourself first: they show the substitution but hide the value, so students must finish the arithmetic. ## Why it matters

> A formula is a recipe for a number. Put in the values you know, and it gives you the one you want: a perimeter, a cost, a speed.

<!-- Speaker note: To **substitute** into a formula is to replace each letter with its value, then work out the answer. A number written next to a letter, such as $2l$, means multiply: $2 \times l$. -->

## Area

Here is how to substitute, step by step.

$$P = 2l + 2w$$

1) $l = 5, \; w = 3$ — Write down the values you know.
2) $P = 2 \times 5 + 2 \times 3$ — Replace each letter with its value. Write in the × signs that were hidden in $2l$ and $2w$.
3) $P = 10 + 6$ — Multiply before adding.
4) $P = 16 \text{ cm}$ — Add, and give the units.
