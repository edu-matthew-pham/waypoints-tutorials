# Words into expressions

Algebra Foundations · Expressions

<!-- Speaker note: Students turn words such as more than, less than, twice and divided by into expressions, writing the operations in the order the words say them and checking with a number (AC9M7A02, node 1). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** words such as "more than", "less than", "twice" and "divided by" each mean an operation. Write the operations in the order the words say them, then check with a number.

- I can say what operation each word in a sentence about a number means.
- I can write an expression from words, with the operations in the right order.
- I can check my expression by trying a number I can work out in words.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. They hide the formula on purpose: the board does what the words say and shows the result, so students check their expression against it. The new move is turning each word into an operation. The most common error is "less than" (Ana's mistake): the numbers are written in the order they are spoken, 7 − n, when the number you start from comes first, n − 7. Always check with a real number: 7 less than 10 is 3. The pizza question in Practise is the opposite on purpose: "n slices are eaten from 8" starts from 8, so 8 − n is correct. Ask students what is being taken away from what. -->

## Why it matters

> "Twice as many", "5 less than", "shared between 4": every maths sentence about a number can be written with a letter.

<!-- Speaker note: A letter can stand for any number, so a sentence about "a number" can be written as an **expression**. Each word that tells you what to do to the number, such as *more than*, *less than*, *twice* or *divided by*, becomes an operation. The skill is writing the operations in the order the words give them. -->

## Three more than twice n

Here is how to turn words into an expression, one step at a time.

$$\text{3 more than twice } n$$

1) $\text{twice } n$ — Start with the part said first: twice a number is $2 \times n$.
2) $2n$ — A number next to a letter means multiply, so write $2n$.
3) $\text{3 more than } 2n$ — "More than" means add 3 to that.
4) $2n + 3$ — The expression.
5) $n = 4: \; 2 \times 4 + 3 = 11$ — Check with a number: twice 4 is 8, and 3 more is 11.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Line 2: write 2n and say "A number next to a letter means multiply, so write 2n." Line 3: write 3 more than 2n and say ""More than" means add 3 to that." Line 4: write 2n + 3 and say "The expression." Line 5: write n = 4: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11 and say "Check with a number: twice 4 is 8, and 3 more is 11." -->
