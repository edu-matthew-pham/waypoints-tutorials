# Solving harder equations

Algebra Foundations · Equations and inequalities

<!-- Speaker note: Students solve linear equations with x on both sides, brackets, negative answers and fractions by doing the same to both sides, writing one step per line, and verify every answer by substitution; a graph shows the solution as where the two sides meet (AC9M8A02, node 2). ## Today

**We are learning to** get the x terms on one side and the numbers on the other, doing the same to both sides; expand any brackets first. Then check by substituting into the first equation.

- I can solve an equation with x on both sides, including negatives.
- I can solve an equation with brackets by expanding first.
- I can check my solution by substituting it into the first equation.

## Why it matters

> Two phone plans: 7 dollars plus 3 a gigabyte, or 5 a gigabyte minus a 9 dollar credit. When do they cost the same? 3x + 7 = 5x − 9.

<!-- Speaker note: In Year 7 you solved equations with the balance method, even with $x$ on both sides. Year 8 equations add negatives, brackets and fractions, where blocks on a scale no longer help. The rule is the same: **do the same to both sides**, until $x$ is on its own. Then **check**: put your answer back into the first equation. If both sides are equal, it is right. -->

## Solve, then check

Here is how to solve with $x$ on both sides.

Now solve it yourself, one step per line. The checker marks the first line that does not follow.

$$3x + 7 = 5x - 9$$

1) $7 = 2x - 9$ — Take $3x$ from both sides (the smaller $x$ term, so $2x$ stays positive).
2) $16 = 2x$ — Add 9 to both sides: this undoes $- 9$.
3) $x = 8$ — Divide both sides by 2.
4) $3(8) + 7 = 31,\; 5(8) - 9 = 31$ — Check in the first equation: both sides are 31.

## Solve, then check

**Solve $3x + 7 = 5x - 9$. Write one step per line.**

* Answer: 3x + 7 = 5x - 9 → 7 = 2x - 9 → 16 = 2x → x = 8

<!-- Speaker note: Take 3x from both sides: 7 = 2x − 9. Add 9: 16 = 2x. Halve: x = 8. -->

## Find the mistake

**Ben solves $2x + 5 = 4x - 7$. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  2x + 5 = 4x − 7
 2  Take 2x from both sides: 5 = 2x − 7
 3  Take 7 from both sides: −2 = 2x
 4  So x = −1.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: to undo − 7, add 7 to both sides: 12 = 2x, so x = 6. Check: 2 × 6 + 5 = 17 and 4 × 6 − 7 = 17. -->
