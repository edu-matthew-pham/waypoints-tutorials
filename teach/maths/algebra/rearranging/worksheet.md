# Changing the subject

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A formula gives you one thing from the others. Sometimes you need it to work the other way round.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students rearrange formulas to change the subject by undoing the steps in reverse order on both sides, and check each rearrangement by substituting numbers, on a formula board that works the original forwards (AC9M8A01, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can make a given letter the subject of a formula by undoing the steps in reverse order. I can do the same to the whole of both sides, so I divide every term. I can check a rearranged formula by substituting numbers.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Undo in reverse order


:::keep-together

### Write it down

**Which formula records what you did with the fence?**

☐ $w = (P - 2l) \div 2$

☐ $w = (P + 2l) \div 2$

☐ $w = P - 2l \div 2$

☐ $w = P \div 2 - 2l$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $w = (P - 2l) \div 2$

Take off the two lengths (P − 2l), then halve what is left: w = (P − 2l) ÷ 2. The brackets keep 'what is left' together.

:::


:::keep-together

### Undo the last step first

$x$ was multiplied by 3, then 5 was added. Undo in reverse order: take away 5, then divide by 3.

1. $y = 3x + 5$ — $x$ was multiplied by 3, then 5 was added.
2. $y - 5 = 3x$ — Undo the last step first: take 5 from both sides.
3. $\frac{y - 5}{3} = x$ — Undo $\times 3$: divide the whole of both sides by 3.
4. $x = \frac{y - 5}{3}$ — Write the subject on the left.
5. $y = 11: x = \frac{11 - 5}{3} = 2$ — Check by substituting: $3 \times 2 + 5 = 11$. ✓

Try one: make $x$ the subject of $y = 2x + 7$.

**Make $x$ the subject of $y = 2x + 7$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** x = (y - 7)/2

y − 7 = 2x, then halve both sides: x = (y − 7)/2. Check: y = 17 gives x = 5, and 2 × 5 + 7 = 17.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Jo makes $x$ the subject of $y = 4x - 3$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Make x the subject of y = 4x − 3.
 2  Divide both sides by 4: y ÷ 4 = x − 3.
 3  Add 3 to both sides: y ÷ 4 + 3 = x.
 4  So x = y/4 + 3.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: dividing the right side by 4 divides both terms, so (4x − 3) ÷ 4 = x − 3/4, not x − 3. Undo − 3 first: y + 3 = 4x, so x = (y + 3)/4.

:::


:::keep-together

### Your working

**Make $x$ the subject of $y = 3x + 5$. Write one step per line.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** y = 3x + 5 → y - 5 = 3x → x = (y - 5)/3

Undo + 5: y − 5 = 3x. Undo × 3: x = (y − 5)/3.

## Rearrange and check


:::keep-together

### A formula in context

**A plumber charges $c = 50h + 80$ dollars for $h$ hours. Make $h$ the subject.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** h = (c - 80)/50

c − 80 = 50h, so h = (c − 80)/50. Check: h = 2 gives c = 180, and (180 − 80)/50 = 2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Full working

**Make $w$ the subject of $P = 2l + 2w$. Write one step per line.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** P = 2l + 2w → P - 2l = 2w → w = (P - 2l)/2

Undo + 2l: P − 2l = 2w. Halve both sides: w = (P − 2l)/2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Use it

**Use your formula: what is $w$ when $P = 30$ and $l = 9$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

(30 − 18) ÷ 2 = 6. Check: 2 × 9 + 2 × 6 = 30.

:::

## Make: a target pace, on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. The distance and time are made up for the task, and a real run has hills and a tired finish. Write the formula, rearrange it for the pace, put the numbers in, check, and type the pace.

**Also used in**

- **Running pace.** Time = pace × distance. Turn it around to find the pace you need for a target time.
- **A US weather forecast.** The conversion formula gives °F from °C. Turned around, it gives °C from °F.
- **Science.** Speed = distance ÷ time, turned around for how long a trip takes.
