# Changing the subject

Algebra Foundations · Brackets

<!-- Speaker note: Students rearrange formulas to change the subject by undoing the steps in reverse order on both sides, and check each rearrangement by substituting numbers, on a formula board that works the original forwards (AC9M8A01, node 3). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** a formula gives one letter from the others. To get a different letter, undo the steps in reverse order, doing the same to both sides: y = 3x + 5 becomes x = (y − 5)/3. Check by substituting.

- I can make a given letter the subject of a formula by undoing the steps in reverse order.
- I can do the same to the whole of both sides, so I divide every term.
- I can check a rearranged formula by substituting numbers.

## Undo the last step first

$x$ was multiplied by 3, then 5 was added. Undo in reverse order: take away 5, then divide by 3.

Try one: make $x$ the subject of $y = 2x + 7$.

$$y = 3x + 5$$

1) $y - 5 = 3x$ — Undo the last step first: take 5 from both sides.
2) $\frac{y - 5}{3} = x$ — Undo $\times 3$: divide the whole of both sides by 3.
3) $x = \frac{y - 5}{3}$ — Write the subject on the left.
4) $y = 11: x = \frac{11 - 5}{3} = 2$ — Check by substituting: $3 \times 2 + 5 = 11$. ✓

## Find the mistake

**Jo makes $x$ the subject of $y = 4x - 3$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Make x the subject of y = 4x − 3.
 2  Divide both sides by 4: y ÷ 4 = x − 3.
 3  Add 3 to both sides: y ÷ 4 + 3 = x.
 4  So x = y/4 + 3.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: dividing the right side by 4 divides both terms, so (4x − 3) ÷ 4 = x − 3/4, not x − 3. Undo − 3 first: y + 3 = 4x, so x = (y + 3)/4. -->
