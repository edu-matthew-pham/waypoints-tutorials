# Factorising

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 6 x-tiles and 9 ones. Can you share them into equal rows, with nothing left over? The number of rows goes outside the bracket.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students share algebra tiles into equal rows to factorise sx + t, learn to take out the highest common factor (numbers, then letters) and check by expanding (AC9M8A01, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can factorise an expression by taking out a common factor. I can take out the highest common factor, including a letter, so the expression is fully factorised. I can check a factorisation by expanding it.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The highest common factor


:::keep-together

### Take out the HCF

Here is how to factorise, then check.

1. $12x - 8$ — Find a number that goes into every term.
2. $\text{HCF of } 12 \text{ and } 8 = 4$ — Take out the biggest: the highest common factor.
3. $12x \div 4 = 3x$ — Divide every term by 4.
4. $-8 \div 4 = -2$ — The sign comes too.
5. $4(3x - 2)$ — The HCF goes outside; what is left goes inside.
6. $4 \times 3x - 4 \times 2 = 12x - 8$ — Check by expanding.

Try one: factorise $10x + 15$.

**Factorise $10x + 15$ fully.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5(2x + 3)

The HCF of 10 and 15 is 5: 10x ÷ 5 = 2x and 15 ÷ 5 = 3. Check: 5 × 2x + 5 × 3 = 10x + 15.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ava factorises $12x - 8$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Factorise 12x − 8.
 2  The biggest number that goes into 12 and 8 is 2.
 3  12 ÷ 2 = 6 and 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
 4  So 12x − 8 = 2(6x − 4).
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: 2 goes into both, but 4 is the biggest. 6x − 4 still shares a 2, so it is not fully factorised: 12x − 8 = 4(3x − 2).

:::


:::keep-together

### Check it

**How can you check that $4(3x - 2)$ is right for $12x - 8$?**

☐ Expand it and compare

☐ Check that 3 and 2 are both prime

☐ Add 4 to each term

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Expand it and compare

Expand: 4 × 3x = 12x and 4 × (−2) = −8. You are back to 12x − 8.
