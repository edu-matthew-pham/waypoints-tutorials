## Today

**We are learning to** factorising is expanding backwards. Share the terms into equal rows: 6x + 9 is 3 rows of 2x + 3, so 3(2x + 3). Take out the highest common factor, then check by expanding.

- I can factorise an expression by taking out a common factor.
- I can take out the highest common factor, including a letter, so the expression is fully factorised.
- I can check a factorisation by expanding it.

## Take out the HCF

Here is how to factorise, then check.

Try one: factorise $10x + 15$.

$$12x - 8$$

1) $\text{HCF of } 12 \text{ and } 8 = 4$ — Take out the biggest: the highest common factor.
2) $12x \div 4 = 3x$ — Divide every term by 4.
3) $-8 \div 4 = -2$ — The sign comes too.
4) $4(3x - 2)$ — The HCF goes outside; what is left goes inside.
5) $4 \times 3x - 4 \times 2 = 12x - 8$ — Check by expanding.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ## Find the mistake

**Ava factorises $12x - 8$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Factorise 12x − 8.
 2  The biggest number that goes into 12 and 8 is 2.
 3  12 ÷ 2 = 6 and 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
 4  So 12x − 8 = 2(6x − 4).
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: 2 goes into both, but 4 is the biggest. 6x − 4 still shares a 2, so it is not fully factorised: 12x − 8 = 4(3x − 2). -->

## Check it

**How can you check that $4(3x - 2)$ is right for $12x - 8$?**

- Expand it and compare
- Check that 3 and 2 are both prime
- Add 4 to each term

* Answer: Expand it and compare

<!-- Speaker note: Expand: 4 × 3x = 12x and 4 × (−2) = −8. You are back to 12x − 8. -->
