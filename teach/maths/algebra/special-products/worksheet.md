# Special products

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Can you square 51 in your head, and work out 49 × 51, with no calculator?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students take two strips off a square lawn and see why (x − 3)² has a corner to put back, then meet the difference of two squares, where the strips cancel, and use both to square 51 and multiply 49 × 51 in their heads (AC9M10A01, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can expand a bracket squared, with a plus or a minus, and say why there are two strips and a corner. I can expand (x + a)(x − a) and say why there's no x term. I can use the patterns to work out something like 49 × 51 in my head.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**A square lawn is $x$ metres each way. A path 3 m wide takes a strip off two sides, so the lawn that's left is $x - 3$ metres each way. Which expression is its area?**

☐ $x^2 - 9$

☐ $x^2 - 6x - 9$

☐ $x^2 - 3x + 9$

☐ $x^2 - 6x + 9$

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** x² − 6x + 9. Start with the whole x by x square and take off the two strips of 3x. The 3 by 3 corner was in both strips, so it's been taken off twice: put 9 back. x² − 9 only squares each part; x² − 6x − 9 forgets to put the corner back.

:::

## Two strips and a corner


:::keep-together

### Square a bracket

Here's one worked out, piece by piece.

1. $(x + 4)^2$ — Means (x + 4)(x + 4): a square, x + 4 each way.
2. $x \times x = x^2$ — The big square.
3. $x \times 4 = 4x$ — One strip.
4. $4 \times x = 4x$ — The other strip, the same size.
5. $4 \times 4 = 16$ — The corner.
6. $x^2 + 4x + 4x + 16 = x^2 + 8x + 16$ — Collect the two strips.

Now try one on paper, piece by piece like the example: $(x + 6)^2$. Then type your answer.

**Expand $(x + 6)^2$. Type $x^2$ as x^2.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** x^2 + 12x + 36

x² + 6x + 6x + 36 = x² + 12x + 36.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Dev expands $(x - 4)^2$. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Expand (x − 4)².
 2  (x − 4)² means (x − 4)(x − 4).
 3  x times x is x², and −4 times −4 is 16.
 4  So (x − 4)² = x² + 16.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: Dev left out the two strips, −4x and −4x. (x − 4)(x − 4) = x² − 4x − 4x + 16 = x² − 8x + 16.

:::


:::keep-together

### No x term

**Why does $(x + 6)(x - 6)$ have no $x$ term?**

☐ The two strips are +6x and −6x, so they cancel

☐ 6 and −6 are small numbers

☐ Both brackets start with x

☐ There's only one strip when the signs differ

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The two strips are +6x and −6x, so they cancel

x² − 6x + 6x − 36: the strips are opposites, so they add to nothing and the answer is x² − 36.

:::

### Name the pattern

**Which of these is a difference of two squares: a square take away a square?**

☐ $x^2 - 16x + 64$

☐ $x^2 - 64$

☐ $x^2 + 64$

☐ $x^2 - 60$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $x^2 - 64$

x² − 64 is x² − 8², which is (x + 8)(x − 8).

:::

Answer: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** (x + 4)(x + 5)

4 and 5 multiply to 20 and add to 9.
