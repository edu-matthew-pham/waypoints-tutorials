# Equations as a balance

Algebra Foundations · Equations

<!-- Speaker note: Students treat an equation as a balance, find the value that balances it, and check a value by substitution (AC9M7A03, node 1). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 10 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Make 8 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** an equation says two things are equal. Find the value that makes it true, then check it.

- I can check whether a value is a solution by putting it in place of the letter and working out both sides.
- I can tell an expression from an equation.
- I can explain that a solution makes both sides of an equation equal.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. The surprise is the last Modify task: $2x = 7$ never balances with a whole number, and that is the point. Be ready to ask "what would $x$ have to be?" and accept 3.5 without teaching a method yet: solving step by step is the next two lessons. Teacher: Say 2x aloud as 'two times x', never 'two x', for the whole lesson. It prevents the 24-for-8 slip in the second task. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Shops, recipes and sports tables all hide an equation: a total that has to match. Finding the missing number, and checking it, is what solving means.

<!-- Speaker note: An **expression** is a calculation with a letter in it, such as $2x + 3$. An **equation** puts an = sign between two expressions: it says they are equal, like a balance scale with the same weight on each side. The **solution** is the value of the letter that makes it true. -->

## Check by substitution

To check a value, put it in place of the letter and work out each side.

$$2x + 3 = 11$$

1) $2(4) + 3$ — Put 4 in place of $x$ on the left. The brackets show that $2x$ means 2 times $x$.
2) $= 8 + 3 = 11$ — Work out the left side.
3) $11 = 11$ — The left side equals the right side.
4) $\text{So } x = 4 \text{ is a solution.}$ — If the two sides were different, it would not be.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ## Check by substitution

**Is $x = 5$ a solution of $3x - 2 = 13$? Start by working out the left side, $3x - 2$, when $x = 5$.**

* Answer: 13

<!-- Speaker note: 3 × 5 − 2 = 15 − 2 = 13. The right side is 13 too, so x = 5 is a solution. -->

## Find the mistake

**Sam decides that $x = 4$ is not a solution of $2x + 3 = 11$, but it balanced the scale. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Is x = 4 a solution of 2x + 3 = 11?
 2  2x + 3 = 24 + 3
 3         = 27
 4  27 is not 11, so x = 4 is not a solution.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Sam wrote 2 and 4 side by side to get 24. 2x means 2 × x, so it is 8, and 8 + 3 = 11. x = 4 is a solution after all. -->

## Expression or equation?

**Which one is an equation?**

- $2x + 3$
- $2x + 3 = 11$
- $x + 7 - 2$
- $5x$

* Answer: $2x + 3 = 11$

<!-- Speaker note: An equation has an = sign: it says two things are equal. The others are expressions: they have a value, but they do not say that anything is equal. -->

## New scales



- **Four bags:** Each task loads a new scale. Find the value of $x$ that balances it.
- **$x$ on both sides:** Now both sides change when $x$ changes. The solution still makes the two sides equal.
- **A scale that will not balance:** Try every value. What happens between $x = 3$ and $x = 4$?

## Both sides

**Is $x = 2$ a solution of $4x + 1 = 3x + 4$?**

- No: the left side is 9 and the right side is 10
- No: the left side is 42 and the right side is 34
- Yes: both sides are 9

* Answer: No: the left side is 9 and the right side is 10

<!-- Speaker note: 4 × 2 + 1 = 9 and 3 × 2 + 4 = 10. The sides are different, so x = 2 is not a solution. (x = 3 is: both sides are 13.) -->

## Exit ticket

**Is x = 7 a solution of 3x − 5 = 16? Show how you know.**

* Yes. 3 × 7 − 5 = 21 − 5 = 16, which equals the right side.
