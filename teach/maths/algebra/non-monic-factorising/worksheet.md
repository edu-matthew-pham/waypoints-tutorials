# Factorising when x² has a number

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A frame shop's price list gives a frame's whole area as 4x² + 20x + 24. Can you get the two lengths back?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students pack 2x² + 7x + 3 into a rectangle, find that the two numbers multiply to a × c and not c, split the strips and factorise by grouping, then come back to a frame's area and share a common factor between the brackets (AC9M10A01, node 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can factorise a quadratic with a number in front of x² by finding two numbers that multiply to a × c. I can split the strips and take out what each pair has in common. I can take out a common factor first, and check by expanding.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**What happens:** x + 3. (2x + 1)(x + 3) = 2x² + 6x + x + 3. The 2x across and the x down make the two big squares; the strips are 6x and x, which make 7x; the corner is 1 × 3. x + 6 takes the strips as 7 − 1 and misses the squares; 2x + 3 would make four big squares.

## Multiply, then split


:::keep-together

### Multiply, then split

Here's how the tiles pack, step by step.

1. $2x^2 + 7x + 3$ — Two big squares, 7 strips, 3 ones: pack them into a rectangle.
2. $2 \times 3 = 6$ — The strips go on two sides, so look at the x² number times the last number.
3. $1 \times 6 = 6, \; 1 + 6 = 7$ — A pair that multiplies to 6 and adds to 7.
4. $2x^2 + 6x + x + 3$ — Split the 7 strips into 6 and 1.
5. $2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)$ — Take out what each pair has in common.
6. $(2x + 1)(x + 3)$ — Both pairs share x + 3. That's one side; what's left is the other.

Now try one on paper, step by step like the example: $3x^2 + 10x + 8$. Then type your answer.

**Factorise $3x^2 + 10x + 8$. Type $x^2$ as x^2.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (3x + 4)(x + 2)

3 × 8 = 24, and 4 and 6 multiply to 24 and add to 10. 3x² + 6x + 4x + 8 = 3x(x + 2) + 4(x + 2) = (3x + 4)(x + 2).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Priya tries to factorise $2x^2 + 7x + 3$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Factorise 2x² + 7x + 3.
 2  I need two numbers that multiply to 3 and add to 7.
 3  The pairs that make 3 are 1 and 3, and they add to 4.
 4  So there's no pair, and 2x² + 7x + 3 can't be factorised.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: with 2x² the two numbers multiply to 2 × 3 = 6, not 3. 1 and 6 multiply to 6 and add to 7, so it factorises: (2x + 1)(x + 3).

:::


:::keep-together

### Check by expanding

**Expand $(2x + 3)(x + 1)$, to see whether it could be a factorisation of $2x^2 + 7x + 3$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2x^2 + 5x + 3

2x² + 2x + 3x + 3 = 2x² + 5x + 3. It's close, but the strips make 5x, not 7x, so it isn't.

## Split the strips


:::keep-together

### Both positive

**Factorise $2x^2 + 9x + 4$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (2x + 1)(x + 4)

2 × 4 = 8, and 1 and 8 add to 9. 2x² + 8x + x + 4 = 2x(x + 4) + 1(x + 4) = (2x + 1)(x + 4).

:::


:::keep-together

### Another positive

**Factorise $3x^2 + 7x + 2$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (3x + 1)(x + 2)

3 × 2 = 6, and 1 and 6 add to 7. 3x² + 6x + x + 2 = 3x(x + 2) + 1(x + 2) = (3x + 1)(x + 2).

:::


:::keep-together

### A negative middle

**Factorise $5x^2 - 13x + 6$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (5x - 3)(x - 2)

5 × 6 = 30, and −3 and −10 multiply to 30 and add to −13. 5x² − 10x − 3x + 6 = 5x(x − 2) − 3(x − 2) = (5x − 3)(x − 2).

:::


:::keep-together

### A negative end

**Factorise $2x^2 - 5x - 3$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (2x + 1)(x - 3)

2 × (−3) = −6, and −6 and 1 multiply to −6 and add to −5. 2x² − 6x + x − 3 = 2x(x − 3) + 1(x − 3) = (2x + 1)(x − 3).

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: factorise $x^2 - 49$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (x - 7)(x + 7)

−7 and 7 multiply to −49 and add to 0: the strips cancel.

:::
