# Factorising when x² has a number

Algebra Foundations · Special products and factorising

<!-- Speaker note: Students pack 2x² + 7x + 3 into a rectangle, find that the two numbers multiply to a × c and not c, split the strips and factorise by grouping, then come back to a frame's area and share a common factor between the brackets (AC9M10A01, node 4). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

- I can factorise a quadratic with a number in front of x² by finding two numbers that multiply to a × c.
- I can split the strips and take out what each pair has in common.
- I can take out a common factor first, and check by expanding.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need Factorising quadratics (Year 9) and Special products. The one idea: with a number in front of x², the two numbers for the strips multiply to a × c, then split the middle term and group. The common mistake is looking for a pair that multiplies to c as in the monic case (Priya's working, planted after the worked example). Completing the square is not in this lesson; it comes next. ## Multiply, then split

Here's how the tiles pack, step by step.

Now try one on paper, step by step like the example: $3x^2 + 10x + 8$. Then type your answer.

$$2x^2 + 7x + 3$$

1) $2 \times 3 = 6$ — The strips go on two sides, so look at the x² number times the last number.
2) $1 \times 6 = 6, \; 1 + 6 = 7$ — A pair that multiplies to 6 and adds to 7.
3) $2x^2 + 6x + x + 3$ — Split the 7 strips into 6 and 1.
4) $2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)$ — Take out what each pair has in common.
5) $(2x + 1)(x + 3)$ — Both pairs share x + 3. That's one side; what's left is the other.

## Find the mistake

**Priya tries to factorise $2x^2 + 7x + 3$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Factorise 2x² + 7x + 3.
 2  I need two numbers that multiply to 3 and add to 7.
 3  The pairs that make 3 are 1 and 3, and they add to 4.
 4  So there's no pair, and 2x² + 7x + 3 can't be factorised.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: with 2x² the two numbers multiply to 2 × 3 = 6, not 3. 1 and 6 multiply to 6 and add to 7, so it factorises: (2x + 1)(x + 3). -->
