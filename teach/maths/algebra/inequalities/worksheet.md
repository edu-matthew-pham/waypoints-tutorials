# Inequalities

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You need more than 50 points to pass, and a ride needs riders at least 120 cm tall. Not one answer: a whole range of them.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students solve one-variable inequalities like equations, reversing the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative, show solutions on a number line with open or closed circles, and check graphically and by substitution (AC9M8A02, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can solve an inequality, reversing the inequality sign when I multiply or divide by a negative. I can show a solution on a number line with the right circle (open or closed) and direction. I can check a solution by substituting a value, or with a graph.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Mia solves $-2x &gt; 6$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Solve −2x > 6.
 2  Divide both sides by −2.
 3  x > −3.
 4  Check x = 0: −2 × 0 = 0, and 0 > 6 is false.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: dividing by −2 reverses the inequality sign, so x &lt; −3. Her check on line 4 fails, which is the clue: x = 0 should work if x &gt; −3 were right.

### Divide by a negative

**Which is the solution of $-3x \le 12$?**

☐ $x \ge 4$

☐ $x \le -4$

☐ $x \ge -4$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $x \ge -4$

Divide by −3 and reverse: x ≥ −4. Check x = 0: 0 ≤ 12. ✓

## Make: a trailer load, on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. The rating and masses are made up for the task, and on a real trailer the rating plate says whether it counts the trailer's own weight. Write the load, the inequality, solve it, then decide what to do with 16.5 and type the number of boxes.

**Also used in**

- **Trailers and lifts.** A rating plate is a limit: the load must be at most that, so "how many boxes" is an inequality.
- **Budgets.** "How many rides can I afford after the entry fee?" has the same shape: the cost can be at most what you have.
- **Next.** Two limits at once, such as a budget and a time, make a region on a graph in Year 10.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
