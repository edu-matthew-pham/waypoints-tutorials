Algebra Foundations · Equations and inequalities

## Today

**We are learning to** solve an inequality like an equation, doing the same to both sides, but when you multiply or divide by a negative, reverse the inequality sign. Show the answer on a number line: open circle for < and >, closed for ≤ and ≥. Check with a value.

- I can solve an inequality, reversing the inequality sign when I multiply or divide by a negative.
- I can show a solution on a number line with the right circle (open or closed) and direction.
- I can check a solution by substituting a value, or with a graph.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Investigate board yourself first. Students need Solving harder equations, and Lines and regions (Linear relations) for the graph. > You need more than 50 points to pass, and a ride needs riders at least 120 cm tall. Not one answer: a whole range of them.

<!-- Speaker note: An **inequality** compares two sides with $<$, $>$, $\le$ or $\ge$. Its solution is usually a whole range of values, such as $x < 5$, which you can show on a number line. ## Find the mistake

**Mia solves $-2x &gt; 6$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Solve −2x > 6.
 2  Divide both sides by −2.
 3  x > −3.
 4  Check x = 0: −2 × 0 = 0, and 0 > 6 is false.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: dividing by −2 reverses the inequality sign, so x < −3. Her check on line 4 fails, which is the clue: x = 0 should work if x > −3 were right. -->
