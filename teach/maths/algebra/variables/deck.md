# Letters that vary

Algebra Foundations · Expressions

<!-- Speaker note: Students tell the variables in a formula from the constants, and see that in an equation a letter can be one unknown number (AC9M7A01, node 1). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** in a formula, letters stand for quantities that can change (variables) and numbers stay the same (constants). In an equation, a letter can stand for one unknown number.

- I can say which letters in a formula are variables and which numbers are constants.
- I can say what a constant means in a real formula, such as a call-out fee.
- I can tell a variable (can take many values) from an unknown (one value makes the equation true).

## Why it matters

> Recipes, phone bills and the perimeter of a garden bed all follow a formula. The letters are what changes; the numbers are what stays.

## Reading a formula

Here is how to read the parts of a formula.

$$P = 2l + 2w$$

1) $P, \; l, \; w$ — These letters are **variables**: quantities that change from one rectangle to the next.
2) $2$ — The 2s are **constants**: they are the same for every rectangle.
3) $l = 5, \; w = 3$ — For one particular rectangle, each variable has a value.
4) $3x + 2 = 14$ — In an equation, a letter can be an **unknown**: one particular number.
5) $x = 4$ — Only one value makes this equation true.

## Reading a formula

**A plumber charges $C = 5h + 20$ dollars for a job of $h$ hours. Which are the variables?**

- 5 and 20
- $C$ and $h$
- Only $h$

* Answer: $C$ and $h$

<!-- Speaker note: C and h change from job to job. 5 (dollars an hour) and 20 (the call-out fee) are constants: the same for every job. -->

## Find the mistake

**Kim works out the perimeter. Click the line where her thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  P = 2l + 2w
 2  l is the 12th letter of the alphabet, so l = 12.
 3  w is the 23rd letter, so w = 23.
 4  P = 2 × 12 + 2 × 23 = 70.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: a letter stands for a quantity, here the length, not its place in the alphabet. l can be any length: you need to measure or be told it. -->

## Unknown or variable?

**In the equation $3x + 2 = 14$, what does $x$ stand for?**

- An unknown: one number makes it true
- The letter x, not a number
- A variable that can be any number

* Answer: An unknown: one number makes it true

<!-- Speaker note: In an equation, the letter is an unknown: only x = 4 makes 3x + 2 = 14 true. In a formula, a letter is a variable that can take many values. -->

## What the constant means

**Water in a tank: $V = 3t + 10$ litres after $t$ minutes. What does the 10 mean?**

- The tank fills in 10 minutes
- 10 litres in the tank at the start
- 10 litres added every minute

* Answer: 10 litres in the tank at the start

<!-- Speaker note: When t = 0, V = 10: the 10 litres were there at the start. 3 litres are added each minute. -->

## Variables

**Which letters are variables in $T = 12n + 6$ (the cost of $n$ concert tickets)?**

- $T$ and $n$
- 12 and 6
- Only $T$

* Answer: $T$ and $n$

<!-- Speaker note: T and n change with the order. 12 (dollars a ticket) and 6 (the booking fee) stay the same. -->

## Unknown

**In $2y + 1 = 9$, what is $y$?**

- An unknown: $y = 4$ makes it true
- A variable: any value works
- A constant

* Answer: An unknown: $y = 4$ makes it true

<!-- Speaker note: It is an equation, so y is an unknown: 2 × 4 + 1 = 9, and no other value works. -->

## Exit ticket

**A taxi charges T = 2d + 4 dollars for d km. Name the variables and the constants, and say what the 4 means.**

* Variables: T and d. Constants: 2 and 4. The 4 is the fee to get in, paid even for 0 km.
