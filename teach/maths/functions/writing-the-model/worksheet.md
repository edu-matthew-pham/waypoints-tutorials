## The growth factor


:::keep-together

### Rate to factor

Here's how to write the model from the words.

1. $\$2000 \text{ at } 3\% \text{ a year}$ — Write the balance after n years.
2. $a = 2000$ — The starting amount.
3. $b = 1 + 0.03 = 1.03$ — Keep the balance and add 3% of it.
4. $A = 2000(1.03^n)$ — Starting amount times the factor, n times.
5. $A = 2000(1.03^5)$ — After 5 years.
6. $A = 2318.55$ — Rounded to the cent. Always put the unit: dollars.

Now try one on paper: $500$ at $4\%$ a year. Find the balance after 2 years, in dollars.

**An account grows by $6\%$ a year. What do you multiply the balance by each year?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1.06

The whole balance stays (1) and 6% of it is added (0.06): 1.06.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Maya writes the model for $1,000 at 5% a year. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  An account pays 5% a year. I start with $1,000. What is the model?
 2  5% is 0.05, so each year the balance is multiplied by 0.05.
 3  So A = 1000 x 0.05^n, where n is the number of years.
 4  After 1 year that gives 1000 x 0.05 = 50.
 5  Fifty dollars is less than I started with, but that can't be right. Maybe I rounded.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: multiplying by 0.05 keeps only 5% of the balance. To grow by 5% you keep all of it and add 5% more: multiply by 1.05.

:::

### Two years

**$200$ is invested at $10\%$ a year. What is the balance after 2 years, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 242

200 × 1.1 = 220 and 220 × 1.1 = 242.

## Make: back to the savings


**Compound against simple.** Back to the savings. Work this out on paper first, then type your answer.

**How much to trust it.** The rate and the $1,000$ are made up. The shape is the point: a rate each year multiplies, so the last years add the most.

Sources: the $1,000$ and the 5% a year are made up for this lesson and are not an offer. Your class can check real figures with a savings calculator, such as [MoneySmart's compound interest calculator](https://moneysmart.gov.au/saving/compound-interest-calculator) (ASIC); it also shows the effect of fees and tax.

**Also used in**

- **Money.** Loans grow the same way when interest is added to what is owed.
- **Science.** A population growing 3% a year, or a drug leaving the body at 12% an hour, uses the same model.
