# Comparing integers

Number Foundations · Integers

## Today

**We are learning to** on a number line, the number further left is less. So −7 is less than −2, even though 7 is more than 2.

- I can put a set of integers, including negatives, in order from least to greatest.
- I can write a comparison between two integers with &lt; or &gt;.
- I can explain why −10 is less than −1, even though 10 is more than 1.

## Why it matters

> Thredbo at −9 °C, Canberra at −3 °C: which is colder? Below zero, the number that looks bigger is the smaller one.

<!-- Speaker note: Numbers below zero are **negative**: $-1, -2, -3$ and so on. Together with zero and the positive whole numbers they are the **integers**. On a number line, negatives sit to the left of zero, with $-1$ next to zero. In this lesson you find out how to tell which of two integers is less, and write it with $<$ ("is less than") or $>$ ("is greater than"). -->

## Ordering on the line

Here is how to put integers in order, using where each one sits on the number line.

$$-2, \; -8, \; 3, \; 0$$

1) $\text{place each on the line}$ — Negatives go left of zero: $-2$ is 2 left, $-8$ is 8 left.
2) $-8 \text{ is furthest left}$ — Furthest left is least, so $-8$ comes first.
3) $-8, \; -2, \; 0, \; 3$ — Read the line from left to right.
4) $-8 < -2$ — Between two negatives, the one further from zero is less.
