# Comparing integers

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** On a cold morning Thredbo is at −9 °C and Canberra at −3 °C (made-up temperatures for the task). Which is colder, and which number is bigger?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place integers on a number line, find that further left is less (so −7 < −2), and compare and order integers with < and > (AC9M7N07, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can put a set of integers, including negatives, in order from least to greatest. I can write a comparison between two integers with &lt; or &gt;. I can explain why −10 is less than −1, even though 10 is more than 1.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Further left is less


:::keep-together

### Ordering on the line

Here is how to put integers in order, using where each one sits on the number line.

1. $-2, \; -8, \; 3, \; 0$ — Put these in order from least to greatest.
2. $\text{place each on the line}$ — Negatives go left of zero: $-2$ is 2 left, $-8$ is 8 left.
3. $-8 \text{ is furthest left}$ — Furthest left is least, so $-8$ comes first.
4. $-8, \; -2, \; 0, \; 3$ — Read the line from left to right.
5. $-8 < -2$ — Between two negatives, the one further from zero is less.

**Which is true?**

☐ $-5 = -1$

☐ $-5 &lt; -1$

☐ $-5 &gt; -1$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $-5 &lt; -1$

−5 is further left than −1, so −5 &lt; −1.

:::


:::keep-together

## Make: a week of frosts on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. The temperatures are made up for the task, but the question is real: weather reports list frosty lows, and you have to say which night was coldest. Mark the nights on a line, then order them. The page marks the two reasons you pick.

**Also used in**

- **Weather.** Overnight lows, and heights above or below sea level, are compared on a line with zero in the middle.
- **Buildings.** Car park levels below ground are negative: level −3 is lower than level −1.
- **Money.** An account at −\$50 owes more than one at −\$10, though 50 is the bigger number.
