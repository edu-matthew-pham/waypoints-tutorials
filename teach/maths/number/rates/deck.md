# Rates

Number Foundations · Rates

## Today

**We are learning to** a rate compares two quantities with different units: 60 km per hour, $4 per kg, 80 beats per minute. The unit rate is how much for 1 of the second quantity: divide the first by the second. A ratio compares quantities in the same units. Speeds convert with 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s.

- I can write a rate with its two units.
- I can find a unit rate by dividing in the right order.
- I can convert a speed between km/h and m/s, and tell a rate from a ratio.

## Why it matters

> Speed limits, the price of petrol, your heart rate and how fast your phone downloads: all rates. Each one says 'this much per one of that'.

<!-- Speaker note: A **rate** compares two quantities measured in **different units**: 60 km per hour (km/h), $4.50 per kilogram ($/kg), 80 beats per minute. A **ratio** compares quantities in the **same** units: 2 cups of flour to 3 cups of milk, 2 : 3. The **unit rate** is the amount for **one** of the second quantity. Divide the first by the second, and keep the units: $$150 \text{ km in } 2.5 \text{ h} \;\Rightarrow\; 150 \div 2.5 = 60 \text{ km per h}$$ Rates convert like units. For speed: 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s, so $54 \text{ km/h} = 54 \times 1000 \div 3600 = 15 \text{ m/s}$ (divide by 3.6). -->

## Rate or ratio

Here is the routine.

$$\text{distance} \div \text{time}$$

1) $150 \div 2.5 = 60$ — 150 km in 2.5 h.
2) $60 \text{ km/h}$ — Keep both units in the answer.
3) $60 \div 3.6 \approx 16.7 \text{ m/s}$ — To convert km/h to m/s, × 1000 ÷ 3600, which is ÷ 3.6.
