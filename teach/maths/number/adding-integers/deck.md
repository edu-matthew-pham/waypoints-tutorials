# Adding and subtracting integers

Number Foundations · Integers

<!-- Speaker note: Students add and subtract integers as moves on a number line, find that adding a negative moves left and that subtracting moves the opposite way to adding, so 5 − (−3) = 8 (AC9M7N07, node 2). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** start at the first number and move along the line. Adding a negative moves left; subtracting moves the opposite way to adding, so subtracting a negative moves right.

- I can add and subtract integers by moving along a number line.
- I can explain why adding a negative number moves left.
- I can explain why 5 − (−3) is 8, not 2.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board and the Make task yourself first. Students need the last lesson: further left is less. The board draws the "adding" direction faintly above a subtraction, so students can see the turn-round. The lesson does not teach "two minuses make a plus". ## Why it matters

> A temperature that rises 9 degrees from −4 °C, a lift that goes down 5 floors from level 2, a bank balance below zero: all of them are moves along a number line.

## Moves on the line

Here is how to use the line for each kind of sum.

$$-2 + 5$$

1) $-2 + 5 = 3$ — Adding a positive moves right: 5 to the right lands on 3.
2) $4 + (-6) = -2$ — Adding a negative moves left: from 4, left 6 lands on $-2$.
3) $5 - (-3)$ — Subtracting moves the opposite way to adding.
4) $\text{adding } {-3} \text{ is left 3, so subtracting it is right 3}$ — Find the way adding would go, then turn round.
5) $5 - (-3) = 8$ — From 5, right 3 lands on 8. Subtracting $-3$ is the same as adding 3.

## Right or left?

**From the first number, does each sum move right or left?**

Groups: Moves right · Moves left

2 + 3 · 2 + (−3) · 2 − 3 · 2 − (−3) · −5 + 4 · −2 − 4

* Answer: 2 + 3 → Moves right; 2 + (−3) → Moves left; 2 − 3 → Moves left; 2 − (−3) → Moves right; −5 + 4 → Moves right; −2 − 4 → Moves left
