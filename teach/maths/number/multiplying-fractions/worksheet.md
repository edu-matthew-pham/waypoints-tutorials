**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Half of a pizza is left, and you eat a third of what is left. How much of the whole pizza did you eat? Fractions of fractions are multiplications.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use a fraction area square to see a fraction of a fraction, multiply tops and bottoms, learn that multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes smaller, and divide by counting how many fit (AC9M7N06, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can multiply two fractions and simplify the answer. I can divide a fraction by a whole number and by a fraction. I can say whether a product will be bigger or smaller than the number I started with.

## Multiply, and divide


:::keep-together

### Multiply across; divide by flipping

Here is how each kind works.

1. $\tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{2 \times 3}{3 \times 4} = \tfrac{6}{12} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ — Multiply the tops and the bottoms, then simplify.
2. $\tfrac{3}{4} \times 20 = \tfrac{60}{4} = 15$ — A whole number is over 1: 20 = 20/1. 15 is less than 20.
3. $\tfrac{1}{2} \div 3 = \tfrac{1}{6}$ — Share a half into 3: each share is a sixth (the same as × 1/3).
4. $3 \div \tfrac{1}{4} = 3 \times 4 = 12$ — How many quarters in 3? Dividing by 1/4 is multiplying by 4.
5. $\tfrac{2}{3} \div \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{2}{3} \times 6 = 4$ — Divide by a fraction: multiply by it flipped (1/6 flipped is 6/1 = 6).
6. $0.4 \times 3 = 1.2$ — Decimals: 4 tenths × 3 = 12 tenths.

Now try one: $\tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{3}{5}$.

**Work out $\tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{3}{5}$ in simplest form.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2/5

(2 × 3)/(3 × 5) = 6/15 = 2/5.

:::


:::keep-together
