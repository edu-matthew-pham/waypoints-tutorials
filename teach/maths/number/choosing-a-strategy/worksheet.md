# Choosing a strategy

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two students work out 25% of 48. One writes a long multiplication; the other halves twice in their head. Both get 12. Which would you rather do in a shop?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare strategies for calculations with fractions, decimals and percentages: changing the order (any order for multiplying), changing the form (0.75 = 3/4), and splitting (the distributive law), with an estimate to check (AC9M7N06, node 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can choose a quick strategy for a calculation and explain why it is quick. I can use any order for multiplying, a friendly fraction, or splitting a number. I can estimate to check that my answer is about right.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Three moves


:::keep-together

### Three moves

Here are the three moves, each with a check.

1. $4 \times 2.7 \times 25 = (4 \times 25) \times 2.7 = 270$ — Change the order: 4 × 25 = 100. Check: 2.7 is about 3, and 100 × 3 = 300.
2. $0.75 \times 36 = \tfrac{3}{4} \times 36 = 27$ — Change the form: 36 ÷ 4 = 9, × 3 = 27. Check: a bit less than 36.
3. $12 \times 2.5 = 12 \times 2 + 12 \times 0.5 = 24 + 6 = 30$ — Split a number: 2.5 = 2 + 0.5. Check: between 12 × 2 and 12 × 3.

Your working can be short notes. Then choose the reason for the key step.

**Work out $4 \times 2.7 \times 25$ without a calculator. Write your steps.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $4 \times 25 = 100$ (multiplying can be done in any order, so pair the 4 and the 25); $100 \times 2.7 = 270$ (multiplying by 100 makes each digit 100 times bigger)

4 × 25 = 100, and 100 × 2.7 = 270.

:::


:::keep-together

### Change the form

**Work out $0.75 \times 36$ without a calculator. Write your steps.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $0.75 = \tfrac{3}{4}$ (a decimal can be written as a fraction); $\tfrac{1}{4}$ of 36 is 9, so $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of 36 is 27 (36 divides by 4, so the fraction is quick)

0.75 = 3/4. 36 ÷ 4 = 9, × 3 = 27.

:::


:::keep-together

### Split

**Which way works out $25 \times 3.2$ by splitting?**

☐ $25 \times 3 + 25 \times 0.2 = 75 + 5 = 80$

☐ $25 \times 3 + 0.2 = 75.2$

☐ $25 \times 3 \times 0.2 = 15$

☐ $20 \times 3.2 + 5 \times 3 = 79$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $25 \times 3 + 25 \times 0.2 = 75 + 5 = 80$

3.2 = 3 + 0.2, so 25 × 3.2 = 25 × 3 + 25 × 0.2 = 75 + 5 = 80 (the distributive law).

:::

## Quick ways


:::keep-together

### Reorder

**Work out $8 \times 1.25$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10

1.25 = 1 1/4, so 8 + 2 = 10. Or 8 × 1.25 = 8 × 5/4 = 10.

:::


:::keep-together

### Friendly percentage

**Find 20% of 35.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

20% is a fifth: 35 ÷ 5 = 7.

:::


:::keep-together

### Near a round number

**Work out $99 \times 0.4$. (Try $100 \times 0.4$ first.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 39.6

99 × 0.4 = 100 × 0.4 − 1 × 0.4 = 40 − 0.4 = 39.6.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: percentages

**Review: a $40 bag is 15% off. What is the sale price, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 34

10% is $4 and 5% is $2, so $6 off: $34. Or 40 × 0.85 = 34.
