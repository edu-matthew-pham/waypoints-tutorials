# Using rates

Number Foundations · Rates

## Today

**We are learning to** use a rate to find a total (rate × amount), or the amount (total ÷ rate). For speed: distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. Show the units at every step, and check the answer makes sense.

- I can use a rate to find a total or an amount.
- I can use distance, speed and time in any order.
- I can check an answer with its units and its size.

## Why it matters

> How much will it cost to fill the car? How long is the drive to the coast? How many dollars for your holiday money? Each answer comes from one rate, used the right way round.

<!-- Speaker note: A rate links two quantities, so you can go either way: - **total = rate × amount**: 30 L of petrol at $2.05 per L costs $30 × 2.05$ = \$61.50; - **amount = total ÷ rate**: \$61.50 at $2.05 per L buys $61.50 \div 2.05 = 30$ L. For speed, **distance = speed × time** and **time = distance ÷ speed**. Write the units at each step: they tell you whether to multiply or divide (km ÷ km/h = h). Then check: is the answer about the right size? -->

## Time

Here is the routine.

$$\text{time} = \text{distance} \div \text{speed}$$

1) $200 \div 80 = 2.5$ — Divide: 200 km at 80 km/h.
2) $2.5 \text{ h} = 2 \text{ h } 30 \text{ min}$ — Interpret: half an hour is 30 minutes.
3) $80 \times 2.5 = 200$ — Check by going back.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script.
