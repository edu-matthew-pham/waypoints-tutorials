# Ratio and rate models

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Painting a room, mixing cordial for a party, filling a pool, sharing the cost of a gift: the maths is easy once you choose the right model and remember what the answer means.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students model practical problems as a ratio or a rate (paint coverage, cordial, fuel, sharing, filling), solve them and interpret the answer in the situation (AC9M8M07, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can decide whether a problem needs a ratio or a rate. I can set up and solve a ratio or rate model. I can interpret the answer in the situation, such as rounding up to whole tins.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**A litre of paint covers 12 m². How much paint do you need for a 30 m² wall?**

☐ 0.4 L

☐ 2.5 L

☐ 42 L

☐ 360 L

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 2.5 L: 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5. (360 multiplies the area by the rate; that would be m² × m² per L, which makes no sense.)

## Choosing the model


:::keep-together

### Ratio or rate

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{need: litres of paint for 20 m}^2$ — Say what you are finding, with units.
2. $\text{12 m}^2 \text{ per L: a rate}$ — Different units, so a rate.
3. $20 \div 12 \approx 1.7 \text{ L}$ — m² ÷ (m² per L) = L.
4. $\text{buy 2 one-litre tins}$ — Interpret: tins are whole, and 1 would not be enough.

**Cordial is mixed 1 part cordial to 4 parts water. Is this a ratio or a rate model?**

☐ A rate: litres per litre

☐ A percentage

☐ A ratio: both parts are litres of liquid

☐ Neither

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A ratio: both parts are litres of liquid

Same kind of quantity, same units: a ratio, 1 : 4.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ana buys paint. Click the line where her answer stops making sense.**

```
 1  Ana needs 2.4 L of paint for her wall.
 2  The paint is sold in 1 L tins.
 3  2.4 rounds to 2, so she buys 2 tins.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: 2.4 does round to 2, but 2 tins hold only 2 L, not enough. Here she must round up: 3 tins.

:::


:::keep-together

### Fuel use

**A car uses 7 L of petrol per 100 km. How many litres does a 350 km trip use?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 24.5

350 km is 3.5 lots of 100 km: 3.5 × 7 = 24.5 L.

## Models


:::keep-together

### Paint

**5 L of paint at 12 m² per litre covers how many square metres?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

5 × 12 = 60 m².

:::


:::keep-together

### Cordial

**Cordial is mixed 1 : 4 with water. How many litres of cordial are in 3 L of drink?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.6

5 parts make 3 L, so one part is 0.6 L of cordial.

:::


:::keep-together

### Buses

**A bus seats 45 people. How many buses are needed for 200 people?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

200 ÷ 45 = 4.4…, and a part bus is still a bus: 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: percentage increase

Answer: {{field-15}}

50 × 1.1 = $55.
