# Ratio and rate models

Number Foundations · Modelling

<!-- Speaker note: Students model practical problems as a ratio or a rate (paint coverage, cordial, fuel, sharing, filling), solve them and interpret the answer in the situation (AC9M8M07, node 1). ## Today

**We are learning to** model a practical problem: say what you need, decide whether it is a ratio (parts of the same kind, like cordial to water) or a rate (two different units, like m² per litre), set it up, solve, then interpret the answer in the situation, such as rounding up to whole tins.

- I can decide whether a problem needs a ratio or a rate.
- I can set up and solve a ratio or rate model.
- I can interpret the answer in the situation, such as rounding up to whole tins.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need ratios (Year 7) and rates (previous lessons). Wider: ratios in First Nations string and cordage making (fibre lengths and twisting), taught with local knowledge where you can; radiocarbon dating as a ratio of isotopes. -->

## Why it matters

> Painting a room, mixing cordial for a party, filling a pool, sharing the cost of a gift: the maths is easy once you choose the right model and remember what the answer means.

<!-- Speaker note: To **model** a practical problem: 1. **Say what you need to find**, with its units. 2. **Choose the model.** Quantities of the **same kind** (litres of cordial to litres of water) make a **ratio**. Quantities with **different units** (square metres per litre, litres per minute) make a **rate**. 3. **Set it up and solve.** 4. **Interpret** the answer in the situation: paint comes in whole tins, people come in whole numbers, money is to the cent. -->

## Ratio or rate

Here is the routine.

$$\text{need: litres of paint for 20 m}^2$$

1) $\text{12 m}^2 \text{ per L: a rate}$ — Different units, so a rate.
2) $20 \div 12 \approx 1.7 \text{ L}$ — m² ÷ (m² per L) = L.
3) $\text{buy 2 one-litre tins}$ — Interpret: tins are whole, and 1 would not be enough.

## Ratio or rate

- A percentage

- Neither

* Answer: A ratio: both parts are litres of liquid

## Exit ticket

**A recipe uses 2 cups of rice to 3 cups of water. How much water for 5 cups of rice? Is this a ratio or a rate?**

* A ratio (cups to cups): 5 × 1.5 = 7.5 cups of water.
