# Modelling with money

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A $200 bike is 20% off, and then a coupon takes a further 10% off the new price. Is that the same as 30% off?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students model money problems (discounts, rises, markups, profit and sharing) by formulating what is known and unknown, choosing a representation, solving, and interpreting and justifying the answer in dollars (AC9M7N09, nodes 1 to 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what is known and unknown in a money problem and choose how to model it. I can solve problems with fractions, percentages and ratios, including percentages in two steps. I can write my answer in dollars and explain why I chose that representation.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Why is 10% of the new price less than 10% of $200?

:::keep-together

**A $200 bike is 20% off, then a coupon takes 10% off the new price. What is the final price?**

☐ $144

☐ $160

☐ $140

☐ $150

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** $144. 20% off $200 is $160, then 10% off $160 is $144. $140 would be 30% off $200; the 10% is of a smaller amount.

:::

## Formulate, solve, interpret


:::keep-together

### Say what you know

**Known: a $60 jumper is 25% off. Unknown: the sale price. What is it, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 45

25% of $60 = $15, so $45. Or 60 × 0.75 = 45.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ben finds the percentage profit on a scarf. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A scarf costs $5 to make and sells for $8.
 2  Profit = 8 − 5 = $3
 3  Percentage profit = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
 4  Answer: 37.5% profit.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: profit is a percentage of the cost, the original amount: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6 = 60%. Ben divided by the selling price.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which representation?

**Shop A takes $\tfrac{1}{3}$ off a $90 jacket. Shop B takes 30% off. Which is the better deal, and why?**

☐ Shop A: $\tfrac{1}{3}$ is about 33%, more than 30%

☐ They are the same

☐ Shop B: 30 is bigger than 3

☐ You cannot tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Shop A: $\tfrac{1}{3}$ is about 33%, more than 30%

$\tfrac{1}{3}$ of $90 = $30 off; 30% of $90 = $27 off. Writing both as percentages (33⅓% and 30%) makes the comparison immediate.

:::

## Money models


:::keep-together

### Fractions of a prize

**A $240 prize is shared: $\tfrac{1}{4}$ to Ann, and the rest equally among 3 others. How much does each of the other three get, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

Ann gets $60, leaving $180. $180 ÷ 3 = $60 each.

:::


:::keep-together

### Saving up

**A bike costs $400. You have saved 65% of it. How many more dollars do you need?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 140

65% of $400 = $260 saved. $400 − $260 = $140. Or 35% of $400.

:::


:::keep-together

### Bulk buying

**A pack of 12 pens costs $9, while one pen costs $1. What percentage cheaper is each pen in the pack?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 25

12 singles cost $12; the pack costs $9, so each pen costs 75% as much. That is 25% cheaper.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: sharing

**Review: share $84 in the ratio $3 : 4$. What is the bigger share, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 48

3 + 4 = 7 parts. 84 ÷ 7 = 12. The bigger share is 4 × 12 = 48.

:::
