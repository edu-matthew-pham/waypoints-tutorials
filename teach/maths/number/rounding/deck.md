# Rounding decimals

Number Foundations · Place value and estimating

<!-- Speaker note: Students round decimals on a number line to a given number of decimal places, then choose how much to round for a context such as time, money, tins of paint and a ruler's accuracy (AC9M7N05, nodes 1 and 2). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** 3.746 to 2 decimal places is 3.75: look at the next digit, 6, and round up. How much to round depends on what the number is for.

- I can round a decimal to a number of decimal places.
- I can say which digit decides, and why halfway rounds up.
- I can choose how much to round for the job, and explain my choice.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need decimal place value (the place value lesson and Year 6). The one idea: rounding picks the nearer of two numbers with the right number of places, and the digit straight after the place shows which. The common mistakes are cutting digits off (3.74), letting later digits push the deciding digit up (Kai's double rounding), and counting places wrongly (rounding to 1 place when 2 was asked). Node 2 is judgement: rounding to the cent for money, up for tins of paint, to the ruler's accuracy for measurements. Accept any justified answer in class discussion. -->

---

## Why it matters

> A stopwatch says 12.846 seconds. Do you report 12.846, 12.85, 12.8 or 13? It depends who is asking, and what the timer can really measure.

<!-- Speaker note: To **round** a decimal to a number of **decimal places**, find the two numbers with that many places that it sits between, and pick the nearer one. The shortcut is to look at *one* digit, the one straight after the place you are rounding to: - 5 or more: round **up**. - 4 or less: round **down** (the digits stay). So 3.746 to 2 decimal places: the digit after the hundredths is 6, so round up to 3.75. A number exactly halfway rounds up. -->
