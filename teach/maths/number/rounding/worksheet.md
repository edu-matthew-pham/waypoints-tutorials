# Rounding decimals

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A stopwatch says 12.846 seconds. Do you report 12.846, 12.85, 12.8 or 13? It depends who is asking, and what the timer can really measure.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students round decimals on a number line to a given number of decimal places, then choose how much to round for a context such as time, money, tins of paint and a ruler's accuracy (AC9M7N05, nodes 1 and 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can round a decimal to a number of decimal places. I can say which digit decides, and why halfway rounds up. I can choose how much to round for the job, and explain my choice.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Find the mistake

**Kai rounds 2.349 to 1 decimal place. Click the line where the working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Round 2.349 to 1 decimal place.
 2  The 9 rounds the 4 up: 2.349 is about 2.35.
 3  The 5 rounds the 3 up: 2.35 is about 2.4.
 4  Answer: 2.4
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: only the digit straight after the tenths matters, and that is 4, so round down. Kai let the 9 push the 4 up, then rounded again. 2.349 to 1 decimal place is 2.3.

:::
