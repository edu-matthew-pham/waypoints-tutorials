# Prime factors

Number Foundations · Squares, powers and primes

<!-- Speaker note: Students grow factor trees on the board until every branch is prime, write the prime factorisation in exponent notation, check it by expanding, and see that every tree for a number ends with the same primes (AC9M7N02, node 2). ## Today

**We are learning to** split a number into factor pairs until every branch is a prime: 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2² × 3². Every tree for 36 ends with the same primes.

- I can use a factor tree to write a number as a product of primes.
- I can write the prime factorisation with exponents and check it by expanding.
- I can explain why different trees for a number give the same primes.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first, twice, with different first splits. Students need factor pairs, primes (Year 6) and exponent notation (previous lesson). The one idea: split until every branch is prime; the primes are the same whichever way you split (uniqueness, informally). The common mistake is stopping at composite branches (Leo's mistake). This is the hinge for HCF and LCM in the next lesson: the method depends on there being one factorisation to compare. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Every whole number above 1 is built from primes in exactly one way, like a code. 60 is 2² × 3 × 5, and no other number has that code.

<!-- Speaker note: A **prime** has exactly two factors, 1 and itself: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, … (1 is not prime.) Every whole number above 1 that is not prime can be split into primes. Writing it that way, with exponents, is its **prime factorisation**: $$36 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2^2 \times 3^2$$ A **factor tree** finds it: split the number into any factor pair, then split each branch that is not prime, until every branch ends in a prime. -->

## Split, then write with exponents

Here is a tree for 60, written as working.

Now try one: which is the prime factorisation of 60?

$$60 = 6 \times 10$$

1) $6 = 2 \times 3, \quad 10 = 2 \times 5$ — Split every branch that is not prime.
2) $60 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$ — Every branch is prime: collect them, smallest first.
3) $60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5$ — Write repeated primes with exponents. Check: 4 × 3 × 5 = 60.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Line 2: write 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 and say "Every branch is prime: collect them, smallest first." Line 3: write 60 = 2^2 × 3 × 5 and say "Write repeated primes with exponents. ## Split, then write with exponents

**What is the prime factorisation of 60?**

- $2 \times 3 \times 10$
- $2 \times 30$
- $4 \times 15$
- $2^2 \times 3 \times 5$

* Answer: $2^2 \times 3 \times 5$

## Find the mistake

**Leo writes 36 as a product of prime factors. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Write 36 as a product of prime factors.
 2  36 = 4 × 9
 3  4 and 9 are factors of 36, so the tree is finished.
 4  36 = 4 × 9
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: 4 and 9 are not prime, so the tree is not finished. 4 = 2 × 2 and 9 = 3 × 3, so 36 = 2² × 3². -->

## Primes only

**Why is $2 \times 2 \times 9$ not the prime factorisation of 36?**

- The 2s should be written first
- 9 is not prime: it splits into 3 × 3
- It does not equal 36

* Answer: 9 is not prime: it splits into 3 × 3

<!-- Speaker note: Every factor must be prime: 9 = 3 × 3, so 36 = 2² × 3². -->
