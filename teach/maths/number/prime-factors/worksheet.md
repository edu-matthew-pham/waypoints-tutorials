# Prime factors

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Every whole number above 1 is built from primes in exactly one way, like a code. 60 is 2² × 3 × 5, and no other number has that code.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students grow factor trees on the board until every branch is prime, write the prime factorisation in exponent notation, check it by expanding, and see that every tree for a number ends with the same primes (AC9M7N02, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can use a factor tree to write a number as a product of primes. I can write the prime factorisation with exponents and check it by expanding. I can explain why different trees for a number give the same primes.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## All the way down


:::keep-together

### Split, then write with exponents

Here is a tree for 60, written as working.

1. $60 = 6 \times 10$ — Start with any factor pair.
2. $6 = 2 \times 3, \quad 10 = 2 \times 5$ — Split every branch that is not prime.
3. $60 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$ — Every branch is prime: collect them, smallest first.
4. $60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5$ — Write repeated primes with exponents. Check: 4 × 3 × 5 = 60.

Now try one: which is the prime factorisation of 60?

**What is the prime factorisation of 60?**

☐ $2 \times 3 \times 10$

☐ $2 \times 30$

☐ $4 \times 15$

☐ $2^2 \times 3 \times 5$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $2^2 \times 3 \times 5$

60 = 6 × 10 = 2 × 3 × 2 × 5 = 2² × 3 × 5. Check: 4 × 3 × 5 = 60.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Leo writes 36 as a product of prime factors. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Write 36 as a product of prime factors.
 2  36 = 4 × 9
 3  4 and 9 are factors of 36, so the tree is finished.
 4  36 = 4 × 9
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: 4 and 9 are not prime, so the tree is not finished. 4 = 2 × 2 and 9 = 3 × 3, so 36 = 2² × 3².

:::


:::keep-together

### Primes only

**Why is $2 \times 2 \times 9$ not the prime factorisation of 36?**

☐ The 2s should be written first

☐ 9 is not prime: it splits into 3 × 3

☐ It does not equal 36

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9 is not prime: it splits into 3 × 3

Every factor must be prime: 9 = 3 × 3, so 36 = 2² × 3².

## Prime factorisations


:::keep-together

### Ninety

**What is the prime factorisation of 90?**

☐ $2 \times 3 \times 15$

☐ $2 \times 5 \times 9$

☐ $2 \times 3^2 \times 5$

☐ $9 \times 10$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $2 \times 3^2 \times 5$

90 = 9 × 10 = 3 × 3 × 2 × 5 = 2 × 3² × 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Back to the number

**Which number has the prime factorisation $2^3 \times 5$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40

2³ = 8, and 8 × 5 = 40.

### One hundred

**What is the prime factorisation of 100?**

☐ $2 \times 5^2$

☐ $4 \times 25$

☐ $2^2 \times 5^2$

☐ $10^2$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $2^2 \times 5^2$

100 = 10 × 10 = 2 × 5 × 2 × 5 = 2² × 5².
