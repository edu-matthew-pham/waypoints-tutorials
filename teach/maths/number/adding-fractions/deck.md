# Adding and subtracting fractions

Number Foundations · Fractions and decimals

<!-- Speaker note: Students cut two fraction bars into the same number of parts to add and subtract fractions with different denominators, use the LCM as the lowest common denominator, and extend the idea to mixed numerals and decimals (AC9M7N06, node 1). ## Today

**We are learning to** parts can be counted together only when they are the same size. Cut both fractions into the same parts first: 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. The lowest common multiple of the bottoms is the smallest choice.

- I can add and subtract fractions with different denominators using a common denominator.
- I can add and subtract mixed numerals and decimals.
- I can explain why the tops and the bottoms are not simply added.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need equivalent fractions (Equivalent fractions) and the LCM (HCF and LCM). ## Why it matters

> A recipe needs 1/3 of a cup of milk and 1/4 of a cup of cream. Will it all fit in a half-cup measure? You cannot tell until the parts are the same size.

<!-- Speaker note: You can add 2 apples and 3 apples, but 2 apples and 3 pears do not make 5 of anything. Fractions are the same: $\tfrac{2}{7} + \tfrac{3}{7} = \tfrac{5}{7}$ (sevenths and sevenths), but thirds and quarters are different sizes. To add or subtract fractions with different bottoms, cut both into the **same parts** first, using equivalent fractions. The new bottom is a **common denominator**: a common multiple of the two bottoms, and the lowest common multiple is the smallest. -->

## Same parts, then count

Here is the method, then a mixed numeral and a decimal.

Now try one: $\tfrac{2}{5} + \tfrac{1}{3}$.

$$\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{4}{12} + \tfrac{3}{12}$$

1) $= \tfrac{7}{12}$ — Same parts: add the tops, keep the bottom.
2) $2\tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{10}{4} - \tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{7}{4} = 1\tfrac{3}{4}$ — A mixed numeral: write it as quarters first.
3) $3.45 + 2.70 = 6.15$ — Decimals: line up the points (the same parts: tenths, hundredths).

## Find the mistake

**Ella adds $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3}$. Click the line where her method goes wrong.**

```
 1  Work out 1/2 + 1/3.
 2  Add the tops and add the bottoms.
 3  1 + 1 = 2 and 2 + 3 = 5
 4  So 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: halves and thirds are different sizes, so they cannot be counted together. 1/2 = 3/6 and 1/3 = 2/6: 5/6. (2/5 is less than 1/2 on its own.) -->
