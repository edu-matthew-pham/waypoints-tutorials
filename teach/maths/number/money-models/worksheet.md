# Money models

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** People sometimes turn down a pay rise because it 'puts them in a higher tax bracket'. Do they lose money? A tax model answers it.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students model pay and income tax in brackets, compare marginal and effective rates, and judge what a simple tax model leaves out (AC9M8N05, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can work out income tax across brackets. I can explain the difference between the marginal and effective rate. I can model pay with a rate, hours and overtime, and check the answer makes sense.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Marginal and effective


:::keep-together

### Tax on $30 000

Here is the routine.

1. $60\,000 - 45\,000 = 15\,000$ — The part in the 30% bracket.
2. $26\,800 \times 0.15 = 4020$ — The part from $18 200 to $45 000, at 15%.
3. $15\,000 \times 0.30 = 4500$ — The top part, at 30%.
4. $4020 + 4500 = 8520$ — Add the parts: the tax.
5. $8520 \div 60\,000 = 14.2\%$ — The effective rate.

**What is the tax on an income of $30 000, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1770

30 000 − 18 200 = 11 800, and 15% of 11 800 = $1770.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Jay works out his tax. Click the line where his model goes wrong.**

```
 1  Jay earns $50 000 a year.
 2  His income reaches the 30% bracket.
 3  So his tax is 30% of $50 000, which is $15 000.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: only the part above $45 000 is taxed at 30%. Tax = 15% of 26 800 + 30% of 5000 = 4020 + 1500 = $5520.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two rates

**Someone earns $60 000. Why is their effective tax rate (14.2%) lower than their marginal rate (30%)?**

☐ Because of the Medicare levy

☐ Effective rates are always half the marginal rate

☐ The marginal rate is wrong

☐ Most of their income is taxed at 0% or 15%; only the top part is taxed at 30%

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Most of their income is taxed at 0% or 15%; only the top part is taxed at 30%

The lower brackets pull the average down.

## Money models


:::keep-together

### Tax on $100 000

**What is the tax on $100 000, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20520

4020 + 30% of 55 000 = 4020 + 16 500 = $20 520.

:::


:::keep-together

### Effective rate

**What is the effective tax rate on $100 000, as a percentage to 2 decimal places?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20.52

20 520 ÷ 100 000 = 20.52%.

:::


:::keep-together

### Improve the model

**How could this tax model be improved to match a real tax bill?**

☐ Remove the tax-free part

☐ Use one rate for everyone

☐ Add the Medicare levy and tax offsets

☐ Tax everything at the top rate

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Add the Medicare levy and tax offsets

The model leaves out the Medicare levy (usually 2%) and offsets, so real tax is a bit different.

### An overdrawn account

**A bank account is overdrawn: its balance is −$120. Then $85 is paid in and a $40 fee is taken. What is the balance now, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -75

−120 + 85 − 40 = −75: still overdrawn, by $75.

:::keep-together

**Tax is 0% on the first $18 200 of income, 15% on the next part up to $45 000, and 30% on the part from $45 000 to $135 000. Find the tax on $50 000.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15% of (45 000 − 18 200) = 15% of 26 800 = $4020, plus 30% of 5000 = $1500: $5520 in all.
