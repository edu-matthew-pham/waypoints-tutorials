:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place positive and negative fractions, decimals and mixed numerals on number lines split into halves, quarters and tenths, including lines without zero in the middle, and order them (AC9M7N04, node 3).

### Placing −3/4 and −1 1/4

Here is how to place a negative fraction and a negative mixed numeral.

1. $-\tfrac{3}{4}$ — The denominator is 4, so split each unit into 4 equal parts (quarters).
2. $\text{count 3 quarters left from } 0$ — Negative means left of zero.
3. $-\tfrac{3}{4} \text{ is between } -1 \text{ and } 0$ — It is 3 of the 4 quarters from 0 to $-1$.
4. $-1\tfrac{1}{4} = -1 - \tfrac{1}{4}$ — A negative mixed numeral is a whole number and a part, both below zero.
5. $\text{count 1 unit left, then 1 quarter further left}$ — The quarter is further from zero, not back towards it.
6. $-1\tfrac{1}{4} \text{ is between } -2 \text{ and } -1$ — Just past $-1$, a quarter of the way to $-2$.

Then: which is less, $-\tfrac{3}{4}$ or $-\tfrac{1}{4}$?

**Which is less, $-\tfrac{3}{4}$ or $-\tfrac{1}{4}$?**

☐ $-\tfrac{1}{4}$

☐ $-\tfrac{3}{4}$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $-\tfrac{3}{4}$

−3/4 is three quarters left of 0, further left than −1/4, so it is less.
