# What is a ratio?

Number Foundations · Ratios

<!-- Speaker note: Students build ratios from rows of equal blocks, write part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios in the order asked, and link part to whole to a fraction (AC9M7N08, node 1). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** 2 red counters and 3 blue: the ratio of red to blue is 2 : 3 (part to part); red to all the counters is 2 : 5 (part to whole). Order matters.

- I can write a ratio of two quantities in a : b form, in the order asked.
- I can tell part to part from part to whole.
- I can write a part-to-whole ratio as a fraction.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need fractions as parts of a whole (Equivalent fractions). The one idea: a ratio compares quantities; the order in which they are named is the order of the numbers. The common mistakes are writing part to part when part to whole was asked (Pip), reversing the order, and using the whole (5) as a part. Use the same bag all lesson so students see 2 : 3 and 2 : 5 about the same objects. -->

---

## Why it matters

> A fruit punch is 2 cups of juice for every 3 cups of lemonade. Swap them and it tastes completely different. How do we write a recipe like that down?

<!-- Speaker note: A **ratio** compares two (or more) quantities of the same kind. "2 red to 3 blue" is written $2 : 3$. The order matters: red first means 2 comes first. There are two kinds of comparison: - **Part to part:** red to blue is $2 : 3$. - **Part to whole:** red to *all* the counters is $2 : 5$, because there are $2 + 3 = 5$ counters in all. This is the same as the fraction $\tfrac{2}{5}$. -->

## Find the mistake

**Pip finds the ratio of apples to all the fruit. Click the line where the working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A bowl has 4 apples and 3 pears. Find the ratio of apples to all the fruit.
 2  There are 4 + 3 = 7 pieces of fruit altogether.
 3  Apples to all the fruit: 4 : 3
 4  So 4 of every 7 pieces are apples.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: 4 : 3 is apples to pears. Apples to all the fruit is 4 : 7, the 7 from line 2. -->
