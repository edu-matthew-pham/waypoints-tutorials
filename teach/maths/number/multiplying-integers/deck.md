# Multiplying and dividing integers

Number Foundations · Integers

<!-- Speaker note: Students find the sign rules for multiplying and dividing integers from the pattern a × (−2) and from jumps on a number line (a negative number of jumps goes the opposite way), and apply them, including powers of −1 (AC9M8N04, node 1). ## Today

**We are learning to** a × b is a jumps of b from zero. A negative number of jumps goes the opposite way, so a negative times a negative is positive. Division undoes it with the same signs.

- I can multiply and divide integers, including two negatives.
- I can explain why a negative times a negative is positive, from a pattern or the number line.
- I can tell the rule for multiplying apart from the rule for adding negatives.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need Adding and subtracting integers (subtracting goes the opposite way), which this lesson echoes: a negative number of jumps goes the opposite way. Two justifications are offered: the pattern (3 × (−2), 2 × (−2), … goes up by 2 each time, so it keeps going past zero) and the line. Use whichever lands; the debt model (taking away 3 debts of $2 leaves you $6 better off) is a good third in class. The common mistake is to use the addition rule for multiplication ("two negatives make a bigger negative"); it is planted after the method, in Kai's working, and the review question puts −4 + (−3) next to −4 × (−3). ## Why it matters

> A tank loses 3 litres an hour. Three hours ago, did it hold more or less than now? A negative rate times a negative time: the answer is a positive change.

## What happened?

* 6. 3 jumps of −2 would go left to −6; a negative number of jumps goes the opposite way, to 6. The pattern 3 × (−2) = −6, 2 × (−2) = −4, 1 × (−2) = −2, 0 × (−2) = 0 goes up by 2 each time, so −1 × (−2) = 2 and −3 × (−2) = 6.

## Signs from the pattern

Here is how the sign rules come from the pattern and the line.

Then: which is $-12 \div 3$?

$$3 \times (-2) = -6$$

1) $-3 \times (-2) = 6$ — A negative number of jumps goes the opposite way: right 6.
2) $\text{same signs} \to +$ — $3 \times 2 = 6$ and $-3 \times (-2) = 6$.
3) $\text{different signs} \to -$ — $3 \times (-2) = -6$ and $-3 \times 2 = -6$.
4) $-12 \div 3 = -4$ — Dividing undoes multiplying: $3 \times (-4) = -12$, so the same rule holds.
5) $-12 \div (-3) = 4$ — Same signs, positive: check $-3 \times 4 = -12$.

## Find the mistake

**Kai works out $-4 \times (-3)$. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Work out −4 × −3.
 2  4 × 3 = 12.
 3  Both numbers are negative, so the answer is negative, like −4 + −3.
 4  So −4 × −3 = −12.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: that is the rule for adding (−4 + −3 = −7). For multiplying, two negatives give a positive: −4 × (−3) = 12. -->
