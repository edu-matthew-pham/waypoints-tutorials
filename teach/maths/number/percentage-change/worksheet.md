# Percentage change

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A shop puts a price up 20% in March and has a 20% sale in April. The sign says 'back to the old price'. Is it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students model percentage increases and decreases as multipliers, chain two changes, and explain why equal percentages up and down do not cancel (AC9M8N05, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can use a multiplier for a percentage increase or decrease. I can work out the result of two changes, one after the other. I can explain why up 20% then down 20% is not back to the start.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Multipliers


:::keep-together

### Multipliers

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{up } 20\%$ — \times 1.2
2. $\text{down } 20\%$ — \times 0.8
3. $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$ — Multiply the multipliers.
4. $0.96 = 96\%$ — 4% less than the start: an overall decrease of 4%.

**What do you multiply by for a 15% increase?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1.15

100% + 15% = 115% = 1.15.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam works out the jacket's final price. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A $200 jacket goes up 10%, then the new price goes down 10%.
 2  Up 10% adds 10% of $200, which is $20, so it costs $220.
 3  Down 10% takes off $20, so it costs $200 again.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the 10% decrease is 10% of the new price, $220, which is $22. The jacket costs $198.

:::


:::keep-together

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 92

### Decrease multiplier

**What do you multiply by for a 35% decrease?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.65

100% − 35% = 65% = 0.65.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two steps

**A $250 bike goes up 20%, then has a 10% discount. What does it cost now, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 270

250 × 1.2 = 300, then 300 × 0.9 = $270.

:::


:::keep-together
