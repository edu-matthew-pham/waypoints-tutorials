# Percentage change

Number Foundations · Modelling

<!-- Speaker note: Students model percentage increases and decreases as multipliers, chain two changes, and explain why equal percentages up and down do not cancel (AC9M8N05, node 1). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 8 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** an increase of p% multiplies by (1 + p/100); a decrease multiplies by (1 − p/100). One change after another means multiplying the multipliers, so up 20% then down 20% is × 1.2 × 0.8 = × 0.96: a 4% fall overall, not back to the start.

- I can use a multiplier for a percentage increase or decrease.
- I can work out the result of two changes, one after the other.
- I can explain why up 20% then down 20% is not back to the start.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need percentages of amounts and increases and decreases (Number Foundations). The board draws the three amounts as bars with each multiplier between them; the Mark-up task uses the percentage bar. ## Why it matters

> A shop puts a price up 20% in March and has a 20% sale in April. The sign says 'back to the old price'. Is it?

<!-- Speaker note: A percentage change is a multiplication by a **multiplier**: - an **increase** of $p\%$: multiply by $1 + \frac{p}{100}$ (up 15%: × 1.15); - a **decrease** of $p\%$: multiply by $1 - \frac{p}{100}$ (down 15%: × 0.85). When one change follows another, the second is a percentage of the **new** amount, so multiply the multipliers: up 20% then down 20% is $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$, a 4% decrease overall. Ask "a percentage of what?" every time. -->

## Multipliers

Here is the routine.

$$\text{up } 20\%$$

1) $\text{down } 20\%$ — \times 0.8
2) $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$ — Multiply the multipliers.
3) $0.96 = 96\%$ — 4% less than the start: an overall decrease of 4%.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Start: write up 20\% and say "× 1.2." Line 1: write down 20\% and say "× 0.8." Line 2: write 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 and say "Multiply the multipliers." Line 3: write 0.96 = 96\% and say "4% less than the start: an overall decrease of 4%." -->

## Find the mistake

**Sam works out the jacket's final price. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A $200 jacket goes up 10%, then the new price goes down 10%.
 2  Up 10% adds 10% of $200, which is $20, so it costs $220.
 3  Down 10% takes off $20, so it costs $200 again.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the 10% decrease is 10% of the new price, $220, which is $22. The jacket costs $198. -->
