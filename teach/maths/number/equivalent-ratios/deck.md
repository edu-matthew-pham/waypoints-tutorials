# Equivalent and simplest ratios

Number Foundations · Ratios

<!-- Speaker note: Students make copies of a ratio on the board to see that multiplying both terms keeps the ratio, find missing terms, and simplify by dividing both terms by a common factor (AC9M7N08, node 2). ## Today

**We are learning to** 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9: multiply or divide both numbers by the same number. 12 : 18 simplifies to 2 : 3 by dividing both by 6.

- I can find an equivalent ratio by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number.
- I can write a ratio in simplest form.
- I can scale a recipe up using equivalent ratios.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need equivalent fractions (the same multiplying and dividing) and highest common factors. The one idea: equivalent ratios are copies of the pattern: both terms multiplied (or divided) by the same number. The common mistake is additive thinking (Ana: 2 → 6 is +4, so 3 → 7). This lesson is the hinge for sharing in a ratio: students who cannot scale and simplify will struggle next. Link to equivalent fractions: 2 : 3 behaves like 2/3 for scaling, but a ratio is not a fraction (part to part). -->

## Why it matters

> A recipe for 4 uses 2 cups of flour and 3 of milk. How much milk do you need for 10 cups of flour?

<!-- Speaker note: Doubling a recipe keeps the taste the same. Two ratios are **equivalent** when one is the other with *both* numbers multiplied (or both divided) by the same number: $$2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9 \qquad (\times 2,\ \times 3)$$ A ratio is in **simplest form** when no number greater than 1 divides both terms. To simplify, divide both by their highest common factor: $12 : 18$, divide by 6, gives $2 : 3$. It is the same idea as equivalent fractions. Adding the same number to both terms does **not** keep the ratio: $2 : 3$ is not $4 : 5$. -->

## Find the mistake

**Ana scales a recipe. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 3 cups of milk.
 2  For 6 cups of flour, how much milk?
 3  Flour went from 2 to 6: that is + 4.
 4  So milk goes from 3 to 7: 3 + 4.
 5  Answer: 7 cups of milk.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: adding 4 to the milk too is wrong. Flour went × 3 (2 to 6), so milk goes × 3: 3 × 3 = 9 cups. -->
