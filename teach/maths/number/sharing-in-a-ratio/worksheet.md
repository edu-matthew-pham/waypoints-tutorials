# Sharing in a ratio

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Ana and Ben earn $60 washing cars. Ana did 2 cars, Ben did 3. They agree to share the money in that ratio. How much does each get?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students share a total in a given ratio by cutting it into equal parts (add the parts, find one part, multiply), with two and three people, and work backwards from a share to the total (AC9M7N08, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can share an amount in a given ratio, with two or three people. I can find the total number of parts and the value of one part. I can work backwards from one share to the other share or the total.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Parts first


:::keep-together

### One part

Here is the whole routine for $60 in the ratio 2 : 3.

1. $2 + 3 = 5$ — Add the parts of the ratio.
2. $\$60 \div 5 = \$12$ — One part is the total divided by the number of parts.
3. $\text{Ana: } 2 \times \$12 = \$24$ — Each share is its number of parts times one part.
4. $\text{Ben: } 3 \times \$12 = \$36$ — Ben has 3 parts.
5. $\$24 + \$36 = \$60$ — Check that the shares add back to the total.

Now try one: $40 in the ratio 1 : 3.

**$40 is shared in the ratio $1 : 3$. How much is one part, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10

1 + 3 = 4 parts. $40 ÷ 4 = $10 for one part.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Dee shares $60 between Ana and Ben in the ratio 2 : 3. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Share $60 between Ana and Ben in the ratio 2 : 3.
 2  Ana: 60 ÷ 2 = $30
 3  Ben: 60 ÷ 3 = $20
 4  Check: 30 + 20 = $50, near enough to 60.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: dividing $60 by Ana's 2 does not give her share. First add the parts (2 + 3 = 5), find one part ($12), then Ana has 2 × $12 = $24. The check on line 4 shows the shares do not add to $60.

:::


:::keep-together

### The bigger share

**35 stickers are shared in the ratio $3 : 4$. How many stickers are in the bigger share?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20

3 + 4 = 7 parts. 35 ÷ 7 = 5 stickers a part. The bigger share is 4 × 5 = 20.

## Sharing in a ratio


:::keep-together

### The bigger share

**$72 is shared in the ratio $5 : 4$. What is the bigger share, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40

5 + 4 = 9 parts. 72 ÷ 9 = 8. The bigger share is 5 × 8 = 40.

:::


:::keep-together

### The smaller share

**45 marbles are shared in the ratio $2 : 3$. How many are in the smaller share?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 18

2 + 3 = 5 parts. 45 ÷ 5 = 9. The smaller share is 2 × 9 = 18.

:::


:::keep-together

### Three people

**$120 is shared among three people in the ratio $1 : 2 : 3$. What is the largest share, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

1 + 2 + 3 = 6 parts. 120 ÷ 6 = 20. The largest has 3 parts: 60.

:::


:::keep-together
