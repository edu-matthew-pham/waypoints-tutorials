# Sharing in a ratio

Number Foundations · Ratios

<!-- Speaker note: Students share a total in a given ratio by cutting it into equal parts (add the parts, find one part, multiply), with two and three people, and work backwards from a share to the total (AC9M7N08, node 3). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** share $60 in the ratio 2 : 3. Add the parts (5), find one part ($60 ÷ 5 = $12), then multiply: $24 and $36.

- I can share an amount in a given ratio, with two or three people.
- I can find the total number of parts and the value of one part.
- I can work backwards from one share to the other share or the total.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need equivalent ratios (previous lesson) and division. The one idea is the three-step routine: add the parts, divide the total by the number of parts to get one part, then multiply by each person's number. Insist on the check that the shares add back to the total. The commonest mistake is dividing the total by each term (Dee, $30 and $20), which is caught by the check. Working backwards (Modify) finds one part from the known share first. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Ana and Ben earn $60 washing cars. Ana did 2 cars, Ben did 3. They agree to share the money in that ratio. How much does each get?

<!-- Speaker note: To **share a total in a ratio**, think of the total cut into equal **parts**: 1. **Add the parts:** $2 + 3 = 5$ parts. 2. **Find one part:** $\$60 \div 5 = \$12$. 3. **Multiply:** Ana gets $2 \times \$12 = \$24$, Ben $3 \times \$12 = \$36$. Check: $\$24 + \$36 = \$60$. The shares must add to the total. -->

## One part

Here is the whole routine for $60 in the ratio 2 : 3.

Now try one: $40 in the ratio 1 : 3.

$$2 + 3 = 5$$

1) $\$60 \div 5 = \$12$ — One part is the total divided by the number of parts.
2) $\text{Ana: } 2 \times \$12 = \$24$ — Each share is its number of parts times one part.
3) $\text{Ben: } 3 \times \$12 = \$36$ — Ben has 3 parts.
4) $\$24 + \$36 = \$60$ — Check that the shares add back to the total.

## Find the mistake

**Dee shares $60 between Ana and Ben in the ratio 2 : 3. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Share $60 between Ana and Ben in the ratio 2 : 3.
 2  Ana: 60 ÷ 2 = $30
 3  Ben: 60 ÷ 3 = $20
 4  Check: 30 + 20 = $50, near enough to 60.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: dividing $60 by Ana's 2 does not give her share. First add the parts (2 + 3 = 5), find one part ($12), then Ana has 2 × $12 = $24. The check on line 4 shows the shares do not add to $60. -->
