# Modelling with ratios

Number Foundations · Modelling

<!-- Speaker note: Students model practical problems with ratios (mixes, scale drawings, models), say what is known and unknown before calculating, solve by finding one part or the scale factor, and answer in the situation's units (AC9M7M06, nodes 1 and 2). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** mixes, scale drawings and models: say what is known and unknown, choose the ratio, solve, and answer in the units of the situation.

- I can say what is known and unknown in a ratio problem before I calculate.
- I can solve mixing and scale problems with ratios.
- I can give my answer with the right units and check that it makes sense.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need sharing in a ratio and unit conversions between mm, cm, m and L, mL. The one idea: ratio problems in measurement are the same structure as sharing in a ratio, plus scale (every 1 unit on the drawing is n units in real life). The modelling habit is the point: formulate, solve, interpret. Common mistakes are dividing by one term (Raj), multiplying when scaling down (and dividing when scaling up), and leaving the answer in the wrong unit. Let students choose their own context for the Make task. A First Nations context can be added by the teacher: land and resource proportions, with local advice. -->

## Why it matters

> A builder mixes mortar as 1 part cement to 3 parts sand. She needs 20 litres of mix. How much of each does she need to buy?

<!-- Speaker note: **Modelling** means turning a real problem into maths you can solve, solving it, and turning the answer back into the real situation. Three steps keep you on track: 1. **Formulate:** What is *known* (the ratio, the total)? What is *unknown*? What is the *relationship* (parts of a ratio)? 2. **Solve:** Find one part, then each amount. Estimate first to know what to expect. 3. **Interpret:** Give the answer in the situation's own units, with a sensible amount of rounding, and check that it makes sense. Ratios turn up in mixes (mortar, paint, fertiliser), scale drawings and maps (1 : 50), and models. -->

## Find the mistake

**Raj mixes fertiliser and water in the ratio 1 : 4 to make 2 L. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mix fertiliser and water in the ratio 1 : 4 to make 2 L.
 2  Fertiliser = 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5 L
 3  Water = 2 − 0.5 = 1.5 L
 4  Answer: 0.5 L of fertiliser.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: 2 ÷ 4 divides by the water's number. There are 1 + 4 = 5 parts, so one part is 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 L: fertiliser 0.4 L and water 1.6 L. -->

## What first?

**Orange paint is red and yellow in the ratio 2 : 3. You need 10 L. What should you do first?**

- Multiply 2 × 3 × 10
- Divide 10 by 2
- Say what is known (ratio, total 10 L) and what is unknown (litres of red and yellow)
- Start mixing and see

* Answer: Say what is known (ratio, total 10 L) and what is unknown (litres of red and yellow)

<!-- Speaker note: Known: ratio 2 : 3 and total 10 L. Unknown: litres of each. Relationship: 5 equal parts of 2 L, so red is 4 L and yellow 6 L. -->

## Exit ticket

**A recipe mixes juice and water 2 : 7. How much juice is in a 900 mL drink?**

* 2 + 7 = 9 parts. 900 ÷ 9 = 100 mL a part. Juice is 200 mL.
