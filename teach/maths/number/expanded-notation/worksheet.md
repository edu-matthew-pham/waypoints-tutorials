## Columns and powers


:::keep-together

### Read the powers

The board shows the columns. Here is 4052 written out.

1. $4052 = 4000 + 0 + 50 + 2$ — Each digit is worth its digit times its column.
2. $4000 = 4 \times 1000 = 4 \times 10^3$ — The thousands column is 10³.
3. $50 = 5 \times 10 = 5 \times 10^1$ — The tens column is 10¹.
4. $2 = 2 \times 1 = 2 \times 10^0$ — The ones column is 10⁰.
5. $4052 = 4 \times 10^3 + 5 \times 10^1 + 2 \times 10^0$ — The 0 in the hundreds adds nothing, so we leave it out.

Now try one: which power goes with the 5?

**In 4052, the 5 is in the tens column. What is the exponent of its power of 10? (The 5 is worth $5 \times 10^{?}$.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1

The ones column is 10⁰, the tens column 10¹, the hundreds 10². So 5 tens is 5 × 10¹ = 50.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam writes 4052 in expanded notation. Click the line where the working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Write 4052 in expanded notation.
 2  4 is in the thousands: 4 × 10³
 3  0 is in the hundreds: nothing to add
 4  5 is in the tens: 5 × 10²
 5  2 is in the ones: 2 × 10¹
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: the tens column is 10¹, not 10². Sam counted the ones column as 10¹. The ones column is 10⁰.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why 10⁰?

**Why is $10^0 = 1$?**

☐ It is not allowed

☐ It is the ones column: 1 one, so the power is 1

☐ Any number to the power 0 is 0

☐ 10⁰ means 10 × 0

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It is the ones column: 1 one, so the power is 1

Each step down divides by 10: 1000, 100, 10, then 1. So 10⁰ = 1, the value of the ones column.

## Expanded notation


:::keep-together

### The value of a digit

**What is the value of the 2 in 52 300?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2000

The 2 is in the thousands column: 2 × 10³ = 2000.

:::

### Back to a number

**Write $6 \times 10^5 + 4 \times 10^2$ as a number.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 600400

600 000 + 400 = 600 400.

:::


:::keep-together

### Times 100

**Why is $6300 \times 100$ the same as $6300 \times 10^2$, and why is it easy?**

☐ × 10² means × 10 × 2 = × 20

☐ It is not the same

☐ 10² = 100, and × 10² moves every digit two columns left (add two zeros)

☐ 10² = 20, so it doubles

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10² = 100, and × 10² moves every digit two columns left (add two zeros)

10² = 100. Multiplying by 100 moves each digit two columns left: 630 000.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: powers

**Review: what is $2^5$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 32

### Millions

**Write 3 000 000 as $3 \times 10^{?}$. What is the exponent?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

The exponent counts the zeros: 3 000 000 = 3 × 10⁶, and 10⁶ is the millions column.
