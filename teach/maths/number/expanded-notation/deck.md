# Place value and powers of 10

Number Foundations · Place value and estimating

## Today

**We are learning to** each place value column is a power of 10: the thousands column is 10³, the ones column is 10⁰ = 1. So 4052 = 4 × 10³ + 5 × 10¹ + 2 × 10⁰.

- I can say which power of 10 each place value column is.
- I can write a number in expanded notation using powers of 10.
- I can turn expanded notation back into a number.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need exponent notation (Powers lesson) and place value for whole numbers (Year 6). The common mistake is counting from 1 (Sam), so every exponent is one too big. The empty column (a 0 digit) may be left out of expanded notation; writing 0 × 10² is also correct, and this lesson's answers leave it out. ## Why it matters

> The 4 in 4052 is worth 4000, but the 5 is worth only 50. What does the column a digit sits in do to its value?

<!-- Speaker note: You already know that the 5 in 4052 is worth 50, not 5. A number is built from its digits, each worth *digit × its column*. Every column is ten times the one to its right, so every column is a **power of 10**: | Column | ones | tens | hundreds | thousands | ten thousands | | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | | Power of 10 | $10^0$ | $10^1$ | $10^2$ | $10^3$ | $10^4$ | | Value | 1 | 10 | 100 | 1000 | 10 000 | The exponent counts the zeros in the value: $10^3 = 1000$ has three zeros. Writing a number as a sum of *digit × power of 10* is called **expanded notation**. -->

## Read the powers

The board shows the columns. Here is 4052 written out.

Now try one: which power goes with the 5?

$$4052 = 4000 + 0 + 50 + 2$$

1) $4000 = 4 \times 1000 = 4 \times 10^3$ — The thousands column is 10³.
2) $50 = 5 \times 10 = 5 \times 10^1$ — The tens column is 10¹.
3) $2 = 2 \times 1 = 2 \times 10^0$ — The ones column is 10⁰.
4) $4052 = 4 \times 10^3 + 5 \times 10^1 + 2 \times 10^0$ — The 0 in the hundreds adds nothing, so we leave it out.

## Why 10⁰?

**Why is $10^0 = 1$?**

- It is not allowed
- It is the ones column: 1 one, so the power is 1
- Any number to the power 0 is 0
- 10⁰ means 10 × 0

* Answer: It is the ones column: 1 one, so the power is 1

<!-- Speaker note: Each step down divides by 10: 1000, 100, 10, then 1. So 10⁰ = 1, the value of the ones column. -->

## Times 100

**Why is $6300 \times 100$ the same as $6300 \times 10^2$, and why is it easy?**

- × 10² means × 10 × 2 = × 20
- It is not the same
- 10² = 100, and × 10² moves every digit two columns left (add two zeros)
- 10² = 20, so it doubles

* Answer: 10² = 100, and × 10² moves every digit two columns left (add two zeros)
