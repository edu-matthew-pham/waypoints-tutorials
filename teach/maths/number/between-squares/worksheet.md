# Between the squares

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A square vegetable patch needs 43 m² of soil. No whole number squares to 43, but a builder still needs to know the side, to the nearest 10 cm.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students bound a square root between two whole numbers using the perfect squares either side, estimate it to one decimal place by squaring tenths on a grid board, and solve area and perimeter problems with roots (AC9M7N01, nodes 3 and 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can say which two whole numbers a square root is between, with a reason. I can estimate a square root to one decimal place by squaring decimals. I can solve problems with the side, area and perimeter of a square.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The squares either side


:::keep-together

### Bound it, then get closer

Here is the method for $\sqrt{43}$.

1. $36 < 43 < 49$ — Find the perfect squares either side of 43.
2. $6 < \sqrt{43} < 7$ — Take their roots: √43 is between 6 and 7.
3. $6.5^2 = 42.25 \quad 6.6^2 = 43.56$ — Square some tenths. 6.5 is under and 6.6 is over.
4. $\sqrt{43} \approx 6.6$ — 43.56 is closer to 43 (0.56 over) than 42.25 (0.75 under).

Now try one: between which whole numbers is $\sqrt{70}$?

**$\sqrt{70}$ is between 8 and which whole number?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9

8² = 64 and 9² = 81, and 70 is between them, so √70 is between 8 and 9.

:::


:::keep-together

### With reasons

**A square garden bed has area 90 m². Between which two whole numbers of metres is its side? Write each step with its reason.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $9^2 = 81$ and $10^2 = 100$ (these are the perfect squares either side of 90); $81 &lt; 90 &lt; 100$, so $9 &lt; \sqrt{90} &lt; 10$ (a bigger square has a bigger side)

81 &lt; 90 &lt; 100, so the side is between 9 m and 10 m (about 9.5 m: 9.5² = 90.25).

:::
