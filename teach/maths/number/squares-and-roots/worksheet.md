## Squaring and its inverse


:::keep-together

### Undo the square

The square root asks: what number, times itself, makes this?

1. $6^2 = 6 \times 6 = 36$ — Squaring: multiply the number by itself (a square with side 6).
2. $\sqrt{36} = 6$ — The square root goes back: 6 × 6 = 36, so the side is 6.
3. $1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36,\ 49,\ 64,\ 81,\ 100,\ 121,\ 144$ — The perfect squares from 1² to 12². Learn them.

Now try one: $\sqrt{81}$.

**Work out $\sqrt{81}$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9

9 × 9 = 81, so √81 = 9.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ava finds the area of a square with side 5 cm. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A square has side 5 cm. Find its area.
 2  Area = side squared.
 3  5² = 5 × 2 = 10
 4  So the area is 10 cm².
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: 5² means 5 × 5 = 25, not 5 × 2. The area is 25 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Why it works

**Why is $\sqrt{64} = 8$?**

☐ Because $8 \times 8 = 64$

☐ Because 64 is even

☐ Because $64 \div 8 = 8$ and $64 \div 2 = 32$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Because $8 \times 8 = 64$

The square root of 64 is the number that times itself makes 64: 8 × 8 = 64.
