## Predict

**A triangle has angles 30°, 60° and 90°. It is enlarged with a scale factor of 2. What are the angles of the enlarged triangle?**

- 15°, 30° and 45°
- 30°, 60° and 90°
- 60°, 120° and 180°
- 30°, 60° and 120°

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: 30°, 60° and 90°. An enlargement keeps the angles; only the lengths change. Angles in a triangle must still add to 180°, which the doubled angles (360°) would not. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What happened?

* 30°, 60° and 90°. An enlargement keeps the angles; only the lengths change. Angles in a triangle must still add to 180°, which the doubled angles (360°) would not.

## Kept and changed

$$\text{angles: } 40°, 60°, 80° \to 40°, 60°, 80°$$

1) $\text{sides: } 3, 4, 5 \to 6, 8, 10$ — Changed: every side is multiplied by 2.
2) $\text{perimeter: } 12 \to 24$ — Also multiplied by 2.
3) $\text{area: } 6 \to 24$ — Changed by more: 2 × 2 = 4 times bigger.

## Kept and changed

**A rectangle is 4 cm by 3 cm. It is enlarged by a scale factor of 3. How long is the longer side now? (cm)**

* Answer: 12

**Which of these does an enlargement keep?**

- Side lengths

```
 1  The triangle has angles 40°, 60° and 80°.
 2  It is enlarged with a scale factor of 2.
 3  So the new angles are 80°, 120° and 160°.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the angles stay at 40°, 60°, 80° because the shape is the same. If all three doubled they would add to 360°. -->
