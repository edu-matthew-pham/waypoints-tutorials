# Classifying triangles

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Roof trusses, bridges and set squares are all triangles, but not the same kind. Builders name them by their sides and their angles, because that tells you how they fit together.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students drag triangles on a dot grid and see them named by sides (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and by angles (acute, right, obtuse), combine the two names, and reason about impossible pairs (AC9M7SP02, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name a triangle by its sides and by its angles. I can explain why some triangles are impossible, like a right-angled equilateral triangle. I can use equal sides and equal angles to find missing angles.

## Two names


:::keep-together

### Sides, then angles

Here is the routine for naming a triangle.

1. $90^\circ, 45^\circ, 45^\circ$ — A triangle with these angles: two are equal, so the sides opposite them are equal.
2. $\text{isosceles}$ — Sides name: two equal sides.
3. $\text{right-angled}$ — Angles name: the biggest angle is 90°. Give both: isosceles, right-angled.
4. $60^\circ, 60^\circ, 60^\circ$ — Equilateral means equal angles too, so an equilateral triangle is always acute.

Now try one: sides 5, 5 and 8.

**A triangle has sides 5 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm. Name it by its sides.**

☐ Right-angled

☐ Isosceles

☐ Scalene

☐ Equilateral

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Isosceles

Two equal sides: isosceles.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zara names a triangle by its angles. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  A triangle has angles 110°, 40° and 30°.
 2  It has two acute angles, 40° and 30°.
 3  So it is an acute triangle.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: every triangle has at least two acute angles. The biggest angle decides: 110° is more than 90°, so it is obtuse.

### Impossible pairs

**Which of these triangles is impossible?**

☐ An obtuse isosceles triangle

☐ An acute scalene triangle

☐ A right-angled scalene triangle

☐ A right-angled equilateral triangle

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A right-angled equilateral triangle

An equilateral triangle has three equal angles, 60° each: none can be 90°.

## Naming triangles


:::keep-together

### Equal angles

**A triangle has angles 50°, 50° and 80°. Name it by its sides and its angles.**

☐ Isosceles and obtuse

☐ Scalene and acute

☐ Isosceles and acute

☐ Equilateral and acute

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Isosceles and acute

Two equal angles sit opposite two equal sides: isosceles. The biggest angle, 80°, is under 90°: acute.

:::


:::keep-together

### A right angle

**A triangle has angles 25°, 65° and 90°. Name it.**

☐ Scalene and right-angled

☐ Isosceles and right-angled

☐ Scalene and obtuse

☐ Scalene and acute

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Scalene and right-angled

No equal angles, so no equal sides: scalene. One 90° angle: right-angled.

:::


:::keep-together

### Base angles

**An isosceles triangle has one angle of 40° between its two equal sides. What is each of the other two angles, in degrees?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 70

180 − 40 = 140, shared equally: 70° each.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: angle sum

**Review: a triangle has angles 35° and 85°. What is the third, in degrees?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

180 − 35 − 85 = 60°.

:::
