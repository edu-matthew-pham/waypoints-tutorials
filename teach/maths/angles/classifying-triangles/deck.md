## Today

**We are learning to** name a triangle twice: by its sides (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and by its biggest angle (acute, right, obtuse). Equal sides sit opposite equal angles, so some pairs are impossible: no right-angled equilateral triangle.

- I can name a triangle by its sides and by its angles.
- I can explain why some triangles are impossible, like a right-angled equilateral triangle.
- I can use equal sides and equal angles to find missing angles.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the triangle angle sum (Angles in a triangle) and the words acute, right and obtuse for angles. The one idea: every triangle has a sides name and an angles name, and equal sides sit opposite equal angles. The common mistake is naming by any acute angle (Zara's mistake): every triangle has at least two. A square grid cannot make an exact equilateral triangle; the board counts sides within 2% as equal and says so. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Roof trusses, bridges and set squares are all triangles, but not the same kind. Builders name them by their sides and their angles, because that tells you how they fit together.

<!-- Speaker note: Every triangle gets **two** names. By its **sides**: - **scalene**: no sides equal; - **isosceles**: two sides equal (marked with the same tick); - **equilateral**: all three sides equal. By its **angles**: - **acute**: every angle is less than 90°; - **right-angled**: one angle is 90° (marked ∟); - **obtuse**: one angle is more than 90°. Equal sides sit opposite equal angles: an isosceles triangle has two equal angles, and an equilateral triangle has three, each 60°. -->

## Sides, then angles

Here is the routine for naming a triangle.

Now try one: sides 5, 5 and 8.

$$90^\circ, 45^\circ, 45^\circ$$

1) $\text{isosceles}$ — Sides name: two equal sides.
2) $\text{right-angled}$ — Angles name: the biggest angle is 90°. Give both: isosceles, right-angled.
3) $60^\circ, 60^\circ, 60^\circ$ — Equilateral means equal angles too, so an equilateral triangle is always acute.

## Impossible pairs

**Which of these triangles is impossible?**

- An obtuse isosceles triangle
- An acute scalene triangle
- A right-angled scalene triangle

* Answer: A right-angled equilateral triangle

## Base angles

**An isosceles triangle has one angle of 40° between its two equal sides. What is each of the other two angles, in degrees?**

* Answer: 70
