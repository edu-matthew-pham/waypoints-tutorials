# Views of a solid

Angles and shapes · Solids

<!-- Speaker note: Students build piles of cubes and compare the isometric drawing with the top, front and side views, finding what each view hides (AC9M7SP01, nodes 2 and 3). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 10 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** a pile of cubes can be drawn in 3D or as top, front and side views. A front or side view shows only the tallest pile in each column; a top view with numbers shows every pile.

- I can draw or read the top, front and side views of a pile of cubes.
- I can say what a view hides.
- I can say one advantage and one disadvantage of a net and of a 3D drawing.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Build a small pile from linking cubes to hold up. The one idea: a view shows only what you can see from one side, so the front and side views show the tallest pile in each column and hide the rest. The common mistake is adding the piles (Noor's mistake). The board's rows run from the back (top of the grid) to the front. The front view looks from the front; the side view looks from the right. The top view puts the back row at the top. The view orientation is the hinge for this node: students who mix up front and side cannot compare representations. -->

---

## Why it matters

> An architect shows a building from above, from the front and from the side. Why not just one drawing?

<!-- Speaker note: An **isometric drawing** shows a solid in 3D on isometric (triangle) paper. **Views** are flat drawings seen from one side: the **top** view (also called the plan), the **front** view and the **side** view (together called elevations). A view shows what you can see; cubes hidden behind others do not show. -->

**Noor works out the front view of a column of piles. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The first column has piles of 3, 1 and 2 cubes.
 2  Noor draws the front view of that column.
 3  She adds the piles, so the column shows 6 squares.
 4  The front view is 6 squares tall.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: a front view shows only the tallest pile in each column, not all of them added up. -->

**Which drawing tells you exactly how many cubes there are?**
