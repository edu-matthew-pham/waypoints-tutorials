## Predict

The $z$-axis points up. A point is $(x, y, z)$:

- $x$: how far along the x-axis
- $y$: how far along the y-axis
- $z$: how far up

The **origin** is $(0, 0, 0)$. Points with $z = 0$ are on the floor (the x-y plane). The order is always $x$, then $y$, then $z$.

Real systems use the same idea with other names:

| System | Three numbers |
|---|---|
| a drone | metres east, metres north, metres up |
| a building | metres across, metres back, metres up (about 3 m a floor) |
| a plane | latitude, longitude, altitude |

Each real system has its own fixed order. Latitude (how far north or south) is always written before longitude (how far east or west), then altitude.

## Reading points


:::keep-together

### Reading a point

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{5 along } x$ — The first number.
2. $\text{0 along } y$ — The second number: no distance is 0.
3. $\text{2 up}$ — The third number, z.
4. $(5, 0, 2)$ — Write them in order, in brackets.

**P is 4 along the x-axis, 1 along the y-axis and 6 up. What are its coordinates?**

☐ (4, 6, 1)

☐ (6, 4, 1)

☐ (1, 4, 6)

☐ (4, 1, 6)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (4, 1, 6)

Always x, then y, then z: (4, 1, 6).

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the order is always (x, y, z), so the drone is at (5, 2, 3). Anyone reading (3, 5, 2) puts it 3 m east and 2 m up.

☐ The altitude

**Answer:** The altitude

Latitude and longitude place it over the ground; altitude says how high, like z.

## 3D positions


:::keep-together

### A plane climbs

**A plane is at latitude 33.9° S, longitude 151.2° E and altitude 9000 m. It climbs straight up 1000 m. Where is it now?**

☐ 33.9° S, 152.2° E, 9000 m

☐ 33.9° S, 151.2° E, 10 000 m

☐ 34.9° S, 151.2° E, 9000 m

☐ 33.9° S, 151.2° E, 1000 m

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 33.9° S, 151.2° E, 10 000 m

Only the altitude changes: 9000 + 1000 = 10 000 m. Latitude and longitude still place it over the same spot (near Sydney).

:::


:::keep-together

### Flying up

**A drone at (6, 4, 10) flies straight up 5 m. What is its new z-coordinate?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

Up changes only z: 10 + 5 = 15. x and y stay 6 and 4.

:::


:::keep-together

### A 3D printer

**A 3D printer's nozzle is at (20, 35, 2) mm. It prints the next layer 0.2 mm higher. Where is it?**

☐ (20.2, 35, 2)

☐ (20.2, 35.2, 2.2)

☐ (20, 35, 2.2)

☐ (20, 35.2, 2)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (20, 35, 2.2)

A new layer is higher, so only z changes: 2 + 0.2 = 2.2 mm.

:::

### Review: views

**Review: a pile of cubes is seen from above, from the front and from the side. Which view shows how tall the pile is?**

☐ All three equally

☐ The top view

☐ The front view (or the side view)

☐ None of them

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The front view (or the side view)

From above you see the floor (x and y); from the front or side you see the heights (z).
