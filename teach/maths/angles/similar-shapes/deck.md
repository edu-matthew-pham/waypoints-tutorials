# Similar shapes

Angles and shapes · Congruence and similarity

<!-- Speaker note: Students scale a triangle by a scale factor, find missing sides of similar shapes, and compare rectangles that are similar, congruent or neither (AC9M8SP01, nodes 2 and 3). ## Today

**We are learning to** similar shapes are the same shape but can be different sizes, like a photo and its enlargement. Find the scale factor, use it to work out missing sides, and learn the conditions that make two triangles similar.

- I can find a scale factor from a pair of matching sides.
- I can use a scale factor to find a missing side.
- I can say whether two shapes are congruent, similar or neither, and why.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need congruent triangles (last lesson) and multiplying by decimals. The one idea: similar means every length times the same scale factor, with the angles unchanged. The common mistake is adding a fixed amount to every side (Mia's mistake). Similarity conditions for triangles: AA, SSS (all sides in one ratio) and SAS (two sides in one ratio, the included angle equal). Only AA is practised much here; SSS appears in Practise. The link to transformations: translations, reflections and rotations give congruent shapes; an enlargement gives a similar shape. Wider: measure something tall outside with shadows and a metre ruler. -->

> How can you find the height of a tree without climbing it? Its shadow and your own shadow make two triangles of the same shape.

## A missing side

Here is the routine.

$$\text{original } 2, 3, 4;\ \text{copy shortest} = 5$$

1) $k = 5 \div 2 = 2.5$ — Scale factor: copy ÷ original.
2) $4 \times 2.5 = 10$ — Multiply the original side by k.
3) $10 \text{ cm}$ — Check: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 as well.

## A missing side

**A triangle has sides 4 cm, 6 cm and 8 cm. A similar triangle's shortest side is 6 cm. How long is its longest side, in cm?**

* Answer: 12

<!-- Speaker note: k = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5, so the longest side is 8 × 1.5 = 12 cm. -->

## Find the mistake

**Mia enlarges a photo. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A 4 cm by 6 cm photo is enlarged so the 4 cm side becomes 7 cm.
 2  7 − 4 = 3, so 3 cm is added to every side.
 3  The 6 cm side becomes 6 + 3 = 9 cm.
 4  The enlargement is 7 cm by 9 cm.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: an enlargement multiplies. k = 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75, so the 6 cm side becomes 6 × 1.75 = 10.5 cm. -->

## Angles only

**Two triangles both have angles of 40° and 75°. Are they similar?**

- Yes, by AA: the third angles are both 65°
- No: you need the sides
- Only if they are the same size
- You cannot tell

* Answer: Yes, by AA: the third angles are both 65°

<!-- Speaker note: 180 − 40 − 75 = 65 in both, so all three angles match: the same shape (AA). -->

**A triangle's sides are 5, 7 and 9 cm. A similar triangle's matching sides are 15, 21 and 27 cm. What is the scale factor?**

* Answer: 3

## Transformations

**A triangle is moved by a transformation, and its image is similar but not congruent. Which transformation was it?**

- A rotation
- A translation
- A reflection
- An enlargement

* Answer: An enlargement

<!-- Speaker note: Translations, reflections and rotations keep every length, so they give congruent shapes. Only an enlargement (k ≠ 1) changes the size. -->

## Exit ticket

**A 2 m by 3 m garden bed is enlarged so the 2 m side becomes 5 m. How long is the 3 m side now?**

* k = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5, so 3 × 2.5 = 7.5 m.
