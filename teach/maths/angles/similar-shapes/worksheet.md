## Scale factors


:::keep-together

### A missing side

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{original } 2, 3, 4;\ \text{copy shortest} = 5$ — Match the sides: shortest with shortest.
2. $k = 5 \div 2 = 2.5$ — Scale factor: copy ÷ original.
3. $4 \times 2.5 = 10$ — Multiply the original side by k.
4. $10 \text{ cm}$ — Check: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 as well.

**A triangle has sides 4 cm, 6 cm and 8 cm. A similar triangle's shortest side is 6 cm. How long is its longest side, in cm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12

k = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5, so the longest side is 8 × 1.5 = 12 cm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia enlarges a photo. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A 4 cm by 6 cm photo is enlarged so the 4 cm side becomes 7 cm.
 2  7 − 4 = 3, so 3 cm is added to every side.
 3  The 6 cm side becomes 6 + 3 = 9 cm.
 4  The enlargement is 7 cm by 9 cm.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: an enlargement multiplies. k = 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75, so the 6 cm side becomes 6 × 1.75 = 10.5 cm.

:::

## Similar shapes


:::keep-together

### Scale factor

**A triangle's sides are 5, 7 and 9 cm. A similar triangle's matching sides are 15, 21 and 27 cm. What is the scale factor?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

15 ÷ 5 = 21 ÷ 7 = 27 ÷ 9 = 3 (SSS similarity).

:::


:::keep-together

### Smaller copies

**A 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle is reduced so the 10 cm side becomes 5 cm. How long is the 6 cm side now, in cm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

k = 0.5, so 6 × 0.5 = 3 cm. A scale factor less than 1 makes the copy smaller.

:::


:::keep-together

### Transformations

**A triangle is moved by a transformation, and its image is similar but not congruent. Which transformation was it?**

☐ A rotation

☐ A translation

☐ A reflection

☐ An enlargement

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** An enlargement

Translations, reflections and rotations keep every length, so they give congruent shapes. Only an enlargement (k ≠ 1) changes the size.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: congruence

**Review: two triangles have equal angles of 50° and 60°, and the sides between those angles are both 8 cm. Are they congruent?**

☐ No, they are only similar

☐ Yes, by ASA

☐ Yes, by SSS

☐ You cannot tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Yes, by ASA

Two angles and the side between them: ASA.

:::

## Make: a tree height, on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. The heights and shadows are made up for the task, and it only works on level ground with the shadows measured at the same moment. Draw both triangles, label the matching sides, then type the tree's height.

**Also used in**

- **Plans and models.** Floor plans and scale models are similar to the real thing: every length is scaled by the same factor and the angles stay.
- **Photos and screens.** Enlarging a photo keeps its shape only if both sides use the same scale factor.
