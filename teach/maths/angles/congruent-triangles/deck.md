# Congruent triangles

Angles and shapes · Congruence and similarity

<!-- Speaker note: Students build triangles from given parts and see that SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS and RHS fix one triangle while SSA can give two (AC9M8SP01, node 1). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 10 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** congruent triangles are the same shape and size. You don't need all six parts to check: some sets of three parts fix the triangle (the congruence tests: SSS, SAS, ASA or AAS, RHS), and one set of three can fit two different triangles.

- I can name the four congruence tests (SSS, SAS, ASA or AAS, RHS).
- I can choose the test that shows two triangles are congruent.
- I can explain why SSA is not a congruence test.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first: try SSA with a short BC. Students need triangle classification and the angle sum (Year 7). The one idea: a test is a set of parts that fixes the triangle, so any two triangles with those parts are congruent. The common mistake is treating SSA as SAS (Tom's mistake). The board draws every triangle the parts allow: none, one, or two (only for SSA). Wider: show two triangles formed by a rectangle's diagonal are congruent (it leads to the quadrilateral proofs in two lessons). -->

## Why it matters

> A builder cuts a hundred identical roof trusses. To check two are the same, does she need to measure every side and every angle, or are three measurements enough?

<!-- Speaker note: Two shapes are **congruent** when one fits exactly on the other: the same shape and the same size. For triangles, matching sides and matching angles are equal. Write $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle PQR$, with the corners in matching order. Do you need all six measurements to be sure? -->

## Which test

Here is the routine.

$$\text{AB} = 6,\ \text{BC} = 4,\ \angle B = 70^\circ$$

1) $\angle B \text{ is between AB and BC}$ — Where is the angle?
2) $\text{SAS}$ — Two sides and the included angle.
3) $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle PQR$ — Write the congruence with matching corners in order.

## Which test

**In both triangles, AB = 5 cm, AC = 7 cm and angle A = 40°. Which test shows they are congruent?**

- SSS
- ASA
- They might not be congruent

## Find the mistake

**Tom decides two triangles are congruent. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Two triangles each have a 6 cm side, a 5 cm side and a 40° angle.
 2  In both, the 40° angle is opposite the 5 cm side, not between the two sides.
 3  Two sides and an angle match, so they are congruent by SAS.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: SAS needs the angle between the two sides. Here it is not (SSA), and SSA can give two different triangles. -->

## Angles only

**Two triangles both have angles of 50°, 60° and 70°. Are they congruent?**

- Yes, by ASA
- Yes: all three angles match
- Not necessarily: they are the same shape but can be different sizes
- No, never

* Answer: Not necessarily: they are the same shape but can be different sizes

<!-- Speaker note: Equal angles make them similar (same shape). A matching side is needed for congruence. -->
