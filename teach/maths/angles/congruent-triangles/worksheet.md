# Congruent triangles

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A builder cuts a hundred identical roof trusses. To check two are the same, does she need to measure every side and every angle, or are three measurements enough?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build triangles from given parts and see that SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS and RHS fix one triangle while SSA can give two (AC9M8SP01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the four congruence tests (SSS, SAS, ASA or AAS, RHS). I can choose the test that shows two triangles are congruent. I can explain why SSA is not a congruence test.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

You do not need all six. These **congruence tests** each fix a triangle, so two triangles that share them are congruent. The angle between two sides is the **included angle**.

| Test | Equal parts |
|---|---|
| **SSS** | all three sides |
| **SAS** | two sides and the included angle (the angle **between** them) |
| **ASA** / **AAS** | two angles and a matching side |
| **RHS** | a right angle, the hypotenuse and one other side |

:::keep-together

**Two triangles both have sides of 8 cm and 6 cm and a 40° angle. Must they be congruent?**

☐ Yes: two sides and an angle are enough

☐ No: you always need all three sides

☐ Not always: it depends where the angle is

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Not always. With the angle between the two sides (SAS) the triangle is fixed. With the angle somewhere else (SSA), two different triangles can fit.

:::

## Which test


:::keep-together

### Which test

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{AB} = 6,\ \text{BC} = 4,\ \angle B = 70^\circ$ — List the equal parts.
2. $\angle B \text{ is between AB and BC}$ — Where is the angle?
3. $\text{SAS}$ — Two sides and the included angle.
4. $\triangle ABC \equiv \triangle PQR$ — Write the congruence with matching corners in order.

**In both triangles, AB = 5 cm, AC = 7 cm and angle A = 40°. Which test shows they are congruent?**

☐ SSS

☐ ASA

☐ They might not be congruent

☐ SAS: angle A is between AB and AC

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** SAS: angle A is between AB and AC

Angle A is where AB and AC meet: two sides and the included angle.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Tom decides two triangles are congruent. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Two triangles each have a 6 cm side, a 5 cm side and a 40° angle.
 2  In both, the 40° angle is opposite the 5 cm side, not between the two sides.
 3  Two sides and an angle match, so they are congruent by SAS.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: SAS needs the angle between the two sides. Here it is not (SSA), and SSA can give two different triangles.

:::


:::keep-together

### Angles only

**Two triangles both have angles of 50°, 60° and 70°. Are they congruent?**

☐ Yes, by ASA

☐ Yes: all three angles match

☐ Not necessarily: they are the same shape but can be different sizes

☐ No, never

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Not necessarily: they are the same shape but can be different sizes

Equal angles make them similar (same shape). A matching side is needed for congruence.

## Congruence tests


:::keep-together

### SSS

**Both triangles have sides 4, 6 and 7 cm. Which test?**

☐ SSS

☐ SAS

☐ RHS

☐ Not enough to tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** SSS

Three equal sides: SSS.

:::


:::keep-together

### AAS

**Both triangles have angles 30° and 80°, and the side opposite the 80° angle is 9 cm. Which test?**

☐ SSS

☐ AAS

☐ SAS

☐ Not enough to tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** AAS

Two angles and a side that is not between them: AAS.

:::


:::keep-together

### SSA

**Both triangles have sides 9 cm and 6 cm, and a 35° angle opposite the 6 cm side (not between the two sides). Must they be congruent?**

☐ Yes, by RHS

☐ Yes, by SSS

☐ No: SSA is not a congruence test

☐ Yes, by SAS

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: SSA is not a congruence test

Two sides and a non-included angle can make two different triangles.

:::


:::keep-together
