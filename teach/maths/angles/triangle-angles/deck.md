# Angles in a triangle

Angles and shapes · Angle sums

<!-- Speaker note: Students drag triangles of every shape on a dot grid, see that the three angles always add to 180°, and explain why by tearing off the corners to make a straight line (AC9M7M05, node 1). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** however you change a triangle, its three angles add to 180°. Tear off the corners and they make a straight line.

- I can show that the angles of a triangle add to 180°, by measuring and by tearing off the corners.
- I can explain why this is true for every triangle, whatever its shape or size.
- I can tell whether three angles could be the angles of a triangle.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board and the tear-off task yourself first. Students should know that angles on a straight line add to 180° (Year 6). The board measures to the nearest degree and shares out the rounding so the shown angles add to the shown total. With a real protractor, totals of 178° to 182° are normal: say so when students measure by hand (Practise has a question on it). If you have time, do the paper version too: every student draws a different triangle, tears off the corners and lines them up. Seeing 25 different triangles all make a straight line is the strongest evidence. The formal proof with parallel lines comes with AC9M7M04. Finding a missing angle from the other two is the next step in class (node 3), not this lesson. -->

## Why it matters

> A roof truss, a bike frame, a folded paper plane: triangles of every shape. One thing about their angles never changes.

<!-- Speaker note: An **angle** measures a turn, in degrees ($°$). A full turn is $360°$, and the angles on a straight line add to $180°$. A triangle has three corners, so three angles inside it. In this lesson you find out what happens to their total when you change the triangle's shape. -->

## Find the mistake

**Kai compares a small triangle with one twice as big. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Kai draws a small triangle, then one with every side twice as long.
 2  Small triangle: 50° + 60° + 70° = 180°.
 3  The big triangle has longer sides, so its angles are bigger.
 4  So the big triangle's angles add to more than 180°.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: making every side twice as long does not change the angles, only the size. The big triangle's angles are still 50°, 60° and 70°, adding to 180°. -->

## Why a straight line?

**Why do the three torn corners always make a straight line?**

- Because every triangle has a $90°$ angle
- Because the angles add to $180°$, and a straight line is $180°$
- Because the corners are all the same size

* Answer: Because the angles add to $180°$, and a straight line is $180°$

<!-- Speaker note: Side by side at one point, the three angles fill a straight line exactly. Angles on a straight line add to 180°, so the triangle's angles do too. -->

**Which three angles could be the angles of a triangle?**

- $50°, 60°, 80°$
- $90°, 90°, 10°$

## Measuring by hand

**Lia measures a paper triangle with a protractor: $57°, 68°, 56°$. That adds to $181°$. What is the best explanation?**

- She has measured the wrong angles
- Her triangle is a special one that adds to $181°$
- Her measurements are a little out: a protractor reads to about a degree

* Answer: Her measurements are a little out: a protractor reads to about a degree

<!-- Speaker note: Real measurements are rounded. 181° is within a degree or two of 180°, which is what measuring three angles by hand gives. -->

## Bigger triangle

**Triangle P has sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. Triangle Q has sides 30 cm, 40 cm and 50 cm. How do their angle sums compare?**

- Q's is 10 times bigger
- They are the same: $180°$
- Q's is bigger, but not 10 times

* Answer: They are the same: $180°$

<!-- Speaker note: Every triangle's angles add to 180°. Q is P made 10 times bigger, with exactly the same angles. -->

## Exit ticket

**A student says the angles of a very large triangle add to more than 180°. Explain why they are wrong.**

* Every triangle's angles add to 180°: tear off the corners and they fill a straight line. Size changes the sides, not the angles.
