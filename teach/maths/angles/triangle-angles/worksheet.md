# Angles in a triangle

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A roof truss, a bike frame, a folded paper plane: triangles of every shape. One thing about their angles never changes.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students drag triangles of every shape on a dot grid, see that the three angles always add to 180°, and explain why by tearing off the corners to make a straight line (AC9M7M05, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can show that the angles of a triangle add to 180°, by measuring and by tearing off the corners. I can explain why this is true for every triangle, whatever its shape or size. I can tell whether three angles could be the angles of a triangle.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Why 180°?


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Kai compares a small triangle with one twice as big. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Kai draws a small triangle, then one with every side twice as long.
 2  Small triangle: 50° + 60° + 70° = 180°.
 3  The big triangle has longer sides, so its angles are bigger.
 4  So the big triangle's angles add to more than 180°.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: making every side twice as long does not change the angles, only the size. The big triangle's angles are still 50°, 60° and 70°, adding to 180°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why a straight line?

**Why do the three torn corners always make a straight line?**

☐ Because every triangle has a $90°$ angle

☐ Because the angles add to $180°$, and a straight line is $180°$

☐ Because the corners are all the same size

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Because the angles add to $180°$, and a straight line is $180°$

Side by side at one point, the three angles fill a straight line exactly. Angles on a straight line add to 180°, so the triangle's angles do too.

:::

## Use the rule


:::keep-together

### Possible?

**Which three angles could be the angles of a triangle?**

☐ $50°, 60°, 80°$

☐ $90°, 90°, 10°$

☐ $40°, 60°, 80°$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $40°, 60°, 80°$

40° + 60° + 80° = 180°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Measuring by hand

**Lia measures a paper triangle with a protractor: $57°, 68°, 56°$. That adds to $181°$. What is the best explanation?**

☐ She has measured the wrong angles

☐ Her triangle is a special one that adds to $181°$

☐ Her measurements are a little out: a protractor reads to about a degree

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Her measurements are a little out: a protractor reads to about a degree

Real measurements are rounded. 181° is within a degree or two of 180°, which is what measuring three angles by hand gives.

:::


:::keep-together

### Bigger triangle

**Triangle P has sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. Triangle Q has sides 30 cm, 40 cm and 50 cm. How do their angle sums compare?**

☐ Q's is 10 times bigger

☐ They are the same: $180°$

☐ Q's is bigger, but not 10 times

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They are the same: $180°$

Every triangle's angles add to 180°. Q is P made 10 times bigger, with exactly the same angles.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: two angles sit side by side on a straight line. One is $130°$. How big is the other?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 50

180° − 130° = 50°.

:::


## Make: one angle equals the other two

Think first: if one angle is the other two added together, what fraction of $180°$ is it? Then build it.

Stretch: can a triangle have one angle that is exactly twice the sum of the other two? How big is it?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
