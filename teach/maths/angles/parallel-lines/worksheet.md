# Angles at parallel lines

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Railway tracks, the lines on a car park, the rungs of a ladder: parallel lines crossed by another line. The angles they make come in pairs.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students turn a transversal across two parallel lines, see that the same place at each crossing gives the same angle, and learn to name corresponding, alternate and co-interior pairs in any orientation, keeping them apart from vertically opposite angles (AC9M7M04, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles where a line crosses two parallel lines. I can tell these pairs apart from vertically opposite angles. I can explain that the names say where the angles are, and parallel lines make the pairs equal or add to 180°.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The three pairs


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia names a pair of angles. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia looks at angles b and c.
 2  b and c are on opposite sides of the crossing line.
 3  So b and c are alternate angles.
 4  Alternate angles are equal, so b = c.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: b and c are at the same crossing, opposite each other, so they are vertically opposite. Alternate angles are one at each crossing, between the parallel lines. (Vertically opposite angles are equal too, so line 4's b = c is true, for a different reason.)

:::


:::keep-together

### Co-interior

**Which pair is co-interior?**

☐ $c$ and $e$

☐ $c$ and $g$

☐ $c$ and $f$

☐ $b$ and $c$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $c$ and $e$

c and e are both between the lines and both on the left of the crossing line: co-interior.

### Review: a straight line

**Review: $a$ and $b$ are on a straight line. $a = 115^\circ$. What is $b$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 65

Angles on a straight line add to 180°: 180 − 115 = 65°.

## Make: a pair that adds to 180°

Stretch: which pairs add to $180^\circ$ but are not between the lines?

**A transversal crosses two parallel lines. Which angle pairs are equal, and which pair adds to 180°?**

{{lines-3}}

**Answer:** Corresponding angles and alternate angles are equal (so are vertically opposite angles); co-interior angles add to 180°.
