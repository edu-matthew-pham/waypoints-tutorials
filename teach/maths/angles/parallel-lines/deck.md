# Angles at parallel lines

Angles and shapes · Parallel lines

<!-- Speaker note: Students turn a transversal across two parallel lines, see that the same place at each crossing gives the same angle, and learn to name corresponding, alternate and co-interior pairs in any orientation, keeping them apart from vertically opposite angles (AC9M7M04, node 1). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** a line crossing two parallel lines makes eight angles. Pairs in the same place at each crossing are corresponding; between the lines on opposite sides, alternate; between the lines on the same side, co-interior.

- I can find corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles where a line crosses two parallel lines.
- I can tell these pairs apart from vertically opposite angles.
- I can explain that the names say where the angles are, and parallel lines make the pairs equal or add to 180°.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board and the Investigate board yourself first. Students need angles on a straight line and vertically opposite angles (Year 6). The one idea: each pair takes one angle from each crossing, and the name says where they are (same place; between and opposite sides; between and same side). The common mistake is naming a pair at one crossing (vertically opposite) as alternate; it is planted after the names, in Mia's reasoning. The boards in Investigate, Modify and Make do not name the pairs: students pick, and the check says whether the pair is right. F, Z and C shapes are given as memory aids only; the Modify board leans the other way so students do not rely on them. -->

## Why it matters

> Railway tracks, the lines on a car park, the rungs of a ladder: parallel lines crossed by another line. The angles they make come in pairs.

<!-- Speaker note: Two lines are **parallel** when they go the same way and never meet, like railway tracks. Arrows (>>) on the lines mark them as parallel. A third line that crosses them is a **transversal**. The crossings make eight angles, named $a$ to $h$ here. In this lesson you learn the names of the pairs and how to spot them in any diagram. -->

## Predict

**$a$ is above the top line, on the left of the crossing line. $e$ is in the same place at the bottom crossing. If you turn the crossing line, what happens to $a$ and $e$?**

- One grows while the other shrinks
- Nothing links them: they change separately
- They stay the same size as each other

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: They stay the same size as each other. The parallel lines cross the transversal the same way, so the bottom crossing is a copy of the top one, slid along. a and e are corresponding angles. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Find the mistake

**Mia names a pair of angles. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia looks at angles b and c.
 2  b and c are on opposite sides of the crossing line.
 3  So b and c are alternate angles.
 4  Alternate angles are equal, so b = c.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: b and c are at the same crossing, opposite each other, so they are vertically opposite. Alternate angles are one at each crossing, between the parallel lines. (Vertically opposite angles are equal too, so line 4's b = c is true, for a different reason.) -->

## Co-interior

**Which pair is co-interior?**

- $c$ and $e$
- $c$ and $g$
- $c$ and $f$
- $b$ and $c$

* Answer: $c$ and $e$

<!-- Speaker note: c and e are both between the lines and both on the left of the crossing line: co-interior. -->

## Exit ticket

**A transversal crosses two parallel lines. Which angle pairs are equal, and which pair adds to 180°?**

* Corresponding angles and alternate angles are equal (so are vertically opposite angles); co-interior angles add to 180°.
