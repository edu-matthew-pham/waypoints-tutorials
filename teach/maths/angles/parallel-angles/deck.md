# Finding angles with parallel lines

Angles and shapes · Parallel lines

<!-- Speaker note: Students use the parallel-line rules (corresponding and alternate equal, co-interior add to 180°) with straight-line and vertically opposite angles to find unknown angles from one given angle, naming a reason for each (AC9M7M04, node 2). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** when the lines are parallel, corresponding angles are equal, alternate angles are equal, and co-interior angles add to 180°. From one angle you can find all eight, giving a reason for each.

- I can state the rules: with parallel lines, corresponding and alternate angles are equal and co-interior angles add to 180°.
- I can find an unknown angle using one of these rules, and say which rule.
- I can find angles in two steps, giving a reason for each step.

## Why it matters

> A carpenter measures one angle where a brace crosses two parallel shelves. Without measuring again, she knows every other angle. How?

## Equal pairs

Here is how one angle gives all eight, with a reason for each.

Then find $f$ in the picture.

$$b = 70^\circ$$

1) $c = 70^\circ$ — Vertically opposite $b$: equal.
2) $a = 110^\circ$ — On a straight line with $b$: $180 - 70$.
3) $f = 70^\circ$ — Corresponding to $b$: equal.
4) $e = 110^\circ$ — Co-interior with $c$: they add to $180^\circ$.
5) $g = 70^\circ,\; h = 110^\circ$ — Corresponding to $c$ and to $d$ (or vertically opposite $f$ and $e$).

## Find the mistake

**Leo finds $f$ when $d = 110^\circ$. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  d = 110° (given).
 2  d and f are between the parallel lines, on the same side.
 3  So d and f are co-interior angles.
 4  Co-interior angles are equal, so f = 110°.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: co-interior angles add to 180°, so f = 180° − 110° = 70°. Only corresponding and alternate angles are equal. -->
