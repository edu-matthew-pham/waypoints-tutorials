# Finding angles with parallel lines

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A carpenter measures one angle where a brace crosses two parallel shelves. Without measuring again, she knows every other angle. How?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use the parallel-line rules (corresponding and alternate equal, co-interior add to 180°) with straight-line and vertically opposite angles to find unknown angles from one given angle, naming a reason for each (AC9M7M04, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can state the rules: with parallel lines, corresponding and alternate angles are equal and co-interior angles add to 180°. I can find an unknown angle using one of these rules, and say which rule. I can find angles in two steps, giving a reason for each step.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

Corresponding and alternate angles are equal. Is a co-interior pair the same?

:::keep-together

**Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. $c = 65^\circ$, and $e$ is co-interior with $c$. What is $e$?**

☐ $65^\circ$

☐ $295^\circ$

☐ $25^\circ$

☐ $115^\circ$

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 115°. Co-interior angles are not equal: they add to 180°, so e = 180° − 65° = 115°.

:::

## From one angle to all eight


:::keep-together

### Equal pairs

Here is how one angle gives all eight, with a reason for each.

1. $b = 70^\circ$ — Given.
2. $c = 70^\circ$ — Vertically opposite $b$: equal.
3. $a = 110^\circ$ — On a straight line with $b$: $180 - 70$.
4. $f = 70^\circ$ — Corresponding to $b$: equal.
5. $e = 110^\circ$ — Co-interior with $c$: they add to $180^\circ$.
6. $g = 70^\circ,\; h = 110^\circ$ — Corresponding to $c$ and to $d$ (or vertically opposite $f$ and $e$).

Then find $f$ in the picture.

**Find $f$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 72

b and f are corresponding angles, so f = 72°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Leo finds $f$ when $d = 110^\circ$. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  d = 110° (given).
 2  d and f are between the parallel lines, on the same side.
 3  So d and f are co-interior angles.
 4  Co-interior angles are equal, so f = 110°.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: co-interior angles add to 180°, so f = 180° − 110° = 70°. Only corresponding and alternate angles are equal.

:::


:::keep-together

### Co-interior

**Find $f$ correctly.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 70

Co-interior angles add to 180°: f = 180° − 110° = 70°.

### The reason

**$e = 120^\circ$ and $c = 60^\circ$. Which reason links them?**

☐ Alternate angles are equal

☐ Co-interior angles add to 180°

☐ Corresponding angles are equal

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Co-interior angles add to 180°

c and e are between the lines on the same side, and 60° + 120° = 180°.

:::
