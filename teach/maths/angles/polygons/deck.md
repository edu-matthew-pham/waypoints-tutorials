## Today

**We are learning to** a polygon is named by its number of sides (pentagon 5, hexagon 6, octagon 8, decagon 10). It is regular when all its sides and all its angles are equal, and concave when a corner points in.

- I can name polygons from 3 to 10 sides.
- I can tell whether a polygon is regular, and explain why.
- I can tell a convex polygon from a concave one.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the quadrilateral family (previous lesson) and the polygon angle sum (Angles in a polygon). The one idea: regular means equal sides **and** equal angles; concave means a corner points in. The common mistake is equal sides alone (Kim's mistake with a rhombus). A dart (a kite with one corner pushed in) is named a kite by the board, as its sides fit the kite's definition; some textbooks keep kites convex and call the concave one a dart. ## Why it matters

> Stop signs are octagons, honeycomb cells are hexagons, and a star is a polygon with corners that point in. What makes some of them regular?

<!-- Speaker note: A **polygon** is a closed shape with straight sides. Its name counts the sides: | Sides | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | Name | triangle | quadrilateral | pentagon | hexagon | heptagon | octagon | nonagon | decagon | A polygon is **regular** when **all its sides are equal and all its angles are equal**; otherwise it is **irregular**. It is **convex** when every corner points out (every angle less than 180°), and **concave** when at least one corner points in (an angle more than 180°). -->

## Count, then check

Here is the routine for naming a polygon.

Now match the names.

$$\text{6 sides} \Rightarrow \text{hexagon}$$

1) $\text{sides equal and angles equal?}$ — Both: regular. Either one fails: irregular.
2) $\text{an angle} > 180^\circ?$ — Yes: concave (a corner points in). No: convex.
3) $\text{square} = \text{regular quadrilateral}$ — A rhombus has equal sides but not equal angles; a rectangle the reverse.

## Concave

**How can you tell that a polygon is concave?**

- At least one corner points in, with an angle more than 180°
- It has a right angle
- It has more than six sides
- Its sides are not all equal

* Answer: At least one corner points in, with an angle more than 180°

<!-- Speaker note: A concave polygon has a corner that points in: a reflex angle, over 180°. -->

## Exit ticket

**A pentagon has angles 100°, 110°, 110°, 100° and 120°. Is it regular? Convex or concave?**

* Irregular (its angles are not equal), convex (every angle under 180°).
