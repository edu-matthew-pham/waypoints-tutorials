## Names and properties


:::keep-together

### Count, then check

Here is the routine for naming a polygon.

1. $\text{6 sides} \Rightarrow \text{hexagon}$ — Count the sides for the name.
2. $\text{sides equal and angles equal?}$ — Both: regular. Either one fails: irregular.
3. $\text{an angle} > 180^\circ?$ — Yes: concave (a corner points in). No: convex.
4. $\text{square} = \text{regular quadrilateral}$ — A rhombus has equal sides but not equal angles; a rectangle the reverse.

Now match the names.

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: regular needs equal angles too, and 60° and 120° are not equal. The only regular quadrilateral is a square.

### Concave

**How can you tell that a polygon is concave?**

☐ At least one corner points in, with an angle more than 180°

☐ It has a right angle

☐ It has more than six sides

☐ Its sides are not all equal

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** At least one corner points in, with an angle more than 180°

A concave polygon has a corner that points in: a reflex angle, over 180°.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Nonagon

9 sides: a nonagon.

### Regular

**Which is a regular polygon?**

☐ A rhombus with angles 70° and 110°

☐ An equilateral triangle

☐ A rectangle 4 cm by 6 cm

☐ An isosceles triangle

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** An equilateral triangle

An equilateral triangle has equal sides and equal angles (60° each).

:::


:::keep-together

### A regular hexagon

**Each angle of a regular hexagon is the same. The angles add to 720°. How big is each one, in degrees?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 120

720 ÷ 6 = 120°.

:::
