## K, k² and k³


:::keep-together

### Lengths, areas, volumes

1. $\text{length} \times k$ — Every side is multiplied by k.
2. $\text{area} \times k^2$ — Two directions, so k × k.
3. $\text{volume} \times k^3$ — Three directions, so k × k × k.
4. $k = 10 \Rightarrow \times 10, \times 100, \times 1000$ — The monster.

**A square has area 5 cm². It is enlarged by a scale factor of 4. What is the new area? (cm²)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 80

5 × 4² = 5 × 16 = 80.

:::


:::keep-together

### Area and volume

**A box has volume 10 cm³. It is enlarged by a scale factor of 2. What is the new volume? (cm³)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 80

10 × 2³ = 10 × 8 = 80.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ray enlarges a box by a scale factor of 3. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A box has surface area 40 cm². It is enlarged with a scale factor of 3.
 2  Lengths are multiplied by 3, so the area is too.
 3  New area = 40 × 3 = 120 cm².
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: area is multiplied by k², which is 9, not 3. Only lengths are multiplied by 3.

## Scale it


:::keep-together

### Area

**A square has side 3 cm. Enlarge it by a scale factor of 5. What is the new area? (cm²)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 225

The new side is 15, so the area is 225.

:::


:::keep-together

### Volume

**A cube has volume 4 cm³. Enlarge it by a scale factor of 3. What is the new volume? (cm³)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 108

4 × 3³ = 4 × 27 = 108.

:::
