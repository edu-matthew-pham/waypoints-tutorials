# Rotations

Angles and shapes · Transformations

<!-- Speaker note: Students turn a shape about a centre and find the image of each corner for quarter and half turns (AC9M7SP03, node 3). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 8 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** a rotation turns a shape about a centre by an angle in a direction. About the origin, a quarter turn anticlockwise changes (x, y) to (−y, x) and a half turn changes it to (−x, −y).

- I can rotate a shape 90°, 180° or 270° about the origin.
- I can describe a rotation by its centre, angle and direction.
- I can use the pattern in the coordinates to check an answer.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need translations and reflections (previous lessons). The one idea: a rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction. About the origin, an anticlockwise quarter turn is $(x, y) \to (-y, x)$; the half turn changes both signs. The common mistake is only swapping the coordinates (Lee's mistake). The board turns anticlockwise only; clockwise turns are the questions in Modify and Make (90° clockwise is 270° anticlockwise). Rotation is the hardest transformation to reason about: let students trace and turn before using the rule. The rule is a check on the answer, not the method. Turning about another centre (the second Modify task) is done by the picture: the rule does not apply. -->

---

## Why it matters

> The hands of a clock, a Ferris wheel, a spinning fan. Each turns about a centre by a certain angle, in a certain direction.

<!-- Speaker note: A **rotation** turns a shape about a fixed point, the **centre of rotation**. To describe one, give the **centre**, the **angle** and the **direction** (clockwise or anticlockwise). The shape keeps its size and shape. About the origin, an anticlockwise turn of 90° changes $(x, y)$ to $(-y, x)$; 180° (a half turn) changes it to $(-x, -y)$; 270° changes it to $(y, -x)$. -->

## Find the mistake

**Lee turns P (3, 1) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The point P is (3, 1). Lee turns it 90° anticlockwise about the origin.
 2  A quarter turn is a big move, so P goes round to the next quadrant.
 3  A quarter turn just swaps the coordinates.
 4  So the image of P is (1, 3).
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: a quarter turn also changes a sign. P turns up and to the left, so the image is (−1, 3), not (1, 3). -->

## What describes a rotation

**What three things do you need to describe a rotation?**

- The angle only
- The centre, the angle and the direction
- The centre and the size of the shape
- The axis and the angle

* Answer: The centre, the angle and the direction
