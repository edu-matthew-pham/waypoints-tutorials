# Rotations

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The hands of a clock, a Ferris wheel, a spinning fan. Each turns about a centre by a certain angle, in a certain direction.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students turn a shape about a centre and find the image of each corner for quarter and half turns (AC9M7SP03, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can rotate a shape 90°, 180° or 270° about the origin. I can describe a rotation by its centre, angle and direction. I can use the pattern in the coordinates to check an answer.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## How to turn a point


:::keep-together

### A half turn

**B is at (4, 1). It turns 180° about the origin. Where does B′ land?**

☐ (4, −1)

☐ (1, 4)

☐ (−4, 1)

☐ (−4, −1)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (−4, −1)

A half turn changes both signs: (−4, −1).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Lee turns P (3, 1) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The point P is (3, 1). Lee turns it 90° anticlockwise about the origin.
 2  A quarter turn is a big move, so P goes round to the next quadrant.
 3  A quarter turn just swaps the coordinates.
 4  So the image of P is (1, 3).
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: a quarter turn also changes a sign. P turns up and to the left, so the image is (−1, 3), not (1, 3).

:::


:::keep-together

### What describes a rotation

**What three things do you need to describe a rotation?**

☐ The angle only

☐ The centre, the angle and the direction

☐ The centre and the size of the shape

☐ The axis and the angle

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The centre, the angle and the direction

The same angle about a different centre, or in the other direction, gives a different image.

## Rotations


:::keep-together

### A half turn

**B is at (4, 1). After a 180° turn about the origin, what is the x-coordinate of B′?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -4

x changes sign: −4. The image is (−4, −1).

:::


:::keep-together

### Which turn

**Which turn about the origin takes (2, 1) to (−1, 2)?**

☐ 180°

☐ 90° clockwise

☐ 90° anticlockwise

☐ 270° anticlockwise

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 90° anticlockwise

(2, 1) to (−1, 2) is the rule (x, y) → (−y, x): a quarter turn anticlockwise.

:::


:::keep-together

### A quarter turn

**The point (3, 1) turns 90° anticlockwise about the origin. What is the y-coordinate of the image?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

(x, y) → (−y, x), so (3, 1) becomes (−1, 3).

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: reflections

**Review: the point (3, 5) is reflected in the x-axis. What is the y-coordinate of the image?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -5

(3, 5) becomes (3, −5).

:::
