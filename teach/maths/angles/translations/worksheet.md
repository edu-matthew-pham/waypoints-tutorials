# Translations

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A chess piece slides across the board, a lift goes up, a character steps left on a screen. Each is a translation: every point moves the same way.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students slide a triangle on the coordinate plane and describe the move by the change in each coordinate (AC9M7SP03, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can translate a shape and write the new coordinates. I can describe a translation as so many units left or right and up or down. I can say what changes and what stays the same.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Add to x for right, take from y for down.

:::keep-together

**Point A is at (1, 1). The triangle is translated 3 right and 2 down. Where does A land?**

☐ (3, 2)

☐ (4, −1)

☐ (−2, −1)

☐ (4, 3)

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** (4, −1). Right adds 3 to x: 1 + 3 = 4. Down takes 2 from y: 1 − 2 = −1.

:::

## Reading the move


:::keep-together

### Where does B land?

**The triangle has corners A (1, 1), B (3, 1) and C (3, 2). It moves 3 right and 2 down. Where does B land?**

☐ (6, −1)

☐ (6, 3)

☐ (4, −1)

☐ (0, −1)

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** (6, −1)

B′ = (3 + 3, 1 − 2) = (6, −1).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ravi translates A (2, 3) by 4 left and 1 up. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The point A is (2, 3). The translation is 4 left and 1 up.
 2  Left is a move along the x-axis, so I add 4 to x.
 3  Up means add 1 to y.
 4  The image of A is (6, 4).
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: moving left makes x smaller, so take 4 away: 2 − 4 = −2. The image is (−2, 4).

:::


:::keep-together

### What stays the same

**What stays the same when a shape is translated?**

☐ Its coordinates

☐ Only its size

☐ Its position

☐ Its size, its shape and the way it faces

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Its size, its shape and the way it faces

A translation only changes the position. The image is congruent to the object.

## Translations


:::keep-together

### The x-coordinate

**C is at (3, 2). After a translation of 3 right and 2 down, what is the x-coordinate of C′?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

3 + 3 = 6.

:::


:::keep-together

### The y-coordinate

**With the same translation, what is the y-coordinate of C′?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0

2 − 2 = 0, so C′ = (6, 0).

:::


:::keep-together

### Between two points

**Which translation takes (2, 5) to (−1, 3)?**

☐ 1 left and 3 down

☐ 3 right and 2 down

☐ 3 left and 2 up

☐ 3 left and 2 down

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3 left and 2 down

x goes from 2 to −1 (3 left); y goes from 5 to 3 (2 down).

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: quadrants

**Review: in which quadrant is the point (−3, 2)?**

☐ Top right

☐ Bottom left

☐ Top left

☐ Bottom right

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Top left

A negative x is left of the y-axis and a positive y is above the x-axis.

:::
