# Translations

Angles and shapes · Transformations

<!-- Speaker note: Students slide a triangle on the coordinate plane and describe the move by the change in each coordinate (AC9M7SP03, node 1). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 8 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** a translation slides every point of a shape the same distance in the same direction. Right adds to x, left takes away; up adds to y, down takes away. The shape does not change size or turn.

- I can translate a shape and write the new coordinates.
- I can describe a translation as so many units left or right and up or down.
- I can say what changes and what stays the same.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need to plot and read coordinates in all four quadrants (Year 6). The one idea: every point moves the same way, so the same amount is added to every x and every y. The common mistake is adding for left or down (Ravi's mistake). This is the hinge for the transformations node: a coordinate slip here carries into reflections and rotations, so check the Practise answers. Combining two translations (the last Modify question) is a wider idea. -->

---

## Why it matters

> A chess piece slides across the board, a lift goes up, a character steps left on a screen. Each is a translation: every point moves the same way.

<!-- Speaker note: A **translation** slides a shape without turning or flipping it. Every point moves the same distance in the same direction, so the shape keeps its size and shape. The shape is called the **object** and its new position the **image**; a point A becomes A′ (said "A prime"). On the coordinate plane, a move right or up **adds** to a coordinate and a move left or down **takes away**. A translation of 3 right and 2 down changes $(x, y)$ to $(x + 3, y - 2)$. -->

## Find the mistake

**Ravi translates A (2, 3) by 4 left and 1 up. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The point A is (2, 3). The translation is 4 left and 1 up.
 2  Left is a move along the x-axis, so I add 4 to x.
 3  Up means add 1 to y.
 4  The image of A is (6, 4).
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: moving left makes x smaller, so take 4 away: 2 − 4 = −2. The image is (−2, 4). -->

- Its coordinates
