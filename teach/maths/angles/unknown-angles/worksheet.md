# Unknown angles in shapes

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A roof truss, a kite, a bridge brace: builders work out the angle they cannot measure from the ones they can. Every step needs a reason.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find unknown angles in triangles, quadrilaterals and pentagons in steps, with a reason for each (angle sum, straight line, isosceles), and explain why an exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles; working is their own, the answer and a key reason are marked (AC9M7M05, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find an unknown angle in a triangle or polygon using its angle sum. I can combine angle sums with straight-line and parallel-line angles, giving a reason for each step. I can explain why an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Angle sums in steps


:::keep-together

### Triangle, then a straight line

Here is the routine: know, choose the rule, say why.

1. $a = 59^\circ,\; b = 45^\circ$ — Know: the given angles.
2. $c = 180^\circ - 59^\circ - 45^\circ = 76^\circ$ — Choose: $a$, $b$, $c$ are a triangle's angles. Why: angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$.
3. $x = 180^\circ - 76^\circ = 104^\circ$ — Choose: $c$ and $x$ sit on one straight line. Why: angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.
4. $x = 59^\circ + 45^\circ$ — A shortcut you can now explain: an exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

Now find $x$ yourself, your own way.

**Find $x$. Write each step with its reason.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $c = 180^\circ - 59^\circ - 45^\circ = 76^\circ$ (angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$); $x = 180^\circ - 76^\circ = 104^\circ$ ($c$ and $x$ are on a straight line)

c = 76° (angles in a triangle), x = 104° (a straight line with c). Check: 59° + 45° = 104°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zoe finds the fourth angle of a quadrilateral. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  a = 76°, b = 68°, c = 102° (given).
 2  The angles of a shape add to 180°.
 3  d = 180° − 76° − 68° − 102°.
 4  d = −66°.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: a quadrilateral's angles add to (4 − 2) × 180° = 360°, not 180°. d = 360° − 76° − 68° − 102° = 114°. A negative angle on line 4 is the clue that something earlier went wrong.

:::


:::keep-together

### A quadrilateral

**Find $d$, with your reason.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $a + b + c + d = 360^\circ$ (angles in a quadrilateral: $(4 - 2) \times 180^\circ$); $d = 360^\circ - 76^\circ - 68^\circ - 102^\circ = 114^\circ$ (subtract the angles you know)

(4 − 2) × 180° = 360°, so d = 360° − 246° = 114°.

:::

## Unknown angles


:::keep-together

### Triangle

**A triangle has angles of $35^\circ$ and $85^\circ$. Find the third angle.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 60

180° − 35° − 85° = 60°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Quadrilateral

**Three angles of a quadrilateral are $90^\circ$, $85^\circ$ and $120^\circ$. Find the fourth.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 65

360° − 295° = 65°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Exterior angle backwards

**A triangle has an exterior angle of $130^\circ$ at $C$, and $a = 70^\circ$. Find $b$, with your reasons.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $c = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$ ($c$ and the exterior angle are on a straight line); $b = 180^\circ - 70^\circ - 50^\circ = 60^\circ$ (angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$)

c = 50°, b = 60°. Or: 130° = a + b, so b = 60°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: parallel lines

**Review: parallel lines, $c = 70^\circ$. Which is a correct first step to find $e$?**

☐ $e = 70^\circ$: alternate to $c$

☐ $e = 70^\circ$: corresponding to $c$

☐ $e = 110^\circ$: co-interior with $c$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $e = 110^\circ$: co-interior with $c$

Co-interior angles add to 180°: e = 110°.

:::
