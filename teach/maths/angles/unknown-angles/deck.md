# Unknown angles in shapes

Angles and shapes · Angle sums

## Today

**We are learning to** find unknown angles in triangles and polygons in steps, with a reason for each: the angle sum of the shape, angles on a straight line, equal angles in an isosceles triangle. An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

- I can find an unknown angle in a triangle or polygon using its angle sum.
- I can combine angle sums with straight-line and parallel-line angles, giving a reason for each step.
- I can explain why an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

## Triangle, then a straight line

Here is the routine: know, choose the rule, say why.

Now find $x$ yourself, your own way.

$$a = 59^\circ,\; b = 45^\circ$$

1) $c = 180^\circ - 59^\circ - 45^\circ = 76^\circ$ — Choose: $a$, $b$, $c$ are a triangle's angles. Why: angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$.
2) $x = 180^\circ - 76^\circ = 104^\circ$ — Choose: $c$ and $x$ sit on one straight line. Why: angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.
3) $x = 59^\circ + 45^\circ$ — A shortcut you can now explain: an exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

## Find the mistake

**Zoe finds the fourth angle of a quadrilateral. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  a = 76°, b = 68°, c = 102° (given).
 2  The angles of a shape add to 180°.
 3  d = 180° − 76° − 68° − 102°.
 4  d = −66°.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: a quadrilateral's angles add to (4 − 2) × 180° = 360°, not 180°. d = 360° − 76° − 68° − 102° = 114°. A negative angle on line 4 is the clue that something earlier went wrong. -->

## A quadrilateral

**Find $d$, with your reason.**

* Answer: $a + b + c + d = 360^\circ$ (angles in a quadrilateral: $(4 - 2) \times 180^\circ$); $d = 360^\circ - 76^\circ - 68^\circ - 102^\circ = 114^\circ$ (subtract the angles you know)

## Exterior angle backwards

**A triangle has an exterior angle of $130^\circ$ at $C$, and $a = 70^\circ$. Find $b$, with your reasons.**

* Answer: $c = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$ ($c$ and the exterior angle are on a straight line); $b = 180^\circ - 70^\circ - 50^\circ = 60^\circ$ (angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$)

<!-- Speaker note: c = 50°, b = 60°. Or: 130° = a + b, so b = 60°. -->

## Exit ticket

**A triangle has angles of 48° and 67°. Find the third angle, and the exterior angle next to it, with reasons.**

* 180° − 48° − 67° = 65° (angles in a triangle). Exterior angle 180° − 65° = 115° (a straight line). Check: 48° + 67° = 115°.
