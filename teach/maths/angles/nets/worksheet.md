# Nets

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A cereal box starts as one flat piece of card. Which flat shapes fold up into a box, and which ones jam?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build nets on a grid and watch the board fold them into a cube, then find the net area of a triangular prism (AC9M7SP01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell whether a net folds into a cube, and say how I know. I can name the faces in the net of a prism or a pyramid. I can find the surface area of a triangular prism from its net.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Picture wrapping the row round a cube: where does the fifth square go?

:::keep-together

**Six squares joined in a straight row. Will they fold into a cube?**

☐ Only if the row is bent

☐ Yes: a cube has six faces

☐ No: some squares would land on top of each other

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** No. Fold the row round and the sixth square lands on the first. A net needs six squares on six different faces.

## Six squares is not enough


:::keep-together

### How many squares?

**A net folds into a cube. How many squares does it have?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

A cube has 6 faces, so its net has 6 squares.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zara says her block of six squares folds into a cube. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Zara draws six squares in a 2 by 3 block.
 2  A cube has six faces.
 3  Her pattern has six squares.
 4  So her pattern folds into a cube.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: six squares is needed, but they must land on six different faces. A 2 by 3 block has squares that fold on top of each other.

:::

### The test

**What is the best way to test a net for a cube?**

☐ Check that it is a rectangle

☐ Fold it (or roll a cube over it) and check that every face is covered once

☐ Count that it has six squares

☐ Check that it is symmetrical

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Fold it (or roll a cube over it) and check that every face is covered once

Fold it, in your head or on the board: each face must be covered exactly once.

## Nets


:::keep-together

### A pyramid

**What is the net of a square pyramid made of?**

☐ 4 squares and 1 triangle

☐ 2 squares and 4 triangles

☐ 1 square and 4 triangles

☐ 5 squares

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1 square and 4 triangles

A square base and four triangles that meet at the point.

:::


:::keep-together

### Net area

**Read the net's area on the board. The prism's surface area is how many square centimetres?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 168

The net is the surface laid flat, so its area is the surface area.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: triangle area

**Review: a triangle has base 8 cm and height 5 cm. What is its area in square centimetres?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20

(8 × 5) ÷ 2 = 20 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### A box

**How many rectangles are in the net of a rectangular prism (a box)?**

☐ 3

☐ 4

☐ 5

☐ 6

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

A box has 6 faces, all rectangles, in 3 matching pairs (some may be squares).
