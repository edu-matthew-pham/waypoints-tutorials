## Today

**We are learning to** a net is a flat pattern that folds into a solid. Six squares make a cube only if they land on six different faces; a triangular prism has two triangles and three rectangles.

- I can tell whether a net folds into a cube, and say how I know.
- I can name the faces in the net of a prism or a pyramid.
- I can find the surface area of a triangular prism from its net.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first, then fold a real cube net from card if you have time. The one idea: a net works only if every face is covered exactly once. Counting six squares is necessary but not enough (Zara's mistake). The board folds by rolling a cube over the squares, so the colour on each square is the face it becomes. **Six squares joined in a straight row. Will they fold into a cube?**

- Yes: a cube has six faces
- No: some squares would land on top of each other

## The test

**What is the best way to test a net for a cube?**

- Check that it is a rectangle
- Fold it (or roll a cube over it) and check that every face is covered once
- Count that it has six squares
- Check that it is symmetrical

* Answer: Fold it (or roll a cube over it) and check that every face is covered once

## Exit ticket

**A net of six squares has four in a row, one above and one below. Does it fold into a cube? How can you tell?**

* Yes. The row of four wraps round the sides, and the extra two squares are the top and the bottom, so every face is covered once.
