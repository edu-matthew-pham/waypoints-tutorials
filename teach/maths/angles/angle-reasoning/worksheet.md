# Reasoning with angles

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A detective does not guess: every step has evidence. Finding an angle is the same. Each angle you find needs a reason, and the reasons are what make the answer trustworthy.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find unknown angles at parallel lines in two-step chains, with a reason for each step, writing their working their own way; the platform marks the answer and the reason at the key step, then shows a model to compare with (AC9M7M04, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find an unknown angle in two or more steps, using parallel-line, straight-line and vertically opposite angles. I can give a reason for each step, naming the rule. I can use angles to show whether two lines are parallel.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Reasons for every step


:::keep-together

### Know, choose, say why

Here is the routine for every step: what you know, the rule that links it to a new angle, and the reason.

1. $b = 70^\circ$ — Know: the given angle.
2. $f = 70^\circ$ — Choose: $f$ is in the same place as $b$ at the other crossing. Say why: corresponding angles are equal.
3. $h = 180^\circ - 70^\circ$ — Choose: $h$ is next to $f$ on the bottom line. Say why: angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.
4. $h = 110^\circ$ — Done: two steps, two reasons.

Now find $h$ yourself. Write it your own way; the checker marks your answer and the reason for the first step, then shows you a model to compare.

**Find $h$. Write each step with its reason.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $f = 70^\circ$ (corresponding to $b$: corresponding angles are equal); $h = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$ ($f$ and $h$ are on a straight line)

f = 70° (corresponding to b), h = 110° (a straight line with f). Another route: d = 110° (a straight line with b), h = 110° (corresponding to d).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Kai finds $e$ when $b = 70^\circ$. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  b = 70° (given).
 2  b and e look like a pair, so b and e are alternate angles.
 3  Alternate angles are equal, so e = 70°.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: b and e are not a named pair (b is above the top line, e above the bottom line, on different sides). Looking related is not a reason. e = 110°: f = 70° (corresponding to b), then e = 180° − 70° (a straight line).

:::


:::keep-together

### Another chain

**Find $g$. Write each step with its reason.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $e = 115^\circ$ (alternate to $d$: alternate angles are equal); $g = 180^\circ - 115^\circ = 65^\circ$ ($e$ and $g$ are on a straight line)

e = 115° (alternate to d), g = 65° (a straight line with e). Or: f = 65° (co-interior with d), g = 65° (vertically opposite f).

:::
