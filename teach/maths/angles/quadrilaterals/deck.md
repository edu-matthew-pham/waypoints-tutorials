Angles and shapes · Classifying shapes

## Today

**We are learning to** quadrilaterals are named by their properties: parallel sides, equal sides and right angles. A shape that has all of another's properties belongs to it too: every square is a rectangle and a rhombus, and every rectangle is a parallelogram.

- I can name a quadrilateral from its properties.
- I can explain why a square is also a rectangle and a rhombus.
- I can use parallel sides to find angles in a parallelogram.

## Why it matters

> Is a square a rectangle? People argue about it, but mathematicians agree: yes. A name is a list of properties, and a square has every property on the rectangle's list.

<!-- Speaker note: A **quadrilateral** has four sides. Its name comes from its **properties**: | Name | Properties | |---|---| | **trapezium** | at least one pair of parallel sides | | **parallelogram** | two pairs of parallel sides (opposite sides equal) | | **rhombus** | four equal sides (so two pairs of parallel sides) | | **rectangle** | four right angles (so two pairs of parallel sides) | | **square** | four equal sides and four right angles | | **kite** | two pairs of equal sides next to each other | The board marks **parallel** sides with matching arrows, **equal** sides with matching ticks, and right angles with a small square. When a shape has every property of another, it belongs to that family too: a square is also a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram. -->

## Properties first

Here is how to name a quadrilateral: check the properties, from the most special.

Now match each name to its property.

$$\text{4 equal sides, 4 right angles} \Rightarrow \text{square}$$

1) $\text{4 right angles} \Rightarrow \text{rectangle}$ — Right angles but sides not all equal.
2) $\text{4 equal sides} \Rightarrow \text{rhombus}$ — Equal sides but no right angles.
3) $\text{2 pairs parallel} \Rightarrow \text{parallelogram}$ — Then parallelogram, trapezium (at least one pair), kite.

## Properties first

**Match each quadrilateral to its defining property.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `trapezium` | A. four equal sides |
| `parallelogram` | B. two pairs of parallel sides |
| `rhombus` | C. two pairs of equal sides next to each other |
| `rectangle` | D. four right angles |
| `kite` | E. at least one pair of parallel sides |

* Answer: trapezium → E. at least one pair of parallel sides; parallelogram → B. two pairs of parallel sides; rhombus → A. four equal sides; rectangle → D. four right angles; kite → C. two pairs of equal sides next to each other

## Find the mistake

**Sam decides whether a square is a rectangle. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  A square has four right angles.
 2  A rectangle is a quadrilateral with four right angles.
 3  A square also has two pairs of parallel sides, like a rectangle.
 4  But a square is not a rectangle, because all its sides are equal.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: having an extra property (equal sides) does not take away the rectangle's properties. A square has four right angles, so it is a rectangle: a special one. -->

## Always true?

**Which is always true?**

- Every kite is a rhombus
- Every rhombus is a parallelogram
- Every rectangle is a square
- Every parallelogram is a rhombus

* Answer: Every rhombus is a parallelogram
