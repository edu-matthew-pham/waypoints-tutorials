# Angles in a polygon

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Stop signs are octagons, honeycomb cells are hexagons, a soccer ball is pentagons and hexagons. The triangle rule tells you the angles of all of them.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students split polygons into triangles from one corner, find that a shape with n sides makes n − 2 triangles, and use (n − 2) × 180° for the angle sum (AC9M7M05, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can split a polygon into triangles from one corner and find its angle sum. I can explain why a shape with n sides has an angle sum of (n − 2) × 180°. I can find the number of sides from an angle sum.

## A triangle for each side, less two


:::keep-together

### Splitting from one corner

Here is the method for a pentagon.

1. $\text{A pentagon has 5 corners}$ — Choose one corner, $A$.
2. $\text{Lines from A to C and to D}$ — Join $A$ to every corner that is not next to it.
3. $3 \text{ triangles}$ — The two lines split the pentagon into 3 triangles.
4. $\text{Their angles fill the pentagon's corners}$ — Every triangle's corners are at the pentagon's corners, and together they fill them exactly.
5. $3 \times 180° = 540°$ — So the pentagon's angles add to 540°.
6. $(n - 2) \times 180°$ — A shape with $n$ sides splits into $n - 2$ triangles this way.

**Split shapes on the board, or in your head, and fill in the table.**

{{rows 1}}

| sides | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| triangles from one corner | 1 |   |   |   |   |
| angle sum (°) | 180 |   |   |   |   |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2, 3, 4, 6, 360, 540, 720, 1080

Each shape makes 2 fewer triangles than it has sides, and each triangle adds 180°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ben finds the angle sum of a pentagon. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ben wants the angle sum of a pentagon.
 2  He puts a point in the middle and draws a line to each corner.
 3  That makes 5 triangles.
 4  5 × 180° = 900°, so the pentagon's angles add to 900°.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: his 5 triangles also have angles around the middle point, a full turn of 360°, which are not angles of the pentagon. 900° − 360° = 540°.

### Equal angles

**A regular hexagon has 6 equal angles. How big is each one?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 120

The angles add to 720°, so each is 720° ÷ 6 = 120°.

:::


:::keep-together

### Backwards

**A polygon's angles add to $900°$. How many sides does it have?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

900° ÷ 180° = 5 triangles, and a shape with 7 sides makes 7 − 2 = 5 triangles.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: a triangle has angles $35°$ and $75°$, and a third angle. Do the three add to more than, less than, or exactly $180°$?**

☐ Exactly $180°$

☐ Less than $180°$: $35 + 75 = 110$

☐ It depends on the third angle

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Exactly $180°$

Every triangle's angles add to 180°, so the third angle is 180° − 110° = 70°.
