## Today

**We are learning to** every point on the number line is a real number, rational or irrational. To place √30, find the perfect squares either side: 25 < 30 < 36, so √30 is between 5 and 6, nearer 5. A rounded value like 5.48 is close, not exact.

- I can find the two whole numbers a square root is between.
- I can place a root or π to the nearest tenth.
- I can tell an exact value from an approximation.

## Why it matters

> A carpenter needs a brace across a 1 m by 2 m gate: √5 metres. Where is √5 on a tape measure, and how close is close enough?

<!-- Speaker note: The **real numbers** are every point on the number line: the rational numbers and the irrational ones together. Between any two numbers, however close, there are both. To place a square root, find the **perfect squares** either side: $$25 < 30 < 36 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 5 < \sqrt{30} < 6$$ 30 is a little nearer 25 than 36, so $\sqrt{30}$ is a bit under 5.5 (check: $5.5^2 = 30.25$). To get closer, square decimals: $5.4^2 = 29.16$ and $5.5^2 = 30.25$; then $5.47^2 = 29.92$ and $5.48^2 = 30.03$, so $\sqrt{30} \approx 5.48$. A rounded value is an **approximation**; the exact value is $\sqrt{30}$. -->
