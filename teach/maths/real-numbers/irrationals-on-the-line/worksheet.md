# Irrational numbers on the line

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A carpenter needs a brace across a 1 m by 2 m gate: √5 metres. Where is √5 on a tape measure, and how close is close enough?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place square roots and π on the number line using the perfect squares either side and decimal squares, and decide when an approximation is enough (AC9M8N01, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the two whole numbers a square root is between. I can place a root or π to the nearest tenth. I can tell an exact value from an approximation.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Squares either side

### Squares either side

**√50 is between two whole numbers. What is the smaller one?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

7² = 49 and 8² = 64, so √50 is between 7 and 8 (just over 7).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ben places √50 on a number line. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ben places √50 on a number line.
 2  Half of 50 is 25, so √50 is about 25.
 3  He marks it between 25 and 26.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: a square root is the number that multiplies by itself to give 50, not half of it. 7² = 49, so √50 is just over 7.

:::

### Bounds

**√20 is between two whole numbers. What is the smaller one?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4

16 &lt; 20 &lt; 25, so √20 is between 4 and 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Compare

**Which is bigger: √8 or 2.8?**

☐ They are equal

☐ √8

☐ 2.8

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** √8

2.8² = 7.84 &lt; 8, so √8 &gt; 2.8 (√8 = 2.828…).

:::


:::keep-together

### Round

**Write √2 rounded to 2 decimal places.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

√2 = 1.41421…, so 1.41.
