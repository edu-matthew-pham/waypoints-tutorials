# Exact or approximate?

Irrational numbers and index laws · Year 10: approximation

<!-- Speaker note: Students find that multiplying exact roots gives √50 × √2 = 10 while rounded decimals give 9.9687, and learn that an approximation swaps an exact number for a nearby different one (AC9M10N01, node 1). ## Today

- I can say which of two numbers is exact and which is an approximation.
- I can say what is lost when a root is replaced by a decimal.

## Exact first

Here's the builder's way, and what rounding does to it.

Now try one on paper: a patch of 18 m² has a path of $\sqrt{18} \times \sqrt{2}$. Work it out exactly, then type it.

$$\sqrt{50} \times \sqrt{2}$$

1) $= \sqrt{50 \times 2}$ — Two roots multiply into one root.
2) $= \sqrt{100}$ — Multiply under the root.
3) $= 10$ — A whole number. Nothing was rounded, so nothing was lost.
4) $7.07 \times 1.41 = 9.9687$ — Round each root first and the 10 is gone: close, not exact.

## Find the mistake

**Mia works out the path with rounded numbers. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  A square patch has an area of 50 m². Find the path from corner to corner.
 2  The side is √50, which is about 7.07 m.
 3  The path is the side times √2, which is about 7.07 × 1.41 = 9.9687 m.
 4  So the path is exactly 9.9687 m.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: 7.07 × 1.41 = 9.9687 is the product of two rounded numbers, so it's close but not exact. The path is exactly √50 × √2 = 10 m. -->

## What did rounding change?

**What changes when $\sqrt{50}$ is replaced by 7.07?**

- Nothing: they are the same number
- It becomes a bit smaller, by exactly 0.01
- It becomes a negative number
- It becomes a different number, one that stops after 2 places

* Answer: It becomes a different number, one that stops after 2 places

<!-- Speaker note: 7.07 is the fraction 707/100. It stops, but √50 never does, so no decimal is exactly √50. -->
