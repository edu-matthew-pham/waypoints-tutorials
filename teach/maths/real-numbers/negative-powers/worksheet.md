> **Key question:** Scientists write a nanometre as 10⁻⁹ metres. How can a power be negative, and what does it mean?

**What happens:** 10⁻⁶. A negative power is not a negative number. It's a reciprocal: 10⁻⁶ = 1/10⁶ = 0.000001, a millionth. 10⁶ is a million; −10⁶ is minus a million.

## Reciprocals


:::keep-together

### Reciprocal

Here's the pattern that makes negative powers make sense.

1. $10^3 = 1000$ — Start high.
2. $10^2 = 100$ — Divide by 10 each time.
3. $10^1 = 10$ — Still dividing by 10.
4. $10^0 = 1$ — The pattern says one.
5. $10^{-1} = 1 \div 10 = \tfrac{1}{10}$ — Keep going.
6. $10^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{100}$ — A hundredth.

Now try one on paper: $10^{-4}$ as a fraction and as a decimal. Then type the decimal.

**Work out $2^{-3}$. Give a fraction.**

**Answer:** line 3
