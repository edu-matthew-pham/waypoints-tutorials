## Today

**We are learning to** dividing a fraction out gives a decimal that either stops (terminates) or repeats a block of digits forever (recurs). In long division, a remainder of 0 means it stops; a remainder that comes back means it repeats. A bar or dots mark the repeating digits.

- I can tell whether a fraction's decimal terminates or recurs.
- I can write a recurring decimal with a bar or dots.
- I can explain why a remainder coming back means the digits repeat.

## Why it matters

> Type 1 ÷ 7 into a calculator and the screen fills up. Does it ever stop? Long division can tell you, and it does not need a bigger screen.

<!-- Speaker note: A fraction is a division: $\frac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8$. Doing the division gives a decimal that does one of two things: - it **terminates** (stops): $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$; - it **recurs** (repeats a block of digits forever): $\frac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$, $\frac{1}{7} = 0.142857142857\ldots$ Long division shows why. Each step has a **remainder**, and the remainder is always less than the denominator. If a remainder is 0, the decimal stops. If not, a remainder must come back sooner or later, and from then on the same digits repeat. The repeating block is marked with a **bar** over it, or **dots** over its first and last digits: $0.\overline{142857}$ or $0.\dot{1}4285\dot{7}$; $0.1\overline{6}$ or $0.1\dot{6}$ means $0.1666\ldots$ -->

## Notation

Here is the routine.

$$1 \div 6$$

1) $40 \div 6 = 6 \text{ r } 4$ — Remainder 4 again.
2) $\tfrac{1}{6} = 0.1\overline{6}$ — The 6 repeats forever; the 1 does not.
3) $\tfrac{1}{8} = 0.125$ — A remainder of 0: it stops.

## Find the mistake

**Zoe decides whether 1/7 is a rational number (a fraction). Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Zoe divides 1 by 7 on a calculator.
 2  The screen shows 0.1428571429.
 3  The digits go on past the end of the screen.
 4  A decimal that never stops is irrational, so 1/7 is irrational.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: 1/7 is a fraction, so it is rational. Its decimal never stops, but it repeats (142857). Only a decimal that never stops and never repeats is irrational. -->
