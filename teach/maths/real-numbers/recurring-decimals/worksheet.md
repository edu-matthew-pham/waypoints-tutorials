> **Key question:** Type 1 ÷ 7 into a calculator and the screen fills up. Does it ever stop? Long division can tell you, and it does not need a bigger screen.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students divide fractions out on a long-division board, watch the remainders, and see why decimals either stop or repeat, writing recurring decimals with bars and dots (AC9M8N03, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell whether a fraction's decimal terminates or recurs. I can write a recurring decimal with a bar or dots. I can explain why a remainder coming back means the digits repeat.

## Why it repeats


:::keep-together

### Notation

Here is the routine.

1. $1 \div 6$ — Bring down a 0: 10 ÷ 6 = 1 remainder 4.
2. $40 \div 6 = 6 \text{ r } 4$ — Remainder 4 again.
3. $\tfrac{1}{6} = 0.1\overline{6}$ — The 6 repeats forever; the 1 does not.
4. $\tfrac{1}{8} = 0.125$ — A remainder of 0: it stops.

**How should 0.272727… be written with a bar?**

☐ A bar over 2 only

☐ A bar over 27: 0.27 with the bar over both digits

☐ A bar over the last 7 only

☐ 0.27 with no bar

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A bar over 27: 0.27 with the bar over both digits

The repeating block is 27, so the bar goes over both digits.

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: 1/7 is a fraction, so it is rational. Its decimal never stops, but it repeats (142857). Only a decimal that never stops and never repeats is irrational.
