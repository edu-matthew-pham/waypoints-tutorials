# Which decimals stop?

Irrational numbers and index laws · Recurring decimals

<!-- Speaker note: Students predict whether a fraction's decimal terminates from the prime factors of its simplified denominator, and check on the long-division board (AC9M8N03, node 2). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 8 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** write the fraction in simplest form and break the denominator into primes. If its only prime factors are 2 and 5, the decimal terminates; any other prime factor makes it recur. It works because 10 = 2 × 5.

- I can predict whether a fraction terminates without dividing.
- I can explain the rule using 10 = 2 × 5.
- I can simplify a fraction before using the rule.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need prime factors (Number Foundations) and the previous lesson. The common mistake is skipping the simplifying (Max's mistake). The board shows the simplest form and the prime factors with the long division. Wider: in base 12 (12 = 2 × 2 × 3), thirds would terminate and fifths would recur. -->

## Why it matters

> Without dividing anything, you can tell that 7/40 stops and 7/30 goes on forever. The secret is in the denominator's prime factors.

<!-- Speaker note: A terminating decimal is a fraction over 10, 100, 1000…: $0.175 = \frac{175}{1000}$. Since $10 = 2 \times 5$, those denominators are made only of 2s and 5s. So: 1. Write the fraction in **simplest form**. 2. Break the denominator into **prime factors**. 3. Only 2s and 5s: it **terminates**. Any other prime (3, 7, 11…): it **recurs**. $\frac{7}{40}$: $40 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 5$, so it terminates (0.175). $\frac{7}{30}$: $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$ has a 3, so it recurs (0.2333…). -->

## Why it works

**Why do only 2s and 5s in the denominator give a terminating decimal?**

- Because 2 + 5 = 7
- 2 and 5 are the smallest primes
- 10 = 2 × 5, so only those can multiply up to 10, 100, 1000…
- Because even numbers always terminate

* Answer: 10 = 2 × 5, so only those can multiply up to 10, 100, 1000…
