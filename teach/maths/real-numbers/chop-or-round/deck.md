# Chop or round?

Irrational numbers and index laws · Year 10: approximation

<!-- Speaker note: Students chop and round four sale prices, see that chopping always goes down so the errors pile up while rounding errors go both ways and cancel, and work out what a chopping till loses over a week (AC9M10N01, node 2). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can chop and round a price to the cent and get the right answers.
- I can say which of chopping and rounding is biased, and why.
- I can work out what a bias adds up to over many sales.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run step yourself first. Students need to know how to round to a place (Number Foundations, Rounding) and Exact or approximate? (the lesson before). The one idea: chopping always moves a number down, so the errors all point one way and add up; rounding moves numbers up or down roughly equally, so the errors mostly cancel. The common mistake is calling chopping "rounding" (Rosa's working). Everything here is made up: the prices ($2.37, $4.59, $3.94, $6.28 with 15% off), the number of sales (4000 a day) and the six-day week. The half-a-cent average assumes the digits past the cent are spread evenly; across eight made-up prices the chop lost 0.41 cents per sale, a little under that, which is expected for a small sample. No real figures, so no sources. Teacher: Paper: the try-one after the worked example and the week in Make are worked on paper first. Books out, and look for the digit after the cent being checked before rounding. -->

## Round it properly

Here's one price chopped and rounded, and how far each one is from the exact price.

Now try one on paper: round $5.3380 to the nearest cent, and see how far from the exact price it is. Then type the rounded price.

$$\$3.3490$$

1) $\text{chop} \to \$3.34$ — Cut after the cent. $0.0090 below the exact price.
2) $\text{round} \to \$3.35$ — The next digit is 9, so go up. $0.0010 above.
3) $\$1.5555$ — Another price.
4) $\text{chop} \to \$1.55$ — $0.0055 below.
5) $\text{round} \to \$1.56$ — The next digit is 5, so go up. $0.0045 above.

## Find the mistake

**Rosa rounds $1.5555 to the nearest cent. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Round $1.5555 to the nearest cent.
 2  The cents are 1.55, and 55 is left over after them.
 3  Drop the left over digits, so the rounded price is $1.55.
 4  Rounded to the nearest cent: $1.55.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: dropping the left over digits is chopping, not rounding. The next digit is 5, so round up: $1.56. -->

## Which way does it drift?

**A till handles thousands of prices with digits all over the place. Which method keeps drifting in the same direction?**

- Rounding, because it always goes up
- Chopping, because it always goes down
- Neither: they both come out even
- Both drift the same amount

* Answer: Chopping, because it always goes down

<!-- Speaker note: Every chop removes something, so the errors all add. Rounding's errors point up or down about equally, so they mostly cancel. -->
