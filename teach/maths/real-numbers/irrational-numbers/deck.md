## Today

**We are learning to** a rational number is a fraction: its decimal stops or repeats. An irrational number cannot be written as a fraction: its decimal never stops and never repeats. √2, √3 and π are irrational; √16 = 4 is not.

- I can say what makes a number rational or irrational.
- I can sort numbers such as √9, √5, π and 0.333… correctly.
- I can explain why a rounded value is not the exact number.

## Why it matters

> The diagonal of a 1 m square tile is exactly √2 metres long. You can measure it, but you can never write it down as a fraction or a decimal that ends.

<!-- Speaker note: A **rational** number can be written as a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ of integers (whole numbers or their negatives, with $b \ne 0$): 0.25, $\frac{1}{7}$, −3, $0.\overline{3}$. Its decimal **terminates** or **recurs**. An **irrational** number cannot be written as a fraction. Its decimal goes on **forever without repeating**. Examples: $\sqrt{2} = 1.41421356\ldots$, $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{5}$, and $\pi = 3.14159265\ldots$ The square root of a whole number is irrational unless the number is a perfect square: $\sqrt{16} = 4$ is rational. Irrational numbers are ordinary numbers you can calculate with; we use the exact form ($\sqrt{2}$, $\pi$) or a rounded value (1.414, 3.14). -->

## Sort them

Here is the routine.

$$\text{a fraction?}$$

1) $\text{decimal stops or repeats?}$ — Yes: rational (it is a fraction).
2) $\sqrt{n},\ n \text{ a perfect square?}$ — √16 = 4: rational. √15: irrational.
3) $\pi,\ \sqrt{2},\ \sqrt{3}$ — Never stop, never repeat: irrational.

## Using them

**Can you calculate with irrational numbers?**

- Only with a calculator that has infinite digits
- No: they never end
- Only if you round them to whole numbers
- Yes: use the exact form (√2, π) or a rounded value (1.414, 3.14)

* Answer: Yes: use the exact form (√2, π) or a rounded value (1.414, 3.14)
