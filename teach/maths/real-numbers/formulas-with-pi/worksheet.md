## Π stays exact


:::keep-together

### Keep it exact

Here's the comparison without ever using π's digits.

1. $r = 15$ — Large: half of 30.
2. $A = \pi \times 15^2 = 225\pi$ — Keep π.
3. $r = 11.5$ — Medium: half of 23.
4. $A = \pi \times 11.5^2 = 132.25\pi$ — One medium.
5. $2 \times 132.25\pi = 264.5\pi$ — Two mediums.
6. $264.5 > 225$ — The π is on both sides: compare the numbers.

Now try one on paper: one circle of radius 6 cm or two of radius 4 cm. Which is bigger, and by how many π? Type the difference in π units (a number).

**What's the exact area of a circle of radius 5 cm?**

☐ $25$ cm²

☐ $10\pi$ cm²

☐ $78.5$ cm²

☐ $25\pi$ cm²

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $25\pi$ cm²

A = πr² = π × 25 = 25π cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Pia finds the large pizza's area. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Area of the large pizza, radius 15 cm.
 2  A = π × 15².
 3  Use π = 3.
 4  3 × 225 = 675, so the area is exactly 675 cm².
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: with π = 3 the area is only approximate. The exact area is 225π cm², about 707 cm².

:::


:::keep-together

### The bigger number

**A pizza of radius 15 cm has area $225\pi$. Two mediums have area $264.5\pi$ in total. Which has the bigger number in front of π? Type that number.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 264.5

264.5 is bigger than 225, so two mediums are more pizza. The π is the same on both sides, so it doesn't matter what its digits are.
