## Why it matters

> A large pizza or two medium ones: which is more pizza? Your answer depends on a number that never ends.

## Predict

**A shop sells a large pizza that's 30 cm across and a medium that's 23 cm across. Is one large pizza more pizza than two mediums?**

- They're about the same
- Yes: one large is more pizza
- No: two mediums are more pizza
- You can't tell without the price

## What happened?

* No: two mediums are more. The large has area π × 15² = 225π ≈ 707 cm². A medium has π × 11.5² = 132.25π, so two have 264.5π ≈ 831 cm².

## Keep it exact

Here's the comparison without ever using π's digits.

Now try one on paper: one circle of radius 6 cm or two of radius 4 cm. Which is bigger, and by how many π? Type the difference in π units (a number).

$$r = 15$$

1) $A = \pi \times 15^2 = 225\pi$ — Keep π.
2) $r = 11.5$ — Medium: half of 23.
3) $A = \pi \times 11.5^2 = 132.25\pi$ — One medium.
4) $2 \times 132.25\pi = 264.5\pi$ — Two mediums.
5) $264.5 > 225$ — The π is on both sides: compare the numbers.

## Find the mistake

**Pia finds the large pizza's area. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Area of the large pizza, radius 15 cm.
 2  A = π × 15².
 3  Use π = 3.
 4  3 × 225 = 675, so the area is exactly 675 cm².
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: with π = 3 the area is only approximate. The exact area is 225π cm², about 707 cm². -->

## The bigger number

**A pizza of radius 15 cm has area $225\pi$. Two mediums have area $264.5\pi$ in total. Which has the bigger number in front of π? Type that number.**

* Answer: 264.5

<!-- Speaker note: 264.5 is bigger than 225, so two mediums are more pizza. The π is the same on both sides, so it doesn't matter what its digits are. -->
