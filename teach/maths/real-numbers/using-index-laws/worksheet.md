# Using the index laws

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Scientists write the distance to the Sun as 1.5 × 10⁸ km. Multiplying numbers like that takes seconds with the index laws, as long as you only join powers of the same base.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students combine the index laws one step at a time, check that the bases match before joining powers, and use the laws with powers of 10 (AC9M8N02, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can simplify an expression using more than one index law. I can tell when powers can be joined (same base) and when they cannot. I can multiply numbers in scientific notation using the laws.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## One step at a time


:::keep-together

### Step by step

Here is the routine.

1. $(2^3)^2 \times 2^4 \div 2^5$ — Powers of powers first.
2. $= 2^6 \times 2^4 \div 2^5$ — 3 × 2 = 6.
3. $= 2^{10} \div 2^5$ — Then left to right: 6 + 4 = 10.
4. $= 2^5 = 32$ — 10 − 5 = 5.

**Simplify 5⁴ × 5³ ÷ 5⁵. 5 to what power?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2

5⁴ × 5³ = 5⁷, then 5⁷ ÷ 5⁵ = 5².

:::


:::keep-together

```
 1  Lia simplifies 2⁴ × 5².

 3  Multiply the bases: 2 × 5 = 10.
 4  So 2⁴ × 5² = 10⁶.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the indices only add for the same base. 2⁴ × 5² = 16 × 25 = 400, not a million.

## Using the laws


:::keep-together

### Power then product

**Simplify (3²)³ × 3. 3 to what power?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7

(3²)³ = 3⁶, and 3⁶ × 3¹ = 3⁷.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two divisions

**Simplify 6⁸ ÷ 6³ ÷ 6². 6 to what power?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

8 − 3 − 2 = 3: 6³.

:::


:::keep-together

### Adding powers

**What is 2³ + 2³?**

☐ 2⁶ (64)

☐ 2⁴ (16)

☐ 4³

☐ 4⁶

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2⁴ (16)

8 + 8 = 16 = 2⁴. Adding two equal powers doubles one.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: evaluate

**Review: what is 4³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

4 × 4 × 4 = 64.
