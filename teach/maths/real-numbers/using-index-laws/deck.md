# Using the index laws

Irrational numbers and index laws · Index laws

<!-- Speaker note: Students combine the index laws one step at a time, check that the bases match before joining powers, and use the laws with powers of 10 (AC9M8N02, node 2). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 8 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** simplify one step at a time: powers of powers first, then multiply and divide from left to right, keeping each base. The laws only join powers of the same base: 2³ × 3² cannot become one power. They also make scientific notation quick.

- I can simplify an expression using more than one index law.
- I can tell when powers can be joined (same base) and when they cannot.
- I can multiply numbers in scientific notation using the laws.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the index laws (previous lesson). The one idea: apply one law at a time (powers of powers, then multiply and divide left to right), and only for powers of the same base. The common mistake is joining different bases (Lia's mistake). Adding powers (2³ + 2³) is not covered by the laws: a good discussion. Wider: dividing in scientific notation; a systematic order for long expressions. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Scientists write the distance to the Sun as 1.5 × 10⁸ km. Multiplying numbers like that takes seconds with the index laws, as long as you only join powers of the same base.

<!-- Speaker note: Simplify an expression with several powers one law at a time, keeping track of each base: $$(2^3)^2 \times 2^4 \div 2^5 = 2^6 \times 2^4 \div 2^5 = 2^{10} \div 2^5 = 2^5$$ The laws only work for powers of the **same base**. $2^3 \times 3^2 = 8 \times 9 = 72$: it is not $6^5$, and it cannot be written as one power of a whole number by the laws. In **scientific notation**, the powers of 10 follow the laws: $(3 \times 10^4) \times (2 \times 10^3) = 6 \times 10^7$. -->

## Step by step

Here is the routine.

$$(2^3)^2 \times 2^4 \div 2^5$$

1) $= 2^6 \times 2^4 \div 2^5$ — 3 × 2 = 6.
2) $= 2^{10} \div 2^5$ — Then left to right: 6 + 4 = 10.
3) $= 2^5 = 32$ — 10 − 5 = 5.
