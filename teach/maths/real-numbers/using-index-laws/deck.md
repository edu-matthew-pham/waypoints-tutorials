## Why it matters

> Scientists write the distance to the Sun as 1.5 × 10⁸ km. How do you multiply numbers like that quickly?

<!-- Speaker note: Simplify an expression with several powers one law at a time, keeping track of each base: $$(2^3)^2 \times 2^4 \div 2^5 = 2^6 \times 2^4 \div 2^5 = 2^{10} \div 2^5 = 2^5$$ In **scientific notation**, the powers of 10 follow the laws (the Sun is about 150 million km from Earth, or $1.5 \times 10^8$ km: [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/)): $(3 \times 10^4) \times (2 \times 10^3) = 6 \times 10^7$. -->

## Step by step

Here is the routine.

$$(2^3)^2 \times 2^4 \div 2^5$$

1) $= 2^6 \times 2^4 \div 2^5$ — 3 × 2 = 6.
2) $= 2^{10} \div 2^5$ — Then left to right: 6 + 4 = 10.
3) $= 2^5 = 32$ — 10 − 5 = 5.
