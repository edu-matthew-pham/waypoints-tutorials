## Three laws and zero


:::keep-together

### Divide

Here is the routine.

1. $3^5 \div 3^2 = \frac{3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3}{3 \times 3}$ — Write both powers out.
2. $= 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 3^3$ — Two pairs cancel: 5 − 2 = 3 factors left.
3. $(2^3)^2 = 2^3 \times 2^3 = 2^6$ — A power of a power: 2 groups of 3 factors, 3 × 2.
4. $2^3 \div 2^3 = 1 = 2^0$ — Everything cancels: so 2⁰ = 1.

**5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5 to what power?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4

Two pairs of 5s cancel, leaving four: 5⁴.

:::


:::keep-together

### Zero

**Why is 5⁰ equal to 1?**

☐ 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1, and the quotient law gives 5³⁻³ = 5⁰

☐ Because any number to a power is at least 1

☐ It is a rule with no reason

☐ Because 0 × 5 = 0, and then add 1

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1, and the quotient law gives 5³⁻³ = 5⁰

Dividing anything by itself gives 1, and the law says it is a⁰.
