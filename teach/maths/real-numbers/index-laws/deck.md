# Index laws

Irrational numbers and index laws · Index laws

<!-- Speaker note: Students write powers out as factors on the board and discover the product, quotient and power laws and why a⁰ = 1 (AC9M8N02, node 1). About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** write powers out as factors and the laws appear: multiplying powers of the same base adds the indices, dividing subtracts them, and a power of a power multiplies them. Anything (except 0) to the power 0 is 1.

- I can explain each index law by writing the powers out.
- I can use the laws to simplify powers of the same base.
- I can explain why a⁰ = 1.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need exponent notation (Number Foundations: Powers). The quotient board keeps m at least n (negative indices are Year 9). Wider: the pattern 2³, 2², 2¹, 2⁰ (halving each time) also gives 2⁰ = 1, and leads to 2⁻¹ = ½. -->

## Why it matters

> 2¹⁰ × 2¹⁰ has over a million as its answer, but you can write it down in a second: 2²⁰. Write the powers out once, and you will see why.

<!-- Speaker note: A power is repeated multiplication: $2^3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2$. The **base** is 2 and the **index** (exponent) is 3. Writing powers out as factors gives the **index laws** for powers of the **same base**: | Law | Why | Example | |---|---|---| | $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$ | the factors join | $2^3 \times 2^2 = 2^5$ | | $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$ | pairs of factors cancel | $3^5 \div 3^2 = 3^3$ | | $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ | $n$ groups of $m$ factors | $(2^3)^2 = 2^6$ | | $a^0 = 1$ | $a^n \div a^n = 1$ and $= a^0$ | $7^0 = 1$ | -->

## Divide

Here is the routine.

$$3^5 \div 3^2 = \frac{3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3}{3 \times 3}$$

1) $= 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 3^3$ — Two pairs cancel: 5 − 2 = 3 factors left.
2) $(2^3)^2 = 2^3 \times 2^3 = 2^6$ — A power of a power: 2 groups of 3 factors, 3 × 2.
3) $2^3 \div 2^3 = 1 = 2^0$ — Everything cancels: so 2⁰ = 1.

## Zero

**Why is 5⁰ equal to 1?**

- 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1, and the quotient law gives 5³⁻³ = 5⁰
- Because any number to a power is at least 1
- It is a rule with no reason
- Because 0 × 5 = 0, and then add 1

* Answer: 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1, and the quotient law gives 5³⁻³ = 5⁰

<!-- Speaker note: Dividing anything by itself gives 1, and the law says it is a⁰. -->
