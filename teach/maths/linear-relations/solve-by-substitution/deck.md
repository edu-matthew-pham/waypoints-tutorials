# Solving by substitution

Linear relations · Year 10: two variables

<!-- Speaker note: Students solve two equations in two letters by substitution: one equation says what a letter is, they replace that letter in the other (in brackets), solve, and find the second letter (AC9M10A02, node 3, first of two lessons on the hinge). They also meet a pair with no solution and a pair with infinitely many. The plan prices are made up. About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 16 min · Modify 11 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can replace one letter with an expression from the other equation, using brackets.
- I can solve for one letter, then find the other.
- I can tell what a false result such as 10 = 14, or a true one such as 0 = 0, means.

## Why it matters

> Two phone plans, two price rules, one cost they share. Feed one rule into the other and a pair of unknowns becomes one.

## Setting the rules equal

**Lite is $c = 12 + 4g$ and Big is $c = 30 + g$. Replace $c$ in the Big rule with the Lite rule. Which equation do you get?**

- $12 + 4g + 30 + g = c$
- $12 + 4g = 30 + g$
- $c = 42 + 5g$
- $4g = 30 + g$

* Answer: $12 + 4g = 30 + g$

<!-- Speaker note: Big says c = 30 + g. Where c = 12 + 4g too, the two right-hand sides are equal: 12 + 4g = 30 + g. -->

## Solving for g

**Solve $12 + 4g = 30 + g$ for $g$.**

* Answer: 6

## Find the mistake

**Zoe solves the pair $c = g + 3$ and $5c - 2g = 21$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Solve c = g + 3 and 5c − 2g = 21.
 2  Replace c in the second equation by g + 3: 5g + 3 − 2g = 21.
 3  Collect the g terms: 3g + 3 = 21, so 3g = 18 and g = 6.
 4  Then c = 6 + 3 = 9.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: she replaced c by g + 3 but only multiplied the g by 5. 5c is 5(g + 3) = 5g + 15. With the brackets, 3g + 15 = 21, so g = 2 and c = 5. -->

## Try one on paper

Here is the whole routine, on a pair with a bracket in it.

Now try one on paper with the example beside you: the pair $y = x - 5$ and $4y = 3x$. Find $x$, then type it.

$$y = x - 4,\; 3y = 2x$$

1) $3(x - 4) = 2x$ — Replace $y$ by $x - 4$. Put it in brackets so the 3 multiplies all of it.
2) $3x - 12 = 2x$ — Expand the bracket.
3) $x = 12$ — Take $2x$ from both sides and add 12.
4) $y = 12 - 4 = 8$ — Put $x$ into the simpler equation to find $y$.
5) $3 \times 8 = 24 = 2 \times 12$ — Check in the equation you did not use for $y$.

## Try one on paper

**Solve the pair $y = x - 5$ and $4y = 3x$ on paper by substituting. What is $x$?**

* Answer: $4(x - 5) = 3x$ (replace y by x − 5, in brackets); $4x - 20 = 3x$ (expand: 4 times both parts); $x = 20$ (take 3x from both sides and add 20)
