# Solving by substitution

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two phone plans, two price rules, one cost they share. Feed one rule into the other and a pair of unknowns becomes one.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students solve two equations in two letters by substitution: one equation says what a letter is, they replace that letter in the other (in brackets), solve, and find the second letter (AC9M10A02, node 3, first of two lessons on the hinge). They also meet a pair with no solution and a pair with infinitely many. The plan prices are made up.

**Students should be able to say:** I can replace one letter with an expression from the other equation, using brackets. I can solve for one letter, then find the other. I can tell what a false result such as 10 = 14, or a true one such as 0 = 0, means.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Put one rule into the other


:::keep-together

### Setting the rules equal

**Lite is $c = 12 + 4g$ and Big is $c = 30 + g$. Replace $c$ in the Big rule with the Lite rule. Which equation do you get?**

☐ $12 + 4g + 30 + g = c$

☐ $12 + 4g = 30 + g$

☐ $c = 42 + 5g$

☐ $4g = 30 + g$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $12 + 4g = 30 + g$

Big says c = 30 + g. Where c = 12 + 4g too, the two right-hand sides are equal: 12 + 4g = 30 + g.

:::


:::keep-together

### Solving for g

**Solve $12 + 4g = 30 + g$ for $g$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

Take g from both sides: 12 + 3g = 30. Take 12: 3g = 18. So g = 6.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zoe solves the pair $c = g + 3$ and $5c - 2g = 21$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Solve c = g + 3 and 5c − 2g = 21.
 2  Replace c in the second equation by g + 3: 5g + 3 − 2g = 21.
 3  Collect the g terms: 3g + 3 = 21, so 3g = 18 and g = 6.
 4  Then c = 6 + 3 = 9.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: she replaced c by g + 3 but only multiplied the g by 5. 5c is 5(g + 3) = 5g + 15. With the brackets, 3g + 15 = 21, so g = 2 and c = 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Try one on paper

Here is the whole routine, on a pair with a bracket in it.

1. $y = x - 4,\; 3y = 2x$ — One equation already says what $y$ is. Use it to replace $y$ in the other.
2. $3(x - 4) = 2x$ — Replace $y$ by $x - 4$. Put it in brackets so the 3 multiplies all of it.
3. $3x - 12 = 2x$ — Expand the bracket.
4. $x = 12$ — Take $2x$ from both sides and add 12.
5. $y = 12 - 4 = 8$ — Put $x$ into the simpler equation to find $y$.
6. $3 \times 8 = 24 = 2 \times 12$ — Check in the equation you did not use for $y$.

Now try one on paper with the example beside you: the pair $y = x - 5$ and $4y = 3x$. Find $x$, then type it.

**Solve the pair $y = x - 5$ and $4y = 3x$ on paper by substituting. What is $x$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $4(x - 5) = 3x$ (replace y by x − 5, in brackets); $4x - 20 = 3x$ (expand: 4 times both parts); $x = 20$ (take 3x from both sides and add 20)

4(x − 5) = 3x gives 4x − 20 = 3x, so x = 20. Then y = 20 − 5 = 15. Check: 4 × 15 = 60 and 3 × 20 = 60.

:::

## Pairs of equations


:::keep-together

### Equal rules

**Solve the pair $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = x + 6$. What is $x$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

2x + 1 = x + 6 gives x = 5. Then y = 11.

:::


:::keep-together

### With a bracket

**Solve the pair $y = x - 2$ and $3y + x = 14$. What is $y$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

3(x − 2) + x = 14 gives 4x − 6 = 14, so x = 5. Then y = 5 − 2 = 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### The first step

**The pair is $x = 2y$ and $3x + y = 21$. Which is the correct first step?**

☐ $2y + y = 21$

☐ $3(2y) + y = 21$

☐ $3 \times 2 + y = 21$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $3(2y) + y = 21$

Replace x by 2y in brackets: 3(2y) + y = 21, so 7y = 21 and y = 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: expand $4(x - 3)$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4x-12

4 × x = 4x and 4 × (−3) = −12.
