## Is it on the path?

Here's checking a point against a line, using the gradient.

Now try one on paper: the line from $(0, 1)$ to $(6, 4)$. Find its gradient, then type it. (Then check whether $(4, 3)$ is on it.)

$$A(1, 2),\; B(7, 10),\; T(4, 6)$$

1) $m_{AB} = \frac{10 - 2}{7 - 1} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}$ — The gradient of the whole line.
2) $m_{AT} = \frac{6 - 2}{4 - 1} = \frac{4}{3}$ — The gradient from A to T.
3) $\frac{4}{3} = \frac{4}{3}$ — Same gradient from the same point A.
4) $\text{T is on the line AB}$ — Yes.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Start: write A(1, 2), B(7, 10), T(4, 6) and say "Is T on the line from A to B?" Line 1: write m_AB = 10 - 2 ÷ 7 - 1 = 8 ÷ 6 = 4 ÷ 3 and say "The gradient of the whole line." Line 2: write m_AT = 6 - 2 ÷ 4 - 1 = 4 ÷ 3 and say "The gradient from A to T." Line 3: write 4 ÷ 3 = 4 ÷ 3 and say "Same gradient from the same point A." Line 4: write T is on the line AB and say "Yes." -->

## Find the mistake

**Mia checks whether a post at $(5, 6)$ is on the path from $G(2, 1)$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Is the post (5, 6) on the path from G(2, 1) to P(6, 9)?
 2  Gradient of the path: (9 - 1) / (6 - 2) = 8 / 4 = 2
 3  Gradient from G to the post: 6 / 5 = 1.2
 4  1.2 is not 2, so the post is not on the path.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: she divided the post's own coordinates, 6 ÷ 5. The gradient from G uses the differences: (6 − 1) ÷ (5 − 2) = 5/3, which isn't 2, so the post is not on the path. -->
