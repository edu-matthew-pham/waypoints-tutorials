## Three tools, one plan


:::keep-together

### Is it on the path?

Here's checking a point against a line, using the gradient.

1. $A(1, 2),\; B(7, 10),\; T(4, 6)$ — Is T on the line from A to B?
2. $m_{AB} = \frac{10 - 2}{7 - 1} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}$ — The gradient of the whole line.
3. $m_{AT} = \frac{6 - 2}{4 - 1} = \frac{4}{3}$ — The gradient from A to T.
4. $\frac{4}{3} = \frac{4}{3}$ — Same gradient from the same point A.
5. $\text{T is on the line AB}$ — Yes.

Now try one on paper: the line from $(0, 1)$ to $(6, 4)$. Find its gradient, then type it. (Then check whether $(4, 3)$ is on it.)

**What is the gradient of the path from $G(2, 1)$ to $P(6, 9)$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2

Rise 9 − 1 = 8, run 6 − 2 = 4. 8 ÷ 4 = 2.

:::


:::keep-together

### How long?

**How long is the straight path from $G(2, 1)$ to $P(6, 9)$, in metres to 1 decimal place?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8.9

Across 4, up 8: 16 + 64 = 80. √80 ≈ 8.9 m.

:::


:::keep-together

### The bin

**A bin goes halfway along the path. Where is it?**

☐ $(2, 4)$

☐ $(4, 8)$

☐ $(8, 10)$

☐ $(4, 5)$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $(4, 5)$

(2 + 6) ÷ 2 = 4 and (1 + 9) ÷ 2 = 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia checks whether a post at $(5, 6)$ is on the path from $G(2, 1)$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Is the post (5, 6) on the path from G(2, 1) to P(6, 9)?
 2  Gradient of the path: (9 - 1) / (6 - 2) = 8 / 4 = 2
 3  Gradient from G to the post: 6 / 5 = 1.2
 4  1.2 is not 2, so the post is not on the path.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: she divided the post's own coordinates, 6 ÷ 5. The gradient from G uses the differences: (6 − 1) ÷ (5 − 2) = 5/3, which isn't 2, so the post is not on the path.

:::

## Mixed problems


:::keep-together

### A midpoint

**A path goes from $(1, 2)$ to $(5, 10)$. What is the $y$-coordinate of its midpoint?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

(2 + 10) ÷ 2 = 6.

:::


:::keep-together

### At right angles

**A path goes from $(0, 0)$ to $(6, 8)$. A second path goes at right angles to it. What is the gradient of the second path as a decimal?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -0.75

The first gradient is 8/6 = 4/3. Turn it upside down and change the sign: −3/4 = −0.75.

:::


:::keep-together

### On the line?

**Is $(5, 7)$ on the line from $(1, 1)$ to $(9, 13)$?**

☐ No: it's the wrong distance

☐ Yes: both gradients are 1.5

☐ No: its gradient from (1, 1) is 2

☐ You can't tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Yes: both gradients are 1.5

Line: 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5. From (1, 1) to (5, 7): 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Same gradient, so yes.

:::


:::keep-together
