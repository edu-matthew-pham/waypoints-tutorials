# Solving by elimination

Linear relations · Year 10: two variables

<!-- Speaker note: Students solve two equations in two letters by elimination: add or subtract the equations so one letter cancels, multiplying one or both first if no coefficients match, then find the other letter (AC9M10A02, node 3, second of two lessons on the hinge). Edge cases: a false result (no solution) and a true result (infinitely many). The gym prices are made up. About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 16 min · Modify 11 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can subtract or add two equations so one letter cancels.
- I can multiply an equation so two coefficients match, then cancel.
- I can find the second letter and check it in the other equation.

## Compare the receipts

**Let $v$ be the price of a visit and $k$ the price of a class. Ava: $4v + 2k = 44$. Ben: $4v + 5k = 62$. Subtract Ava's equation from Ben's. What is left?**

- $3k = 18$
- $7k = 106$
- $8v + 7k = 106$
- $k = 18$

* Answer: $3k = 18$

## Find the mistake

**Sam solves $5p + 3q = 29$ and $2p + 3q = 17$ by subtracting. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Solve 5p + 3q = 29 and 2p + 3q = 17.
 2  Subtract the second equation from the first: (5p − 2p) + (3q + 3q) = 29 − 17.
 3  So 3p + 6q = 12.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: he subtracted the p terms but added the q terms. Subtracting means every term of the second equation is taken away: 3q − 3q = 0. Then 3p = 12, p = 4 and q = 3. -->

## Try one on paper

Here is the routine with the first two receipts.

Now try one on paper with the example beside you: Cara and Dan. Find the price of one class, then type it.

$$4v + 2k = 44,\; 4v + 5k = 62$$

1) $(4v + 5k) - (4v + 2k) = 62 - 44$ — Take away every term of the first equation from the second.
2) $3k = 18$ — The $v$ terms cancel. $5k - 2k = 3k$.
3) $k = 6$ — Divide by 3.
4) $4v + 2 \times 6 = 44$ — Put $k$ into one equation to find $v$.
5) $v = 8$ — $4v = 32$, so $v = 8$.
6) $4 \times 8 + 5 \times 6 = 62$ — Check in the other equation.

**At the same gym, Cara paid 29 dollars for 3 visits and 2 classes and Dan paid 41 dollars for 3 visits and 5 classes. Solve $3v + 2k = 29$ and $3v + 5k = 41$ on paper. What does one class cost?**

* Answer: $3v + 5k - (3v + 2k) = 41 - 29$ (subtract Cara's equation from Dan's); $3k = 12$ (the 3v cancels; 5k − 2k and 41 − 29); $k = 4$ (divide by 3)
