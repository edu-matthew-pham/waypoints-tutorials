## Why it matters

> Every line in a car park has to be parallel or at a right angle to its neighbour. Can the plan check that with numbers alone?

## Predict

**On a car-park plan, one line goes through (0, 1) and (4, 3). Another goes through (1, 0) and (5, 2). Are the lines parallel?**

- Yes: they go up at the same rate
- No: one is longer
- No: they start at different places
- You can't tell without drawing them

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Yes. The first goes up 2 for 4 across, so its gradient is 2 ÷ 4 = ½. The second also goes up 2 for 4 across: ½. Same gradient, so they're parallel. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Same gradient

Here's finding a perpendicular gradient.

Now try one on paper: a line has gradient $5$. Find the gradient of a perpendicular line, then type it.

$$m_1 = \frac{3}{2}$$

1) $\frac{2}{3}$ — Turn it upside down.
2) $m_2 = -\frac{2}{3}$ — Change the sign.
3) $\frac{3}{2} \times \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) = -1$ — Check: perpendicular gradients multiply to −1.

## Same gradient

**A line goes through $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 7)$. Another goes through $(1, 0)$ and $(3, 6)$. Are they parallel?**

- No: they start at different places
- No: one has gradient 3 and the other 6
- Yes: both have gradient 3
- You can't tell

* Answer: Yes: both have gradient 3

<!-- Speaker note: (7 − 1) ÷ (2 − 0) = 3 and (6 − 0) ÷ (3 − 1) = 3. Same gradient, so parallel. -->

## Upside down and change the sign

**A line has gradient $4$. What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to it?**

* Answer: -0.25

<!-- Speaker note: Turn 4 (that is 4/1) upside down: 1/4. Change the sign: −1/4, or −0.25. Check: 4 × (−¼) = −1. -->

```
 1  A line has gradient 3. Find the gradient of a perpendicular line.

 3  Check: 3 x (-3) = -9
 4  -9 is not -1, so I'll leave it as -3.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: changing only the sign gives −3, but 3 × (−3) = −9, not −1. Turn it upside down too: −1/3. -->

## Which pair?

**Line A goes through $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 6)$. Line B goes through $(0, 5)$ and $(6, 3)$. Which is true?**

- They're parallel
- They're perpendicular
- Neither

* Answer: They're perpendicular
