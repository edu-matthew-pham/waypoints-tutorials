## Parallel and perpendicular


:::keep-together

### Same gradient

Here's finding a perpendicular gradient.

1. $m_1 = \frac{3}{2}$ — The gradient of the first line.
2. $\frac{2}{3}$ — Turn it upside down.
3. $m_2 = -\frac{2}{3}$ — Change the sign.
4. $\frac{3}{2} \times \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) = -1$ — Check: perpendicular gradients multiply to −1.

Now try one on paper: a line has gradient $5$. Find the gradient of a perpendicular line, then type it.

**A line goes through $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 7)$. Another goes through $(1, 0)$ and $(3, 6)$. Are they parallel?**

☐ No: they start at different places

☐ No: one has gradient 3 and the other 6

☐ Yes: both have gradient 3

☐ You can't tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Yes: both have gradient 3

(7 − 1) ÷ (2 − 0) = 3 and (6 − 0) ÷ (3 − 1) = 3. Same gradient, so parallel.

:::


:::keep-together

### Upside down and change the sign

**A line has gradient $4$. What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to it?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -0.25

Turn 4 (that is 4/1) upside down: 1/4. Change the sign: −1/4, or −0.25. Check: 4 × (−¼) = −1.

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: changing only the sign gives −3, but 3 × (−3) = −9, not −1. Turn it upside down too: −1/3.

## Parallel or perpendicular?


:::keep-together

### Parallel

**A line is parallel to $y = 3x + 7$. What is its gradient?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

Parallel lines have the same gradient: 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### Perpendicular

**A line has gradient $\frac{1}{2}$. What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to it?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -2

Turn ½ upside down to get 2, then change the sign: −2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which pair?

**Line A goes through $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 6)$. Line B goes through $(0, 5)$ and $(6, 3)$. Which is true?**

☐ They're parallel

☐ They're perpendicular

☐ Neither

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They're perpendicular

A: 6 ÷ 2 = 3. B: (3 − 5) ÷ (6 − 0) = −1/3. 3 × (−1/3) = −1: perpendicular.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: what is the gradient of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 8)$?**

☐ −3

☐ 1/3

☐ 3

☐ 6

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

Rise 6, run 2: 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

:::
