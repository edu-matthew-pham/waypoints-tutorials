# Lines and regions

Linear relations · Lines and their rules

<!-- Speaker note: Students graph vertical and horizontal lines and linear inequalities as regions, choosing a dashed or solid boundary and testing a point to decide which side to shade (AC9M8A02, node 1). ## Today

**We are learning to** y = 2x + 1 is a line; y < 2x + 1 is every point below it. Draw the boundary (dashed if points on it are not included, solid if they are), then test a point to see which side to shade. Vertical lines are x = a; horizontal lines are y = a.

- I can graph lines such as x = 3 and y = −2.
- I can graph an inequality such as y &lt; 2x + 1 as a region, with the right boundary (dashed or solid).
- I can test a point to decide whether it is in a region.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board and the Make task yourself first. Students need Lines and their rules (gradient and intercept). The common mistake is the wrong boundary style; it is planted in Ana's working. -->

## Why it matters

> A delivery zone, a speed limit, a budget: often the answer is not one point but a whole region of points that work.

<!-- Speaker note: You can graph a line from its rule, such as $y = 2x + 1$. Now: what about $y < 2x + 1$? Every point whose $y$ is less than $2x + 1$ makes it true, and those points fill a whole **region** of the graph. You also meet lines with only one letter: $x = 3$ (vertical) and $y = -2$ (horizontal). -->

## Boundary and test

Here is how to graph an inequality.

Solid or dashed for $y \ge x - 2$?

$$y < 2x + 1$$

1) $\text{dashed}$ — $<$ does not include the line's points (for $\le$ or $\ge$, draw it solid).
2) $(0, 0):\; 0 < 2 \times 0 + 1$ — Test a point not on the line. $0 < 1$ is true.
3) $\text{shade the side with } (0, 0)$ — True: shade the test point's side. (False: shade the other side.)
4) $x = 3,\; y = -2$ — One letter: $x = 3$ is vertical (every point has $x$ = 3); $y = -2$ is horizontal.

## Boundary and test

**For $y \ge x - 2$, is the boundary line solid or dashed?**

- Solid: ≥ includes the points on the line
- Dashed: points on the line are not included
- There is no boundary

* Answer: Solid: ≥ includes the points on the line

**Ana graphs $y &gt; x + 1$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Graph y > x + 1.
 2  Draw the line y = x + 1 as a solid line.
 3  Test (0, 0): is 0 > 0 + 1? No.
 4  Shade the side without (0, 0).
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: > does not include the line itself, so the boundary is dashed. Her test and shading are right. -->
