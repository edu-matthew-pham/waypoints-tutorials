# Solving equations with graphs

Linear relations · Solving and modelling with lines

<!-- Speaker note: Students solve linear equations by drawing each side as a line and reading the x where the lines meet, then check by substitution (AC9M8A02). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 15 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** draw each side of an equation as a line. Where the lines meet, both sides are equal, so the x there is the solution.

- I can solve an equation by drawing each side as a line and finding where they meet.
- I can explain why the solution is the x of the meeting point, not the y.
- I can check a graph's answer by substitution, and recognise parallel lines as no solution.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. ## Why it matters

> A graph can solve an equation you have no method for yet: draw both sides and look where they meet. Calculators, spreadsheets and engineers do exactly this.

<!-- Speaker note: An equation such as $2x + 3 = 11$ says two things are equal. Each side can be drawn as a line: $y = 2x + 3$, and $y = 11$ (a flat line, because 11 does not change with $x$). In the Equations unit you solved equations with a balance and by backtracking. Now you solve them by looking at a graph. -->

## Solving with a graph

Here is the method, one line at a time.

$$2x + 3 = 11$$

1) $y = 2x + 3, \quad y = 11$ — Draw each side as a line.
2) $(4, 11)$ — Find where they meet. There, both sides have the same value, 11.
3) $x = 4$ — The solution is the $x$ of the meeting point, not the $y$.
4) $2(4) + 3 = 11 \; \checkmark$ — Check by substitution in the first equation.

## Which equation?

**The lines $y = x + 5$ and $y = 2x + 1$ meet at $(4, 9)$. Which equation has the solution $x = 4$?**

- $x + 5 = 2x + 1$
- $2x + 1 = 4$
- $x + 5 = 4$

* Answer: $x + 5 = 2x + 1$

<!-- Speaker note: The meeting point is where the two sides are equal, so it solves x + 5 = 2x + 1. Check: 4 + 5 = 9 and 2 × 4 + 1 = 9. -->

## Change the equation



- **Subtraction:** Each task draws both sides of a new equation. Solve $3x - 4 = 8$.
- **A negative answer:** Solve $2x + 3 = 1$.
- **$x$ on both sides:** Solve $x + 5 = 3x + 1$. Now both sides are sloping lines.
- **Between the lines:** Solve $2x + 1 = 6$.
- **Never meeting:** $2x + 1 = 2x + 4$. Look at the two lines. What does the last lesson say about them?

## Make: an equation with a chosen answer

Your line is $y = mx + c$. The green point shows where it meets $y = 2x + 1$. Move it so they meet at $x = 3$. (Your line cannot be $y = 2x + 1$ itself.)

Stretch: write down your equation, then check $x = 3$ by substitution.
