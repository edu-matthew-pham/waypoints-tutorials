# Regions from a budget

Linear relations · Year 10: two variables

<!-- Speaker note: Students turn a spending limit with two kinds of item into an inequality in two letters, find where its boundary meets each axis, and read the shaded region as the set of plans that fit (AC9M10A02, node 2). The prices are made up. About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can write a budget as an inequality such as 24p + 12d ≤ 120.
- I can find where the boundary meets each axis by putting the other letter equal to 0.
- I can tell which plans fit by reading the shaded region.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board and the paper try-one yourself first. Students need Lines and regions (Year 8) and the idea of a line through two intercepts. The one idea: the budget line joins the two extreme plans, all pizzas and no packs, and all packs and no pizzas. Each intercept uses its own price. The common mistake is the same price on both axes (Mia's working). The prices (24 dollars and 12 dollars) and the budget (120 dollars) are made up so the intercepts are whole numbers. Real prices are not so tidy. The region includes points such as 1.5 pizzas; only whole-number points are real plans (the edge-case task). Teacher: Paper: the try-one after the worked example (a 96 dollars budget) and the party plan in Make are worked on paper first. Books out, example beside them. -->

## Why it matters

> A fixed budget never gives one answer. It gives a whole set of plans, and some of them are better than others.

<!-- Speaker note: A class party has a fixed amount of money, and every pizza and every pack of drinks takes some of it. The prices in this lesson are made up so the numbers are easy to follow; real prices change from shop to shop. -->

## Where the line crosses the d-axis

**Buy no pizzas ($p = 0$). How many packs of drinks does $24p + 12d = 120$ allow? This is where the line crosses the $d$-axis.**

* Answer: 10

## Find the mistake

**Mia graphs $24p + 12d \le 120$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Graph 24p + 12d <= 120 (pizzas $24, packs $12, budget $120).
 2  Where does the boundary cross the p-axis? Put d = 0: 24p = 120, so p = 5.
 3  Where does it cross the d-axis? Put p = 0: 24d = 120, so d = 5.
 4  Draw a solid line through (5, 0) and (0, 5), test (0, 0) and shade the side with it.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: with p = 0 the equation is 12d = 120, not 24d = 120. Packs cost 12 dollars, so d = 10. Mia used the pizza price on the d-axis. -->

## Which side?

**The boundary of $24p + 12d \le 120$ crosses the axes at $(5, 0)$ and $(0, 10)$. Which side is shaded?**

- The side without the origin, because the line is solid
- Neither: solid boundaries have no shading
- The side with the origin $(0, 0)$, because $0 \le 120$ is true

* Answer: The side with the origin $(0, 0)$, because $0 \le 120$ is true

<!-- Speaker note: Test the origin: 24 × 0 + 12 × 0 = 0, and 0 ≤ 120 is true, so shade the side with (0, 0): the plans that cost less. -->

## Try it on paper

Here is the whole routine, from the budget to the region.

Now try one on paper: the class has 96 dollars instead, so the boundary is $24p + 12d = 96$. Find where it crosses the $p$-axis, with the example beside you, and then type the number.

$$24p + 12d \le 120$$

1) $p = 0:\; 12d = 120,\; d = 10$ — Where the boundary meets the $d$-axis: no pizzas, so divide by the pack price.
2) $d = 0:\; 24p = 120,\; p = 5$ — Where it meets the $p$-axis: no packs, so divide by the pizza price.
3) $\text{solid line through } (5, 0) \text{ and } (0, 10)$ — $\le$ includes the line: plans that spend exactly $120$ are allowed.
4) $(0, 0):\; 0 \le 120$ — Test a point not on the line. True, so shade the side with the origin.

## Try it on paper

**The class has 96 dollars instead. The boundary of $24p + 12d \le 96$ crosses the $p$-axis where $d = 0$. What is $p$ there?**

* Answer: $d = 0$: $24p = 96$ (no packs, so only the pizza price is left); $p = 96 \div 24$ (divide by the pizza price); $p = 4$ (four pizzas spend all 96 dollars)

**Is the plan $(3, 4)$ inside $24p + 12d \le 120$?**

## More than

**Which inequality means 'the total is more than 60 dollars'?**

- $24p + 12d \ge 60$, with a dashed boundary
- $24p + 12d &lt; 60$, with a solid boundary
- $24p + 12d &gt; 60$, with a dashed boundary

* Answer: $24p + 12d &gt; 60$, with a dashed boundary

<!-- Speaker note: 'More than' is >: the boundary is dashed, because exactly 60 dollars is not more than 60 dollars. -->

## Exit ticket

**Tickets cost 10 dollars and snacks 5 dollars. The budget is 60 dollars. Where does the boundary of 10t + 5s ≤ 60 meet each axis?**

* t-axis (s = 0): 10t = 60, t = 6. s-axis (t = 0): 5s = 60, s = 12. The line joins (6, 0) and (0, 12).
