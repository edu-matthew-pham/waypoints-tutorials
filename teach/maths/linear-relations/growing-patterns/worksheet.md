# Growing patterns

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Tiles along a path, seats around joined tables, fence posts and rails: each one grows by the same amount every time. A rule tells you how many you need for any length.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find the rule for a visually growing pattern from what each figure adds, use it for a figure they have not drawn, and work backwards from a count (AC9M7A05, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find what each figure of a growing pattern adds, and write a rule like s = 3n + 1. I can use a rule to work out a figure I have not drawn. I can work backwards from a count to the figure number.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## From pattern to rule


:::keep-together

### Finding the rule

Here is how to turn the squares pattern into a rule.

1. $n: 1, 2, 3, 4 \;\to\; s: 4, 7, 10, 13$ — Count the sticks in the first few figures and put them in a table.
2. $+3 \text{ each time}$ — Each new square needs 3 more sticks: a top, a bottom and a side.
3. $3n$ — So figure $n$ has $n$ lots of 3 sticks...
4. $3n + 1$ — ...plus the first stick on the left, which every figure has.
5. $s = 3n + 1$ — The rule: sticks = 3 × figure number + 1.
6. $n = 10: \; s = 3(10) + 1 = 31$ — Use the rule for a figure you have not drawn.

**The triangle pattern: Figure 1 has 3 sticks, Figure 2 has 5, Figure 3 has 7. Fill in the table.**

{{rows 1}}

| figure n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sticks s | 3 | 5 | 7 |   |   |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9, 11

Each new triangle adds 2 sticks: 7, 9, 11.

:::

### Find the mistake

**Ben works out Figure 10 of the squares pattern. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Figure 1 has 4 sticks.
 2  Figure 10 has 10 squares, so it is 10 lots of Figure 1.
 3  So Figure 10 has 4 × 10 = 40 sticks.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the squares share sides, so Figure 10 is not 10 separate copies of Figure 1. Each new square adds only 3 sticks: 3 × 10 + 1 = 31.

## Patterns and rules


:::keep-together

### Table to rule

**A pattern has 5, 9, 13, 17 dots in Figures 1 to 4. Write a rule for the dots $s$ in figure $n$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4n + 1

It adds 4 each time, and Figure 1 is 4 + 1: s = 4n + 1.

:::


:::keep-together

### A far figure

**A pattern's rule is $s = 5n + 3$. How many are in Figure 12?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 63

5 × 12 + 3 = 60 + 3 = 63.

### Backwards again

**Using $s = 2n + 1$ (the triangles), which figure has 25 sticks?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12

2n + 1 = 25, so 2n = 24 and n = 12.
