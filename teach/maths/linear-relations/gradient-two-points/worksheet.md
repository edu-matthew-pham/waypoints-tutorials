# Gradient between two points

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A wheelchair ramp can only be so steep. How do you put a number on steep, using just two points?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find the gradient between two points as rise over run, including negative and zero gradients, and compare a ramp to a legal limit (AC9M9A03, node 1).

## Rise over run


:::keep-together

### Rise over run

Here's finding a gradient from two points.

1. $A(1, 2),\; B(5, 10)$ — The two points.
2. $\text{rise} = 10 - 2 = 8$ — Up from A to B: the y values.
3. $\text{run} = 5 - 1 = 4$ — Across from A to B: the x values, in the same order.
4. $\text{gradient} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$ — Rise over run: up 2 for each 1 across.

Now try one on paper: the points $(2, 7)$ and $(6, 3)$. Work out the rise, the run and the gradient, then type the gradient.

**A line goes through $(2, 3)$ and $(6, 11)$. What is its gradient?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2

Rise 11 − 3 = 8. Run 6 − 2 = 4. 8 ÷ 4 = 2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which way up?

**A line goes up 3 for every 2 across. Which is its gradient?**

☐ $5$

☐ $\frac{2}{3}$

☐ $1$

☐ $\frac{3}{2}$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $\frac{3}{2}$

Rise over run: 3 up over 2 across is 3/2, or 1.5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zoe finds the gradient between $(1, 7)$ and $(4, 1)$. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Find the gradient between (1, 7) and (4, 1).
 2  Rise: 1 - 7 = -6
 3  Run: 1 - 4 = -3
 4  Gradient: -6 / -3 = 2
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: she did 1 − 7 for the rise but 1 − 4 for the run, starting from different points. Both must start from the same point: (1 − 7) ÷ (4 − 1) = −6 ÷ 3 = −2.

:::

## Find the gradient


:::keep-together

### Up

**Find the gradient of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 11)$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

Rise 11 − 2 = 9, run 4 − 1 = 3. 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### Down

**Find the gradient of the line through $(0, 8)$ and $(4, 0)$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -2

Rise 0 − 8 = −8, run 4 − 0 = 4. −8 ÷ 4 = −2: the line goes down.

:::


:::keep-together

### Flat

**Find the gradient of the line through $(2, 5)$ and $(7, 5)$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0

Rise 5 − 5 = 0. 0 ÷ 5 = 0: a flat line.

:::


:::keep-together

## Make: the ramp


**1 in what?.** Back to the ramp. Work it out on paper first, then type your answer.

**Allowed?.** The ramp measurements here are made up for the task. The limit is real: ramps up to 9 m long can be at most 1 in 14 ([Queensland Health, Ramps to the house and slopes in the garden](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0029/1477208/Ramps-to-the-house-and-slopes-in-the-garden-slides.pdf), citing AS 1428.1).

**Also used in**

- **Road signs.** A sign saying 8% means 8 m up for every 100 m along.
- **Roofs and stairs.** Builders give a roof's pitch and a staircase's steepness as rise over run.
