# Rules from real situations

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Taxi fares, phone plans, gym memberships and a phone battery running down all follow the same pattern: where you start, and how much changes each time.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students turn a situation that is a starting amount plus a rate into a rule y = mx + c, read m and c back into words, and fit lines through real values (AC9M8A03, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can write a rule like T = 2d + 4 from a situation described in words. I can explain what m and c mean in a situation (the rate and the starting amount). I can use a rule to work out a value, and check it in words.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

☐ $T = 4d + 2$

☐ $T = 2d$

☐ $T = 2d + 4$

My prediction, and why:

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** T = 2d + 4. The 4 dollars is paid once, before you travel: it is where the line starts (c). The 2 dollars is paid for each km: it is the rate (m).

## From words to a rule


:::keep-together

### A phone plan

Here is how to turn words into a rule.

1. $\text{Fee } 20, \text{ then } 5 \text{ per GB}$ — A phone plan costs 20 dollars a month, plus 5 dollars for each GB used. Find a rule for the cost $T$.
2. $g = 0: \; T = 20$ — With no data used, you still pay the fee. That is the value when $g$ is 0.
3. $c = 20$ — The starting amount is $c$.
4. $m = 5$ — Each extra GB adds 5 dollars. "Per", "each" or "every" signals the rate, $m$.
5. $T = 5g + 20$ — Put them together.
6. $g = 3: \; T = 5(3) + 20 = 35$ — Check with a value you can work out in words: 3 GB costs 20 + 15 = 35 dollars.

**A gym charges a 30 dollar joining fee and 15 dollars a week. Write a rule for the total cost $T$ after $w$ weeks.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15w + 30

T = 15w + 30: 30 dollars once (c), then 15 dollars for each week (m).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zoe writes a rule for a taxi fare. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  A taxi costs 5 dollars to get in, plus 3 dollars per km.
 2  m is the first number in the question, so m = 5.
 3  c is the other number, so c = 3.
 4  The rule is T = 5d + 3.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: m is the rate, the amount for each km, which is 3. It does not matter which number comes first in the question. The rule is T = 3d + 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### What the numbers mean

**A phone plan costs $T = 5g + 20$ dollars a month for $g$ GB of data. What does the 5 mean?**

☐ 5 dollars for each GB used

☐ 5 GB of data are included

☐ The monthly fee is 5 dollars

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5 dollars for each GB used

5 is m, the rate: each extra GB adds 5 dollars. The 20 is c, the fee you pay even with no data.

## Rules and meanings


:::keep-together

### Tickets

**Concert tickets cost 12 dollars each, plus a 6 dollar booking fee for the order. Write a rule for the total $T$ for $n$ tickets.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12n + 6

T = 12n + 6: 12 dollars for each ticket (m) and 6 dollars once (c).

:::


:::keep-together

### What the 60 means

**A plumber charges $T = 80h + 60$ dollars for a job of $h$ hours. What does the 60 mean?**

☐ The cost of a 60-hour job

☐ 60 dollars for each hour

☐ A 60 dollar call-out fee, paid once

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A 60 dollar call-out fee, paid once

60 is c: what you pay even for 0 hours, the call-out fee. 80 is the hourly rate.

:::


:::keep-together

### Using the rule

**Using $T = 80h + 60$, what does a 3-hour job cost, in dollars?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 300

80 × 3 + 60 = 240 + 60 = 300 dollars.

:::
