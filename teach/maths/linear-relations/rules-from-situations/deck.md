# Rules from real situations

Linear relations · Solving and modelling with lines

## Today

**We are learning to** many real costs and amounts are a starting amount plus a rate. The starting amount is c and the rate is m, so the rule is y = mx + c.

- I can write a rule like T = 2d + 4 from a situation described in words.
- I can explain what m and c mean in a situation (the rate and the starting amount).
- I can use a rule to work out a value, and check it in words.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. Students should already know what m and c do to a line (the first two lessons of this course). The new move is translation: the starting amount (paid once, the value when nothing has happened) is c; the rate ("per", "each", "every") is m. The order of the numbers in the words does not matter, and Zoe's working shows the mistake of assuming it does. The boards use the situation's own letters (T for total, d for distance), labelled on the axes. The graphs still behave exactly like y = mx + c. The next lesson uses these rules: two plans, where their lines cross, and when a rule stops being a good model. -->

## Why it matters

> Taxi fares, phone plans, gym memberships and a phone battery running down all follow the same pattern: where you start, and how much changes each time.

## A phone plan

Here is how to turn words into a rule.

$$\text{Fee } 20, \text{ then } 5 \text{ per GB}$$

1) $g = 0: \; T = 20$ — With no data used, you still pay the fee. That is the value when $g$ is 0.
2) $c = 20$ — The starting amount is $c$.
3) $m = 5$ — Each extra GB adds 5 dollars. "Per", "each" or "every" signals the rate, $m$.
4) $T = 5g + 20$ — Put them together.
5) $g = 3: \; T = 5(3) + 20 = 35$ — Check with a value you can work out in words: 3 GB costs 20 + 15 = 35 dollars.

**Zoe writes a rule for a taxi fare. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  A taxi costs 5 dollars to get in, plus 3 dollars per km.
 2  m is the first number in the question, so m = 5.
 3  c is the other number, so c = 3.
 4  The rule is T = 5d + 3.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: m is the rate, the amount for each km, which is 3. It does not matter which number comes first in the question. The rule is T = 3d + 5. -->

## What the numbers mean

**A phone plan costs $T = 5g + 20$ dollars a month for $g$ GB of data. What does the 5 mean?**

- 5 dollars for each GB used
- 5 GB of data are included
- The monthly fee is 5 dollars

* Answer: 5 dollars for each GB used

<!-- Speaker note: 5 is m, the rate: each extra GB adds 5 dollars. The 20 is c, the fee you pay even with no data. -->
