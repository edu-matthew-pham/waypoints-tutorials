# Changing one thing at a time

Linear relations · Rules, tables and graphs

<!-- Speaker note: Students vary one variable at a time in V = lwh, d = st and A = s², write the results as a table and describe the effect (AC9M7A06, nodes 1 and 2). About 35 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 8 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 9 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** to see what one variable does to a formula, change only that one and hold the rest still. Doubling h in V = lwh doubles V; doubling the side of a square makes its area four times as big.

- I can change one variable and hold the others still.
- I can describe in words what happens to the output.
- I can say which variable has the biggest effect, and why.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need to substitute into a formula (Substituting into formulas in Algebra). The wider idea is that a variable's effect depends on the formula: in V = lwh, doubling any one factor doubles V; in A = s², doubling s makes A four times as big. ## Why it matters

> A scientist testing a plant changes the light, but not the water. In a formula, that is how you find what each letter does.

<!-- Speaker note: A **formula** has several variables, such as $V = l \times w \times h$. ## A fair test?

**Tom wants to know what h does to V. Click the line where his test stops being fair.**

```
 1  Tom wants to know what h does to V = lwh.
 2  He sets l = 2, w = 3 and h = 1, so V = 6.
 3  Then he changes h to 2 and w to 4, so V = 16.
 4  V went from 6 to 16, so doubling h made V go up by 10.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: he changes w as well as h, so he cannot tell which caused the change in V. Change h alone: V = 2 × 3 × 2 = 12. -->

## What to change

**To find the effect of $w$ on $V = lwh$, what should you do?**

- Change w and h together
- Change w only, and keep l and h the same
- Change l, w and h together
- Change l and h only

* Answer: Change w only, and keep l and h the same
