# Changing one thing at a time

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A scientist testing a plant changes the light, but not the water. How would you test one letter in a formula the same way?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students vary one variable at a time in V = lwh, d = st and A = s², write the results as a table and describe the effect (AC9M7A06, nodes 1 and 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can change one variable and hold the others still. I can describe in words what happens to the output. I can say which variable has the biggest effect, and why.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**A box has $l = 2$, $w = 3$ and $h = 2$, so $V = 12$. What is $V$ when $h$ is doubled to 4 and the rest stay the same?**

☐ 14

☐ 16

☐ 24

**What happens:** 24. V = 2 × 3 × 4 = 24: doubling one factor doubles the product.

## A fair test


:::keep-together

### The pattern in the table

**With $l = 2$ and $w = 3$, $V$ is 6, 12, 18 when $h$ is 1, 2, 3. By how much does $V$ go up for each extra 1 in $h$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

V goes up by 6 each time, which is l × w = 2 × 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### A fair test?

**Tom wants to know what h does to V. Click the line where his test stops being fair.**

```
 1  Tom wants to know what h does to V = lwh.
 2  He sets l = 2, w = 3 and h = 1, so V = 6.
 3  Then he changes h to 2 and w to 4, so V = 16.
 4  V went from 6 to 16, so doubling h made V go up by 10.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: he changes w as well as h, so he cannot tell which caused the change in V. Change h alone: V = 2 × 3 × 2 = 12.

:::


:::keep-together

### What to change

**To find the effect of $w$ on $V = lwh$, what should you do?**

☐ Change w and h together

☐ Change w only, and keep l and h the same

☐ Change l, w and h together

☐ Change l and h only

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Change w only, and keep l and h the same

Isolate one variable at a time: that is a fair test.

## Vary and describe


:::keep-together

### Find V

**$V = lwh$ with $l = 2$, $w = 3$, $h = 4$. What is $V$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 24

2 × 3 × 4 = 24.

:::


:::keep-together

### Change w

**Now change only $w$, from 3 to 4. What is $V$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 32

2 × 4 × 4 = 32.

:::


:::keep-together

### Change t

**$d = st$ with $s = 60$ km/h. The time doubles from 2 to 4 hours. What is $d$ now, in km?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 240

60 × 4 = 240 km, double the 120 km for 2 hours.

:::

### Review: substitution

**Review: $P = 2l + 2w$ with $l = 6$ and $w = 4$. What is $P$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20

2 × 6 + 2 × 4 = 12 + 8 = 20.

## Make: change the ticket price on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. Organisers test one number at a time like this in a budget spreadsheet: "what if we raise the price by a dollar?" Work out both profits, then type the new one.

**Also used in**

- **Event budgets.** A spreadsheet tests one change at a time: the price, the numbers or the costs.
- **Science experiments.** A fair test changes one thing and keeps everything else the same.
- **Spreadsheets and simulations.** Every "what if" is one input changed while the rest are held still.
