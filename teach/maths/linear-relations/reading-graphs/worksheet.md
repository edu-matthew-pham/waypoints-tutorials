# Reading a graph

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Weather maps, phone battery meters and sports stats all use graphs. Before you can use one, you need to read it: which number is on which axis?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read values from graphs of real data, starting from either axis, and say what a point means in a sentence with units (AC9M7A04, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a point on a graph of real data means, with units. I can find a value on one axis and read the matching value from the other. I can tell the graph's variables from its axis labels.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Which axis is the first number read from? What are the units of each number?

:::keep-together

**A graph shows temperature (°C) up the side and time (h) along the bottom. One point on it is $(15, 24)$. What does that point say?**

☐ At 24 hours the temperature is 15 °C

☐ At 15 hours the temperature is 24 °C

☐ It is 15 °C for 24 hours

☐ The temperature went up by 15, then by 24

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** At 15 hours the temperature is 24 °C. The first number is read along the bottom axis (Time), the second up the side (Temperature), just as when you plotted points. (At 24 hours the temperature is 15 °C swaps the axes.)

:::

## Reading values


:::keep-together

### Temperature

Here is how to read a graph, step by step.

1. $\text{Axes: Time (h) across, Temperature (°C) up}$ — Check the labels first.
2. $\text{Time} = 9 \text{ h}$ — Find the value you know on its own axis.
3. $(9, \; ?)$ — Go up from 9 to the line.
4. $\text{Temperature} = 14 \text{ °C}$ — Read across to the other axis.
5. $\text{At 9 h the temperature is 14 °C.}$ — Say it in a sentence, with the units.

**What is the temperature at 9 hours?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 14

At 9 h the line is at 14 °C, so the point is (9, 14).

:::


:::keep-together

### The highest point

**At what time (in hours) is the temperature highest?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

The highest point is (15, 24): the temperature is highest at 15 h.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Priya reads the temperature at 6 hours from the graph. Click the line where she goes wrong.**

```
 1  Read the temperature at 6 hours.
 2  Find 6 on the Time axis.
 3  Go up to the line.
 4  Read across: the line is at 9, so at 9 hours the temperature is 6 °C.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: the line is at 9 when the time is 6, and 9 is read up the side, so it is the temperature. At 6 hours the temperature is 9 °C. Priya swapped the two numbers.

### Which sentence?

**A graph of cost ($) against time (hours) has the point $(4, 30)$. Which sentence is true?**

☐ 30 hours cost $4

☐ 4 hours cost $30

☐ The cost went up by $4 over 30 hours

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4 hours cost $30

The first number is the time (4 hours) and the second is the cost ($30).
