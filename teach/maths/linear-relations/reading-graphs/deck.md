# Reading a graph

Linear relations · Rules, tables and graphs

<!-- Speaker note: Students read values from graphs of real data, starting from either axis, and say what a point means in a sentence with units (AC9M7A04, node 1). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 12 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 13 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** a point on a graph of real data pairs two measurements. Find one value on its axis, go to the line, and read across to the other axis.

- I can say what a point on a graph of real data means, with units.
- I can find a value on one axis and read the matching value from the other.
- I can tell the graph's variables from its axis labels.

## Why it matters

> Weather maps, phone battery meters and sports stats all use graphs. Before you can use one, you need to read it: which number is on which axis?

## Temperature

Here is how to read a graph, step by step.

$$\text{Axes: Time (h) across, Temperature (°C) up}$$

1) $\text{Time} = 9 \text{ h}$ — Find the value you know on its own axis.
2) $(9, \; ?)$ — Go up from 9 to the line.
3) $\text{Temperature} = 14 \text{ °C}$ — Read across to the other axis.
4) $\text{At 9 h the temperature is 14 °C.}$ — Say it in a sentence, with the units.

## Find the mistake

**Priya reads the temperature at 6 hours from the graph. Click the line where she goes wrong.**

```
 1  Read the temperature at 6 hours.
 2  Find 6 on the Time axis.
 3  Go up to the line.
 4  Read across: the line is at 9, so at 9 hours the temperature is 6 °C.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: the line is at 9 when the time is 6, and 9 is read up the side, so it is the temperature. At 6 hours the temperature is 9 °C. Priya swapped the two numbers. -->
