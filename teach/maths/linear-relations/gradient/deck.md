# What m does to a line

Linear relations · Lines and their rules

<!-- Speaker note: Students vary m in y = mx + 1 on a graph board and find that m is how far the line goes up for each step right (AC9M8A04, node 1). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** in y = mx + c, the number in front of x sets how steep the line is and which way it goes.

- I can describe what happens to the line y = mx + c when m gets bigger, is 0, or is negative.
- I can read m from a rule as how far the line goes up for each step right.
- I can find m for a line through (0, 1) and another point.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. Students should already be able to plot a line from a rule or a table, and work with negative numbers. Only m changes in this lesson: c stays at 1, so every line passes through (0, 1). What c does, and parallel lines, is the next lesson. If students ask about c, say "hold that thought": separating the two is the point. The lesson says "up for each step right" rather than "rise over run" or "gradient". The primer has the formal words if you want to introduce them at the end. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Ramps, roofs, ski runs and phone bills all have a steepness: how much more for each step along. In a rule like y = 3x + 1, one number decides it.

<!-- Speaker note: A rule such as $y = 3x + 1$ gives a **straight line**: work out $y$ for each value of $x$, plot the points, and join them. Every rule of the form $y = mx + c$ gives a straight line. $m$ is the number in front of $x$, and $c$ is the number on its own. In this lesson $c$ stays at 1, and you find out what $m$ does. -->

## Predict

**The line $y = x + 1$ has $m = 1$. If $m$ becomes 3, so the rule is $y = 3x + 1$, what happens to the line?**

- It moves up by 2, and is just as steep
- It gets less steep
- It gets steeper, and still crosses the $y$-axis at 1
- It moves 2 to the right

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: It gets steeper. When x = 0, y is still 1 (3 × 0 + 1 = 1), so the line still crosses the y-axis at 1, but now y goes up 3 for each step right instead of 1. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Up for each step right

Here is how to read $m$ from a rule, by working out $y$ one step at a time.

$$y = 3x + 1$$

1) $x = 0: \; y = 3(0) + 1 = 1$ — Work out $y$ where $x$ is 0.
2) $x = 1: \; y = 3(1) + 1 = 4$ — One step right: $x$ goes from 0 to 1.
3) $4 - 1 = 3$ — $y$ went up by 3.
4) $x = 2: \; y = 3(2) + 1 = 7, \; 7 - 4 = 3$ — Another step right: up 3 again.
5) $m = 3$ — Each step right adds one more lot of 3, so $y$ goes up by $m$, the number in front of $x$.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ## Find the mistake

**Lee decides which line is steeper. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Which line is steeper: y = 2x + 4 or y = 3x + 1?
 2  y = 2x + 4: when x = 0, y = 4
 3  y = 3x + 1: when x = 0, y = 1
 4  4 is bigger than 1, so y = 2x + 4 is steeper.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: 4 and 1 are where the lines cross the y-axis, not how steep they are. Steepness is the number in front of x: 3 is more than 2, so y = 3x + 1 is steeper. -->

**On the line $y = 3x + 1$, how far does $y$ go up from $x = 5$ to $x = 6$?**

* Answer: 3

## Change m on purpose



- **Steeper:** Each task puts a point $T$ on the board. Find the $m$ that takes the line through $T(2, 9)$. Change only $m$.
- **Flat:** Now take the line through $T(3, 1)$. What does the line do?
- **Down:** $T(2, -3)$ is below the start of the line. Which way must it go?
- **Less than 1:** Take the line through $T(4, 3)$. The slider now moves in halves.

## Exit ticket

**Without plotting, describe the line y = −2x + 1: where does it cross the y-axis, which way does it go, and how steep is it?**

* It crosses the y-axis at 1 and goes down 2 for each step to the right (m = −2).
