# The midpoint

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two friends live at opposite ends of town and want to meet halfway. Where on the map is halfway?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find the midpoint of two points by averaging each coordinate, and work backwards to a missing end point (AC9M9A03, node 4).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the point halfway between two points. I can average the x-coordinates and the y-coordinates separately. I can find an end point when I know the midpoint.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Averaging


:::keep-together

### Halfway between numbers

Here's finding a midpoint.

1. $A(2, 3),\; B(10, 9)$ — The two points.
2. $x = \frac{2 + 10}{2} = 6$ — Average the x-coordinates.
3. $y = \frac{3 + 9}{2} = 6$ — Average the y-coordinates.
4. $M(6, 6)$ — The midpoint.

Now try one on paper: the points $(-4, 1)$ and $(6, 9)$. Find the midpoint, then type its $x$-coordinate.

**Find the number halfway between $x = 4$ and $x = 12$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

(4 + 12) ÷ 2 = 8.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which midpoint?

**What is the midpoint of $(-2, 5)$ and $(4, -1)$?**

☐ $(1, 2)$

☐ $(-1, 3)$

☐ $(2, 4)$

☐ $(3, 2)$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $(1, 2)$

x: (−2 + 4) ÷ 2 = 1. y: (5 + −1) ÷ 2 = 2. The midpoint is (1, 2).

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Tam finds the midpoint of $(2, 3)$ and $(10, 9)$. Click the line where his working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Find the midpoint of (2, 3) and (10, 9).
 2  Add the x-coordinates: 2 + 10 = 12
 3  Add the y-coordinates: 3 + 9 = 12
 4  The midpoint is (12, 12)
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: he stopped after adding. (2 + 10) ÷ 2 = 6 and (3 + 9) ÷ 2 = 6, so the midpoint is (6, 6).

## Find the midpoint


:::keep-together

### A midpoint

**Find the midpoint of $(0, 4)$ and $(8, 10)$.**

☐ $(4, 3)$

☐ $(4, 7)$

☐ $(8, 14)$

☐ $(4, 14)$

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $(4, 7)$

(0 + 8) ÷ 2 = 4, (4 + 10) ÷ 2 = 7.

:::


:::keep-together

### The y-coordinate

**Find the $y$-coordinate of the midpoint of $(3, -6)$ and $(5, 2)$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -2

(−6 + 2) ÷ 2 = −4 ÷ 2 = −2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Working backwards

**The midpoint of $A$ and $B(9, 5)$ is $(6, 4)$. What is the $y$-coordinate of $A$?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

(y + 5) ÷ 2 = 4, so y + 5 = 8 and y = 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: how far is it between $(0, 0)$ and $(6, 8)$?**

☐ 7

☐ 10

☐ 14

☐ 48

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10

6² + 8² = 100, and √100 = 10.
