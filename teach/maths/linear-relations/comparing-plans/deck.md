# Comparing two plans

Linear relations · Solving and modelling with lines

<!-- Speaker note: Students put two linear rules on one graph, find where they are equal, decide which is cheaper on each side in the situation's words, and say where a model makes sense (AC9M8A03, node 2). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

## Today

**We are learning to** put two rules on one graph. Where the lines cross they cost the same; on either side, the lower line is the cheaper plan. Then check where the model makes sense.

- I can find where two plans cost the same from their graphs, and say what that means.
- I can decide which plan is cheaper for a given amount, and explain why in words.
- I can say which values make sense for a model, and spot an answer that does not.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. This lesson uses the last two: solving with graphs (where lines meet) and writing rules from situations. The new move is interpretation. The crossing answers "when are they the same?"; the lower line answers "which is cheaper?"; and every model has values where it makes sense (no negative data, whole tickets, a battery that stops at 0%). Answers should be sentences in the situation's words, not just numbers. The lines start at 0 on purpose: the graph itself shows where the model applies. -->

## Why it matters

> Phone plans, gyms and streaming services all ask you to choose between a bigger fee and a lower rate. Two lines on one graph answer it for any amount.

## Comparing the plans

Here is how to compare two plans and answer in words.

Answer in words: "Up to 4 GB, Plan A is cheaper. At 4 GB they both cost 40 dollars. Above 4 GB, Plan B is cheaper."

$$T = 5g + 20, \quad T = 40$$

1) $5g + 20 = 40$ — They cost the same where the two rules are equal: where the lines cross.
2) $g = 4, \; T = 40$ — The lines cross at $(4, 40)$. Check: $5 \times 4 + 20 = 40$.
3) $g < 4: \text{ A is cheaper}$ — Left of the crossing, Plan A's line is lower.
4) $g > 4: \text{ B is cheaper}$ — Right of the crossing, Plan B's line is lower.
5) $g \ge 0$ — The model only makes sense from 0 GB: you cannot use negative data.

**Leo compares the plans. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Plan A: 20 dollars a month plus 5 dollars per GB.
 2  Plan B: 40 dollars a month, any amount of data.
 3  Plan A has the lower monthly fee.
 4  So Plan A is always the cheaper plan.
```

* Answer: line 4

## Does it make sense?

**The rule $T = 5g + 20$ gives $T = 10$ when $g = -2$. Why does that answer not make sense?**

- Phone plans can cost less than their fee
- You cannot use negative data, so the rule only works for g of 0 or more
- The rule is wrong: it should be T = 5g − 20

* Answer: You cannot use negative data, so the rule only works for g of 0 or more

<!-- Speaker note: A model only fits the situation for some values. Data used cannot be negative, so the rule (and its line) starts at g = 0. -->

## Exit ticket

**Plan X costs 15 dollars plus 6 per GB; Plan Y costs 33 dollars flat. Which is cheaper for 2 GB and for 5 GB? Where do they cost the same?**

* 2 GB: X (27 dollars vs 33). 5 GB: Y (45 vs 33). They cost the same at 3 GB (33 dollars).
