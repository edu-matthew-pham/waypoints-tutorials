# Comparing two plans

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Phone plans, gyms and streaming services all ask you to choose between a bigger fee and a lower rate. Two lines on one graph answer it for any amount.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students put two linear rules on one graph, find where they are equal, decide which is cheaper on each side in the situation's words, and say where a model makes sense (AC9M8A03, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can find where two plans cost the same from their graphs, and say what that means. I can decide which plan is cheaper for a given amount, and explain why in words. I can say which values make sense for a model, and spot an answer that does not.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

Work out what each plan costs for 6 GB. Then think: is there an amount of data where they cost the same?

:::keep-together

**Plan A costs 20 dollars a month plus 5 dollars per GB. Plan B costs 40 dollars a month for any amount of data. Mia uses 6 GB a month. Which plan is cheaper for her?**

☐ Plan B

☐ They cost the same

☐ Plan A: it has the lower fee

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Plan B. For 6 GB, Plan A costs 20 + 5 × 6 = 50 dollars and Plan B costs 40. A's lower fee only wins for small amounts of data.

:::

## Answering in words


:::keep-together

### Comparing the plans

Here is how to compare two plans and answer in words.

1. $T = 5g + 20, \quad T = 40$ — Plan A and Plan B, as rules. Which is cheaper, and when?
2. $5g + 20 = 40$ — They cost the same where the two rules are equal: where the lines cross.
3. $g = 4, \; T = 40$ — The lines cross at $(4, 40)$. Check: $5 \times 4 + 20 = 40$.
4. $g < 4: \text{ A is cheaper}$ — Left of the crossing, Plan A's line is lower.
5. $g > 4: \text{ B is cheaper}$ — Right of the crossing, Plan B's line is lower.
6. $g \ge 0$ — The model only makes sense from 0 GB: you cannot use negative data.

Answer in words: "Up to 4 GB, Plan A is cheaper. At 4 GB they both cost 40 dollars. Above 4 GB, Plan B is cheaper."

**For 2 GB a month, which plan is cheaper?**

☐ Plan A

☐ Plan B

☐ They cost the same

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Plan A

For 2 GB, Plan A costs 20 + 5 × 2 = 30 dollars and Plan B costs 40. Left of the crossing, Plan A's line is lower.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Leo compares the plans. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Plan A: 20 dollars a month plus 5 dollars per GB.
 2  Plan B: 40 dollars a month, any amount of data.
 3  Plan A has the lower monthly fee.
 4  So Plan A is always the cheaper plan.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: Plan A's lower fee only wins up to 4 GB. After that its 5 dollars per GB adds up, and Plan B is cheaper. Which plan is cheaper depends on how much data you use.

:::


:::keep-together

### Does it make sense?

**The rule $T = 5g + 20$ gives $T = 10$ when $g = -2$. Why does that answer not make sense?**

☐ Phone plans can cost less than their fee

☐ You cannot use negative data, so the rule only works for g of 0 or more

☐ The rule is wrong: it should be T = 5g − 20

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** You cannot use negative data, so the rule only works for g of 0 or more

A model only fits the situation for some values. Data used cannot be negative, so the rule (and its line) starts at g = 0.

:::

## Compare and check


:::keep-together

### When they are equal

**Plan C costs 10 dollars plus 8 dollars per GB. Plan D costs 34 dollars for any amount. At how many GB do they cost the same?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

10 + 8g = 34, so 8g = 24 and g = 3. Check: 10 + 24 = 34.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which is cheaper?

**Using Plans C and D, which is cheaper for 5 GB?**

☐ Plan C

☐ They cost the same

☐ Plan D

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Plan D

Plan C: 10 + 8 × 5 = 50 dollars. Plan D: 34 dollars. Above 3 GB, D is cheaper.

:::


:::keep-together

### Values that make sense

**Concert tickets cost $T = 12n + 6$ dollars for $n$ tickets. Which values of $n$ make sense?**

☐ Whole numbers from 1 up

☐ Any number, including 2.5

☐ Any number, including negatives

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Whole numbers from 1 up

You buy whole tickets, and at least one. The line joins the points, but only the whole numbers are real orders.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: an electrician charges a 25 dollar call-out fee plus 9 dollars an hour. Write a rule for the total $T$ for $h$ hours.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9h + 25

T = 9h + 25: 9 dollars for each hour (m) and 25 dollars once (c).

:::
