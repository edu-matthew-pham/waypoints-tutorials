# Setting up equations

Linear relations · Year 10: two variables

<!-- Speaker note: Students turn a rate card into a rule with named letters and one unit, set two plans equal, solve, and say the answer in the words of the situation, checking it makes sense (AC9M10A02, node 4). The electricity plans and the sample bill are made up; students can repeat the Make task with a real bill from home. About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 16 min · Modify 11 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can name the letters, with units, and write a rule from a rate card.
- I can keep every quantity in the same unit before I set up an equation.
- I can say a solution in the words of the situation and check it makes sense.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board and the Make task yourself first. Students need Solving by substitution and Solving by elimination in this unit. This lesson is mostly setting up and interpreting, not new algebra. The one idea: the maths is only half the work. The letters need meanings and units, the answer needs to be said in the situation's words, and it needs a sense check (a negative number of kWh, or half a ticket, is not an answer). The common mistake is mixing units (Tess's working). The rate cards, the sample bill (90 days, 1,260 kWh) and all prices are made up so the crossing is a whole number. In the Make task, students can use a real bill from home, but they need not: a sample is enough, and no one should be asked to show a bill. Real plans add time-of-use rates, discounts and solar credits that this model ignores. Teacher: Paper: the try-one after the worked example (Plans C and D, then A and B) and the bill in Make are worked on paper first. Books out, example beside them; look for one unit throughout. -->

## Predict

**A household's bill covers 90 days and 1,260 kWh of electricity (14 kWh a day). Plan A charges 120 cents a day plus 24 cents per kWh. Plan B charges 72 cents a day plus 28 cents per kWh. Which plan costs this household less?**

- Plan A, with the higher daily charge and the lower rate
- Plan B, with the lower daily charge and the higher rate
- They cost the same

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Plan A, by 7.20 dollars. Plan A: 90 × 120 + 1,260 × 24 = 41,040 cents. Plan B: 90 × 72 + 1,260 × 28 = 41,760 cents. A household that uses a lot saves more on the rate than it pays extra in daily charges. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Write the rule

**Let $k$ be the kWh used in a day and $c$ the cost for the day in cents. Plan A charges 120 cents a day plus 24 cents per kWh. Which rule is Plan A?**

- $c = 144k$
- $c = 120 + 24 + k$
- $c = 120 + 24k$
- $c = 120k + 24$

* Answer: $c = 120 + 24k$

<!-- Speaker note: The fixed part is 120 cents a day. The rate part is 24 cents times the kWh used: c = 120 + 24k. -->

## Find the mistake

**Tess writes Plan A as a rule. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  Plan A: the daily charge is 1.20 dollars and the rate is 24 cents per kWh.
 2  Let k be the kWh used in a day and c the cost for the day in dollars.
 3  So c = 1.20 + 24k.
 4  For k = 10 that gives c = 241.20 dollars for the day.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the 1.20 is in dollars but the 24 is in cents, so the rule mixes units. Write both in cents: c = 120 + 24k. Then 10 kWh costs 360 cents, which is 3.60 dollars. -->

## Try one on paper

Here is the whole routine on a different pair of plans: letters, rules, solve, then say it in words.

Now try one on paper with the example beside you: Plans A and B. Find the $k$ where they cost the same, then type it.

$$c = 100 + 30k,\; c = 80 + 35k$$

1) $100 + 30k = 80 + 35k$ — Both rules give $c$, so set them equal.
2) $20 = 5k$ — Take $30k$ and $80$ from both sides.
3) $k = 4$ — Divide by 5.
4) $c = 100 + 30 \times 4 = 220$ — Put $k$ into one rule for the cost.
5) $\text{Both cost 220 cents a day at 4 kWh a day.}$ — Say it in the words of the situation, with its units. Below 4 kWh Plan D is cheaper; above it, Plan C.

## Try one on paper

**Plan A is $c = 120 + 24k$ and Plan B is $c = 72 + 28k$ (cents for a day, with $k$ kWh in the day). Find $k$ where the two plans cost the same, on paper, with the example beside you. Then type $k$.**

* Answer: $120 + 24k = 72 + 28k$ (both rules give c, so set them equal); $48 = 4k$ (take 24k and 72 from both sides); $k = 12$ (divide by 4; then $c = 120 + 24 \times 12 = 408$ cents)
