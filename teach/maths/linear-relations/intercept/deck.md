# What c does to a line

Linear relations · Lines and their rules

<!-- Speaker note: Students vary c in y = 2x + c on a graph board, find that c moves the line up or down and is where it crosses the y-axis, and that lines with the same m are parallel (AC9M8A04, node 2). About 45 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 15 min · Modify 12 min · Practise and Make 10 min). -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** in y = mx + c, the number on its own moves the line up or down without changing its steepness, so lines with the same m are parallel.

- I can describe what happens to the line y = mx + c when c changes.
- I can read where a line crosses the y-axis from its rule.
- I can tell from their rules whether two lines are parallel.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run and Modify boards yourself first. This lesson follows "What m does to a line": students should already read m as "up for each step right". Here m stays at 2 and only c changes, so every line is parallel to the dashed y = 2x + 1. The last Modify task is the first time both sliders appear: it is the stretch point of the lesson. The lesson says "where the line crosses the y-axis". The primer has the formal word, y-intercept, if you want to introduce it at the end. -->

## Why it matters

> Two phone plans with the same price per gigabyte but different monthly fees never meet: their graphs are parallel. The fee is c.

<!-- Speaker note: In the last lesson $c$ stayed at 1 and you changed $m$, the steepness. Now $m$ stays at 2 and you change $c$, the number on its own. -->

## Where it crosses

Here is how to read $c$ from a rule.

$$y = 2x + 4$$

1) $x = 0: \; y = 2(0) + 4 = 4$ — The $y$-axis is where $x = 0$. Put 0 in place of $x$.
2) $c = 4$ — $2x$ is 0 there, so $y$ is just $c$: the line crosses at 4.
3) $x = 1: \; 2(1) + 4 = 6, \; 2(1) + 1 = 3$ — Compare the two rules at $x = 1$.
4) $6 - 3 = 3$ — $y$ is 3 more, and it is 3 more at every $x$, because only $c$ changed.
5) $\text{same } m \Rightarrow \text{parallel}$ — The line moved up 3 without turning: lines with the same $m$ are parallel.

## Where it crosses

**Where does the line $y = 3x - 5$ cross the $y$-axis? Give the value of $y$.**

* Answer: -5

**Ana decides whether two lines are parallel. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Are y = 2x + 5 and y = 5x + 2 parallel?

 3  So they have the same numbers.
 4  So the lines are parallel.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: parallel lines need the same m, the number in front of x. Here m is 2 and 5, so one is steeper than the other: they are not parallel. -->

## Parallel

**Which line is parallel to $y = 3x + 1$?**

- $y = x + 3$
- $y = 3x - 4$

## Change c on purpose



- **Below the axis:** Each task puts a point $T$ on the board. Set $c$ so the line passes through $T(0, -2)$.
- **Through the origin:** Now take the line through $T(2, 4)$. What is $c$?
- **Not on the axis:** $T(1, 6)$ is not on the $y$-axis. Work out $2x$ at $T$ first.
- **Parallel:** Two sliders now. Make a line parallel to the dashed one, through $T(0, -1)$.
