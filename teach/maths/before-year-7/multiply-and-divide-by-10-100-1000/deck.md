# Multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000

Before Year 7 maths · Number tools

<!-- Speaker note: Students see multiplying and dividing by powers of ten as every digit moving places, and drop the "add a zero" rule. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what happens to each digit when I multiply by 10.
- I can multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1000.
- I can say why "add a zero" does not work for decimals.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: each place is worth 10 times the one on its right, so × 10 moves every digit one place left. The common mistake is "add a zero" (3.5 × 10 = 3.50). The Think first question meets it. The board is the place value board from the Year 7 lesson *Expanded notation*. It shows whole numbers only. Use the decimals questions to carry the idea over. If you have base ten blocks, show that 10 tenths make a one. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students whether 3.50 is bigger than 3.5. Teacher: Try: ask what happens to the 3 when you press × 10. Teacher: Apply: look for "every digit moves". -->

---

## Why it matters

> Move the decimal point or add a zero? One of these rules is wrong.

<!-- Speaker note: Our number system is built on tens. Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right. So multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place to the left, to a place worth 10 times more. -->

## The zero rule

**Why is "add a zero" a bad rule for 3.5 × 10?**

- You should add a zero at the front
- The answer is 35, not 3.50: the digits move, nothing is added
- The rule works for decimals but not whole numbers
- 3.5 × 10 is 3.50

* Answer: The answer is 35, not 3.50: the digits move, nothing is added

<!-- Speaker note: The digits move one place left: 3.5 × 10 = 35. -->
