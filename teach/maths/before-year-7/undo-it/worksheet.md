# Undo it

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** I think of a number, double it and add 3. I get 11. What was my number?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students use inverse operations to undo steps backwards, and drop the idea of undoing in the same order as the steps.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say which operations undo each other. I can undo a two-step puzzle. I can say why I undo the last step first.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

Add and take away undo each other. Multiply and divide undo each other. To undo a list of steps, go **backwards**: undo the last step first, then the one before it.

:::keep-together

### The opposite

**Which operation undoes "add 5"?**

☐ Divide by 5

☐ Take away 5

☐ Add 5 again

☐ Multiply by 5

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Take away 5

Taking away 5 undoes adding 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Add 6

**I think of a number, add 6 and get 15. What was my number?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9

15 take away 6 is 9. Check: 9 + 6 = 15.

:::


:::keep-together

### Two steps

**A number is multiplied by 3, then 2 is taken away. How do you undo that?**

☐ Take away 2, then divide by 3

☐ Add 2, then divide by 3

☐ Add 2, then multiply by 3

☐ Divide by 3, then add 2

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Add 2, then divide by 3

Undo the last step first: add 2. Then undo the multiply: divide by 3.

:::

### Multiply and add

**I think of a number, multiply it by 4 and add 1. I get 21. What was my number?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

21 - 1 = 20, and 20 ÷ 4 = 5. Check: 5 × 4 + 1 = 21.

:::


:::keep-together

### Halve and add

**I think of a number, halve it and add 3. I get 7. What was my number?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

7 - 3 = 4, and double 4 is 8. Check: half of 8 is 4, plus 3 is 7.

:::
