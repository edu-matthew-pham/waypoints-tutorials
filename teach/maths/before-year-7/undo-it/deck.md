# Undo it

Before Year 7 maths · Unknowns

<!-- Speaker note: Students use inverse operations to undo steps backwards, and drop the idea of undoing in the same order as the steps. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say which operations undo each other.
- I can undo a two-step puzzle.
- I can say why I undo the last step first.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: undo the steps backwards. The common mistake is undoing only one step, or in the same order. The Think first question meets it. The board is the function machine from the Year 7 lesson *Solving by backtracking*. It checks the answer, and gives a nudge for a wrong undo. Putting on socks and shoes is a good picture: to undo it, take off the shoes first. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to put on and take off a jacket and a scarf in the right order. Teacher: Try: ask them to say each undo aloud. Teacher: Apply: look for "last step first". -->

## Why it matters

> I think of a number, double it and add 3. I get 11. What was my number?

<!-- Speaker note: Some operations undo each other. **Adding 5** is undone by **taking away 5**. **Multiplying by 3** is undone by **dividing by 3**. These pairs are called **inverse operations**. -->

## What happened?

* 4. Undo the last step first: 11 take away 3 is 8. Then undo the doubling: 8 divided by 2 is 4. Check: 4 doubled is 8, plus 3 is 11.

## The opposite

**Which operation undoes "add 5"?**

- Divide by 5
- Take away 5
- Add 5 again
- Multiply by 5

* Answer: Take away 5

<!-- Speaker note: Taking away 5 undoes adding 5. -->

## Two steps

**A number is multiplied by 3, then 2 is taken away. How do you undo that?**

- Take away 2, then divide by 3
- Add 2, then divide by 3
- Add 2, then multiply by 3
- Divide by 3, then add 2

* Answer: Add 2, then divide by 3

<!-- Speaker note: Undo the last step first: add 2. Then undo the multiply: divide by 3. -->

## Multiply and add

**I think of a number, multiply it by 4 and add 1. I get 21. What was my number?**

* Answer: 5

<!-- Speaker note: 21 - 1 = 20, and 20 ÷ 4 = 5. Check: 5 × 4 + 1 = 21. -->

## Halve and add

**I think of a number, halve it and add 3. I get 7. What was my number?**

* Answer: 8

## Exit ticket

**I think of a number, add 4 and double it. The answer is 18. What was my number?**

* 5 (18 ÷ 2 = 9, then 9 − 4 = 5).
