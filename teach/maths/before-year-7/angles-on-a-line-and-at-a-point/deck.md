# Angles on a line and at a point

Before Year 7 maths · Unknowns

<!-- Speaker note: Students use 180 for a straight line and 360 round a point to find missing angles. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say how many degrees are on a straight line.
- I can say how many degrees are round a point.
- I can find a missing angle.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: a straight line is 180 and a full turn is 360. The common mistake is mixing the two totals. The Think first question meets it. The board is the parallel line board from the Year 7 lessons *Angles at parallel lines*. Here the lines are only a straight line to read angles from, and the angle sizes are hidden so they can pick pairs. A paper circle folded in half and quarters shows the turns. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to turn on the spot through half a turn and a full turn. Teacher: Try: ask which angles together make a straight line. Teacher: Apply: look for the total taken away. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Two angles sit side by side on a straight line. One is 70 degrees. How big is the other?

<!-- Speaker note: A straight line is half a turn, which is $180°$. A full turn is $360°$. So **angles on a straight line** add to 180° and **angles round a point** add to 360°. -->

## Think first

**Two angles are next to each other on a straight line. One is 70°. How big is the other?**

- 20°
- 70°
- 110°
- 290°

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: 110°. Angles on a straight line add to 180°, and 180 - 70 = 110. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## What happened?

* 110°. Angles on a straight line add to 180°, and 180 - 70 = 110.

## On a line

**Two angles on a straight line are 125° and ?°. What is the missing angle?**

* Answer: 55

<!-- Speaker note: 180 - 125 = 55. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Round a point

**Angles around a point are 90°, 120°, 100° and ?°. What is the missing angle?**

* Answer: 50

<!-- Speaker note: 90 + 120 + 100 = 310, and 360 - 310 = 50. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## A full turn

**How big is a full turn?**

- 90°
- 100°
- 180°
- 360°

* Answer: 360°

<!-- Speaker note: A full turn is 360°. Half a turn is 180° and a quarter turn is 90°. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## On a line

**On a straight line, 40° and ?° sit side by side. What is the missing angle?**

* Answer: 140

<!-- Speaker note: 180 - 40 = 140. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Round a point

**Angles round a point are 150°, 150° and ?°. What is the missing angle?**

* Answer: 60

<!-- Speaker note: 150 + 150 = 300, and 360 - 300 = 60. -->

**Angles on a line are 100° and ?°. What is ?, and what do angles round a point add to?**
