# Probability from 0 to 1

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Pick a counter from a bag with 3 red and 1 blue. Is red likely?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place chances on a scale from 0 to 1 and find a simple probability as a fraction, and drop the idea that every event is a 50-50 chance.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what 0 and 1 mean for a chance. I can work out a simple chance as a fraction or a decimal. I can say which events are likely and unlikely.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

**Probability** = how many ways it can happen, out of all the ways. It goes from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain), and can be a fraction, a decimal or a percentage. $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is an even chance.

:::keep-together

### How likely?

**How likely is it?**

Groups: Impossible (0), Unlikely, Even chance, Likely, Certain (1)

- A coin lands heads or tails: {{field-15}}
- A coin lands on heads: {{field-15}}
- It snows in Canberra in January: {{field-15}}
- A die shows a 7: {{field-15}}
- A die shows a number less than 6: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A coin lands heads or tails → Certain (1); A coin lands on heads → Even chance; It snows in Canberra in January → Unlikely; A die shows a 7 → Impossible (0); A die shows a number less than 6 → Likely

### Counters in a bag

**A bag has 10 counters and 3 are red. What is the chance of red, as a decimal?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.3

3 out of 10 is 3/10, which is 0.3.

:::


:::keep-together

### Not a probability

**Which of these cannot be a probability?**

☐ 0

☐ 0.5

☐ 1

☐ 1.5

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1.5

A probability is never more than 1, because certain is 1.

:::
