# Factors and multiples

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Is 24 a factor of 12 or a multiple of 12? It is one of them, and mixing them up is easy.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students tell factors from multiples and find factor pairs, and drop the idea that the two words mean the same.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a factor is. I can say what a multiple is. I can find factor pairs of a number.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

**Factors** of 12 divide into 12 exactly: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. **Multiples** of 12 come from counting in 12s: 12, 24, 36, 48. A number is a factor and a multiple of itself.

:::keep-together

### Counting in jumps

**Is 15 a multiple of 4?**

☐ No, because 4 does not make 15 when you count in 4s

☐ Yes, because 15 is bigger than 4

☐ No, because 15 is odd and 4 is even

☐ Yes, because 4 and 15 are both in the 4 times table

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No, because 4 does not make 15 when you count in 4s

Counting in 4s goes 4, 8, 12, 16, so 15 is skipped.
