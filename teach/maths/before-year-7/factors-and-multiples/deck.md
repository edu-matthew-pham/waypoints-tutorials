# Factors and multiples

Before Year 7 maths · Number tools

<!-- Speaker note: Students tell factors from multiples and find factor pairs, and drop the idea that the two words mean the same. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a factor is.
- I can say what a multiple is.
- I can find factor pairs of a number.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: factors go into a number and multiples come out of it. The common mistake is mixing the two words (24 is a factor of 12). ## Why it matters

> Is 24 a factor of 12 or a multiple of 12? It is one of them, and mixing them up is easy.

<!-- Speaker note: A **factor** of a number divides into it with nothing left over. A **multiple** of a number is what you get when you count in jumps of that number: 12, 24, 36, and so on. -->

## Factor or multiple?

**Is it a factor of 12 or a multiple of 12?**

Groups: Factor of 12 · Multiple of 12

3 · 6 · 24 · 36 · 2 · 48

* Answer: 3 → Factor of 12; 6 → Factor of 12; 24 → Multiple of 12; 36 → Multiple of 12; 2 → Factor of 12; 48 → Multiple of 12

## Factor pairs

**How many factor pairs does 20 have? (1 × 20, 2 × 10, 4 × 5, …)**

* Answer: 3

## Counting in jumps

**Is 15 a multiple of 4?**

- No, because 4 does not make 15 when you count in 4s
- Yes, because 15 is bigger than 4
- No, because 15 is odd and 4 is even
- Yes, because 4 and 15 are both in the 4 times table

* Answer: No, because 4 does not make 15 when you count in 4s

<!-- Speaker note: Counting in 4s goes 4, 8, 12, 16, so 15 is skipped. -->

## Multiples of 7

**Which number is a multiple of 7?**

- 17
- 27
- 35
- 47

* Answer: 35
