# Fractions on a number line

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You walk 5 quarters of a kilometre. Is that more or less than one kilometre?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place fractions on a number line, including past 1, and see the bottom number as the size of the parts.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what the top and bottom numbers mean on a number line. I can put a fraction on a number line. I can say a fraction past 1 in two ways.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

To place a fraction, cut each whole into the number of parts on the bottom, then count that many parts from zero. $\tfrac{5}{4}$ is 5 quarters, which is the same as $1\tfrac{1}{4}$.

:::keep-together

### Counting quarters

**How many quarters are in 1 1/4?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

1 whole is 4 quarters, and 1 more quarter makes 5 quarters: 1 1/4 = 5/4.

:::


:::keep-together

### Past one

**Where is 5/4 on the number line?**

☐ Just past 1, one quarter further on

☐ Just before 1

☐ Halfway between 0 and 1

☐ At 5

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Just past 1, one quarter further on

4/4 is 1, so 5/4 is one quarter past 1.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which is bigger?

**Which is bigger, 7/4 or 1 1/2?**

☐ 1 1/2

☐ 7/4

☐ They are the same

☐ You cannot tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7/4

1 1/2 is 6/4 and 7/4 is one quarter more.

:::

### Halves

**How many halves are in 2 1/2?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

2 wholes = 4 halves, plus 1 half = 5 halves.

### Thirds

**A line is cut into thirds. Where is 4/3?**

☐ Halfway between 1 and 2

☐ One third past 1

☐ One third before 1

☐ At 4

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** One third past 1

3/3 is 1, so 4/3 is one third further.

:::
