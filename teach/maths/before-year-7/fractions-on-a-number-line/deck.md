# Fractions on a number line

Before Year 7 maths · Parts of a whole

<!-- Speaker note: Students place fractions on a number line, including past 1, and see the bottom number as the size of the parts. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what the top and bottom numbers mean on a number line.
- I can put a fraction on a number line.
- I can say a fraction past 1 in two ways.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: count equal parts from zero. The common mistakes are counting the marks (so the first mark is "1") and stopping at 1. The Think first question and the board meet them. The board is the number line used in the Year 7 lesson *Fractions and decimals on the line*, with fractions on and only positive numbers. A ruler or a metre strip marked in quarters is a good thing to hold. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to say where 3/4 would go on a ruler. Teacher: Try: ask how many quarters to reach 1. Teacher: Apply: look for the words "count the parts from zero". -->

---

## Why it matters

> You walk 5 quarters of a kilometre. Is that more or less than one kilometre?

<!-- Speaker note: Fractions are numbers, so they have a place on a number line. The bottom number says how many equal parts each whole is cut into. The top number says how many of those parts to count from zero. -->

## Think first

**A number line from 0 to 1 is cut into 4 equal parts. Where is 3/4?**

- Halfway between 0 and 1
- At 4
- 3 whole steps from 0
- 3 parts along from 0

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: 3 parts along from 0. Each part is a quarter, so you count 3 quarters. It is the mark just before 1. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Counting quarters

**How many quarters are in 1 1/4?**

* Answer: 5

<!-- Speaker note: 1 whole is 4 quarters, and 1 more quarter makes 5 quarters: 1 1/4 = 5/4. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Past one

**Where is 5/4 on the number line?**

- Just past 1, one quarter further on
- Just before 1
- Halfway between 0 and 1
- At 5

* Answer: Just past 1, one quarter further on

<!-- Speaker note: 4/4 is 1, so 5/4 is one quarter past 1. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Which is bigger?

**Which is bigger, 7/4 or 1 1/2?**

- 1 1/2
- 7/4
- They are the same
- You cannot tell

* Answer: 7/4

<!-- Speaker note: 1 1/2 is 6/4 and 7/4 is one quarter more. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Halves

**How many halves are in 2 1/2?**

* Answer: 5

<!-- Speaker note: 2 wholes = 4 halves, plus 1 half = 5 halves. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Thirds

**A line is cut into thirds. Where is 4/3?**

- Halfway between 1 and 2
- One third past 1
- One third before 1
- At 4

* Answer: One third past 1

<!-- Speaker note: 3/3 is 1, so 4/3 is one third further. -->

## Exit ticket

**Where does 5/4 go, and what is another way to write it?**

* One quarter past 1, and it is 1 1/4.
