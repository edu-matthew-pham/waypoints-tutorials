# Tenths and hundredths

Before Year 7 maths · Parts of a whole

<!-- Speaker note: Students see a decimal as a number of tenths and hundredths, and drop the idea that a longer decimal is a bigger one. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a tenth and a hundredth are.
- I can change tenths into hundredths.
- I can say which of two decimals is bigger and why.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: tenths and hundredths are parts of a whole, and a tenth is 10 hundredths. The common mistake is that 0.15 is bigger than 0.5 because 15 is bigger than 5. The Think first question meets it. The board is the fraction bar and hundred grid from the Year 7 lesson *Fractions, decimals and percentages*. Use it here only to see 3 tenths shade 30 squares. If students are shaky, hold up a 10 by 10 grid and shade one row for one tenth. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to say it aloud as "fifteen hundredths" and "five tenths". Teacher: Try: ask how many squares one tenth shades. Teacher: Apply: look for answers that change both to hundredths. -->

---

## Why it matters

> Is 0.15 bigger than 0.5? The 15 looks bigger, but is it?

<!-- Speaker note: A **tenth** is one part when you share a whole into 10 equal parts. A **hundredth** is one part when you share it into 100. Decimals write these parts after a point: $0.3$ is 3 tenths, and $0.03$ is 3 hundredths. -->

## Think first

**Which is bigger, 0.15 or 0.5?**

- 0.5
- They are the same
- 0.15
- You cannot tell

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: 0.5. It is 5 tenths, which is the same as 50 hundredths. 0.15 is only 15 hundredths. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Counting tenths

**What is 0.7 as a number of tenths?**

- 7 hundredths
- 70 tenths
- 7 tenths
- 0.7 tenths

* Answer: 7 tenths

<!-- Speaker note: 0.7 is 7 tenths: 7 of the 10 equal parts of a whole. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Tenths into hundredths

**0.3 is the same as how many hundredths?**

* Answer: 30

<!-- Speaker note: 1 tenth is 10 hundredths, so 3 tenths is 30 hundredths: 0.3 = 0.30. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Which is bigger?

**Which is bigger, 0.4 or 0.09?**

- 0.09
- 0.4
- They are the same
- You cannot tell

* Answer: 0.4

<!-- Speaker note: 0.4 is 40 hundredths and 0.09 is 9 hundredths, so 0.4 is bigger. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## In order

**Which list goes from smallest to biggest?**

- 0.6, 0.16, 0.06
- 0.06, 0.6, 0.16
- 0.16, 0.06, 0.6
- 0.06, 0.16, 0.6

* Answer: 0.06, 0.16, 0.6

<!-- Speaker note: In hundredths they are 6, 16 and 60. Smallest first: 0.06, 0.16, 0.6. -->

## Hundredths

**0.8 is the same as how many hundredths?**

* Answer: 80

## Exit ticket

**Which is bigger, 0.7 or 0.07, and how do you know?**

* 0.7 is bigger. It is 7 tenths, or 70 hundredths, and 0.07 is only 7 hundredths.
