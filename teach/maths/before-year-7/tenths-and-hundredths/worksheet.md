# Tenths and hundredths

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Is 0.15 bigger than 0.5? The 15 looks bigger, but is it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see a decimal as a number of tenths and hundredths, and drop the idea that a longer decimal is a bigger one.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a tenth and a hundredth are. I can change tenths into hundredths. I can say which of two decimals is bigger and why.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

A **tenth** is 10 **hundredths**, so $0.3 = 0.30$. To compare two decimals, write them with the same kind of part. Having more digits does not make a decimal bigger.

:::keep-together

### Counting tenths

**What is 0.7 as a number of tenths?**

☐ 7 hundredths

☐ 70 tenths

☐ 7 tenths

☐ 0.7 tenths

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7 tenths

0.7 is 7 tenths: 7 of the 10 equal parts of a whole.

:::


:::keep-together

### Tenths into hundredths

**0.3 is the same as how many hundredths?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 30

1 tenth is 10 hundredths, so 3 tenths is 30 hundredths: 0.3 = 0.30.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which is bigger?

**Which is bigger, 0.4 or 0.09?**

☐ 0.09

☐ 0.4

☐ They are the same

☐ You cannot tell

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.4

0.4 is 40 hundredths and 0.09 is 9 hundredths, so 0.4 is bigger.

:::

### In order

**Which list goes from smallest to biggest?**

☐ 0.6, 0.16, 0.06

☐ 0.06, 0.6, 0.16

☐ 0.16, 0.06, 0.6

☐ 0.06, 0.16, 0.6

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.06, 0.16, 0.6

In hundredths they are 6, 16 and 60. Smallest first: 0.06, 0.16, 0.6.

:::


:::keep-together
