# Same amount, different fraction

Before Year 7 maths · Parts of a whole

<!-- Speaker note: Students see that different fractions can name the same amount, and drop the idea that bigger numbers mean a bigger fraction. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say two fractions are the same amount.
- I can find a fraction equivalent to one I am given.
- I can say why the amount does not change.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: cut every part into the same number of smaller parts and the amount is the same. Paper folding works well: fold a strip in half, then in half again, and shade. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to draw 1/2 and 4/8 of the same rectangle. ## Why it matters

> You eat 2 of 4 slices, your friend eats 1 of 2 slices of the same pizza. Who ate more?

<!-- Speaker note: A fraction like $\tfrac{1}{2}$ can have many names. $\tfrac{2}{4}$, $\tfrac{4}{8}$ and $\tfrac{5}{10}$ are all the same amount. They are called **equivalent fractions**. -->

## Think first

**Is 4/8 of a pizza more than 1/2 of the same pizza?**

- Yes, because 4 and 8 are bigger numbers
- No, they are the same amount
- No, 1/2 is bigger
- You cannot tell

## Same as a half?

**Is it the same amount as 1/2?**

Groups: Same as 1/2 · Not the same

2/4 · 3/6 · 1/3 · 5/10 · 2/3

* Answer: 2/4 → Same as 1/2; 3/6 → Same as 1/2; 1/3 → Not the same; 5/10 → Same as 1/2; 2/3 → Not the same

## The missing number

**2/3 is the same as ?/12. What is the missing number?**

* Answer: 8

<!-- Speaker note: 3 × 4 = 12, so 2 × 4 = 8. 2/3 = 8/12. -->

---

<span style="background:#dcfce7; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #22c55e; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">✅ Check</span>

## Why it works

**Why does 2/4 show the same amount as 1/2?**

- It only works for 1/2
- Each half is cut into 2 parts, so there are twice as many parts and twice as many shaded
- 4 is bigger than 2, so it is a bigger fraction
- You add 2 to the top and the bottom

* Answer: Each half is cut into 2 parts, so there are twice as many parts and twice as many shaded

<!-- Speaker note: Cutting every part into 2 gives twice as many parts, and twice as many shaded. The amount does not change. -->

## Not the same

**Which is NOT the same amount as 3/4?**

- 6/8
- 9/12
- 12/16
- 4/5

* Answer: 4/5

<!-- Speaker note: 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12 = 12/16. 4/5 is a different amount. -->

## Tenths

**3/5 is the same as ?/10. What is the missing number?**

* Answer: 6

## Exit ticket

**Write one fraction that is the same amount as 3/4 and say how you got it.**

* 6/8: I doubled the top and the bottom.
