# Adding fractions with the same parts

Before Year 7 maths · Parts of a whole

<!-- Speaker note: Students add fractions with the same bottom by adding the tops, and drop the idea that the bottoms are added too. About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what the bottom number tells me.
- I can add fractions with the same bottom.
- I can say what to do when the sum is more than 1.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: the bottom is the size of the part, so it stays the same when you add. The common mistake is adding both tops and bottoms (1/5 + 2/5 = 3/10). The Think first question meets it. The board is the fraction adding board from the Year 7 lesson *Adding fractions*. Here both bottoms are 5 and the cut is fixed at 5, so no common bottom is needed yet. Say "two fifths plus one fifth" aloud, as "two apples plus one apple". Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to draw 2/5 + 1/5 on a strip. Teacher: Try: ask them to make a sum past 1 and say it as a mixed numeral. Teacher: Apply: look for "still quarters". -->

---

## Why it matters

> You eat 2 fifths of a block of chocolate and your friend eats 1 fifth. How much is gone?

<!-- Speaker note: Fractions with the same bottom number are made of the same size of part. To add them, count the parts. The bottom number says the size of the part, so it stays the same. -->

## Eighths

**What is 1/8 + 3/8?**

- 3/16
- 4/16
- 3/8
- 4/8

* Answer: 4/8

<!-- Speaker note: 1 eighth + 3 eighths = 4 eighths. -->

## Fifths past one

**2/5 + 4/5 = ?/5. What is the missing number?**

* Answer: 6

<!-- Speaker note: 2 fifths + 4 fifths = 6 fifths, which is 1 1/5. -->

## Why the bottom stays

**Why does the bottom stay the same when you add?**

- You add the bottoms but leave one out
- The bottom is not important
- It only stays the same when the answer is under 1
- The bottom is the size of the parts, and you are still counting the same size of part

* Answer: The bottom is the size of the parts, and you are still counting the same size of part

<!-- Speaker note: The bottom names the part. 2 fifths + 1 fifth is 3 fifths, as 2 apples + 1 apple is 3 apples. -->

## Exit ticket

**What is 3/8 + 4/8, and why does the bottom stay 8?**

* 7/8, because we are still counting eighths.
