# Find the missing number

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 8 + 4 = □ + 5. What goes in the box? Many people say 12, but that is not it.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read the equals sign as both sides being worth the same, and find missing numbers by balancing, not by reading it as "the answer comes next".

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what the equals sign means. I can find a missing number so both sides are equal. I can check my answer.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea

The **equals sign** means both sides are worth the same. Work out the side with no missing number, then find the number that makes the other side match. You can check by putting it back in.

:::keep-together

### What = means

**What does the equals sign mean?**

☐ The answer comes next

☐ Add the numbers

☐ Both sides are worth the same

☐ The problem is finished

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Both sides are worth the same

The sides are the same amount, like a balanced scale.

:::
