# Find the missing number

Before Year 7 maths · Unknowns

<!-- Speaker note: Students read the equals sign as both sides being worth the same, and find missing numbers by balancing, not by reading it as "the answer comes next". About 17 minutes (Think first 3 min · Try 4 min · The idea 5 min · Check 3 min · Apply 2 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what the equals sign means.
- I can find a missing number so both sides are equal.
- I can check my answer.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: = means both sides are worth the same. The common mistake is reading it as "the answer comes next", so 8 + 4 = □ + 5 gives 12. The Think first question meets it. The board is the balance from the Year 7 lessons *Equations as balance* and *The balance method*. Here it only needs one side filled in. This lesson may overlap with Year 7 Algebra. Use it only for students who need it. Not yet tried with a class. Teacher: Think first: ask students to say what = means in their own words. Teacher: Try: ask which side is worked out first. Teacher: Apply: look for "worth the same". -->

---

## Why it matters

> 8 + 4 = □ + 5. What goes in the box? Many people say 12, but that is not it.

<!-- Speaker note: The equals sign (=) says that two things are **worth the same**, like a balanced scale. A missing number is the one that makes the scale balance. -->

* 7. The left side is 8 + 4 = 12, so the right side must be 12 as well. 7 + 5 = 12.

## What = means

**What does the equals sign mean?**

- The answer comes next
- Add the numbers
- Both sides are worth the same
- The problem is finished

* Answer: Both sides are worth the same

<!-- Speaker note: The sides are the same amount, like a balanced scale. -->

## Both sides

**6 + □ = 10 + 3. What goes in the box?**

* Answer: 7

<!-- Speaker note: The right side is 13, so 6 + □ = 13 and □ = 7. -->
