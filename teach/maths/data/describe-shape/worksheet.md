# Shape: symmetric or skewed

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Waiting times at a clinic: lots of short waits, a few very long ones. What shape is that?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A distribution's shape says where the values pile up and where the long tail runs. A tail to the right is skewed right and pulls the mean above the median.

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe a distribution as symmetric or skewed. I can name the direction by the tail. I can link the skew to the mean and the median.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Name the shape


:::keep-together

### Skewed left

1. $\text{most waits } 5\text{--}10,\ \text{a few } 22, 30$ — Bulk on the left, tail to the right.
2. $\text{skewed right: mean} > \text{median}$ — The tail pulls the mean up.
3. $\text{symmetric: mean} \approx \text{median}$ — Balanced both sides.

**The tail of a distribution is on the left, towards the small values. What is it called?**

☐ Symmetric

☐ Skewed right

☐ Skewed left

☐ Uniform

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Skewed left

The long tail is on the left, so it is skewed left.

:::


:::keep-together

### Mean and median

**A distribution is skewed left. How do the mean and median compare?**

☐ They are equal

☐ The mean is below the median

☐ The mean is above the median

☐ They can't be compared

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The mean is below the median

The low tail pulls the mean down below the median.

:::


:::keep-together

## Shape and average


:::keep-together

### Mean above median

**A set has mean 52 and median 48. What shape is most likely?**

☐ Symmetric

☐ Skewed right

☐ Skewed left

☐ Uniform

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Skewed right

Mean above median means a tail to the right.

:::

**Answer:** Skewed right
