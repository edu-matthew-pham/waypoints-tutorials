# Late and rainy: estimating "and" and "or"

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Is the bus more often late on rainy days, and how often is it both?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A table of what happened on 40 days gives estimates for "late and rainy", "late or rainy" and "late on a dry day", and lets you predict how many days to expect.

**Students should be able to say:** I can read an "and" and an "or" probability from a two-way table of records. I can use it to predict. I can say it is only an estimate.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## And, or and not


:::keep-together

### Count the days

1. $\text{late and rainy} = 10$ — One cell of the table.
2. $\text{late or rainy} = 14 + 14 - 10 = 18$ — Add, then take off the overlap once.
3. $\tfrac{18}{40} = 0.45$ — Relative frequency of "late or rainy".

**How many days were late or rainy (or both)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 18

10 + 4 + 4 = 18 days (all except the 22 dry and on-time days).

:::


:::keep-together

### Make it a probability

**What is the relative frequency of late or rainy (a decimal)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.45

18 ÷ 40 = 0.45.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Kim estimates the chance a day is late or rainy. Click the line where she goes wrong.**

```
 1  Late on 14 of the 40 days, and rainy on 14 of the 40 days.
 2  So late or rainy is 14 + 14 = 28 days out of 40.
 3  That is 28/40 = 0.7.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the 10 days that were both are in the late total and in the rainy total. Adding 14 + 14 counts them twice. Late or rainy = 14 + 14 − 10 = 18 days.
