# The right graph for the data

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Favourite fruit, daily temperature, heights of students: do they all want the same graph?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Each kind of data has a display that suits it: bars for categories, pies for parts of a whole, lines for change over time, histograms for measurements and scatter plots for two numbers.

**Students should be able to say:** I can match a display to the kind of data. I can say why a wrong display misleads. I can calculate pie chart angles.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Match the display


:::keep-together

### Categories

1. $\text{categories} \to \text{bar chart}$ — Names with counts.
2. $\text{parts of a whole} \to \text{pie or bar}$ — Shares that add to 100%.
3. $\text{change over time} \to \text{line};\ \text{measurements} \to \text{histogram}$ — Order and shape.

**Favourite fruit of 30 students (apple, banana, orange). Which display suits categories?**

☐ A bar chart

☐ A line graph

☐ A scatter plot

☐ A histogram of the heights

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A bar chart

A bar chart, with a separate bar for each category.

:::


:::keep-together

### Measurements

**The heights (in cm) of 120 students. Which display suits measurements spread over a range?**

☐ A histogram

☐ A line graph over time

☐ A pie chart

☐ A bar chart of names

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A histogram

A histogram groups the measurements into equal-width intervals and shows the shape.

:::


:::keep-together
