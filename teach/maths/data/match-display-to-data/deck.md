# The right graph for the data

Data and chance · Year 9: statistics

<!-- Speaker note: Each kind of data has a display that suits it: bars for categories, pies for parts of a whole, lines for change over time, histograms for measurements and scatter plots for two numbers. About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

## Predict

**Which display best shows what share of canteen sales each food type makes up?**

- A scatter plot
- A line graph
- A pie chart or a bar chart

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: A pie chart (or bar chart). The parts add up to a whole, so a pie shows the share well; bars let you compare sizes. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Categories

$$\text{categories} \to \text{bar chart}$$

1) $\text{parts of a whole} \to \text{pie or bar}$ — Shares that add to 100%.
2) $\text{change over time} \to \text{line};\ \text{measurements} \to \text{histogram}$ — Order and shape.

**Favourite fruit of 30 students (apple, banana, orange). Which display suits categories?**

- A bar chart

- A scatter plot

## Measurements

**The heights (in cm) of 120 students. Which display suits measurements spread over a range?**

- A histogram
- A line graph over time
- A pie chart
- A bar chart of names

* Answer: A histogram

<!-- Speaker note: A histogram groups the measurements into equal-width intervals and shows the shape. -->

## Find the mistake

**Sia graphs the favourite fruits. Click the line where she goes wrong.**

```
 1  The data are favourite fruits: apple, banana, orange.
 2  I will join the three points with a line graph.
 3  The line shows how popular fruit changes.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: fruits are categories with no order, so a line joining them suggests a trend that is not there. Use a bar chart. -->

## Pie angle

**Out of 60 students, 15 walk. What angle would the walking slice of a pie chart have (degrees)?**

* Answer: 90

<!-- Speaker note: 15 ÷ 60 × 360 = 90. -->

## Review

**Review: set A has range 6 and set B has range 15. How much wider is B?**

* Answer: 9
