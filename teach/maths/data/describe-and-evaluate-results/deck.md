## Why it matters

> The simulation says she scores all five about a third of the time. In real shootouts she did it 3 times in 10. Is the simulation wrong?

**The five groups' relative frequencies ranged from 29/50 = 0.58 to 35/50 = 0.70. What is the range (a decimal)?**

## Reasons for a gap

**Which is NOT a good reason for a real season to differ from the simulation?**

- Pressure may change how well the striker plays
- The simulation is always wrong
- A real season is a short run of trials, so chance varies
- The scoring rate used might be a bit off

* Answer: The simulation is always wrong

<!-- Speaker note: A simulation is not always wrong: it is a model. Differences come from chance, from what the model leaves out and from the numbers fed into it. -->

## Find the mistake

**Ivy compares one group with the theory. Click the line where she goes wrong.**

```
 1  The theory says P(two goals) = 0.64.
 2  Group 1 got 29 two-goal tries out of 50, so 0.58.
 3  That is not 0.64, so the theory is wrong.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: 29/50 = 0.58 is within the normal spread of 50 trials. One group can't show the model is wrong. Pool the groups: 160/250 = 0.64. -->

## Pool the groups

**Four groups got 18, 22, 20 and 24 successes in 40 trials each. What is the pooled relative frequency (a decimal)? **

* Answer: 0.525

<!-- Speaker note: 84 ÷ 160 = 0.525. -->

## The gap

**The counted probability is 0.5. The pooled result is 0.525. By how much do they differ?**

* Answer: 0.025

<!-- Speaker note: 0.525 − 0.5 = 0.025. -->

- **Reviews and ratings.** A product with 3 reviews can look perfect or terrible by chance; 3000 reviews settle down.
