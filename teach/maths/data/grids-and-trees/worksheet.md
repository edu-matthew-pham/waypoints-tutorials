## List every pair


:::keep-together

### Count the pairs

Here is the routine.

1. $\text{Spinner with equal red and blue parts};\ \text{then a coin}$ — Two events, one after the other.
2. $\text{red} \to \text{H, T};\ \text{blue} \to \text{H, T}$ — Split each branch into every outcome of the second.
3. $2 \times 2 = 4 \text{ outcomes}$ — Count the leaves of the tree.
4. $P(\text{blue, H}) = \tfrac{1}{4}$ — One leaf out of four.

**A spinner has 3 colours and a dice has 6 faces. How many outcomes when you spin, then roll?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 18

3 × 6 = 18 pairs.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Max finds the chance of one head and one tail with two coins. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  Max tosses two coins and lists what can happen.
 2  The equally likely outcomes are: HH, TT, and one of each.
 3  That is 3 outcomes, and one of them is "one of each".
 4  So P(one head and one tail) = 1/3.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: one of each can happen two ways, HT and TH. The four equally likely outcomes are HH, HT, TH, TT, so P(one of each) = 2/4 = 1/2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Three coins

**You toss three coins. Which is the best way to list the outcomes?**

☐ A tree diagram

☐ A list of the totals

☐ A Venn diagram

☐ A grid

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A tree diagram

A tree can keep branching: a column of branches for each event, 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 outcomes.
