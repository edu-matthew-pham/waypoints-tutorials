> **Key question:** The forecast says a 30% chance of rain. What is the chance it stays dry? You know straight away, but why does it work?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students choose events on a dice and two dice, show their complements, and use P(not A) = 1 − P(A), including for 'at least one' (AC9M8P01, nodes 1 and 2).

## Using the complement


:::keep-together

### Not late

Here is the routine.

1. $P(\text{red}) = \tfrac{3}{8}$ — A bag has 3 red counters out of 8.
2. $P(\text{not red}) = 1 - \tfrac{3}{8}$ — Not red is every other counter.
3. $= \tfrac{5}{8}$ — Check: 5 counters are not red.

**The chance a bus is late is 0.15. What is the chance it is not late?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.85

P(not late) = 1 − 0.15 = 0.85.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Ben works with two events on a dice. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ben rolls a dice. Event A is "roll a 1" and event B is "roll a 6".
 2  A and B cannot both happen on the same roll.
 3  So A and B are complementary events.
 4  So P(B) = 1 − P(A) = 1 − 1/6 = 5/6.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: A and B can't happen together, but they don't cover every outcome (2, 3, 4 and 5 are in neither). Complementary events must cover everything. P(B) = 1/6.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which pair

**On one roll of a dice, which pair of events is complementary?**

☐ Roll a 6; roll a 5 or a 6

☐ Roll more than 3; roll less than 3

☐ Roll a 1; roll a 6

☐ Roll an even number; roll an odd number

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Roll an even number; roll an odd number

Every number is even or odd, never both: together they cover every outcome once.

## Complements


:::keep-together

### Win or not

**P(a team wins) = 0.45. A draw is not possible. What is P(the team does not win)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.55

1 − 0.45 = 0.55.

:::


:::keep-together

### Complement in words

**On a dice, what is the complement of 'roll at least 4'?**

☐ Roll a 4

☐ Roll more than 4

☐ Roll less than 4 or more than 4

☐ Roll 3 or less

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Roll 3 or less

At least 4 is 4, 5, 6, so the complement is 1, 2, 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### Cards

**A card is drawn from a pack. A is 'a heart' and B is 'a spade'. Are A and B complementary?**

☐ Yes: they are both suits

☐ Yes: they can't both happen

☐ No: they can both happen

☐ No: some cards are in neither

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No: some cards are in neither

They can't both happen, but clubs and diamonds are in neither. Complementary events cover every outcome: P(not a heart) = 3/4, not P(spade).

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: expected count

**Review: a dice is rolled 120 times. How many sixes do you expect?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20

1/6 × 120 = 20.
