## Redraw it honestly

Now try one on paper: the canteen chart, with sales of $480 in week 1 and $520 in week 8 and the axis starting at $470. Work out how much sales really grew, as a percentage.

$$\text{Axis starts at } 205$$

1) $212 \text{ and } 224$ — The real values are the labels.
2) $224 - 212 = 12$ — The real change in members.
3) $12 \div 212 \approx 0.057$ — As a fraction of the starting members.
4) $\approx 5.7\%$ — The honest growth is under 6%.

## Redraw it honestly

**A canteen chart shows weekly sales of $480 in week 1 and $520 in week 8 on bars whose axis starts at $470. By what percentage did sales really grow? (1 decimal place) (Made up for the task.)**

* Answer: $520 - 480 = 40$ (the real difference in dollars); $40 \div 480 \times 100 = 8.3$ (divide by the starting value, then make it a percentage)

<!-- Speaker note: 40 ÷ 480 = 0.0833, so sales grew about 8.3%. The axis starts at $470, so the bars look 10 and 50 tall: five times as tall for a real growth of under a tenth. -->

## Find the mistake

**Kai reads the club's graph. Click the line where the working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The 2022 point is 7 units above the bottom of the axis, and the 2026 point is 19 units above it.
 2  19 ÷ 7 is about 2.7.
 3  So 2026 has about 2.7 times as many members as 2022.
 4  The newsletter's "soaring" is fair.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the heights are measured from 205, not from 0, so 19 ÷ 7 compares how far above 205 the points are. The members are 224 and 212, a ratio of about 1.06. -->

## Icons that grow

**A pictograph shows twice as many cars sold this year by drawing the car icon twice as wide and twice as tall. How many times as big, by area, does the icon look?**

* Answer: 4

<!-- Speaker note: Twice as wide and twice as tall is 2 × 2 = 4 times the area. The eye reads the whole picture, so the icon looks four times as big for twice the cars. -->

## Exit ticket

**A bar chart's axis starts at 50. The bars show 80 and 60. How many times as tall does the first bar look as the second?**

* (80 − 50) ÷ (60 − 50) = 3 times, though 80 is only about 1.3 times 60.
