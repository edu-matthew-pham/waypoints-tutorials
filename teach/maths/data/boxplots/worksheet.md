**In one sentence:** Students draw and read a boxplot from the five numbers: the box is the middle half, the bar is the median, the whiskers reach the furthest values inside the 1.5 × IQR fences, and values beyond them are outliers (AC9M10ST02, node 2).

## Reading and drawing a boxplot


:::keep-together

### Reading the fences

1. $0, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 6, 8, 15$ — Route 1's eleven days, in order.
2. $\text{lowest } 0,\ Q_1 = 2,\ \text{median } 4,\ Q_3 = 6,\ \text{highest } 15$ — The five numbers.
3. $6 - 2 = 4$ — The box's length, the interquartile range.
4. $6 + 1.5 \times 4 = 12$ — The upper fence: values beyond it are outliers.
5. $15 > 12$ — So 15 is an outlier. It is drawn as a cross.
6. $\text{upper whisker to } 8$ — The whisker stops at the highest value inside the fence.

Now try one on paper: a bus's lateness has five numbers 1, 3, 4, 8 and 20 minutes. Work out the upper fence and decide whether the 20 is an outlier.

**A bus's lateness has five numbers 1, 3, 4, 8 and 20 (minutes, made up). What is the upper fence, and is 20 an outlier?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $8 - 3 = 5$ (the interquartile range, upper quartile take away lower quartile); $1.5 \times 5 = 7.5$ (the distance allowed beyond the box); $8 + 7.5 = 15.5$ (the upper fence); $20 &gt; 15.5$, so 20 is an outlier (it is beyond the fence)

IQR = 8 − 3 = 5, and 1.5 × 5 = 7.5, so the upper fence is 8 + 7.5 = 15.5. The 20 is beyond it, so it is an outlier.

:::


:::keep-together

### Box ends

**A boxplot's five numbers are 2, 5, 8, 13 and 21. Which two numbers are the ends of the box?**

☐ 5 and 13

☐ 2 and 21

☐ 5 and 8

☐ 8 and 13

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5 and 13

The box runs from the lower quartile (5) to the upper quartile (13), with the median bar at 8. The whiskers reach to 2 and 21 if neither is an outlier.

### Find the mistake

**Dev compares two bus routes' boxplots. Click the line where the reading goes wrong.**

```
 1  Route 1's box runs from 2 to 6 minutes late.
 2  Route 2's box runs from 4 to 6 minutes late, so it is narrower.
 3  A narrower box means fewer buses ran on Route 2.
 4  So Route 1 is the busier route.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the width of a box is how far apart the quartiles are, so it shows the spread of the middle half. Each box holds half of that route's days, whatever its width.

### The median bar

**The median bar in a boxplot sits close to the left end of the box (the quartiles are 3 and 12 and the median is 5). What does this tell you?**

☐ The data has no outliers

☐ The mean equals the median

☐ The values between 3 and 5 are bunched closer together than the values between 5 and 12

☐ More days are on the left than on the right

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The values between 3 and 5 are bunched closer together than the values between 5 and 12

A quarter of the days lie on each side of the median. If the median is near the left end, that quarter is squeezed into a small range, while the other quarter is spread out more.

## Reading and fences


:::keep-together

### Box length

**A boxplot's five numbers are 4, 9, 13, 20 and 31. What is the interquartile range?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 11

The quartiles are 9 and 20, so the IQR is 20 − 9 = 11.

:::


:::keep-together

### Upper fence

**For those five numbers (4, 9, 13, 20, 31), what is the upper fence?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 36.5

20 + 1.5 × 11 = 20 + 16.5 = 36.5. The highest value, 31, is inside it, so it isn't an outlier.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: nine values in order are 3, 5, 8, 10, 12, 14, 17, 19 and 25. What is the lower quartile?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6.5

The median is 12. The lower half is 3, 5, 8, 10 and its median is (5 + 8) ÷ 2 = 6.5.
