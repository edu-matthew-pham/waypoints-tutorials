## Change the size


**Bigger samples.** Here is how to describe sampling variation.

1. $\text{10 samples of } 20:\ 0.40 \text{ to } 0.75$ — The spread of results.
2. $\text{10 samples of } 100:\ 0.52 \text{ to } 0.63$ — Bigger samples: a narrower spread.
3. $\text{Both centred near } 0.57$ — Fair samples scatter around the population's value.
4. $\text{Bigger sample} \Rightarrow \text{more reliable estimate}$ — The pattern.

## Variation


:::keep-together

### Small samples

**Two classes each take a random sample of 10 students from the same school. One finds 0.3 own a dog; the other finds 0.6. What is the best explanation?**

☐ Sampling variation: samples of 10 vary a lot

☐ The second class made a mistake

☐ The first class made a mistake

☐ Dogs changed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sampling variation: samples of 10 vary a lot

Small samples vary a lot. Larger samples would agree more closely.

:::


:::keep-together

### Bigger samples

**Ten samples of 25 give means from 8.4 to 9.2 hours. Ten samples of 100 are taken. What will most likely happen to the spread of their means?**

☐ It stays the same

☐ It disappears completely

☐ It gets narrower

☐ It gets wider

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It gets narrower

Bigger samples give means closer together.

:::


:::keep-together

### A census

**A census of the whole school is taken twice on the same day. How do the two results compare?**

☐ They are very different

☐ You can't take a census twice

☐ They vary like samples

☐ They are the same: a census has no sampling variation

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They are the same: a census has no sampling variation

Sampling variation comes from who happens to be in a sample. A census includes everyone.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: a claim

**Review: which is the best claim from a random sample of 40 students where the median sleep was 9 hours?**

☐ Students should sleep 9 hours

☐ The school's median is exactly 9 hours

☐ Every student sleeps 9 hours

☐ Students at the school sleep about 9 hours a night

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Students at the school sleep about 9 hours a night

A sample gives an estimate: 'about'.
