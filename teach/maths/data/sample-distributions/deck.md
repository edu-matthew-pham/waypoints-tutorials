# Describing a sample

Data and chance · Samples

<!-- Speaker note: Students take samples of a simulated school, describe each sample's shape, centre and spread, compare two samples, and report an estimate with its method and size (AC9M8ST02, node 2). ## Today

**We are learning to** display a sample's data, then report its shape, centre and spread, and what it suggests about the population: an estimate, with the sampling method and size stated.

- I can describe a sample's shape, centre and spread.
- I can compare two samples by their centres and spreads.
- I can write a report that gives an estimate for the population and says how the sample was chosen.

## Why it matters

> A news story says 'Teens sleep 8 hours a night.' Is that every teen, most teens, or a sample's average? A good report says which, and how the sample was chosen.

<!-- Speaker note: A **report** on a sample describes its **distribution**, then says what it suggests about the population: - **shape**: symmetric (balanced about the middle), skewed (a long tail to one side), any clusters, gaps or outliers - **centre**: the mean and median - **spread**: the range (highest − lowest) - **what it suggests**: an estimate for the population, with how the sample was chosen and its size Data you collect yourself is **primary** data; data someone else collected (the ABS, a website, a study) is **secondary** data. -->

## Median

Here is the routine for a report.

$$\text{Random sample of } 20 \text{ Year 8s: minutes reading per day}$$

1) $\text{Shape: most } 10 \text{ to } 30,\ \text{one outlier at } 120$ — Describe the shape.
2) $\text{Median } 20,\ \text{mean } 25,\ \text{range } 120 - 5 = 115$ — Centre and spread (the outlier pulls the mean up).
3) $\text{Year 8s read about } 20 \text{ minutes a day}$ — An estimate for the population, with its limits.

## Find the mistake

**Leo reports on his sample. Click the line where his report goes wrong.**

```
 1  Leo takes a random sample of 30 students and finds their mean sleep is 8.9 hours.
 2  He reports: "The mean sleep of all 200 students is exactly 8.9 hours."
 3  He adds that the sample was chosen at random from the school roll.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: a sample's mean estimates the population's; it isn't exactly the same. He should write 'about 8.9 hours, from a random sample of 30'. -->

## Shape

**A sample's dot plot has most values between 2 and 5, and a long tail of a few values up to 15. How would you describe its shape?**

- Symmetric
- Evenly spread
- Two clusters
- Skewed: a long tail to the high side

* Answer: Skewed: a long tail to the high side

<!-- Speaker note: A tail stretching to the high values is skewed to the right (positively skewed); the mean is pulled above the median. -->

## A claim about the school

**From a random sample of 50 students, 30 walk to school. What is the best claim about the school?**

- Nobody can say anything
- Exactly 60% walk
- 30 students walk
- About 60% of the school's students walk to school

* Answer: About 60% of the school's students walk to school

<!-- Speaker note: 30/50 = 60% is an estimate for the school: 'about 60%'. -->

## Review: systematic

**Review: a club has 60 members listed alphabetically. Which gives a systematic sample of 10?**

- Every 6th member from a random start
- 10 chosen from a hat
- The first 10 on the list
- 10 from each age group

* Answer: Every 6th member from a random start

<!-- Speaker note: 60 ÷ 10 = 6: every 6th from a random start. -->
