## From data to report


:::keep-together

### Median

Here is the routine for a report.

1. $\text{Random sample of } 20 \text{ Year 8s: minutes reading per day}$ — Say the sample: method and size.
2. $\text{Shape: most } 10 \text{ to } 30,\ \text{one outlier at } 120$ — Describe the shape.
3. $\text{Median } 20,\ \text{mean } 25,\ \text{range } 120 - 5 = 115$ — Centre and spread (the outlier pulls the mean up).
4. $\text{Year 8s read about } 20 \text{ minutes a day}$ — An estimate for the population, with its limits.

**A sample of 7 students' sleep: 8.0, 9.5, 10.0, 7.5, 9.0, 8.5, 9.0 hours. What is the median, in hours?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 9

In order, the 4th of 7 is 9.0 hours.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Leo reports on his sample. Click the line where his report goes wrong.**

```
 1  Leo takes a random sample of 30 students and finds their mean sleep is 8.9 hours.
 2  He reports: "The mean sleep of all 200 students is exactly 8.9 hours."
 3  He adds that the sample was chosen at random from the school roll.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: a sample's mean estimates the population's; it isn't exactly the same. He should write 'about 8.9 hours, from a random sample of 30'.

:::

## Describing samples


:::keep-together

### Mean

**A sample's values are 4, 6, 6, 7, 9, 10, 14. What is the mean?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

Total 56 ÷ 7 = 8.

:::


:::keep-together

### Shape

**A sample's dot plot has most values between 2 and 5, and a long tail of a few values up to 15. How would you describe its shape?**

☐ Symmetric

☐ Evenly spread

☐ Two clusters

☐ Skewed: a long tail to the high side

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Skewed: a long tail to the high side

A tail stretching to the high values is skewed to the right (positively skewed); the mean is pulled above the median.

:::


:::keep-together

### A claim about the school

**From a random sample of 50 students, 30 walk to school. What is the best claim about the school?**

☐ Nobody can say anything

☐ Exactly 60% walk

☐ 30 students walk

☐ About 60% of the school's students walk to school

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** About 60% of the school's students walk to school

30/50 = 60% is an estimate for the school: 'about 60%'.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: systematic

**Review: a club has 60 members listed alphabetically. Which gives a systematic sample of 10?**

☐ Every 6th member from a random start

☐ 10 chosen from a hat

☐ The first 10 on the list

☐ 10 from each age group

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Every 6th member from a random start

60 ÷ 10 = 6: every 6th from a random start.

:::
