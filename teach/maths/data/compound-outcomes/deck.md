# Listing every outcome of two choices

Data and chance · Year 9: chance

<!-- Speaker note: A meal deal is a main and a drink. Listing the deals in an order (every main with every drink) shows that two-step outcomes multiply, and stops you missing one. About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can list every outcome of two choices without missing one.
- I can count them by multiplying.
- I can explain why two choices multiply rather than add.

## What happened?

## Count the pairs

$$\text{4 mains, 3 drinks}$$

1) $\text{burger} \to \text{water, juice, milk}$ — Take one main and pair it with every drink.
2) $4 \times 3 = 12$ — Every main has 3 deals, so multiply.

## Find the mistake

**Sam counts the deals for 4 mains and 3 drinks. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  There are 4 mains and 3 drinks.
 2  A deal is a main and a drink, so 4 + 3 = 7 deals.
 3  So there are 7 different meal deals.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: each main goes with each drink, so the deals are 4 × 3 = 12, not 4 + 3. -->

## List without missing any

**What is the safest way to list every meal deal?**

- List the drinks only
- Count the mains, then count the drinks and add
- Write deals as they come to mind
- Take one main at a time and pair it with each drink

* Answer: Take one main at a time and pair it with each drink

## Make: back to the canteen

Check by listing: a row of 3 for each main. Which row did you cross out?

**Also used in**

- **Passwords and PINs.** A 4-digit PIN is four choices of 10 digits in a row: 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10 000 PINs.
- **Computing.** Testing every setting of a program means listing every combination, then asking if you can test fewer.
- **Next lesson.** Draws where something is not put back, so the choices change.
