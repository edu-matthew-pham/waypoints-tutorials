## Today

- I can plot a dot from two measurements, putting the first across and the second up.
- I can read the two numbers of a dot from a scatterplot.
- I can say whether a dot is above, on or below the line where the two measurements are equal.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. P snaps to whole numbers; the arrow buttons under the graph move it a step at a time. ## Why it matters

> Is your arm span the same as your height? Measure a whole class and you get a lot of numbers. A picture can show them all at once.

## Five students

Here is how to plot one dot, then compare it with the dashed line.

Now try five students on paper first, with the worked example beside you.

$$\text{arm span } 172, \text{ height } 169$$

1) $\text{across to } 172$ — Start at the corner and move right to 172 on the arm span axis.
2) $\text{up to } 169$ — From there move up to 169 on the height axis and mark the dot.
3) $169 < 172$ — On the dashed line, height equals arm span. Here the height is smaller, so the dot is below the line.

## Five students

**Five students (made up) have these (arm span, height) pairs in cm: $(150, 155)$, $(160, 158)$, $(170, 170)$, $(175, 180)$, $(182, 179)$. Plot them on paper with the dashed line height = arm span. How many of the five dots are above the line?**

* Answer: $(150, 155)$: $155 &gt; 150$ (height is bigger than arm span, so the dot is above the line); $(175, 180)$: $180 &gt; 175$ (height is bigger again, so this dot is above too); $(160, 158)$ and $(182, 179)$: height is smaller (these two dots are below the line); $(170, 170)$: the numbers are equal (this dot is on the line, so it is not above it); $2$ dots are above the line (count the dots above)

## Find the mistake

**Mia plots Tom's dot. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Tom has an arm span of 150 cm and a height of 175 cm.
 2  The axes are arm span across the bottom and height up the side.
 3  I start at the corner and go across to 175, because 175 is the bigger number.
 4  Then I go up to 150 and put my dot there.
 5  That dot is below the dashed line, so Tom's arm span is longer than his height.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: she put the bigger number across because it is bigger. The first measurement named, arm span (150), goes across, and the second, height (175), goes up. That puts Tom above the line, not below it. -->

## Exit ticket

**A student has an arm span of 168 cm and a height of 171 cm. Which two numbers do you use to place the dot, and is it above or below the line height = arm span?**
