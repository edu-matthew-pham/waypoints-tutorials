# Scatterplots

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Is your arm span the same as your height? Measure a whole class and you get a lot of numbers. A picture can show them all at once.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students plot a dot from two measurements (first across, second up), read a dot's coordinates, and compare a dot with the line height = arm span. The context is a class's arm span and height with made-up numbers (AC9M10ST03, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can plot a dot from two measurements, putting the first across and the second up. I can read the two numbers of a dot from a scatterplot. I can say whether a dot is above, on or below the line where the two measurements are equal.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Reading and plotting


:::keep-together

### Five students

Here is how to plot one dot, then compare it with the dashed line.

1. $\text{arm span } 172, \text{ height } 169$ — One student gives two numbers. The first, arm span, goes across.
2. $\text{across to } 172$ — Start at the corner and move right to 172 on the arm span axis.
3. $\text{up to } 169$ — From there move up to 169 on the height axis and mark the dot.
4. $169 < 172$ — On the dashed line, height equals arm span. Here the height is smaller, so the dot is below the line.

Now try five students on paper first, with the worked example beside you.

**Five students (made up) have these (arm span, height) pairs in cm: $(150, 155)$, $(160, 158)$, $(170, 170)$, $(175, 180)$, $(182, 179)$. Plot them on paper with the dashed line height = arm span. How many of the five dots are above the line?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** $(150, 155)$: $155 &gt; 150$ (height is bigger than arm span, so the dot is above the line); $(175, 180)$: $180 &gt; 175$ (height is bigger again, so this dot is above too); $(160, 158)$ and $(182, 179)$: height is smaller (these two dots are below the line); $(170, 170)$: the numbers are equal (this dot is on the line, so it is not above it); $2$ dots are above the line (count the dots above)

(150, 155) and (175, 180) are above, (160, 158) and (182, 179) are below, and (170, 170) is on the line. So 2 are above.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia plots Tom's dot. Click the line where her reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  Tom has an arm span of 150 cm and a height of 175 cm.
 2  The axes are arm span across the bottom and height up the side.
 3  I start at the corner and go across to 175, because 175 is the bigger number.
 4  Then I go up to 150 and put my dot there.
 5  That dot is below the dashed line, so Tom's arm span is longer than his height.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: she put the bigger number across because it is bigger. The first measurement named, arm span (150), goes across, and the second, height (175), goes up. That puts Tom above the line, not below it.

### Reading a height

**Find the student with arm span $170$ cm. What is their height, in cm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 168

The dot at 170 across is at 168 up.

### Below or above

**A student has arm span $158$ cm and height $156$ cm. Which is true of their dot?**

☐ Above the dashed line, as 158 is bigger

☐ On the dashed line

☐ Below the dashed line, as the height is less than the arm span

☐ Further right than the dot at arm span 160

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Below the dashed line, as the height is less than the arm span

Height 156 is less than arm span 158, so the dot is below the line, and it sits left of the 160 dot.

:::

### Review

**Review: the heights of four students are $150$, $157$, $156$ and $163$ cm. What is their mean height, in cm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 156.5

150 + 157 + 156 + 163 = 626 and 626 ÷ 4 = 156.5.
