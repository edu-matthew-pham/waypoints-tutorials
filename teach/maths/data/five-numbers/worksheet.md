> **Key question:** Property reports quote the middle of the market, not the very top. How do you find it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find the five-number summary (lowest, lower quartile, median, upper quartile, highest) by the median-of-halves method, say what each number means, and see why reports quote the middle half rather than the extremes (AC9M10ST02, node 1).

## Finding the five numbers


:::keep-together

### Five numbers

1. $3, 4, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 21$ — Ten waiting times in order (made up).
2. $\text{lowest } 3,\ \text{highest } 21$ — The two ends.
3. $\tfrac{7 + 9}{2} = 8$ — Ten values: the median is halfway between the 5th and 6th.
4. $Q_1 = 4$ — The lower half is 3, 4, 4, 6, 7. Its median is 4.
5. $Q_3 = 12$ — The upper half is 9, 10, 12, 13, 21. Its median is 12.
6. $12 - 4 = 8$ — The interquartile range is the length of the middle half.

Now try one on paper: nine waiting times in order, 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 14, 15 and 20. With nine values the median is the fifth one, and it is left out when you split into halves.

**Nine students' waiting times at the canteen, in minutes and in order, are 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 14, 15 and 20 (made up). What is the interquartile range?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** median $= 9$ (the 5th of 9) (the middle value splits the data in two); lower half $2, 5, 6, 8$ gives $Q_1 = 5.5$; upper half $11, 14, 15, 20$ gives $Q_3 = 14.5$ (the middle value is left out of both halves); $14.5 - 5.5 = 9$ (the interquartile range is $Q_3 - Q_1$)

Median 9; lower half 2, 5, 6, 8 has median (5 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5.5; upper half 11, 14, 15, 20 has median (14 + 15) ÷ 2 = 14.5. The IQR is 14.5 − 5.5 = 9.

:::


:::keep-together

### Lower quartile

**Eight values in order are 4, 6, 9, 11, 14, 18, 21 and 25. What is the lower quartile?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 7.5

Eight values split into two halves of four. The lower half is 4, 6, 9, 11 and its median is (6 + 9) ÷ 2 = 7.5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Mia finds the lower quartile of nine waiting times. Click the line where the working goes wrong.**

```
 1  The nine times in order are 3, 4, 6, 7, 12, 13, 15, 16, 20, and the median is the fifth one, 12.
 2  The lowest time is 3.
 3  The lower quartile is halfway between the lowest time and the median.
 4  (3 + 12) ÷ 2 = 7.5, so the lower quartile is 7.5.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the lower quartile is the median of the lower half of the values, not the point halfway between the lowest value and the median. The lower half is 3, 4, 6, 7 and its median is 5.

:::


:::keep-together

### What a quartile says

**The suburb's five numbers are 480, 510, 560, 640 and 1450 (thousands of dollars). What does the 510 tell you?**

☐ Half of the sales were for $510 000 or less

☐ The cheapest sale was $510 000

☐ The average sale was $510 000

☐ About a quarter of the sales were for $510 000 or less

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** About a quarter of the sales were for $510 000 or less

The lower quartile has a quarter of the data at or below it, and the upper quartile has a quarter above it. Between them lies the middle half.

## Quartiles and the spread of the middle half


:::keep-together

### Upper quartile

**Seven values in order are 3, 6, 7, 10, 12, 15 and 18. What is the upper quartile?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

The median is 10 (the 4th). The upper half is 12, 15, 18 and its median is 15.

:::


:::keep-together

### Interquartile range

**Eight values in order are 2, 5, 7, 9, 12, 14, 20 and 26. What is the interquartile range?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 11

Lower half 2, 5, 7, 9 has median 6. Upper half 12, 14, 20, 26 has median 17. The IQR is 17 − 6 = 11.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: what is the mean of the eleven sale prices (480, 495, 510, 520, 545, 560, 590, 610, 640, 720, 1450)? (nearest thousand dollars)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 647

The prices add to 7120 and 7120 ÷ 11 is about 647. That is higher than the upper quartile, 640, because the one very high sale drags the mean up.
