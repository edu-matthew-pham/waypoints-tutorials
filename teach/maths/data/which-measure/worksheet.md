# Which measure?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** One student gets 40 dollars a week; the rest get 5 to 8. 'The average is 12 dollars,' says the newspaper. Is that fair?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see an outlier drag the mean but not the median on a dot plot, and choose and justify the most useful measure (mean, median or mode) for different data and questions (AC9M7ST01, node 3).

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how an outlier affects the mean and the median. I can choose the most useful measure of centre for a data set and a question, and justify it.

## Choosing a measure


:::keep-together

### Look for outliers first

Here is how to choose.

1. $5, 6, 6, 7, 8, 40$ — Look first: 40 is far from the rest, an outlier.
2. $\text{mean} = \tfrac{72}{6} = 12$ — The mean uses every value, so the outlier pulls it up: above 5 of the 6.
3. $\text{median} = \tfrac{6 + 7}{2} = 6.5$ — The median only uses the order: a typical value.
4. $\text{no outlier: } 5, 6, 6, 7, 8$ — Mean 6.4 and median 6 now agree: the mean is a fair summary.
5. $\text{most common?} \to \text{mode}$ — For "which is most popular?", use the mode.

Now choose a measure for the house prices.

**Five house prices (\$ thousands): $600, 620, 640, 650, 3200$. Which measure best describes a typical house?**

☐ The mean, 1142

☐ The mode

☐ The median, 640

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The median, 640

One mansion is an outlier. The median, 640, is a typical price; the mean, 1142, describes no house in the street.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam describes the street's house prices. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  House prices in a street ($ thousands): 600, 620, 640, 650, 3200.
 2  The mean uses every price, so it is always the best measure.
 3  The mean is 1142, so a typical house costs about $1 142 000.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: using every value is the mean's weakness here: one outlier (3200) pulls it above four of the five prices. With an outlier, the median (640) is the better description.

:::

### The most common

**A shoe shop records the sizes it sells: $7, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 10, 11$. Which measure tells it which size to stock most of?**

☐ The mean, 8.75

☐ The median, 8.5

☐ The mode, 8

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The mode, 8

The mode, 8, is the size most people bought: the shop's real question.

## Choose and justify


:::keep-together

### Outlier

**Times (s) to run 100 m: $13.2, 13.5, 13.8, 14.0, 22.4$ (one runner fell). Which describes a typical time best?**

☐ The median, 13.8 s

☐ The mean, 15.38 s

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The median, 13.8 s

The outlier pulls the mean up; the median 13.8 s is typical.

:::


:::keep-together

### How much it adds

**Find the mean of $2, 3, 3, 4, 28$, then the mean without the $28$. How much does the $28$ add to the mean? (Type the difference.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 5

With it: 40 ÷ 5 = 8. Without: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. The outlier adds 5.

:::


:::keep-together
