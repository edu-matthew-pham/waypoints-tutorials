# Which measure?

Data and chance · Data

<!-- Speaker note: Students see an outlier drag the mean but not the median on a dot plot, and choose and justify the most useful measure (mean, median or mode) for different data and questions (AC9M7ST01, node 3). ## Today

**We are learning to** choose the measure that tells the truth about the data. An outlier drags the mean, so the median is better for data with extreme values; the mean uses every value when there are none; the mode suits the most common choice.

- I can explain how an outlier affects the mean and the median.
- I can choose the most useful measure of centre for a data set and a question, and justify it.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board first. Students need Mean, median, mode and range. The one idea: look for outliers first. ## Why it matters

> One student gets 40 dollars a week; the rest get 5 to 8. 'The average is 12 dollars,' says the newspaper. Is that fair?

<!-- Speaker note: Mean, median and mode all describe a "typical" value, but they can disagree. Which is most useful depends on the data and the question: - An **outlier** (a value far from the rest) pulls the **mean** towards it; the **median** hardly moves. - With no outliers, the **mean** uses every value, so it is often the best summary. - The **mode** answers "which is most common?": the size a shop should stock most. -->

## Look for outliers first

Here is how to choose.

Now choose a measure for the house prices.

$$5, 6, 6, 7, 8, 40$$

1) $\text{mean} = \tfrac{72}{6} = 12$ — The mean uses every value, so the outlier pulls it up: above 5 of the 6.
2) $\text{median} = \tfrac{6 + 7}{2} = 6.5$ — The median only uses the order: a typical value.
3) $\text{no outlier: } 5, 6, 6, 7, 8$ — Mean 6.4 and median 6 now agree: the mean is a fair summary.
4) $\text{most common?} \to \text{mode}$ — For "which is most popular?", use the mode.

## Exit ticket

**Salaries in a small company ($ thousands): 50, 52, 55, 58, 400. Which measure would you report as typical, and why?**

* The median, 55: the 400 is an outlier that pulls the mean up to 123, more than four of the five salaries.
