Data and chance · Samples

## Today

**We are learning to** bias is anything in the way data is collected that pushes the result one way. It can come from who is asked, how they are asked, or how things are measured, and a bigger sample does not fix it.

- I can spot bias in the way data is collected.
- I can say which way a bias pushes the result.
- I can change a plan to make it fairer.

## Kinds of bias

Here is a routine for spotting bias.

$$\text{Survey: people leaving a gym, 'How often do you exercise?'}$$

1) $\text{Who? People at a gym exercise more}$ — Who was asked: sampling bias.
2) $\text{How? People may say more than they do}$ — How they were asked: response bias.
3) $\text{Result: too high. Fix: random people, anonymous answers}$ — Say which way it pushes, and a fix.

## Kinds of bias

**Which kind of bias is each?**

Groups: Who is asked (sampling bias) · How they are asked (response bias) · How it is measured (measurement bias)

Surveying football fans about the town's favourite sport · Asking 'Don't you agree litter is a disgrace?' · Weighing parcels on scales that read 200 g too heavy · Asking students in front of their teacher how much homework they really do · Only people who chose to reply to an online poll (they selected themselves)

* Answer: Surveying football fans about the town's favourite sport → Who is asked (sampling bias); Asking 'Don't you agree litter is a disgrace?' → How they are asked (response bias); Weighing parcels on scales that read 200 g too heavy → How it is measured (measurement bias); Asking students in front of their teacher how much homework they really do → How they are asked (response bias); Only people who chose to reply to an online poll (they selected themselves) → Who is asked (sampling bias)

<!-- Speaker note: Sampling bias: the sample isn't like the population. Response bias: the question or setting pushes answers. Measurement bias: the tool or method is off. -->

## Find the mistake

**A radio station runs a poll. Click the line where the reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  A radio station asks listeners to phone in: "Should the speed limit be raised?"
 2  1200 people call, and 80% of them say yes.
 3  With so many people, the result must show what most drivers think.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the callers chose themselves, and people who want a change are more likely to call. 1200 biased answers are still biased: a bigger sample doesn't fix bias. -->

## A fairer library survey

**How could the library survey be made less biased?**

- Ask the librarians
- Ask students chosen at random from the whole school roll
- Ask only Year 7s
- Ask more students in the library

* Answer: Ask students chosen at random from the whole school roll

<!-- Speaker note: Random choice from the whole population gives every student the same chance of being asked. -->

## Online poll

**An online poll gets 50 000 votes. Why might it still be biased?**

- Online polls can't count
- 50 000 is too few
- It isn't biased: it is so big
- Voters choose themselves, so people who care a lot are overrepresented

* Answer: Voters choose themselves, so people who care a lot are overrepresented

<!-- Speaker note: Self-selected samples are biased however big they are. -->

## A fair sample

**Which is a fair sample of 30 from a school's 600 students?**

- One Year 9 class of 30
- 30 students picked by a random number generator from the roll
- 30 volunteers
- The first 30 students at the gate

* Answer: 30 students picked by a random number generator from the roll
