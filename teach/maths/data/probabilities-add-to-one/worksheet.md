# Every outcome, and the total of 1

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A bag holds 2 red counters, a blue and a green. Draw two without looking. Can you say the chance of every possible result, and do they add up?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Give each outcome its probability, then add them up. If the total is not 1, an outcome was missed or counted wrongly.

**Students should be able to say:** I can give each outcome its probability. I can check that the probabilities of all outcomes add to 1. I can use the total to find a missing probability.

## All outcomes, total 1


:::keep-together

### Exactly one red

1. $P(RR) = \tfrac{2}{12},\ P(\text{one red}) = \tfrac{8}{12},\ P(\text{no red}) = \tfrac{2}{12}$ — Count the outcomes of each kind.
2. $\tfrac{2}{12} + \tfrac{8}{12} + \tfrac{2}{12} = 1$ — The three kinds cover every outcome.

**Two reds come out in 2 of the 12 outcomes. How many outcomes have exactly one red?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

R1 or R2 with blue or green, in either order: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. (12 − 2 two-red outcomes − 2 no-red outcomes = 8.)

:::


:::keep-together

### Check the total

**P(two reds) = 1/6 and P(no red) = 1/6. What is P(exactly one red)? (Decimal, 3 places.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.667

All three cases cover everything, so they add to 1: 1 − 1/6 − 1/6 = 2/3 ≈ 0.667.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Lee lists the colours that can come out and their probabilities. Click the line that goes wrong.**

```
 1  Two counters are drawn from 2 red, 1 blue and 1 green. There are 12 outcomes.
 2  P(two reds) = 2/12, P(red and blue) = 4/12, P(red and green) = 4/12.
 3  That is every kind of result, and the total is 10/12, which is fine because probabilities do not have to total 1.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: probabilities of all the outcomes always add to 1. Lee's total is 10/12, so he left out blue and green (2/12).

## Totals and missing outcomes


:::keep-together

### The fourth outcome

**A spinner has outcomes with probabilities 0.1, 0.25 and 0.4, and a fourth outcome. What is the fourth probability?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.25

0.1 + 0.25 + 0.4 = 0.75, so the fourth is 1 − 0.75 = 0.25.

:::


:::keep-together

### A total of 1.2

**A student's list of probabilities adds to 1.2. What does that tell you?**

☐ Some probability is counted too much

☐ It is correct

☐ An outcome is missing

☐ The event is certain

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Some probability is counted too much

The total of all outcomes can't pass 1, so something is counted twice or too big.

:::


:::keep-together
