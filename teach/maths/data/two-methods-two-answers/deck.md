# Two samples, two answers

Data and chance · Year 9: statistics

## Why they differ

$$\text{A: } \tfrac{9}{30} = 0.3$$

1) $\text{B: } \tfrac{21}{30} = 0.7$ — Random sample from the roll.
2) $0.7 \times 900 = 630$ — Scale the more trustworthy proportion to the school.

## Estimate for the school

**Using Group B, what is the estimate of the number of the 900 students who buy hot lunch?**

* Answer: 630

## Find the mistake

**Ben compares the two groups. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  Group A asked 30 students in the library and Group B drew 30 names at random.
 2  Group A found 0.3 and Group B found 0.7.
 3  Both samples have 30 students, so they are equally good and the truth is 0.5.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: equal size does not mean equal quality. Group A's method leaves out most students, so it is biased no matter how many are asked. -->

## Best sample

**To estimate how many students own a bike, which is the best sample?**

- 50 students at the bike racks
