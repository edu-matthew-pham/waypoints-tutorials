# Two samples, two answers

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Ask at the library, or draw names from the roll: do you get the same answer about the canteen?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Ask the same question of the same school with two different sampling methods and you get two different results. The method, not just the size, decides which to trust.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say how two sampling methods give different results. I can pick the more trustworthy one. I can use it to estimate for a population.

## Which method, which answer


:::keep-together

### Why they differ

1. $\text{A: } \tfrac{9}{30} = 0.3$ — Library sample.
2. $\text{B: } \tfrac{21}{30} = 0.7$ — Random sample from the roll.
3. $0.7 \times 900 = 630$ — Scale the more trustworthy proportion to the school.

**Why might the library sample give a low proportion who buy hot lunch?**

☐ Students there are older

☐ The library is quiet

☐ The sample is too big

☐ Students at the library at lunch are not in the canteen

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Students at the library at lunch are not in the canteen

Students who spend lunch in the library are the ones less likely to buy lunch, so the sample is not typical.

:::


:::keep-together

### Estimate for the school

**Using Group B, what is the estimate of the number of the 900 students who buy hot lunch?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 630

21 ÷ 30 = 0.7 and 0.7 × 900 = 630.

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: equal size does not mean equal quality. Group A's method leaves out most students, so it is biased no matter how many are asked.

## Compare sampling methods


:::keep-together

### Scale up

**A random sample of 50 students found 20 walk to school. Estimate how many of the 800 students walk.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 320

20 ÷ 50 = 0.4 and 0.4 × 800 = 320.

:::


:::keep-together

### Best sample

**To estimate how many students own a bike, which is the best sample?**

☐ 50 students at the bike racks

☐ The first 50 to arrive

☐ 50 friends

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 50 chosen at random from the roll

The random draw from the full roll.
