## Fair reporting


:::keep-together

### Same fact, two frames

**A council report says: "Only 12% of residents are unhappy with the new bus timetable." A second report on the same survey says: "Nearly one in eight residents are unhappy with the new bus timetable." What is the best description?**

☐ The same fact, framed in two ways that can push readers differently

☐ One of them must have the wrong number

☐ Neither report can be trusted without a bigger sample

☐ The second report is dishonest because it uses a fraction

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The same fact, framed in two ways that can push readers differently

12% and one in eight are the same fact. "Only" and "nearly" are chosen to push the reader towards 'not many' or 'quite a lot', so the framing shapes the claim. (The survey is made up.)

:::


:::keep-together

### True but misleading

**A company reports: "Our drink has no added sugar!" Another company's report on its own drink says: "Cuts sugar by 40%!" Both figures are true. Which is the most important ethical question to ask about each?**

☐ How many people wrote the report

☐ Whether the percentage is a whole number

☐ What does the claim leave out, and who benefits from it being believed?

☐ Which company has the bigger factory?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** What does the claim leave out, and who benefits from it being believed?

A claim can be true and still misleading. Ask what it leaves out (is there other sugar? 40% of what?) and who gains if you believe it. (Both drinks and claims are made up.)

:::


:::keep-together

## Collecting data about people


:::keep-together

### A fair plan

**You want to find out how many hours your classmates sleep. Which plan is the most ethical?**

☐ Look at who is tired in class and record your guess

☐ Ask the whole class to say their hours out loud, one by one

☐ Ask volunteers to answer without their names, and tell them how the results will be used

☐ Ask teachers to read out each student's answers

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Ask volunteers to answer without their names, and tell them how the results will be used

Taking part should be voluntary, answers should not be traced to a person, and people should know what the data is for. (The classroom survey is made up.)

:::


:::keep-together
