# Given

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The sky is grey this morning. Should that change what you say about the chance of rain this afternoon?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read 'given', 'if … then' and 'knowing that' as pointing at a smaller group, and find the chance inside it. They see that P(rain | cloudy) and P(cloudy | rain) use different groups. The context is 100 made-up spring days sorted by rain and cloudy mornings (AC9M10P01, node 1).

**Students should be able to say:** I can say which event is given and which is asked about. I can find a chance inside the group the 'given' picks. I can say why P(A | B) and P(B | A) are usually different.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Which group do you look at


:::keep-together

### Two ways to say it

In this lesson $P(\text{rain} \mid \text{cloudy})$ is read "the chance of rain, given a cloudy morning". Words such as "given", "if", "knowing that" and "of the" all do the same job: they point at the group you look in.

1. $P(\text{bike} \mid \text{scooter})$ — In a survey of 40 students (made up), 24 own a bike, 16 own a scooter and 10 own both. Read "given scooter": the scooter owners are the group.
2. $10 \text{ of the } 16 \text{ scooter owners also own a bike}$ — Count the students with both, but only inside that group.
3. $P(\text{bike} \mid \text{scooter}) = \tfrac{10}{16} = \tfrac{5}{8}$ — The part over the group.
4. $P(\text{scooter} \mid \text{bike}) = \tfrac{10}{24} = \tfrac{5}{12}$ — Swap the words and the group changes to the 24 bike owners, so the answer changes.

Now try one on paper first, with the example beside you. In a different survey (made up), 50 students were asked about two sports. Then type your answer.

**In a survey of 50 students (made up), 30 play basketball, 20 play volleyball and 8 play both. What is the chance a student plays volleyball, given that they play basketball? Give a decimal to 2 places.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Given basketball: the group is the 30 basketball players (the given event picks the group); 8 of those 30 also play volleyball (count the 'both' students inside the group); $8 \div 30 = 0.27$ (the part over the group)

The group is the 30 basketball players, and 8 of them also play volleyball: 8 ÷ 30 = 0.27.

:::

### Clear mornings

**Of the 100 days, 40 had a clear morning and, of those, 8 had rain (made up). What is the chance of rain, given a clear morning? Give a decimal.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.2

8 ÷ 40 = 0.2.

:::


:::keep-together

### Hockey given netball

**In a class of 30, 18 play netball, 12 play hockey and 6 play both (made up). What is P(hockey | netball), as a decimal?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.33

Given netball, the group is 18 students and 6 also play hockey: 6 ÷ 18 = 0.33.

:::

### A review

**Review: use the 100 spring days (10 rain only, 30 both, 20 cloudy only, 40 neither). What is P(rain or cloudy)? Give a decimal.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.6

10 + 30 + 20 = 60 of 100 days had rain or a cloudy morning: 0.6.
