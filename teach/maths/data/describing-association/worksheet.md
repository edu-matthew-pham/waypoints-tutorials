# Describing association

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Four pairs of measurements, four different plots. How would you describe a plot to someone who can't see it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students describe a scatterplot with four words: direction, strength, form and outliers. They draw a line by eye, find an outlier from the gaps to a line, and separate how steep a line is from how strong the association is. The data are made up (AC9M10ST03, node 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can describe a scatterplot using direction, strength, form and outliers. I can tell a strong association from a steep one, and a negative one from a weak one. I can find an outlier and say what I would check about it.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

{{lines-3}}

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** A and C are equally strong. Strength is how tightly the dots follow one pattern, and in both A and C the dots hug a line. A rises (positive) and C falls (negative), but that is the direction, not the strength. B has no pattern, so it is the weakest.

## Finding the words


:::keep-together

### Furthest from the line

Here is how to find an outlier, using the twelve students from the last lesson.

1. $\text{Line: height = arm span}$ — Use a line the dots should follow. A dot's gap from it is its height minus its arm span.
2. $150 - 152 = -2$ — Work out the gap for every dot, with its sign. The first student is 2 cm below the line.
3. $\text{Gaps: } -2, 2, -2, 3, -2, 2, -3, 3, -2, 2, -4, 2$ — Twelve dots, and every gap is small.
4. $\text{Biggest size: } 4$ — The furthest dot is only 4 cm from the line, close to the rest, so there is no outlier.

Now try eight students on paper first, with the worked example beside you. One of them is not like the others.

**Eight students (made up) have these (arm span, height) pairs in cm: $(152, 150)$, $(158, 157)$, $(163, 164)$, $(167, 166)$, $(171, 172)$, $(176, 175)$, $(169, 181)$, $(180, 179)$. The dashed line is height = arm span. How many cm is the furthest dot from the line? Give the size, ignoring the sign.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The gaps (height − arm span) are $-2, -1, 1, -1, 1, -1, 12, -1$ (a dot's gap from the line is its height minus its arm span); Seven of the gaps have a size of $2$ or less (those dots follow the pattern); The biggest size is $12$, for $(169, 181)$ (this dot is far from the line: it is an outlier)

The gaps are -2, -1, 1, -1, 1, -1, 12 and -1. The biggest size is 12, for (169, 181): seven dots are within 2 cm of the line and one is 12 cm away, so it is an outlier.

:::

### Sleep and reaction time

**Which description of the sleep and reaction time plot is best?**

☐ Negative, strong and not linear

☐ Negative, moderate, roughly linear: more sleep tends to go with a faster reaction, with plenty of scatter

☐ Positive, strong and linear

☐ No association

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Negative, moderate, roughly linear: more sleep tends to go with a faster reaction, with plenty of scatter

The dots fall from left to right (negative), they drift rather than hug a line (moderate), and a straight line suits them (linear). No dot is far from the rest.

:::

### Age and height

**Age against height: which sentence describes the form?**

☐ Positive and strong, but not a straight line: it rises quickly, then levels off

☐ No association

☐ Negative and linear

☐ Positive and linear: a straight line fits perfectly

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Positive and strong, but not a straight line: it rises quickly, then levels off

Height rises with age (positive), the dots hug a curve (strong), and the curve is steep at first and then flat, so it is not linear.

### Find the mistake

**Ben describes the study time plot. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The plot shows study time across and test mark up, for 12 students.
 2  The dots rise from left to right, so the direction is positive.
 3  The line through them rises by only about 2 marks for every extra hour. That is a gentle slope.
 4  A gentle slope means the dots are not following the pattern much, so the association is weak.
 5  So this is a weak positive association.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: how steep a line is has nothing to do with how strong the association is. These dots sit very close to a gently rising line, so the association is strong and positive. Strength is how tightly the dots follow the pattern.

## Using the four words


:::keep-together

### No pattern

**Which sentence describes the month of birth and height plot?**

☐ Weak and positive

☐ Strong and negative

☐ No association: the dots show no pattern

☐ Strong and positive

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** No association: the dots show no pattern

The dots are spread over the whole plot with no pattern: when the month changes, the height does not tend to change. No association.

:::


:::keep-together

### Falling and strong

**The dots of a plot lie very close to a line that falls from the top left to the bottom right. Zoe says: 'It falls, so the association is weak.' Which description is best?**

☐ Strong, negative, linear

☐ Weak, because it falls

☐ Negative, so it is a poor result

☐ Strong, positive, linear

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Strong, negative, linear

Close to a line means strong, a falling line means negative, and a line means linear.

**Review: a line passes through $(5, 339)$ and $(10, 289)$. What is its gradient?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** -10

(289 − 339) ÷ (10 − 5) = −50 ÷ 5 = −10: reaction time falls by 10 ms for every extra hour of sleep.
