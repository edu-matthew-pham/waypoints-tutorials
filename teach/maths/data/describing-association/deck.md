## Today

- I can describe a scatterplot using direction, strength, form and outliers.
- I can tell a strong association from a steep one, and a negative one from a weak one.
- I can find an outlier and say what I would check about it.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. A and B move up and down only; the line is drawn through them. The check ticks for a line within about 15 ms of the best-fit line at 5 and 10 hours. The one idea: four separate words. Direction is which way the dots lean, strength is how tightly they follow the pattern, form is a line or a curve, and an outlier is a dot apart from the rest. Students tend to merge them (falling means weak; gentle means weak). Strength is judged by eye here. There is no correlation coefficient in this node and none is used. If students ask, strong means the dots all sit close to the line, moderate means they follow it with a lot of scatter, and weak means a pattern is hard to see. Every data set is made up: the sleep and reaction times, ages and heights, month of birth, study time and the eight students. Real reaction-time tests (an online test, or a ruler drop) would give real data; the Make question avoids a cause on purpose: the next lesson is about that. Hidden: the line by eye is not exact. Different students will draw slightly different lines; the check is a range, not one line. Teacher: Paper: the eight-student outlier task is worked on paper first, with the worked example beside them. Look for the gap worked out without its sign. -->

## Furthest from the line

Here is how to find an outlier, using the twelve students from the last lesson.

Now try eight students on paper first, with the worked example beside you. One of them is not like the others.

$$\text{Line: height = arm span}$$

1) $150 - 152 = -2$ — Work out the gap for every dot, with its sign. The first student is 2 cm below the line.
2) $\text{Gaps: } -2, 2, -2, 3, -2, 2, -3, 3, -2, 2, -4, 2$ — Twelve dots, and every gap is small.
3) $\text{Biggest size: } 4$ — The furthest dot is only 4 cm from the line, close to the rest, so there is no outlier.

## Furthest from the line

**Eight students (made up) have these (arm span, height) pairs in cm: $(152, 150)$, $(158, 157)$, $(163, 164)$, $(167, 166)$, $(171, 172)$, $(176, 175)$, $(169, 181)$, $(180, 179)$. The dashed line is height = arm span. How many cm is the furthest dot from the line? Give the size, ignoring the sign.**

* Answer: The gaps (height − arm span) are $-2, -1, 1, -1, 1, -1, 12, -1$ (a dot's gap from the line is its height minus its arm span); Seven of the gaps have a size of $2$ or less (those dots follow the pattern); The biggest size is $12$, for $(169, 181)$ (this dot is far from the line: it is an outlier)

## Find the mistake

**Ben describes the study time plot. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The plot shows study time across and test mark up, for 12 students.
 2  The dots rise from left to right, so the direction is positive.
 3  The line through them rises by only about 2 marks for every extra hour. That is a gentle slope.
 4  A gentle slope means the dots are not following the pattern much, so the association is weak.
 5  So this is a weak positive association.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: how steep a line is has nothing to do with how strong the association is. These dots sit very close to a gently rising line, so the association is strong and positive. Strength is how tightly the dots follow the pattern. -->
