# A graph for who and why

Data and chance · Year 9: statistics

## Why it matters

> The canteen's sales data for the principal, and for a poster: same data, same graph?

## For the principal

**The principal wants to compare the exact numbers of drinks, hot food, sandwiches and fruit. Which is clearest?**

- A 3-D pie chart without numbers
- A line graph over the week
- A bar chart with the numbers labelled
- An unlabelled picture

* Answer: A bar chart with the numbers labelled

<!-- Speaker note: A labelled bar chart lets the principal compare sizes and read exact values. -->

## For the poster

**A noticeboard poster should be...**

- Simple, big and quick to read
- In tiny print
- Packed with small numbers
- As complex as possible

* Answer: Simple, big and quick to read

<!-- Speaker note: A poster has seconds to make its point, so keep it simple and clear. -->

## Find the mistake

**Max reads the sales. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  The canteen has one set of sales data.
 2  So one graph is the best graph for everyone who reads it.
 3  I will use the same pie chart for the principal and the poster.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: the best display depends on who reads it and why. The same graph can suit one reader and not another. -->

**Of 180 sales, 70 were hot food. What percentage is that (1 decimal place)?**
