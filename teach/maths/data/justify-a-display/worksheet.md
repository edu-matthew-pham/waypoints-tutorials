## Audience and purpose


:::keep-together

### For the principal

1. $\tfrac{45}{180} = 25\%$ — Drinks as a share of sales.
2. $\text{principal} \to \text{labelled bar chart}$ — Compare exact values.
3. $\text{poster} \to \text{simple picture, one message}$ — Catch the eye.

**The principal wants to compare the exact numbers of drinks, hot food, sandwiches and fruit. Which is clearest?**

☐ A 3-D pie chart without numbers

☐ A line graph over the week

☐ A bar chart with the numbers labelled

☐ An unlabelled picture

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A bar chart with the numbers labelled

A labelled bar chart lets the principal compare sizes and read exact values.

:::


:::keep-together

### For the poster

**A noticeboard poster should be...**

☐ Simple, big and quick to read

☐ In tiny print

☐ Packed with small numbers

☐ As complex as possible

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Simple, big and quick to read

A poster has seconds to make its point, so keep it simple and clear.

:::


:::keep-together

## Shares and readers


:::keep-together

### Hot food share

**Of 180 sales, 70 were hot food. What percentage is that (1 decimal place)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 38.9

70 ÷ 180 × 100 = 38.9.

:::


:::keep-together

### Fruit angle

**Of 180 sales, 25 were fruit. What pie-chart angle is that (degrees, nearest whole)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 50

25 ÷ 180 × 360 = 50.

:::
