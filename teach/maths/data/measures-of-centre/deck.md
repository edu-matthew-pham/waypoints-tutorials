## Today

**We are learning to** four numbers that sum up a data set: the mean (add, then share equally), the median (the middle value once sorted), the mode (the most common) and the range (highest minus lowest).

- I can calculate the mean, median, mode and range of a data set.
- I can find the median of an even number of values.
- I can handle data with two modes or no mode.

## Why it matters

> A team scored 3, 5, 5, 6 and 11 goals. What is a typical game? There are at least three good answers, and they are all useful.

<!-- Speaker note: A data set can have many values. To describe it in a few numbers, statisticians use **measures of centre** (what is typical) and of **spread** (how varied): - the **mean**: add all the values, then divide by how many there are; - the **median**: sort the values; it is the middle one (or halfway between the two middle ones); - the **mode**: the value that appears most often; - the **range**: highest minus lowest, a simple measure of spread. -->

## Mean, median, mode, range

Here is each measure for the goals data.

Now find the median of $8, 3, 9, 4, 6, 7$.

$$3, 5, 5, 6, 11$$

1) $\text{mean} = \tfrac{3 + 5 + 5 + 6 + 11}{5} = \tfrac{30}{5} = 6$ — Add, then divide by how many.
2) $\text{median} = 5$ — The middle (3rd of 5) value of the sorted list.
3) $\text{mode} = 5$ — The most common value (it appears twice).
4) $\text{range} = 11 - 3 = 8$ — Highest minus lowest.
5) $8, 3, 9, 4, 6, 7 \to 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9$ — An even count: sort, then the median is halfway between the two middle values, $\tfrac{6 + 7}{2} = 6.5$.
