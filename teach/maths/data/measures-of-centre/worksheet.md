## The four measures


:::keep-together

### Mean, median, mode, range

Here is each measure for the goals data.

1. $3, 5, 5, 6, 11$ — The data, sorted (the median and range need it sorted).
2. $\text{mean} = \tfrac{3 + 5 + 5 + 6 + 11}{5} = \tfrac{30}{5} = 6$ — Add, then divide by how many.
3. $\text{median} = 5$ — The middle (3rd of 5) value of the sorted list.
4. $\text{mode} = 5$ — The most common value (it appears twice).
5. $\text{range} = 11 - 3 = 8$ — Highest minus lowest.
6. $8, 3, 9, 4, 6, 7 \to 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9$ — An even count: sort, then the median is halfway between the two middle values, $\tfrac{6 + 7}{2} = 6.5$.

Now find the median of $8, 3, 9, 4, 6, 7$.

**Find the median of $8, 3, 9, 4, 6, 7$.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6.5

Sorted: 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9. The two middle values are 6 and 7, so the median is 6.5.

:::

## Make: a power bill on paper

Do this one in your book. The readings are made up, but your family's bill hides the same thing: an average that one hot day can lift. Work out the median and the mean, type how much higher the mean is, then choose the reason.

**Also used in**

- **Bills.** The 'average daily use' on an electricity bill is a mean. It hides hot and cold days and how many people live in the house.
- **House prices and wages.** Reports often give a median, so that a few very high values do not drag the typical figure up.
- **Sport.** Goals per game, runs per over and points per match are all summarised the same way.
