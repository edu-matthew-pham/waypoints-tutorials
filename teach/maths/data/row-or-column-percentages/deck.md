## Today

- I can fill in a two-way table and check its totals.
- I can work out a row percentage and say what group it is a percentage of.
- I can tell a row percentage from a column percentage.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Investigate tasks yourself. The table is 200 Year 10 students (made up): Ride 30 play sport and 10 do not (40), Walk 33 and 27 (60), Bus or car 40 and 60 (100). 103 play sport in all. The one idea: a percentage needs a whole. Row percentages divide by the row total, so they compare the groups (75%, 55%, 40%). Column percentages divide by the column total and answer a different question: of the 103 players, 29.1% ride, 32.0% walk and 38.8% come by bus or car. The common slips: dividing by the grand total (Dev: 33 ÷ 200 = 16.5%), dividing by the other total (Mia: 30 ÷ 103), and choosing the group with the biggest count. ## One row's percentage

Here is how to work out a row percentage.

$$\text{Pick the group, a row}$$

1) $\text{The row total is the whole group}$ — 33 + 27 = 60 walkers.
2) $\frac{33}{60} = 0.55$ — Count in the cell divided by the row total.
3) $0.55 \times 100 = 55\%$ — Turn it into a percentage and say it: 55% of walkers play sport.

## Find the mistake

**Dev works out the percentage of walkers who play sport. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  In the survey, 33 students walk to school and play a sport.
 2  There are 200 students in the survey altogether.
 3  So the percentage of walkers who play sport is 33 out of 200.
 4  33 ÷ 200 = 0.165, which is 16.5%.
 5  So 16.5% of the walkers play sport.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: Dev divides by all 200 students. The walkers are a group of 60, so he should work out 33 out of 60. His 16.5% is the share of all students who both walk and play sport. -->

## A fair comparison

**Riders: 30 of 40 play sport. Bus or car: 40 of 100. Which comparison is fair?**

- 75% of riders against 40% of bus or car students, because each is a percentage of its own group
- 30 against 40, because the more the better
- 30 against 100, because 100 is the biggest total
- Riders can't be compared with bus or car students

* Answer: 75% of riders against 40% of bus or car students, because each is a percentage of its own group

## Which way?

**Mia wants the percentage of riders who play sport. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  I want to know what percentage of riders play sport.
 2  30 riders play sport, and 103 students play sport altogether.
 3  30 ÷ 103 = 0.29, which is about 29%.
 4  So 29% of riders play sport.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: Mia divides by 103, everyone who plays sport. That gives the share of players who ride, a different question. For the percentage of riders who play sport, divide by the 40 riders. -->

## Exit ticket

**In a survey, 18 of the 30 students in Year 9 are in a band, and 24 of the 60 students in Year 10. Which year has the higher percentage in a band?**

* Year 9: 18 ÷ 30 = 60%, against 24 ÷ 60 = 40% for Year 10.
