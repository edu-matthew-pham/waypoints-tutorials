# Row or column percentages

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** More bus and car students play sport than riders do. So are bus and car students more sporty?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare groups of different sizes with row percentages, and learn to say what each percentage is of. The context is how Year 10 students travel to school and whether they play sport, with made-up survey counts (AC9M10ST04, nodes 1 and 2).

**Students should be able to say:** I can fill in a two-way table and check its totals. I can work out a row percentage and say what group it is a percentage of. I can tell a row percentage from a column percentage.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## A percentage of its own group


:::keep-together

### One row's percentage

Here is how to work out a row percentage.

1. $\text{Pick the group, a row}$ — The question says walkers, so use the Walk row.
2. $\text{The row total is the whole group}$ — 33 + 27 = 60 walkers.
3. $\frac{33}{60} = 0.55$ — Count in the cell divided by the row total.
4. $0.55 \times 100 = 55\%$ — Turn it into a percentage and say it: 55% of walkers play sport.

**What percentage of the walkers play sport?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 55

33 ÷ 60 = 0.55, which is 55%. Out of the walkers, 55% play sport.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Dev works out the percentage of walkers who play sport. Click the line where he goes wrong.**

```
 1  In the survey, 33 students walk to school and play a sport.
 2  There are 200 students in the survey altogether.
 3  So the percentage of walkers who play sport is 33 out of 200.
 4  33 ÷ 200 = 0.165, which is 16.5%.
 5  So 16.5% of the walkers play sport.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Dev divides by all 200 students. The walkers are a group of 60, so he should work out 33 out of 60. His 16.5% is the share of all students who both walk and play sport.

:::


:::keep-together

### A fair comparison

**Riders: 30 of 40 play sport. Bus or car: 40 of 100. Which comparison is fair?**

☐ 75% of riders against 40% of bus or car students, because each is a percentage of its own group

☐ 30 against 40, because the more the better

☐ 30 against 100, because 100 is the biggest total

☐ Riders can't be compared with bus or car students

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 75% of riders against 40% of bus or car students, because each is a percentage of its own group

Groups of different sizes are compared by percentages of their own totals. 75% of riders play sport and 40% of bus or car students.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which way?

**Mia wants the percentage of riders who play sport. Click the line where her working goes wrong.**

```
 1  I want to know what percentage of riders play sport.
 2  30 riders play sport, and 103 students play sport altogether.
 3  30 ÷ 103 = 0.29, which is about 29%.
 4  So 29% of riders play sport.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Mia divides by 103, everyone who plays sport. That gives the share of players who ride, a different question. For the percentage of riders who play sport, divide by the 40 riders.

## Fair comparisons


:::keep-together

### Bus or car

**In the survey, what percentage of the bus or car students play sport?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40

40 ÷ 100 = 0.4, which is 40%.

:::keep-together

### Highest rate

**Which group has the highest percentage playing sport: ride (30 of 40), walk (33 of 60) or bus or car (40 of 100)?**

☐ Ride, 75%

☐ Bus or car, 40%

☐ Bus or car, because 40 is the most players

☐ Walk, 55%

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Ride, 75%

30 ÷ 40 = 75%, 33 ÷ 60 = 55%, 40 ÷ 100 = 40%. Riders have the highest percentage.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review

**Review: a club has $80$ members and $45\%$ are in Year 10. How many members is that?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 36

45% of 80 = 0.45 × 80 = 36.

**The sentence.** All the figures in this lesson are made up: the school, the 200 students and their travel and sport.

**Also used in**

- **News and surveys.** 'Women are twice as likely...' claims are about a percentage of each group; always ask 'percentage of what?'.
- **Health.** Rates in a two-way table, such as vaccinated or not against caught the illness or not, are compared by row percentages.
