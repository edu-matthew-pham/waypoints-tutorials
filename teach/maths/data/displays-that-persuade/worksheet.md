> **Key question:** An ad shows its battery lasting more than twice as long as the rival's. Does it?

## Read the axis


:::keep-together

### Spot the trick

1. $\tfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$ — Bar heights with the axis starting at 90.
2. $\tfrac{100}{94} \approx 1.06$ — The real ratio.
3. $\tfrac{100 - 94}{94} \approx 6\%$ — X lasts about 6% longer.

**What is wrong with the chart?**

☐ The labels are too small

☐ There are only two bars

☐ The bars are the wrong colour

☐ The vertical axis starts at 90 hours, not 0

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The vertical axis starts at 90 hours, not 0

A bar's length should be in proportion to its value, which needs the axis to start at 0.

:::


:::keep-together

### Redraw

**If the axis started at 0 and X were 100 mm tall, how tall (mm) would Y be?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 94

94 mm: the bars would be in the ratio 100 to 94.

:::


:::keep-together

{{lines-2}}

**Answer:** line 3

## Bars from a cut axis


:::keep-together

### Cut axis

**Two bars have axis starting at 50. Values 80 and 60. How many times taller does the first bar look?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 3

(80 − 50) ÷ (60 − 50) = 30 ÷ 10 = 3.

:::


:::keep-together

### True difference

**The real values are 80 and 60. How many percent bigger is 80 than 60? (1 decimal)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 33.3

20 ÷ 60 = 0.333.

:::keep-together
