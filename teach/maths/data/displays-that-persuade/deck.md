**The chart shows X as about 2.5 times the height of Y. The labels say 100 hours and 94 hours. How much longer does X really last?**

## Spot the trick

$$\tfrac{10}{4} = 2.5$$

1) $\tfrac{100}{94} \approx 1.06$ — The real ratio.
2) $\tfrac{100 - 94}{94} \approx 6\%$ — X lasts about 6% longer.

## Spot the trick

**What is wrong with the chart?**

- The labels are too small
- There are only two bars
- The bars are the wrong colour
- The vertical axis starts at 90 hours, not 0

* Answer: The vertical axis starts at 90 hours, not 0

<!-- Speaker note: A bar's length should be in proportion to its value, which needs the axis to start at 0. -->

## Redraw

**If the axis started at 0 and X were 100 mm tall, how tall (mm) would Y be?**

* Answer: 94

<!-- Speaker note: 94 mm: the bars would be in the ratio 100 to 94. -->

## Find the mistake

**Pia reads the chart. Click the line where she goes wrong.**

```
 1  Brand X is 100 hours and brand Y is 94 hours; the chart axis starts at 90.
 2  The X bar is 10 units tall and the Y bar is 4 units tall.
 3  So X lasts 2.5 times as long as Y.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the bars show only the parts above 90, so their heights are in the ratio 10 : 4. The real values are 100 and 94, a difference of 6%. -->

## Cut axis

**Two bars have axis starting at 50. Values 80 and 60. How many times taller does the first bar look?**

* Answer: 3

<!-- Speaker note: (80 − 50) ÷ (60 − 50) = 30 ÷ 10 = 3. -->

## True difference

**The real values are 80 and 60. How many percent bigger is 80 than 60? (1 decimal)**

* Answer: 33.3
