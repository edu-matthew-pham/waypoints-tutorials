## Fair, ethical, honest


:::keep-together

### Ethics

Here is the routine for an inference.

1. $\text{Random sample of } 50 \text{ Year 9s at our school}$ — The sample and how it was chosen.
2. $\text{Median screen time } 3 \text{ hours, range } 6 \text{ hours}$ — What the sample shows.
3. $\text{Year 9s at our school: about } 3 \text{ hours}$ — Infer about the population sampled only.
4. $\text{A sample of 50 can be a little off; more students would narrow it}$ — State the uncertainty.

**Which is the most important ethical step in a survey about students' sleep?**

☐ Teachers read the answers aloud

☐ Every student must answer

☐ Students agree to take part and their answers are kept anonymous

☐ Names are written on each form

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Students agree to take part and their answers are kept anonymous

Ethical data collection asks permission, keeps answers private, and does no harm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Zara reports her investigation. Click the line where her inference goes too far.**

```
 1  Zara takes a random sample of 40 students at her school and asks about phone use after lights out.
 2  Her sample's median is 45 minutes, and the range is 120 minutes.
 3  She concludes that teenagers across Australia use their phones for about 45 minutes after lights out.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: her sample came from one school, so she can infer about her school, not all Australian teenagers.

:::

### Saying how sure

**Which sentence states the uncertainty best?**

☐ From a random sample of 40, students at our school probably sleep about 8.8 hours; a bigger sample would give a closer estimate

☐ Students sleep exactly 8.8 hours

☐ We are certain students sleep 8.8 hours

☐ Students sleep 8.8 hours, which proves they are tired

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** From a random sample of 40, students at our school probably sleep about 8.8 hours; a bigger sample would give a closer estimate

Name the sample, use 'about' or 'probably', and say how the estimate could improve.

## Investigations


:::keep-together

### One school

**A random sample of 30 Year 10 students at one school finds most want a later start time. What can be inferred?**

☐ Nothing

☐ Most Year 10s at that school probably want a later start

☐ Most students in Australia want a later start

☐ Every Year 10 at the school wants a later start

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Most Year 10s at that school probably want a later start

Infer about the population sampled: Year 10 at that school.

:::


:::keep-together

### A fair question

**Which question is fair to ask in an anonymous survey about exercise?**

☐ Why are you so lazy?

☐ Don't you think you should exercise more?

☐ What is your name and weight?

☐ How many minutes did you exercise yesterday?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** How many minutes did you exercise yesterday?

Neutral, specific and respectful questions give honest data.

:::


:::keep-together

### More certain

**How can an investigation make its inference more certain?**

☐ Round the answer

☐ Use a convenience sample

☐ Ask only friends

☐ Use a bigger random sample

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Use a bigger random sample

Bigger fair samples vary less, so the estimate is more certain.

:::


:::keep-together

### Review: variation

**Review: two random samples of 25 from the same school give means of 8.6 and 9.0 hours. What explains the difference?**

☐ A measuring mistake

☐ Sampling variation

☐ One sample was biased

☐ The school changed

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sampling variation

Random samples hold different students, so their means differ a little.

:::
