# Investigating with samples

Data and chance · Samples

<!-- Speaker note: Students estimate a hidden population value from samples, then plan an ethical sample investigation and state an inference with its uncertainty (AC9M8ST04, nodes 1 and 2). ## Today

**We are learning to** a sample investigation asks a question about a population, plans a fair and ethical sample, collects and describes the data, then makes an inference about the population and says how sure it can be.

- I can plan an investigation with a fair, ethical sample.
- I can make an inference about a population from a sample.
- I can say how sure I am, and why.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Run board yourself first. Students need sampling methods, describing samples and sampling variation (the last three lessons), and the Year 7 investigation cycle. The one idea: an inference estimates the population that was sampled, with its uncertainty stated (size and method). The common mistake is inferring beyond the population (Zara's one school to all Australian teenagers). The board hides the school's value until Show the population, so students commit to an estimate first. Ethics: consent, anonymity, no harm, and neutral questions; school approval for real surveys. The full investigation (collecting real data) is a class task: the Make step plans it. Use a class sample of a short anonymous form on a non-sensitive topic. -->

## Why it matters

> You can't see the whole school's answer, only your sample's. How close is your estimate, and how would you say so honestly?

## Ethics

Here is the routine for an inference.

$$\text{Random sample of } 50 \text{ Year 9s at our school}$$

1) $\text{Median screen time } 3 \text{ hours, range } 6 \text{ hours}$ — What the sample shows.
2) $\text{Year 9s at our school: about } 3 \text{ hours}$ — Infer about the population sampled only.
3) $\text{A sample of 50 can be a little off; more students would narrow it}$ — State the uncertainty.

## Saying how sure

**Which sentence states the uncertainty best?**

- From a random sample of 40, students at our school probably sleep about 8.8 hours; a bigger sample would give a closer estimate
- Students sleep exactly 8.8 hours
- We are certain students sleep 8.8 hours
- Students sleep 8.8 hours, which proves they are tired

* Answer: From a random sample of 40, students at our school probably sleep about 8.8 hours; a bigger sample would give a closer estimate

## A fair question

**Which question is fair to ask in an anonymous survey about exercise?**

- Why are you so lazy?
- Don't you think you should exercise more?
- What is your name and weight?
- How many minutes did you exercise yesterday?

* Answer: How many minutes did you exercise yesterday?

## More certain

**How can an investigation make its inference more certain?**

- Round the answer
- Use a convenience sample
- Ask only friends
- Use a bigger random sample

* Answer: Use a bigger random sample

<!-- Speaker note: Bigger fair samples vary less, so the estimate is more certain. -->

## Review: variation

**Review: two random samples of 25 from the same school give means of 8.6 and 9.0 hours. What explains the difference?**

- A measuring mistake
- Sampling variation
- One sample was biased
- The school changed

* Answer: Sampling variation

<!-- Speaker note: Random samples hold different students, so their means differ a little. -->
