## Today

- I can say how much higher one group's percentage is, in points and in times as likely.
- I can write a claim from a table that names its groups and stops short of 'causes'.
- I can say what more I would need to find a cause.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Investigate tasks yourself. The table is the same as Row or column percentages (200 Year 10 students, made up): Ride 30 play sport of 40 (75%), Walk 33 of 60 (55%), Bus or car 40 of 100 (40%). The one idea: a table shows who differs, not why. The careful claim names the group, gives the percentage of each, says 'more likely' and says 'in this survey'. Percentage points are the gap between two percentages (35); 'times as likely' divides them (1.875). Third things the students might name: living close to school, having a bike at home, a parent's attitude to exercise. The point is to show that a link leaves several explanations open, as in the ice-cream and drowning lesson. Every figure is made up: the school, the survey and the breakfast quiz table. ## Writing it carefully

Here is how to build the sentence.

$$\text{Name the groups}$$

1) $75\% \text{ against } 40\%$ — Give both percentages, each of its own group.
2) $\text{Say 'more likely', not 'because'}$ — The table shows who plays sport more often, not why.
3) $\text{Say where it comes from}$ — 'In this survey' keeps the claim to the 200 students asked.

## Find the step too far

**Ben writes the school's report. Click the line where his reasoning goes further than the table.**

```
 1  The table shows 75% of riders play sport and 40% of bus or car students do.

 3  So riding to school makes students play sport.
 4  The school should give every student a bike.
```

* Answer: line 3

## A third thing

**Which is a possible third thing that could sit behind both riding to school and playing sport?**

- Living close to school, so riding is easy and there is time and a nearby field
- Having a bike is always the cause of playing sport
- Nothing: a link in a table has no other explanation
- Playing sport makes the bike lighter

* Answer: Living close to school, so riding is easy and there is time and a nearby field

<!-- Speaker note: Students who live close can ride, and may also have time and a place to play. That would give the link without riding causing anything. -->

## The careful sentence

**Which sentence is the most careful claim from the table?**

- Bus and car students don't like sport
- Riding to school makes students play sport
- Every student who rides plays sport
- In this survey, students who ride were more likely to play sport (75%) than students who come by bus or car (40%)

* Answer: In this survey, students who ride were more likely to play sport (75%) than students who come by bus or car (40%)

## Choosing the words

**A table shows students who eat breakfast pass a quiz more often than those who don't. Which word fits the table best?**

- Students who eat breakfast are more likely to pass
- Breakfast makes students pass
- Breakfast is unrelated to passing
- Passing makes students eat breakfast

* Answer: Students who eat breakfast are more likely to pass

<!-- Speaker note: 'More likely' says what the table shows. 'Makes' needs more than a table. -->
