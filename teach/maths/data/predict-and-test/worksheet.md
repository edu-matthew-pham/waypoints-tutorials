> **Key question:** Roll a dice 60 times. Exactly ten sixes? Probably not. But how far off is normal, and when should you suspect the dice?

## Expected and observed


:::keep-together

### Expected, then observed

Here is how to predict and test.

1. $P(6) = \tfrac{1}{6}$ — The probability from the sample space.
2. $60 \times \tfrac{1}{6} = 10$ — Expected count: probability × trials.
3. $\text{observed: } 7 \text{ sixes}$ — Run the experiment and record the count.
4. $\tfrac{7}{60} \approx 0.12$ — Relative frequency = count ÷ trials, the experiment's estimate.
5. $0.12 \text{ against } 0.17$ — Close but not equal: a short experiment varies.

Now: how many heads in 50 flips?

**You flip a fair coin 50 times. How many heads do you expect?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 25

50 × 1/2 = 25.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Noah tests a dice. Click the line where his reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  I rolled a dice 60 times.
 2  P(6) = 1/6, so I expect 60 × 1/6 = 10 sixes.
 3  I got 7 sixes, not 10.
 4  So the dice must be unfair.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: in 60 rolls the count of sixes often lands a few away from 10; 7 is common for a fair dice. The expected count is what happens on average, not every time. To test a dice, roll it many more times.

:::


:::keep-together

### Relative frequency

**In an experiment, a coin landed heads 13 times in 20 flips. What is the relative frequency of heads? (A decimal.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.65

13 ÷ 20 = 0.65, more than the probability 0.5: chance varies in 20 flips.

**A bag has 2 red and 8 blue counters. You take one and put it back, 40 times. How many reds do you expect?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** 8

P(red) = 2/10 = 1/5, and 40 × 1/5 = 8.

**A spinner landed on green 18 times in 60 spins. What is the relative frequency of green? (A decimal.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.3

18 ÷ 60 = 0.3.
