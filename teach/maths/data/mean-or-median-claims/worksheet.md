# "The average is..." Which average, from what?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** "The average house in this suburb sells for $834 000." Is that what a typical house costs?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A claim about an average depends on which average was used and how the data was collected. One very high value drags the mean but hardly moves the median.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say which average a claim uses. I can say when an outlier makes the mean misleading. I can ask how a claim's data was collected.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict

**Six houses sold for about $520 000 to $640 000 and one for $2 400 000. Which average better shows a typical house?**

My prediction, and why:

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The median. The one expensive sale drags the mean up to $834 000, but the median stays at $580 000, in the middle of the usual prices.

## Which average and how collected


:::keep-together

### The median

1. $\text{mean} = \tfrac{5840}{7} \approx 834$ — Add the prices and divide by 7.
2. $\text{median} = 580$ — The middle of the ordered prices.
3. $\text{mean without } 2400 = \tfrac{3440}{6} \approx 573$ — Remove the outlier and the mean falls.

**What is the median of the 7 prices (in thousands of dollars)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 580

Ordered: 520, 540, 560, 580, 600, 640, 2400. The middle (4th) is 580.

:::


:::keep-together

### Question the claim

**A headline says "The average Australian teenager spends 6 hours a day on screens." Which question should you ask FIRST?**

☐ Is 6 a nice number?

☐ What colour is the report?

☐ Is it more than 5?

☐ How was it measured and who was asked?

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** How was it measured and who was asked?

Ask how the data was collected (self-report or app data) and from whom, then which average was used.

Line: {{field-15}}

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: with one sale far above the others, the mean (about 834) is higher than six of the seven sales, so it is not typical. The median (580) is.

## Mean and median claims


:::keep-together

### Median

**Five phone prices: 400, 420, 450, 480, 1500 (dollars, made up). What is the median?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 450

The middle of five ordered values is the 3rd: 450.

:::


:::keep-together

### Mean

**Same prices: what is the mean?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 650

(400 + 420 + 450 + 480 + 1500) ÷ 5 = 3250 ÷ 5 = 650.

:::
