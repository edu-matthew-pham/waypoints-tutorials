# "The average is..." Which average, from what?

Data and chance · Year 9: statistics

<!-- Speaker note: A claim about an average depends on which average was used and how the data was collected. One very high value drags the mean but hardly moves the median. About 40 minutes (Predict and Run 8 min · Investigate 10 min · Modify 10 min · Practise and Make 12 min). -->

## Why it matters

> "The average house in this suburb sells for $834 000." Is that what a typical house costs?

<!-- Speaker note: Seven houses in a suburb sold last month for these prices, in thousands of dollars: 520, 540, 560, 580, 600, 640 and 2400 (made up for the task). Reports of house prices usually give the median, because a few very high sales drag the mean up ([Westpac explains why](https://www.westpac.com.au/personal-banking/home-loans/median-house-prices-explained/)). Have a guess whether the mean or the median says more about a typical house here, and we'll come back to it at the end. -->

## What happened?

* The median. The one expensive sale drags the mean up to $834 000, but the median stays at $580 000, in the middle of the usual prices.

## The median

$$\text{mean} = \tfrac{5840}{7} \approx 834$$

1) $\text{median} = 580$ — The middle of the ordered prices.
2) $\text{mean without } 2400 = \tfrac{3440}{6} \approx 573$ — Remove the outlier and the mean falls.
