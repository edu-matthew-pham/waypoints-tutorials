# Putting it back, or not

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two names come out of a hat for the class jobs. Can the same name come out twice?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** When something drawn is put back the choices stay the same; when it is not, they shrink. Listing the outcomes both ways shows what changes.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether a draw is with or without put-back. I can list and count the outcomes both ways. I can explain why the second choice shrinks.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## One fewer choice


:::keep-together

### Count the draws

1. $\text{4 names, first not put back}$ — A draw with no put-back.
2. $4 \times 3 = 12 \text{ outcomes}$ — 4 choices, then 3 left.
3. $P(\text{Ana first, then Ben}) = \tfrac{1}{12}$ — One of the 12 equally likely outcomes.

**5 names are in a hat and two are drawn for two jobs, with nothing put back. How many outcomes are there?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 20

5 choices for the first job, 4 for the second: 5 × 4 = 20.

:::


:::keep-together

### Ordered prizes

**8 people are in a group and 2 of them get a prize each, in order, with no repeat. How many ordered outcomes are there?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 56

### Both from your table

**8 people are in a group. 3 are from your table. How many ordered outcomes have both prize winners from your table?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

3 × 2 = 6 (the first from the 3, the second from the 2 left).

## Make: back to the hat

Compare with putting the name back: would the chance of both from your table go up or down?

**Also used in**

- **Card games.** Cards are dealt and not put back, so each card changes what is left.
- **Quality checks.** A tester pulls two items from a box without putting them back, and the chance of a faulty second depends on the first.
