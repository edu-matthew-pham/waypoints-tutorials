# Records as probabilities

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The 8:05 bus has been late on some days. How likely is it to be late tomorrow?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** When you cannot count outcomes, a record of what happened gives an estimate: the relative frequency. More records give a steadier estimate.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find a relative frequency from a record. I can use it as an estimate and to predict. I can say why more records are better.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## How good is the estimate


:::keep-together

### Work it out

1. $\text{late on 8 of 40 days}$ — The record.
2. $\tfrac{8}{40} = 0.2$ — Relative frequency = times it happened ÷ number of trials.
3. $0.2 \times 50 = 10$ — In 50 more days, expect about 10 late.

**The bus was late on 15 of 60 days. What is the relative frequency (a decimal)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.25

15 ÷ 60 = 0.25.

:::


:::keep-together

### Trust the estimate

**Which estimate of the chance of a late bus can you trust most?**

☐ Late on 2 of 5 days

☐ They are equally good

☐ Late on 80 of 400 days

☐ Late on 1 of 2 days

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Late on 80 of 400 days

The longer record. Relative frequencies from lots of trials are steadier.

:::


:::keep-together

## Estimating from records


:::keep-together

### Credit sales

**A shop sells 63 of its 300 phones in a month on credit. What is the relative frequency of a credit sale (a decimal)? (Made up for the task.)**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 0.21

63 ÷ 300 = 0.21.

:::


:::keep-together

### Faulty items

**A machine's records give P(faulty) = 0.03. About how many faulty items in the next 1000?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 30

0.03 × 1000 = 30.

:::

**Review: 6 names in a hat, two drawn in order with no put-back. How many outcomes?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 30

6 × 5 = 30.
