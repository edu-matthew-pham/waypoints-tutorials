# Is that really Mum?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A few seconds of someone's voice can be enough to clone it. If a call or message from 'family' asks for money or urgent help, hang up and call back on a number you already know.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Voice clones: a few seconds of voice can be enough (Scamwatch). Spot pressure and secrecy, and check outside the call by calling back on a known number. Typical: the Arup deepfake video call. Wider: a voice clone as phishing by voice (links to Cyber safety).

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the warning signs of a voice-clone scam: urgency, secrecy, money to a new account, a new number. I can check by hanging up and calling back on a number I already have. I can explain why listening harder doesn't help.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: check outside the call

It's the same idea as the last two lessons: **don't trust how it looks or sounds; check where it came from.** For a call or message, that means **hanging up and contacting the person another way** you already know is real.

Families can also agree what they'd **never** ask for by phone or text, like money to a new account.

:::keep-together

### Why it works

**Why does calling back on a number you already know work, even if the voice was perfect?**

**Answer:** The scammer can't answer Mum's real phone

## Elaborate: it happens to companies too

In 2024, CNN reported that a worker at the engineering company **Arup** in Hong Kong sent **about US$25 million** after a video call where **fake voices and images** were used to pretend to be senior staff.

Same rule, bigger stakes: **check outside the call**. Why might a company make a rule that big payments always need a second person to confirm?

:::keep-together

**In 2024, a worker at the company Arup joined a video call with what looked and sounded like senior colleagues. They were fakes, and about US$25 million was sent to scammers. Which check would have helped?**

☐ Looking harder at the faces on the video

☐ Making the call longer

☐ Confirming the request another way, with a person they could reach directly

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Confirming the request another way, with a person they could reach directly

:::

## Make: Elaborate: phishing by voice

In [Cyber safety › Spot the phish](../../../systems/cyber/phishing/), the signs were: someone pretending to be trusted, pressure, a request for money or a password, and a link or number that isn't what it seems. A voice clone is **phishing with a voice**.

Make a table: the signs of a phishing email and the signs of a voice-clone call. Which defences work for both? Which only work for one?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
