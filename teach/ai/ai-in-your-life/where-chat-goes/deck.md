# Where does your chat go?

AI in your life · What you give away

## Today

**We are learning to** what you type into an AI app travels to a company, can be kept, can be read by people, and can become training data. Follow one question, and find the settings that change it.

- I can explain where a chat with an AI app can go: sent, stored, reviewed, trained on.
- I can find and change the settings about history and training.
- I can leave out personal details a question doesn't need.

## Training

**Why does 'used for training' matter more than 'stored'?**

- Training makes the chat public on the internet
- A stored chat can be deleted, but once an AI has learned from it, it can't easily be taken back out
- It doesn't matter more

* Answer: A stored chat can be deleted, but once an AI has learned from it, it can't easily be taken back out

## Elaborate: can they change the rules quietly?

**A company wants to start training its AI on chats people already typed. It quietly updates its privacy policy. Is that enough, according to Australia's privacy regulator?**

- Yes: updating the policy is enough
- Generally not: a quiet policy update or notice isn't enough by itself
- Companies can't train AI at all

* Answer: Generally not: a quiet policy update or notice isn't enough by itself

## Make: Elaborate: a code for children

Australia is writing a **Children's Online Privacy Code**. It must be registered by **10 December 2026**. The draft says services likely to be used by children should collect only what is **strictly necessary** and give **age-appropriate** notices.

Look at Club Helper again. Which of its settings would you change for users under 18? Should "use my chats to improve the AI" start **on** or **off**? Write the notice you'd show a Year 7 student, in two sentences. (Then look up whether the code has been registered.)
