# Where did it come from?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Instead of hunting for glitches, check the source: who posted it first, whether others confirm it, and whether it carries a record of where it came from. Even then, a record isn't proof of truth.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Provenance instead of glitch-hunting: trace a made-up viral photo back to its source, check other sources, and don't share until checked. Typical: Content Credentials (optional, removable). Wider: provenance doesn't prove truth.

**Students should be able to say:** I can trace a picture or video back towards where it came from. I can explain why checking around a picture works better than looking for glitches. I can explain what Content Credentials can and can't show.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: check outside the picture

A fake can look perfect. It's much harder to fake **where it came from**: the first post, the person who took it, and other sources that agree. That's why fact-checkers **leave the picture** and check **around it**.

Until you've checked, **don't share it**.

:::keep-together

## Elaborate: a label of where it came from

**Content Credentials** (from a group called C2PA) are a record attached to a picture or video: what made it, and how it was changed. Some cameras and AI apps add them. But they're **optional**, and they can be **stripped off**, for example when a picture is screenshotted or re-uploaded.

So a credential can help, and a missing one proves nothing. Which of your four checks still work when there's no credential?

:::keep-together

**Content Credentials can be attached to a picture to record which camera or app made it and how it was edited. What's their biggest weakness?**

☐ They make pictures blurry

☐ They're optional and can be removed, so a picture without them isn't proof of anything

☐ Only AI can read them

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They're optional and can be removed, so a picture without them isn't proof of anything

:::

## Make: Elaborate: a record isn't truth

The C2PA's own explainer says **provenance information alone cannot tell you whether** content is true. A real, unedited photo, with a perfect record, can still be **misleading**: an old photo shared as new, or a real photo with a false caption.

Write an example of a **real photo with a perfect record** that still misleads. What check would catch it?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
