# Where did it come from?

AI in your life · Is it real?

<!-- Speaker note: Provenance instead of glitch-hunting: trace a made-up viral photo back to its source, check other sources, and don't share until checked. Typical: Content Credentials (optional, removable). Wider: provenance doesn't prove truth. About 35 minutes. -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** instead of hunting for glitches, check the source: who posted it first, whether others confirm it, and whether it carries a record of where it came from. Even then, a record isn't proof of truth.

- I can trace a picture or video back towards where it came from.
- I can explain why checking around a picture works better than looking for glitches.
- I can explain what Content Credentials can and can't show.

## Why it works

**Why does checking the source work better than checking the picture?**

- A fake can look perfect, but it's harder to fake where it came from and what other sources say
- Sources are always honest
- Pictures can't be faked

* Answer: A fake can look perfect, but it's harder to fake where it came from and what other sources say

## Elaborate: a label of where it came from

- They make pictures blurry
- They're optional and can be removed, so a picture without them isn't proof of anything
- Only AI can read them

## Make: Elaborate: a record isn't truth

The C2PA's own explainer says **provenance information alone cannot tell you whether** content is true. A real, unedited photo, with a perfect record, can still be **misleading**: an old photo shared as new, or a real photo with a false caption.

Write an example of a **real photo with a perfect record** that still misleads. What check would catch it?
