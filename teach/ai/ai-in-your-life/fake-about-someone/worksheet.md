# When a fake is about someone you know

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A fake picture or video of a real person can hurt them even when everyone knows it's fake. What a bystander can do: don't share, support, tell, report.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A made-up, non-sexual case: a fake video of a classmate shared in a group chat. A fake can hurt even when known to be fake; the key question is consent; bystanders don't share, support, tell and report. Typical: shared responsibility. Wider: when a fake is a crime. FLAGGED for wellbeing review.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why a fake about a real person can hurt even when people know it's fake. I can name four things a bystander can do: don't share, support, tell, report. I can say where to report a fake and where to get help.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Explain: consent

The question isn't "is it real?" but **"did they agree?"**. Using someone's face without their agreement to embarrass them is harmful, whoever made it and however it was made.

If something like this happens to you or someone you know, talk to an adult you trust, the school's wellbeing team, or **Kids Helpline (1800 55 1800)**.

:::keep-together

### Consent

**What's the key question to ask before making or sharing any picture or video with a real person's face?**

☐ Does it look real?

☐ Is it funny?

**Answer:** Did they agree to it?

## Elaborate: who is responsible?

Responsibility is **shared**: the maker, everyone who shares, and the apps that host and make these videos. Who has the most power to stop it **once it's posted**?

:::keep-together

**Who could have stopped Kai's video at each point?**

Groups: The person who made it, People who shared it, The app

- Not forwarding it to another chat: {{field-15}}

**Answer:** Deciding not to make a video of a real person without asking → The person who made it; Not forwarding it to another chat → People who shared it; Making it easy to report, and taking it down quickly → The app; Asking 'would Kai be OK with this?' first → The person who made it

## Make: Elaborate: when it's a crime

Some fakes are **crimes**. eSafety's Youth Council says **explicit deepfakes are illegal**, and they can be reported to eSafety (**esafety.gov.au/report**). Fakes used to bully, embarrass or spread lies can also be reported, to the school, the app and, for serious cases, eSafety.

Why might the law treat some fakes as crimes and leave others to schools and apps? What would you want an app to do when someone reports a fake of them?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
