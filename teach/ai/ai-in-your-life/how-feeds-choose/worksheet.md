# How does a feed choose?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Be the algorithm: a feed ranks posts by predicting what will keep you watching, from signals like watch time, likes and shares. What it counts decides what rises.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Be the algorithm: on a made-up feed, students set how much each signal counts and see posts Sam asked for less of rise when the app ranks for watching. What a feed counts decides what rises.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a feed ranks posts from signals like watch time, likes and shares. I can explain why posts I didn't ask for can rise to the top. I can tell a signal I send from the app's choice about how much it counts.

## Explain: what it counts decides what rises

A feed is a model that **predicts** what you'll engage with. What it **counts** decides what rises. If it counts watch time and arguments most, posts that make people angry can rise, even if you asked for less of them.

Real apps use **thousands of signals**, learned from what millions of people do. The idea is the same.

:::keep-together

## Make: Elaborate: what amplifies

Researchers studied Twitter's feed (2023). Ranking by engagement **amplified emotionally charged, out-group hostile content** (posts angry at "the other side") that users said they **didn't prefer**.

Why might a feed show more of what people don't want? Who gains from it, and who loses? If you designed a feed, what would you count instead, and what would you lose?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
