# Which kind of AI is it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A short recap of the four kinds of AI from AI Foundations, using the apps and tools in this course, so you know where to look when something goes wrong.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A short recap of the four kinds of AI (rules, predict or sort, make new content, take actions) using this course's cases: feeds, chatbots, fakes, voice clones.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say which kind of AI is in an everyday app. I can say where to look when each kind goes wrong.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: where it goes wrong

- **Rules:** the rule is wrong, for everyone at once.
- **Predict or sort:** wrong or missing examples, or predicting the wrong thing (what keeps you watching, not what you want).
- **Makes new content:** convincing but untrue, or a fake of a real person.
- **Takes actions:** it acts before anyone checks.

:::keep-together

### The made-up fact

**A chatbot tells you a fact that turns out to be made up. Which kind of mistake is that?**

☐ Takes actions: it acted before checking

☐ Rules: someone wrote a wrong rule

☐ Makes new content: it made something convincing that isn't true

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Makes new content: it made something convincing that isn't true

:::


:::keep-together

### The feed

**A feed keeps showing you things you said you didn't want. Where would you look?**

☐ At a rule about bedtimes

☐ At what it's predicting: what keeps people watching, not what you asked for

☐ At a picture it made

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** At what it's predicting: what keeps people watching, not what you asked for

:::
