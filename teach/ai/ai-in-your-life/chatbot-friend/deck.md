## Engage: predict

**A chatbot says: 'I missed you! How was your day?' Did it miss you?**

- Only if you chatted yesterday
- Yes: it has feelings
- No: it was designed to say things that feel friendly

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: No. It writes likely-sounding replies, and companion apps are designed to sound warm and keep you talking. It doesn't wait for you or feel anything. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Make: Elaborate: design for engagement or for wellbeing?

A US survey (2025) found **72% of US teens had used an AI companion at least once**, but it counted general chatbots too, so the number depends on the definition. Strong evidence about what companions do to under-18s is still thin.

If a company designed a chatbot for **wellbeing** instead of **engagement**, what would it do differently? (Think about the 'Help you' lines.) What rule would you want for companion apps, and who should make it?
