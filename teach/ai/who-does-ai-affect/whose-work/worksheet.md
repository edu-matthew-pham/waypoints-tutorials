# Whose work trained it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** AI that makes content learned from huge amounts of people's work. Whether that's fair, and who should be paid, is being decided right now. A few things are settled in Australia.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Copyright and AI training: a made-up student artist, two settled points in Australia (copyright needs a human author; no text-and-data-mining exception), music creators' worries and the Bartz v Anthropic settlement. The law is being decided now.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why AI that makes content raises questions about the work it learned from. I can say two things that are settled in Australia and one that is still open. I can argue both sides of whether training AI on people's work is fair.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: decided now

When something is **being decided now**, a good answer says:

- what **is** settled (in Australia: copyright needs a human author; no special training exception);
- what **isn't** (whether training on people's work is fair everywhere, and how creators get paid);
- **who decides** (courts, governments, and sometimes deals between companies and creators).

:::keep-together

## Elaborate: musicians and authors

- **Music:** in a 2024 survey by APRA AMCOS, **82%** of Australian and New Zealand music creators were worried about **no longer being able to make a living** because of AI.
- **Books:** in the US case **Bartz v Anthropic**, the judge ruled that training on books could be fair use, but **copies taken from pirate websites** were not. In July 2026 the court approved a **US$1.5 billion** settlement, about **US$3,000 per book**.

(Anthropic makes the AI Claude.) Use the lens on the authors' case: who gained, who lost, who decided?

:::keep-together

**A US court approved a US$1.5 billion settlement to authors whose books were taken from pirate websites to train an AI. The judge had said training itself could be fair use, but the pirated copies were not. What does that tell you?**

☐ How the work was obtained mattered, not only how it was used

☐ Training AI is always illegal

☐ Authors lost the case

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** How the work was obtained mattered, not only how it was used

## Make: Elaborate: is learning the same as copying?

Argument for: people learn by studying others' work too, and the AI makes something new. Argument against: an AI can learn from millions of works in days, without asking or paying, then compete with the people it learned from.

Write the strongest case for each side. Then: what should creators get (a choice, payment, credit)? Who should decide: courts, governments, or deals between companies and creators? What would change your mind?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
