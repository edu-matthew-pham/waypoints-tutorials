# Will AI take jobs?

Who Does AI Affect? · What happens next?

<!-- Speaker note: Jobs as bundles of tasks: exposed is not lost, augment versus automate, no widespread displacement yet in Australia (JSA 2025). Typical: call-centre novices gained most. Wider: the contested junior-jobs evidence. About 40 minutes. -->

---

## Today

- I can split a job into tasks and say which AI could do, help with, or not do.
- I can explain the difference between a job being exposed to AI and being lost to AI.
- I can explain augment and automate.

## Displacement

**Jobs and Skills Australia (2025) found 'no current evidence of widespread displacement in Australia'. What does that mean?**

- So far, AI hasn't replaced large numbers of workers here
- Everyone has lost their job to AI
- AI will never change jobs

## Augment

**The International Labour Organization says generative AI is more likely to augment jobs than automate them. What does 'augment' mean?**

- Replace the whole job
- Help people do their job, changing some tasks
- Make the job harder

* Answer: Help people do their job, changing some tasks

## Elaborate: who gains most?

**In a call centre, an AI helper raised workers' productivity by 14% on average, and by much more for new workers. Who gained most, and why might that be?**

- Nobody: productivity didn't change
- New workers: the AI shared tips that experienced workers already knew
- Experienced workers: they knew how to use it

* Answer: New workers: the AI shared tips that experienced workers already knew

## Make: Elaborate: the junior-jobs question

- **Stanford researchers** (revised 2026): in the US, workers **aged 22 to 25** in the jobs most exposed to AI were **about 19% below** where their employment would be expected. The authors say this **describes** a pattern; it doesn't prove AI caused it.
- **Yale Budget Lab** (2025): the US labour market **has not experienced a discernible disruption** since ChatGPT arrived.

Can both be true? (One looks at a small group, the other at the whole economy.) Why might new workers both **gain most** from AI and be **hired less**? What would you want to measure next?

## Exit ticket

**What's the difference between a job being exposed to AI and being lost to AI?**

* Exposed means AI could do or help with some tasks; lost means the job is gone. So far, AI is mostly changing tasks, not removing whole jobs.
