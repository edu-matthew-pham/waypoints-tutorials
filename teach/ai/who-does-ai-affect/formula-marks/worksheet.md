# When a formula marks you

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** In 2020 a formula changed many English students' exam grades. One rule applied to everyone can be unfair to some groups, so people need a way to appeal.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** England's 2020 A-level grades: a formula adjusted teachers' grades, relied more on teachers for small classes, lowered about 39%, and was reversed with an apology. One rule applied to everyone can be unfair to some groups.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what the 2020 exam formula did, and who gained and lost. I can explain why one rule used on everyone can go wrong for a whole group at once. I can say what automated decisions about people need: a person who checks, a fair appeal, and someone responsible.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: one rule for everyone

A rule written by people goes wrong in a special way: **it goes wrong for everyone at once.** And a group can lose out even when nobody meant it to.

That is why automated decisions about people need:

- a **person who checks**, especially when the result is surprising;
- a **way to appeal** that is quick and fair;
- someone who is **responsible** for the rule.

:::keep-together

## Elaborate: a job decided by tests

Services Australia, a government agency, used **AI-assisted and automated** tests to decide who got promoted, with **no interview panel**. People could ask for a review. The Merit Protection Commissioner **overturned 11 promotions**.

Use the lens: who decides here? What's the catch? (The report criticised the whole process, not just the AI part.)

:::keep-together

**In 2021–22, Services Australia ran a promotion round using AI-assisted and automated tests, with no interview panel. Eleven promotions were overturned on review. What does the review show?**

☐ Reviews are a waste of time

☐ The AI was always right

☐ Someone could check the decisions, and found some were wrong

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Someone could check the decisions, and found some were wrong

## Make: Elaborate: Robodebt

Australia's **Robodebt** scheme used a computer calculation to send people debt notices. The Royal Commission called it "a crude and cruel mechanism, neither fair nor legal". It used no AI: it was a rule written by people, used on many people at once.

Compare Robodebt with the 2020 exam formula. What did each get wrong? What did each need: a person who checks, a fair appeal, someone responsible? Why can "automated" be dangerous even without AI?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
