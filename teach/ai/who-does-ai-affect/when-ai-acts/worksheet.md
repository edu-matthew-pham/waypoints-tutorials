# When AI acts

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** When AI answers or acts for a company or a person, someone is still responsible, and a person should confirm anything that can't easily be undone.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Moffatt v Air Canada (a company is responsible for its chatbot's advice), then a made-up booking agent: which actions need a person to confirm first. Typical: Australian guidance on human control. Wider: lab tests versus real events.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain who is responsible when an AI answers or acts for a company. I can sort an agent's actions into 'just do it' and 'ask me first'. I can tell a lab test from a real event in a news story about AI.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: confirm before you can't undo

Two ideas:

1. **Someone is responsible.** An AI isn't a person. The company or person who uses it is responsible for what it says and does.
2. **Confirm before you can't undo.** An agent should stop and ask a person before it pays, shares details, deletes or sends something that matters.

:::keep-together

### Which steps?

**Which steps should an AI agent ask about first?**

☐ None: it should just finish the job

☐ Every step, even searching

☐ Ones that are hard or impossible to undo, like paying, sharing details or deleting

**Answer:** Ones that are hard or impossible to undo, like paying, sharing details or deleting

## Elaborate: keep a person in control

In 2025 the Australian Government's **Guidance for AI Adoption** told businesses to **decide who is accountable** and to **integrate meaningful human oversight and control** of AI.

Use the lens on an AI agent at a shop that can give refunds on its own. Who gains? Who could be harmed? Who decides what it's allowed to do?

:::keep-together

**Australian government guidance for businesses using AI says to keep meaningful human oversight and control. What would that look like for the bus-booking agent?**

☐ A person can see its plan, approve payments, and stop it

☐ The agent works faster

☐ Nobody checks it, so it saves time

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A person can see its plan, approve payments, and stop it

:::

## Make: Elaborate: headline or lab?

In 2025 an AI company published research in which AI agents, in made-up test situations, did harmful things such as threatening a fictional person. Headlines called it "rogue AI". The company's own report said the behaviours **occurred in controlled simulations**, and called one test "extremely contrived".

Why do researchers test agents in made-up situations? What's the difference between "this happened in a lab test" and "this happened to real people"? Write a headline that is true to the research.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
