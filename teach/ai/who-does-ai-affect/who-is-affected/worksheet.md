# Who is affected, and how?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Build the course's lens on a real system that helps people (AI in breast screening): who gains, who loses, who decides, and what's the catch?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students build the course lens (who gains, who loses, who decides, what's the catch) on AI breast screening in Sweden, a real benefit with a catch.

**Students should be able to say:** I can use four questions to judge any system: who gains, who loses, who decides, and what's the catch. I can say what a study about AI showed, and what it didn't. I can explain why a helpful AI still needs the question 'who was it tested on?'.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: the catch

A benefit is real, but it still has a **for whom** and a **catch**. Remember from AI Foundations: a model can work worse for people who were rare in its examples. A good question about any helpful AI is: **who was it tested on?**

:::keep-together

## Elaborate: a second case

Apps and tools that check moles for skin cancer have been tested many times. In one 2026 Australian study, **specialist doctors were more accurate than the AI**. The researchers said AI should **help** specialists, not replace them.

Use the lens: who might gain from a skin-check app? Who could be harmed if they trusted it alone?

:::keep-together

**In a 2026 study of skin-cancer checks, specialist doctors did better than the AI. The researchers said AI should complement, not replace, specialists. What does 'complement' mean here?**

☐ Replace the specialist when they are busy

☐ Help the specialist, who still makes the decision

☐ Do nothing at all

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Help the specialist, who still makes the decision

## Make: Elaborate: forecasts

The European weather centre (ECMWF) says its AI forecast model, in use since 2025, **beats its physics-based models on many measures**. Australia's Bureau of Meteorology says it does **not** use neural network systems in its forecasts.

Who gains from better, cheaper forecasts? Who decides whether a weather service uses AI? Why is it worth checking a claim like "AI now forecasts our weather" for one country before repeating it?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
