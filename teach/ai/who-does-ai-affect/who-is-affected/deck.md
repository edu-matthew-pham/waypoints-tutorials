# Who is affected, and how?

Who Does AI Affect? · Who is affected?

<!-- Speaker note: Students build the course lens (who gains, who loses, who decides, what's the catch) on AI breast screening in Sweden, a real benefit with a catch. About 35 minutes. -->

---

## Today

- I can use four questions to judge any system: who gains, who loses, who decides, and what's the catch.
- I can say what a study about AI showed, and what it didn't.
- I can explain why a helpful AI still needs the question 'who was it tested on?'.

## One study

**Why does 'one study, one country, one AI system' matter?**

- Another hospital, with other patients or another system, might not get the same result
- It means the study was wrong
- It doesn't matter: AI works the same everywhere

* Answer: Another hospital, with other patients or another system, might not get the same result

## Elaborate: a second case

**In a 2026 study of skin-cancer checks, specialist doctors did better than the AI. The researchers said AI should complement, not replace, specialists. What does 'complement' mean here?**

- Replace the specialist when they are busy
- Help the specialist, who still makes the decision
- Do nothing at all

* Answer: Help the specialist, who still makes the decision

## Make: Elaborate: forecasts

The European weather centre (ECMWF) says its AI forecast model, in use since 2025, **beats its physics-based models on many measures**. Australia's Bureau of Meteorology says it does **not** use neural network systems in its forecasts.

Who gains from better, cheaper forecasts? Who decides whether a weather service uses AI? Why is it worth checking a claim like "AI now forecasts our weather" for one country before repeating it?
