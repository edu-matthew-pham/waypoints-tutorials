# Your own case

Who Does AI Affect? · Your own case

<!-- Speaker note: The course challenge: students choose a case (made-up, real with teacher approval, or one from the course) and judge it with the kind of AI, the lens, the course's questions, one rule, and (wider) a verdict with the other side. About 50 minutes. -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** choose a case and judge it with everything from the course: which kind of AI it is, who gains, who loses, who decides, what's the catch, and one rule you'd make.

- I can judge a new AI system with the course's questions: which kind, who gains, who loses, who decides, and what's the catch.
- I can link a worry about a system to the right question from the course.
- I can make one rule for a system and say who checks it.

## Explain: what the course taught you to ask

**Which lesson's question fits each worry about a case?**

Groups: Who decides? (one rule for everyone, appeals) · Learned from the past (bias) · When AI acts (confirm first) · Who pays the cost? (energy, labour, creators)

The AI marks every student with the same rule, and there's no way to ask a teacher · The library AI was trained on what past students borrowed, mostly from one group · The refund chatbot sends money before anyone checks · Nobody knows who labelled the photos the study-notes app learned from

## Evaluate: your rule

Finish with **one rule** for your case, and who checks it's working (lesson 10). Share your lens with a partner: did they find a gain, a loss or a catch you missed?

Look back at your prediction: which question was hardest?

**Course complete.** Every new AI system you meet, ask: which kind is it, who gains, who loses, who decides, and what's the catch?
