# Does it help learning?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A company pitches StudyPal, a made-up AI study app, to the school. What problem would it solve, what kind of AI is it, and does its evidence show learning? Plan a small fair trial.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students read StudyPal's (made-up) pitch and its one small study like a committee: name the problem, the kind of AI and what the evidence does and doesn't show, then plan a small fair trial measuring learning without the tool, later.

**Students should be able to say:** I can ask what problem a tool solves before asking if it's good. I can tell evidence that a task got done from evidence of learning. I can plan a small fair trial: compared with what, measured when, without the tool.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: four questions before trust

Before anything else, a committee asks:

1. **What problem are we solving?** Name it first. A tool looking for a problem is a warning sign.
2. **What kind of AI is it?** StudyPal **makes new content** (it writes answers). That tells you how it can go wrong: made-up steps that look right.
3. **Does the evidence show learning?** Compared with what? Measured when, and without the tool? Who chose to use it?
4. **What would a small fair trial look like?** If the evidence is thin, a trial can find out.

Test the answers against the charter, starting with **learning first**.

:::keep-together

## Elaborate: hint mode

A tool can be a poor fit as it comes, and a better fit with a different setting. Ask:

- Can the **school** switch it on, or only each student?
- Does it **stay** on?
- Has anyone tested hint mode, or only answer mode?

:::keep-together

**StudyPal has a setting, off by default: 'Hint mode: give a hint, not the answer.' How does that change the decision?**

☐ It may fit 'learning first' better, if the school can switch it on for every student and check it stays on

☐ No difference: it's the same app

☐ It makes the app perfect

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It may fit 'learning first' better, if the school can switch it on for every student and check it stays on

:::

## Make: Elaborate: who chose to use it?

In StudyPal's study, students **chose** whether to use the app. Students who choose a study app may already be the ones who do their homework carefully, so they'd score higher with or without it.

A fairer study decides **who uses it before anyone starts** (for example, by class or at random), measures learning **without the tool**, and checks again **later**. Does AI Help Us Learn? looks at this in [Are researchers asking the right questions?](../../does-ai-help-us-learn/research-design/)

Look at an education product's claims in an ad or on a website (your teacher may give you one). Which of these questions does its evidence answer?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
