# Recommend

## Today

**We are learning to** put the evidence and the fine print together: adopt, trial, change something first, or don't use it. Write a recommendation with conditions, checked against each charter principle, with what would change your mind.

- I can choose between adopt, trial, change something first and don't use it, and give reasons.
- I can write conditions and say what would change our mind.
- I can check a recommendation against every principle in our charter.

## Make: Elaborate: compared with what else?

A committee shouldn't only ask "this tool or nothing?". Compare it with other ways to solve the same problem:

- a teacher's hint sheet or a homework help session;
- a general AI assistant the school already allows, given a careful prompt to give hints, not answers;
- a different tool.

What does StudyPal add that the alternatives don't? Is it worth its data, cost and age limits? Teachers' guide: [Choosing AI tools for learning](../../../choosing-ai-tools/) compares dedicated tools with a well-prompted general assistant in the same way.

**Why should a recommendation say what would change the committee's mind?**
