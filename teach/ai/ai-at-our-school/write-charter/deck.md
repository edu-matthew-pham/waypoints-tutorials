**We are learning to** critique a made-up school's draft charter, then write your committee's own: five to seven principles, each with how you'd check it, dated and ready to share for comment.

**Should a school's AI charter be the same for every year level, or have different rules for younger students?**

- What's the cost of having lots of different rules?

## Dates and reviews

**Why should a charter say who wrote it, the date, and when it will be reviewed?**

- AI changes fast, so readers need to know how current it is and when it will be checked
- So the committee gets credit
- Charters must have dates by law

* Answer: AI changes fast, so readers need to know how current it is and when it will be checked

## Make: Elaborate: when principles clash

Principles can clash. An AI feature that reads text aloud helps **fair for everyone** (students who find reading hard can take part) but may send student work to a company, which tests **our data stays ours**.

Some committees **order** their principles (learning first, then safety, then the rest). Others **weigh each case** and write down why. Which would you choose? What are the risks of each?
