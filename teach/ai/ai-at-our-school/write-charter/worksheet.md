# Write the charter

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Critique a made-up school's draft charter, then write your committee's own: five to seven principles, each with how you'd check it, dated and ready to share for comment.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students critique Westbrook's flawed first-draft charter (vague values, a rule about one tool, a clash), write their own five to seven checkable principles, and learn what makes a charter usable: short, checkable, principles not decisions, dated, reviewed and shared for comment.

**Students should be able to say:** I can spot what's wrong with a draft charter. I can write a charter of five to seven principles, each with a way to check it. I can explain why a charter is dated, reviewed and shared for comment.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: a charter people can use

A usable charter:

1. has **five to seven** principles, short enough to remember;
2. gives each a way to **check it's working** for the school (not a way to check up on each student);
3. contains **principles, not decisions** about particular tools;
4. says **who wrote it, the date, and when it will be reviewed**;
5. is **shared for comment** with the people it affects before it's final.

:::keep-together

## Make: Elaborate: when principles clash

Principles can clash. An AI feature that reads text aloud helps **fair for everyone** (students who find reading hard can take part) but may send student work to a company, which tests **our data stays ours**.

Some committees **order** their principles (learning first, then safety, then the rest). Others **weigh each case** and write down why. Which would you choose? What are the risks of each?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
