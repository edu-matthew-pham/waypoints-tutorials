## Today

- I can check a tool's data, defaults and age terms against our charter.

## Think, pair, share

**If a tool helps learning a lot, is it OK if it also uses students' chats to train its AI?**

- Who gains from the training? Who decides?
- Would it change your mind if the school could switch it off?

## Engage: predict

## Partners

**StudyPal's page says 'We may share data with our partners' but doesn't say who they are. What should the committee write?**

- Partners are always safe
- Unknown: we don't know who gets student data. Ask the company before any use
- Fine: 'may' means they probably won't

* Answer: Unknown: we don't know who gets student data. Ask the company before any use

## Make: Elaborate: the privacy principles

In Australia, many organisations that handle personal information must follow the **Australian Privacy Principles**: for example, telling people what they collect and why, using it only for that purpose, keeping it secure, and letting people see it. Digital systems › Cyber safety teaches them in [The Australian Privacy Principles](../../../systems/cyber/privacy/).

Check StudyPal's short privacy page against two of them. Which questions would the school need answered before any student uses it? Then look at a real school app's privacy page (your teacher may give you one, dated) the same way.
