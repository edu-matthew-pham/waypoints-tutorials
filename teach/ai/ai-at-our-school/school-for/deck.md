## Make: Elaborate: help that turns into a crutch

Researchers have found that support which helps beginners (worked examples, step-by-step help) can **get in the way** for students who already know the skill. They call it the **expertise reversal effect**. AI support is a kind of help, so the same may apply.

What does that mean for a school rule that is the same for Year 7 and Year 12? Should support be **switched down** as students get better? Who would decide when?

<!-- Speaker note: For students who need more support: Give two columns first (help / keep the thinking), then add 'depends' once they've sorted three. For early finishers: Take one 'depends' task and write two rules for it: one for Year 7, one for Year 10. The hard part, on purpose: Students want a single rule ('AI is cheating' or 'AI is fine'). Let them argue the 'depends' items; don't settle them for the class. When students get stuck: A student puts everything in 'keep the thinking'. Ask: What is the spelling check for, in a science poster? Fix: Ask what the task is meant to teach; tasks that aren't the learning can take help. / A student says AI is fine because 'you still learn from reading it'. Ask: Could you do the next one without it? Fix: That's the test: learning shows up later, without the help. -->
