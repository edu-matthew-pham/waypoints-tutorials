# Keep it under review

AI at our school · When AI is already here

## Today

**We are learning to** aI changes faster than any policy. Decide who notices new AI, how students report problems, what triggers an early review, and when the charter is revisited. Then finish the response plan.

- I can say who should notice new AI and how students can report problems safely.
- I can tell which changes need an early review and which can wait.
- I can write a review cycle with a date, and link it to the school's own policy review.
- I can tell a rule problem from a know-how problem.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **Core idea:** decisions about AI are dated and reviewed; a review cycle says who notices, how problems are reported (simply, without blame), what triggers an early review, when everything is revisited, and whether people can apply the rules. Committees tend to answer every problem with a new rule; the rule vs know-how sort is there to stop that. Test: give the case to whoever knows the rule best; if they still can't apply it, fix the rule; if they can and others can't, it's know-how (guidance, worked examples, a briefing, a lesson for students). The committee notices know-how gaps; it doesn't run staff training itself. The safety-report item in the sort says to follow the school's wellbeing steps too: if a student raises a real incident in class, follow your school's procedures, not the committee's. **For the class plan:** find out when the school's AI policy (or the policy it sits in) is next reviewed, and who leads it. The committee's products go there, dated, after lesson 11 refines the policy. The national framework's yearly review is sourced in Who Does AI Affect? › Making rules (checked 18 Oct 2026). Re-check each January. Teacher: Evaluate: finish the response plan. -->

## Rule or know-how?

**A rule problem means the rule itself is wrong, missing or contradicts another. A know-how problem means the rule is fine, but people don't yet know how to apply it. Which is each?**

Groups: Rule problem: fix the rule · Know-how problem: give guidance or examples

The integrity policy says 'no AI in assessed work'; the AI statement says 'every task says how much AI is allowed' · Teachers set AI levels for tasks very differently from each other · No policy says whether staff may put student work into an AI tool · Students don't say when they used AI, because they don't know what to write · A teacher accepts an online quiz as evidence without knowing AI could finish it

* Answer: The integrity policy says 'no AI in assessed work'; the AI statement says 'every task says how much AI is allowed' → Rule problem: fix the rule; Teachers set AI levels for tasks very differently from each other → Know-how problem: give guidance or examples; No policy says whether staff may put student work into an AI tool → Rule problem: fix the rule; Students don't say when they used AI, because they don't know what to write → Know-how problem: give guidance or examples; A teacher accepts an online quiz as evidence without knowing AI could finish it → Know-how problem: give guidance or examples

## Make: Elaborate: rules that review themselves

Australia's framework for generative AI in schools is **reviewed every year** (*Who Does AI Affect?* looks at it in [Making rules](../../who-does-ai-affect/making-rules/)). A school's own policies usually have review dates too.

When is your school's AI policy next reviewed? How would your committee's charter feed into it? Who on staff would you send it to, and when?
