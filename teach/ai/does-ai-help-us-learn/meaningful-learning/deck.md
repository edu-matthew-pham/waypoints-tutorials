# What makes learning meaningful?

Does AI Help Us Learn? · What does it mean to learn?

<!-- Speaker note: Use perspective-taking and dialectical dialogue to explore independent competence, sociocultural participation, creativity, productivity and agency as educational aims. About 40 minutes. -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** meet four learners, sort what we can and can't tell about their learning, and compare different views of what learning is worth.

- I can describe more than one account of meaningful learning.
- I can identify what a scenario demonstrates and what remains unknown.
- I can develop and challenge an argument about what education should value.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **Core idea:** There are different kinds of learning, and each kind of evidence shows some and misses some. ## Think, pair, share

**Should school care most about what you can do alone, or what you can do with people and tools?**

- Which would matter more in a job? In an exam? In a community project?
- What would we lose if school only valued one of them?

## Four learners, four corners <span style="font-size:.6em;color:#64748b">(5 min)</span>

Go to the corner of the learner whose learning you value most: Maya, Jules, Aria or Sam. In your corner, agree on one reason and one thing you still don't know about that learner.

<!-- Speaker note: Sam's corner may be empty; ask what Sam might have learned anyway, and what evidence would tell us. -->

## Make: Elaborate: argue both sides

In pairs, look at this claim:

**"Schools should care most about what students can do alone, even when tools could help them do more."**

One person argues for it. The other argues against it. Then swap. Use Maya, Jules, Aria and Sam as examples. Ask your partner one honest question before you answer them.

Then talk about:

- What could we lose if school only valued what you can make with tools?
- What could we lose if school only valued what you can do alone?
- Who decides which learning counts?

**Inquiry record (optional):** write one argument that got stronger as you talked, and one question you still can't answer.

<!-- Speaker note: For students who need more support: Allow students to select only two learners and give argument sentence starters: 'This suggests...', 'However...', 'We cannot yet tell...'. For early finishers: Introduce an equity or institutional question: whose achievements do our assessment systems make visible, and whose might they miss? The hard part, on purpose: Students may assume that valuing sociocultural learning means abandoning individual knowledge, or that valuing independent capability means rejecting collaboration. When students get stuck: A student treats the perspectives as mutually exclusive. Ask: Could someone become more independently capable through collaboration? Fix: Revisit Aria and Maya; separate the way learning happens from the capabilities later demonstrated. / A student claims Sam learned nothing because AI wrote the report. Ask: What evidence is missing, and what might Sam conceivably have learned? Fix: Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence. -->

## Evaluate: compare two learners

Choose **two** of the four learners. Write what each one may have learned, what evidence shows it, and what you still don't know. If you think one learned something more valuable, say why.

**End of Unit 1:** go back to the question *Does AI help us learn?* What do you now need to say about the word *learn* before you can answer it?

**Next unit:** researchers make claims about AI and learning. How do we know if the evidence backs them up?

## Exit ticket

**Why might reasonable people disagree about what constitutes meaningful learning?**

* They may value different aims such as individual capability, creative agency, productive achievement or participation; different evidence is needed for each.
