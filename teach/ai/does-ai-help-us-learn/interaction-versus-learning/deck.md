# Does more interaction mean better learning?

Does AI Help Us Learn? · When should we use AI?

<!-- Speaker note: Students answer one negative-number question, compare four kinds of help with it, then tell visible activity apart from thinking, responsiveness and evidence of learning. About 40 minutes. -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** try one maths question, compare four kinds of help with it, and tell clicking from thinking and from learning.

- I can tell the difference between what a learner does (clicks, answers) and what they think about.
- I can explain how different kinds of help respond to a learner, with or without AI.
- I can say what evidence would show that someone learned.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **Core idea:** Being busy is not the same as learning: what counts is the thinking the help makes you do. The minimum path is one maths question and four kinds of help; changing the learner is typical. All four kinds of help are made up, not captured from real apps or a real AI conversation. The student text names no products. If you want real examples, choose ones your students know (quiz games, maths practice sites, tutorial platforms); their features and AI use change often, so treat them as examples, not categories or endorsements. No account or platform is needed. Research words behind the four dimensions: behavioural interaction (what you do), cognitive engagement (what you think about), instructional responsiveness (how the help responds), and evidence of learning. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Which helps learning more: twenty energetic quiz responses or one carefully explained worked example?**

- Which learner and learning goal?
- What thinking happens during each?
- What would you check later?

<!-- Speaker note: Do not rank the tools in advance. Quiz practice can be right for remembering; being busy is not proof of learning. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Engage: predict

**One learner answers twenty quick quiz questions. Another explains one worked example. Who learns more?**

- The explaining learner: deeper thinking
- The quiz learner: more practice
- It depends on what each needs to learn and what we check afterwards

## Jo and Ana

**Jo answered 40 quiz questions in ten minutes, mostly by guessing. Ana answered 5 and explained each one. Which tells you more about what they understand?**

- Ana's explanations
- Neither: you can only tell from a test
- Jo's 40 answers

## What AI adds

**What can AI chat add that the others can't?**

- Proof that you'll remember it later
- Explanations that are always correct
- An answer to a question nobody prepared for, though that answer can be wrong

## Make: Elaborate: change the learner

Two learners need help with negative numbers:

- **Learner 1** already understands why, but needs to get quicker.
- **Learner 2** keeps using the wrong sign rule with confidence, and needs to see why it works.

Which kind of help (A, B, C or D) would you choose for each? What if the AI's explanation is wrong? What if a non-AI tutorial already fixes the mistake?

Think of a learning app or game you have used. What does it make you do: click, think, or both? How does it respond to your mistakes?

**Can a worked example on paper sometimes be better than an interactive quiz?**

* Yes. It depends on the goal, the learner and how learning is checked afterwards.
