# Is doing well the same as learning?

Does AI Help Us Learn? · What does it mean to learn?

<!-- Speaker note: Students try a question with help and a new one without, then sort evidence into doing with help, using it on something new, and remembering it later. About 35 minutes. -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** try a question with help, then a new one without, and compare what each attempt shows about learning.

- I can explain the difference between doing a task with help, using an idea on something new, and remembering it later.
- I can say what a right answer with help does and does not show.
- I can suggest a better way to check learning and say what it would still miss.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: **Core idea:** Doing well with help is not the same as being able to do it alone, on something new, or later. The minimum path is the student's own try with help, then without; the fair-test discussion is typical. Teaching move: experience the contrast first, then question the measurement itself. The board is the offline AI Foundations practice board: prepared hints and model answers, not a live model or a tested AI tutor. The one-board activity is a classroom illustration, not a controlled study. The new question is a little different from the first, so difficulty changes as well as help. A student who struggles may still have learned something; a correct answer does not prove lasting understanding. If the arithmetic gets in the way, a paper version works: a worked example for one third of 12, then a fresh question such as two fifths of 15, written with "how I know". Terms used for students: transfer ("use it on something new") and retention ("still know it later"). -->

## Think, pair, share

**If you get the right answer with AI's help, have you learned it?**

- What would you need to see tomorrow, without help, to be sure?
- Can you learn from help even if you can't repeat the task yet?

## Mia's two tries

**Mia gets 1/2 + 1/4 right while looking at the worked example. The next day she gets 1/3 + 1/6 wrong without it. What does this show most clearly?**

## Leo's check

**Leo got the new question right today. Which extra check would tell you most about whether he will still remember the method?**

- Giving him the same question again straight away
- A similar question in two weeks, without help
- Asking him how sure he feels today

## Make: Elaborate: is this test fair?

Discuss this claim:

**"If you can't do it again without help, the help didn't teach you anything."**

Give the best reason **for** it. Then give the best reason **against** it. Think about two students: one learning to add fractions, and one using a game maker to try out an idea.

Then plan a better check for one student. What would you look at today, next week, and in a new situation? What would your check still miss?

**Inquiry record (optional):** look at your first answer from lesson 1. Add one example where help hid a gap, and one where help led to real understanding.

## Exit ticket

**Why might a right answer with AI's help not be enough evidence that you learned it?**

* It shows what you could do with help. A new question shows whether you can use it, and a later question shows whether you remember it.
