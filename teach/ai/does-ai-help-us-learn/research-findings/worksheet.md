# Does research show that AI improves learning?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Compare two real studies with different results, find what each one compared, and spot headlines that go too far.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sort facts from two real studies, then learn to ask what was compared, what was measured, and whether a claim fits.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what two real studies of AI and learning compared and measured. I can spot which part of a headline goes further than a study shows. I can explain why 'not clearly different' is not the same as 'exactly equal'.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: read a claim carefully

When you read a claim about a study, ask three questions.

1. **Compared with what?** Better than no help? Better than books? Better than a good teacher?
2. **Measured how?** A score while practising, a test straight after, or a test weeks later without help?
3. **Does the claim fit?** A headline can say more than the study showed.

:::keep-together

### Not clearly different

**In Study A, the AI tutor group's later score was not clearly different from the books-and-notes group. What can we say?**

☐ The AI tutor harmed learning

☐ We can't tell them apart in this study; a small difference might still exist

☐ The AI tutor and books and notes work exactly as well as each other

### The headline

**A newspaper says: 'AI tutors beat teachers for good, study finds.' It is about Study B. Which part goes furthest beyond what the study found?**

☐ 'study finds': it was a real study

☐ 'AI tutors': the study used an AI tutor

☐ 'for good': the study only tested straight after each topic

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 'for good': the study only tested straight after each topic

## Make: Elaborate: two careful claims

Look at these two claims:

- "Study A is a good reason to be careful about plain AI chatbots during practice."
- "Study B is a good reason to keep testing well-designed AI tutors."

Could both be fair? Which bigger claims would **not** follow? Neither study compared AI with a really good non-AI computer tutor. Does that change how sure we should be?

**Inquiry record (optional):** write a headline for one study that is true and not misleading. Then name one limit you kept in view.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
