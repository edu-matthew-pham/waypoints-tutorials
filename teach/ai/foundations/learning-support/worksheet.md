# Ask for the right kind of help

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Compare hints, worked examples and independent attempts to select assistance that develops understanding.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Choose among hints, worked examples and independent practice based on the learning goal.

**Students should be able to say:** I can select assistance that leaves appropriate thinking for me to practise. I can check learning by completing a related task independently.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

:::keep-together

☐ You understood the worked example while reading it

☐ You get the new question, 1/3 + 1/6, right without looking back

**Answer:** You get the new question, 1/3 + 1/6, right without looking back

## Make: Elaborate: design a helpful request

Choose something you are currently learning. Write three possible AI requests: **a hint**, **feedback on your attempt** and **a worked example**. Decide when each might be useful. Then write a fourth request that asks for a new question so you can test whether you learned the skill.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?

{{box-4}}

:::


:::wider

## Evaluate: decide independently

Without the board, explain the difference between *completing a task* and *learning to complete that kind of task*. Include one example of appropriate assistance.

:::


:::wider

## Learning with AI: keep the learner active

Before asking AI for help, consider what you already know and what you need to practise. A useful assistant may give a question or feedback rather than the answer. But requiring struggle without support is not the goal either. Adjust the assistance as your understanding changes.

:::
