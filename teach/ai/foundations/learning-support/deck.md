## Today

**We are learning to** compare hints, worked examples and independent attempts to select assistance that develops understanding.

- I can check learning by completing a related task independently.

## Make: Elaborate: design a helpful request

Choose something you are currently learning. Write three possible AI requests: **a hint**, **feedback on your attempt** and **a worked example**. Decide when each might be useful. Then write a fourth request that asks for a new question so you can test whether you learned the skill.

<!-- Speaker note: For students who need more support: Model one run aloud: attempt, one hint, attempt, new question. For early finishers: Apply the decision to a different subject or to performance rather than learning mode. The hard part, on purpose: Students may think every finished example is harmful, or that looking at an answer proves learning. When students get stuck: A student goes straight to the worked example every time. Ask: What could you try before looking? Which part are you stuck on? Fix: Ask for one hint, write the next step, then decide if you need more. -->

## Evaluate: decide independently

Without the board, explain the difference between *completing a task* and *learning to complete that kind of task*. Include one example of appropriate assistance.

---

## Learning with AI: keep the learner active

Before asking AI for help, consider what you already know and what you need to practise. A useful assistant may give a question or feedback rather than the answer. But requiring struggle without support is not the goal either. Adjust the assistance as your understanding changes.

## Exit ticket

**You used the worked example. How can you tell whether you learned the method?**

* Try a new question without looking back. If you can do it, the example helped you learn; if not, you followed it without learning it yet.
