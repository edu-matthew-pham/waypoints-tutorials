# Check what AI tells you

AI Foundations · Getting good help from AI

<!-- Speaker note: Distinguish a plausible AI claim from a claim justified by independent evidence. About 30 minutes. -->

---

## Today

**We are learning to** compare confident claims with independent evidence and distinguish supported, contradicted and insufficiently evidenced statements.

- I can compare a claim with independent evidence rather than relying on confidence.
- I can explain the limits of a small dataset and identify missing evidence.

## Identify the claim

**Claim 1 says every eight-hour seedling grew more than every four-hour seedling. Which measurement shows it is wrong?**

- The eight-hour average was higher
- One eight-hour seedling grew 6 cm
- A four-hour seedling grew 5 cm, more than two of the eight-hour seedlings

* Answer: A four-hour seedling grew 5 cm, more than two of the eight-hour seedlings

## Examine evidence

**A dataset records plant height but not plant health. Can it confirm that one group is healthier?**

- Yes, if AI says so
- No, health needs relevant evidence beyond these heights
- Yes, height always equals health

* Answer: No, health needs relevant evidence beyond these heights

## Make: Elaborate: investigate a new claim

Create a short table of fictional observations (for example, time spent practising and quiz results). Write one claim that the table supports and one that it does **not** justify. Swap with a partner, who must explain what the evidence can and cannot establish. For claims about the real world, use appropriate trustworthy sources rather than invented data.

When students get stuck: A student marks a claim supported because it sounds reasonable. Ask: Which numbers in the record back it up? ## Evaluate: decide independently

Without using the board, explain why *supported by these observations* is different from *always true*. Identify one way to verify a claim independently.

---

## Learning with AI: independent checking matters

An AI can help you identify questions to investigate, but **asking the same model to confirm itself is not independent verification**. For claims that matter, look at the original evidence or a source you trust. Even apparently credible citations may be missing, irrelevant or misrepresented.

---

## Optional: try it with an AI tool

Pick a claim you have read or heard, from an AI, a website or a friend, and use the prompt below. Compare the reply with the board: did it help you find a check of your own, or just tell you what to believe? Asking the same AI whether it was right is not checking.

**No AI tool?** Do the same with a partner: one asks the questions, the other answers.

## Exit ticket

**A dataset records plant height but not plant health. Can it support 'the eight-hour plants were healthier'?**

* No. Height is not health; you would need measurements of health to support that claim.
