# Decide what to hand over

AI Foundations · Getting good help from AI

<!-- Speaker note: Make situational decisions about independent work, AI assistance and delegated execution. About 30 minutes. -->

---

## Today

- I can distinguish task completion from developing my own capability.
- I can decide what to delegate and describe how I will check its output.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Avoid treating delegation as inherently bad. The same task may be appropriate to delegate during performance but important to practise during learning. Follow school expectations for independent work and assessment. The core board is offline and requires no AI account. These habits apply to your own AI use too. Keep the parts you need to understand or control. The optional AI-tool step (typical width) is never needed to finish, and every check can be done without AI. Students use an AI tool only if the school allows it and you say so; check the tool's terms first, since many require users to be 13 or older. The step gives a no-AI version (a partner or a table). -->

## Think, pair, share

**Is it cheating to let AI do part of your work?**

- Does it depend on which part?
- Does it depend on what the task is for?

<!-- Speaker note: Let students disagree. The lesson's answer is that it depends on the goal: handing over the part you are meant to learn loses the learning; handing over checked busywork may not. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Engage: predict

**You have already verified a list of dates and need to format it as a table. What might be sensible?**

- Never use tools for formatting
- Ask AI to invent missing dates and trust them
- Ask AI to format the list, then check that each date is unchanged

## Purpose

**On the board, the timetable dates were already checked. Why is formatting them a reasonable job to hand over?**

- Formatting is too boring to do yourself
- Formatting isn't the skill you're learning, and you can check the dates didn't change
- AI never makes mistakes with dates

* Answer: Formatting isn't the skill you're learning, and you can check the dates didn't change

## Responsibility

**On the board, your goal is to understand controlled variables. Which choice keeps the thinking you need?**

- Ask AI to write the explanation to hand in

* Answer: Write your own explanation, then ask for feedback

## Make: Elaborate: design your own division of work

Choose an authentic school task. Divide it into **thinking you need to learn**, **assistance you could request** and **execution that may be safely delegated**. Explain how you will check anything produced by AI. Then consider how your choices would change if this were a timed performance task rather than a practice activity.

## Evaluate: decide independently

Without the board, describe one situation where delegation helps and another where it might undermine a specific learning goal. Explain the difference.

---

## Learning with AI: take responsibility for the outcome

Self-regulation is not simply doing everything alone. It is deciding when to attempt, when to ask for help and when to delegate. Even if AI performs some work, **you remain responsible for deciding whether the result is appropriate and sufficiently checked**.
