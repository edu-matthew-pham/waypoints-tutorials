# What did the AI say?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You ask a chatbot when the Riverbend library opens on Saturday. It answers at once, sounding sure. Is that an answer, or a lead?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students treat an AI answer as a source with no author: they sort scripted replies into ones to check and ones to judge for themselves, choose an independent check, find the mistake of asking the AI to check itself, and open a named source rather than trusting it.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why an AI answer is a source to check, not a check. I can check an AI fact in a place that would know. I can explain why asking the same AI again is not checking.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: check it like any source


:::keep-together

### Check the sprouting

**A scripted reply says bean seeds sprout in about 2 days. What is the best way to check it?**

☐ Ask a different chatbot

☐ Look it up in a place that would know, such as a garden club guide or a science book

☐ Trust it: it gave a number

☐ Ask the AI: Are you sure?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Look it up in a place that would know, such as a garden club guide or a science book

Leave the reply and find a place that would know. Asking an AI again is not an independent check.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Eli's mistake

**Eli's report has a made-up quote in it. Click the line where Eli first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Eli asks a chatbot for a famous quote about science for his report.
 2  It gives him a quote and the name of a famous scientist.
 3  He asks the same chatbot if the quote is real, and it says yes, so he counts that as checking it.
 4  He puts the quote in his report with the scientist's name.
 5  His teacher cannot find the quote anywhere.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Eli asked the same AI whether the quote was real and took its 'yes' as a check. The AI cannot check itself. He needed to find where the scientist actually said it.

:::

## Elaborate: a reply with a source


:::keep-together

**A scripted reply says: According to riverbend-council.example, the pool opens on 1 December. What should you do?**

☐ Ask the AI for a second source

☐ Ignore it: AI can't name sources

☐ Trust it: it named a source

☐ Open the council's page and see if it really says that

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Open the council's page and see if it really says that

A named source is something you can check. Open it: it might not exist, or it might say something else.
