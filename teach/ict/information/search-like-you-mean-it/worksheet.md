# Search like you mean it

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You want to know when the Riverbend library opens on Saturday. You type the whole question and click the first result. What could go wrong?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students turn a question into a few strong search words (the thing, the place, the detail) and read a results page before clicking: adverts, dates, addresses and snippets. Every result is made up, with .example addresses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can turn a question into a few strong search words. I can find the advert, the date and the address on a results page before I click. I can say why the top result is not always the best one.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**You want to know when the Riverbend library opens on Saturday. Which search would you type?**

☐ when does the library open

☐ library

☐ can you please tell me what time the library in Riverbend opens on Saturday morning

☐ Riverbend library Saturday hours

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Riverbend library Saturday hours. It has the thing (library), the place (Riverbend) and the detail (Saturday hours). 'When does the library open' has no place, so it could find any library. One word finds far too much. A long sentence works in many search engines, but the extra words can pull in pages that only match 'please' and 'morning'.

:::

## Explain: what a results page tells you


:::keep-together

### What Ad means

**What does the word Ad on a result tell you?**

☐ It is a news story

☐ Someone paid to put it there, so its place says nothing about how good it is

☐ The search engine checked it

☐ It is the best answer

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Someone paid to put it there, so its place says nothing about how good it is

An advert is paid for. It may be useful, but its place at the top is bought.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Leo's mistake

**Leo turns up at the library and it is closed. Click the line where Leo first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Leo wants to know when the Riverbend library opens on Saturday.
 2  He types Riverbend library Saturday hours.
 3  He clicks the first result, a 2019 news story, without reading its date or address.
 4  It says the library opens on Saturday afternoons, so he goes at 2 pm.
 5  The library is closed.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Leo clicked the first result without reading its date or address. Being first is not a check. The library's own page, further down, had this month's hours.

## Elaborate: make the words stronger


:::keep-together

### The pool

**You want to know if the Riverbend pool is open in winter. Which search is strongest?**

☐ Riverbend pool winter opening

☐ pool

☐ swimming fun Riverbend best pool ever

☐ is it open

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Riverbend pool winter opening

The thing (pool), the place (Riverbend) and the detail (winter opening).

:::


:::keep-together

### Nothing useful

**Your search for Riverbend netball training times finds nothing useful. What is a good next move?**

☐ Type the same words again

☐ Give up: it is not online

☐ Click the adverts

☐ Change one word, such as training to practice, or add the club name

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Change one word, such as training to practice, or add the club name

Different pages use different words. Swap one word, or add a name, and search again.
