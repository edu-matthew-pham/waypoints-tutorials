# Pictures out of place

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A post shows a crowd and says hundreds queued for the new skate park. The picture is real. Can the post still be wrong?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that real pictures mislead through a new caption, an old date or a crop, sort three made-up posts by how they mislead, and write a source line (who, where, when) for a picture.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a real picture can mislead through its caption, date or crop. I can ask where a picture first appeared, who took it and when. I can decide what to do before sharing a surprising picture.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: trace it back


:::keep-together

### The first question

**What is the best first question to ask about a surprising picture?**

☐ Where did it first appear, and when?

☐ Is it in colour?

☐ How many people have shared it?

☐ Is it a big picture?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Where did it first appear, and when?

Where it first appeared and when tells you what it really shows. A reverse image search can help find that on a real device.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Noor's mistake

**Noor shares a post that turns out to be wrong. Click the line where Noor first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Noor sees a post: a picture of a crowd with the caption Hundreds queue for the new skate park!
 2  She decides it is true because the picture looks real.
 3  She shares it with her class group.
 4  Later someone finds the picture first appeared in 2019, at a school fair.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Noor decided the post was true because the picture looked real. A real picture can carry a wrong caption, an old date or a crop. She needed to ask where it first appeared.

:::

## Elaborate: the crop


:::keep-together

**A wide photo shows one person waving a flag at an empty sports ground. A post crops it close to the person and the flag and writes Riverbend fans pack the ground! What did the crop hide?**

☐ The colour of the flag

☐ The person's face

☐ That the ground was empty

☐ Nothing important

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** That the ground was empty

Cutting off the empty seats changed the story from one fan to a packed ground. Ask what was outside the frame.

:::
