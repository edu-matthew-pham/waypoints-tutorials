# Who made it, and why?

Investigating and judging information · Who made it?

<!-- Speaker note: Students find who made a page, why (to inform, sell, persuade or entertain) and when, by sorting six made-up pages by purpose, finding a planted mistake and deciding when a date matters. About 30 minutes (Engage and Explore 8 min · Explain 6 min · Elaborate and Practise 8 min · discussion and reflection 8 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can find who made a page, and say why they made it.
- I can find a page's date and say when it matters.
- I can explain why a page that sells or persuades is read with care.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: every page has a maker, a purpose and a date. Purpose is not a verdict: a page that sells can be right, and a page that informs can be wrong. Every page here is made up (.example addresses). The sort on the page uses clear cases only. Pages with two purposes (a review site that earns money when you buy, the bike shop's safety tips) are for discussion: accept either purpose when the student names the clue on the page. Contested topics: if a student names a real page or a real argument, do not judge the topic. Ask the three questions about it instead: who made it, why, when? The method is the lesson. Years 5 to 6: the floor and Practise. Years 7 to 8: add the wider step (?width=wider). -->

---

## Why it matters

> Two pages both say beans grow best in full sun. One is from a garden club, the other from a shop selling grow lamps. Does it matter who said it?

<!-- Speaker note: Every page was made by someone, for a reason. Before you use a page, find three things: - **Who** made it: the address, a name on the page, or its About page. - **Why** they made it: to **inform** (tell you facts), to **sell** (get you to buy), to **persuade** (change what you think or do) or to **entertain** (make you laugh or enjoy it). Many pages do more than one. - **When**: the date it was written or last updated. A page that sells or persuades can still be right. You read it knowing what it wants from you. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Is a page that wants to sell you something always less trustworthy than one that doesn't?**

- Can you think of a time a shop gave you good advice?
- Can a page with nothing to sell still be wrong?

<!-- Speaker note: Purpose is a clue about what to check, not a verdict. A shop's advice can be right, and a page with no adverts can be careless or out of date. Bring it back to: what would you check, and where? Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Engage: predict

**Two pages both say beans grow best in full sun. One is from a garden club. The other is from a shop selling grow lamps. Which would you trust more on this?**

- The shop: it sells garden things, so it knows
- They are the same: the words are the same
- I would need to know more about both
- The garden club: the shop wants to sell lamps

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: There is no single right answer. Many people trust the garden club more because the shop has something to sell, and that is a fair reason. 'I would need to know more' is a strong answer too: who is in the club, and when was each page written? The words are the same, but the makers want different things. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

<!-- Speaker note: Teacher: take a vote, then ask the 'need to know more' students what they would want to know. -->

## Where to look

**Where is the best place to find out who made a page?**

- Its About page, the address and any name on it
- How many adverts it has
- How long the page is
- The colours and pictures

* Answer: Its About page, the address and any name on it

<!-- Speaker note: The About page, the address and names on the page say who made it. If you cannot find out, that is a clue too. -->

## Find Mia's mistake

**Mia's teacher asks why she trusted her page. Click the line where Mia first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia wants to know what her bean plants need to grow.
 2  She searches bean plant needs and opens the first page that is not an advert.
 3  She copies "every plant needs plant food each week" into her report, without asking who made the page or why.
 4  The page was made by a shop that sells plant food.
 5  Her teacher asks how she knows it is true.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: Mia used the page without asking who made it or why. It was a shop selling plant food, so it had a reason to say plants need plant food. It might still be right, but she should have known what it wanted. -->

## No name, no date

**You cannot find any name, About page or date on a page. What does that tell you?**

- Nothing at all
- You cannot check who made it or when, so trust it less and look for another page
- It is from the government
- It must be true: it has nothing to hide

* Answer: You cannot check who made it or when, so trust it less and look for another page

## Make: Elaborate: a page with two purposes



<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: A page with two purposes adds a context: deciding what to use from it and what to check. For students who need more support: A card with three questions: Who? Why? When? Tick each before using a page. For early finishers: Find a made-up page in this lesson that does two things at once. Write one sentence for each purpose. The hard part, on purpose: Students think a page that sells cannot be useful, or that a page with no adverts has no purpose. Ask what each page wants the reader to do. When students get stuck: A student says the shop page must be lying. Ask: Could the tips still be right? How would you check? Fix: Purpose tells you what to check, not what is false. / A student cannot find who made a page. Ask: What does the address say? Is there an About page? Fix: Look at the address, the bottom of the page and the About page. / A student says a review site that earns money from sales only informs. Ask: What does the note at the end say they earn money from? Fix: Both are fair. The note shows it also wants you to buy. -->
