# Numbers and charts that mislead

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A made-up newsletter says 'Fruit sales soar!' and shows a column three times taller than the other. The numbers are true. Is the headline?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students fix a made-up chart whose axis starts at 50 so a 6% rise looks like a tripling, spot a pie whose parts add to 140%, and ask how many were counted before trusting a percentage.

**Students should be able to say:** I can check where a chart's axis starts and say what that does to the picture. I can tell when a pie chart is not one whole. I can ask how many were counted before trusting a percentage.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: three checks


:::keep-together

### The pie

**A pie chart of 'What students do at lunch' has slices for sport 60%, reading 30% and talking 50%. What is wrong?**

☐ Pie charts can't show percentages

☐ The parts add to 140%, so they are not parts of one whole: students could pick more than one

☐ There should be more colours

☐ Nothing: the biggest slice is sport

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The parts add to 140%, so they are not parts of one whole: students could pick more than one

A pie shows parts of one whole, adding to 100%. When people can choose more than one thing, a column chart of each choice fits better.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Kai's mistake

**Kai's poster says something the survey cannot show. Click the line where Kai first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Kai wants to know what students think of the new canteen menu.
 2  He asks four of his friends, and three say they love it.
 3  He writes on a poster: 75% of students in the school love the new menu!
 4  His teacher asks how many students he asked.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Kai wrote that most students in the school love the new menu, from a survey of four friends. Four people can't speak for a whole school, and friends may think alike. 75% of 4 is 3 people.

:::

**Answer:** How many kids were asked, and who asked them
