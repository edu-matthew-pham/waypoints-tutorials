# Fact, opinion or advert?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** "Kestrel helmets are the best helmets in the world." Is that a fact, an opinion or an advert? Does it matter which?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students tell facts (statements you could check, right or wrong), opinions (views with reasons) and adverts (lines that want a sale) apart, and decide what to do with each: check, weigh or remember what it wants.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell a fact I could check from an opinion or an advert. I can say what to do next with each kind. I can pull the checkable part out of a mixed statement.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Engage


:::keep-together

**"Kestrel helmets are the best helmets in the world." Which kind is it?**

☐ A fact

☐ An advert, or an opinion if a rider says it

☐ It depends on who says it

☐ None of them

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Best in the world can't be measured, so it isn't a fact you can check. On a Kestrel page it is an advert. If a friend says it, it is an opinion, and you can ask their reasons. 'It depends on who says it' is a good answer: who says it changes what it is for.

:::

### Find Ava's mistake

**Ava's science report has a mistake in it. Click the line where Ava first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ava is writing a report on bike safety.
 2  She reads "Kestrel helmets: the safest choice for every rider!" and writes it down as a fact, because it sounds sure.
 3  She does not say where it came from.
 4  Her report says Kestrel helmets are the safest helmets.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Ava treated 'the safest choice for every rider' as a fact because it sounded sure. It is an advert, and there is no way to check 'safest for every rider'. Sounding sure does not make a statement a fact.

## Elaborate: mixed statements


:::keep-together

### Pull out the fact

**"The Riverbend pool is 25 metres long, so it's the best pool for training." Which part could you check?**

☐ Neither part

☐ That it is the best pool for training

☐ That it is 25 metres long

☐ Both parts

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** That it is 25 metres long

The length is a fact you could check. Best for training is an opinion built on it.

:::


:::keep-together

### A reason

**A good opinion gives reasons. Which opinion gives the best reason?**

☐ The library is a good place to study, trust me

☐ The library is a good place to study because it is the best

☐ The library is a good place to study because it is quiet and has free computers

☐ The library is a good place to study!!!

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The library is a good place to study because it is quiet and has free computers

Quiet and free computers are reasons you could check. You might still disagree, but you know why they think so.
