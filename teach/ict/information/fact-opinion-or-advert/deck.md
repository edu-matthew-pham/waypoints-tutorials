# Fact, opinion or advert?

Investigating and judging information · Check the claim

<!-- Speaker note: Students tell facts (statements you could check, right or wrong), opinions (views with reasons) and adverts (lines that want a sale) apart, and decide what to do with each: check, weigh or remember what it wants. About 30 minutes (Engage and Explore 8 min · Explain 6 min · Elaborate and Practise 8 min · discussion and reflection 8 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can tell a fact I could check from an opinion or an advert.
- I can say what to do next with each kind.
- I can pull the checkable part out of a mixed statement.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: The one idea: a fact is checkable (and can be wrong), an opinion is a view (a good one gives reasons), an advert wants a sale. All statements are made up. Real writing mixes the kinds; that is the Elaborate step. Students often think "fact" means "true". Keep saying "a fact-type statement you could check". "The pool is 100 metres long" is a wrong fact, not an opinion. Contested topics: if a student offers a real statement on an argued topic, sort its kind, not its truth. "Could we check this? Who would know?" is enough; you do not need to decide it in class. If a view is hurtful, deal with that under class rules, separately. Years 5 to 6: the floor. Years 7 to 8: add the wider sponsored-story step (?width=wider). -->

---

## Why it matters

> "Kestrel helmets are the best helmets in the world." Is that a fact, an opinion or an advert? Does it matter which?

<!-- Speaker note: Three kinds of statement can look alike: - A **fact** is a statement you can **check**. It might be right or wrong, but there is a way to find out. *The Riverbend pool is 25 metres long.* - An **opinion** is a **view**. People can disagree, and a good opinion gives reasons. *The Riverbend pool is the best place to spend a Saturday.* - An **advert** wants you to **buy** or sign up. It often sounds like a fact. *Kestrel helmets: the safest choice for every rider!* A fact is not always true: *The pool is 100 metres long* is a fact-type statement, and it is wrong. You find out by checking. -->

**If most people agree with an opinion, does it become a fact?**

- Most people in our class like pizza. Is 'pizza is the best food' now a fact?
- What would make it a fact you could check?

<!-- Speaker note: 'Most people in our class like pizza' is a fact you could check by asking. 'Pizza is the best food' stays an opinion however many agree. Keep it on what can be checked. ## Engage: predict

**"Kestrel helmets are the best helmets in the world." Which kind is it?**

- A fact
- An advert, or an opinion if a rider says it
- It depends on who says it
- None of them

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Best in the world can't be measured, so it isn't a fact you can check. On a Kestrel page it is an advert. If a friend says it, it is an opinion, and you can ask their reasons. 'It depends on who says it' is a good answer: who says it changes what it is for. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

| | |
|---|---|
| `A fact` | A. Remember it wants you to buy |

## Find Ava's mistake

**Ava's science report has a mistake in it. Click the line where Ava first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ava is writing a report on bike safety.
 2  She reads "Kestrel helmets: the safest choice for every rider!" and writes it down as a fact, because it sounds sure.
 3  She does not say where it came from.
 4  Her report says Kestrel helmets are the safest helmets.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Ava treated 'the safest choice for every rider' as a fact because it sounded sure. It is an advert, and there is no way to check 'safest for every rider'. Sounding sure does not make a statement a fact. -->

## Pull out the fact

**"The Riverbend pool is 25 metres long, so it's the best pool for training." Which part could you check?**

- Neither part
- That it is the best pool for training
- That it is 25 metres long
- Both parts

* Answer: That it is 25 metres long

## A reason

**A good opinion gives reasons. Which opinion gives the best reason?**

- The library is a good place to study, trust me
- The library is a good place to study because it is the best
- The library is a good place to study because it is quiet and has free computers
- The library is a good place to study!!!

* Answer: The library is a good place to study because it is quiet and has free computers

## Make: Elaborate: an advert that looks like a story



<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: An advert written like a news story adds a context: spotting the clues that it is selling. For students who need more support: One question on a card: Could I check this? Yes: fact. People could disagree: opinion. Wants my money: advert. For early finishers: Write one sentence of each kind about your school. Swap with a partner to sort. The hard part, on purpose: Students call a statement a fact when they agree with it, and an opinion when they don't. Ask: could you check it, whether or not you agree? When students get stuck: A student says a wrong statement is an opinion. Ask: Could you check it? Fix: If you could check it, it is a fact-type statement, even when it is wrong. / A student calls a statement they agree with a fact. Ask: Could someone reasonably disagree? Could you measure it? Fix: Agreeing does not make it checkable. / A student calls every advert a lie. Ask: Could an advert contain a true fact? Fix: Adverts can contain facts. Check them, and remember what the advert wants. -->

## Exit ticket

**Is 'The pool is 100 metres long' a fact or an opinion, if it is wrong?**

* A fact-type statement, because you could check it. Checking shows it is wrong.
