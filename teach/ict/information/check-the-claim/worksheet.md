# Check the claim

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A post says Riverbend Primary's netball team has never lost a game in ten years. How would you find out, without asking the person who posted it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students check a made-up claim by reading sideways: they leave the page, look at three other places, tell independent sources from copies, order the checking steps and decide when to stop.

**Students should be able to say:** I can check a claim by leaving the page and seeing what others say. I can tell a source that found something out from one that only copied it. I can say when I have checked enough, and when I could not check it.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

### Find Raj's mistake

**Raj tells his class the claim is true. Click the line where Raj first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Raj reads that Riverbend Primary has never lost a netball game in ten years.
 2  He searches for the claim in a new tab.
 3  He finds netball-legends.example saying the same thing, and counts it as a second source.
 4  He stops there and tells his class the claim is true.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Raj counted Place 3 as a second source, but it gave no source and appeared after the blog. A copy is not a check. Two places that found it out for themselves would be.

## Elaborate: when do you stop?


:::keep-together

### The pool

**A post says the Riverbend pool opens on 1 December. The council's page and the pool's own page both say 1 December. What now?**

☐ Trust it: two places that would know agree

☐ Ask the person who posted it

☐ Keep searching for ten more pages

☐ Don't trust it: it was a post

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Trust it: two places that would know agree

The council and the pool would know. When places like that agree, you can stop.

:::


:::keep-together

### Nothing anywhere

**You search for a claim and find nothing about it anywhere else. What does that tell you?**

☐ Trust it less: no one else says it, so you cannot check it

☐ It must be a secret, so it is true

☐ It is true: no one has said it is wrong

☐ It is false

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Trust it less: no one else says it, so you cannot check it

If nothing else mentions it, you cannot check it. Hold off, and say you could not check it.

:::

## copy or check?
