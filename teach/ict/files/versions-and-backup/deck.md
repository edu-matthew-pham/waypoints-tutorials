# Versions and backup

Files and systems · Keep it safe

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that saving over a file replaces the old one, that a version is an earlier state of a file and a backup is a copy in a different place, and that sync is not a backup on its own because it copies deletions and mistakes. ## Why it matters

> A student spends a week on a report, then saves a bad edit over the top and closes it. Is the good version gone?

<!-- Speaker note: Two different things keep your work safe: - A **version** is an earlier state of the same file. Saving over a file replaces it, unless the app or the folder keeps earlier versions. **Version history** lists them so you can go back. Without it, you can save a copy with a new date in its name before a big change. - A **backup** is a second copy in a **different place**, so if one place fails, is lost or is deleted, the work is still somewhere. Two copies in the same folder are not a backup. A **synced** folder keeps a copy on your device and a copy online, and changes in one appear in the other. That includes a deletion or a mistake. Sync is not a backup on its own. -->

## Think, pair, share

**A student says her work is safe because it is in the cloud. Another says it is safe because she has three copies on her laptop. Who is safer, and what would you ask each?**

- What happens to each if the laptop is lost?
- What happens to each if she deletes the file?

<!-- Speaker note: Neither is fully safe. The first has another place but sync can copy a delete. The second has three copies but one place. The best is both: versions, and a second approved place. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Two ways to be safe

Now: what protects against a broken laptop?

$$\text{Before a big change}$$

1) $\text{After the work}$ — Save a copy in a second place that is approved by the school.
2) $\text{Check}$ — Open the second copy. Does it open, and is it the one you expect?
3) $\text{Often}$ — Little and often. The newest work is the work you lose.

## Find the mistake

**Lin lost her whole project. Click the line where Lin first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Lin works on her science project for a week.
 2  She saves it on her laptop each day.
 3  She keeps only that one copy, on the laptop, and does not put it anywhere else.
 4  Her laptop breaks, and the project is gone.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: Lin kept her only copy on the laptop, and had no second place. When the laptop failed, there was nothing else. A backup in another approved place would have saved the week. -->

## Sync

**A friend says her cloud folder is her backup. She deletes a file from her laptop by mistake. What happens in the cloud folder?**

- Nothing, the cloud is separate
- The file comes back on its own
- The deletion usually syncs, and the file goes from the cloud copy too
- The cloud keeps it for ever

* Answer: The deletion usually syncs, and the file goes from the cloud copy too

<!-- Speaker note: Sync copies deletions and mistakes as well. Find out whether your service keeps old versions or deleted files, and for how long. -->
