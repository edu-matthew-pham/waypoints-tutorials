# When something goes wrong

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Zara gets a nasty message from an account she doesn't know. Her first idea is to delete it and never tell anyone. What might happen if she does?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students predict what happens when a problem is deleted and kept secret, sort first moves into helps and makes it harder, and learn a five-step plan as a diagram: stop, keep it, tell a trusted adult, block and report, look after yourself, with 'made a mistake? tell anyway'. Typical width (Years 7 and 8): choose the next step in harder situations.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say the plan for when something goes wrong online. I can explain why keeping it and telling helps more than deleting. I can explain why telling helps even if I made a mistake.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: the plan


:::keep-together

![A flowchart of the plan. Something online upsets or worries you. Stop: don't reply. Keep it: don't delete it. Tell a trusted adult. Block and report, together. Get support and look after yourself. A side note: made a mistake yourself? Tell anyway, it can be fixed; it joins the plan at Tell a trusted adult.](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/_pictures/diagrams/ict/safe/when-something-goes-wrong/step-3.svg?v=fe755f5984a798c1)

*When something goes wrong*

:::

When something online upsets or worries you, you don't have to work it out alone:

1. **Stop.** Don't reply.
2. **Keep it.** Don't delete it. A screenshot or the username helps an adult help you. (If it's a picture that shouldn't be shared, such as someone without clothes on, don't save it or send it on: just tell an adult straight away.)
3. **Tell a trusted adult.** A parent or carer, a teacher, the school counsellor.
4. **Block and report,** with the adult. Apps have a report button; the adult knows other places to report too.
5. **Look after yourself.** Feeling upset is normal. Do something you enjoy, and keep talking.

**Made a mistake yourself?** Tell anyway. Everyone makes mistakes online, and most can be fixed, more easily if you tell early. You can also call **Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800**, free, any time.

:::keep-together

### Ollie's mistake

**Ollie made a mistake. Click the line where his thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ollie clicks a link in a game chat that promised free coins.
 2  His game account starts sending strange messages to his friends.
 3  He thinks: if I tell anyone, I'll get in trouble, so I'll just hide it.
 4  He says nothing, and a week later he can't get into the account at all.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Ollie thinks telling will get him in trouble, so he hides it. Telling early is how things get fixed. Adults would much rather know, and an account can often be got back if someone acts quickly.

### Keep it

**Why does the plan say keep it, not delete it?**

☐ So you can read it again later

☐ So a trusted adult can see what happened and help report it

☐ So you can send it to friends

☐ Because deleting is against the rules

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** So a trusted adult can see what happened and help report it

A screenshot or the username helps an adult help you, and helps an app act on a report.
