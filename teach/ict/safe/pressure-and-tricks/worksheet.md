# Pressure and tricks

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A pop-up in Leo's game flashes: 'You've WON 10,000 free gems! Claim in the next 60 seconds!' His finger is already on the button. What should he do first?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sort made-up messages by the push they use (hurry, a prize you didn't enter, everyone has it, no push), find the mistake in treating a countdown as a sign it's real, and learn stop, check, ask, and never to send a password or code. Typical width (Years 7 and 8): mark a made-up inbox Safe or Report, checking where links really go.

**Students should be able to say:** I can spot hurry, a prize I didn't enter, and 'everyone has it'. I can explain why pressure to hurry means slow down. I can say why I never send a password or code to anyone.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

## Explain: slow down, check, ask

When something pushes you to hurry, that's the moment to **slow down**:

1. **Stop.** Take your finger off the button. A real offer can wait.
2. **Check.** Who is it really from? Did you enter, order or ask for this?
3. **Ask.** Show a trusted adult before you click, pay, or give a password, a code or personal details.

Never send a password or a code to anyone, even someone friendly. Nobody real minds you checking. How computers work has more on messages that pretend to be someone you trust: [Spot the phish](../../../systems/cyber/phishing/).

:::keep-together

### The password

**A message asks Leo for his password 'to send the gems'. What should he do?**

☐ Give it, because the gems are free

☐ Never give it; close the message and ask a trusted adult

☐ Give only half of it

☐ Give it but change it later

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Never give it; close the message and ask a trusted adult

Passwords and codes are only typed into the real app or site, never sent to anyone. Real companies don't ask for them in a message.
