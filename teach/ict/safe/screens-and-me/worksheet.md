# Screens and me

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Noah sits down to watch one video before dinner. When his dad calls him, it's dark outside. Where did the time go?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sort made-up screen times by how they left someone feeling, sort app features into keeps you going and helps you stop, and choose their own small habit privately. No targets, no hours, no diagnosis. Typical width (Years 7 and 8): switch features on a made-up app to fit a planned session.

**Students should be able to say:** I can notice how screen time feels during and after. I can name features designed to keep me going, and ones that help me stop. I can choose a small habit that fits me.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: notice, then choose

Two ideas help:

1. **Notice.** How do I feel during and after? Tired, on edge, left out, or happy, calm, connected?
2. **Choose a habit that fits you.** Decide before you start. Charge the device outside the bedroom at night. Turn off autoplay or notifications you don't need. Swap one scroll for a call, a game with a friend, or going outside.

There is no right number of minutes, and screens are not bad. If screens ever start to feel like they're taking over, or you feel worried or low, talk to a trusted adult. You can also call Kids Helpline on 1800 55 1800, which is free for young people.

:::keep-together
