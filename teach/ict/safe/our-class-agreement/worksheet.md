# Our class agreement

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A made-up Year 6 class wrote 25 rules for being online. Nobody could remember them by Friday. How could they make an agreement people actually keep?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A short, teacher-led lesson that closes the course. Students sort draft lines into good lines and lines that need work, read a made-up class's five-line agreement, then the class writes and agrees its own. The site holds only ticks; the agreement itself is the class's, kept wherever the teacher chooses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what makes a good line for an agreement. I can help write our class's agreement. I can say one line from it and who to tell if something goes wrong.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: a made-up class's agreement

**Room 12's online agreement** (made up)

1. We pause before we post, and choose the kinder version.
2. We ask before we share a photo or message about someone else.
3. We keep personal details for people we know in real life.
4. We tell a trusted adult when something worries us, and help friends to tell.
5. If someone is having a hard time online, we don't add to it, and we check on them.

Every line came from a lesson in this course. Your class's agreement can look quite different. What matters is that **you write it together** and that **everyone can keep it**.

:::keep-together

**Read the made-up class's agreement below. Which course habit is missing from it?**

☐ Telling a trusted adult

☐ Pausing before you post

☐ Asking before sharing a photo of someone

☐ Slowing down when something pushes you to hurry

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Slowing down when something pushes you to hurry

Nothing in it is about pressure and tricks. A class could add one, or decide five lines is enough.

:::


## Make: our agreement

Your teacher will lead this part. In small groups, write one or two lines, then the class chooses and agrees five or so. Use the course's habits if they help:

- **A day's trail:** can I take it back, does it say where I am, is it about someone else?
- **Who can see this?** The smallest audience that does the job.
- **Pause before you post:** how will they read it?
- **Who is on the other end?** Friendly is not the same as known.
- **Screens and me:** notice, then choose.
- **Pressure and tricks:** stop, check, ask.
- **When something goes wrong:** stop, keep it, tell, block and report.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
