# Our class agreement

Staying safe and well · Our class agreement

<!-- Speaker note: A short, teacher-led lesson that closes the course. - I can say what makes a good line for an agreement.
- I can help write our class's agreement.

## Why it matters

> A made-up Year 6 class wrote 25 rules for being online. Nobody could remember them by Friday. How could they make an agreement people actually keep?

## Think, pair, share

**Should a class agreement be about what we do, or what we don't do?**

- Which is easier to remember: 'We pause before we post' or 'Don't post mean things'?
- Which tells you what to do in the moment?

<!-- Speaker note: Positive lines say what to do. Both can work; a mix is fine if most are positive. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Make: our agreement

Your teacher will lead this part. In small groups, write one or two lines, then the class chooses and agrees five or so. Use the course's habits if they help:

- **A day's trail:** can I take it back, does it say where I am, is it about someone else?
- **Who can see this?** The smallest audience that does the job.
- **Pause before you post:** how will they read it?
- **Who is on the other end?** Friendly is not the same as known.
- **Screens and me:** notice, then choose.
- **Pressure and tricks:** stop, check, ask.
- **When something goes wrong:** stop, keep it, tell, block and report.

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: The class writes and agrees its own lines, led by the teacher; the site holds a tick, not the agreement. For students who need more support: Sentence starters on the board: We pause... We ask... We tell... We help... For early finishers: Write the agreement for a younger class, using words they would understand. The hard part, on purpose: Groups write rules for other people ('people shouldn't...') or long lists. Ask: what will we do? Can you say it in under ten words? When students get stuck: A group writes a list of don'ts. Ask: What would we do instead? Fix: Turn each don't into a do: 'Don't be mean' becomes 'We choose the kinder version'. / A line points at a real person or event. Ask: Could anyone in any class keep this line? Fix: Make it general, and follow up privately if it hints at something real. / The class can't agree on a line. Ask: Can everyone keep it? Fix: Leave it out or reword it; five lines everyone keeps beats ten that some don't. -->

## Write our lines <span style="font-size:.6em;color:#64748b">(10 min)</span>

In groups of three, write one or two lines for our agreement. Keep each under ten words if you can. Start with "We...".

<!-- Speaker note: Paper. Circulate and nudge don'ts into dos. No line names a real person or event. -->

---

<span style="background:#dbeafe; color:#1a1a1a; border:1.5px solid #3b82f6; padding:6px 18px; border-radius:8px; font-size:1em; font-weight:500;">💬 Discussion</span>

## Choose and agree <span style="font-size:.6em;color:#64748b">(10 min)</span>

Groups read their lines. Put similar lines together. Choose about five that everyone can keep.

<!-- Speaker note: Read lines without naming groups. A thumbs up, sideways or down vote works; aim for lines everyone can keep. -->

## Evaluate: keep it going

**A private think** (just for you): which line of the agreement will be easiest for you to keep? Which will be hardest?

## Where it lives

Our agreement will be kept where we can all see it. We'll look at it again in a few weeks.
