## Explain: three questions before you add to the trail

Three questions help before you add something to your trail:

1. **Can I take it back?** Once others have seen it, it may be saved or passed on.
2. **Does it say where I am?** A street sign, a school uniform or a live location can.
3. **Is it about someone else?** Then ask them first.

You do not need to worry about every game score or recipe search. A trail is something to notice, not something to fear. How computers work has a lesson on everything apps record, even what you never type: [Your digital footprint](../../../systems/cyber/footprint/).

:::keep-together

### Mia's mistake

**Mia is in a hurry. Click the line where her thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia takes a silly photo of her friend Jo at lunch.
 2  She thinks: I'll post it now, and if it's a problem I'll delete it later.
 3  She posts it to the class group chat without asking Jo.
 4  Ten minutes later Jo asks her to take it down. Mia deletes it, but two people have already saved it.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Mia thinks she can delete it later if it is a problem. By then others may have seen, saved or passed it on. The time to think is before posting.

:::

### Photos of other people

**Which question helps most before posting a photo with other people in it?**

☐ Is it a good photo?

☐ Would they be happy for this to be shared?

☐ Is my phone charged?

☐ Will it get lots of likes?

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Would they be happy for this to be shared?

When a post is about someone else, it is partly theirs. Ask first.
