# A day's trail

Staying safe and well · Your trail and who sees it

## Today

- I can say why something I delete may not be gone.
- I can name three questions to ask before I post.

## Think, pair, share

**Kai's dog photo was deleted after a minute. Two people had already saved it. Whose job was it to think first: Kai's, or the people who saved it?**

- What could Kai have done before posting?
- What could the people who saved it do now?

<!-- Speaker note: Both. The poster can pause; the viewers can choose not to keep or pass things on. Keep it about Kai, not about students' own chats. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Engage: predict

**Kai sends a funny photo of the dog to a group chat, then deletes it a minute later. Is it gone?**

- Maybe not: someone may have seen it, saved it or taken a screenshot
- Yes, if Kai deletes it within a minute
- Yes: deleting takes it back from everyone

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: Maybe not. Deleting takes it off Kai's screen. Anyone who saw it in that minute could have saved it, and the chat app may keep a copy for a while. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Mia's mistake

**Mia is in a hurry. Click the line where her thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia takes a silly photo of her friend Jo at lunch.
 2  She thinks: I'll post it now, and if it's a problem I'll delete it later.
 3  She posts it to the class group chat without asking Jo.
 4  Ten minutes later Jo asks her to take it down. Mia deletes it, but two people have already saved it.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Mia thinks she can delete it later if it is a problem. By then others may have seen, saved or passed it on. The time to think is before posting. -->

<!-- Speaker note: Teacher: read Mia's lines aloud; ask where she could have stopped. -->

## Photos of other people

**Which question helps most before posting a photo with other people in it?**

- Is it a good photo?
- Would they be happy for this to be shared?
- Is my phone charged?
- Will it get lots of likes?

* Answer: Would they be happy for this to be shared?

<!-- Speaker note: When a post is about someone else, it is partly theirs. Ask first. -->

## Which question?

**A photo of your team in uniform, with the school's name on it. Which question matters most?**

- Does it say where I am?
- Can I take it back?
- Is it a good photo?

* Answer: Does it say where I am?

## Elaborate: older trails

**Kai is in Year 8 now. Which question matters most for each?**

Groups: Can I take it back? · Does it say where I am? · Is it about someone else?

A voice message in a group chat of 40 people, joking about a teacher · A photo at the bus stop every morning at the same time · A video of a friend singing badly at a sleepover · A username with a full name and birth year in it

* Answer: A voice message in a group chat of 40 people, joking about a teacher → Can I take it back?; A photo at the bus stop every morning at the same time → Does it say where I am?; A video of a friend singing badly at a sleepover → Is it about someone else?; A username with a full name and birth year in it → Can I take it back?
