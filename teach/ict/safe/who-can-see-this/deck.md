# Who can see this?

Staying safe and well · Your trail and who sees it

<!-- Speaker note: Students match ways of sharing a photo to who sees it first, find the mistake in treating a private group as private for ever, and learn to choose the smallest audience that does the job while picturing who could end up seeing it. Typical width (Years 7 and 8): set a made-up account's privacy settings on the club app board. About 35 minutes (Engage and Explore 8 min · Explain 10 min · Elaborate 10 min · Evaluate 7 min). -->

## Today

- I can say who sees something first when I share it.
- I can explain why the audience can grow after I share.
- I can choose the smallest audience that does the job.

## Why it matters

> Lena takes a photo of her art project. She could send it to her mum, to the class group chat, or post it for anyone. Who sees it each time, and who could see it later?

<!-- Speaker note: Lena and everyone else here are made up. You do not need to say what apps you use. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Theo's cartoon was forwarded by a friend. Who is responsible for it reaching the bigger group: Theo, the friend who forwarded it, or the cousin who shared it again?**

- What could each person have done differently?
- Does forwarding something make it partly yours?

## Engage: predict

**Lena posts a photo to a private group of 12 friends. What is the most people who could end up seeing it?**

- Exactly 12: the group is private
- More than 12: anyone in the group can screenshot or forward it
- Only Lena: it is her photo

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: More than 12. The setting chose 12 people, and each of them can screenshot or forward it to others. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

<!-- Speaker note: Teacher: ask what 'private' means to them before revealing the answer. -->

## Theo's mistake

**Theo shares something he thinks is safe. Click the line where his thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Theo draws a funny cartoon of his teacher.
 2  He thinks: it's a private group of friends, so nobody else will ever see it.
 3  He posts it in the group.
 4  One friend screenshots it and sends it to her cousin, who shares it with a bigger group.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Theo thinks a private group means nobody else will ever see it. Anyone in the group can screenshot or forward it. -->

<!-- Speaker note: Teacher: draw two circles on the board, chosen audience inside, possible audience outside. -->

## Grandparents only

**Lena wants to share her art with her grandparents only. Which is the best choice?**

- Put it in the class group chat and ask them to join
- Post it publicly and tell them to look
- Send it to them in a message
- Make it her profile picture

* Answer: Send it to them in a message

<!-- Speaker note: A direct message reaches the smallest audience. Choose the smallest audience that does the job. -->

## Smallest audience

**Lena wants feedback on her art from her art teacher only. Which is best?**

- Send it through the school's own system to the teacher
- Post it publicly
- Put it in the class group chat

* Answer: Send it through the school's own system to the teacher
