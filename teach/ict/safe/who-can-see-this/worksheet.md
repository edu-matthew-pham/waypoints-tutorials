# Who can see this?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Lena takes a photo of her art project. She could send it to her mum, to the class group chat, or post it for anyone. Who sees it each time, and who could see it later?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students match ways of sharing a photo to who sees it first, find the mistake in treating a private group as private for ever, and learn to choose the smallest audience that does the job while picturing who could end up seeing it. Typical width (Years 7 and 8): set a made-up account's privacy settings on the club app board.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say who sees something first when I share it. I can explain why the audience can grow after I share. I can choose the smallest audience that does the job.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (Engage, Explore, Explain, Evaluate). Typical and Wider sections add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Explain: the audience you chose, and the one you might get

Every post has two audiences:

- **The audience you chose:** the person, group or followers you sent it to.
- **The audience you might get:** anyone they show, screenshot or forward it to.

Choose the **smallest audience that does the job**, and picture the second audience before you share. Settings help with the first audience: a private account, approved followers, asking before you are tagged. Nothing controls the second audience except the people in the first one, so you can help others too, by not passing on what was not meant for you.

:::keep-together

### Theo's mistake

**Theo shares something he thinks is safe. Click the line where his thinking first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Theo draws a funny cartoon of his teacher.
 2  He thinks: it's a private group of friends, so nobody else will ever see it.
 3  He posts it in the group.
 4  One friend screenshots it and sends it to her cousin, who shares it with a bigger group.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Theo thinks a private group means nobody else will ever see it. Anyone in the group can screenshot or forward it.

### Grandparents only

**Lena wants to share her art with her grandparents only. Which is the best choice?**

☐ Put it in the class group chat and ask them to join

☐ Post it publicly and tell them to look

☐ Send it to them in a message

☐ Make it her profile picture

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Send it to them in a message

A direct message reaches the smallest audience. Choose the smallest audience that does the job.
