# Report on an investigation

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A class grew bean plants in three places and measured every one. Now they have fifteen numbers and a question: does light matter? Turn the numbers into an answer someone else can check.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A challenge that brings the course together. Students summarise a class investigation (beans grown in dark, shade and sun) with AVERAGE, MAX, MIN and COUNTIF written once and copied across, chart the group averages with a title and axis titles, and pick a conclusion that matches the evidence. They then repeat it in the spreadsheet app the school uses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can summarise groups of measurements with formulas and functions. I can chart a comparison with a title and axis titles with units. I can write a conclusion that matches the data and no more.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Plan: what to work out


:::keep-together

### Typical height

**You want to compare how tall a typical bean is in each group. Which function on each column of five heights do you use?**

☐ SUM

☐ MIN only

☐ COUNT

☐ AVERAGE

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** AVERAGE

AVERAGE gives the typical height of each group, so the three groups can be compared fairly.

:::


:::keep-together

### Catching a slip

**A ruler slip makes one Sun plant read 145 cm instead of 14.5. Which summaries would show something is wrong?**

☐ Only the count of plants

☐ Only the shortest

☐ The tallest, and the average

☐ None of them

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The tallest, and the average

A slip far from the others shows in the tallest (MAX) and pulls the average. Checking the tallest and shortest is a quick check on the data.

:::
