## Why it matters

> A slide about beans in the sun has a photo of a crowd of people on it, because it looked good. What does the audience learn from it?

<!-- Speaker note: A picture can do something words cannot: show how big a thing is, what it looks like or how numbers compare. A good picture does that job. A picture added to fill space does not. Three questions to ask about a picture: - **Does it show the idea?** If the slide would be as clear without it, it is decoration. - **Is it big enough to see?** Small pictures are hard to make out from the back. - **Can someone who cannot see it still get the idea?** That is what **alt text** is for. It is a short description attached to the picture, and a screen reader reads it aloud. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Choose a picture for a slide about how fast beans grow in the sun. What would make it a good one, and what would make it a bad one?**

- What does the picture show that the words cannot?
- Could someone who cannot see it still get the idea?

<!-- Speaker note: A good one shows the growth, such as a chart or a seedling beside a taller one. A bad one is a pretty picture with nothing to do with beans. Alt text carries the idea for the listener. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Does it show the idea?

Now: what makes alt text good?

$$\text{Ask what it shows}$$

1) $\text{Make it big enough}$ — Use the whole width if you need to, so the back row can see it.
2) $\text{Alt text}$ — Write what it shows, in a short sentence, for someone who cannot see it.
3) $\text{If it only fills space}$ — Take it out. A slide with words alone is better than a slide with a distraction.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Line 1: write Make it big enough and say "Use the whole width if you need to, so the back row can see it." Line 2: write Alt text and say "Write what it shows, in a short sentence, for someone who cannot see it." Line 3: write If it only fills space and say "Take it out. A slide with words alone is better than a slide with a distraction." -->

## Does it show the idea?

**Which alt text is best for a bar chart that shows beans in the sun grew 13 cm and beans in the dark grew 4 cm?**

- A chart
- A bar chart: beans in the sun grew 13 cm, beans in the dark 4 cm
- Image of chart
- chart.png

* Answer: A bar chart: beans in the sun grew 13 cm, beans in the dark 4 cm

## Find the mistake

**Kai's slide about beans in the sun has a picture that teaches the audience nothing. Click the line where Kai first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Kai makes a slide about how beans grow in the sun.
 2  Kai adds a big picture of a crowd of people because it looks good.
 3  The slide now has a title and the picture.
 4  The audience asks what the people have to do with beans.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Kai added the picture because it looked good, not because it showed anything about the beans. A picture should show the idea, not fill space. -->
