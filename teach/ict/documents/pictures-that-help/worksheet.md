# Pictures that help

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A slide about beans in the sun has a photo of a crowd of people on it, because it looked good. What does the audience learn from it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn to ask whether a picture shows the idea or only fills space, to make it large enough to see, and to write alt text that says what it shows for someone who cannot see it. They add alt text on a slide and repeat it in the slide app the school uses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether a picture shows the idea or only decorates. I can write alt text that says what a picture shows. I can make a picture large enough to see from the back.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: does it help?


:::keep-together

### Does it show the idea?

1. $\text{Ask what it shows}$ — A bar chart shows how the heights compare. A crowd of people shows nothing about beans.
2. $\text{Make it big enough}$ — Use the whole width if you need to, so the back row can see it.
3. $\text{Alt text}$ — Write what it shows, in a short sentence, for someone who cannot see it.
4. $\text{If it only fills space}$ — Take it out. A slide with words alone is better than a slide with a distraction.

Now: what makes alt text good?

**Which alt text is best for a bar chart that shows beans in the sun grew 13 cm and beans in the dark grew 4 cm?**

☐ A chart

☐ A bar chart: beans in the sun grew 13 cm, beans in the dark 4 cm

☐ Image of chart

☐ chart.png

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A bar chart: beans in the sun grew 13 cm, beans in the dark 4 cm

Good alt text gives the idea: what kind of picture it is and what it shows.

:::


:::keep-together
