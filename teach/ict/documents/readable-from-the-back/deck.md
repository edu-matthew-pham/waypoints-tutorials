# Readable from the back

Documents and slides · Slides

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that slides are read from the back of a room, so text must be large (about 24 points or more) and the text and background must differ strongly in lightness (a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1), and that words that do not fit are cut, not shrunk. They set sizes and colours on a slide and repeat it in the slide app the school uses. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 7 min · Practise 8 min · Make and own app 12 min). -->

## Today

- I can choose text and background colours that can be read from the back of a room.
- I can set text large enough for a slide.
- I can cut words when a slide does not fit.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: pick yellow text on white, then white on navy, and watch the contrast number change. The one idea: slides are read from across a room. Large text, strong contrast, few words. The board's contrast number is the standard WCAG ratio between the two colours, and 4.5 to 1 is the usual threshold for readable text. The 24-point rule is a rule of thumb, not a standard. The board's checks count sizes and whether text fits; standing at the back of the room is the real test. Teacher: Try: ask which pair they would choose and what the number says. Teacher: Worked example: ask Priya how she could have tested it. Teacher: Own app: students often shrink text to fit. Ask what happens at the back. -->

## Why it matters

> A slide looks lovely on your screen: pale grey words on white. Then you present it in the hall. What does the back row see?

<!-- Speaker note: You make a slide at a desk, close to the screen. Your audience sees it across a room. Two things decide whether they can read it: - **Size.** Text on a slide should be large, about 24 points or more. Too small and it cannot be read from the back. - **Contrast.** The text and the background must be very different in lightness. Black on white is strong. Pale grey on white, or yellow on white, is weak. Also, text that is large takes more room, so there have to be fewer words. If the words run off the slide, cut words, not size. The board shows a number for contrast. A ratio of **4.5 to 1 or more** is the usual standard for readable text. -->

## Checking a slide

Now: which pair is easiest to read?

$$\text{Size}$$

1) $\text{Contrast}$ — Choose colours far apart in lightness, such as white on dark blue or black on white.
2) $\text{Fit}$ — If the words do not fit at that size, cut words.
3) $\text{Test it}$ — Stand back from the screen. If you cannot read it, neither can the back row.

## Exit ticket

**Your words do not fit on a slide at 28 points. What should you do?**

* Cut words, or split the slide. Do not shrink the text, because the back row cannot read it.
