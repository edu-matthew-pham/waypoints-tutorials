# Readable from the back

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A slide looks lovely on your screen: pale grey words on white. Then you present it in the hall. What does the back row see?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that slides are read from the back of a room, so text must be large (about 24 points or more) and the text and background must differ strongly in lightness (a contrast of at least 4.5 to 1), and that words that do not fit are cut, not shrunk. They set sizes and colours on a slide and repeat it in the slide app the school uses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can choose text and background colours that can be read from the back of a room. I can set text large enough for a slide. I can cut words when a slide does not fit.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: size and contrast


:::keep-together

### Checking a slide

1. $\text{Size}$ — Make the text large, 24 points or more, so it can be read from the back.
2. $\text{Contrast}$ — Choose colours far apart in lightness, such as white on dark blue or black on white.
3. $\text{Fit}$ — If the words do not fit at that size, cut words.
4. $\text{Test it}$ — Stand back from the screen. If you cannot read it, neither can the back row.

Now: which pair is easiest to read?

**Which pair is easiest to read from the back of a room?**

☐ Light blue text on grey

☐ Pale grey text on white

☐ Black text on white

☐ Yellow text on white

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Black text on white

Black on white has the biggest gap in lightness, so it is the easiest to read.

:::


:::keep-together
