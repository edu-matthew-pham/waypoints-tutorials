# Headings say what a part is

Documents and slides · Structure

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a heading is a style, not a look. A line that is only bold and big is still body text to the app, so it does not appear in the headings panel or contents list and a screen reader cannot jump to it. They give styles to a report's parts on a page and repeat it in the word processor the school uses. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 7 min · Practise 8 min · Make and own app 12 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what a heading style tells the app that bold and big text does not.
- I can give a part of a document the right heading style.
- I can find the headings panel in my own word processor.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: choose Heading 1 for Aim, then look at the headings panel on the left. The one idea: a heading is a style that says what a part is. Bold and big is only a look. The page on the screen is a small teaching model with only what every word processor shares: styles, a headings panel, lists and tables. It is not Word or Docs, and nothing here depends on either. The last step asks students to do the same in the app your school uses: styles sit in the toolbar's styles list in Word, Docs and Pages, and the headings panel is the Navigation pane (Word) or Document outline (Docs). Years 6–8. Use a real report from another subject for the last step if you have one. Teacher: Try: ask what changed on the page when they chose Heading 1. Nothing visible changes, so what did? Teacher: Worked example: ask Mia what she was trying to do and what she should have chosen. Teacher: Own app: the common slip is pressing Bold again. Ask them to open the headings panel to check. -->

## Why it matters

> Two lines in a report look exactly like headings. A reader opens the headings panel to jump to the Method. It is empty. What went wrong?

<!-- Speaker note: A document is made of **parts**: a title, headings, paragraphs, lists. Each part can have a **style**, which is a name the app understands, such as *Heading 1* or *Body text*. The style tells the app what the part **is**. The look (big, bold) comes from the style. Here is the start of a Year 7 science report. *Aim* and *Method* are big and bold. The headings panel on the left lists the headings in the document. -->

**Two students hand in reports that look identical. One used Heading 1 and one made the words bold. Which report is easier to use, and how would you find out?**

- What could you do with one that you cannot do with the other?
- Who else besides the writer uses the headings?

## Choosing Heading 1

Now: what does a heading style tell the app?

$$\text{Select the line}$$

1) $\text{Open the styles}$ — In the styles list, choose Heading 1 instead of pressing Bold.
2) $\text{Look at the panel}$ — The headings panel lists the part, and a contents list can use it.
3) $\text{A part inside it}$ — A smaller part under Method, such as Equipment, gets Heading 2, so it sits under Method.

**Mia's contents list is missing the Method. Click the line where Mia first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia types the word Method on its own line, above her method.

 4  Method is missing from the contents list.
```

* Answer: line 2

## Why headings?

**Why do apps and screen readers use headings?**

- To check the spelling
- To make the pages the same length
- To make the file smaller
- To jump straight to a part without reading everything

* Answer: To jump straight to a part without reading everything

## Make: In your own app

Word, Google Docs and Pages all have a styles list near the top with Title, Heading 1 and Heading 2. Look for the headings panel under View, as the Navigation pane or Document outline. If it is empty, the lines are probably still Body text.
