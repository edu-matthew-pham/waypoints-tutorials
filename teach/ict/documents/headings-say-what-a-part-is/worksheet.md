# Headings say what a part is

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two lines in a report look exactly like headings. A reader opens the headings panel to jump to the Method. It is empty. What went wrong?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a heading is a style, not a look. A line that is only bold and big is still body text to the app, so it does not appear in the headings panel or contents list and a screen reader cannot jump to it. They give styles to a report's parts on a page and repeat it in the word processor the school uses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what a heading style tells the app that bold and big text does not. I can give a part of a document the right heading style. I can find the headings panel in my own word processor.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: choose the style


:::keep-together

### Choosing Heading 1

1. $\text{Select the line}$ — Click the words you want to turn into a heading.
2. $\text{Open the styles}$ — In the styles list, choose Heading 1 instead of pressing Bold.
3. $\text{Look at the panel}$ — The headings panel lists the part, and a contents list can use it.
4. $\text{A part inside it}$ — A smaller part under Method, such as Equipment, gets Heading 2, so it sits under Method.

Now: what does a heading style tell the app?

**What does a heading style tell the app that big bold text does not?**

☐ This line is important

☐ This line should be printed

☐ This line is the first one

☐ This line names a part of the document

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** This line names a part of the document

A heading style says this line names a part. The headings panel, the contents list and screen readers use that.

:::


:::keep-together

A reader, or a screen reader, can list the headings and jump to the part they want.

## Make: In your own app

Word, Google Docs and Pages all have a styles list near the top with Title, Heading 1 and Heading 2. Look for the headings panel under View, as the Navigation pane or Document outline. If it is empty, the lines are probably still Body text.
