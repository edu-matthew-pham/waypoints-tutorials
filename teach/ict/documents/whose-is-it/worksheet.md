# Whose is it?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A student finds a good picture of a bean plant by searching the web, copies it onto her slide and says it is hers because she found it. Is it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that pictures and words found online were made by someone, that a licence says what may be done with them, and that a credit gives who made it, where it was found and the licence. They learn what needs a credit (other people's pictures, copied words and ideas) and what does not (their own work and common knowledge), and write credits on a slide.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say why something I find online still has an owner. I can write a credit with who, where and the licence. I can tell what needs a credit and what does not.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: when to credit


:::keep-together

### Writing a credit

1. $\text{Find the maker}$ — The page the picture is on usually says who made it. If it does not, be careful.
2. $\text{Check the licence}$ — It says what you may do. Some say you must give credit, some say no.
3. $\text{Write the credit}$ — Who, where and the licence, in a line under the picture.
4. $\text{If you cannot find them}$ — Do not use it. Draw your own or choose one that has the details.

Now: what does a good credit give?

**What three things does a good credit give?**

☐ Your name, the school and the date

☐ The file name, the size and the date

☐ The title, the colour and the owner

☐ Who made it, where you found it, and the licence

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Who made it, where you found it, and the licence

Who made it, where you found it and the licence. That is enough to check the original and see what you may do.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Omar's teacher says he cannot use his picture. Click the line where Omar first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Omar needs a picture of a bean plant for his slide.
 2  Omar searches the web and clicks the first picture.
 3  He puts it on his slide without finding out who made it or checking the licence, because it was on the internet.
 4  His teacher says he cannot use it.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Omar used the picture without finding out who made it or checking the licence. Being on the internet does not make a picture free. Someone made it, and the licence says what you may do.

:::

## Make: In your own app

Many sites that offer free pictures show the maker and the licence on the picture's page, and some give you a credit line to copy. Check that what you copy says who, where and the licence. If a site shows none of them, choose another picture or draw your own.
