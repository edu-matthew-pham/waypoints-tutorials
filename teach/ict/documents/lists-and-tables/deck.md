# Lists and tables

Documents and slides · Structure

## Today

- I can choose a bulleted list, a numbered list or a table to fit the facts.
- I can say what a table's header row is for.
- I can make each of them in my own word processor.

## Why it matters

> A report gives each condition and its height as three sentences. A reader wants to compare them. What would make that quick?

<!-- Speaker note: Words in a paragraph are good for explaining. Facts often fit better in a **list** or a **table**. Which one depends on what the reader needs to do. - A **bulleted list** is for items with no order, such as the equipment you need. - A **numbered list** is for steps that happen in order, or things in a ranking. - A **table** is for items that each have several facts, so the reader can compare down a column. -->

## Think, pair, share

**A recipe, a shopping list and a class timetable. Which is a numbered list, which is a bulleted list and which is a table, and why?**

- What does the reader do with each one?
- What would be lost if the timetable were a bulleted list?

<!-- Speaker note: Recipe: numbered, because order matters. Shopping list: bulleted, no order. Timetable: a table, since each slot has a day and a time, and the reader looks across and down. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

<!-- Speaker note: The steps lose meaning when shuffled. The shopping list does not. That is the difference between a numbered and a bulleted list. -->

## Choosing the shape

Now: which fits three beans with a colour and a height?

$$\text{Steps in order}$$

1) $\text{Items with no order}$ — Use a bulleted list, such as the equipment you need.
2) $\text{Items with several facts}$ — Use a table, so a reader can compare down a column.
3) $\text{Name the columns}$ — Make the first row a header row, so each column is named.

## Find the mistake

**Ravi's classmates cannot tell which step comes first. Click the line where Ravi first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Ravi writes the four steps of his experiment: plant, water, wait, measure.
 2  Ravi puts them in a bulleted list because bullets look neat.
 3  He shares the report with a partner.
 4  His partner does not know which step to do first.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: the steps have an order, but Ravi used bullets. Bullets say 'these are all here', not 'do this first'. A numbered list shows the order. -->

## What is a header row?

**What does the header row of a table do?**

- Sorts the rows
- Adds up the numbers below
- Names what each column holds
- Makes the table bigger

* Answer: Names what each column holds

<!-- Speaker note: The header row names the columns, and the app can tell the header from the data (for a screen reader, too). -->

## Make: In your own app

Bulleted and numbered lists are buttons near the styles list in every word processor. Tables are under Insert. Most apps have a Header row option in the table's settings, and it is what screen readers use to read column names.

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Making a list and a table in the real app shows the idea is the same everywhere. -->
