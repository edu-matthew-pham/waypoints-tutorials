# Room to read

Documents and slides · Room to read

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that layout is for the reader: space between parts, margins that keep lines short enough to follow, and a left edge for paragraphs. ## Why it matters

> A report is a page and a bit long. A student squeezes it onto one page. Her teacher says it is now harder to read. How can less paper be worse?

<!-- Speaker note: Once the parts are right, the **layout** is how they sit on the page. Three things matter most for a reader: - **Space between parts**, so each part is easy to find. - **Margins**, the empty edge of the page, so lines are not too long to follow. - **Alignment**: paragraphs that start at the same left edge are easy to read, while centred paragraphs start somewhere new on every line. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Look at two pages with the same words. One is crowded and one has room. Where does your eye go first on each, and why?**

- Which part could you find fastest?
- What is the page trying to tell you?

<!-- Speaker note: With room, the eye lands on headings and then paragraphs. Crowded, it has no place to start. White space is how a page tells you where to look. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Opening up the page

Now: why does a left edge help?

$$\text{Space between parts}$$

1) $\text{Margins}$ — Keep them wide enough that lines are not too long to follow.
2) $\text{Alignment}$ — Align body text to the left, so each line starts at the same edge.
3) $\text{The look is for the reader}$ — Fewer pages is not the goal. Use more room if the reader needs it.

## Opening up the page

**Why is a paragraph that starts at the same left edge on every line easier to read than a centred one?**

- It uses less paper
- It looks fancier
- It is shorter
- The eye knows where each new line starts

* Answer: The eye knows where each new line starts

<!-- Speaker note: With a left edge the eye jumps straight back to the same place. With centred lines it has to hunt for the start of each one. -->

## Find the mistake

**Jay's teacher says the report is hard to read. Click the line where Jay first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Jay's report is one page and a bit long, and the limit is one page.
 2  Jay sets the margins to narrow and the space between parts to tight so it fits.
 3  It now fits on one page.
 4  His teacher says it is hard to read.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Jay narrowed the margins and tightened the space to save paper. That made the page crowded. White space is part of the layout, not wasted paper. -->

## Helps or harder?

**Does each choice help a reader, or make the page harder to read?**

Groups: Helps · Harder to read

Space between a heading and the part above it · Lines of text that run edge to edge of a wide page · Body text that starts at the same left edge · No gap between paragraphs

* Answer: Space between a heading and the part above it → Helps; Lines of text that run edge to edge of a wide page → Harder to read; Body text that starts at the same left edge → Helps; No gap between paragraphs → Harder to read

## Make: In your own app

Margins are under Layout (Word) or File › Page setup (Docs). Space between paragraphs and line spacing are in the paragraph settings, and alignment is a button in the toolbar. The defaults are usually a good starting point.
