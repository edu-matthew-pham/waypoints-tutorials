# One idea per slide

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A student copies a paragraph from her report onto a slide and reads it out. Half the class stops listening. What should she have done?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a slide has one idea, a title that says it and few words, because an audience reads a slide while trying to listen. They split a crowded slide by idea, shorten a long bullet and repeat it in the slide app the school uses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can split a slide so each one holds one idea. I can write a title that says the idea. I can cut a bullet to a few words.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: split by idea


:::keep-together

### Splitting a slide

1. $\text{List the ideas}$ — Light, water and soil are three different ideas.
2. $\text{One slide for each}$ — Start a new slide where a new idea starts.
3. $\text{A title that says it}$ — Each slide gets a short title that says its idea.
4. $\text{Few words}$ — Keep a few words for each bullet. You say the rest.

Now: why one idea per slide?

**Why does a slide hold one idea?**

☐ Slides can only be a few words long

☐ The software only allows one

☐ The audience takes in one idea at a glance while listening

☐ It makes the file smaller

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The audience takes in one idea at a glance while listening

People read a slide in a few seconds while listening. One idea is what they can take in.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Half of Lena's audience stopped listening. Click the line where Lena first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Lena has a report paragraph about how beans grow.
 2  Lena copies the whole paragraph onto one slide and reads it aloud.
 3  Her slide has nine lines of small text.
 4  Half of her class stop listening.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Lena copied a paragraph onto the slide. The audience reads it while she talks, so they cannot do both. A slide holds a few words for one idea.

:::


:::keep-together

### A good title

**Which is the better title for a slide with the heights of three beans?**

☐ Slide 3

☐ Bean heights table

☐ Beans grew tallest in the sun

☐ Results

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Beans grew tallest in the sun

A title that says the idea tells the audience what to take away before you start speaking.
