# One idea per slide

Documents and slides · Slides

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a slide has one idea, a title that says it and few words, because an audience reads a slide while trying to listen. They split a crowded slide by idea, shorten a long bullet and repeat it in the slide app the school uses. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 7 min · Practise 8 min · Make and own app 12 min). -->

## Today

- I can split a slide so each one holds one idea.
- I can write a title that says the idea.
- I can cut a bullet to a few words.

## Why it matters

> A student copies a paragraph from her report onto a slide and reads it out. Half the class stops listening. What should she have done?

<!-- Speaker note: A document is read by someone with time. A slide is shown to a group who are also trying to listen to you. So a slide has a different job: - **One idea.** If you have three ideas, make three slides. - **A title that says the idea.** "Beans grew tallest in the sun" tells more than "Results". - **Few words.** A few words to help people remember, not a paragraph for them to read. -->

## Think, pair, share

**You are listening to a talk. The slide has 80 words on it. What do you do, and what do you stop doing?**

- Can you read and listen at the same time?
- What would you rather see on the slide?

<!-- Speaker note: Most people read and stop listening. That is what a crowded slide does. A few words and a clear title let the audience listen to the speaker. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Splitting a slide

Now: why one idea per slide?

$$\text{List the ideas}$$

1) $\text{One slide for each}$ — Start a new slide where a new idea starts.
2) $\text{A title that says it}$ — Each slide gets a short title that says its idea.
3) $\text{Few words}$ — Keep a few words for each bullet. You say the rest.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Line 2: write A title that says it and say "Each slide gets a short title that says its idea." Line 3: write Few words and say "Keep a few words for each bullet. You say the rest." -->

```
 1  Lena has a report paragraph about how beans grow.

 3  Her slide has nine lines of small text.
 4  Half of her class stop listening.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Lena copied a paragraph onto the slide. The audience reads it while she talks, so they cannot do both. A slide holds a few words for one idea. -->

## Exit ticket

**A slide has a title and a paragraph of 60 words. Name two fixes.**

* Cut it to a few words, and if there is more than one idea, split it into slides. Say the rest aloud.
