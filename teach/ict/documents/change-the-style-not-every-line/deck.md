# Change the style, not every line

Documents and slides · Structure

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a style is one definition used by every part that has it, so changing the style once changes them all and any added later, while changes made by hand stay on top of the style. They set the look of a report through its styles and repeat it in the word processor the school uses. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 7 min · Practise 8 min · Make and own app 12 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can change a style once so every part with it changes.
- I can say why changing each part by hand goes wrong.
- I can tell when formatting by hand is the right choice.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: raise Heading 1 to 22 pt in the Styles panel and watch all three headings change. Then select one heading and use Bigger on it, and see that a later change to the style does not reach it. The one idea: look lives in the style. Define it once. By hand is for a one-off. In Word, change a style by right-clicking it in the styles gallery and choosing Modify. In Docs, change one example, then use Update Heading 1 to match. In Pages, use the paragraph styles panel. The page on the screen is a teaching model of that, not any one app. Teacher: Try: ask how many things they changed and how many things moved. Teacher: Worked example: ask Sam what she could have done once instead of six times. Teacher: Own app: the common slip is changing a selected heading and not updating the style. Ask them to add another heading to test. -->

## Why it matters

> A student makes six headings bigger, one click at a time. Next week she adds a seventh part. Will its heading match the others?

<!-- Speaker note: In the last lesson a heading got its **style**. A style is also where the look lives: its size, its font, whether it is bold. Every part with that style looks the same, because they all read the same definition. Here is a report with three Heading 1 parts. The **Styles** panel above the page lets you change a style, and the **Bold** and **Bigger** buttons on a selected part change that one part by hand. -->

## Change a style once

Now: why change the style?

$$\text{Open the style}$$

1) $\text{Change the look}$ — Make it bigger, change the font or turn bold on or off.
2) $\text{Every part follows}$ — All the Heading 1 parts change together, and any you add later start with the new look.
3) $\text{By hand only for one}$ — Use Bold on a selected part only for a single exception, such as one word that needs to stand out.

## Change a style once

**Why change the style instead of each heading?**

- It saves the document
- One change reaches every part with that style
- It makes the headings bold
- It checks the spelling

<!-- Speaker note: A style is one definition, so one change reaches every part that uses it, now and later. -->

## Find the mistake

**Sam's seventh heading does not match the other six. Click the line where Sam first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Sam's report has six parts, and each has a Heading 1 heading.
 2  Sam wants every heading bigger, so she clicks each heading and makes it bigger by hand.
 3  The six headings now look the same.
 4  A week later she adds a seventh part and gives it Heading 1.
 5  The seventh heading is smaller than the other six.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Sam changed each heading by hand. The style itself did not change, so the seventh heading, which uses the style, still has the old look. -->

## What stays on top?

**Sam makes one heading bold by hand, then you remove bold from the Heading 1 style. What happens to Sam's heading?**

- It turns into body text
- It stays bold
- It disappears from the headings panel
- It stops being bold

* Answer: It stays bold

<!-- Speaker note: Formatting by hand stays on top of the style. That is why mixing the two goes wrong. -->

## Style or by hand?

**You want every caption under a picture to be small and grey. Which fits?**

- A style
- By hand on each caption
- Either is fine
- Neither

* Answer: A style

## Make: In your own app

In Word and Docs you change a style by changing one example and telling the app to **update the style** to match. In Pages it is the paragraph styles panel. Either way, the aim is to change it once, not at every heading.

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Changing a style in Word or Docs shows the idea is the same in every app. -->
