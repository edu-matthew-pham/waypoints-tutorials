# Purpose shapes the text

How texts are built · Stages and phases

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a text's purpose decides its stages and its language. ## Today

- I can say how a text's purpose decides its stages.
- I can spot language that fits a recount, a report or an argument.
- I can notice when a text drifts away from its purpose.
- I can write a report with a general statement and descriptions.

## Why it matters

> You know ten facts about frogs. Would you put them in the same order to teach Year 3, to win a debate, or to tell your family about an excursion?

<!-- Speaker note: A text's **purpose** decides its **stages** and its **language**. - **Retell what happened: a recount.** Orientation, events, ending. Past tense and time words. - **Describe what something is like: a report.** General statement, then descriptions. Present tense and technical words. - **Explain how or why: an explanation.** Opening statement, then steps in order. When, then, as a result. - **Persuade: an argument.** Position, reasons, position restated. Should, would, because. -->

**Your class found frogs at the creek. You want the principal to let you build a frog pond. Which shape will your text take?**

- Any shape works if the facts are right
- What you want, your reasons, then what you want again
- What happened at the creek, in time order

## Purpose decides the stages

**Match each purpose to the stages that fit it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `To retell what happened` | A. General statement, descriptions |
| `To describe what something is like` | B. Position, reasons, position restated |
| `To explain how or why` | C. Opening statement, steps in order |
| `To persuade` | D. Orientation, events, ending |

* Answer: To retell what happened → D. Orientation, events, ending; To describe what something is like → A. General statement, descriptions; To explain how or why → C. Opening statement, steps in order; To persuade → B. Position, reasons, position restated

<!-- Speaker note: Each purpose needs different stages. A writer chooses the stages by asking what the text is for. -->

## Sort the language

**Which kind of text does each sentence's language belong to?**

Groups: Recount · Report · Argument

On Friday, we visited the museum. · After that, we ate lunch in the park. · Wombats dig burrows with their front claws. · Eucalypts are evergreen trees. · Our school should build a frog pond. · A pond would let every class study frogs.

* Answer: On Friday, we visited the museum. → Recount; After that, we ate lunch in the park. → Recount; Wombats dig burrows with their front claws. → Report; Eucalypts are evergreen trees. → Report; Our school should build a frog pond. → Argument; A pond would let every class study frogs. → Argument

<!-- Speaker note: Recounts use the past tense and time words, reports the present tense and technical words, and arguments words such as should and would. -->

## Find Nib's drift

**Here is Nib's report on frogs, one sentence per line. Click the line where Nib drifts away from the report's purpose.**

```
 1  Frogs are amphibians that live in water and on land.
 2  Many frogs have long back legs for jumping.
 3  Last week our class went to the creek, and Mr Ali slipped in the mud.
 4  Most frogs lay their eggs in water.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3 retells the excursion: past tense, our class, a funny moment. That belongs in a recount. A report describes frogs in general, so Nib should cut it or move it to a recount of the trip. -->

## Exit ticket

**A text about bushfires is in the present tense, starts 'Bushfires are fires that burn through bush and grassland', and uses technical words such as ember. What is its purpose and type?**

* To describe: it is a report. The general statement and the present tense and technical words fit that purpose.
