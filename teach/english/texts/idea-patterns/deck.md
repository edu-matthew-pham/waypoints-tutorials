# Patterns for ideas

How texts are built · Organising ideas

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn four patterns that organise ideas inside a text: cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution, and claim and support. They label paragraphs by pattern, match signal words, catch a comparison that only covers one side, choose a pattern for a purpose, colour alike and different signals, label a problem and solution paragraph, and write their own paragraph in one pattern. About 50 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 7 min · Practise 7 min · Make and own writing 16 min). -->

## Today

- I can name the pattern a paragraph uses and the signal words that show it.
- I can check that a pattern is carried out, such as both sides of a comparison.
- I can choose a pattern that fits what my text is for.
- I can write a paragraph in one pattern.

## Why it matters

> 'Both' and 'unlike' appear in the first two sentences of a paragraph. What is the paragraph going to do?

<!-- Speaker note: Inside a text, ideas are organised in **patterns**. Four common ones: - **Cause and effect:** what happened and why. Signals: because, so, as a result. - **Compare and contrast:** how two things are alike and different. Signals: both, also, unlike, whereas, but. - **Problem and solution:** a problem, then a way to fix it. Signals: the problem is, one way to fix this. - **Claim and support:** a point, then evidence for it. Signals: for example, this shows. -->

## Predict

**A paragraph starts: 'Cats and dogs both make good pets, but they need very different care.' What will it most likely do next?**

## Signals to patterns

Now match the signals.

$$\text{Ask how the ideas connect}$$

1) $\text{Look for signal words}$ — As a result, unlike, one way to fix this, for example.
2) $\text{Check it is carried out}$ — A comparison needs both sides; a cause needs its effect.
3) $\text{Choose for the purpose}$ — Pick the pattern that does what the text is for.

## Signals to patterns

**Match each set of signal words to its pattern.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `because, so, as a result` | A. Compare and contrast |
| `both, unlike, whereas` | B. Claim and support |
| `the problem is, one way to fix this` | C. Problem and solution |
| `for example, this shows` | D. Cause and effect |

* Answer: because, so, as a result → D. Cause and effect; both, unlike, whereas → A. Compare and contrast; the problem is, one way to fix this → C. Problem and solution; for example, this shows → B. Claim and support

<!-- Speaker note: Signal words tell the reader how the next idea connects to the last, so they show which pattern the writer is using. -->

**Here is Nib's paragraph, one sentence per line. The first line promises to compare possums and sugar gliders. Click the first line where Nib should compare but talks about only one animal.**

## Choose a pattern

**A science text needs to show why a creek floods after heavy storms. Which pattern fits best?**

- Cause and effect
- Claim and support
- Compare and contrast

## Make: your own pattern

In your own document, write a paragraph of at least three sentences in one pattern: cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution, or claim and support. Choose the pattern first. Use at least two signal or joining words, such as *because*, *so*, *as a result*, *but*, *also*, *whereas* or *unlike*.

Here is one example, in cause and effect: "Our street flooded last week **because** the drains were blocked with leaves. **As a result**, three houses had water in their garages. The water **then** took two days to drain away."

Then swap with a partner. Without being told, name each other's pattern and the signal words that showed it. Is the pattern carried through the whole paragraph?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing a paragraph in one chosen pattern, with its signal words, makes the pattern a writing tool. Hints, in order: Choose a pattern first. Cause and effect: something that happened and why. Compare and contrast: two things. Problem and solution: a problem you know. Claim and support: a point and an example. / Use at least two signal or joining words, such as because, so, as a result, but, also, whereas or unlike. -->
