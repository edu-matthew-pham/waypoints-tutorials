# Patterns for ideas

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'Both' and 'unlike' appear in the first two sentences of a paragraph. What is the paragraph going to do?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn four patterns that organise ideas inside a text: cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution, and claim and support. They label paragraphs by pattern, match signal words, catch a comparison that only covers one side, choose a pattern for a purpose, colour alike and different signals, label a problem and solution paragraph, and write their own paragraph in one pattern.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the pattern a paragraph uses and the signal words that show it. I can check that a pattern is carried out, such as both sides of a comparison. I can choose a pattern that fits what my text is for. I can write a paragraph in one pattern.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: signals and choices


:::keep-together

### Signals to patterns

1. $\text{Ask how the ideas connect}$ — Does one cause another? Are two things set side by side? Is a problem fixed? Is a point backed up?
2. $\text{Look for signal words}$ — As a result, unlike, one way to fix this, for example.
3. $\text{Check it is carried out}$ — A comparison needs both sides; a cause needs its effect.
4. $\text{Choose for the purpose}$ — Pick the pattern that does what the text is for.

Now match the signals.

**Match each set of signal words to its pattern.**

A. Compare and contrast

B. Claim and support

C. Problem and solution

D. Cause and effect

{{columns 60% 40%}}

{{rows 1}}

| Part | Letter |
|---|---|
| `because, so, as a result` | |
| `both, unlike, whereas` | |
| `the problem is, one way to fix this` | |
| `for example, this shows` | |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** because, so, as a result → D. Cause and effect; both, unlike, whereas → A. Compare and contrast; the problem is, one way to fix this → C. Problem and solution; for example, this shows → B. Claim and support

Signal words tell the reader how the next idea connects to the last, so they show which pattern the writer is using.

:::

### Find Nib's slip

**Here is Nib's paragraph, one sentence per line. The first line promises to compare possums and sugar gliders. Click the first line where Nib should compare but talks about only one animal.**

```
 1  Possums and sugar gliders are both marsupials that live in trees.
 2  Possums are about the size of a cat.
 3  They have thick fur and a long tail that grips branches.
 4  They eat leaves, flowers and fruit.
 5  Possums are often seen in city gardens at night.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Nib describes possums and never mentions gliders again. A compare and contrast paragraph needs both sides: 'Possums are about the size of a cat, whereas sugar gliders could sit in your hand.'

:::

### Choose a pattern

**A science text needs to show why a creek floods after heavy storms. Which pattern fits best?**

☐ Cause and effect

☐ Claim and support

☐ Compare and contrast

☐ Problem and solution

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Cause and effect

The purpose is to show why, so the writer needs causes and their effects. A different purpose, such as proposing a fix, would call for problem and solution.

## Problem and solution


## Make: your own pattern

In your own document, write a paragraph of at least three sentences in one pattern: cause and effect, compare and contrast, problem and solution, or claim and support. Choose the pattern first. Use at least two signal or joining words, such as *because*, *so*, *as a result*, *but*, *also*, *whereas* or *unlike*.

Here is one example, in cause and effect: "Our street flooded last week **because** the drains were blocked with leaves. **As a result**, three houses had water in their garages. The water **then** took two days to drain away."

Then swap with a partner. Without being told, name each other's pattern and the signal words that showed it. Is the pattern carried through the whole paragraph?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
