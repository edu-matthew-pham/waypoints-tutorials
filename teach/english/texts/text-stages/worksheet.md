# Every text has a plan

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A friend starts telling you about their weekend with 'Then we went home.' What's missing?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** An optional warm-up from Years 4 and 5. Students learn that a text is built in stages that each do a job, using the recount: orientation (who, where, when), events in time order, and an ending. They label the stages, put a jumbled recount back in order, find the stages inside an email, and write and label their own recount.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the stages of a recount and say what each one does. I can put a recount's events in time order. I can tell a text's form (an email, a speech) from its stages. I can write a recount with all three stages.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: find the stages


:::keep-together

### Finding the stages

1. $\text{Ask what the text is for}$ — A recount retells something that happened.
2. $\text{Find the orientation}$ — Who was there, where and when. It sets the scene.
3. $\text{Find the events}$ — What happened, in time order. Time words (First, Next, After lunch) mark them.
4. $\text{Find the ending}$ — How it finished, or what the writer thought of it.

Now: what is the orientation's job?

**What is the orientation's job?**

☐ To list everything that happened

☐ To say how it all ended

☐ To give the writer's opinion of the day

☐ To tell the reader who, where and when

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** To tell the reader who, where and when

The orientation sets the scene, so the reader can picture the events that follow.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's slip

**Here is Nib's recount, one sentence per line. Click the line where the reader needed an orientation but didn't get one.**

```
 1  Then we ate our lunch next to the lake.
 2  After that, we fed the ducks some oats.
 3  Later, it started to rain, so we ran to the car.
 4  It was a great day, even with the rain.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 1

Line 1: Nib starts with Then, in the middle of the day. The reader doesn't know who went, where the lake is or when it happened. Nib needs an orientation first, such as 'On Saturday, my class went to the lake for a picnic.'

:::


:::keep-together

### Form or stage?

**Is each one a form (what the text looks like) or a stage (a part with a job)?**

Groups: Form, Stage

- an email: {{field-15}}
- a speech: {{field-15}}
- a poster: {{field-15}}
- the orientation: {{field-15}}
- the events: {{field-15}}
- the ending: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** an email → Form; a speech → Form; a poster → Form; the orientation → Stage; the events → Stage; the ending → Stage

The form is what a text looks like or how it is delivered. The stages are the parts inside it, each with a job. A recount keeps its stages whether it's an email, a speech or a story told at dinner.
