How texts are built · Stages and phases

## Why it matters

> You're explaining to a Year 2 student how rain forms. Where do you start, and how many steps are there?

<!-- Speaker note: A text's big parts are its **stages**. Inside a stage there can be smaller moves called **phases**. An **explanation** says how or why something happens. It has two stages: - An **opening statement:** what is being explained. - An **explanation sequence:** the steps, in order. Each step is a **phase**. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Why do explanations need their steps in order, when a description doesn't?**

- What happens to a reader if a step comes before the step it depends on?
- Could you describe a library in any order? Could you explain rain in any order?

<!-- Speaker note: An explanation follows a process or a chain of causes, so the order is the meaning. A description can be organised many ways (top to bottom, most to least important). Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## What is a phase?

Now: what is a phase?

$$\text{Find the stages}$$

1) $\text{Find the phases}$ — Inside a stage, ask what smaller moves build it, such as each step.
2) $\text{Check the order}$ — In an explanation, each step should depend only on steps before it.
3) $\text{Use the signals}$ — Words such as when, then and as a result mark the phases.

## What is a phase?

**What is a phase?**

- The last stage of a text, after the others
- Another name for a paragraph in a long text
- Any sentence that has a joining word in it
- A smaller move inside a stage, such as one step

* Answer: A smaller move inside a stage, such as one step

<!-- Speaker note: Stages are the big parts. Phases are the smaller moves that build each stage. -->

## Find Nib's slip

**Here is Nib's explanation of how a seed grows, one sentence per line. Click the first line that comes before a step it depends on.**

```
 1  A seed is a tiny plant with a store of food inside a tough coat.
 2  The shoot pushes up through the soil towards the light.
 3  When the seed is in damp, warm soil, it soaks up water and swells.
 4  The coat splits, and a root grows down into the soil.
 5  Once its first leaves open, the plant makes its own food from sunlight.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: the shoot can't push up before the seed has soaked up water and split its coat (lines 3 and 4). In an explanation, each phase depends on the one before, so the order must follow the process. -->

## Exit ticket

**In an explanation of how a volcano erupts, which phase must come before 'melted rock bursts out of the top'?**
