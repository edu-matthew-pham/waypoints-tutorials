# Stages and phases

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You're explaining to a Year 2 student how rain forms. Where do you start, and how many steps are there?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that stages have smaller phases inside them. They label an explanation's opening statement and the phases of its explanation sequence, catch phases written out of order, match signal words to stages, put a jumbled explanation back in order, label the stages of a review, and write and label their own explanation.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the stages of an explanation and a review. I can find the phases inside a stage and put them in order. I can use signal words to tell where I am in a text. I can write an explanation with an opening statement and steps in order.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: phases and their signals


:::keep-together

### What is a phase?

1. $\text{Find the stages}$ — Ask what job each big part does: name it, or explain it?
2. $\text{Find the phases}$ — Inside a stage, ask what smaller moves build it, such as each step.
3. $\text{Check the order}$ — In an explanation, each step should depend only on steps before it.
4. $\text{Use the signals}$ — Words such as when, then and as a result mark the phases.

Now: what is a phase?

**What is a phase?**

☐ The last stage of a text, after the others

☐ Another name for a paragraph in a long text

☐ Any sentence that has a joining word in it

☐ A smaller move inside a stage, such as one step

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A smaller move inside a stage, such as one step

Stages are the big parts. Phases are the smaller moves that build each stage.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's slip

**Here is Nib's explanation of how a seed grows, one sentence per line. Click the first line that comes before a step it depends on.**

```
 1  A seed is a tiny plant with a store of food inside a tough coat.
 2  The shoot pushes up through the soil towards the light.
 3  When the seed is in damp, warm soil, it soaks up water and swells.
 4  The coat splits, and a root grows down into the soil.
 5  Once its first leaves open, the plant makes its own food from sunlight.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the shoot can't push up before the seed has soaked up water and split its coat (lines 3 and 4). In an explanation, each phase depends on the one before, so the order must follow the process.

:::


:::keep-together
