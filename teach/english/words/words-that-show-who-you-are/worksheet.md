# Words that show who you are

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A surfer, a farmer and a nurse each have words the others would not follow. What do a group's words say about the people who use them?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that the words we use show the groups we belong to and help make that belonging. They label club jargon, match features to what they signal, tell showing from making belonging, spot a judgement made from speech, choose wording for a newcomer and write a short scene.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say how words show the groups I belong to. I can explain how using a group's words helps make belonging. I can explain why how someone speaks does not show their character. I can write a scene where a character's words show their group.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: what do the words signal?


:::keep-together

### Match the feature to the signal

**Match each way of speaking to what it can signal.**

A. Which place you may be from

B. That you know each other well

C. That you are in a formal setting

D. That you are part of that group

{{columns 60% 40%}}

| Part | Letter |
|---|---|
| `Words only the group uses` | |

| `Switching to formal words` | |
| `Calling someone by a nickname` | |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Words only the group uses → D. That you are part of that group; A local way of speaking → A. Which place you may be from; Switching to formal words → C. That you are in a formal setting; Calling someone by a nickname → B. That you know each other well

Each feature signals something about the speaker's group or situation. None signals how good or bad a person is.

:::

### Showing and making

**Zoe joins the chess club and starts saying 'gambit' and 'castle'. What is happening?**

☐ Her words only show who she already was before joining

☐ She is using the wrong words for her old friends

☐ Her words show her new group, and using them helps her join it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Her words show her new group, and using them helps her join it

Language shows a group and also helps make belonging.

:::


:::keep-together

### Spot the judgement

**Here is Nib's thinking about Jo, one step at a time. One step judges a person from how he speaks. Click it.**

```
 1  Jo spoke to the visitor in short, quick sentences.
 2  Jo did not use a long greeting.
 3  So Jo must be rude.
 4  I will say hello to Jo.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Step 3 says Jo 'must be rude' from short sentences alone. People speak in many valid ways, and how someone speaks tells you nothing about their character.

## Make: a character speaks

In your own document, write a short scene of at least four sentences with a character who speaks at least twice. Put the spoken words in quotation marks. Choose words that show which group the character belongs to, such as a club, a team or a crew.

Here is one example: Dev unrolled the sail and shouted, 'Ready about!' 'Ready,' called Priya from the tiller. They waited for the wind to fill the sail.

Then swap with a partner. Which group does the character belong to, and which words told you?
