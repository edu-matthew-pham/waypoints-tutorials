## Why it matters

> A surfer, a farmer and a nurse each have words the others would not follow. What do a group's words say about the people who use them?

<!-- Speaker note: We speak and write differently in different groups. A sailing club, a chess club and a drama club each have their own words. These are the special words only that group uses. Our words do two things: - They **show** which groups we belong to. - They **help make** that belonging, because using a group's words is part of joining it. People speak in many ways, and every way works for the family, group or place that uses it. How a person speaks tells you nothing about whether they are clever, kind or rude. -->

## Match the feature to the signal

**Match each way of speaking to what it can signal.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Words only the group uses` | A. Which place you may be from |
| `A local way of speaking` | B. That you know each other well |
| `Switching to formal words` | C. That you are in a formal setting |
| `Calling someone by a nickname` | D. That you are part of that group |

* Answer: Words only the group uses → D. That you are part of that group; A local way of speaking → A. Which place you may be from; Switching to formal words → C. That you are in a formal setting; Calling someone by a nickname → B. That you know each other well

<!-- Speaker note: Each feature signals something about the speaker's group or situation. None signals how good or bad a person is. -->

## Make: a character speaks

In your own document, write a short scene of at least four sentences with a character who speaks at least twice. Put the spoken words in quotation marks. Choose words that show which group the character belongs to, such as a club, a team or a crew.

Here is one example: Dev unrolled the sail and shouted, 'Ready about!' 'Ready,' called Priya from the tiller. They waited for the wind to fill the sail.

Then swap with a partner. Which group does the character belong to, and which words told you?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing a short scene with a character shows how word choice builds a character's group and place. Hints, in order: Which group does your character belong to? / Which words would they use that others would not? -->
