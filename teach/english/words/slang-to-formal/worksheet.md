## Worked example: polite or formal?


:::keep-together

### Polite or formal?

1. $\text{Look for short forms and slang}$ — Do the words sound relaxed or official?
2. $\text{Look for warmth}$ — A warm, polite line can still be casual.
3. $\text{Decide the step}$ — Polite is about kindness. Formal is about how official the words are.

**Is each line polite but not formal, or formal?**

Groups: Polite but not formal, Formal

- Thanks so much, that's really kind!: {{field-15}}
- I wish to express my gratitude for your assistance.: {{field-15}}
- Could you give me a hand, please?: {{field-15}}
- Kindly refrain from using mobile telephones.: {{field-15}}
- Would you mind shutting the window, please?: {{field-15}}
- The board requests that members be seated.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Thanks so much, that's really kind! → Polite but not formal; I wish to express my gratitude for your assistance. → Formal; Could you give me a hand, please? → Polite but not formal; Kindly refrain from using mobile telephones. → Formal; Would you mind shutting the window, please? → Polite but not formal; The board requests that members be seated. → Formal

:::

### Spot the casual thank-you

**Here is Nib's formal letter to Mr Hall, one line at a time. One line is friendly and polite, but not formal. Click it.**

```
 1  Dear Mr Hall,
 2  I am writing to report on the canteen survey.
 3  Thanks a heap for letting us run it, it was great!
 4  The results are attached.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 thanks Mr Hall in a warm, casual way ('Thanks a heap', 'it was great!'). That is polite but not formal. A formal line would be: 'Thank you for permitting us to run it.'

:::

### Name a feature of formal language

**Which is a feature of formal writing?**

☐ Full forms such as do not, and exact words

☐ Slang that shows you belong to the group

☐ Short forms such as don't and can't, to sound natural

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Full forms such as do not, and exact words

Formal writing uses full forms, exact words and no slang.

## Make: write it formally

Here is a message a student wrote to the principal, Mr Hall. It sounds like a text to a friend.

> Hey Mr Hall! We reckon the oval's heaps too muddy for footy, so can we use the gym instead? It'd be awesome!

In your own document, copy the message and rewrite it formally, in at least three sentences. Use full forms, such as *do not* and not *don't*. Leave out slang and exclamation marks, and keep the same news and the same request.

Here is one example of a different message: instead of "Yeah, we're gonna be late, sorry!", write "We will arrive a little after the start. We apologise for the delay."

Then swap with a partner. Underline any word they would not say to the principal, and check for short forms hiding in words such as *oval's*.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
