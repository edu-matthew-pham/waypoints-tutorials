# From slang to formal

Language for people and situations · Formality, identity and roles

<!-- Speaker note: Students place sentences on a four-step scale from slang to formal, separate polite from formal, spot a casual line in a formal letter, name features of formal language, choose formal wording and rewrite an informal message formally. ## Why it matters

> Two students describe the same excursion, one to a friend and one in a report. Would their words be the same?

<!-- Speaker note: Words change with the situation. A useful way to see it is a scale with four steps: - **Slang**: very casual words used by a close group. *That's sick!* - **Colloquial**: everyday, relaxed words. *Too easy, mate.* - **Conversational**: ordinary speech that most people would not mind hearing anywhere. *I'm heading to the library.* - **Formal**: full forms, exact words and no slang. *I shall be at the library until noon.* **Polite** and **formal** are different. You can be polite in a casual way (*Cheers, that's really kind of you!*), and you can be formal without being warm. -->

## Name a feature of formal language

**Which is a feature of formal writing?**

- Full forms such as do not, and exact words
- Slang that shows you belong to the group
- Short forms such as don't and can't, to sound natural

## Make: write it formally

Here is a message a student wrote to the principal, Mr Hall. It sounds like a text to a friend.

> Hey Mr Hall! We reckon the oval's heaps too muddy for footy, so can we use the gym instead? It'd be awesome!

In your own document, copy the message and rewrite it formally, in at least three sentences. Use full forms, such as *do not* and not *don't*. Leave out slang and exclamation marks, and keep the same news and the same request.

Here is one example of a different message: instead of "Yeah, we're gonna be late, sorry!", write "We will arrive a little after the start. We apologise for the delay."

Then swap with a partner. Underline any word they would not say to the principal, and check for short forms hiding in words such as *oval's*.
