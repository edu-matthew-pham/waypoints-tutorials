Language for people and situations · Formality, identity and roles

## Why it matters

> Two volunteers in the same group talk to a newcomer. One explains every word, and one uses words nobody has told the newcomer. How will the newcomer feel?

<!-- Speaker note: In a group, words do more than carry information. They also **build roles**: who leads, who is an equal and who helps. - **Acronyms** (short names made from first letters) and **specialist terms** (exact words for the group's work) can help a newcomer or confuse them. - **Forms of address** are the names and titles used to speak to a person, such as *Coach* or *Ms Ortiz*. They show a relationship and who is more senior. - **Pronouns** such as *we*, *us* and *I* place the speaker beside others or above them. A *we* or *us* that includes the listener is an **inclusive pronoun**. A group's words are not good or bad. The question is what they do for the people hearing them. -->

## Predict

**A new member of a bushwalking club is told, 'Put it in the log under the usual.' How does the new member most likely feel?**

- Confused, because nobody has explained the words
- Welcome, because the club shares special words and the instruction is short
- Excited, because a short instruction means a trusted member

## Make: a club announcement

In your own document, write an announcement for a made-up club, at least four sentences. Write for readers who include newcomers. Use *I*, *me*, *my*, *we*, *us* or *our* at least twice, and explain any special club word.

Here is one example: "**We** are starting a chess club at lunchtime on Tuesdays. Everyone is welcome, including people who have never played. **I** will bring spare boards and show you the basics. **Our** first game is next week in the library."

Then swap with a partner. Would a newcomer follow every word? What role do your pronouns give you?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing an announcement for a made-up club puts roles and welcoming wording to use. Hints, in order: Who is the announcement for, including newcomers? / Where could you say we or our to include the readers? -->
