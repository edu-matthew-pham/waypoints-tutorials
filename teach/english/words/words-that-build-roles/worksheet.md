# Words that build roles

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two volunteers in the same group talk to a newcomer. One explains every word, and one uses words nobody has told the newcomer. How will the newcomer feel?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see how words build roles in a group. They label made-up reef crew lines by kind of word, sort lines that welcome or leave out a newcomer, find unexplained acronyms, spot the line that sounds like a boss, choose welcoming wording, place lines as boss, equal or helper and write a club announcement.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name acronyms, specialist terms, forms of address and pronouns. I can tell when a group's words welcome or leave out a newcomer. I can explain how pronouns and commands build roles. I can write an announcement a newcomer can follow.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: welcome or leave out?

### Welcomes or leaves out?

1. $\text{Find the group's words}$ — Acronyms, specialist terms and 'the usual'.
2. $\text{Ask who they are for}$ — Does a newcomer know them?
3. $\text{Fix it}$ — Explain them once, or use plain words.

### Who sounds like the boss?

**Which line makes the speaker sound like the boss?**

☐ Let us share the survey: you hold the tape and I will record the numbers.

☐ Leave the survey to me; you just hold the tape.

☐ I would like to learn how you hold the tape, if that is all right.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Leave the survey to me; you just hold the tape.

Leave it to me and you just ... put the speaker above the others. The pronouns and commands build the role.

## Boss, equal or helper?


## Make: a club announcement

In your own document, write an announcement for a made-up club, at least four sentences. Write for readers who include newcomers. Use *I*, *me*, *my*, *we*, *us* or *our* at least twice, and explain any special club word.

Here is one example: "**We** are starting a chess club at lunchtime on Tuesdays. Everyone is welcome, including people who have never played. **I** will bring spare boards and show you the basics. **Our** first game is next week in the library."

Then swap with a partner. Would a newcomer follow every word? What role do your pronouns give you?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
