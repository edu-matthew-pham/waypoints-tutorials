# Who is it for?

Language for people and situations · Formality, identity and roles

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that familiarity (how well you know someone) and power (their authority) both shape how formal to be. ## Why it matters

> Two messages ask for the same favour, one to a cousin and one to a stranger who runs the hall. Would the words match?

<!-- Speaker note: Two things shape how formal your words should be. - **Familiarity**: how well you know the person. Close friends get relaxed words. - **Power**: who has more authority in the situation. A principal, a judge or a coach can decide things about you. The two cues can agree or pull in different directions. Think about both before you choose words. -->

## Familiarity or power?

$$\text{Ask: how well do I know them?}$$

1) $\text{Ask: who decides?}$ — The one with authority gets respect.
2) $\text{Combine them}$ — A warm request can be both relaxed and respectful.

## Choose the opening

**Ava has played for her coach for three years and they joke often. She emails him to ask to miss training. Which opening fits?**

- Hey Coach, I'm skipping Thursday.
- Hi Coach, I hope you're well. Could I please miss training on Thursday?
- Dear Mr Wilson, I respectfully request permission to be absent from Thursday's training session.

* Answer: Hi Coach, I hope you're well. Could I please miss training on Thursday?

<!-- Speaker note: Warm because Ava knows the coach well, and a request because the coach has the authority to say yes or no. -->

## Make: three openings

In your own document, write three openings for three readers: a friend, a coach you know well and the principal. Write two sentences for each, at least six sentences in all. Think about how well you know each person and whether they have authority.

Here is one example, for a friend: "Hey Sam, are you free after school? I found a good spot for the project." Your coach and the principal need different words.

Then swap with a partner. For each opening, say how well the writer knows the person and whether the person has authority.
