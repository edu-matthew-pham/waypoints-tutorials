# Same message, different words

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You text your friend 'On my way!' Would you send those words to the principal?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that the same message can be worded in many ways and that the words that fit depend on the reader and the purpose. They place ways of asking on a scale, sort messages by reader, spot wording pitched for the wrong reader, choose openings, choose inclusive wording and write two messages about one event.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell informal wording from formal wording. I can choose words that fit who is reading. I can explain why formal is not simply more correct. I can choose wording that includes every reader.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Max texts a friend, 'Cheers for the lift!' and writes to a guest speaker, 'Thank you for visiting our school.' Is either one wrong?**

☐ Both are wrong, because a message should read the same for everyone

☐ The letter is wrong, because it is too stiff for a school

☐ Neither is wrong: each fits the person who reads it

☐ The text is wrong, because it is not formal

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Neither is wrong. 'Cheers for the lift!' suits a friend, and 'Thank you for visiting our school.' suits a guest. Formal language is not more correct; it fits some readers and jobs, and informal language fits others.

## Worked example: which words fit the reader?


:::keep-together

### Which words fit the reader?

1. $\text{Who is reading?}$ — A friend, or someone you do not know well?
2. $\text{What is the message for?}$ — To chat, to ask, or to thank?
3. $\text{Choose the words}$ — Pick the ones that fit both.

**Does each message fit a friend or the principal?**

Groups: Fits a friend, Fits the principal

- Can't come, sorry mate!: {{field-15}}
- I regret that I am unable to attend.: {{field-15}}
- See ya tomorrow!: {{field-15}}
- I look forward to seeing you tomorrow.: {{field-15}}
- No worries, it's all good.: {{field-15}}
- That is quite all right; please do not apologise.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Can't come, sorry mate! → Fits a friend; I regret that I am unable to attend. → Fits the principal; See ya tomorrow! → Fits a friend; I look forward to seeing you tomorrow. → Fits the principal; No worries, it's all good. → Fits a friend; That is quite all right; please do not apologise. → Fits the principal

:::


:::keep-together

### Spot the words for the wrong reader

**Here is Nib's request to the principal, one sentence per line. One line is pitched for a friend, not the principal. Click it.**

```
 1  Dear Ms Tran,
 2  I am writing to ask whether our class may use the hall on Friday.
 3  We reckon it'll be heaps good for everyone.
 4  Thank you for considering my request.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 uses 'We reckon it'll be heaps good', which is relaxed wording for a friend. The other lines are careful and complete. Nib wrote the same way to everyone.

:::


:::keep-together

### Choose the opening

**Nib wants to ask the mayor for a new bin in the park. Which opening fits best?**

☐ Mayor Rossi, you need to put a bin in Elm Park now.

☐ Hey Mayor, put a bin in the park.

☐ Dear Mayor Rossi, I am writing to ask for a bin in Elm Park.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Dear Mayor Rossi, I am writing to ask for a bin in Elm Park.

Nib is writing to someone with authority whom Nib does not know. Careful words and a polite request fit that reader and that job.

## Make: two messages about one event

In your own document, pick one event, such as an excursion or a match. Write two short messages about it: a text to a friend and a note to a teacher. Write at least four sentences in all. Use at least one short form, such as *I'm* or *can't*, in the text to your friend.

Here is one example: "Hey Zoe, our excursion to the museum was awesome! We **didn't** even have to wait in line." and "Dear Mr Lee, thank you for organising the excursion to the museum. The class learned a great deal about the dinosaur display."

Then swap with a partner. Point to the words that fit each reader. What changed between the two messages?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
