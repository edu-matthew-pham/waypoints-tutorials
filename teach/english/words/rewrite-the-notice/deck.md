# Challenge: rewrite the notice

Language for people and situations · Challenge: rewrite the notice

<!-- Speaker note: The challenge. Students move Nib's notice up the scale in four parts: place the sentences, choose formal wording, colour the informal words and rewrite the whole notice formally, then check a real message of their own. About 45 minutes (Predict 3 min · Parts 1 to 3 15 min · Part 4 15 min · Own writing 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can place sentences on the scale from slang to formal.
- I can choose formal wording for a reader.
- I can find informal words in a notice.
- I can rewrite a message so it fits its readers.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do Part 4 yourself first: rewrite Nib's notice for parents. ## Why it matters

> A notice with the right news in the wrong voice. Can you make it fit its readers?

<!-- Speaker note: Nib wrote a notice about training moving to the gym. It is going to parents and the principal, but Nib wrote it the way Nib would text a friend. ## Make: Part 4: rewrite the notice

Here is Nib's whole notice.

> Hey everyone! The oval's heaps too wet, so training's in the gym on Thursday at four. Bring ya runners and a drink bottle. No worries if you're late. Cheers, Nib

In your own document, copy the notice and rewrite it for parents and the principal, in at least four sentences. Use full forms, such as *do not* and not *don't*. Leave out slang and exclamation marks. Keep the news: where, when, what to bring and what to do if late.

Here is a model after you have tried: "Training will now be held in the gym on Thursday at four, because the oval is too wet to use. Students should bring their runners and a drink bottle. Students who arrive late may join the session when they arrive. Regards, Nib"

Then swap with a partner. Underline any word they would not say to the principal. Check for short forms hiding in words such as *oval's*. Does every piece of news still make it through?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Rewriting the whole notice formally puts the scale and the reader to use together. Hints, in order: Which words in Nib's notice have you found are informal? / Who is reading, and what do they need to know? For students who need more support: A list of Nib's informal words with a blank beside each for a formal replacement. For early finishers: Rewrite the notice a second time for students in Year 3. The hard part, on purpose: Students fix the slang but keep short forms, or make the notice stiff and unfriendly. Ask 'who is reading, and what do they need to know?'. When students get stuck: A student keeps 'No worries' because it is polite. Ask: Polite or formal? Fix: It is polite and relaxed. A formal notice would say 'Late arrivals are welcome'. -->
