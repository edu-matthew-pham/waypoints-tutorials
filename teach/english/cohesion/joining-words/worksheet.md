# Words that join ideas

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'It rained all morning. So the match went ahead.' Something is wrong, but every word is spelled right. What is it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that joining words (connectives) tell the reader how two ideas connect: adding, turning against, showing a result, or putting things in time order. They are not interchangeable. Students choose joining words in gaps, sort them by job, and use three different ones in their own recount.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say what job a joining word does (adds, turns, result or time). I can choose a joining word that fits how two ideas connect. I can use different joining words in my own writing.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: how do they connect?


:::keep-together

### Choosing a joining word

1. $\text{Read both ideas}$ — Read the sentence before the gap and the sentence after it.
2. $\text{Ask how they connect}$ — Does the second add, turn against, follow as a result, or come next in time?
3. $\text{Pick a word for that job}$ — Adds: also, in addition. Turns: however, but. Result: so, as a result. Time: first, then, finally.
4. $\text{Read it aloud}$ — Say both sentences with the word in. If it sounds wrong, check the job again.

Now sort some joining words by their job.

**Sort each joining word by the job it does.**

Groups: Adds, Turns against, Shows a result, Shows time order

## Steps in order


## Make: your own joins

In your own document, write a recount of four or five sentences about something you made or did, step by step. Use at least three different joining words. At least one should not be about time: try *however*, *so*, *also* or *as a result*.

Here is one example: "We made pancakes for breakfast. **First**, we mixed flour, eggs and milk. **Then** we heated the pan. The first pancake stuck, **so** we added more butter. **Finally**, we ate them with lemon."

Then swap with a partner. Name the job of each other's joining words: adds, turns against, shows a result or shows time order. Do you agree? Where you don't, did the writer mean a different job?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
