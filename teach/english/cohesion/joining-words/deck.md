# Words that join ideas

Holding writing together · Warm-up: pointing and joining

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that joining words (connectives) tell the reader how two ideas connect: adding, turning against, showing a result, or putting things in time order. They are not interchangeable. Students choose joining words in gaps, sort them by job, and use three different ones in their own recount. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 6 min · Practise 6 min · Make and own writing 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can say what job a joining word does (adds, turns, result or time).
- I can choose a joining word that fits how two ideas connect.
- I can use different joining words in my own writing.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: the gaps each offer three joining words that do different jobs. The one idea: a joining word says how the next idea connects to the last one, so swapping one changes the meaning. This is an optional warm-up from Year 4 (AC9E4LA04 nodes 2 and 3: connectives between sentences, and temporal connectives). The four jobs (adds, turns, result, time) are a simplified set. AC9E4LA04 also names conditional connectives (if, unless), which this lesson leaves to class; Year 9 (AC9E9LA04) widens the set further to conclusion and expansion. What changed the meaning? -->

## Why it matters

> 'It rained all morning. So the match went ahead.' Something is wrong, but every word is spelled right. What is it?

<!-- Speaker note: Two sentences side by side don't say how they connect. A **joining word** (a connective) tells the reader: the next idea **adds** to the last one, **turns against** it, **follows from** it, or **comes next** in time. -->

**Can two sentences connect without a joining word at all?**

- Read 'It rained. We stayed in.' How do you know how these connect?
- When does a reader need the joining word to be written down?

## Predict

**'It rained all morning. However, the match went ahead.' Change However to So. What happens to the meaning?**

- It now says the rain stopped
- It now says the match went ahead because of the rain, which sounds odd
- Nothing: both just join the sentences
- It becomes more formal

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: So says the second idea happened because of the first: the rain caused the match to go ahead. However says it happened in spite of the rain. Same words, different meaning. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Choosing a joining word

Now sort some joining words by their job.

$$\text{Read both ideas}$$

1) $\text{Ask how they connect}$ — Does the second add, turn against, follow as a result, or come next in time?
2) $\text{Pick a word for that job}$ — Adds: also, in addition. Turns: however, but. Result: so, as a result. Time: first, then, finally.
3) $\text{Read it aloud}$ — Say both sentences with the word in. If it sounds wrong, check the job again.

## Find Nib's slip

**Nib joined two sentences: 'Our team trained every afternoon. ___ we won the final.' Click the line where Nib first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib reads both sentences: training every afternoon, then winning the final.
 2  Nib picks however, because it sounds like a grown-up word.
 3  Nib writes: Our team trained every afternoon. However, we won the final.
 4  Nib reads it back and it sounds a bit strange.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: Nib chose however because it sounds grown-up, without asking how the ideas connect. Winning followed from training, so the right job is a result: so, or as a result. -->

## Make: your own joins

In your own document, write a recount of four or five sentences about something you made or did, step by step. Use at least three different joining words. At least one should not be about time: try *however*, *so*, *also* or *as a result*.

Here is one example: "We made pancakes for breakfast. **First**, we mixed flour, eggs and milk. **Then** we heated the pan. The first pancake stuck, **so** we added more butter. **Finally**, we ate them with lemon."

Then swap with a partner. Name the job of each other's joining words: adds, turns against, shows a result or shows time order. Do you agree? Where you don't, did the writer mean a different job?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing their own short recount with three different joining words moves the choice from picking to producing. Hints, in order: Think of something you made or did. Write what happened, step by step. / Use at least three different joining words, and at least one that is not about time: however, so, also, as a result. -->
