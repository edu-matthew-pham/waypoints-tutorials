# Word chains

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'Grandpa bought a kelpie. The dog follows him everywhere.' The second sentence never says kelpie. How do you know it is the same animal?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a general word (the dog, the animal, the boat) can stand in for a specific one the reader has already met, making a word chain that keeps a text moving without repeating the same word. They colour word chains, trace general words back with arrows, and build a word chain in their own paragraph.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find a general word that stands in for a specific one. I can say what specific thing a general word points back to. I can use a word chain in my own writing, with the specific word first.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: general for specific


:::keep-together

### Linking a general word

1. $\text{Spot a general word}$ — Look for a word for a group: dog, animal, vehicle, city, tool, team.
2. $\text{Look back}$ — Find the specific thing it could stand for: a kelpie, the tractor, Hobart.
3. $\text{Check it's the only one}$ — If two things could fit (two dogs), the reader can't tell. Use the name instead.
4. $\text{Check the order}$ — The specific word must come first, or the general word has nothing to link to.

Now match some specific words to the general words that could stand in for them.

**Match each specific word to a general word that could stand in for it later.**

A. the dog

B. the tool

C. the city

D. apples

**Answer:** Granny Smiths → D. apples; a kelpie → A. the dog; Hobart → C. the city; a hammer → B. the tool

Each general word names the group the specific one belongs to, so a reader can link them.

## Three chains


## Make: your own chain

In your own document, write four sentences about a specific thing: your pet, a favourite place or a team. Name it first. Then use a general word for it at least once (the dog, the town, the team).

Then use the highlighter in your document. Colour the chain for your thing in one colour, and a second chain in another colour if you have one.

Here is one example: "**Ollie** is our **cat**. **The animal** sleeps on **the couch** all day. **That sofa** is covered in fur." *Ollie*, *cat* and *the animal* are one chain. *The couch* and *That sofa* are a second one.

Then swap with a partner. Does every general word come after the specific one it stands for? Could any of them be a different thing?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
