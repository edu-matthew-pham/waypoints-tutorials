# Word chains

Holding writing together · Words that hold it together

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a general word (the dog, the animal, the boat) can stand in for a specific one the reader has already met, making a word chain that keeps a text moving without repeating the same word. They colour word chains, trace general words back with arrows, and build a word chain in their own paragraph. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 6 min · Practise 6 min · Make and own writing 10 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can find a general word that stands in for a specific one.
- I can say what specific thing a general word points back to.
- I can use a word chain in my own writing, with the specific word first.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: Biscuit, a kelpie, the dog and this clever animal are one chain. ## Why it matters

> 'Grandpa bought a kelpie. The dog follows him everywhere.' The second sentence never says kelpie. How do you know it is the same animal?

<!-- Speaker note: A text can talk about the same thing with different words. A **general** word (dog, animal, boat, place) can stand in for a **specific** one the reader met earlier (a kelpie called Biscuit, a red kayak, Hobart). Together these words make a **word chain**. -->

**How many different words can a text use for the same thing before the reader gets lost?**

- What helps the reader keep up?
- When does a new word start to sound like a new thing?

<!-- Speaker note: As many as you like, if each new word clearly belongs to the thing already named. A word that could be something new (a second dog) breaks the chain. ## Linking a general word

Now match some specific words to the general words that could stand in for them.

$$\text{Spot a general word}$$

1) $\text{Look back}$ — Find the specific thing it could stand for: a kelpie, the tractor, Hobart.
2) $\text{Check it's the only one}$ — If two things could fit (two dogs), the reader can't tell. Use the name instead.
3) $\text{Check the order}$ — The specific word must come first, or the general word has nothing to link to.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. ## Linking a general word

**Match each specific word to a general word that could stand in for it later.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Granny Smiths` | A. the dog |
| `a kelpie` | B. the tool |
| `Hobart` | C. the city |
| `a hammer` | D. apples |

* Answer: Granny Smiths → D. apples; a kelpie → A. the dog; Hobart → C. the city; a hammer → B. the tool

<!-- Speaker note: Each general word names the group the specific one belongs to, so a reader can link them. -->

## Why swap?

**Why do writers swap a specific word for a general one?**

- Because specific words are less correct
- To keep talking about the same thing without repeating the same word
- To start a new topic
- To make the reader guess what it is

* Answer: To keep talking about the same thing without repeating the same word

<!-- Speaker note: A word chain keeps the reader on the same thing with a little variety, as long as the specific word came first. -->

## Make: your own chain

In your own document, write four sentences about a specific thing: your pet, a favourite place or a team. Name it first. Then use a general word for it at least once (the dog, the town, the team).

Then use the highlighter in your document. Colour the chain for your thing in one colour, and a second chain in another colour if you have one.

Here is one example: "**Ollie** is our **cat**. **The animal** sleeps on **the couch** all day. **That sofa** is covered in fur." *Ollie*, *cat* and *the animal* are one chain. *The couch* and *That sofa* are a second one.

Then swap with a partner. Does every general word come after the specific one it stands for? Could any of them be a different thing?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing a paragraph with a deliberate word chain, including a general word, is the node's production step. Hints, in order: Choose one specific thing, such as your pet, a favourite place or a team, and name it in your first sentence. / Use a general word for it later (the dog, the town, the team). Does the reader already know what it stands for? -->
