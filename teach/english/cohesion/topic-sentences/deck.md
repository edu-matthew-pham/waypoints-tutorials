# Tell the reader what's coming

Holding writing together · Guiding the reader

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a topic sentence tells the reader what a paragraph is about, and the other sentences are details that belong to it. Topic sentences are often first, but not always. Students label topic sentences and details, find one at the end of a paragraph, move it to the start, and write and label their own paragraph. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 7 min · Make and own writing 12 min). -->

## Today

- I can find the topic sentence of a paragraph, wherever it is.
- I can say how the details belong to the topic sentence.
- I can write a paragraph with a topic sentence and details that belong to it.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: click a sentence and choose Topic sentence or Detail. The one idea: the topic sentence is the one the others belong to, wherever it sits. ## Why it matters

> You have one minute to find out what a two-page report says. Which sentences do you read?

<!-- Speaker note: A **topic sentence** tells the reader what a paragraph is about. The other sentences are **details**: examples, facts and reasons that belong to it. A reader in a hurry can read only the topic sentences and still follow the text. Topic sentences are often first in a paragraph, but not always. -->

## Think, pair, share

**If every paragraph had its topic sentence first, would that be better writing?**

- Who gains when the topic sentence is first?
- When might a writer save the point for the end?

<!-- Speaker note: First is easiest for a reader in a hurry and in information texts. Last can build suspense or a case, as in a persuasive paragraph. Both are choices. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Predict

**A report has three paragraphs. Their first sentences are: 'Lunchtime clubs give students a chance to try something new.' 'Clubs also help students make friends outside their class.' 'Some clubs struggle to find teachers to run them.' What is the report about?**

- Teachers
- Lunchtime clubs: what they offer and a problem they have
- Making friends
- You can't tell without reading everything

## Finding the topic sentence

Now: what makes a sentence a topic sentence?

$$\text{Read the whole paragraph}$$

1) $\text{Ask what they share}$ — What are most of the sentences about? Say it in a few words.
2) $\text{Find the sentence that says it}$ — The sentence that says what they share is the topic sentence.
3) $\text{Check the details belong}$ — Every other sentence should be an example, fact or reason for it.

## Finding the topic sentence

**What makes a sentence a topic sentence?**

- It says what the whole paragraph is about, and the other sentences belong to it
- It is always the first sentence
- It has the most interesting fact
- It is the longest sentence

* Answer: It says what the whole paragraph is about, and the other sentences belong to it

<!-- Speaker note: A topic sentence is the one the other sentences belong to. Its place in the paragraph can vary. -->

## Find Nib's slip

**Here is Nib's paragraph, one sentence per line. Click the sentence that doesn't belong to the topic sentence.**

```
 1  Dogs need exercise every day.
 2  A short walk helps them stay at a healthy weight.
 3  Running at the park lets them use up energy.
 4  My favourite colour is blue.
 5  Dogs that don't get exercise can become bored and chew things.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: Nib's favourite colour has nothing to do with dogs needing exercise. Every detail should belong to the topic sentence. It may belong in a different paragraph, or nowhere. -->

## At the end?

**A paragraph ends with 'All of this shows that our school wastes a lot of water.' Could that be its topic sentence?**

- Only if the paragraph is short
- No: topic sentences must come first
- Yes: it says what the paragraph is about, even though it comes last
- No: it starts with All

* Answer: Yes: it says what the paragraph is about, even though it comes last

<!-- Speaker note: A topic sentence at the end sums up what the details have been building to. It still tells the reader what the paragraph is about. -->

## Belongs or not?

**Topic sentence: 'Our library is a great place to study.' Which detail does not belong?**

- The library opens at 8 am.
- It has quiet desks by the window.
- Laptops can be borrowed for free.
- My brother plays basketball.

* Answer: My brother plays basketball.

## Exit ticket

**Which is the topic sentence? 'Koalas sleep up to 20 hours a day. Eucalyptus leaves give them little energy. Koalas spend most of their lives resting to save energy.'**

* Koalas spend most of their lives resting to save energy. The other two sentences are details that explain it, even though it comes last.
