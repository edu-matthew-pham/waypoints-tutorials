# Inside a paragraph

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Someone says 'Our team is the best' and shows you a photo of a trophy. Are you convinced? What else would you want to know?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn the parts inside an argued paragraph: a claim, evidence (an example or a quotation) and an explanation of how the evidence supports the claim. They label the parts in a model paragraph about a short story written for the lesson, add a missing explanation, label a history paragraph, and write and label their own.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find the claim, the evidence and the explanation in a paragraph. I can explain how a piece of evidence supports a claim. I can write a paragraph with a claim, evidence and an explanation.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: claim, evidence, explain


:::keep-together

### Building the paragraph

1. $\text{Claim}$ — Say what you are arguing: Mei is braver than she first seems.
2. $\text{Evidence}$ — Give an example in your own words, or a short quotation in the writer's exact words.
3. $\text{Explain}$ — Say how the evidence shows the claim: This shows that... or Running into the rain suggests...
4. $\text{Check the link}$ — Read the claim and the explanation together. Do they connect without the evidence in between?

Now: what is the explanation's job?

**What is the explanation's job in a paragraph?**

☐ To tell the reader the story again

☐ To add a new claim

☐ To say how the evidence supports the claim

☐ To repeat the quotation in other words

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** To say how the evidence supports the claim

The explanation joins the evidence back to the claim: how does this show that?

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's slip

**Here is Nib's working on a paragraph. Click the line where Nib first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Nib writes a claim: Mei is kind.
 2  Nib adds a quotation about the wallet: she "handed it back, dripping."
 3  Nib starts the next paragraph, because the quotation proves the claim.
 4  Nib's teacher writes in the margin: how does this show she is kind?
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib moves on as if the quotation explains itself. A reader may not see how the evidence shows the claim. Nib needs a sentence such as 'Running into the rain shows that...'

:::


:::keep-together

### Explaining or evidence?

**Which sentence explains, rather than giving more evidence?**

☐ This shows that Mei stops hiding once someone needs her.

☐ She never looked at the driver.

☐ 'She handed it back, dripping.'

☐ She sat down at the front of the bus.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** This shows that Mei stops hiding once someone needs her.

'This shows that...' joins the evidence back to the claim. The others are more evidence.
