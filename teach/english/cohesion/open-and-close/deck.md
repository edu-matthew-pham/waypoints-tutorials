# Open and close

Holding writing together · Guiding the reader

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that an overview (introduction) tells the reader where a whole text is going, body paragraphs each take one part in that order, and a conclusion pulls it together with nothing new. They label paragraphs by their job, reorder a shuffled text by following its overview, and write an overview and a conclusion for given body paragraphs. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 6 min · Practise 8 min · Make and own writing 13 min). -->

- I can say what an overview and a conclusion do for a reader.
- I can check that the body paragraphs follow the overview's order.
- I can write an overview and a conclusion that guide a reader through my text.

## Why it matters

> A friend gives you directions: turn left, go past three houses, turn right. Would it help if they first said 'It's the blue house at the end of the next street'?

<!-- Speaker note: A topic sentence tells the reader what a **paragraph** is about. A whole text has the same thing on a bigger scale: - An **overview** (or introduction) at the start says what the text is about and where it is going. - **Body paragraphs** each take one part, in order. - A **conclusion** at the end pulls it together, so the reader knows where they have been. -->

## Predict

**A report starts straight in with 'A female lays one to three eggs in a burrow.' Another starts with 'This report looks at three surprises about the platypus.' Which gets the reader ready faster?**

- Neither: the title does that job
- The one that starts with a fact
- Both are the same
- The one that says what the report will look at

<!-- Speaker note: What happens: The one with the overview. The fact on its own leaves the reader asking whose eggs, and why we are talking about them. The overview says what is coming, so every fact has a place to go. Take two or three predictions before anyone runs it. -->

## Checking a whole text

Now: what does an overview do for the reader?

$$\text{Read the overview}$$

1) $\text{Match the body}$ — Does each body paragraph take one of those parts, in that order?
2) $\text{Read the conclusion}$ — Does it pull the parts together, with nothing new?
3) $\text{Fix what's missing}$ — Add a missing overview, reorder the body, or move a new fact out of the conclusion.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Start: write Read the overview and say "What is the text about? What parts does it promise to cover, and in what order?" Line 1: write Match the body and say "Does each body paragraph take one of those parts, in that order?" Line 2: write Read the conclusion and say "Does it pull the parts together, with nothing new?" Line 3: write Fix what's missing and say "Add a missing overview, reorder the body, or move a new fact out of the conclusion." -->

## Checking a whole text

**What does an overview do for the reader?**

- Says what the reader should think at the end
- Repeats the title
- Says what the text is about and what it will cover, often in order
- Gives the most interesting fact first

* Answer: Says what the text is about and what it will cover, often in order

<!-- Speaker note: The overview is a map: what the text is about and the parts it will cover. -->

## Find Nib's slip

**Here is the plan for Nib's report, one paragraph per line. Click the paragraph that does the wrong job for its place.**

```
 1  Overview: Wombats are strong diggers with a clever way to protect themselves. This report covers their burrows and their tough back end.
 2  Body: Wombats dig burrows up to 30 metres long, with tunnels and rooms.
 3  Body: A wombat's rump is tough, so it can block its burrow with its back end.
 4  Body: When a predator chases it, the wombat dives into its burrow and plugs the entrance.
 5  Conclusion: Wombats also have cube-shaped droppings, which is very strange.
```

* Answer: line 5

<!-- Speaker note: Line 5: the conclusion brings in a new fact (wombats have cube-shaped droppings) that no paragraph covered. A conclusion pulls together what the text has already said. A new fact belongs in a body paragraph. -->

## Echo the overview

**Why might a conclusion repeat some words from the overview?**

- So the reader sees the text has come back to where it started
- Because the writer ran out of ideas
- Conclusions should never repeat the overview
- To make the text longer

* Answer: So the reader sees the text has come back to where it started

<!-- Speaker note: Echoing the overview tells the reader the journey is complete: this is the point the text set out to make. -->

## Vary: put it in order



[Open this step](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/english/cohesion/open-and-close/?full=step-4#step-4)

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Reordering a shuffled text by following the overview's own order shows that the overview sets the order of the body. Hints, in order: Click a paragraph, then use Move up and Move down. / The overview names the surprises in an order: eggs, electricity, venom. The body paragraphs should follow it. For students who need more support: A card: Overview = map. Body = one part each, in the map's order. Conclusion = pull it together, nothing new. For early finishers: Write a different overview for the platypus report that names the surprises in a different order. Then reorder the body to match. The hard part, on purpose: Students often write a conclusion as a last new fact, or an overview that just repeats the title. Point them to the job: map at the start, pull together at the end. When students get stuck: A student's overview repeats the title. Ask: What parts will your text cover? Can you name them in order? Fix: Add the parts: 'This text explains what a rip is and what to do if one catches you.' / A student's conclusion adds a new fact. Ask: Did any body paragraph say that? Fix: Move the new fact into a body paragraph, or leave it out. / A student can't decide the body order in Vary. Ask: What order does the overview name things in? Fix: Follow the overview's order. -->

## Exit ticket

**A conclusion says 'Sharks have been around for 400 million years.' No paragraph mentioned this. What is wrong?**

* It adds a new fact. A conclusion pulls together what the text already said; the new fact belongs in a body paragraph.
