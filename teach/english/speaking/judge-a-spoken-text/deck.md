Speaking to an audience · Voice, visuals and feedback

## Why it matters

> You can read a speech, but you cannot hear it. What can the words alone tell you, and what do they leave out?

<!-- Speaker note: A **transcript** is a talk written down, word for word. You can use it to study how a speaker built the talk, because the words are all there. In a transcript you can find: - **Structure**: the opening, the points, the close and the signposts that join them. - **Language features**: a question to the listeners, a repeat, a fact, a call to act. What a transcript does **not** show is how the talk sounded. Pace, pitch, volume, tone and pauses are not on the page unless someone has marked them. So a careful judge says what the words show, and says what they cannot. To **judge** a feature, name it, say what it does for the listeners and say how well it works, with a reason. -->

**Can you judge a talk you cannot hear?**

- What would you still be able to say about the talk?

## Name, effect, reason

Now: which can a transcript show?

$$\text{Name the feature}$$

1) $\text{Say what it does}$ — What does it do for the listeners?
2) $\text{Judge how well}$ — Does it work? Why?
3) $\text{Use the words}$ — Point to the line.
4) $\text{Say what you cannot tell}$ — Voice is not on the page.

**Which of these can you judge from a transcript alone?**

- How the speech is structured
- What tone the speaker used
- Whether the speaker paused for effect
- How loud the speaker was

## Find the unsupported note

**Here are Nib's notes on the quiet-corner speech, one note each. Click the note that a transcript cannot support.**

```
 1  Note 1: The speech opens with a question to the listeners.
 2  Note 2: The words loud, loud, loud are a repeat that makes the noise feel everywhere.
 3  Note 3: The speaker spoke too fast because she was nervous.
 4  Note 4: The noise-meter reading is a fact that listeners could check.
 5  Note 5: The last line is a call to act: give us a quiet corner.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Note 3: 'Spoke too fast because she was nervous' is about how the speech sounded and how the speaker felt. The transcript has the words only. Nib can judge the repeat, the fact and the call to act, which are all in the words. -->

## What makes a good judgement?

**Which is the best judgement of a feature?**

- I liked it because it was short.
- The repeat of the word loud makes the noise feel as if it is everywhere, which helps listeners picture it.
- The speech has a repeat in it.
- The repeat of the word loud is good.

* Answer: The repeat of the word loud makes the noise feel as if it is everywhere, which helps listeners picture it.

<!-- Speaker note: A good judgement names the feature, says what it does for the listeners and says how well it works. -->

## What does the repeat do?

**A speaker says, 'The tap is dry. The tap is dry. The tap is dry.' What is the likely effect on listeners?**

- It makes the speech shorter
- It shows the speaker has run out of ideas
- It hammers the point home, so listeners remember the problem
- It tells listeners the tap has been fixed

* Answer: It hammers the point home, so listeners remember the problem

<!-- Speaker note: A repeat puts the same words in front of the listeners again and again, so the point sticks. This is the effect a judge should name. Ask on mini whiteboards or by show of hands before the reveal. -->

## Exit ticket

**A student says a speech was 'clearly nervous'. They only have the transcript. What is wrong with this judgement?**

* A transcript has the words only, not how they sounded. The judgement needs words from the text, or a note that the transcript cannot show it.
