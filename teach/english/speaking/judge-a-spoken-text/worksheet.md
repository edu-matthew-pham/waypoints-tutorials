# Judge a spoken text

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You can read a speech, but you cannot hear it. What can the words alone tell you, and what do they leave out?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students label the features in the transcript of a made-up speech, work out what a transcript can and cannot show, spot the claim in Nib's notes that the words cannot support, judge marked delivery notes, find the words that speak to the listeners, and judge one feature of a short speech.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find features in the transcript of a talk and say what each does for the listeners. I can say what a transcript can show and what it cannot. I can judge a feature with a reason from the words.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: judging with care


:::keep-together

### Name, effect, reason

1. $\text{Name the feature}$ — A question, a signpost, a repeat, a fact, a call to act.
2. $\text{Say what it does}$ — What does it do for the listeners?
3. $\text{Judge how well}$ — Does it work? Why?
4. $\text{Use the words}$ — Point to the line.
5. $\text{Say what you cannot tell}$ — Voice is not on the page.

Now: which can a transcript show?

**Which of these can you judge from a transcript alone?**

☐ How the speech is structured

☐ What tone the speaker used

☐ Whether the speaker paused for effect

☐ How loud the speaker was

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** How the speech is structured

The words show structure: the opening, points and close. They do not show pace, volume, pitch or tone unless the transcript marks them.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the unsupported note

**Here are Nib's notes on the quiet-corner speech, one note each. Click the note that a transcript cannot support.**

```
 1  Note 1: The speech opens with a question to the listeners.
 2  Note 2: The words loud, loud, loud are a repeat that makes the noise feel everywhere.
 3  Note 3: The speaker spoke too fast because she was nervous.
 4  Note 4: The noise-meter reading is a fact that listeners could check.
 5  Note 5: The last line is a call to act: give us a quiet corner.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Note 3: 'Spoke too fast because she was nervous' is about how the speech sounded and how the speaker felt. The transcript has the words only. Nib can judge the repeat, the fact and the call to act, which are all in the words.

:::


:::keep-together

### What makes a good judgement?

**Which is the best judgement of a feature?**

☐ I liked it because it was short.

☐ The repeat of the word loud makes the noise feel as if it is everywhere, which helps listeners picture it.

☐ The speech has a repeat in it.

☐ The repeat of the word loud is good.

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The repeat of the word loud makes the noise feel as if it is everywhere, which helps listeners picture it.

A good judgement names the feature, says what it does for the listeners and says how well it works.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**A student says a speech was 'clearly nervous'. They only have the transcript. What is wrong with this judgement?**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A transcript has the words only, not how they sounded. The judgement needs words from the text, or a note that the transcript cannot show it.

:::
