# Choose your register

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The same news can be said to your friend at lunch and to the principal at assembly. Why would you not say it the same way?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students place spoken lines on a casual-to-formal scale, name subjective and objective language, find the line in a script that does not fit its listeners, choose formal wording, colour casual words, and plan an opening for a chosen audience, giving a reason for each choice.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say how formal a line of speech is. I can choose a register and a stance (subjective or objective) to suit the listeners. I can give a reason for my choice.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**Nib gives a talk to the principal and a room of parents about a problem with the bus queue. Nib uses a joking, casual voice with lots of slang. What will some of the listeners probably think?**

☐ Nothing, because the facts are the same

☐ That Nib is relaxed, so the problem must be small

☐ That Nib is friendly and everyone will agree

☐ That Nib may not be taking the problem or the listeners seriously

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** That Nib may not be taking the problem or the listeners seriously. Register tells listeners how serious the occasion is and how the speaker sees them. The facts are the same, but the message about the speaker is not.

:::

## Worked example: matching the listeners


:::keep-together

### Fit the listeners

1. $\text{Who is listening?}$ — Their age, how well you know them, how important it is.
2. $\text{What is the purpose?}$ — To inform, to persuade, to entertain.
3. $\text{Choose a register}$ — Casual, everyday or formal.
4. $\text{Choose a stance}$ — Subjective (my view) or objective (the facts).
5. $\text{Check it}$ — Would the listeners think I took them seriously?

Now: what kind of language is the opinion about the oval?

**A speaker says, 'I love the new oval, and it's the best thing the school has ever done.' What kind of language is this?**

☐ Formal, because it has a full stop

☐ Casual, because it is short

☐ Objective, because it names the oval

☐ Subjective, because it gives the speaker's feelings and opinion

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Subjective, because it gives the speaker's feelings and opinion

Subjective language says what the speaker feels or thinks. Objective language states facts, such as 'The oval is 120 metres long.'

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the line that does not fit

**Nib is giving a talk to the principal and the school council about the bus queue. Here is Nib's script, one line each. Click the line whose register does not fit.**

```
 1  Good afternoon, Principal and members of the council.
 2  I would like to report a problem with the bus queue after school.
 3  Students wait up to fifteen minutes in the sun before the first bus leaves.
 4  Yo, the queue's a joke, honestly.
 5  We recommend that a second bus lane is opened at three o'clock.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: 'Yo, the queue's a joke' is casual and gives only Nib's feeling. The other lines are formal and state facts. For the council, Nib should say what happens, with a number the council can check.

:::


:::keep-together

### One register for every talk?

**Why not use the most formal register for every talk?**

☐ It is against the rules

☐ It would sound stiff and distant to listeners such as a Year 3 class

☐ Nobody can speak formally

☐ Formal talks are always too long

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It would sound stiff and distant to listeners such as a Year 3 class

The most formal voice is for a formal occasion. With young listeners it would sound cold and keep them from following.

:::

**A speaker tells a Year 3 class, 'We recommend that you adopt a more considered approach to lunchtime.' What is wrong, and what would you change?**

{{lines-3}}
