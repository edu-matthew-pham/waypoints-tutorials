# Choose your register

Speaking to an audience · Prepare: cards and register

<!-- Speaker note: Students place spoken lines on a casual-to-formal scale, name subjective and objective language, find the line in a script that does not fit its listeners, choose formal wording, colour casual words, and plan an opening for a chosen audience, giving a reason for each choice. ## Today

- I can say how formal a line of speech is.
- I can choose a register and a stance (subjective or objective) to suit the listeners.
- I can give a reason for my choice.

## Why it matters

> The same news can be said to your friend at lunch and to the principal at assembly. Why would you not say it the same way?

<!-- Speaker note: **Register** is how formal or casual a speaker sounds. A talk can be: - **Casual**: slang and short forms, the way you would talk to a friend. - **Everyday**: plain and polite, the way you would talk to most people. - **Formal**: careful words and full sentences, for an important occasion or someone you do not know well. Speakers also choose between two ways of speaking about things: - **Subjective**: the speaker's own views and feelings ("I think", "I love", "it was awful"). - **Objective**: facts and figures, stated without opinion ("Nine buses were late"). The right choice depends on the **purpose** of the talk and the **audience**. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Who decides what is the right way to speak?**

- Does a speaker talk the same way to a friend, a principal and a Year 3 class?
- What do listeners expect, and where does that come from?

## Predict

**Nib gives a talk to the principal and a room of parents about a problem with the bus queue. Nib uses a joking, casual voice with lots of slang. What will some of the listeners probably think?**

- Nothing, because the facts are the same
- That Nib is relaxed, so the problem must be small
- That Nib is friendly and everyone will agree
- That Nib may not be taking the problem or the listeners seriously

## Fit the listeners

Now: what kind of language is the opinion about the oval?

$$\text{Who is listening?}$$

1) $\text{What is the purpose?}$ — To inform, to persuade, to entertain.
2) $\text{Choose a register}$ — Casual, everyday or formal.
3) $\text{Choose a stance}$ — Subjective (my view) or objective (the facts).
4) $\text{Check it}$ — Would the listeners think I took them seriously?

## Fit the listeners

**A speaker says, 'I love the new oval, and it's the best thing the school has ever done.' What kind of language is this?**

- Formal, because it has a full stop
- Casual, because it is short
- Objective, because it names the oval
- Subjective, because it gives the speaker's feelings and opinion

* Answer: Subjective, because it gives the speaker's feelings and opinion

<!-- Speaker note: Subjective language says what the speaker feels or thinks. Objective language states facts, such as 'The oval is 120 metres long.' -->

## Find the line that does not fit

**Nib is giving a talk to the principal and the school council about the bus queue. Here is Nib's script, one line each. Click the line whose register does not fit.**

```
 1  Good afternoon, Principal and members of the council.
 2  I would like to report a problem with the bus queue after school.
 3  Students wait up to fifteen minutes in the sun before the first bus leaves.
 4  Yo, the queue's a joke, honestly.
 5  We recommend that a second bus lane is opened at three o'clock.
```

* Answer: line 4

<!-- Speaker note: Line 4: 'Yo, the queue's a joke' is casual and gives only Nib's feeling. The other lines are formal and state facts. For the council, Nib should say what happens, with a number the council can check. -->

## One register for every talk?

**Why not use the most formal register for every talk?**

- It is against the rules
- It would sound stiff and distant to listeners such as a Year 3 class
- Nobody can speak formally
- Formal talks are always too long

* Answer: It would sound stiff and distant to listeners such as a Year 3 class

<!-- Speaker note: The most formal voice is for a formal occasion. With young listeners it would sound cold and keep them from following. -->

## Fact or view?

**A speaker reports the results of a school survey to the council. Which sentence is objective?**

- The survey was really annoying to fill in.
- Everyone will agree the canteen is awful.
- Of the 120 students asked, 84 said the queue is too long.
- I feel the queue is terrible.

* Answer: Of the 120 students asked, 84 said the queue is too long.

<!-- Speaker note: Objective language states what was found, with figures. The others give the speaker's feelings or a view. Ask on mini whiteboards or by show of hands before the reveal. -->

## Exit ticket

**A speaker tells a Year 3 class, 'We recommend that you adopt a more considered approach to lunchtime.' What is wrong, and what would you change?**

* The register is too formal for the listeners. Use short, friendly words: 'Let's try to take our time at lunch.'
