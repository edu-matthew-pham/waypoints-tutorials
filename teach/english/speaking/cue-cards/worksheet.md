# From script to cue cards

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** If you read your talk word for word, you look at the page. If you say it, you look at the people. What would you put on a card to say it?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students pick the key words that bring each part of a script back to mind on the cue board, find the card that is really a script, cut three scripts down with a card limit and a quarter-of-the-script limit, and print cards for a talk of their own.

**Students should be able to say:** I can pick the key words that bring a part of my talk back to mind. I can keep each cue card short enough to take in at a glance. I can print cue cards and say my talk from them.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: what a card is for


:::keep-together

### What goes on a card

1. $\text{Cut the script into its parts}$ — Opening, each point, close.
2. $\text{Find the key words}$ — The few words that bring the part back.
3. $\text{Keep the exact facts}$ — Numbers, names and hard words.
4. $\text{Keep a card short}$ — A glance, not a read.
5. $\text{Test it}$ — Cover the script. Can you say each part?

Now: what is a cue card for?

**What is a cue card for?**

☐ Reminding the speaker what comes next, so they can look up and speak

☐ Proving the speaker wrote the talk

☐ Holding the whole script so the speaker can read it

☐ Showing the audience the main points

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Reminding the speaker what comes next, so they can look up and speak

A cue card gives the speaker the next idea, so their eyes and voice can stay with the listeners.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the card that is a script

**Here are Nib's four cue cards, one per line. Click the card that is really a script.**

```
 1  Card 1: lost property box
 2  Card 2: First, no name, jumper
 3  Card 3: Second, nobody checks the box. Things sit there for months and then get thrown out. It is a waste of good jumpers.
 4  Card 4: name, check, Fridays
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Card 3 is the script with almost nothing cut. Nib will read it, not say it. It needs only the key words, such as: Second, nobody checks, months.

:::


:::keep-together

### What has to be on the card?

**Nib's talk says 17 drink bottles were handed in last term. What should go on the card for that part?**

☐ 17 bottles, since Nib needs to get that exactly right

☐ The word 'bottles'

☐ Nothing, because Nib will remember

☐ The whole sentence about the bottles

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 17 bottles, since Nib needs to get that exactly right

Put on the card what is easy to forget and has to be exact: a number, a name, a hard word. The rest comes back from the key words.

:::

## Keep to a small part of the script

The board counts how many words you picked and how long each card is. It cannot tell if they are the right words. Cover your script and say a part from the card to find out.

## Make: cards for a talk of your own

The board has a script to practise on. To use a script of your own, press **Use my own script**, paste it with one paragraph for each part, then press **Done writing**. **Print cue cards** makes the cards (two across a page), and **Print highlighted script** prints the whole script with your key words marked.
