# Use your voice on purpose

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two speakers say the very same words. One sounds sure, the other sounds like she is guessing. What changed, if the words did not?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students choose the word to stress in an answer, name the voice setting in delivery notes, find the delivery plan that hides the most important line, match the delivery to the words, and plan how to say three lines. They say them aloud in class.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the four voice settings: pace, volume, pitch and tone. I can choose the word to stress in a sentence. I can plan how I will say a line and say what the choice is for.

**Nib says, 'I didn't say Sam took the cap,' and puts the heavy stress on the word I. What will listeners take from it?**

☐ Nib said nothing at all

☐ Sam did not take the cap

☐ The stress changes nothing, because the words are the same

☐ Nib did not say it, but someone else may have

My prediction, and why:

:::teacher-note

**What happens:** Nib did not say it, but someone else may have. Stress on 'I' points to the speaker as the one who did not say it, which leaves room for someone else. Move the stress to 'Sam' and the meaning changes again.

## Worked example: voice as a signal


:::keep-together

### Four settings

1. $\text{Pace}$ — Slow down for what matters. Speed up for what is quick or exciting.
2. $\text{Volume}$ — Louder for emphasis. Softer for a secret or a serious moment.
3. $\text{Pitch}$ — Rising on a yes or no question. Falling to sound sure and finished.
4. $\text{Tone}$ — Warm, serious or excited. Match it to the words.
5. $\text{Stress}$ — The weight on the new information in a sentence.

Now: what does slowing down do for listeners?

**A speaker slows down and gets a little louder just before the most important sentence. What does this do for listeners?**

☐ It makes the talk longer

☐ Nothing, because only the words matter

☐ It signals that this part matters and gives them time to take it in

☐ It shows the speaker has forgotten the words

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It signals that this part matters and gives them time to take it in

Slowing down and a little extra volume tell listeners to pay attention, and they give the ear time to catch the words.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the note that hides the warning

**Here are Nib's delivery notes for a talk about the wet steps, one line each. Click the note that hides the most important line.**

```
 1  Opening: warm tone, medium pace, a smile.
 2  Point 1: steady pace, say 'the steps get wet when it rains'.
 3  Warning: 'Do not run on the wet steps.' Say it fast and quietly so it takes less time.
 4  Close: pause, then a slower, warm 'Please walk.'
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: the warning is the line listeners most need to catch, and Nib plans to say it fast and quietly so it takes less time. It should be slower and clearer than the rest.

:::

### Words and tone

**A speaker says, 'We lost the final,' in a bright, bouncy tone. What might listeners think?**

☐ The speaker is sad

☐ The speaker is being formal

☐ The words and the tone do not agree, so they may think the speaker is joking or does not mean it

☐ Nothing, because the words are the same as a sad version

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The words and the tone do not agree, so they may think the speaker is joking or does not mean it

Listeners read the tone as well as the words. If they disagree, listeners often believe the tone, so the speaker should match them.

:::
