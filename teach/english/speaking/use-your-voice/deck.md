## Why it matters

> Two speakers say the very same words. One sounds sure, the other sounds like she is guessing. What changed, if the words did not?

<!-- Speaker note: Your voice has four settings you can change while you talk: - **Pace**: how fast or slow you go, including where you pause. - **Volume**: how loud or soft you are. - **Pitch**: how high or low your voice sounds, and whether it rises or falls. - **Tone**: the feeling in your voice, such as warm, serious or excited. Each setting is a signal. Slowing down and getting louder says 'this matters'. A steady, warm tone says 'you can trust me'. A voice stuck on one setting says nothing in particular, so listeners decide for themselves what matters, and they often decide wrongly. You also choose where to put the **stress**, the weight on one word in a sentence. Stress changes what listeners understand. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Can a voice say something the words do not?**

- Say 'Thanks a lot' so that it means thanks. Now say it so that it means the opposite.
- What changed? The words did not.

## Predict

**Nib says, 'I didn't say Sam took the cap,' and puts the heavy stress on the word I. What will listeners take from it?**

- Nib said nothing at all
- Sam did not take the cap

- Nib did not say it, but someone else may have

## Four settings

Now: what does slowing down do for listeners?

$$\text{Pace}$$

1) $\text{Volume}$ — Louder for emphasis. Softer for a secret or a serious moment.
2) $\text{Pitch}$ — Rising on a yes or no question. Falling to sound sure and finished.
3) $\text{Tone}$ — Warm, serious or excited. Match it to the words.
4) $\text{Stress}$ — The weight on the new information in a sentence.

## Find the note that hides the warning

**Here are Nib's delivery notes for a talk about the wet steps, one line each. Click the note that hides the most important line.**

```
 1  Opening: warm tone, medium pace, a smile.
 2  Point 1: steady pace, say 'the steps get wet when it rains'.
 3  Warning: 'Do not run on the wet steps.' Say it fast and quietly so it takes less time.
 4  Close: pause, then a slower, warm 'Please walk.'
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: the warning is the line listeners most need to catch, and Nib plans to say it fast and quietly so it takes less time. It should be slower and clearer than the rest. -->

## Words and tone

**A speaker says, 'We lost the final,' in a bright, bouncy tone. What might listeners think?**

- The speaker is sad
- The speaker is being formal
- The words and the tone do not agree, so they may think the speaker is joking or does not mean it
- Nothing, because the words are the same as a sad version

* Answer: The words and the tone do not agree, so they may think the speaker is joking or does not mean it

<!-- Speaker note: Listeners read the tone as well as the words. If they disagree, listeners often believe the tone, so the speaker should match them. -->

## Exit ticket

**A speaker says the most important line of a talk fast and quietly to save time. What would you tell them?**

* Slow down and speak up for that line. Listeners need to catch it, and the voice should show them that it matters.
