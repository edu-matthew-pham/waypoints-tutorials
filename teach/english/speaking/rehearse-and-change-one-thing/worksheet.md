# Rehearse and change one thing

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You say your talk to a partner. They say, 'That was good.' What do you change?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students tell specific feedback from vague feedback, make a vague comment specific, choose the one change to make first and say how they will know it worked. They then rehearse with a partner in class using a short checklist, and tick the step when they have done it.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell feedback I can act on from feedback I cannot. I can give a partner specific feedback about what I heard and saw. I can change one thing and say whether it worked.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: feedback you can use


:::keep-together

### Listen, then change

1. $\text{Say it}$ — Rehearse the talk aloud to a partner.
2. $\text{Listen for specifics}$ — What did they hear or see, and where?
3. $\text{Choose one change}$ — The one that will help the listeners most.
4. $\text{Say it again}$ — Make the change.
5. $\text{Check it}$ — Did it help? How can you tell?

Now: what makes feedback useful?

**What makes feedback useful to a speaker?**

☐ It says what the listener heard or saw, and where

☐ It is about the speaker, not the talk

☐ It is long

☐ It is kind

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It says what the listener heard or saw, and where

Useful feedback names what the listener heard or saw and where. The speaker can then change it.

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Entry 2: 'It was good' does not say what was good or what to change. Nib could not tell what to keep or what to fix. The other entries name what the partner heard or saw.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why one change?

**Why change only one thing between rehearsals?**

☐ Because a speaker can only do one thing at a time

☐ Because the partner will forget the rest

☐ So you can tell whether that change helped

☐ Changing more takes too long

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** So you can tell whether that change helped

With one change, you can compare the two rehearsals and see whether it helped. With three, you cannot tell which one did.
