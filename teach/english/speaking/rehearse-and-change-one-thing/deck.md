# Rehearse and change one thing

Speaking to an audience · Voice, visuals and feedback

## Today

- I can tell feedback I can act on from feedback I cannot.
- I can give a partner specific feedback about what I heard and saw.
- I can change one thing and say whether it worked.

## Why it matters

> You say your talk to a partner. They say, 'That was good.' What do you change?

<!-- Speaker note: **Rehearsing** is saying the talk aloud, to someone, before the real one. It finds what you cannot see yourself: a part that is too quiet, a word you trip on, a spot where you read from the cards. Feedback helps when it is **specific**: it says what the listener heard or saw, and where. - Specific: 'You sped up on the numbers and I lost them.' - Vague: 'It was good.' 'Speak up.' 'Do it better.' Then the speaker changes **one thing** and says it again. One change is enough to find out whether it worked. Three at once and you cannot tell which one helped. ### A checklist for the listener When you listen to a partner, use these questions. Tick what you saw and heard, then write one thing to keep and one thing to change. - I could hear every word, including the last sentence. - The speaker looked up from the cards for most of the talk. - The speaker slowed down or paused on the most important part. - The speaker's tone matched the words. - One thing to keep: … - One thing to change: … -->

## Listen, then change

Now: what makes feedback useful?

$$\text{Say it}$$

1) $\text{Listen for specifics}$ — What did they hear or see, and where?
2) $\text{Choose one change}$ — The one that will help the listeners most.
3) $\text{Say it again}$ — Make the change.
4) $\text{Check it}$ — Did it help? How can you tell?

## Why one change?

**Why change only one thing between rehearsals?**

- Because a speaker can only do one thing at a time
- Because the partner will forget the rest
- So you can tell whether that change helped
- Changing more takes too long

* Answer: So you can tell whether that change helped

<!-- Speaker note: With one change, you can compare the two rehearsals and see whether it helped. With three, you cannot tell which one did. -->

## Make it useful

**A listener says, 'You were a bit all over the place.' Which version could the speaker act on?**

- You were very all over the place.
- You said 'um' before every point, and I noticed it most in point three.
- Being organised is important.
- You seemed organised, I guess.

* Answer: You said 'um' before every point, and I noticed it most in point three.

## Rehearse, listen, change one thing <span style="font-size:.6em;color:#64748b">(12 min)</span>
