# Visuals that help

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** You have a slide up and you are talking. Where do your listeners look, and what do they take in?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students label slide ideas as adding something, repeating the speaker or distracting, choose the visual that suits a point, find the slide in a plan that repeats the talk, and plan two visuals for a talk of their own.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether a visual adds something, repeats the speaker or distracts. I can choose a visual that shows what my words cannot. I can plan two visuals and say what each adds.

## Worked example: a visual with a job


:::keep-together

### A visual with a job

1. $\text{Name the point}$ — What do listeners need to understand?
2. $\text{Ask what words cannot do}$ — Show a shape, a pattern, a picture, a layout.
3. $\text{Choose the visual}$ — A chart, a map, a photo, a drawing.
4. $\text{Keep the writing short}$ — A title or a label, not the script.
5. $\text{Say what is on it}$ — Point to it, then explain what it shows.

Now: what job should a visual have?

**What is the best job for a visual in a talk?**

☐ Holding the whole script so the speaker does not forget it

☐ Showing something the words cannot, such as a shape, a pattern or a picture

☐ Filling the screen so there is no empty space

☐ Making the talk last longer

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Showing something the words cannot, such as a shape, a pattern or a picture

A visual earns its place by showing what words cannot. Otherwise the listener is better off watching the speaker.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the slide that repeats

**Here is Nib's slide plan for a talk about the bike rack, one slide per line. Click the slide that repeats the talk.**

```
 1  Slide 1: A photo of bikes lying on the ground by the fence.
 2  Slide 2: Every sentence Nib will say about the problem, typed out in full.
 3  Slide 3: A bar chart: 40 students ride, 8 bike spots.
 4  Slide 4: A drawing of how the new rack would hold the bikes in two rows.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Slide 2: Nib will say those same sentences. Listeners will read them first and stop listening. A bar chart or a photo would show what the words cannot.

:::


:::keep-together

### How much writing?

**How much writing should a slide have?**

☐ A few words at most, such as a title or a label

☐ None at all, ever

☐ As much as fits, so listeners can read the talk

☐ Whatever the speaker says next, word for word

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A few words at most, such as a title or a label

A few words, such as a title or a label, can orient listeners. Anything more, and they read instead of listen.
