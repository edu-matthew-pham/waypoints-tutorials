# Colons and brackets

Punctuation for meaning · Marks that change meaning

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a colon follows a complete main clause and introduces a list, an explanation or a quotation, and that brackets hold extra information a sentence could do without. They label colon uses, sort colons that belong from colons that split a clause, spot a misplaced colon, test bracketed parts, build a bracketed sentence and write an information note. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 5 min · Practise 6 min · Make in their own document 14 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can use a colon after a complete clause to introduce a list or an explanation.
- I can leave a colon out when the words before it are not a sentence.
- I can put extra information in brackets.
- I can test a colon or brackets in my own writing.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: six sentences, label what the colon introduces. ## Why it matters

> What does a colon promise the reader that a comma does not?

<!-- Speaker note: A **colon** (:) follows a complete main clause and says *here comes the detail*: - **A list:** *The kit has three parts: a lens, a tube and a stand.* - **An explanation:** *The reason was simple: the bus never came.* - **A quotation:** *The sign said: 'Closed today.'* (For something a person says aloud, a comma is usual, as in the last lesson.) If the words before the colon cannot stand alone as a sentence, the colon does not belong. **Brackets** ( ) hold extra information that the sentence can do without: - *The museum (next to the library) opens at nine.* Test: cover the bracketed part. If what is left is still a whole, sensible sentence, the brackets are in the right place. -->

## Does the colon belong?

$$\text{Cover the colon and what follows}$$

1) $\text{If it is}$ — The colon can introduce a list, an explanation or a quotation.
2) $\text{If it is not}$ — A colon splits the clause. Leave it out.

## Does the colon belong?

**Does the colon in each sentence belong?**

Groups: The colon belongs · The colon does not belong

Ravi had one goal: to finish the track. · We stayed home because: it was raining. · Ava bought three things: bread, milk and eggs. · She said that: the bus was late. · The result was clear: our team had won.

* Answer: Ravi had one goal: to finish the track. → The colon belongs; We stayed home because: it was raining. → The colon does not belong; Ava bought three things: bread, milk and eggs. → The colon belongs; She said that: the bus was late. → The colon does not belong; The result was clear: our team had won. → The colon belongs

## Spot the misplaced colon

**Here is Nib's excursion note, one sentence per line. One line has a colon in the middle of a clause. Click it.**

```
 1  Our class will visit the museum on Friday.
 2  Please bring one thing: a drink bottle.
 3  We are going to the museum because: it opens early on Fridays.
 4  Remember the rule: stay with your group.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3 has a colon after 'because', so the colon splits the clause. The words before it are not a sentence on their own. -->

## Take out the brackets

**Which sentence stops making sense when you take out the bracketed part?**

- The race (was cancelled) because of rain.
- Ava (our captain) won the toss.
- The cave (about two hours away) is huge.

* Answer: The race (was cancelled) because of rain.

<!-- Speaker note: Without the bracketed part, 'The race because of rain.' is not a sentence. Brackets hold extra information, not words the sentence needs. -->

**Which sentence has its colon in the right place?**

- Ben wished: to see the sea.

## Make: an information note

In your own document, write a short note for families about an excursion or an event. Write at least four sentences. Use a colon to introduce what to bring, and brackets for extra detail such as a time or a place.

Here is one example: "Our class excursion is on Friday. Please bring three things**:** a hat, a drink bottle and sunscreen. The bus **(**booked by Mr Lee**)** leaves at nine. We will return by three."

Then swap with a partner. Cover the colon and its list, and check that a whole sentence comes before it. Then cover the bracketed part and check that what is left still makes sense.
