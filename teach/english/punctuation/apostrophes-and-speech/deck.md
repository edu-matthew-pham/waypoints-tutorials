# Apostrophes and speech marks

Punctuation for meaning · Warm-up: apostrophes, speech and opening phrases

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that an apostrophe either stands in for missing letters or shows who owns something, and that speech marks wrap spoken words with the closing punctuation inside. ## Why it matters

> Same letters, different meaning: one dog or two? What can an apostrophe tell you?

<!-- Speaker note: An **apostrophe** (') does one of two jobs. - **Letters left out:** *can't* stands in for *cannot*; the apostrophe sits where the letters were. - **Who owns it:** *the pilot's cap* is the cap of one pilot. For more than one pilot, the apostrophe moves after the s: *the pilots' cap*. **Speech marks** (' ') go around the exact words someone said. The comma, question mark or exclamation mark that ends the spoken words sits inside the closing mark: *'Time to go,' said Ravi.* When the speaker's name comes in the middle, the speech carries on in lower case: *'We can leave,' said Ravi, 'if the bus comes.'* -->

## Two jobs for an apostrophe

$$\text{Ask what the apostrophe is for}$$

1) $\text{Count the owners}$ — One owner, apostrophe before the s. More than one, after the s.
2) $\text{Check a plain plural}$ — A word that just means more than one has no apostrophe.

## Where does the comma go?

**'We can start now,' said Mia. Where does the comma go, and why?**

- After said, because that is where the speaker is named in the sentence
- No comma is needed, because said Mia already tells the reader who spoke
- Outside the closing speech mark, because the whole sentence carries on after it
- Inside the closing speech mark, because it ends the spoken words

* Answer: Inside the closing speech mark, because it ends the spoken words

<!-- Speaker note: The comma ends the spoken words, so it sits inside the closing speech mark. Then the speaker is named in lower case. -->

## Make: a scene with talk

In your own document, write a short scene from a story with two characters talking, at a bus stop or a campsite. Write at least four sentences. Put at least one piece of speech in speech marks, and use at least two apostrophes.

Here is one example: "The bus was late again. 'I **can't** wait any longer,' said Zoe. Her **brother's** bag sat on the bench beside her. '**We'll** walk,' he said, 'if it **doesn't** rain.'"

Then swap with a partner. Point to each apostrophe and say its job: letters left out, or an owner. Check that every spoken part ends inside its closing speech mark.

## Exit ticket

**Fix the punctuation: the childrens toys were all over the floor and Mia said dont step on them**

* The children's toys were all over the floor, and Mia said, 'Don't step on them.' (Children's: the apostrophe goes before the s because children is already plural. A full answer needs the capital letters, the comma, the speech marks and the full stop.)
