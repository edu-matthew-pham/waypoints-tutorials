# Semicolons and dashes

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'It was quiet – too quiet.' What does the dash make you do as you read?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a semicolon joins two closely related main clauses, that a pair of dashes sets off an aside and that a single dash adds a pause or a surprise. They label each mark's job, sort joins that work from joins that need fixing, spot two sentences stuck together with a comma, build a sentence with a semicolon, choose marks and write a short description.

**Students should be able to say:** I can join two related main clauses with a semicolon. I can tell when a comma alone is not enough. I can use a pair of dashes for an aside and a single dash for a pause or surprise. I can check my own semicolons and dashes.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: is it joined correctly?


:::keep-together

### Is it joined correctly?

1. $\text{Read each side on its own}$ — Could each side stand alone as a sentence?
2. $\text{If both could}$ — A semicolon can join them, if the ideas are closely related.
3. $\text{If one could not}$ — Use a comma, or no mark, instead.

**Is each sentence joined correctly?**

Groups: Joined correctly, Needs fixing

- Ben loves chess; Ava prefers cards.: {{field-15}}
- Ben loves chess, Ava prefers cards.: {{field-15}}
- The oval was flooded; training was cancelled.: {{field-15}}
- We stayed home; because it was raining.: {{field-15}}
- The oval was flooded, training was cancelled.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Ben loves chess; Ava prefers cards. → Joined correctly; Ben loves chess, Ava prefers cards. → Needs fixing; The oval was flooded; training was cancelled. → Joined correctly; We stayed home; because it was raining. → Needs fixing; The oval was flooded, training was cancelled. → Needs fixing

```
 1  We arrived at the beach just after nine.

 3  After a short wait, we walked along the rocks.
 4  The rock pools were full of tiny crabs.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2 sticks two sentences together with a comma: 'The tide was high' and 'the beach was closed' could both stand alone. A semicolon, or a full stop, fixes it.

### Which pieces can a semicolon join?

**Which two pieces could a semicolon join?**

☐ 'Because it rained' and 'we stayed in'

☐ 'When the bell rang' and 'the class lined up'

☐ 'The sky cleared' and 'the match restarted'

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 'The sky cleared' and 'the match restarted'

A semicolon needs a main clause on each side. 'The sky cleared' and 'the match restarted' each stand alone.

## Commas, semicolons or nothing


## Make: describe a place

In your own document, describe a place you know well, such as a beach, a playground or a street. Write at least four sentences. Use a semicolon between two related sentences, and a pair of dashes for an aside or a single dash for a surprise. Use a spaced en dash (–), or a spaced hyphen ( - ) if that is easier to type.

Here is one example: "The beach at dawn is quiet. The waves are small**;** the sand is cold and smooth. A single gull **–** the only one in sight **–** circles above the rocks. Then the sun rises **–** and everything glows."

Then swap with a partner. Check that each side of the semicolon could stand alone, and say what each dash does.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
