# Opening phrases and shared owners

Punctuation for meaning · Warm-up: apostrophes, speech and opening phrases

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a comma follows a long phrase that opens a sentence and is not needed when the same phrase comes at the end, and that the apostrophe goes on the last name for something shared and on each name for things owned separately. They choose commas, sort sentences, catch a missing comma, match owner phrases and write a newsletter item. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 5 min · Practise 6 min · Make in their own document 14 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can find a phrase that opens a sentence.
- I can put a comma after a long opening phrase.
- I can tell when the same phrase needs no comma.
- I can show one shared owner and separate owners with an apostrophe.

## Why it matters

> 'Beside the pond near the shed a heron stood.' Where does the reader need a pause?

<!-- Speaker note: A **phrase** is a group of words with no verb of its own. When a long phrase **opens** a sentence, a comma follows it: - *Across the paddock behind the school, a mob of kangaroos grazed.* Put the same phrase at the end and the comma goes: - *A mob of kangaroos grazed across the paddock behind the school.* (Very short openers, such as *At dawn*, are often left without a comma. This lesson uses longer phrases, where the comma helps.) Shared owners: - **One thing shared:** the apostrophe goes on the last name. *Mia and Ravi's story* is one story they wrote together. - **One each:** each name has its own. *Mia's and Ravi's stories* are a story each. -->

## Where the comma goes

**Does each sentence need a comma?**

Groups: Comma after the opening phrase · No comma needed

Behind the library near the car park Max found a bike. · Max found a bike behind the library near the car park. · Across the wide river on a rope bridge the group walked. · The group walked across the wide river on a rope bridge. · In the cupboard under the stairs Ravi hid the torch. · Ravi hid the torch in the cupboard under the stairs.

* Answer: Behind the library near the car park Max found a bike. → Comma after the opening phrase; Max found a bike behind the library near the car park. → No comma needed; Across the wide river on a rope bridge the group walked. → Comma after the opening phrase; The group walked across the wide river on a rope bridge. → No comma needed; In the cupboard under the stairs Ravi hid the torch. → Comma after the opening phrase; Ravi hid the torch in the cupboard under the stairs. → No comma needed

```
 1  We left school at three.
 2  On the long bus ride to the camp we sang every song we knew.
 3  Mia sat beside the window.
 4  After a lot of noise, the bus went quiet.
```

## Who owns it?

**Match each phrase to what it means.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `the teacher's room` | A. a room for more than one teacher |
| `the teachers' room` | B. a room for one teacher |
| `Ben and Ava's boat` | C. one boat they share |
| `Ben's and Ava's boats` | D. a boat each |

* Answer: the teacher's room → B. a room for one teacher; the teachers' room → A. a room for more than one teacher; Ben and Ava's boat → C. one boat they share; Ben's and Ava's boats → D. a boat each

## Make: a newsletter item

In your own document, write a short newsletter item about a class excursion or a school event. Write at least four sentences. Start at least two of them with a long opening phrase and put a comma after it. Include at least one apostrophe, for something that belongs to a person or a group.

Here is one example: "On Thursday morning our class visited the botanic gardens. Beside the lily pond near the entrance**,** we sketched the water plants. Mia and Ravi**'s** drawing of the frog won the class prize. After a long day in the sun**,** everyone slept on the bus ride home."

Then swap with a partner. Point to each opening phrase and check a comma follows it. Say what each apostrophe shows.
