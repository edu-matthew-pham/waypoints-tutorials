# Opening phrases and shared owners

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'Beside the pond near the shed a heron stood.' Where does the reader need a pause?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a comma follows a long phrase that opens a sentence and is not needed when the same phrase comes at the end, and that the apostrophe goes on the last name for something shared and on each name for things owned separately. They choose commas, sort sentences, catch a missing comma, match owner phrases and write a newsletter item.

**Students should be able to say:** I can find a phrase that opens a sentence. I can put a comma after a long opening phrase. I can tell when the same phrase needs no comma. I can show one shared owner and separate owners with an apostrophe.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: where the comma goes, and who owns what


:::keep-together

### Where the comma goes

1. $\text{Find the opening phrase}$ — Which words come before the main part of the sentence?
2. $\text{Check its length}$ — A long opening phrase needs a comma after it.
3. $\text{Check its position}$ — The same phrase at the end needs no comma.

**Does each sentence need a comma?**

Groups: Comma after the opening phrase, No comma needed

- Behind the library near the car park Max found a bike.: {{field-15}}
- Max found a bike behind the library near the car park.: {{field-15}}
- Across the wide river on a rope bridge the group walked.: {{field-15}}
- The group walked across the wide river on a rope bridge.: {{field-15}}
- In the cupboard under the stairs Ravi hid the torch.: {{field-15}}
- Ravi hid the torch in the cupboard under the stairs.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Behind the library near the car park Max found a bike. → Comma after the opening phrase; Max found a bike behind the library near the car park. → No comma needed; Across the wide river on a rope bridge the group walked. → Comma after the opening phrase; The group walked across the wide river on a rope bridge. → No comma needed; In the cupboard under the stairs Ravi hid the torch. → Comma after the opening phrase; Ravi hid the torch in the cupboard under the stairs. → No comma needed

:::


:::keep-together

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2 opens with 'On the long bus ride to the camp', so a comma should follow it. Line 4 already has its comma.

### Who owns it?

**Match each phrase to what it means.**

A. a room for more than one teacher

B. a room for one teacher

C. one boat they share

D. a boat each

{{columns 60% 40%}}

{{rows 1}}

| Part | Letter |
|---|---|
| `the teacher's room` | |
| `the teachers' room` | |
| `Ben and Ava's boat` | |
| `Ben's and Ava's boats` | |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** the teacher's room → B. a room for one teacher; the teachers' room → A. a room for more than one teacher; Ben and Ava's boat → C. one boat they share; Ben's and Ava's boats → D. a boat each

The apostrophe goes after the owner. For a shared thing it goes on the last name; for things they each own, each name gets one.

## Commas in a recount


## Make: a newsletter item

In your own document, write a short newsletter item about a class excursion or a school event. Write at least four sentences. Start at least two of them with a long opening phrase and put a comma after it. Include at least one apostrophe, for something that belongs to a person or a group.

Here is one example: "On Thursday morning our class visited the botanic gardens. Beside the lily pond near the entrance**,** we sketched the water plants. Mia and Ravi**'s** drawing of the frog won the class prize. After a long day in the sun**,** everyone slept on the bus ride home."

Then swap with a partner. Point to each opening phrase and check a comma follows it. Say what each apostrophe shows.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
