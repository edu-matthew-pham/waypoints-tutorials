# Build a noun group

Building sentences · Warm-up: clauses, speech and noun groups

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that a noun group is a noun and the words that tell more about it, in the order determiner, describer, classifier, head noun. They label three noun groups, match each part to its job, catch a noun group too thin to picture, ask what a longer one does, build a sentence around one, colour describers and classifiers, and write four sentences of an information report in their own document. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 10 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 5 min · Practise 5 min · Make (in their own document) and own writing 13 min). -->

## Today

- I can find the head noun of a noun group.
- I can name the determiner, describer and classifier, and put them in order.
- I can tell when a noun group is too thin and rebuild it.
- I can use precise noun groups in an information report.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: three noun groups, eleven words. The one idea: a noun group is built around a head noun, and each other word has a job. This lesson covers AC9E5LA06 nodes 1 to 3 (components of a noun group, combining modifiers in order, noun group expansion for effect). ACARA's words are **determiner**, **adjective** and **classifier**. The lesson says **describer** for adjective, and names the term once. A **classifier** says what kind of thing it is (fishing boat, school bus, fire engine); these are all nouns doing the work of an adjective. The board only plants classifiers that are clearly kinds (fishing, school, fire, tree, gum) and avoids materials (wooden, plastic), where describer and classifier can both be argued. Detail after the head noun (the boat from the harbour) is mentioned in the intro but is not labelled on the board. Teacher: Try: after labelling, ask what would change if you removed the describer. The classifier? Teacher: Worked example: Nib's report. Ask what a reader could draw from 'a thing on its face'. Teacher: Vary: ask why 'the kitchen shiny tap' sounds wrong. -->

## Why it matters

> 'The racket' or 'our rusty old tennis racket'. Which one could you draw?

<!-- Speaker note: A **noun group** is a noun and the words around it that tell more about it. Its parts usually come in this order: - **Determiner:** points out or counts the noun (*this*, *some*, *our*, *three*) - **Describer** (adjective): says what it is like (*tiny*, *rusty*) - **Classifier:** says what kind it is (*garden*, *tennis*) - **Head noun:** the main thing (*gate*, *rackets*) Detail can also come after the head noun (*with a loose latch*). -->

## Think, pair, share

**How much detail does a noun group need?**

- Read 'the boat'. Then 'the old fishing boat from the harbour'. What can you picture now?
- When would a long noun group be too much?

<!-- Speaker note: A noun group is as long as the reader needs it to be. Too thin and the reader cannot picture it. Too crowded and it slows the sentence. The text's purpose decides. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Noun group parts

**Match each set of words to the part of a noun group they are.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `the, a, my, those` | A. Describer: what it is like |
| `tiny, shiny, cracked` | B. Head noun |
| `paper, kitchen, sports` | C. Classifier: what kind it is |
| `The word all the others tell you more about` | D. Determiner: points out or counts |

* Answer: the, a, my, those → D. Determiner: points out or counts; tiny, shiny, cracked → A. Describer: what it is like; paper, kitchen, sports → C. Classifier: what kind it is; The word all the others tell you more about → B. Head noun

<!-- Speaker note: Each part has its own job. A determiner points out the thing, a describer says what it is like, a classifier says what kind, and the head noun is the thing itself. -->

## What does the longer group do?

**A hiking shop writes 'our lightweight waterproof hiking backpack' instead of 'our bag'. What does the longer noun group do?**

- Changes the head noun into a describer
- Makes the sentence shorter
- Tells the buyer exactly what it is and why it suits them
- Means the sentence no longer needs a verb

* Answer: Tells the buyer exactly what it is and why it suits them

<!-- Speaker note: Lightweight and waterproof are describers, and hiking is a classifier. Together they let the buyer picture exactly what they are getting. -->

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Building a sentence by placing the parts of a noun group in order shows the order is not a choice: describers come before classifiers. Hints, in order: Start with the determiner. The head noun comes last in the noun group. / A describer says what it is like, so it comes before the classifier, which says what kind. For students who need more support: A noun group card: four boxes in order (determiner, describer, classifier, head noun) with two examples each. For early finishers: Rewrite 'the platypus has a bill' with a noun group of five or more words. Then cut it back to the version that still pictures the bill and does not feel crowded. The hard part, on purpose: Students call every word before the head noun an adjective. Ask 'does this word say what it is like, or what kind of thing it is?' Classifiers are the hard part: try swapping them with 'very' (very old boat works, very fishing boat does not). When students get stuck: A student labels 'fishing' as a describer. Ask: Does it say what the boat is like, or what kind of boat it is? Fix: Classifiers say the kind. You can have a fishing boat that is old, but not a very fishing boat. / A student puts the classifier before the describer. Ask: Read it aloud: the fishing old boat. Does it sound right? Fix: The usual order is determiner, describer, classifier, head noun. / A student's report uses 'nice' and 'good' as describers. Ask: What exactly is nice about it? Could a reader picture it? Fix: Replace vague describers with ones that say something you could check or see. -->

## Make: a report with noun groups

In your own document, write four sentences of an information report about an animal, a place or an object. Make it at least 40 words. Build each noun group with a determiner, at least one describer or classifier, and a head noun.

Here is one example: "The Murray cod is **a large freshwater fish**. It lives in **slow, muddy rivers** across the southern inland. **Its broad, mottled body** helps it hide among fallen logs."

Then swap with a partner. Circle each noun group in each other's report. Does each one have a head noun and at least one describer or classifier?
