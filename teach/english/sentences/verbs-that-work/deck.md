# Verbs that do the work

Building sentences · Detail inside a sentence

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that precise verbs, the right tense and adverb groups make a sentence more exact. They choose a one-word verb for a wordy phrase, match phrases to verbs, catch a plain verb with an adverb, choose the tense a report needs, choose verb forms from time clues, colour adverb groups by what they add and write a match report in their own document. About 50 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 6 min · Practise 6 min · Make (in their own document) and own writing 18 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can replace a plain verb and an adverb with one precise verb.
- I can choose past, present or future for what I am telling.
- I can say what an adverb group adds: how, where, when or how much.
- I can write a match report that uses all three well.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: three wordy sentences, three precise verbs. The one idea: choose the verb, the tense and the adverb group on purpose. This lesson covers AC9E6LA06 nodes 1 to 3 (precise verb selection, elaborated tenses, adverb groups for precision). ACARA's **elaborated tenses** here means the different ways English refers to time: simple present, simple past and future time expressions (will plus a verb). The lesson keeps to those. It does not teach perfect or continuous tenses; 'was planting' appears only as a wrong choice. A precise verb is not always a longer one. 'Said' is often the best verb; the aim is exact, not fancy. The board plants verbs whose meanings are clear from the wordy phrase. An **adverb group** is an adverb with a word that modifies it (very quietly, just outside). The lesson sticks to these; prepositional phrases (along the creek) are covered in other lessons. The facts and sentences are made up for the lesson. Teacher: Try: after choosing, ask when the plain verb and an adverb would be the better choice. Teacher: Worked example: Nib's report. Ask which single verb would say 'ran very fast'. Teacher: Vary: ask what time clue pointed to each tense. -->

## Why it matters

> 'Drank very quickly' or 'gulped'. Which one is the writer's best choice, and why?

<!-- Speaker note: Three tools make a sentence more exact. - **A precise verb:** *gulped* says what *drank very quickly* needs three words for. - **The tense:** *scored* (past), *scores* (present) or *will score* (future) tells the reader when. - **An adverb group:** *very quietly* (how), *just outside* (where), *quite recently* (when) or *very much* (how much) adds detail to a verb. -->

## Think, pair, share

**Is a longer verb always a better verb?**

- Read 'She said she was fine' and 'She insisted she was fine'. Which is more exact? Which is better?
- When is plain 'said' the best choice?

<!-- Speaker note: A precise verb is one that fits the meaning. Plain 'said' is often right, especially in dialogue where the words carry the feeling. The aim is to choose on purpose, not to find a bigger word. Give 30 seconds alone, 2 minutes in pairs, then take answers from both sides. There is no single right answer: ask for a reason, and let students change their minds. -->

## Three ways to sharpen a sentence

Now match the phrases to verbs.

$$\text{Look at the verb}$$

1) $\text{Check the tense}$ — When does this happen: past, present or future?
2) $\text{Add an adverb group only if it helps}$ — How, where, when or how much? Does the reader need it?

**Match each wordy phrase to the one verb that says it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `walked with heavy, angry steps` | A. glared at |
| `looked angrily at` | B. grabbed |
| `laughed in a silly, quiet way` | C. giggled |

* Answer: walked with heavy, angry steps → D. stomped; looked angrily at → A. glared at; laughed in a silly, quiet way → C. giggled; picked up quickly and firmly → B. grabbed

## Make: a match report

In your own document, write a match report of at least five sentences (at least 50 words) about a match or game you know, or one you make up. Tell what happened in the order it happened. Use at least two precise verbs and two adverb groups. Keep to the past tense.

Here is one example: "Our team played Hillside on Saturday morning. The striker **sprinted** down the wing and **crossed** the ball. Hillside's keeper **dived** but missed it. The crowd roared as the ball hit the net. Our captain lifted the trophy, and everyone cheered **very loudly**."

Then swap with a partner. Underline each precise verb and each adverb group in each other's report. Is every verb in the past tense? Does each adverb group help?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing five sentences of a match report uses precise verbs, past tense and adverb groups where they help a reader see the match.
