# Said or reported

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'I am starving,' said Ravi. Ravi said he was starving. Which one lets you hear Ravi?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn two ways to tell what someone said. They label six sentences as direct or indirect, sort the features of each, catch a report that kept the speaker's own words, choose which suits a job, build a reported sentence, choose punctuation for speech, and write a newsletter item in their own document that quotes one person and reports another.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell direct speech from indirect speech. I can report what someone said, changing the tense and the pronouns. I can punctuate direct speech. I can choose which kind of speech suits what I am writing.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: what changes?


:::keep-together

### Two ways to tell what was said

1. $\text{Look for quotation marks}$ — Marks around the exact words mean direct speech.
2. $\text{Check the words inside}$ — In a report, the pronouns and the tense have moved.
3. $\text{Ask what the writer wants}$ — To let the reader hear the speaker, or to sum up what was said?

Now sort the features.

**Sort each feature by the kind of speech it belongs to.**

Groups: Direct speech, Indirect speech

- The speaker's exact words: {{field-15}}
- Quotation marks around the speech: {{field-15}}
- No quotation marks: {{field-15}}
- 'I' changes to 'she' or 'he': {{field-15}}
- The tense usually moves back, as in 'am' to 'was': {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The speaker's exact words → Direct speech; Quotation marks around the speech → Direct speech; No quotation marks → Indirect speech; 'I' changes to 'she' or 'he' → Indirect speech; The tense usually moves back, as in 'am' to 'was' → Indirect speech

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the report that did not change

**Here is Nib's recount, one sentence per line. One line tries to report what someone said but keeps the speaker's own words. Click it.**

```
 1  Ms Lin asked us to line up for the bus.
 2  Ava said that she was tired.
 3  Max told his mum that I am hungry.
 4  Then the bell rang.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: 'Max told his mum that I am hungry.' still has the speaker's I and am. Reported, it becomes 'Max told his mum that he was hungry.'

:::


:::keep-together

### Which suits the job?

**A news report must sum up what four people said at a long meeting in two lines. Which kind of speech fits better?**

☐ Neither, because speech can't be summed up

☐ Direct speech, which quotes every person's exact words

☐ Direct speech, which needs no saying verb

☐ Indirect speech, which sums up what each person said

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Indirect speech, which sums up what each person said

Indirect speech is the writer's summary, so it is quick. Direct speech is for moments when the reader should hear the exact words.

## Punctuate what was said


## Make: a newsletter item

In your own document, write a short newsletter item (at least three sentences) about a school event, such as a sports day, a concert or a visit. Quote one person's exact words inside quotation marks. Report what a second person said, with no quotation marks.

Here is one example: On Friday our school held its swimming carnival. 'I have never heard such cheering,' said the principal. The captain told the crowd that the Blue team **had won**.

Then swap with a partner. Find the quoted sentence and the reported sentence. Does the quote have its marks and its punctuation? Has the report lost its marks, and moved its tense and pronouns?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
