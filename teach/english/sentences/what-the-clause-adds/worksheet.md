# What the extra clause adds

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'Although the canteen costs more, it serves healthier food.' Why does the writer start with the downside?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a dependent clause adds one of five kinds of information (reason, purpose, condition, concession, time) and that the clause that comes first gets noticed first. They label five dependent clauses, sort joining words, catch a joining word that makes the wrong relationship, build one sentence in both orders, choose joining words in context and write a letter to the principal in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name what a dependent clause adds: reason, purpose, condition, concession or time. I can choose the joining word that makes the relationship I mean. I can put a dependent clause first or last, and say what changes. I can use complex sentences to argue in a letter.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: choose the relationship, then the order


:::keep-together

### Which relationship, and what is first?

1. $\text{Find the dependent clause}$ — Look for the joining word.
2. $\text{Name what it adds}$ — Reason, purpose, condition, concession or time?
3. $\text{Ask what comes first}$ — The clause that comes first is what the reader notices first.

Now sort the joining words.

**Sort each joining word by what its clause adds.**

Groups: Reason, Purpose, Condition, Concession, Time

- because: {{field-15}}
- so that: {{field-15}}
- as long as: {{field-15}}
- even though: {{field-15}}
- after: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** because → Reason; so that → Purpose; as long as → Condition; even though → Concession; after → Time

:::

### Find the wrong relationship

**Here is Nib's letter to the principal, one sentence per line. One joining word gives a different relationship from the one Nib means. Click that line.**

```
 1  Our school library closes at 1 pm.
 2  I think it should stay open at lunch because that is when students are free.
 3  It should stay open although many students need a quiet place to read.
 4  I hope you agree, since it would help everyone.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: Nib means 'because many students need a quiet place to read', a reason. Although admits a point against the main idea, so the sentence says the opposite of what Nib means.

:::


:::keep-together

### What does the order put first?

**'Although the walk was long, the group finished before dark.' Compared with 'The group finished before dark although the walk was long,' what does the first put first?**

☐ The difficulty, so the finish comes as a relief

☐ Nothing, because the order makes no difference

☐ The finish, so the difficulty is only an afterthought

☐ Both ideas at once

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The difficulty, so the finish comes as a relief

The first version makes the reader meet the long walk first, so the finish lands as a relief. The second leads with the good news.

## Choose the joining word


## Make: a letter to the principal

In your own document, write a letter to the principal asking for something you would like the school to change. Write at least four sentences. Use at least three different joining words from this lesson, such as *because*, *so that*, *unless*, *although* or *until*. Start at least one sentence with its dependent clause.

Here is one example: "Dear Mrs Hart, I am writing to ask for a longer lunch break. Students need time to eat and play **because** we study hard all morning. **If** lunch were half an hour longer, more of us could join a club. **Although** the timetable is full, a longer lunch would help everyone concentrate in the afternoon."

Then swap with a partner. Point to each joining word and say what its clause adds: a reason, a purpose, a condition, a concession or a time. Which clause does each sentence put first, and why?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
