# Say more in fewer words

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'The pool is old. The pool is closed.' Can you say both in one sentence without repeating 'the pool'?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students fold two choppy sentences about the same thing into one by turning the second into an embedded clause with who, which or that. They choose folded forms, match clauses to their nouns, find a sentence still choppy, build a folded sentence, colour embedded clauses and write a short review in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can fold a second sentence into the first with who, which or that. I can say which noun an embedded clause tells about. I can say what is lost if the clause is taken out. I can write a review that carries its details in embedded clauses.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: what the embedded clause carries


:::keep-together

### Fold in three steps

1. $\text{Find the shared noun}$ — What are both sentences about?
2. $\text{Fold the second in}$ — Put it right after the noun, starting with who, which or that.
3. $\text{Check it}$ — Is the noun named once? Are both facts still there?

**Match each embedded clause to the noun it tells about.**

A. cousin

B. student

C. oval

D. cod

:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The cod that Mia caught was huge. → D. cod; My cousin, who lives in Perth, visits in June. → A. cousin; The oval, which floods every winter, is closed. → C. oval; The student who won the prize smiled. → B. student

An embedded clause sits right after the noun it tells about, so the noun is the word just before the joining word.

## Spot the embedded clause


## Make: a review

In your own document, write a short review of a book, game or place. Write at least four sentences. Say what it is, then fold details in with *who*, *which* or *whose* at least twice. Fold two sentences into one wherever you repeat a noun.

Here is one example: "The Lost Lighthouse is a book that I could not put down. Its hero, **who is only twelve**, finds a map **which leads to a hidden cave**. The cave, **whose walls glitter**, holds the secret of the wrecked ship."

Then swap with a partner. Underline each embedded clause and the noun it follows. Does each clause sit right next to the noun it tells about?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
