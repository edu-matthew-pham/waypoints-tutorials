# Keep the tense steady

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'Yesterday we walked to the beach, and then we swim.' What went wrong, and why does it matter?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that the tense tells the reader when, and that a text keeps one tense unless it changes on purpose. They label tenses, sort verbs, catch a slip in a diary, choose matching verb forms, spot slips in a recount and write a diary entry in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the tense of a verb as past, present or future. I can find a verb that slips out of the tense a text is using. I can say why a slip confuses a reader. I can write a diary entry in a steady past tense.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: why the tense must stay steady


:::keep-together

### Keep the tense steady

1. $\text{Decide when it happens}$ — Past for a recount, present for facts or instructions, future for plans.
2. $\text{Read the verbs}$ — Does each one match the time you chose?
3. $\text{Change tense only on purpose}$ — Use a time word like Tomorrow, so the reader knows.

**Sort each verb form by its tense.**

Groups: Past, Present, Future

- jumped: {{field-15}}
- swims: {{field-15}}
- will run: {{field-15}}
- grew: {{field-15}}
- arrives: {{field-15}}
- will arrive: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** jumped → Past; swims → Present; will run → Future; grew → Past; arrives → Present; will arrive → Future

### Which tense?

**Here is Nib's diary entry about last Saturday, one sentence per line. One line slips into a different tense. Click it.**

```
 1  Last Saturday we went to the creek.
 2  We found a log and sat on it.
 3  Then we walk along the bank to the bridge.
 4  Ava saw a heron by the water.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 says 'we walk' in a diary about last Saturday. It should be 'we walked' to match the other lines.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the slip

**Why does an accidental change of tense confuse a reader?**

☐ They cannot tell if the time has jumped

☐ It breaks a spelling rule

☐ It makes the sentence too long

☐ Present tense is always wrong

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** They cannot tell if the time has jumped

A change of tense signals a change of time. If the writer did not mean it, the reader stops to work out when things happen.

## Make: a diary entry

In your own document, write a diary entry about a real day last week, or one you make up. Write at least five sentences (at least 50 words), in the order things happened. Use the past tense.

Here is one example: "Last Saturday I **woke** up early because the sun **was** already shining. I **ate** breakfast and **packed** my bag. My cousin **arrived** at nine, and we **rode** our bikes to the park."

When you finish, read only the verbs. Then swap with a partner. Underline every verb in each other's entry. Are they all in the past tense?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
