# Keep the tense steady

Building sentences · Shaping sentences

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn that the tense tells the reader when, and that a text keeps one tense unless it changes on purpose. They label tenses, sort verbs, catch a slip in a diary, choose matching verb forms, spot slips in a recount and write a diary entry in their own document. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 10 min · Vary 5 min · Practise 5 min · Make (in their own document) and own writing 17 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can name the tense of a verb as past, present or future.
- I can find a verb that slips out of the tense a text is using.
- I can say why a slip confuses a reader.
- I can write a diary entry in a steady past tense.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: six sentences, three labels. The one idea: choose a tense and keep it, unless the time really changes. This lesson covers AC9E7LA06 nodes 1 and 2 (verb tense forms, consistency of tense for clarity). The lesson names three forms: past, present and future. Other tense forms (continuous, perfect) are left for later. The boards avoid sentences where present tense refers to the future ('The bus leaves at eight' is present), unless the time word makes it clear. Tense can change on purpose. Dialogue in a story ('I am hungry,' said Ava), facts that are still true ('the river runs behind our school') and a time word ('Tomorrow, we will...') are all fine. The lesson's slips are accidental, with no reason for the change. The sentences are made up for the lesson. Teacher: Try: ask what clue gave away each tense. Teacher: Worked example: read the diary aloud and ask where it sounded wrong. -->

## Why it matters

> 'Yesterday we walked to the beach, and then we swim.' What went wrong, and why does it matter?

<!-- Speaker note: The tense of a verb tells the reader when. - **Past:** *walked*, *swam*, *were* - **Present:** *walk*, *swim*, *is* - **Future:** *will walk*, *will swim*, *will be* A diary, recount or story keeps one tense. Changing tense without a reason makes the reader stop and wonder when it happened. -->

**When is it right to change tense in the middle of a text?**

- What if a story gives a character's words in the present?

## Find the slip

**Why does an accidental change of tense confuse a reader?**

- They cannot tell if the time has jumped
- It breaks a spelling rule
- It makes the sentence too long
- Present tense is always wrong

## Make: a diary entry

In your own document, write a diary entry about a real day last week, or one you make up. Write at least five sentences (at least 50 words), in the order things happened. Use the past tense.

Here is one example: "Last Saturday I **woke** up early because the sun **was** already shining. I **ate** breakfast and **packed** my bag. My cousin **arrived** at nine, and we **rode** our bikes to the park."

When you finish, read only the verbs. Then swap with a partner. Underline every verb in each other's entry. Are they all in the past tense?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing a diary entry about a real day in one tense puts steady tense to work in a real text type. Hints, in order: Write about a day last week, in the order it happened. Use the past tense. / When you finish, read only the verbs and check each matches. -->
