# Choose the shape for the job

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'Stop.' or 'Please stop, because there is a hole in the road ahead.' Which would you put on a sign?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn that a dependent clause can elaborate, extend or explain, and that a writer chooses short or long, simple, compound or complex sentences for the job. They label what clauses add, match jobs to shapes, catch a sentence too long for a notice, build an extending sentence, colour short and long sentences and write a school fair notice in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can say whether a dependent clause elaborates, extends or explains. I can choose a short or long sentence for the job. I can mix sentence lengths so the key point stands out. I can write a notice with a deliberate mix of shapes.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: choosing on purpose


:::keep-together

### Pick the shape for the job

1. $\text{Name the job}$ — Warn, give detail, show a reason, or land a point?
2. $\text{Choose the shape}$ — Short for a glance, compound for two equal facts, complex for a reason or detail.
3. $\text{Mix them}$ — Check the paragraph has some short and some long sentences.

**Match each job to the kind of sentence that suits it.**

A. A complex sentence with because

B. A compound sentence

C. A short simple sentence

{{columns 60% 40%}}

{{rows 1}}

| Part | Letter |
|---|---|
| `A warning that must be read in a glance` | |
| `Two facts of equal weight` | |
| `A fact with the reason for it` | |

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A warning that must be read in a glance → C. A short simple sentence; Two facts of equal weight → B. A compound sentence; A fact with the reason for it → A. A complex sentence with because

Each job asks for a different shape: short for a glance, compound for two equal facts, complex for a reason.

Line 2 buries the instruction in a long sentence. A driver needs 'Slow down' first.

### Why mix lengths?

**Why might a writer follow a long sentence with a very short one?**

☐ Short sentences are easier to spell

☐ It makes the text longer

☐ Short sentences are always correct

☐ The short one stands out and lands the point

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The short one stands out and lands the point

After a long sentence full of detail, a short one catches attention and lands the main point.

## Spot the short and the long


## Make: a notice for the school fair

In your own document, write a notice for the school fair. Write at least five sentences (not headings). Say what, when and what to bring. Put the most important instruction in a short sentence of five words or fewer. Use at least one long sentence of fifteen words or more that gives detail or a reason.

Here is one example: "The school fair is on Saturday. It will run from nine until three, and every class has built a stall that sells something they made themselves. **Come early.** The cake stall always sells out before ten, since the baking is so popular. **Bring a bag for everything you buy.**"

Then swap with a partner. Do the short sentences carry the key instructions? Do the long ones carry the detail and the reasons?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
