# Simple, compound, complex

Building sentences · Shaping sentences

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn the four sentence shapes: simple, compound, complex and compound-complex. They label whole sentences, sort sentences, catch a wrong label, build a compound-complex sentence, colour dependent clauses and write a short note home in their own document. About 50 minutes (Predict and Try 10 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 6 min · Practise 6 min · Make (in their own document) and own writing 16 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can find the main and dependent clauses in a sentence.
- I can name a sentence as simple, compound, complex or compound-complex.
- I can build a sentence of each shape.
- I can use a mix of shapes in a short note.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Try board yourself: six sentences, four labels. The one idea: the shape comes from counting main and dependent clauses. This lesson covers AC9E7LA05 nodes 1 and 2 (simple, compound and complex sentences, then compound-complex). ACARA's term for the joining words in a compound sentence is **coordinating conjunctions** (and, but, so, or). The joining words that start a dependent clause are **subordinating conjunctions** (because, when, although, if). The lesson names them as joining words that join equal clauses or add a dependent clause. Sentences are made up for the lesson. Items on the board avoid arguable cases, such as a verb-less fragment or 'and' joining only two verbs ('Ava swam and cheered' is simple with one subject). Teacher: Try: after labelling, ask which clue showed the shape. Teacher: Worked example: ask why a dependent clause cannot make a sentence compound. -->

## Why it matters

> Is a long sentence always a complicated one?

<!-- Speaker note: A clause has a verb that says what someone or something does or is. Count the clauses and see what joins them. - **Simple:** one main clause. *The kite soared.* - **Compound:** two main clauses joined by *and, but, so* or *or*. *The kite soared, and the children cheered.* - **Complex:** a main clause and a dependent clause. *The children cheered when the kite soared.* - **Compound-complex:** two main clauses and a dependent clause. *The kite soared, and the children cheered because the wind was strong.* -->

## Think, pair, share

**Is a longer sentence always a more complicated one?**

- Read 'The extremely tall, friendly lifeguard waved.' How many clauses?
- Read 'We left.' Is it simple? Is it still a sentence?

## Four steps to name a shape

$$\text{Count the verbs}$$

1) $\text{Test each clause}$ — Could it stand alone as a sentence? Then it is a main clause.
2) $\text{Name the shape}$ — One main is simple, two mains compound, a main with a dependent complex, two mains with a dependent compound-complex.

## Four steps to name a shape

**Sort each sentence by its shape.**

Groups: Simple · Compound · Complex

The tide came in. · The tide came in, so we left. · We left because the tide came in. · Ava swam and Ben watched. · Ben watched while Ava swam. · Everyone cheered.

* Answer: The tide came in. → Simple; The tide came in, so we left. → Compound; We left because the tide came in. → Complex; Ava swam and Ben watched. → Compound; Ben watched while Ava swam. → Complex; Everyone cheered. → Simple

## Which shape?

**'The dog barked, and the cat ran.' Why is this compound and not complex?**

- It is a long sentence
- Both parts could stand alone as sentences
- It has a comma
- It has the word and

* Answer: Both parts could stand alone as sentences

<!-- Speaker note: Both 'the dog barked' and 'the cat ran' could stand alone, so they are both main clauses. Two main clauses make a compound sentence. -->

## Make: a note home

In your own document, write a short note to a parent about a school event. Write at least four sentences. Include at least one compound sentence and one complex sentence. Try one compound-complex sentence for the most important news.

Here is one example: "On Friday our class visited the museum. We left early **because** the bus came at eight. The guide showed us a model ship, **and** she explained how it sailed **when** the wind was strong."

Then swap with a partner. Label each sentence simple, compound, complex or compound-complex. Do you agree with each label?

## Exit ticket

**'We packed, and we left because it was late.' Which shape is it?**

* Compound-complex: two main clauses (we packed, we left) and one dependent clause (because it was late).
