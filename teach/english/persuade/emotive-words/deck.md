# Words that push a feeling

Judging and persuading · Words that nudge

## Why it matters

> 'The road carries 2,000 cars a day.' 'The road chokes the town with endless traffic.' Which one wants you to feel something?

<!-- Speaker note: Some sentences **report**: *The new road will carry 2,000 cars a day.* Some sentences **push a feeling**: *The new road will choke the town with endless traffic.* Words that push a feeling are called **emotive words**. Some praise (*marvellous*, *charming*) and some criticise (*ghastly*, *grim*). A writer picks them to move the reader toward a view, so the words **position** the reader for or against the topic. Emotive words are not always wrong. A review or a speech can use them on purpose. The skill is to notice them and ask what they are doing. -->

## Spot the pushing word in the report

**Here is Nib's news report on a council meeting, one sentence per line. A news report should only report. One sentence pushes a feeling. Click it.**

```
 1  The council met on Tuesday.
 2  It voted 5 to 4 to extend the bus route.
 3  Furious residents stormed out of the hall.
 4  The new route will start in March.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3 says 'Furious residents stormed out'. Furious and stormed push a feeling. A report would say that residents left the hall. -->

## How does the wording position the reader?

**A review says the room was 'cramped and filthy'. How does that wording position the reader?**

- It does not position the reader, because it lists facts
- In favour of the room, with warm words
- Against the room, so the reader feels disgusted

* Answer: Against the room, so the reader feels disgusted

## Make: one place, two feelings

In your own document, write at least four sentences about the school canteen: two that praise it, then two that criticise it. Use no more than one exclamation mark.

Here is one example: "The canteen has a **warm**, **friendly** feel. Its fresh rolls are **delicious**. The queue, however, is a **dreary** crawl. The tables are **sticky** and the floor is **filthy**."

Then swap with a partner. Circle the words that push each feeling. Which words could you swap for plain reporting words?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing about one place twice, once to praise and once to criticise, puts emotive words to use. Hints, in order: Which words would make a reader like the canteen? / Which would make a reader dislike it? -->
