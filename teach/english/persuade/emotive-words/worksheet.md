# Words that push a feeling

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'The road carries 2,000 cars a day.' 'The road chokes the town with endless traffic.' Which one wants you to feel something?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students separate reporting words from emotive words, colour praise and criticism, find an emotive word in a news report, see how wording positions a reader, and write one place two ways in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell a reporting word from an emotive word. I can say whether a word praises or criticises. I can say how a word positions the reader. I can write one thing in two feelings.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: praise, criticise or neither?


:::keep-together

### Pushing or reporting?

1. $\text{Read the word}$ — Does it only describe, or does it praise or criticise?
2. $\text{Ask: what feeling?}$ — Admiration, disgust, fear, anger?
3. $\text{Ask: who is it steering?}$ — The word positions the reader for or against.

**Does each word praise, criticise, or only describe?**

Groups: Praises, Criticises, Only describes

- large: {{field-15}}
- magnificent: {{field-15}}
- shabby: {{field-15}}
- wooden: {{field-15}}
- delightful: {{field-15}}
- dreadful: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** large → Only describes; magnificent → Praises; shabby → Criticises; wooden → Only describes; delightful → Praises; dreadful → Criticises

:::


:::keep-together

### Spot the pushing word in the report

**Here is Nib's news report on a council meeting, one sentence per line. A news report should only report. One sentence pushes a feeling. Click it.**

```
 1  The council met on Tuesday.
 2  It voted 5 to 4 to extend the bus route.
 3  Furious residents stormed out of the hall.
 4  The new route will start in March.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 says 'Furious residents stormed out'. Furious and stormed push a feeling. A report would say that residents left the hall.

:::


:::keep-together

### How does the wording position the reader?

**A review says the room was 'cramped and filthy'. How does that wording position the reader?**

☐ It does not position the reader, because it lists facts

☐ In favour of the room, with warm words

☐ Against the room, so the reader feels disgusted

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Against the room, so the reader feels disgusted

Cramped and filthy are emotive. They steer the reader to dislike the room.

:::

## Make: one place, two feelings

In your own document, write at least four sentences about the school canteen: two that praise it, then two that criticise it. Use no more than one exclamation mark.

Here is one example: "The canteen has a **warm**, **friendly** feel. Its fresh rolls are **delicious**. The queue, however, is a **dreary** crawl. The tables are **sticky** and the floor is **filthy**."

Then swap with a partner. Circle the words that push each feeling. Which words could you swap for plain reporting words?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
