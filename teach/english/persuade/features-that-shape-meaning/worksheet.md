## Worked example: what does each feature do?


:::keep-together

### Name it, then say what it does

1. $\text{Name the feature}$ — Talking to you, a question, repetition or a strong verb?
2. $\text{Say the effect}$ — What does it make the reader feel or remember?
3. $\text{Ask: why for this reader?}$ — How does the audience or purpose explain the choice?

**Match each feature to what it does.**

A. Nudges the reader to agree

B. Makes a problem sound serious

C. Makes the idea stick

D. Makes the reader feel part of it

**Answer:** Speaking to 'you' → D. Makes the reader feel part of it; A question with an obvious answer → A. Nudges the reader to agree; Repeating a phrase → C. Makes the idea stick; A strong verb such as strangles → B. Makes a problem sound serious

Each feature has a different job in shaping what the reader feels or remembers.

### Spot the wrongly named feature

**Here is Nib explaining a feature of a poster, one point per line. One line gets the feature wrong. Click it.**

```
 1  The poster says 'Join us this Saturday.'
 2  This speaks straight to the reader.
 3  It is a question, so it asks for an answer.
 4  It makes the reader feel part of the team.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 calls 'Join us this Saturday' a question. It is an invitation that speaks to the reader, not a question.

### Why do features change?

**Why would a letter to the council use fewer 'Join us!' lines than a poster for students?**

☐ A letter reasons with an official, while a poster rallies students

☐ Letters are never allowed to use any features at all

☐ Students are not able to follow any reasons at all

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A letter reasons with an official, while a poster rallies students

The purpose and audience decide the features. A poster rallies. A letter reasons.

## Two kinds of feature


## Make: a short poster

In your own document, write a poster of at least four sentences about keeping a school garden tidy. Include at least two short sentences (five words or fewer). Use at least two language features on purpose, such as a question to the reader, repetition, a direct invitation or strong verbs.

Here is one example: "Our garden needs you. **Who wants weeds?** Pick up your litter. A tidy garden grows better beans and a happier school. **Join us on Friday.**"

Then swap with a partner. Name two features in the poster and say what each does to the reader.
