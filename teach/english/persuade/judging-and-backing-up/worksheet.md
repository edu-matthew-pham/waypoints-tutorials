# Judging and backing it up

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'The play was good.' Why should anyone believe you? What would make it stronger?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sort judging words into feelings, views of people and views of the thing, sort judgements by their backing, find an unbacked judgement, match backing to a judgement, and write a short review with one backed point in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name three kinds of judging word. I can say whether a judgement is backed up. I can back a judgement with a detail from the text. I can write a short review with a backed point.

## Worked example: is the judgement backed?


:::keep-together

### What kind of judging, and is it backed?

1. $\text{Find the judging word}$ — Is it a feeling, a view of a person or a view of the thing?
2. $\text{Look for backing}$ — A detail from the text or a trusted outside source?
3. $\text{Decide the strength}$ — Backed means a reader can check. Unbacked means they must take your word.

**How is each judgement backed up?**

Groups: A detail from the text, An outside source, Not backed up

- The villain is so scary.: {{field-15}}
- Ravi is honest: in chapter four he hands the lost purse to the office.: {{field-15}}
- The book is popular: the library lists it as borrowed 80 times this term.: {{field-15}}
- The setting is amazing.: {{field-15}}
- Tom is cruel, because he laughs when Maya falls.: {{field-15}}
- The writer is skilful: the librarian named it the school's book of the year.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The villain is so scary. → Not backed up; Ravi is honest: in chapter four he hands the lost purse to the office. → A detail from the text; The book is popular: the library lists it as borrowed 80 times this term. → An outside source; The setting is amazing. → Not backed up; Tom is cruel, because he laughs when Maya falls. → A detail from the text; The writer is skilful: the librarian named it the school's book of the year. → An outside source

:::

### Why a quote helps

**Why does adding a quote from the story make a review stronger?**

☐ Quotes show that the reviewer reads a lot

☐ Quotes make the whole review longer

☐ A reader can check the judgement

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A reader can check the judgement

A quote shows where the judgement comes from, so a reader can check it.

## Make: a short review

In your own document, write at least four sentences reviewing a book, film or event. Back up one judgement with a detail in quotation marks (a phrase from the text, or what happened, in quotes).

Here is one example: "The school play was funny and warm. The lead actor was confident because he never forgot a line. My favourite moment was when the dog barked and he said, **'Even the audience is cast tonight.'** The costumes were bright and well made."

Then swap with a partner. Does the detail really back up your judgement?
