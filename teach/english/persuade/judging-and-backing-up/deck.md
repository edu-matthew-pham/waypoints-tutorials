# Judging and backing it up

Judging and persuading · Words that nudge

<!-- Speaker note: Students sort judging words into feelings, views of people and views of the thing, sort judgements by their backing, find an unbacked judgement, match backing to a judgement, and write a short review with one backed point in their own document. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary and Practise 10 min · Make and own writing 15 min). -->

## Today

- I can name three kinds of judging word.
- I can say whether a judgement is backed up.
- I can back a judgement with a detail from the text.
- I can write a short review with a backed point.

## Why it matters

> 'The play was good.' Why should anyone believe you? What would make it stronger?

<!-- Speaker note: When we review a story, a film or an event, we use **judging words**. They come in three kinds: - **How it made me feel**: *amused*, *anxious*. - **What I think of a person's actions**: *generous*, *selfish*. - **How good the thing is**: *smooth*, *dull*. A judgement on its own is easy to ignore. It is stronger when it is **backed up** with a detail from the text, such as what a character does, or with a trusted outside source. A review that does both is easier to believe. -->

## What kind of judging, and is it backed?

$$\text{Find the judging word}$$

1) $\text{Look for backing}$ — A detail from the text or a trusted outside source?
2) $\text{Decide the strength}$ — Backed means a reader can check. Unbacked means they must take your word.

## Make: a short review

In your own document, write at least four sentences reviewing a book, film or event. Back up one judgement with a detail in quotation marks (a phrase from the text, or what happened, in quotes).

Here is one example: "The school play was funny and warm. The lead actor was confident because he never forgot a line. My favourite moment was when the dog barked and he said, **'Even the audience is cast tonight.'** The costumes were bright and well made."

Then swap with a partner. Does the detail really back up your judgement?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing a review with a detail in quotation marks puts judging and backing up to use. Hints, in order: What did you think of it? / Which detail shows that? -->
