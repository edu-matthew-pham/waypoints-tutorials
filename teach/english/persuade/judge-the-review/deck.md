# Challenge: judge the review

Judging and persuading · Challenge: judge the review

<!-- Speaker note: The challenge. Students judge a made-up film review in steps: name each feature, judge whether each part works for parents, judge two sources, and write a verdict with a quotation in their own document, then judge a real review. About 45 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary and Practise 10 min · Make and own writing 15 min). -->

---

## Today

- I can name the features of a review.
- I can say whether a part works for its audience.
- I can judge whether a source supports a claim.
- I can write a verdict with evidence.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do the Make step yourself first: write a verdict on the review. This challenge covers AC9E8LY03 nodes 1 and 2: analysing language features against purpose and audience and judging whether they are effective, and evaluating whether a source supports its claim. The students' own judgement is for a partner or you to read; the Make step has no board. Kit's hinge: node 2 applies the same criteria-based move to sources. The Vary step does that with two claims. The Practise board judges each part by its source, not its wording: nobody named, or a speaker who has not seen the film, is weak; a named person who saw it for themselves works. In the Worked-example sort, 'Some critics say it is good' is a vague source, so weak. The Make step has no board: students write the verdict in their own document, and the brief asks for at least four sentences and a quoted phrase, which the partner checks. All texts, people and shops are made up; no real films or reviewers are used. Teacher: Try to Practise: ask students for the audience each time. -->

## Why it matters

> A review says a film is 'the best of the year' and 'a must-see'. Should you buy a ticket?

<!-- Speaker note: This is a made-up review of a made-up film, *Lantern Cove*, written to persuade **parents choosing a family film**. You will judge it step by step using what you learned in this course: strong words, sources, bare claims and devices. An optional last step asks you to judge a real review you find yourself. A good judgement says **what works, for whom, and why**. A review can be effective for its purpose and still have a weak part. > *Lantern Cove* is the best family film of the year. The brave little tugboat is a heart-melting hero, and the harbour glows in glorious gold light. Critic Tessa Wren, who has reviewed films for twenty years, says the first scene took four months to draw by hand. The school librarian who runs the film club says it kept her group silent for two hours. The final chase lasts about a million years, and you will want every minute. Everyone knows it will win awards. -->

## Judge a part of the review

**For parents choosing a family film, does each part work, or is it weak?**

Groups: Works for this audience · Weak for this audience

* Answer: Critic Tessa Wren, who has reviewed films for twenty years, says the first scene took four months to draw by hand. → Works for this audience; Everyone knows it will win awards. → Weak for this audience; Some critics say it is good. → Weak for this audience; The brave little tugboat is a heart-melting hero. → Works for this audience; ToyBarn, a shop that sells toys from the film, says it is the finest film ever made. → Weak for this audience; The school librarian who runs the film club says it kept her group silent for two hours. → Works for this audience

## Judge the source

**The review quotes a school librarian who watched a group of children. How well does that support the claim that the film holds children's attention?**

- It does not matter who says it
- Well: she watched children watching the film
- Poorly: librarians do not know films

* Answer: Well: she watched children watching the film

<!-- Speaker note: The source saw the very thing the claim is about, so it supports it well. -->

## Make: write your verdict

In your own document, write a verdict of at least four sentences on whether the *Lantern Cove* review persuades parents. Say what works, for whom and why. Quote at least one phrase from the review in quotation marks, and say what it shows.

Here is one example, about a different made-up review: "The review of *Quiet Hill* would persuade teenagers. It says the music is 'loud enough to rattle your ribs', which suits that audience. However, 'a hit with everyone' is weak, because no one is named."

Then swap with a partner. Does the quoted phrase really support the judgement? Is there a reason for each point, and an audience named?

<!-- Speaker note: What this step is for: Writing a verdict with evidence from the review puts judging language and sources to use. Hints, in order: Would the review persuade parents? Why? / Which words from the review show that? -->
