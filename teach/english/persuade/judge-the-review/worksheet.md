# Challenge: judge the review

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A review says a film is 'the best of the year' and 'a must-see'. Should you buy a ticket?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** The challenge. Students judge a made-up film review in steps: name each feature, judge whether each part works for parents, judge two sources, and write a verdict with a quotation in their own document, then judge a real review.

**Students should be able to say:** I can name the features of a review. I can say whether a part works for its audience. I can judge whether a source supports a claim. I can write a verdict with evidence.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: does it work?


:::keep-together

### Judge a part of the review

1. $\text{Name the feature}$ — Strong words, a source, a bare claim or a device?
2. $\text{Name the audience and purpose}$ — Parents choosing a family film.
3. $\text{Judge it with a reason}$ — Works or weak, because...

**For parents choosing a family film, does each part work, or is it weak?**

Groups: Works for this audience, Weak for this audience

- Critic Tessa Wren, who has reviewed films for twenty years, says the first scene took four months to draw by hand.: {{field-15}}
- Everyone knows it will win awards.: {{field-15}}
- Some critics say it is good.: {{field-15}}
- The brave little tugboat is a heart-melting hero.: {{field-15}}
- ToyBarn, a shop that sells toys from the film, says it is the finest film ever made.: {{field-15}}
- The school librarian who runs the film club says it kept her group silent for two hours.: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Critic Tessa Wren, who has reviewed films for twenty years, says the first scene took four months to draw by hand. → Works for this audience; Everyone knows it will win awards. → Weak for this audience; Some critics say it is good. → Weak for this audience; The brave little tugboat is a heart-melting hero. → Works for this audience; ToyBarn, a shop that sells toys from the film, says it is the finest film ever made. → Weak for this audience; The school librarian who runs the film club says it kept her group silent for two hours. → Works for this audience

:::

### Judge the source

**The review quotes a school librarian who watched a group of children. How well does that support the claim that the film holds children's attention?**

☐ It does not matter who says it

☐ Well: she watched children watching the film

☐ Poorly: librarians do not know films

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Well: she watched children watching the film

The source saw the very thing the claim is about, so it supports it well.

## Weak or strong?


## Make: write your verdict

In your own document, write a verdict of at least four sentences on whether the *Lantern Cove* review persuades parents. Say what works, for whom and why. Quote at least one phrase from the review in quotation marks, and say what it shows.

Here is one example, about a different made-up review: "The review of *Quiet Hill* would persuade teenagers. It says the music is 'loud enough to rattle your ribs', which suits that audience. However, 'a hit with everyone' is weak, because no one is named."

Then swap with a partner. Does the quoted phrase really support the judgement? Is there a reason for each point, and an audience named?

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
