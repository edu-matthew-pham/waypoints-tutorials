# Claims and how sure

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'The match will be cancelled.' 'The match could be cancelled.' Same match. What changed about how sure the writer is?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that a bare assertion has nothing behind it, learn four small moves that temper a claim, place sentences on a possible-to-certain scale, and write a softened, backed claim in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can spot a bare assertion. I can name four small moves that soften a claim. I can say why a softened claim earns trust. I can write a claim that fits its evidence.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: bare or backed?


:::keep-together

### Bare or backed?

1. $\text{Read the claim}$ — Does it give a count, a source or a reason?
2. $\text{Look for small words}$ — May, often, some and it seems show how far the claim goes.
3. $\text{Ask about trust}$ — Would a careful reader believe it, and why?

**Is each claim bare, or backed or tempered?**

Groups: Bare assertion, Backed or tempered

- Everyone knows school starts too early.: {{field-15}}
- A survey of 60 students found most feel tired before 9 am.: {{field-15}}
- Cats are better than dogs.: {{field-15}}
- Many students may find early starts hard, the school nurse says.: {{field-15}}

:::

### Spot the bare assertion

**Here are Nib's reasons for a school vegetable garden, one per line. One line is a bare assertion. Click it.**

```
 1  A trial garden grew 40 kilograms of beans last term.
 2  A class survey found most students would join.
 3  Everybody knows gardens make students happier.
 4  The canteen said it could use fresh beans in two dishes.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 says 'Everybody knows' and gives nothing to check. The other lines give a count, a survey or a reason a canteen could act on.

:::


:::keep-together

### Why soften a claim?

**A writer changes 'Homework ruins sleep' to 'Homework may cut into sleep for some students'. Why?**

☐ The claim now sounds more certain, so readers will not question it

☐ The claim now admits its limits, which makes the writer easier to trust

☐ The writer has hidden their view so nobody can disagree

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The claim now admits its limits, which makes the writer easier to trust

May and some show the claim has limits. A reader sees an honest writer and is more likely to trust the claim.

## Find the softening words


## Make: a claim that earns trust

In your own document, write at least three sentences about school lunches. Include a claim with a word that says how sure you are (such as *may*, *might*, *could*, *probably* or *will*), and at least one thing that backs it up.

Here is one example: "School lunches **may** be too small for older students. A survey in my class found that **most** of us are hungry by two o'clock. The canteen **might** sell bigger rolls."

Then swap with a partner. Point to the word that shows how sure the writer is, and to the sentence that backs the claim up. Does the word fit the evidence?
