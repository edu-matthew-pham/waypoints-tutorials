# Claims and how sure

Judging and persuading · Warm-up: fact, opinion and evidence

<!-- Speaker note: Students see that a bare assertion has nothing behind it, learn four small moves that temper a claim, place sentences on a possible-to-certain scale, and write a softened, backed claim in their own document. ## Why it matters

> 'The match will be cancelled.' 'The match could be cancelled.' Same match. What changed about how sure the writer is?

<!-- Speaker note: A **bare assertion** is a claim with nothing behind it: *The new timetable is a disaster.* It sounds sure, but a reader has no reason to believe it. A writer can **back up** a claim with evidence, such as a count, or with a named source. A writer can also **temper** a claim, which means choose words so it is exactly as sure as what the writer knows. Four small moves do this: - A **can-or-will word**: *may*, *might* and *could* leave room for doubt; *will* and *must* leave none. - A **how-often word**: *sometimes* and *often* are careful; *always* and *never* are firm. - A **how-many word**: *some* and *most* are careful; *all* is firm. - A **distancing phrase**: *it seems that*, *research suggests*. Pick the word that fits the evidence. A claim that admits its limits is honest, so readers often trust the writer more. -->

## Why soften a claim?

**A writer changes 'Homework ruins sleep' to 'Homework may cut into sleep for some students'. Why?**

- The claim now sounds more certain, so readers will not question it
- The claim now admits its limits, which makes the writer easier to trust
- The writer has hidden their view so nobody can disagree

* Answer: The claim now admits its limits, which makes the writer easier to trust

<!-- Speaker note: May and some show the claim has limits. A reader sees an honest writer and is more likely to trust the claim. -->

## Make: a claim that earns trust

In your own document, write at least three sentences about school lunches. Include a claim with a word that says how sure you are (such as *may*, *might*, *could*, *probably* or *will*), and at least one thing that backs it up.

Here is one example: "School lunches **may** be too small for older students. A survey in my class found that **most** of us are hungry by two o'clock. The canteen **might** sell bigger rolls."

Then swap with a partner. Point to the word that shows how sure the writer is, and to the sentence that backs the claim up. Does the word fit the evidence?
