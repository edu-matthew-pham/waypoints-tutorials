# Fact or feeling?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** 'The play ran for 40 minutes.' 'The play was tedious.' Which one could you check with a clock?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students separate facts from opinions, find thinking and feeling words, see that any text type can be factual or opinionated, and write a short piece with facts and an opinion in their own document.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell a fact from an opinion. I can find thinking words and feeling words. I can say that any kind of text can use facts or opinions. I can write facts, then an opinion.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: which words give it away?


:::keep-together

### Fact, thought or feeling?

1. $\text{Read the sentence}$ — Could you check it with a count, a clock or a record?
2. $\text{Look for a clue}$ — A thinking word or a feeling word means an opinion.
3. $\text{Think about the purpose}$ — A report needs facts. A review welcomes opinion.

**What does each word do in a sentence?**

Groups: Shows a thought (opinion), Shows a feeling (opinion), Reports a finding

- I believe: {{field-15}}
- dreadful: {{field-15}}
- The survey shows: {{field-15}}
- in my view: {{field-15}}
- delightful: {{field-15}}
- The study found: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** I believe → Shows a thought (opinion); dreadful → Shows a feeling (opinion); The survey shows → Reports a finding; in my view → Shows a thought (opinion); delightful → Shows a feeling (opinion); The study found → Reports a finding

:::


:::keep-together

### Spot the opinion in the report

**Here is Nib's science report on soils, one sentence per line. One sentence slips an opinion into a report. Click it.**

```
 1  The experiment tested three kinds of soil.
 2  The sandy soil drained in 12 seconds.
 3  Clay soil is horrible because it is so sticky.
 4  The loam soil held the most water.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 says clay soil is 'horrible'. That is a feeling word, so it shows an opinion. A report would say how long the clay took to drain.

:::


:::keep-together

### Which text can be factual?

**Which text could use plain, checkable language?**

☐ Only a news article

☐ Any text, such as a review that lists measurements

☐ Only a science report

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Any text, such as a review that lists measurements

Any text can mix facts and opinions. The writer's purpose decides which words to use.

## Find the opinion words


## Make: three facts and an opinion

In your own document, write at least four sentences about a place at school: three facts, then your opinion. Use at least one word such as *I*, *my* or *we* in the opinion.

Here is one example: "The library has four computers. It opens at eight each morning. There are two hundred books about space. **I think** it is the **best** room in the school."

Then swap with a partner. Point to a sentence you could check, and to the one you could argue with.

Work in the tutorial. Plan first: what will you change, and how will you test it?
