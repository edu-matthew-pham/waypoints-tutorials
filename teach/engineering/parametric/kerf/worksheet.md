# Laser cutting and kerf

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A laser cutter is accurate to a tenth of a millimetre, yet your flat-pack box falls apart without glue. Where did the fit go?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** An optional lesson. Students see a 3 mm slot cut wider than 3 mm, draw slots narrower for a press fit, work the drawn size back from the board and the kerf, cut a test comb on the school laser, and find a fit for a different board and kerf.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain what kerf is and how it changes a cut. I can draw a slot for a press fit.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: allow for the kerf


:::keep-together

### Back from the board

1. $\text{cut slot} = \text{drawn} + \text{kerf}$ — Both edges of the slot lose half the kerf.
2. $\text{want: board} + 0.05 = 3.05$ — A press fit.
3. $\text{drawn} = 3.05 - 0.2 = 2.85$ — Take off the kerf.
4. $\text{measure your kerf}$ — Cut a 10 mm square, measure it: it is about a kerf smaller.

**3 mm board, 0.2 mm kerf, 0.05 mm clearance wanted. Draw the slot (mm)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 2.85

Cut slot = 3 + 0.05 = 3.05; drawn = 3.05 − 0.2 = 2.85 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the beam has a width. It burns away about the kerf, so a 3 mm slot is cut wider than 3 mm.

## Kerf


:::keep-together

### A square

**A 50 mm square is cut with a 0.2 mm kerf. About how wide is the cut square (mm)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 49.8

Half the kerf off each edge: 50 − 0.2 = 49.8 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Finger joints

**Finger joints come out loose. What do you change?**

☐ Make the fingers wider and the gaps narrower by about the kerf

☐ Glue them

☐ Cut slower

☐ Use thicker sheet

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Make the fingers wider and the gaps narrower by about the kerf

Allow for the kerf in the drawing: wider fingers, narrower gaps.

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**Why does a laser-cut slot come out wider than drawn?**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The beam burns away a strip of material (the kerf), so each edge of the slot moves out.
