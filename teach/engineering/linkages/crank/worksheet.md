# Cranks and sliders

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A motor turns round and round. A pump needs a piston that goes in and out. How do you get one from the other?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students turn a crank and read the slider's position on a graph, find that the stroke is twice the crank radius whatever the rod's length, work out a pump's flow from stroke, piston area and turns a minute, and size a crank for a pump brief.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a crank and slider turns rotation into back and forth. I can work out a crank's stroke from its radius, and the radius from a stroke. I can work out how much a crank-driven pump moves in a minute.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a pump


:::keep-together

### A 25 mm crank, then a 30 mm crank

1. $\text{stroke} = 2 \times 25 = 50 \text{ mm} = 5 \text{ cm}$ — The pin goes 25 mm one side of the centre and 25 mm the other.
2. $\text{per stroke} = 4 \times 5 = 20 \text{ cm}^3$ — A piston 4 cm² in area moving 5 cm sweeps 20 cm³ of water.
3. $60 \text{ turns a minute} = 60 \text{ strokes a minute}$ — One turn of the crank is one stroke.
4. $20 \times 60 = 1200 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ a minute}$ — That is 1.2 litres a minute.

Now a 30 mm crank and a piston 5 cm² in area, at 40 turns a minute. Start with the water per stroke.

**A 30 mm crank drives a piston 5 cm² in area. How much water does it push each stroke, in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 30

Stroke 2 × 30 = 60 mm = 6 cm, so 5 × 6 = 30 cm³.

:::


:::keep-together

### A minute

**At 40 turns a minute, how much water a minute, in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1200

30 cm³ × 40 strokes = 1200 cm³ (1.2 litres) a minute.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Noah thinks his pump gives 600 cm³ a minute. Click the line where his working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Noah's pump: a 30 mm crank and a piston 5 cm² in area.
 2  The piston moves as far as the crank is long: 30 mm = 3 cm.
 3  Water per stroke = 5 × 3 = 15 cm³.
 4  At 40 turns a minute: 15 × 40 = 600 cm³ a minute.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the piston moves twice the crank's length, 6 cm, because the pin goes from one side of the centre to the other. So 30 cm³ a stroke and 1200 cm³ a minute.

:::

## Cranks


:::keep-together

### Stroke

**A crank of radius 35 mm. What is the stroke, in mm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 70

2 × 35 = 70 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Radius

**A saw blade must move 80 mm each stroke. What crank radius does it need, in mm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 40

80 ÷ 2 = 40 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Speed

**A sewing machine's crank turns 900 times a minute. How many times does the needle go down each second?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 15

900 strokes a minute ÷ 60 = 15 a second.

:::


:::keep-together

### Flow

**A pump with a 4 cm stroke and a 3 cm² piston turns 50 times a minute. How much water a minute, in cm³?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 600

3 × 4 = 12 cm³ a stroke, × 50 = 600 cm³.

:::
