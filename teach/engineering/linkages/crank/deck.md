## Today

**We are learning to** a crank and slider turns rotation into back-and-forth motion: one turn is one stroke out and back, and the stroke is twice the crank's radius, whatever the rod's length. Students find the stroke by turning, work out a pump's flow, and size a crank to a brief.

- I can explain how a crank and slider turns rotation into back and forth.
- I can work out a crank's stroke from its radius, and the radius from a stroke.
- I can work out how much a crank-driven pump moves in a minute.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Do Mechanisms first if you can: this course assumes the idea of a brief and of a mechanism trading one thing for another. ## Why it matters

> A motor turns round and round. A pump needs a piston that goes in and out. How do you get one from the other?

<!-- Speaker note: Motors spin. Many machines need something to go **back and forth** instead: a pump's piston, a saw blade, a sewing machine's needle. A **crank** (an arm or a pin on a wheel) and a **connecting rod** turn one into the other: the **slider** at the end of the rod runs along a straight track. The **stroke** is how far the slider moves from one end of its travel to the other. -->

## A 25 mm crank, then a 30 mm crank

Now a 30 mm crank and a piston 5 cm² in area, at 40 turns a minute. Start with the water per stroke.

$$\text{stroke} = 2 \times 25 = 50 \text{ mm} = 5 \text{ cm}$$

1) $\text{per stroke} = 4 \times 5 = 20 \text{ cm}^3$ — A piston 4 cm² in area moving 5 cm sweeps 20 cm³ of water.
2) $60 \text{ turns a minute} = 60 \text{ strokes a minute}$ — One turn of the crank is one stroke.
3) $20 \times 60 = 1200 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ a minute}$ — That is 1.2 litres a minute.

## Find the mistake

**Noah thinks his pump gives 600 cm³ a minute. Click the line where his working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Noah's pump: a 30 mm crank and a piston 5 cm² in area.
 2  The piston moves as far as the crank is long: 30 mm = 3 cm.
 3  Water per stroke = 5 × 3 = 15 cm³.
 4  At 40 turns a minute: 15 × 40 = 600 cm³ a minute.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: the piston moves twice the crank's length, 6 cm, because the pin goes from one side of the centre to the other. So 30 cm³ a stroke and 1200 cm³ a minute. -->

## Exit ticket

**A crank of radius 15 mm drives a piston 4 cm² in area at 50 turns a minute. What is the stroke, and how much water a minute?**

* Stroke 30 mm = 3 cm; 4 × 3 = 12 cm³ a stroke; 12 × 50 = 600 cm³ a minute.
