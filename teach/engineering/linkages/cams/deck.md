## Today

**We are learning to** a cam is a shaped wheel; a follower rests on it and rises and falls as it turns. The cam's shape decides the follower's path, which a displacement graph shows. Students read graphs from four cams, match cams to jobs, and choose a cam for a toy.

- I can explain how a cam's shape decides its follower's path.
- I can read a displacement graph: rise, fall and rest (dwell).
- I can choose a cam for a motion I need.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Cardboard cams on a pencil axle with a straw follower make this concrete in ten minutes. Automata (cam toys) are a good Make extension in class. ## Why it matters

> A toy bird must pop up, wait, and drop. A motor just turns. What shape of wheel turns one into the other?

## A heart cam's height

Now a snail cam, which rises for the whole turn.

$$\text{heart cam, lift } 20 \text{ mm}$$

1) $90 \div 180 = \tfrac{1}{2}$ — At 90°, the cam has turned half of its rising part.
2) $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 20 = 10 \text{ mm}$ — A steady rise is a straight line, so the height is the same fraction of the lift.
3) $\text{at } 270^\circ\text{: } (360 - 270) \div 180 \times 20 = 10 \text{ mm}$ — On the way down, count the degrees left until 360°.

<!-- Speaker note: Model this live on the board before revealing anything: write each line and say why, pausing to ask the class what comes next. Then click to reveal the lines and compare. Script. Start: write heart cam, lift 20 mm and say "It rises steadily for half a turn (180°), then falls steadily." Line 1: write 90 ÷ 180 = 1 ÷ 2 and say "At 90°, the cam has turned half of its rising part." Line 2: write 1 ÷ 2 × 20 = 10 mm and say "A steady rise is a straight line, so the height is the same fraction of the lift." Line 3: write at 270^: (360 - 270) ÷ 180 × 20 = 10 mm and say "On the way down, count the degrees left until 360°." -->

**A snail cam with a 30 mm lift rises steadily for a whole turn. How high is the follower after 120°, in mm?**

* Answer: 10

## Shape and path

**Match each cam to its follower's path.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Eccentric` | A. Slow steady rise, then a sudden drop |
| `Pear` | B. Steady rise, steady fall |
| `Snail` | C. Rests for half a turn, then up and down |
| `Heart` | D. Smooth wave up and down |

* Answer: Eccentric → D. Smooth wave up and down; Pear → C. Rests for half a turn, then up and down; Snail → A. Slow steady rise, then a sudden drop; Heart → B. Steady rise, steady fall

<!-- Speaker note: Where the edge is the same distance from the axle, the follower rests; where it gets farther away steadily, the follower rises steadily. -->

## One way only

**Why must a snail cam always turn the same way?**

- Turned backwards, the follower would hit the step and jam
- It would not matter
- It would rise too quickly
- The motor can only turn one way

* Answer: Turned backwards, the follower would hit the step and jam

<!-- Speaker note: Forwards, the follower drops off the step. Backwards, it would have to climb a vertical step, so it jams. -->

## Dwell

**On a displacement graph, a flat line means…**

- The follower is falling
- The follower is at its highest
- The follower is resting (a dwell)
- The cam has stopped

* Answer: The follower is resting (a dwell)

<!-- Speaker note: A flat line: the follower's height doesn't change while the cam turns, because that part of the edge is a circle round the axle. -->

## Exit ticket

**A snail cam has a 36 mm lift. How high is the follower at 90°, and what happens at 360°?**
