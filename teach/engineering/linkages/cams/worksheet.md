# Cams and followers

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A toy bird must pop up, wait, and drop. A motor just turns. What shape of wheel turns one into the other?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students turn four cams (eccentric, pear, snail, heart) and read each follower's displacement graph, find that an eccentric cam lifts by twice its offset, calculate heights on straight-line rises, choose cams for toys, and design one.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a cam's shape decides its follower's path. I can read a displacement graph: rise, fall and rest (dwell). I can choose a cam for a motion I need.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::


## Predict


:::keep-together

**A round cam has its axle 10 mm off centre. As it turns once, the follower moves up and down by…**

☐ 0 mm: it is round

☐ 30 mm

☐ 20 mm

☐ 10 mm

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** 20 mm. The edge is 10 mm closer than average on one side and 10 mm farther on the other: twice the offset, like a crank's stroke.

:::

## Worked example: read the graph


:::keep-together

### A heart cam's height

1. $\text{heart cam, lift } 20 \text{ mm}$ — It rises steadily for half a turn (180°), then falls steadily.
2. $90 \div 180 = \tfrac{1}{2}$ — At 90°, the cam has turned half of its rising part.
3. $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 20 = 10 \text{ mm}$ — A steady rise is a straight line, so the height is the same fraction of the lift.
4. $\text{at } 270^\circ\text{: } (360 - 270) \div 180 \times 20 = 10 \text{ mm}$ — On the way down, count the degrees left until 360°.

Now a snail cam, which rises for the whole turn.

**A snail cam with a 30 mm lift rises steadily for a whole turn. How high is the follower after 120°, in mm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10

120 is a third of 360, and a third of 30 is 10 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Shape and path

**Match each cam to its follower's path.**

A. Slow steady rise, then a sudden drop

B. Steady rise, steady fall

C. Rests for half a turn, then up and down

D. Smooth wave up and down

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Eccentric → D. Smooth wave up and down; Pear → C. Rests for half a turn, then up and down; Snail → A. Slow steady rise, then a sudden drop; Heart → B. Steady rise, steady fall

Where the edge is the same distance from the axle, the follower rests; where it gets farther away steadily, the follower rises steadily.

:::


:::keep-together

### One way only

**Why must a snail cam always turn the same way?**

☐ Turned backwards, the follower would hit the step and jam

☐ It would not matter

☐ It would rise too quickly

☐ The motor can only turn one way

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Turned backwards, the follower would hit the step and jam

Forwards, the follower drops off the step. Backwards, it would have to climb a vertical step, so it jams.

## Cams


:::keep-together

### Eccentric

**An eccentric cam's axle is 8 mm off centre. What is the follower's lift, in mm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 16

2 × 8 = 16 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Heart

**A heart cam with a 24 mm lift. How high is the follower at 45°, in mm?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 6

45 ÷ 180 = 1/4, and 1/4 of 24 = 6 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Dwell

**On a displacement graph, a flat line means…**

☐ The follower is falling

☐ The follower is at its highest

☐ The follower is resting (a dwell)

☐ The cam has stopped

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The follower is resting (a dwell)

A flat line: the follower's height doesn't change while the cam turns, because that part of the edge is a circle round the axle.

:::
