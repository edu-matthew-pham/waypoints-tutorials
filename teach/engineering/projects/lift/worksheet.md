# Lift

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The winch lesson worked on paper. Does a real motor, real gears and real friction agree with the numbers?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** A Years 9–10 design project: a motor-driven lift where the gear ratio is chosen against the brief on the gears board, driven both ways through a motor driver, and the measured lift time compared with the model's prediction.

**Students should be able to say:** I can choose a gear ratio against a brief. I can drive a motor both ways through a driver. I can compare a model's prediction with a measurement and explain the gap.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Develop: two ideas first


:::keep-together

### Two ways to do it

**Two gear pairs meet the brief on paper: 8 → 24 lifts up to 600 g in 15 s, and 16 → 40 lifts up to 500 g in 12.5 s. What's the catch with 8 → 24?**

☐ It takes exactly 15 s, so any slowing at all fails the time

☐ It turns the drum the wrong way

☐ It can't lift 450 g

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It takes exactly 15 s, so any slowing at all fails the time

15 s is the limit, and a real lift slows a little with a load and with losses, so 8 → 24 has no time to spare. 16 → 40 has 2.5 s spare, though less extra force.

:::


:::keep-together

### Your own two ideas

**Now two ideas of your own. For each one, say which criterion in the brief it's strongest on and which it's weakest on. Then say which you'll build, and why.**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** One way to write it: Idea 1: 16 → 40 with a timed stop at 12.5 s. Strongest on time: 2.5 s spare. Weakest on stopping at the top: if the lift slows down, the timed stop leaves it short. Idea 2: 16 → 40 with a limit switch at the top. Strongest on stopping in the right place whatever the speed. Weakest on parts: one more switch to mount and wire. I'll build idea 2, because stopping at the top is in the brief and a timed stop only works if nothing changes.

## Develop: plan before you build


:::keep-together

### Margin

**Real gears lose about 10% of the force at each mesh. Which pair has more margin on strength?**

☐ 16 → 40: 500 g against 450 g

☐ 8 → 24: 600 g against 450 g

☐ They are the same

☐ Neither: friction doesn't matter

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8 → 24: 600 g against 450 g

600 × 0.9 = 540 g, still over 450. 16 → 40 drops to exactly 450 with no margin.

:::

## Evaluate: against the brief


:::keep-together

### Which is evidence?

**Your lift takes 17 s; the model predicted 15 s. What is the best explanation to give in your evaluation?**

☐ The gears are broken

☐ The brief was unfair

☐ The stopwatch was wrong

☐ The model ignores friction and the motor slowing under load; measure and say how much

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The model ignores friction and the motor slowing under load; measure and say how much

Real motors slow under load, and every mesh loses a little: the model's 15 s is a best case.
