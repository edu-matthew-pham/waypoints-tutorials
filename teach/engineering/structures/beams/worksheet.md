# Beams and shapes

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A ruler laid flat between two books sags under a pencil case. Turn it on its edge and it barely moves. It's the same ruler. Why?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students compare four cross-sections with about the same wood (a strip flat and on edge, an I-beam, a tube), find depth counts cubed, work out how a beam's supports share a load, and choose a section for a shelf brief.

**Students should be able to say:** I can work out how much each support of a beam holds. I can explain why a deeper beam is much stiffer, and why I-beams are shaped as they are. I can choose a beam section to meet a sag limit.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: supports and stiffness


:::keep-together

### Two rules

1. $R_\text{left} = P \times \frac{b}{L}$ — The load P is a away from the left support and b from the right. The far distance decides each share (as on a lever).
2. $10 \times \frac{0.25}{0.5} = 5 \text{ N each}$ — In the middle, the supports share it equally.
3. $I = \frac{\text{width} \times \text{depth}^3}{12}$ — Stiffness of a rectangle (in mm⁴): depth counts cubed.
4. $\text{flat: } \frac{30 \times 3^3}{12} = 67.5 \quad \text{edge: } \frac{3 \times 30^3}{12} = 6750$ — Same wood, a hundred times the stiffness on its edge.

**A 10 N load sits 0.1 m from the left support of a 0.5 m beam. How much does the left support hold, in newtons?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

10 × 0.4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 N (and the right holds 2 N).

:::


:::keep-together

### Deeper

**A beam is made twice as deep (same width). How many times stiffer is it?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 8

2³ = 8 times stiffer: it sags an eighth as much.

### Shares

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::

3³ = 27.

### I-beams

**Why are steel beams in buildings I-shaped?**

☐ Most of the steel is at the top and bottom, far from the middle, where it adds most stiffness

☐ I-shapes are easier to paint

☐ The middle of a beam does all the work

☐ They weigh more

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Most of the steel is at the top and bottom, far from the middle, where it adds most stiffness

Stiffness comes from material far from the middle. An I-beam puts its flanges there and keeps a thin web between them.

:::
