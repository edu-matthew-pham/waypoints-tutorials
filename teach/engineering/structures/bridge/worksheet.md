# Design a bridge

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** An 8 m gap, a 1000 N load in the middle, and $10 a metre for members. What is the cheapest bridge that won't fail?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find a pinned deck collapses and a big triangle buckles, learn that a member's buckling load falls with its length squared, build a bridge of short triangles that holds 1000 N, then refine it to cost $300 or less and fail only at 1400 N or more.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why long members buckle under a push. I can design a truss whose pushed members are short and whose long members pull. I can test a design to failure and talk about its safety factor.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: short pushes, long pulls


:::keep-together

### Buckling

1. $\text{buckles at } \frac{8000}{L^2}$ — Long pushed members are the weak spot.
2. $L = 4.47 \text{ m: } \frac{8000}{20} = 400 \text{ N}$ — The big triangle's sides push about 1118 N: they buckle.
3. $L = 2 \text{ m: } \frac{8000}{4} = 2000 \text{ N}$ — Short members take five times the push.
4. $\text{pulls don't buckle}$ — So long diagonals should pull; pushed members should be short: a Pratt truss does both.

**On this board, at what push does a 2.83 m member buckle, to the nearest 10 N?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1000

8000 ÷ 2.83² = 8000 ÷ 8 = 1000 N.

:::


:::keep-together

### Shorter

**A pushed member is cut in half by bracing it at its middle. How much bigger a push can each half take?**

☐ 4 times

☐ 2 times

☐ The same

☐ Half as much

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 4 times

Buckling goes with 1 ÷ length², so half the length takes 2² = 4 times the push.

### Safety

**A bridge carries 1000 N and fails at 1400 N. What is its safety factor (failure load ÷ working load)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1.4

1400 ÷ 1000 = 1.4.

:::


:::keep-together
