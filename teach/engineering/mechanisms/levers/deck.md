## Today

**We are learning to** a lever trades force for distance: a small push far from the pivot lifts a big load near it, and your hand moves farther than the load. Students find where to put a pivot by testing, work out the push from turning effects, meet the three classes of lever (including the one in their arm), and design a lever to a brief.

- I can work out the push a lever needs from force × distance on each side of the pivot.
- I can explain that a lever makes my push bigger by the same factor it makes my hand move farther.
- I can say whether a lever is class 1, 2 or 3 from where the pivot, the load and the push are.
- I can design a lever that meets more than one criterion.

## Balance 600 N with the pivot at 0.5 m, then 0.4 m

Now the pivot is 0.4 m from the rock. Start with the push.

$$600 \times 0.5 = 300$$

1) $2 - 0.5 = 1.5 \text{ m}$ — Your push is at the other end: 1.5 m from the pivot.
2) $300 \div 1.5 = 200 \text{ N}$ — Your push must turn as much: push × 1.5 = 300.
3) $\text{advantage} = 600 \div 200 = 3$ — The lever makes your push 3 times as strong (its mechanical advantage).
4) $1.5 \div 0.5 = 3$ — Your hand is 3 times as far from the pivot as the rock…
5) $0.1 \times 3 = 0.3 \text{ m}$ — …so to lift the rock 0.1 m, your hand moves 0.3 m. Less force, more distance.

## Find the mistake

**Leo thinks the push is 120 N. Click the line where Leo's working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  The pivot is 0.4 m from the 600 N rock. I push at the other end of the 2 m bar.
 2  Turning effect of the rock = 600 × 0.4 = 240
 3  My push is 2 m from the end of the bar, so push = 240 ÷ 2 = 120 N.
 4  120 N is less than 200 N, so I can lift it easily.
```

* Answer: line 3

<!-- Speaker note: Line 3: distances are measured from the pivot, not the end of the bar. The push is 1.6 m from the pivot, so it is 240 ÷ 1.6 = 150 N. -->

## Exit ticket

**A 400 N load is 0.25 m from a pivot. You push 1 m from the pivot on the other side. What push balances it, and how far does your hand move to lift the load 0.1 m?**

* 400 × 0.25 = 100, so 100 ÷ 1 = 100 N; the hand is 4 times as far from the pivot, so it moves 0.4 m.
