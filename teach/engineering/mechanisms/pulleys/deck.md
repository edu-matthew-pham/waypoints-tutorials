## Today

**We are learning to** one pulley changes the direction of a pull, not its size. Ropes that hold up a moving block share the load, so the pull shrinks, but you pull more rope by the same factor. Students test pulleys, work out pulls and rope lengths, see that work in equals work out, and choose a pulley system for a brief.

- I can explain that one fixed pulley changes the direction of a pull, not its size.
- I can work out the pull and the rope pulled by counting the ropes that hold the load up.
- I can explain that a pulley system's work in equals its work out.
- I can choose the fewest pulleys that meet a brief.

## Four ropes, then six

Now 6 ropes hold up a 600 N load. Start with the pull.

$$4 \text{ ropes hold the bottom block}$$

1) $400 \div 4 = 100 \text{ N}$ — The ropes share the load: each holds 100 N, and so does your hand.
2) $4 \times 1 = 4 \text{ m}$ — To lift the block 1 m, each of the 4 ropes must get 1 m shorter: you pull 4 m.
3) $100 \times 4 = 400 \quad 400 \times 1 = 400$ — Work = force × distance. Work in (100 N × 4 m) equals work out (400 N × 1 m): no free lunch.

## Find the mistake

**Mia thinks she needs only 80 N. Click the line where Mia's working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Mia's block and tackle: 4 ropes hold up the bottom block, and she pulls a fifth rope that goes over the top.
 2  That is 5 ropes, so it shares the 400 N five ways.
 3  Pull = 400 ÷ 5 = 80 N.
 4  To lift the sack 1 m she pulls 5 × 1 = 5 m of rope.
```

* Answer: line 2

<!-- Speaker note: Line 2: the rope she pulls goes over the top block, so it does not hold the bottom block up. Four ropes share the load: 400 ÷ 4 = 100 N, and she pulls 4 m. -->

## Trade-off

**Which uses less rope to lift a 600 N load 2 m: 3 ropes or 6 ropes?**

- They use the same rope
- 3 ropes: 6 m of rope instead of 12 m, but a 200 N pull instead of 100 N
- 6 ropes: more pulleys always means less work
- You cannot tell without the pulley sizes

* Answer: 3 ropes: 6 m of rope instead of 12 m, but a 200 N pull instead of 100 N

<!-- Speaker note: Rope = ropes × height. Fewer ropes: more pull, less rope. -->

## Same idea everywhere

**Each mechanism trades one thing for another. Match each to what it gives up to make your force bigger.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Gears, small driving big` | A. Length of rope you pull |
| `Lever, pivot near the load` | B. Distance your hand moves |
| `Block and tackle` | C. Speed of the output |

* Answer: Gears, small driving big → C. Speed of the output; Lever, pivot near the load → B. Distance your hand moves; Block and tackle → A. Length of rope you pull

<!-- Speaker note: All three make your force bigger, and all three make you move farther or faster by the same factor: work in equals work out. -->

## Exit ticket

**5 ropes hold up a 1000 N load. What pull lifts it, and how much rope do you pull to lift it 2 m?**
