# Gear up the winch

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A small motor can lift 200 g. The bucket is 450 g. You can't change the motor, but you have a box of gears. How do you lift it, and what do you give up?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students fit gears to a motor-driven winch, find by testing that a small gear driving a big one is slower and stronger by the same factor, work out ratio, speed, strength and lift time, choose pairs for a brief, meet a brief where constraints conflict, then write a brief of their own.

**Students should be able to say:** I can work out a gear ratio as driven teeth ÷ driver teeth. I can explain that a ratio makes the output slower and stronger by the same factor. I can check a design against every criterion in a brief. I can explain what to do when no design meets every constraint.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: does 8 → 24 meet the brief?


:::keep-together

### Check 8 → 24, then 16 → 40

1. $\text{ratio} = 24 \div 8 = 3$ — Ratio = driven teeth ÷ driver teeth. The winch needs 3 motor turns for each of its own.
2. $120 \div 3 = 40 \text{ turns a minute}$ — Slower by the ratio: divide the motor's speed.
3. $200 \times 3 = 600 \text{ g}$ — Stronger by the same ratio: multiply what the motor lifts.
4. $1 \div 0.1 = 10 \text{ winch turns}$ — The drum winds 0.1 m each turn, so 1 m takes 10 turns.
5. $10 \div 40 = 0.25 \text{ min} = 15 \text{ s}$ — Time = turns ÷ turns a minute; times 60 for seconds.
6. $600 \ge 450 \;\checkmark \quad 15 \le 15 \;\checkmark$ — Check both criteria: strong enough and fast enough. 8 → 24 passes.

Now do the same for 16 → 40. The ratio is $40 \div 16 = 2.5$. Start with the winch's speed.

**Now 16 → 40 (ratio 2.5). How many turns a minute does the winch make?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 48

120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 turns a minute.

:::


:::keep-together

### Strength

**16 → 40: what is the heaviest bucket it can lift, in grams?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 500

200 × 2.5 = 500 g.

:::


:::keep-together

### Time and criteria

**16 → 40: how many seconds to lift the bucket 1 m?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 12.5

1 m needs 10 winch turns. 10 ÷ 48 = 0.208 minutes = 12.5 s. It lifts 450 g (500 ≥ 450) within 15 s, so 16 → 40 passes too.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam's winch stalls with the 450 g bucket. Click the line where Sam's working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  Sam puts the 24-tooth gear on the motor and the 8-tooth gear on the winch.
 2  ratio = 24 ÷ 8 = 3
 3  So the winch is 3 times stronger: it lifts 3 × 200 = 600 g.
 4  600 g is more than 450 g, so it will lift the bucket.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the ratio is driven ÷ driver. The 8-tooth gear is now the driven one, so the ratio is 8 ÷ 24 = 1/3. The winch turns 3 times faster and is 3 times weaker: it lifts only about 67 g.

:::

## Ratios


:::keep-together

### Ratio

**The 16-tooth gear on the motor drives the 24-tooth gear on the winch. What is the ratio?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 1.5

24 ÷ 16 = 1.5.

:::


:::keep-together

### Speed

**Motor 120 turns a minute, ratio 5. How many turns a minute does the winch make?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 24

120 ÷ 5 = 24.

:::


:::keep-together

### Strength

**The 24-tooth gear on the motor drives the 16-tooth gear on the winch. The motor lifts 200 g with no gears. What is the heaviest lift now, to the nearest gram?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 133

Ratio 16 ÷ 24 = 2/3, so 200 × 2/3 ≈ 133 g. Faster, but weaker.

:::


:::keep-together

### A bike

**On a bike's lowest gear (small gear at the pedals, big gear at the back wheel) hills feel easy. What do you give up?**

☐ Speed: you pedal many times for each turn of the wheel

☐ Strength: it is harder to push

☐ Grip on the road

☐ Nothing: lower gears are just better

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Speed: you pedal many times for each turn of the wheel

Small driving big: slower and stronger. Easy on hills, slow on the flat.

## Exit ticket

**An 8-tooth gear drives a 40-tooth gear. The motor turns 100 times a minute and lifts 50 g on its own. How fast does the output turn, and what can it lift?**

{{lines-3}}
