# Parts that fit

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The lid you designed is exactly the size of the box's opening. You print both, and the lid won't go on. Nothing was measured wrongly. What happened?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students see that a same-size peg and hole won't fit once printed, open the hole up to a sliding fit, work out a hole size from the peg, the printer's effect and the clearance wanted, design a press fit and a different peg size, print a tolerance test card, and design a lid peg's hole.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain why same-size parts don't fit when printed. I can work out a hole size for a sliding or press fit. I can calibrate a design to our printer with a test card.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: design the hole


:::keep-together

### Work it out

1. $\text{peg drawn } 10 \to \text{printed } 10.1$ — Pegs print about 0.1 mm bigger.
2. $\text{want clearance } 0.3$ — A sliding fit.
3. $\text{hole printed} = 10.1 + 0.3 = 10.4$ — The hole must come out this big.
4. $\text{hole drawn} = 10.4 + 0.2 = 10.6$ — Holes print 0.2 mm smaller, so draw it bigger.

Now check it with the numbers.

**A 10 mm peg, a sliding fit with 0.3 mm of clearance after printing. What size should the hole be drawn (mm)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 10.6

printed peg 10.1, so the printed hole must be 10.4; drawn 10.4 + 0.2 = 10.6 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: the hole needs a planned clearance. Drawn the same, the printed hole is smaller than the printed peg.
