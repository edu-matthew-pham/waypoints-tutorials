# Design to a dimension

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Your Arduino needs a case. You print a box 'about the right size', and it's 2 mm too small. Plastic is cheap; your time and the printer's aren't. How do you get it right first time?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students find a box's inside size from its outside and walls, design a case for an Uno from the part outwards (part, gap, wall), meet Sam's one-wall slip, change the wall thickness, build the case in Tinkercad 3D by typed dimensions, and find the smallest case that fits.

**Students should be able to say:** I can work out a box's inside from its outside and walls. I can design a case from the part outwards. I can set exact sizes in Tinkercad.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: a case for an Uno


:::keep-together

### From the part outwards

1. $\text{Uno width} = 68.6$ — Start from what goes inside.
2. $68.6 + 2 \times 1 = 70.6$ — A 1 mm gap on each side: the inside width.
3. $70.6 + 2 \times 2 = 74.6$ — A 2 mm wall on each side: the outside width.
4. $74.6 \to 75$ — Round up to a size you can set (never down).

Now the depth.

**The Uno is 53.4 mm deep. With a 1 mm gap and a 2 mm wall each side, how deep is the case outside (mm)?**

Answer: {{field-15}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** 59.4

53.4 + 2 × 1 + 2 × 2 = 59.4 mm.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find the mistake

**Sam's 57 mm part won't go in. Click the line where Sam's working first goes wrong.**

```
 1  The box is 60 mm wide on the outside.
 2  The walls are 2 mm thick.
 3  So the inside is 60 − 2 = 58 mm wide.
 4  A 57 mm part will fit with 1 mm to spare.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3: there is a wall on each side, so the inside is 60 − 2 − 2 = 56 mm. The 57 mm part is 1 mm too big.
