## Today

- I can explain one effect of water movement using a specific example.
- I can distinguish environmental, economic and social effects.
- I can identify a benefit and an adverse effect of the same movement.

<!-- Speaker note: Before class: Usually Year 7. The minimum Make explains one effect type using the Snowy Scheme, plus a contrasting benefit and harm. Real facts are limited to the source cards; no current volumes, allocations or particular community viewpoints are asserted. Towns, swimming areas and other comparison cases are made up. Environmental releases are acknowledged so historic diversion is not mistaken for all current operations. Teacher: Try: channel length is a description, not a consequence. Teacher: Worked example: compare the real economic use with the environmental consequence. Teacher: Vary: mark the swimming-town case as hypothetical. -->

## Why it matters

> If water is moved to help one place, does every connected place benefit?

<!-- Speaker note: Moving water can have **environmental effects** on habitats, plants, animals and water quality; **economic effects** on production, jobs and income; and **social effects** on daily life, access and recreation. An **effect** says what changes because water moves. “The channel is long” is a description, not an effect. These categories are lenses. A change can fit more than one; explain which part you mean. -->

## One real change, different effects

The **Snowy Mountains Scheme** captures mountain water and diverts some inland to the **Murray and Murrumbidgee rivers** for irrigation and electricity generation. Irrigation supplies water to crops.

*Source: [Snowy Scheme (NSW Government, Water)](https://www.water.nsw.gov.au/our-work/projects-and-programs/snowy-scheme), checked 30 October 2026.*

NSW Environment and Heritage describes how diversion reduced flows below **Jindabyne Dam** on the Snowy River and changed the channel. Environmental releases help restore river habitat. This is an environmental consequence alongside the economic uses.

*Source: [Managing water for the environment: Snowy and montane rivers (NSW Environment and Heritage)](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/topics/water/water-for-the-environment/catchments/snowy-and-montane/managing), checked 30 October 2026.*

$$\text{Movement}$$

1) $\text{Consequence}$ — Irrigation supplies water to crops.
2) $\text{Effect}$ — Supporting crop production is an economic effect.

## Can both be true?

**A made-up channel raises crop output and reduces water reaching a marsh. Which conclusion is supported?**

- More crops prove that marsh habitat has improved
- Marsh damage proves the crops receive no benefit
- There are no effects because the water still exists
- The crop benefit and habitat harm can both be real

* Answer: The crop benefit and habitat harm can both be real
