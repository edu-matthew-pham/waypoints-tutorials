# Good for some, bad for others

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** If water is moved to help one place, does every connected place benefit?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain an effect of moving water as a change, not a description, and see that one change can benefit some people and places and harm others. They sort effects as environmental, economic or social, treating these as lenses that can overlap. The worked example is the Snowy Mountains scheme; the other cases are made up.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain one effect of water movement using a specific example. I can distinguish environmental, economic and social effects. I can identify a benefit and an adverse effect of the same movement.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the Snowy connection


:::keep-together

### One real change, different effects

1. $\text{Movement}$ — The Snowy Scheme diverts water west into the Murray and Murrumbidgee for irrigation.
2. $\text{Consequence}$ — Irrigation supplies water to crops.
3. $\text{Effect}$ — Supporting crop production is an economic effect.

The **Snowy Mountains Scheme** collects, stores and diverts water westward through tunnels and hydro-electric power stations into the **Murray and Murrumbidgee rivers**, for town water supplies, irrigation and the environment. It also generates hydro-electric power.

*Source: [Moving water through the Snowy Scheme (Snowy Hydro fact sheet)](https://www.snowyhydro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/3-Moving-water-thru-SS_Water-scheme-Factsheet.pdf), checked 1 November 2026.*

NSW Environment and Heritage describes how flows in the Snowy River below **Jindabyne Dam** were cut to a tiny share of their usual amount for decades, and how the river channel changed greatly. Environmental releases now flush sediment out to help reclaim a deeper channel. This is an environmental consequence alongside the economic uses.

*Source: [Managing water for the environment: Snowy and montane rivers (NSW Environment and Heritage)](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/topics/water/water-for-the-environment/catchments/snowy-and-montane/managing), checked 1 November 2026.*

**According to the real-place card, what is one economic effect of the Snowy Scheme’s water movement?**

☐ Water moves west through tunnels into other river systems

☐ Diverted water supplies irrigation for farms downstream

☐ The scheme collects and stores water in its dams

☐ Changed river flow alters conditions in the river channel

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Diverted water supplies irrigation for farms downstream

Diversion inland supplies irrigation that supports crop production.

:::


:::keep-together

### Can both be true?

**A made-up channel raises crop output and reduces water reaching a marsh. Which conclusion is supported?**

☐ More crops prove that marsh habitat has improved

☐ Marsh damage proves the crops receive no benefit

☐ There are no effects because the water still exists

☐ The crop benefit and habitat harm can both be real

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The crop benefit and habitat harm can both be real

Judge each consequence for the affected place or group.
