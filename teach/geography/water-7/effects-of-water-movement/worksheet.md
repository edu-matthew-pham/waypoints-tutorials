# Good for some, bad for others

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** If water is moved to help one place, does every connected place benefit?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Explain an effect of moving water, distinguish benefits and harms, and use environmental, economic and social lenses.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain one effect of water movement using a specific example. I can distinguish environmental, economic and social effects. I can identify a benefit and an adverse effect of the same movement.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the Snowy connection


:::keep-together

### One real change, different effects

1. $\text{Movement}$ — The Snowy Scheme diverts mountain water inland for irrigation.
2. $\text{Consequence}$ — Irrigation supplies water to crops.
3. $\text{Effect}$ — Supporting crop production is an economic effect.

The **Snowy Mountains Scheme** captures mountain water and diverts some inland to the **Murray and Murrumbidgee rivers** for irrigation and electricity generation. Irrigation supplies water to crops.

*Source: [Snowy Scheme (NSW Government, Water)](https://www.water.nsw.gov.au/our-work/projects-and-programs/snowy-scheme), checked 30 October 2026.*

NSW Environment and Heritage describes how diversion reduced flows below **Jindabyne Dam** on the Snowy River and changed the channel. Environmental releases help restore river habitat. This is an environmental consequence alongside the economic uses.

*Source: [Managing water for the environment: Snowy and montane rivers (NSW Environment and Heritage)](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/topics/water/water-for-the-environment/catchments/snowy-and-montane/managing), checked 30 October 2026.*

**According to the real-place card, what is one economic effect of the Snowy Scheme’s water movement?**

☐ Changed river flow alters conditions in the river channel

☐ Mountain water moves inland towards other river systems

☐ Diverted water supports farmers growing crops for sale

☐ The scheme captures water from mountain rivers and streams

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Diverted water supports farmers growing crops for sale

Diversion inland supplies irrigation that supports crop production.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib’s slip

**Nib explains the case. Click the first line where the reasoning goes wrong.**

```
 1  The Snowy Scheme diverts water inland for irrigation and electricity generation.
 2  Irrigation can support crop production.
 3  Nib says: "That benefit proves every connected place is better off."
 4  Nib decides not to check any other effects.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 3

Line 3 turns one benefit into a benefit for every place. The same diversion can help one place and harm another.

### Can both be true?

**A made-up channel raises crop output and reduces water reaching a marsh. Which conclusion is supported?**

☐ More crops prove that marsh habitat has improved

☐ Marsh damage proves the crops receive no benefit

☐ There are no effects because the water still exists

☐ The crop benefit and habitat harm can both be real

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The crop benefit and habitat harm can both be real
