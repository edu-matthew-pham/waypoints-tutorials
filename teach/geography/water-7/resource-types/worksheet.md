# Renewable, non-renewable or continuous?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** The sun comes up every day and a forest grows back, so are they the same kind of resource? Only one of them can run out.


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students learn three kinds of environmental resource by asking two questions: does it come back by itself, and can our use make it run out? Renewable resources (timber, fish, river water) come back but can be used up if taken too fast. Continuous resources (sunlight, wind, tides) cannot be used up. Non-renewable resources (coal, oil) do not come back in our time. They then apply the same test to water in three forms. This is the first of two hinge lessons: if students cannot apply the three-way sort to water, they will misclassify it for the rest of the unit and struggle with sustainability and management in K02 and K03.

**Students should be able to say:** I can sort resources into renewable, continuous and non-renewable and give a reason. I can explain why a renewable resource can be used up but a continuous one cannot. I can classify water in three forms.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: two ways of coming back


:::keep-together

### Two ways of coming back

1. $\text{Ask: can our use make it run out?}$ — Sunlight and wind, no. They keep coming whatever we do. That is continuous.
2. $\text{Ask: does it come back by itself?}$ — Timber, fish and river water, yes. That is renewable, but only if we take no more than comes back.
3. $\text{If it does not come back in our time}$ — Coal and oil took a very long time to form. Once burned they are gone for us. That is non-renewable.
4. $\text{So}$ — Renewable resources can be used up if we take them too fast. Continuous ones cannot.

Now: how many kinds of resource are there, and which one can be over-used?

**A fishing company catches fish faster than they breed. Over the years the fish get harder to find. What does this show?**

☐ A renewable resource can be used up if it is used faster than it comes back

☐ Nothing, because fish always come back

☐ Fish are a non-renewable resource

☐ Fish are a continuous resource

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** A renewable resource can be used up if it is used faster than it comes back

Renewable does not mean it can never run out. It means it comes back, and only if we do not take it faster than it does.

:::

## Make: Wider: is deep groundwater renewable?

The **Great Artesian Basin** is a huge store of water in rock under inland Australia. CSIRO calls it the only reliable source of fresh water for inland Australia, used since the first bores were sunk in the 1800s. In the west, it refills slowly now compared with thousands of years ago, and in parts of New South Wales and south-east Queensland, more water is taken out than refills it. *Source: CSIRO, "Deep water: the health of the Great Artesian Basin" (checked 30 October 2026).*
