# Renewable, non-renewable or continuous?

Water as a resource · Water as a resource

<!-- Speaker note: Students learn three kinds of environmental resource by asking two questions: does it come back by itself, and can our use make it run out? Renewable resources (timber, fish, river water) come back but can be used up if taken too fast. Continuous resources (sunlight, wind, tides) cannot be used up. Non-renewable resources (coal, oil) do not come back in our time. They then apply the same test to water in three forms. This is the first of two hinge lessons: if students cannot apply the three-way sort to water, they will misclassify it for the rest of the unit and struggle with sustainability and management in K02 and K03. About 40 minutes (Predict and Try 8 min · Worked example 12 min · Vary 8 min · Practise 6 min · Make 6 min. Typical and wider steps are extras and not counted in this.). ## Why it matters

> The sun comes up every day and a forest grows back, so are they the same kind of resource? Only one of them can run out.

<!-- Speaker note: A **resource** is something from the environment that people use. Geographers sort resources into three kinds, and the difference between them matters a lot when we ask whether something can run out. - **Renewable**: it comes back by itself within a human lifetime, but only if we take no more than comes back. Timber, fish and river water are examples. - **Continuous**: it is always there, whatever we do. We cannot use it up. Sunlight and wind are examples. - **Non-renewable**: it takes so long to form that, once used, it does not come back in any time that matters to us. Coal and oil are examples. -->

## Two ways of coming back

Now: how many kinds of resource are there, and which one can be over-used?

$$\text{Ask: can our use make it run out?}$$

1) $\text{Ask: does it come back by itself?}$ — Timber, fish and river water, yes. That is renewable, but only if we take no more than comes back.
2) $\text{If it does not come back in our time}$ — Coal and oil took a very long time to form. Once burned they are gone for us. That is non-renewable.
3) $\text{So}$ — Renewable resources can be used up if we take them too fast. Continuous ones cannot.

## Make: Wider: is deep groundwater renewable?

The **Great Artesian Basin** is a huge store of water in rock under inland Australia. CSIRO calls it the only reliable source of fresh water for inland Australia, used since the first bores were sunk in the 1800s. In the west, it refills slowly now compared with thousands of years ago, and in parts of New South Wales and south-east Queensland, more water is taken out than refills it. *Source: CSIRO, "Deep water: the health of the Great Artesian Basin" (checked 30 October 2026).*
