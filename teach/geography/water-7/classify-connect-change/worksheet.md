# Putting it together: classify, connect, change

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Knowing that river water is renewable does not tell us where it goes or what it changes. Can one explanation join those ideas?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students join at least two of the course's three ideas (classifying water as a resource, connecting places through water movement, and physical change by erosion and deposition) in one explanation of a case. Optional steps join all three. The worked example is the Murray Mouth; the other cases are made up.

**Students should be able to say:** I can link at least two of classification, connection and physical change in one case. I can use water forms, named places and process terms accurately. I can distinguish a linked explanation from a list of ideas.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: the Murray Mouth


:::keep-together

### One real case

1. $\text{Connection}$ — Water moves from Lake Alexandrina towards the Murray Mouth.
2. $\text{Change}$ — When outflow is low, sand from the ocean builds up in the mouth.
3. $\text{Link}$ — Build-up can restrict the opening and the connection.
4. $\text{So}$ — Connection and physical change explain parts of the same case.

The **Murray Mouth** in South Australia is where the River Murray meets the sea, after flowing through **Lake Alexandrina**. Barrages (gated barriers) are operated to keep seawater out of the lake. When too little river water flows out, sand from the ocean builds up and the mouth can silt up and close, so dredges have been used to keep it open.

*Sources: [River Murray flow report, 9 July 2021 (SA Department for Environment and Water)](https://www.waterconnect.sa.gov.au/Content/Flow%20Reports/RM-Flow-Report-20210709.pdf); [Murray Mouth barrage outflow and dredging (SA report card, 2023)](https://data.environment.sa.gov.au/Content/Publications/RC2023_RC_WA_SW_MurrayMouthBarrageOutflowAndDredging.pdf), checked 1 November 2026.*

**Which explanation links connection and physical change in the Murray Mouth card?**

☐ Low river outflow prevents any movement of sand, so the opening cannot change shape

☐ Renewable river water guarantees steady outflow that keeps the opening clear throughout the year

☐ Sand build-up proves river flow has increased and the sand must all come from upstream

☐ Low river outflow lets sand build up until the mouth silts up and can close

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Low river outflow lets sand build up until the mouth silts up and can close

It links a water pathway with a physical change that can restrict the opening.

:::


:::keep-together

## A new place


:::keep-together

### Trace Pebblewash Creek

**Made-up Pebblewash Creek flows from a bare slope past a village to a pond, carrying soil. Which sentence explains a connection?**

☐ The village and the slope must always receive identical rainfall

☐ The water is a continuous resource because it can flow downhill

☐ The creek carries soil from the slope past the village to the pond

☐ The slope and the pond are two different named places in this case

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** The creek carries soil from the slope past the village to the pond

It names a starting place, pathway, material and receiving place.

:::


:::keep-together

### Explain its change

**Flow slows in that pond and sand settles. Which physical change follows?**

☐ Sand builds up as a deposit on the pond bed

☐ Nothing changes, because the water stays in the pond

☐ The pond bed is worn away as the water slows down

☐ The sand is carried straight on out of the pond

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Sand builds up as a deposit on the pond bed

Deposition changes the physical landscape.

:::

## Make: Wider: a proposed pipeline

A made-up town proposes taking water from Rill Bay’s river through a pipeline. It would improve town supply but leave less flow downstream. Does it change how the river refills? What connection does it add? How might less flow affect sediment movement? State possibilities rather than certain outcomes without evidence.
