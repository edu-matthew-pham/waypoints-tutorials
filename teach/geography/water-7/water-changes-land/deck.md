- I can distinguish erosion, transport and deposition.

## Why it matters

> A river runs through a valley. Does that explain how the valley became deeper?

<!-- Speaker note: Water flowing downhill gains energy as gravity pulls it down a slope. It can loosen and remove soil or rock: **erosion**. It can carry material along: **transport**. When flow slows enough that it cannot carry some material, particles settle: **deposition**. The carried particles are **sediment**. A process explanation says how a place changes. Naming a valley or saying a river is there does not explain how water changes the land. -->

## Build the process chain

**Which order explains Kerrin’s storm?**

- Downhill flow → deposition → erosion → transport
- Downhill flow → transport → deposition → erosion
- Downhill flow → erosion → transport → deposition
- Deposition → downhill flow → erosion → transport

* Answer: Downhill flow → erosion → transport → deposition

## Explain, rather than locate

**Which explanation tells how a valley can become deeper?**

- The river lies in a low place between areas of higher ground
- A valley is a low area with a river flowing through it
- The valley is below a hill and the river is at its bottom

* Answer: Repeated flows erode the bed and carry loosened material away

## Exit ticket

**Why is the river is in a low place not enough to explain a deepening valley?**

* It gives location, not a process. Flow erodes soil or rock and transports it away; repeated removal can deepen the channel.
