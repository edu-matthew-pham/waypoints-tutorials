## Today

**We are learning to** explain how a water management strategy targets a sustainability problem and why water is hard to manage.

- I can explain how a water strategy works.
- I can match a strategy to the cause of a shortage and the place.
- I can give a trade-off and a reason water is hard to manage.

## Why it matters

> A coastal city gets part of its water from the sea. Should an inland farming district do the same?

<!-- Speaker note: A **sustainable** water supply is one that can keep meeting people's needs without running the source down. A **strategy** is a planned way of getting there. To **diagnose** a shortage is to work out what is causing it, so you can choose a strategy that fits. Each strategy works in its own way: - **Desalination**: removing the salt from seawater, or from salty water pumped up from underground. It needs a supply of salty water, a plant to build and energy to run. - **Stormwater harvesting**: collecting rain that runs off roofs, roads and drains, and storing it to use. - **Recycling grey water**: reusing water from showers, basins and washing machines, for example on gardens. - **Inter-regional transfer**: moving water by pipe or channel from one region to another. - **Reducing water use**: using less, for example with water-saving fittings or limits on watering. Two made-up places: - **Mellick Bay**, a coastal town. Its population is growing, and in dry years its dam runs low. - **The farms around Drybank**, the same made-up inland town from lesson 2, far from the sea. Little rain falls for long periods. -->

## Fit the strategy

$$\text{Name the cause}$$

1) $\text{Know how the strategy works}$ — Desalination needs salty water nearby; transfer needs a region with water to spare; reducing use lowers demand.
2) $\text{Check the place}$ — Coast or inland? Who else uses the water? Does supply change year to year?
3) $\text{Name a trade-off}$ — Cost to build and run, energy, and who might get less.

## Fit the strategy

**Made-up Mellick Bay runs short in dry years and is on the coast. Which strategy uses its location?**

- Inter-regional transfer, because it only works on the coast
- Desalination, because seawater is close by
- Building a bigger dam, because more storage always fixes dry years
- Recycling grey water, because it needs the sea

* Answer: Desalination, because seawater is close by

<!-- Speaker note: Desalination depends on the sea, which Mellick Bay has. It is not the only option: using less or collecting stormwater could also help. -->

## What recycling can do

**Which statement about recycling grey water is accurate?**

- Recycling grey water costs nothing to set up or to run
- It creates a new, unlimited supply of water, so a town can never run short
- Grey water is safe to drink and use for anything, with no treatment needed
- It lets the same water be used again for some jobs, but makes no new water

* Answer: It lets the same water be used again for some jobs, but makes no new water

## New supply or using less?

**Someone says building new supply always beats using less. What is the best reply?**

- They are right: using less never makes a real difference to a water shortage
- Using less always beats building new supply, in every place and for every cause
- Neither matters much, because rivers and dams always refill after a dry spell
- It depends on the cause: if a place uses too much, using less targets it

* Answer: It depends on the cause: if a place uses too much, using less targets it

## Apply: choose and justify

There is more than one defensible answer. A good answer names the cause, says how the strategy works on it, and is honest about its costs. Write it in your own book or document, not on this page.
