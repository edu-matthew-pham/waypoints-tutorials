# Making water last

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A coastal city gets part of its water from the sea. Should an inland farming district do the same?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain how water management strategies work (desalination, stormwater harvesting, recycling grey water, inter-regional transfer, reducing water use), match a strategy to the cause of a shortage and the place, and explain why water is hard to manage when uses are shared and supply varies. They choose and justify a strategy for a made-up place; more than one choice is defensible, so the writing is read by a partner or the teacher, not marked.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain how a water strategy works. I can match a strategy to the cause of a shortage and the place. I can give a trade-off and a reason water is hard to manage.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea: fit the strategy to the cause


:::keep-together

### Fit the strategy

1. $\text{Name the cause}$ — Is the water short (physical), not delivered (economic), or over-used?
2. $\text{Know how the strategy works}$ — Desalination needs salty water nearby; transfer needs a region with water to spare; reducing use lowers demand.
3. $\text{Check the place}$ — Coast or inland? Who else uses the water? Does supply change year to year?
4. $\text{Name a trade-off}$ — Cost to build and run, energy, and who might get less.

**Made-up Mellick Bay runs short in dry years and is on the coast. Which strategy uses its location?**

☐ Inter-regional transfer, because it only works on the coast

☐ Desalination, because seawater is close by

☐ Building a bigger dam, because more storage always fixes dry years

☐ Recycling grey water, because it needs the sea

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Desalination, because seawater is close by

Desalination depends on the sea, which Mellick Bay has. It is not the only option: using less or collecting stormwater could also help.

:::


:::keep-together

**Nib is planning for the farms around Drybank. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Perth is a real city on the coast, and gets part of its water from desalination plants.
 2  So the farms around Drybank should build a desalination plant too.
 3  It will give them all the water they want, with no costs to worry about.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Perth is on the coast, with seawater close by. The farms around Drybank are far inland: they would need salty groundwater, a plant and energy, and other strategies may fit better. Copying a strategy without checking the place and the cause can give a poor fit, and line 3 forgets the cost too.

## Check: strategies and their limits


:::keep-together

### What recycling can do

**Which statement about recycling grey water is accurate?**

☐ Recycling grey water costs nothing to set up or to run

☐ It creates a new, unlimited supply of water, so a town can never run short

☐ Grey water is safe to drink and use for anything, with no treatment needed

☐ It lets the same water be used again for some jobs, but makes no new water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It lets the same water be used again for some jobs, but makes no new water

Recycling stretches the water a place already has.

:::


:::keep-together

### New supply or using less?

**Someone says building new supply always beats using less. What is the best reply?**

☐ They are right: using less never makes a real difference to a water shortage

☐ Using less always beats building new supply, in every place and for every cause

☐ Neither matters much, because rivers and dams always refill after a dry spell

☐ It depends on the cause: if a place uses too much, using less targets it

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** It depends on the cause: if a place uses too much, using less targets it

No single approach suits every place. Match the strategy to the cause.

:::


## Apply: choose and justify

There is more than one defensible answer. A good answer names the cause, says how the strategy works on it, and is honest about its costs. Write it in your own book or document, not on this page.
