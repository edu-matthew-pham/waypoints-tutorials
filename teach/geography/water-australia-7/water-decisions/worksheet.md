# Putting it together: a water plan for one town

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A town wants a long pipeline to bring in water from somewhere else. Is that the first thing it should decide?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students join the course in one explanation for one place: a made-up town, Tarnbridge, in the real Murray–Darling Basin, with a made-up evidence pack. They diagnose the causes before choosing a strategy, check who else is affected, and use distribution, variability, scarcity and sustainability accurately. More than one strategy is defensible, so the final explanation is read by a partner or the teacher, not marked.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain where a place's water comes from and where it is in Australia. I can say what a shortage means for the people there and why it is hard to sustain. I can choose a strategy that targets a cause and use the course's words correctly.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## The idea: diagnose from the evidence


:::keep-together

### Diagnose Tarnbridge

1. $\text{Distribution}$ — Tarnbridge is in the Murray–Darling Basin and takes its water from one of its rivers.
2. $\text{Implications}$ — In dry years the river runs low, the leaking dam holds less, and a growing town needs more.
3. $\text{Why sustainability is hard}$ — Supply varies from year to year, and farms and a wetland downstream share the river.
4. $\text{Strategy}$ — Fixing the leaks targets the economic cause; reducing use targets growing demand; any strategy should be checked for who gets less.

**What is causing Tarnbridge's shortage, going by the made-up evidence?**

☐ Mainly that too many people have moved in, so the town should stop growing

☐ Mainly that it has not rained enough, so the rest of the evidence is not important

☐ Mainly the leaking dam, so fixing the leaks will solve the whole problem by itself

☐ Several things at once: dry years, a leaking dam and growing use

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Several things at once: dry years, a leaking dam and growing use

Tarnbridge has a physical cause (dry years), an economic cause (the leaking dam) and a cause from the ways water is used (growth), with other users downstream.

:::


:::keep-together

### Find Nib's slip

**Nib is writing Tarnbridge's plan. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Tarnbridge runs short of water in dry years.
 2  So the first step is to build a pipeline from another catchment.
 3  Then Tarnbridge will have enough water, whatever happens.
 4  And the other catchment will not notice the water is gone.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 2

Line 2: Nib picks the pipeline before diagnosing the causes. The leaking dam and growing use would still be there, and the catchment the water comes from would get less.

## Check: a strong explanation


:::keep-together

**Which explanation of made-up Tarnbridge is strongest?**

☐ Tarnbridge's water is sustainable, because river water is renewable and the rain always comes back after a dry year, so the river will refill by itself

☐ Tarnbridge is short of water, so it should build the pipeline from another catchment straight away, because bringing in more water will fix the shortage for good

☐ Tarnbridge is a growing town in New South Wales with a river, an old dam that leaks, farms downstream and a wetland that uses the same river

☐ In the Basin, Tarnbridge runs short in dry years as use grows; fixing leaks and using less target those causes, but sharing the river makes it hard to sustain

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** In the Basin, Tarnbridge runs short in dry years as use grows; fixing leaks and using less target those causes, but sharing the river makes it hard to sustain

The strongest explanation covers distribution, implications, why it is hard to sustain and a strategy that targets the cause.
