# Why does water run short?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** A town has a river running right past it, yet its taps keep running dry. How can that be?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students sort the causes of water scarcity three ways (a physical shortage, an economic cause where water is there but cannot be delivered or treated, and the ways water is used) and explain factors that make water vary: climate, seasonality, evaporation, location and topography. This is the second hinge: mixing up physical and economic causes leads to mismatched strategies in lesson 4.

**Students should be able to say:** I can tell a physical cause of water scarcity from an economic one. I can name a cause that comes from the ways water is used. I can explain how two factors, such as seasons and evaporation, change the water in a place.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

**Made-up Corrowin has a river beside it all year, but its taps often run dry. What is the most likely reason?**

☐ The town has no working way to get the river water to its houses

☐ It must not be raining enough at Corrowin to fill the taps

☐ People in Corrowin must be wasting too much water

☐ The river must be too small to supply a whole town

My prediction, and why:

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**What happens:** The water is there. What is missing is a way to get it, clean, into people's houses: pumps, pipes and treatment. That is an economic cause, not a lack of rain.

## Worked example: two dry towns


:::keep-together

### Two questions in order

1. $\text{Is the water physically there?}$ — Drybank, no: little rain for years. That is a physical cause.
2. $\text{Can people reliably get usable water?}$ — Corrowin, no: the river is full, but pumps, pipes or treatment are missing. That is an economic cause.
3. $\text{Is more being taken than the place can supply?}$ — If farms or a factory take more each year, that is a cause from the ways water is used.
4. $\text{Why it matters}$ — Each cause needs a different fix. A dam does not help Corrowin; new pipes do not help Drybank.

**Made-up Drybank has had little rain for three years, and its dam is nearly empty. What kind of cause is this?**

☐ Economic: Drybank's dam must be too small

☐ Physical: there is not enough water in the place

☐ Economic: the town has the wrong pipes

☐ The ways water is used: the town uses too much

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Physical: there is not enough water in the place

The water itself is missing, so it is a physical cause.

:::


:::keep-together

**Nib is explaining Corrowin. Click the first line where Nib's thinking goes wrong.**

```
 1  Corrowin is a small made-up town.
 2  Its river flows past the town all year.
 3  But the taps in Corrowin often run dry.
 4  So it must not be raining enough at Corrowin.
 5  So Corrowin needs a new dam to catch more rain.
```

Line: {{field-15}}

Why is it wrong?

{{lines-2}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** line 4

Line 4: the river is there all year, so the water is physically there. The taps run dry because the town cannot get the water to its houses. Nib's fix in line 5, a dam to catch rain, would not help.

## Name the cause


:::keep-together

### Diagnose again

**Made-up Corrowin fixes its pipes and pumps. Then a long dry spell leaves its river very low. What kind of cause is it now?**

☐ Physical: now the water itself is short

☐ Economic: the pipes must have broken again

☐ The ways water is used: Corrowin must be wasting water

☐ Economic: if the pipes are fixed, it must be the pumps now

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Physical: now the water itself is short

The same town can have different causes at different times. Diagnose from the evidence.

:::


:::keep-together

### Match the fix to the cause

**Old pipes at made-up Corrowin leak, so river water does not reach the houses. Which response targets that cause?**

☐ Repair or replace the pipes

☐ Build a bigger dam to catch more rain

☐ Ask people to wait for wetter years

☐ Ask everyone in Corrowin to use less water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Repair or replace the pipes

An economic cause needs a fix to the system that delivers the water.

:::

## Exit ticket

:::keep-together

**Give one physical and one economic cause of water running short, and say why the same fix would not suit both.**

{{lines-3}}

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Physical: little rain for years, so the water is not there. Economic: the water is there but pipes or treatment are missing. A dam does not fix missing pipes, and new pipes do not make it rain.
