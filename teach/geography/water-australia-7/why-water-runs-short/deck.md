## Today

**We are learning to** distinguish physical and economic causes of water scarcity and explain factors that make water resources vary.

- I can tell a physical cause of water scarcity from an economic one.
- I can name a cause that comes from the ways water is used.
- I can explain how two factors, such as seasons and evaporation, change the water in a place.

## Why it matters

> A town has a river running right past it, yet its taps keep running dry. How can that be?

<!-- Speaker note: **Water scarcity** means people do not have enough usable water for what they need. Geographers often sort its causes three ways. It is a way of sorting, not a law: real places often have more than one cause at once. - **Physical cause**: there is not enough water in the place. Little rain falls, or water evaporates fast. - **Economic cause**: the water is there, but people cannot get it because the pipes, pumps, dams or treatment needed have not been built or have broken down. - **The ways water is used**: more water is taken, by farms, industry or towns, than the place can supply. **Variability** means how much the amount of water changes from season to season and year to year. -->

## Two questions in order

$$\text{Is the water physically there?}$$

1) $\text{Can people reliably get usable water?}$ — Corrowin, no: the river is full, but pumps, pipes or treatment are missing. That is an economic cause.
2) $\text{Is more being taken than the place can supply?}$ — If farms or a factory take more each year, that is a cause from the ways water is used.
3) $\text{Why it matters}$ — Each cause needs a different fix. A dam does not help Corrowin; new pipes do not help Drybank.

## Exit ticket

**Give one physical and one economic cause of water running short, and say why the same fix would not suit both.**

* Physical: little rain for years, so the water is not there. Economic: the water is there but pipes or treatment are missing. A dam does not fix missing pipes, and new pipes do not make it rain.
