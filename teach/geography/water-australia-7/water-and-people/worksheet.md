# Who has access to water?

**Name:** {{field-24}} **Date:** {{field-15}} **Class:** {{field-15}}

> **Key question:** Two places sit in the same area, under the same sky. Do they have the same water?


:::teacher-note

**In one sentence:** Students explain what water distribution means for people by following a chain (source, limit, consequence) for two made-up places in one area: Pennerby, a regional town with piped, treated river water, and Fennow, a small remote settlement on rainwater tanks and a bore. They sort consequences into health, money and work, and daily life, and see that a town's supply can be limited too.

**Students should be able to say:** I can explain an implication as source, limit and consequence. I can explain two implications of water access for a remote place. I can compare water access in a remote place and a town.

**Width:** the unmarked parts are the floor every student does (predict, run, investigate and make). Typical adds the modify practice; wider adds the real-world contexts. They add contexts, not difficulty. Students work in the online tutorial alongside this sheet.

:::

## Worked example: from limit to consequence


:::keep-together

### The chain for Fennow

1. $\text{Source}$ — Fennow's water comes from rainwater tanks and one bore.
2. $\text{Limit}$ — The tanks run low in dry weeks; if the bore pump breaks, parts take days to arrive.
3. $\text{Consequence}$ — Families ration water, and trucked-in water costs a lot.
4. $\text{Compare}$ — Pennerby's treated, piped river water is more reliable day to day, but not limitless.

**Fennow's bore pump breaks and the part takes a week to arrive. What is the most direct consequence for people?**

☐ Families must ration tank water for drinking and washing until it is fixed

☐ The council will build Fennow a new treatment plant straight away

☐ Pennerby's water will stop as well, because the two places share an area

☐ Nothing changes for families, because the water is still there in the bore

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Families must ration tank water for drinking and washing until it is fixed

Source: the bore. Limit: the pump is broken. Consequence: families ration water.

:::


:::keep-together

## Explain a difference


:::keep-together

**Which sentence best explains a difference between made-up Pennerby and Fennow?**

☐ Fennow is in the same area as Pennerby, so both will have the same supply and run into the same problems in a dry spell

☐ Fennow has no water at all, because only places with a river, a treatment plant and pipes can have any water

☐ Pennerby is a bigger town than Fennow, with more houses, more shops and more people living close together beside its river

☐ Pennerby's piped, treated water is more reliable; Fennow's tanks and bore can fail, so families may ration water

:::


:::teacher-note

**Answer:** Pennerby's piped, treated water is more reliable; Fennow's tanks and bore can fail, so families may ration water

Name the difference, then follow it to a consequence for people.
