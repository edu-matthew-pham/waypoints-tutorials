# Who has access to water?

Water in Australia · Water in Australia

## Today

**We are learning to** explain implications of water distribution for people in a remote place and compare them with a town.

- I can explain an implication as source, limit and consequence.
- I can explain two implications of water access for a remote place.
- I can compare water access in a remote place and a town.

## Why it matters

> Two places sit in the same area, under the same sky. Do they have the same water?

<!-- Speaker note: An **implication** is what something means for people: a consequence. To explain one, follow a chain: **where the water comes from**, then **what limits it**, then **the consequence for people**. The two places in this lesson are made up: - **Pennerby**, a regional town. Its water is pumped from a river, cleaned at a treatment plant and piped to every house. - **Fennow**, a small remote settlement. Its water comes from rainwater tanks on the roofs and one bore. The nearest town with a treatment plant is far away. -->

## Predict

**Made-up Pennerby and Fennow are in the same area. What is most likely?**

- They must have exactly the same water, because they are in the same area
- Their water can be very different, because of its source and what has been built
- Fennow will have more reliable water, because a small place needs less of it
- Both will run short at the same time, because the same rain falls on both

## The chain for Fennow

$$\text{Source}$$

1) $\text{Limit}$ — The tanks run low in dry weeks; if the bore pump breaks, parts take days to arrive.
2) $\text{Consequence}$ — Families ration water, and trucked-in water costs a lot.
3) $\text{Compare}$ — Pennerby's treated, piped river water is more reliable day to day, but not limitless.

## The chain for Fennow

**Fennow's bore pump breaks and the part takes a week to arrive. What is the most direct consequence for people?**

- Families must ration tank water for drinking and washing until it is fixed
- The council will build Fennow a new treatment plant straight away
- Pennerby's water will stop as well, because the two places share an area
- Nothing changes for families, because the water is still there in the bore

* Answer: Families must ration tank water for drinking and washing until it is fixed

<!-- Speaker note: Source: the bore. Limit: the pump is broken. Consequence: families ration water. -->

## Explain a difference

**Which sentence best explains a difference between made-up Pennerby and Fennow?**

- Fennow is in the same area as Pennerby, so both will have the same supply and run into the same problems in a dry spell
- Fennow has no water at all, because only places with a river, a treatment plant and pipes can have any water
- Pennerby is a bigger town than Fennow, with more houses, more shops and more people living close together beside its river
- Pennerby's piped, treated water is more reliable; Fennow's tanks and bore can fail, so families may ration water

* Answer: Pennerby's piped, treated water is more reliable; Fennow's tanks and bore can fail, so families may ration water
