# AC9TDI8P14 — Manage digital footprint data

**Year 7-8**
**Strand:** Processes and Production Skills

## ACARA Descriptor

Investigate and manage the digital footprint existing systems and student solutions collect and assess if the data is essential to their purpose

## Prior Knowledge

Students can explain how their digital footprint is created and permanent, and consider privacy when collecting user data (AC9TDI6P10). They understand that a footprint exists but have not yet investigated what specific data systems collect, assessed whether that data is essential, or managed their own footprint settings.

## Progression Endpoint

Students investigate what digital footprint data existing systems and student solutions collect, manage their own footprint by reviewing privacy settings, and assess whether the data collected is essential to the system's purpose.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. What data systems collect ⚑ ★

Students can investigate what digital footprint data a system collects about its users

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who cannot name what categories of data a system collects cannot assess whether that data is essential (Node 2) or make informed decisions about privacy settings (Node 3). The investigation at Node 1 is the factual foundation for both subsequent analytical tasks.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Investigating data collection across different platform types (social media, streaming services, youth-targeted apps) reveals that collection practices vary systematically with business model and audience. Multiple platform contexts help students see the pattern rather than treating a single platform as representative.

**Minimum width:** Given a platform or app, identify at least three types of data it collects about users (e.g. location, browsing history, contacts, usage patterns)

**Typical width:** Research a platform's privacy policy to identify what data is collected, how it is used, and how long it is stored; compare data collection across two platforms

**Wider:**
- Investigate how recommendation algorithms used in music or video streaming services rely on tracking user habits to generate personalised content
- Research how data collection practices differ between platforms marketed to young people vs adults

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies at least three types of data a platform collects
- Includes at least one type of data collected passively (not explicitly provided by the user)

**Related standards:**
- AC9TDI8P13
- AC9TDI8K02

#### 2. Assessing whether data is essential

Students can assess whether the data a system collects is essential to its purpose, or whether it exceeds what is needed

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Assessing whether data is essential requires students to reason from purpose: what does this system do? → what data does it need to do that? → is this collected item necessary for that purpose? Students who cannot articulate the system's purpose cannot make the essential/non-essential distinction — they need the reasoning chain, not just the binary label.

**Minimum width:** Given a system and the data it collects, identify at least one piece of data that is essential to its purpose and one that is not; explain the difference

**Typical width:** Assess the appropriateness of data collected by surveys or platforms — for example, identifying that providing your address is not necessary for a food preferences survey but is appropriate for a Census

**Wider:**
- Evaluate whether the data collected by a student-built solution is essential — apply the same critical lens to their own design decisions
- Research how the Australian Privacy Principles define data minimisation and apply this standard to a real platform

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies at least one essential and one non-essential data item for a given system
- Explains why each is or is not essential to the system's purpose

**Related standards:**
- AC9TDI8P01

#### 3. Managing your digital footprint ★

Students can review and manage their own digital footprint by adjusting privacy settings and making informed decisions about data sharing

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Managing footprint settings across different platforms (social media, streaming, gaming) requires students to apply the same essential/non-essential judgement in different contexts where defaults and trade-offs differ. Breadth here is breadth of platform contexts and privacy trade-off types.

**Minimum width:** Review privacy settings on at least one platform they use; identify at least one setting they would change and explain why

**Typical width:** Review and manage privacy settings across multiple platforms; set defaults to maximum protection; explain the trade-off between functionality and privacy for specific settings

**Wider:**
- Evaluate the risks of sharing personal data given that generative AI can create deepfake content from short videos and audio recordings
- Investigate how privacy settings interact with recommendation systems — what changes in the user experience when data collection is restricted?

**Success criteria:**
- Reviews privacy settings on at least one platform
- Identifies at least one setting to change and explains the reasoning

**Related standards:**
- AC9TDI8P13
- AC9TDI8K02
