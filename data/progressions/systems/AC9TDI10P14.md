# AC9TDI10P14 — Apply australian privacy principles

**Year 9-10**
**Strand:** Processes and Production Skills

## ACARA Descriptor

apply the Australian Privacy Principles to critique and manage the digital footprint that existing systems and student solutions collect

## Prior Knowledge

Students can investigate and manage the digital footprint that existing systems and student solutions collect (AC9TDI8P14). They can identify what data systems collect about them. They have not yet applied the Australian Privacy Principles as a formal framework to critique systems, or used cryptographic techniques to protect user data in student solutions.

## Progression Endpoint

Students apply the Australian Privacy Principles to critique existing systems and manage the digital footprint collected by student solutions, including using appropriate technical controls to protect user data.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Understanding the Australian Privacy Principles ⚑

Students can identify the key Australian Privacy Principles relevant to digital systems and describe what each requires.

**⚑ Hinge:** APP knowledge is directly prerequisite for N2 and N3 — students who cannot identify relevant principles cannot critique systems against them or implement compliant technical controls.

**Response modes:**
- ▣ Build a repertoire: The APPs are a set of distinct principles each with different requirements. Students need to accumulate sufficient examples of each principle in action before they can apply them to critique systems at N2 — a single APP in a single context does not build the evaluative toolkit N2 requires.

**Minimum width:** Identify at least two Australian Privacy Principles and describe what each requires a system to do or refrain from doing.

**Typical width:** Identify the APPs most relevant to digital systems (including those governing collection, use, disclosure, quality, security and access); describe what each requires; explain what type of data each applies to.

**Wider:**
- Compare the Australian Privacy Principles with the EU GDPR — identify the key differences and explain what each framework is trying to achieve
- Investigate what the APPs require for data collected about minors and explain how student-facing systems must address these requirements

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies at least two APPs and their requirements
- Can distinguish between requirements for collection, use and disclosure

#### 2. Critiquing existing systems against the APPs ★

Students can critique the extent to which existing digital systems allow users to control their data in line with the Australian Privacy Principles.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: APP critique requires students to apply a specific legal framework to system behaviour — this is structured legal reasoning, not opinion. A scaffold (identify the APP → what does it require? → what does the system do? → does it comply? → what would compliance look like?) produces rigorous analysis.

**Minimum width:** Critique one feature of an existing system (e.g. social media account settings) against at least one APP; identify whether the system complies and explain why or why not.

**Typical width:** Critique an existing system (e.g. a social media platform, school app) against multiple APPs — identify which requirements are met, which are violated, and what changes would bring the system into compliance.

**Wider:**
- Audit a student-facing platform used at school against the APPs — document findings and propose a set of requirements for school procurement of digital tools
- Investigate how social media platforms' data practices have changed in response to privacy regulations — identify what changed and whether the changes genuinely improve user control or primarily achieve legal compliance

**Success criteria:**
- References a specific APP requirement (not just 'privacy in general')
- Identifies evidence from the system that it does or does not comply
- Proposes at least one specific change that would improve compliance

#### 3. Protecting user data in student solutions ★

Students can apply technical controls in their own solutions to protect user data in line with the Australian Privacy Principles, including using cryptographic techniques for passwords.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Applying APPs to a student solution requires integrating legal understanding (which APPs apply), design decisions (what data is collected and why), and technical implementation (how to protect it). Students who understand each independently often fail to connect them — the integration is applying the framework to their own work systematically rather than as an afterthought.

**Minimum width:** Identify one type of personally identifiable data in a student solution and implement at least one technical control to protect it (e.g. not publicly displaying it, or explaining why cryptographic hashing should be used for passwords).

**Typical width:** Evaluate the student solution against the APPs; implement appropriate technical controls for each identified privacy risk — including using cryptographic hashing for passwords, limiting data collection to what is necessary, and ensuring PII is not publicly accessible without consent.

**Wider:**
- Implement password hashing using a cryptographic hash function in a student solution and explain why storing hashed passwords rather than plaintext prevents a data breach from exposing users to password reuse attacks
- Design a data minimisation strategy for a student solution — identify what data is strictly necessary and remove or anonymise what is not
- Investigate what technical and organisational controls an organisation must combine to fully meet the APP security requirements — explain why technical controls alone are insufficient

**Success criteria:**
- At least one privacy risk in the student solution is identified and addressed with a technical control
- Student can explain what the control protects against and what it would not protect against
