# AC9M8N04 — Four operations with integers

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Number Concepts (Number)
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

use the 4 operations with integers and with rational numbers, choosing and using efficient strategies and digital tools where appropriate

## ACARA Elaborations

- using patterns to assist in establishing the rules for the multiplication and division of integers
- applying and explaining efficient strategies such as using the commutative or associative property for regrouping, partitioning, place value, patterning
- recognising the effect of sign in the multiplication of integers; for example, (−1)⁴ = 1 and (−1)⁵ = −1

## Prior Knowledge

Students can add and subtract integers (from AC9M7N07) and apply the four operations to positive rational numbers (from AC9M7N06). Students understand properties of operations.

## Progression Endpoint

Students apply all four operations to integers and rational numbers fluently, including multiplication and division of negative numbers, using efficient strategies.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Apply sign rules for multiplication and division of integers including negative × negative; calculate results involving mixed negative rational numbers.

**Typical width:** Establish sign rules from patterns; apply all four operations to integers and rational numbers including negative fractions; use commutative, associative and distributive properties to select efficient strategies.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why negative × negative = positive using a number line or debt/removal model as a conceptual justification.
- Explore the pattern (−1)ⁿ for increasing n — connect to sign rules and predict for any integer exponent.
- Apply efficient strategies to multi-step financial calculations involving positive and negative rational quantities.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Multiplication and division of integers ⚑

Students establish and apply the sign rules for multiplication and division of integers, including negative × negative = positive.

**⚑ Hinge — ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here:** Integer multiplication sign rules are directly required in Node 2 — students who confuse the rules when multiplying integers make the same errors when multiplying negative fractions and decimals, producing systematic sign errors across all rational number operations.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: The sign rules for multiplication are counter-intuitive, especially negative × negative = positive. Multiple representations (pattern, number line, debt model) each resolve a different facet of the misconception.

**Minimum width:** Apply sign rules to calculate products and quotients of integers, including two negative numbers.

**Typical width:** Establish sign rules using patterns; apply to multi-step calculations; explain why (−1)² = 1 and (−1)³ = −1.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the pattern (−1)ⁿ for increasing n — connect to the sign rules and predict for any integer exponent.
- Explore why negative × negative = positive using a number line or debt/removal model as a conceptual justification.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly applies sign rules for all combinations of integer multiplication and division.
- Explains the sign rule for negative × negative.
- Does not confuse multiplication sign rules with addition sign rules.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N07 — Add and subtract integers

#### 2. Efficient strategies for rational number operations

Students apply all four operations to rational numbers including negative fractions and decimals, selecting efficient strategies and digital tools appropriately.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth: Fluent application across all four operations with negative rationals requires sustained practice across varied problem types. Efficiency judgments can only develop once the operations themselves are automatic.

**Minimum width:** Calculate results involving mixed negative rational numbers using at least one efficient strategy.

**Typical width:** Apply commutative, associative and distributive properties to simplify multi-step calculations; select between mental, written and digital strategies based on the numbers involved.

**Wider:**
- Investigate a complex multi-step problem where choosing the wrong representation (fraction vs decimal vs percentage) leads to significantly more work — compare approaches.
- Apply efficient strategies to financial calculations: tax, discount, interest across combined positive and negative quantities.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly computes with negative fractions and decimals.
- Selects an efficient strategy and can justify why.
- Does not default to the most laborious method when a simpler one is available.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N06 — Four operations with rational numbers
- AC9M8N05 — Model with rational numbers
