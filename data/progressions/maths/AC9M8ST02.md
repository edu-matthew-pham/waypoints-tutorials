# AC9M8ST02 — Sampling methods and distribution analysis

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Statistics

## ACARA Descriptor

analyse and report on the distribution of data from primary and secondary sources using random and non-random sampling techniques to select and study samples

## Prior Knowledge

Students can describe and compare distributions using displays and summary statistics (from AC9M7ST01, AC9M7ST02). Students understand random sampling informally.

## Progression Endpoint

Students compare random and non-random sampling methods, collect samples, analyse and report on distributions from primary and secondary sources.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Comparing sampling methods ⚑ ★

Students compare simple random, systematic, stratified and convenience sampling methods and evaluate the reliability of each.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 requires students to collect a sample using a valid method and comment on how that method affects the distribution — students who cannot distinguish and evaluate sampling methods cannot make these methodological choices or reflect on their effect.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Understanding the trade-offs between sampling methods requires encountering each in a different context. Students who only know random sampling cannot make informed decisions about stratification or systematic approaches.

**Minimum width:** Describe simple random and systematic sampling; explain which gives a more representative sample and why.

**Typical width:** Compare simple random, systematic, stratified, quota and convenience sampling; evaluate reliability and bias potential for each; select appropriate methods for different contexts.

**Wider:**
- Investigate primary and secondary data sources relating to reconciliation between First Nations Australians and non-Indigenous Australians — analyse the sampling methods used and evaluate their representativeness.
- Explore stratified sampling: demonstrate how stratification improves representativeness when a population has distinct subgroups.

**Success criteria:**
- Describes at least three different sampling methods.
- Explains the reliability advantage of random over convenience sampling.
- Selects and justifies an appropriate sampling method for a given context.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST01
- AC9M8ST03

#### 2. Analysing and reporting on sample distributions

Students collect or obtain a sample, analyse the distribution, and report findings from primary or secondary sources.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Reporting on a sample distribution requires integrating sampling method choice, data collection, display creation, statistical description, and contextual interpretation simultaneously.

**Minimum width:** Collect a sample using a specified method; display the distribution and report key features.

**Typical width:** Analyse distributions from both primary and secondary sources; report on shape, centre and spread; comment on sampling method and its effect on findings.

**Wider:**
- Compare distributions obtained from the same population using different sampling methods — does method affect the observed distribution?
- Investigate a secondary data source (e.g. ABS data) and evaluate the sampling methodology used — how does it affect confidence in the findings?

**Success criteria:**
- Collects or obtains a sample using a valid method.
- Analyses and describes the distribution appropriately.
- Comments on how the sampling method may have affected the distribution.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST02
- AC9M8ST04
