# AC9M7M03 — Pi and circle features

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

describe the relationship between π and the features of circles including the circumference, radius and diameter

## Prior Knowledge

Students are familiar with circle vocabulary (centre, radius, diameter) from informal prior experience. Students can measure length and calculate with decimals (from AC9M6M01, AC9M7N06).

## Progression Endpoint

Students describe π as the constant ratio of circumference to diameter and use this relationship to calculate circumference and diameter.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Features of circles and their relationships ⚑

Students can identify and name the features of a circle (radius, diameter, circumference, chord) and state that diameter = 2 × radius.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who confuse radius and diameter use the wrong measurement when calculating C/d in Node 2, making π appear to be approximately 6.28 or 1.57 — this completely undermines the discovery of π's constancy and all subsequent circumference calculations.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Circle vocabulary and the d = 2r relationship must be automatic before investigating π. Students who confuse radius and diameter make systematic errors in all circle calculations.

**Minimum width:** Label all features on a circle diagram; state the relationship between radius and diameter.

**Typical width:** Measure radius and diameter of several circles; verify d = 2r consistently; identify chord, arc, sector informally.

**Wider:**
- Investigate circles in First Nations Australian contexts: circular designs in weaving, significance of circles in cultural practices.
- Explore how changing the radius changes both the diameter and circumference proportionally.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies and labels all key features of a circle.
- States that diameter = 2 × radius and applies it correctly.
- Does not confuse radius with diameter.

#### 2. Discovering π as a constant ratio ★

Students investigate the ratio C/d for multiple circles and recognise that it is always approximately 3.14 — this constant is π.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The constancy of C/d is not obvious from a single measurement. Multiple circles of very different sizes make the invariance of π compelling rather than arbitrary.

**Minimum width:** Measure the circumference and diameter of at least two circles; calculate C/d and observe the approximate constancy.

**Typical width:** Calculate C/d for several circles with different sizes; record results; discuss why the ratio is constant; state that this constant is named π.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the history of π and how mathematicians approximated it using polygons.
- Explore why π is irrational — it cannot be written as an exact fraction, only approximated.

**Success criteria:**
- Measures and calculates C/d for at least two circles and observes the result is approximately 3.14.
- States that π ≈ 3.14 and explains what it represents.
- Does not treat π as just a button on a calculator without understanding its meaning.

#### 3. Using π to calculate circumference and diameter

Students use C = πd (or C = 2πr) to calculate the circumference given the diameter or radius, and rearrange to find diameter given circumference.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students need varied practical contexts to understand when C = πd is the right tool. Purely procedural practice leads to application failures in novel problems.

**Minimum width:** Calculate the circumference of a circle given its diameter or radius; find the diameter given the circumference.

**Typical width:** Solve practical problems involving circumference: wheel rotations, perimeter of semicircles, circular paths.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how many rotations a wheel of a given diameter makes over a set distance.
- Explore applications of circumference in First Nations contexts: circular fish traps, traditional circular dwelling dimensions.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly calculates circumference given diameter or radius.
- Correctly finds diameter given circumference.
- Uses an appropriate approximation for π and specifies it.
