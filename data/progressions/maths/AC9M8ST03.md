# AC9M8ST03 — Sample size and variation

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Statistics

## ACARA Descriptor

compare variations in distributions and proportions obtained from random samples of the same size drawn from a population and recognise the effect of sample size on this variation

## Prior Knowledge

Students can describe distributions using summary statistics and displays (from AC9M7ST01, AC9M7ST02). Students understand sampling methods (from AC9M8ST02).

## Progression Endpoint

Students compare distributions from multiple samples, recognise natural sampling variation, and understand that larger samples reduce variation.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Sampling variation — same population, different samples ⚑ ★

Students recognise that samples from the same population produce different distributions due to chance, and describe the range of variation.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 requires students to investigate how variation changes as sample size increases — students who believe samples from the same population should be identical will misinterpret the Node 2 investigation, concluding that variation means something is wrong rather than recognising it as the expected phenomenon being studied.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Sampling variation is a counter-intuitive concept — students expect samples from the same population to be identical. Multiple simulation contexts build the understanding that variation is expected and natural.

**Minimum width:** Compare two samples from the same population and explain why the distributions differ.

**Typical width:** Compare multiple samples using digital simulation; describe the variation in means, medians, and proportions across samples; connect to the concept of natural sampling variation.

**Wider:**
- Investigate First Nations Ranger Groups' use of sampling to track species biodiversity — explain why they use sampling rather than census and how variation affects their conclusions.
- Explore how data-driven decision-making uses distributions from multiple samples to identify reliable trends vs noise.

**Success criteria:**
- Explains that samples from the same population will naturally vary.
- Describes the range of variation observed across multiple samples.
- Does not conclude that sampling variation means the data is wrong.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST02
- AC9M7ST01

#### 2. Effect of sample size on variation ★

Students investigate and describe how increasing sample size reduces variation in sample statistics, and apply this to making inferences.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: The sample size-variation relationship requires students to systematically vary sample size, observe the pattern, and reason about why larger samples reduce variation. An explicit investigation scaffold prevents students drawing conclusions from one or two cases.

**Minimum width:** Compare the variation in proportions from small vs large samples from the same population; state which varies less.

**Typical width:** Use digital simulations to investigate how variation changes as sample size increases; describe the pattern; apply to weather forecasting or other prediction contexts.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the effect of adding or removing data on measures of central tendency and spread — connect to understanding of sample size sensitivity.
- Explore how AI and machine learning systems require large training datasets to reduce variation in model performance — connect to the statistical principle.

**Success criteria:**
- States that larger samples produce less variation in statistics.
- Demonstrates the relationship using simulation data.
- Applies the principle to justify a recommendation about sample size in a practical context.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST04
- AC9M7P02
