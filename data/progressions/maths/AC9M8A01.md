# AC9M8A01 — Simplify linear expressions

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Algebra
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

create, expand, factorise, rearrange and simplify linear expressions, applying the associative, commutative, identity, distributive and inverse properties

## ACARA Elaborations

- rearranging and simplifying linear expressions involving variables with integer coefficients and constants; using manipulatives such as algebra tiles
- demonstrating the relationship between factorising and expanding linear expressions using manipulatives, such as algebra tiles or area models
- using the distributive, associative, commutative, identity and inverse properties to expand and factorise algebraic expressions using strategies such as the area model

## Prior Knowledge

Students can formulate algebraic expressions and apply the distributive property informally (from AC9M7A02). Students understand variables, constants and operations in expressions.

## Progression Endpoint

Students fluently expand, factorise, rearrange and simplify linear expressions, applying algebraic properties explicitly.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Expand and simplify a linear expression with one set of brackets; factorise a linear expression by identifying the HCF.

**Typical width:** Create, expand, factorise, rearrange and simplify linear expressions with multiple terms and negative coefficients; apply distributive, associative, commutative, identity and inverse properties explicitly; use algebra tiles or area models to verify.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the distributive property across subtraction — verify a(b − c) = ab − ac using substitution and area models.
- Apply rearrangement to real formulas from science and everyday contexts: rearrange distance = speed × time for speed; area formulas for dimensions.
- Explore common expansion errors (e.g. 3(x + 2) = 3x + 2) — explain why the error occurs and construct counterexamples.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 2 hinges, 1 width opportunity)

#### 1. Expanding and simplifying linear expressions ⚑

Students expand brackets in linear expressions using the distributive property and collect like terms to simplify.

**⚑ Hinge — ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth:** Expansion errors — particularly sign errors with negative coefficients — propagate directly into Node 2 (factorisation requires recognising expanded form) and Node 3 (rearrangement requires reliable expansion to verify). Students who cannot expand fluently cannot check their factorisation or rearrangement by expanding back.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth: Expanding and collecting like terms is a procedural skill requiring fluency before factorisation and rearrangement. Errors in sign handling with negative coefficients are the primary failure point.

**Minimum width:** Expand and simplify a linear expression with one set of brackets (e.g. 3(2x + 4) − x).

**Typical width:** Expand and simplify expressions with multiple brackets and negative coefficients; identify and collect like terms; use algebra tiles or area models to verify.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the distributive property across subtraction: verify that a(b − c) = ab − ac using substitution and area models.
- Explore common student errors in expansion (e.g. 3(x + 2) = 3x + 2) — explain why the error occurs and how to avoid it.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly expands brackets including those with negative coefficients.
- Collects like terms accurately.
- Does not partially distribute (e.g. only multiply the first term).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7A02 — Algebraic expressions

#### 2. Factorising linear expressions ⚑

Students factorise linear expressions by identifying the highest common factor and express as a product of the HCF and a bracket.

**⚑ Hinge — ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly:** Factorisation is required within Node 3 rearrangement (isolating a variable from multi-term expressions requires recognising common factors) — students who cannot factorise reliably cannot complete the rearrangement steps that depend on it.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly: Factorisation requires an explicit search strategy: identify the HCF of all terms, divide each term, write the bracket. Students who approach factorisation without a structured method miss common factors or introduce errors.

**Minimum width:** Factorise a linear expression with an integer HCF (e.g. 6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)).

**Typical width:** Factorise linear expressions with variable and integer common factors; verify by expanding; understand factorisation as the inverse of expansion.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the relationship between factorisation and GCD from number — connect algebraic HCF to numerical HCF.
- Explore factorising expressions with negative leading terms — develop a convention for handling negative HCF.

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies the HCF of all terms in a linear expression.
- Writes the factorised form correctly.
- Verifies by expanding back to the original expression.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02 — Prime factorisation

#### 3. Rearranging linear expressions ★

Students rearrange linear expressions and formulas using inverse operations and algebraic properties, maintaining equivalence.

**★ Width opportunity:** Rearranging real formulas from science and engineering contexts is a rich application for advancing students — the connection between formula rearrangement and solving equations is a significant algebraic insight.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point — components come together here: Rearranging formulas requires simultaneous application of expansion, factorisation, and inverse operations — integrating all prior nodes in this descriptor.

**Minimum width:** Rearrange a two-term linear expression to isolate a specified variable.

**Typical width:** Rearrange multi-term linear formulas to make any variable the subject; apply commutative and inverse properties explicitly; verify by substitution.

**Wider:**
- Apply rearrangement to real formulas from science and everyday contexts: rearrange distance = speed × time for speed; rearrange area formulas for dimensions.
- Investigate how rearrangement connects to solving equations — express the connection between 'rearranging a formula' and 'solving for a variable' explicitly.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly rearranges a linear formula to make a specified variable the subject.
- Applies inverse operations in the correct order.
- Verifies the rearranged formula by substitution.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7A01 — Variables and formulas
- AC9M8A02 — Graph and solve linear equations
