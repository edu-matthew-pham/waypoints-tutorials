# AC9M7ST01 — Data acquisition and measures of centre

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Statistics

## ACARA Descriptor

acquire data sets for discrete and continuous numerical variables and calculate the range, median, mean and mode; make and justify decisions about which measures of central tendency provide useful insights into the nature of the distribution of data

## Prior Knowledge

Students can interpret and compare distributions using mode and range (from AC9M6ST01). Students have planned and conducted statistical investigations (from AC9M6ST03).

## Progression Endpoint

Students acquire data sets, calculate range, median, mean and mode, and justify which measure best describes a given distribution.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Distinguishing discrete and continuous numerical variables

Students distinguish discrete from continuous numerical variables and understand how this affects data acquisition.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The discrete/continuous distinction is abstract until students encounter both in varied contexts. Multiple examples from different domains (counting vs measuring) are needed.

**Minimum width:** Classify a set of variables as discrete or continuous and explain the distinction.

**Typical width:** Identify appropriate methods to acquire data for discrete and continuous variables; consider precision and measurement limitations for continuous data.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how rounding affects continuous data: when is it appropriate to report a measurement to 1dp vs nearest integer?
- Explore data acquisition from secondary sources: what are the limitations of data collected by others?

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly classifies given variables as discrete or continuous.
- Explains the key distinction (countable vs measurable).
- Identifies appropriate units and precision for a continuous variable.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST03

#### 2. Calculating range, median, mean and mode ⚑

Students calculate all four summary statistics for a given data set.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who calculate statistics incorrectly cannot reason about which is most appropriate in Node 3 — their comparison is between wrong values, and any justification built on incorrect statistics is meaningless.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Accurate calculation of all four statistics must be fluent before comparison and justification of which is most useful. Errors in calculation undermine all subsequent reasoning.

**Minimum width:** Calculate range, median, mean and mode for a small data set with an odd number of values.

**Typical width:** Calculate all four statistics for data sets with even counts (median as average of two middle values); handle data sets with no mode or multiple modes.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how the presence of an outlier affects each statistic differently — which is most and least sensitive to outliers?
- Compare datasets with the same mean but different ranges and medians — what does each statistic reveal?

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly calculates range, median, mean and mode for a given data set.
- Handles even-count data sets correctly for median.
- Recognises and handles data sets with no mode or multiple modes.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST02

#### 3. Justifying which measure is most useful ★

Students reason about which measure of central tendency best describes a given distribution, considering outliers, skewness, and the purpose of the summary.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Judgment about which statistic is most useful requires encountering distributions where each statistic tells a different story. Skewed distributions with outliers are essential test cases.

**Minimum width:** For a given data set, identify which measure is most useful and explain why.

**Typical width:** Compare two distributions using appropriate measures; explain how outliers affect the mean more than the median; choose appropriate measures for different contexts.

**Wider:**
- Investigate real contexts where mean is misleading (e.g. average income with extreme wealth) and discuss which measure better represents the population.
- Explore how AI systems use descriptive statistics (mean, outlier detection) — connect to practical data science.

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies which measure best represents a given distribution and justifies the choice.
- Explains the effect of outliers on mean vs median.
- Selects different measures for different contexts appropriately.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST02
