# AC9M7P02 — Repeated experiments and simulation

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Probability

## ACARA Descriptor

conduct repeated chance experiments and run simulations with a large number of trials using digital tools; compare predictions about outcomes with observed results, explaining the differences

## Prior Knowledge

Students can assign probabilities and predict relative frequencies (from AC9M7P01). Students have run simulations with increasing trials (from AC9M6P02).

## Progression Endpoint

Students conduct repeated experiments and simulations to observe that relative frequencies approach theoretical probability as the number of trials increases, and explain differences between predicted and observed results.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Running experiments and recording results ⚑

Students conduct repeated chance experiments and record observed frequencies systematically.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who record inconsistently cannot generate reliable relative frequencies for Node 2 comparison — without a systematic cumulative record, the convergence pattern of the law of large numbers is invisible in the data.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Systematic data recording is prerequisite to any comparison between experimental and theoretical probability. Students who record inconsistently cannot see the law of large numbers pattern.

**Minimum width:** Conduct a simple chance experiment (e.g. coin toss) for at least 20 trials and record results in a frequency table.

**Typical width:** Conduct experiments for increasing numbers of trials; record cumulative results; compare observed frequency to predicted frequency at each stage.

**Wider:**
- Use digital tools to run simulations of the same experiment with 100, 1000, 10000 trials — record and compare results.
- Investigate First Nations Australian children's instructive games as probability experiments — record and analyse outcomes.

**Success criteria:**
- Records observed frequencies in an organised frequency table.
- Calculates relative frequency from observed results.
- Compares observed relative frequency to theoretical probability.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7P01

#### 2. Law of large numbers — observed vs predicted ★

Students observe that relative frequencies become closer to theoretical probability as the number of trials increases, and explain why short-run results may differ from predictions.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The law of large numbers is counter-intuitive to students who expect short runs to match predictions. Multiple experiments with different devices, each showing the same convergence pattern, build the generalised understanding.

**Minimum width:** Explain why 3 heads from 10 coin tosses does not mean the coin is unfair, using the idea that more trials are needed.

**Typical width:** Use simulation data to show relative frequencies converging toward theoretical probability over many trials; describe the pattern in their own words.

**Wider:**
- Investigate: how many trials are needed before the relative frequency is consistently within 5% of the theoretical probability?
- Apply the law of large numbers to real contexts: insurance companies and casinos rely on this — explain how.

**Success criteria:**
- States that relative frequencies approach theoretical probability as trials increase.
- Explains why short-run results can differ significantly from predictions.
- Does not commit the gambler's fallacy (thinking past outcomes affect future probabilities).
