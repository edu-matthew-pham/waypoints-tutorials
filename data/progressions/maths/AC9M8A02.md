# AC9M8A02 — Graph and solve linear equations

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Algebra
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

graph linear relations on the Cartesian plane using digital tools where appropriate; solve linear equations and one-variable inequalities using graphical and algebraic techniques; verify solutions by substitution

## ACARA Elaborations

- recognising that in a table of values, if the first difference between consecutive values of the dependent variable is constant, then it is a linear relationship
- graphing linear functions and relations including inequalities
- completing a table of values, plotting the resulting points on the Cartesian plane and determining whether the relationship is linear
- solving linear equations of the form ax + b = c and one-variable inequalities
- solving linear equations such as 3x + 7 = 6x − 9, representing these graphically, and verifying solutions by substitution

## Prior Knowledge

Students can plot ordered pairs and describe linear relationships on the Cartesian plane (from AC9M7A05). Students can solve one-variable linear equations (from AC9M7A03).

## Progression Endpoint

Students graph linear relations including inequalities, solve linear equations algebraically and graphically, and verify solutions by substitution.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Graph a linear relation y = mx + b from a table of values; solve a two-step linear equation with unknowns on both sides; verify by substitution.

**Typical width:** Graph linear relations and inequalities on the Cartesian plane; solve linear equations requiring expansion; solve one-variable inequalities including sign reversal; represent solutions on a number line; verify graphically and algebraically.

**Wider:**
- Solve linear equations arising from geometry problems — find x given angle relationships or perimeter conditions.
- Investigate equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions — represent graphically and explain what this means.
- Explore compound inequalities (a < x < b) and represent solution sets on a number line.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 2 hinges, 1 width opportunity)

#### 1. Graphing linear relations including inequalities ⚑

Students graph linear relations of the form y = mx + b and one-variable inequalities on the Cartesian plane, identifying key features.

**⚑ Hinge — ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here:** Node 3 requires graphical verification of inequality solutions — students who have not worked with inequality graph forms (including boundary line conventions) cannot use graphical methods to verify their algebraic solutions.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: Students who only graph y = mx + b forms often fail with x = a or inequality forms. Exposure to the full range of linear relation types prevents incomplete understanding.

**Minimum width:** Graph a linear relation y = mx + b from a table of values and identify gradient and y-intercept.

**Typical width:** Graph linear relations and inequalities (x = a, y = a, y ≤ mx + b); identify gradient and intercepts; describe the effect of changing m and b.

**Wider:**
- Investigate what happens to the graph when m is negative, zero, or undefined — connect each case to a physical or real-world interpretation.
- Explore graphing two linear relations on the same axes and identifying the point of intersection — preview of simultaneous equations.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly graphs a linear relation from a rule or table.
- Identifies gradient and y-intercept from graph and equation.
- Correctly represents an inequality including the boundary line convention (solid vs dashed).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7A05 — Tables and graphs from patterns
- AC9M8A04 — Experiment with linear functions

#### 2. Solving linear equations algebraically ⚑

Students solve linear equations of the form ax + b = cx + d algebraically, including equations requiring expansion, and verify by substitution.

**⚑ Hinge — ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth:** Equation-solving fluency and sign handling are directly required in Node 3 inequality solving — students who make systematic sign errors in equations will apply the same errors in inequalities, including failure to reverse the inequality sign when required.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth: Solving equations with unknowns on both sides requires procedural fluency and reliable sign handling. This is a high-error-rate skill that needs sustained practice before graphical methods are layered on.

**Minimum width:** Solve a two-step linear equation with the unknown on both sides (e.g. 3x + 7 = 6x − 9).

**Typical width:** Solve linear equations requiring expansion of brackets; solve equations involving fractions; always verify by substitution.

**Wider:**
- Solve linear equations arising from geometry problems: find the value of x given angle relationships or perimeter conditions.
- Investigate equations with no solution or infinitely many solutions — what do these look like graphically?

**Success criteria:**
- Solves a linear equation with unknowns on both sides correctly.
- Verifies the solution by substitution.
- Does not leave the unknown on both sides after the first step.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7A03 — Solve linear equations

#### 3. Solving inequalities and using graphical methods ★

Students solve one-variable inequalities algebraically and verify using graphical methods; represent solutions on a number line.

**★ Width opportunity:** The justification for inequality reversal when multiplying/dividing by a negative is a rich reasoning task — advancing students who can construct a number line argument are developing genuine algebraic proof reasoning.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly: Inequality solving requires a specific reasoning scaffold around sign reversal — students need an explicit rule and a conceptual justification or they apply it inconsistently.

**Minimum width:** Solve a one-variable inequality of the form ax + b < c and represent the solution on a number line.

**Typical width:** Solve inequalities algebraically including those requiring reversal of the inequality sign when dividing by a negative; represent on number line and verify graphically.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why dividing by a negative reverses the inequality — use a number line argument to justify.
- Explore compound inequalities (a < x < b) and represent their solution sets on a number line.

**Success criteria:**
- Solves a one-variable inequality correctly including sign reversal where needed.
- Represents the solution on a number line with correct open/closed endpoint.
- Verifies the solution graphically or by substitution.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8A04 — Experiment with linear functions
