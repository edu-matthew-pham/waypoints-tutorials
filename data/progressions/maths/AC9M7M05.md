# AC9M7M05 — Angle sum of triangles

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Measurement
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

demonstrate that the interior angle sum of a triangle in the plane is 180° and apply this to determine the interior angle sum of other shapes and the size of unknown angles

## ACARA Elaborations

- using concrete materials to demonstrate that the sum of the interior angles of a triangle is 180°; for example, using paper triangles and tearing to demonstrate
- using decomposition and the angle sum of a triangle to generalise the interior angle sum of an n-sided polygon, as 180(n − 2)

## Prior Knowledge

Students can identify angle relationships on straight lines and at points (from AC9M6M04). Students understand that angles on a straight line sum to 180°.

## Progression Endpoint

Students demonstrate that the interior angle sum of a triangle is 180° and apply this and the decomposition principle to find interior angle sums of polygons and unknown angles.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Demonstrate that the interior angles of a triangle sum to 180° using a concrete method; find an unknown angle in a triangle using the angle sum property.

**Typical width:** Demonstrate the triangle angle sum using paper tearing and dynamic geometry; apply decomposition to find the interior angle sum of quadrilaterals and other polygons; use the general formula (n−2) × 180°; find unknown angles in multi-step problems with geometric reasons.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the exterior angle sum of convex polygons (always 360°) — connect to the interior angle sum and explain the relationship.
- Explore which regular polygons tessellate and why — connect to the interior angle of each regular polygon and whether it divides 360° exactly.
- Apply angle sum reasoning to isosceles triangles where the base angle property combines with the angle sum to find unknowns.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge, 1 width opportunity)

#### 1. Demonstrating the triangle angle sum ⚑

Students demonstrate, using concrete or dynamic methods, that the interior angles of any triangle sum to 180°.

**⚑ Hinge — ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here:** Students who don't believe the 180° sum is universal make errors in Node 2 decomposition — if they think obtuse triangles have a different angle sum, the formula (n-2) × 180° breaks down and polygon calculations become unreliable.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: Students who only see one demonstration believe the angle sum might be different for unusual triangles. Testing acute, obtuse, right-angled, and very flat triangles builds confidence in the universality of the property.

**Minimum width:** Tear off the corners of a triangle and arrange them on a straight line to show they sum to 180°; verify with measurement for two different triangles.

**Typical width:** Demonstrate using paper tearing and using dynamic geometry software; explain why the property holds for any triangle.

**Wider:**
- Investigate what happens to the angle sum if you draw a triangle on a sphere — explore non-Euclidean geometry informally.
- Use the parallel lines result from AC9M7M04 to prove why the angle sum is 180° (formal derivation).

**Success criteria:**
- Demonstrates the angle sum of a triangle is 180° using at least one method.
- States the property applies to all triangles.
- Measures angles in a triangle and verifies the sum is approximately 180°.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M04 — Angles with parallel lines

#### 2. Interior angle sum of polygons by decomposition ★

Students use decomposition of polygons into triangles to determine the interior angle sum formula for n-sided polygons.

**★ Width opportunity:** The generalisation to n-sided polygons and the exterior angle sum offer advancing students structural pattern recognition — connecting decomposition to a general formula is a rich algebraic-geometric link.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly: Decomposition requires a systematic approach: draw diagonals from one vertex, count triangles, multiply by 180°. Students who decompose randomly get inconsistent results.

**Minimum width:** Decompose a quadrilateral and pentagon into triangles and calculate their interior angle sums.

**Typical width:** Derive and apply the formula (n−2) × 180° for any polygon; verify for specific cases.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the exterior angle sum of polygons (always 360° for convex polygons) — connect to interior angle sum.
- Apply to regular polygons: find the interior angle of a regular hexagon, octagon — connection to tessellation.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly decomposes a polygon into triangles and calculates the interior angle sum.
- States the general formula (n−2) × 180° and applies it correctly.
- Can verify their result by measuring.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7SP02 — Classify polygons

#### 3. Finding unknown angles using angle sums

Students use triangle and polygon angle sum properties to find unknown angles, giving reasons.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point — components come together here: Finding unknown angles requires students to select and apply the right angle property from their repertoire simultaneously — a genuine integration of angle sum, supplementary, and parallel line results.

**Minimum width:** Find an unknown angle in a triangle or quadrilateral using the angle sum property.

**Typical width:** Solve multi-step problems combining angle sum, straight line, and parallel line properties; give geometric reasons.

**Wider:**
- Solve problems involving isosceles triangles where the angle sum property combines with the equal base angles property.
- Design angle puzzles for peers — create a figure with multiple unknown angles that can be found step by step.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly finds unknown angles using angle sum properties.
- States the property used as the reason for each step.
- Applies the correct angle sum for the polygon type.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M04 — Angles with parallel lines
- AC9M7SP02 — Classify polygons
