# AC9M7ST02 — Data displays and distribution

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Statistics

## ACARA Descriptor

create different types of numerical data displays including stem-and-leaf plots using software where appropriate; describe and compare the distribution of data, commenting on the shape, centre and spread including outliers and determining the range, median, mean and mode

## Prior Knowledge

Students can calculate range, median, mean and mode (from AC9M7ST01). Students can interpret distributions using mode and range (from AC9M6ST01).

## Progression Endpoint

Students create and interpret stem-and-leaf plots and other numerical data displays, describing and comparing distributions in terms of shape, centre, spread and outliers.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Creating stem-and-leaf plots ⚑

Students construct ordered stem-and-leaf plots from a data set and read values from them accurately.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who construct disordered or incorrect stem-and-leaf plots cannot read summary statistics from them in Node 2 — an unordered display makes median identification impossible and shape description unreliable.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Constructing a correct ordered stem-and-leaf plot is a skill that requires procedural care (ordering leaves, choosing stems). Errors in construction undermine all subsequent interpretation.

**Minimum width:** Construct an ordered stem-and-leaf plot for a given data set of 10–15 values.

**Typical width:** Construct stem-and-leaf plots for larger data sets; split stem-and-leaf plots for side-by-side comparison; use software where appropriate.

**Wider:**
- Construct a back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot to compare two data sets and explain what it shows.
- Investigate what stem width to choose for a given data set to make the display useful.

**Success criteria:**
- Constructs an ordered stem-and-leaf plot with correct stems and leaves.
- Orders the leaves within each stem.
- Includes a key explaining what the stem and leaf represent.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST01

#### 2. Describing distribution shape, centre, spread and outliers ★

Students describe the distribution of data in terms of its shape (symmetrical, skewed), centre, spread and the presence of outliers.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Distribution description requires a vocabulary for varied shapes — symmetrical, positively skewed, negatively skewed, bimodal. Encountering multiple distinct distributions develops this vocabulary.

**Minimum width:** Describe a given distribution using at least three of: shape, centre, spread, outliers.

**Typical width:** Describe distributions from stem-and-leaf and dot plots; connect features of the display (clusters, gaps, symmetry) to appropriate summary statistics.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how outliers affect the appearance of a display and which statistics are most affected.
- Compare the distribution of the same variable measured in two different groups — what do the differences suggest?

**Success criteria:**
- Describes the shape of a distribution using appropriate vocabulary.
- Identifies the appropriate measure of centre for the distribution's shape.
- Identifies outliers and comments on their effect.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST01

#### 3. Comparing distributions ★

Students compare two distributions using displays and summary statistics, making statements about differences in shape, centre and spread.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Comparing distributions requires simultaneous application of display reading, summary statistic calculation, and contextual interpretation — the integration of the whole statistics strand at Year 7.

**Minimum width:** Compare two distributions by identifying at least one difference in centre, spread or shape.

**Typical width:** Systematically compare two distributions using multiple statistics and display features; draw conclusions about what the differences mean in context.

**Wider:**
- Investigate whether a claim about two groups (e.g. 'boys are taller than girls on average') is supported by the data — use statistics and displays to evaluate.
- Explore how sample size affects confidence in comparative claims.

**Success criteria:**
- Makes at least two comparative statements about two distributions (using statistics and display features).
- States conclusions in context, not just in mathematical terms.
- Does not compare individual values as if they represent the distributions.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7ST03
