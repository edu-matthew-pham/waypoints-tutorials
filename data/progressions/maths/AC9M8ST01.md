# AC9M8ST01 — Data collection techniques

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Statistics

## ACARA Descriptor

investigate techniques for data collection including census, sampling, experiment and observation, and explain the practicalities and implications of obtaining data through these techniques

## Prior Knowledge

Students can plan and conduct statistical investigations and identify variable types (from AC9M7ST03, AC9M7ST01). Students understand sampling informally.

## Progression Endpoint

Students distinguish census from sampling, identify appropriate collection techniques for different situations, and explain the practicalities, limitations and bias implications of each.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 2 hinges)

#### 1. Census vs sampling and collection technique selection ⚑ ★

Students distinguish census from sampling and identify when each is appropriate; describe the main data collection techniques.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who cannot evaluate collection techniques for appropriateness cannot identify sources of bias in Node 2 — the bias evaluation depends on first understanding what the technique does and who it includes or excludes.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Judgment about appropriate data collection technique requires encountering a wide range of contexts — scientific experiments, surveys, social studies, AI training. Students who only consider surveys miss the full range.

**Minimum width:** State the difference between census and sampling; identify which is appropriate for a given situation and explain why.

**Typical width:** Compare census, sampling, experiment and observation as data collection techniques; evaluate each for practicality, cost, and appropriateness in varied contexts.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how decisions concerning sampling relate to training AI systems — explain why biased sampling in training data leads to biased AI outputs.
- Explore how digital measuring devices and simulations change the practicalities of data collection compared to manual methods.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly distinguishes census from sampling.
- Identifies an appropriate collection technique for a given context and justifies the choice.
- Explains at least one practical limitation of sampling.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST02
- AC9M7ST03

#### 2. Bias in data collection ⚑

Students recognise and explain sources of bias in data collection and describe how bias affects conclusions.

**⚑ Hinge:** Bias identification and evaluation is prerequisite for the entire AC9M8ST02–ST04 investigation chain — students who cannot recognise sources of bias produce unreliable sampling designs, analyses, and inferences in all subsequent statistics standards.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Identifying bias requires a systematic approach: who is included, who is excluded, how is the data collected, what might influence responses? Students who proceed intuitively miss systematic biases.

**Minimum width:** Identify a source of bias in a described data collection process and explain its likely effect.

**Typical width:** Evaluate data collection designs for potential bias; distinguish sampling bias, response bias, and measurement bias; suggest how to reduce bias.

**Wider:**
- Investigate a real case of biased data collection and its consequences — connect to ethical implications of data misuse.
- Explore how voluntary response bias occurs in online surveys and polls — investigate with a class experiment.

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies a source of bias in a data collection scenario.
- Explains the likely direction and effect of the bias.
- Suggests a concrete method to reduce the identified bias.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST02
- AC9M8ST04
