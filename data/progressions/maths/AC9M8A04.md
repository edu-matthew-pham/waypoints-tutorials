# AC9M8A04 — Experiment with linear functions

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Algebra
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

experiment with linear functions and relations using digital tools, making and testing conjectures and generalising emerging patterns

## ACARA Elaborations

- using graphing software to investigate the effect of systematically varying parameters of linear functions on the corresponding graphs, making and testing conjectures
- using graphing software to systematically contrast the graphs of y = 2x, y = −2x, y = 2x and y = −2x
- using digital tools to investigate integer solutions to equations such as 2x + 3y = 48
- exploring how linear functions are used in linear regression models as a statistical technique in machine learning

## Prior Knowledge

Students can graph linear relations and describe relationships (from AC9M8A02, AC9M7A04, AC9M7A06). Students have used digital tools to vary parameters.

## Progression Endpoint

Students systematically vary parameters of linear functions using digital tools, make and test conjectures, and generalise the effect of parameter changes on the graph.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Using graphing software, vary m in y = mx + b across positive, negative and zero values; describe the effect on the graph and state a general rule.

**Typical width:** Systematically vary both m and b using digital tools; make and test conjectures about parallel lines, gradient, and intercept; generalise the effect of each parameter; investigate integer solutions to two-variable equations.

**Wider:**
- Investigate what value of m produces a line perpendicular to a given line — discover the relationship m₁ × m₂ = −1 informally.
- Explore how linear functions are used in linear regression models in machine learning — describe what the gradient and intercept represent in this context.
- Investigate integer solutions to 2x + 3y = 48 — use digital tools to find all solutions and represent on the Cartesian plane.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge, 1 width opportunity)

#### 1. Effect of gradient parameter on linear graphs ⚑

Students investigate the effect of varying the gradient m in y = mx + b on the graph and generalise the relationship between m and steepness/direction.

**⚑ Hinge — ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly:** Node 2 requires students to vary both m and b systematically and generalise about parallel lines — students who have not acquired the conjecture-testing scaffold in Node 1 produce unfounded generalisations in Node 2 or confuse the effects of the two parameters.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly: Making and testing conjectures requires an explicit investigation scaffold: vary one parameter, hold others constant, record observations, propose a general rule, test with a new case. Students who explore without structure draw weak or incorrect generalisations.

**Minimum width:** Using graphing software, vary m across positive, negative and zero values; describe the effect on the graph.

**Typical width:** Systematically vary m; compare y = 2x and y = −2x; generalise the connection between the sign and magnitude of m and the graph's steepness and direction.

**Wider:**
- Investigate what value of m produces a line perpendicular to a given line — discover the relationship m₁ × m₂ = −1 informally.
- Explore the case m is undefined (vertical lines x = a) — why can't this be written as y = mx + b?

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly describes the effect of varying m on the graph.
- States a general rule for positive, negative and zero gradient.
- Tests the conjecture with at least one new value.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8A02 — Graph and solve linear equations
- AC9M7A06 — Manipulate formulas

#### 2. Effect of intercept and generalising patterns ★

Students investigate the effect of varying the y-intercept b, and generalise patterns including the behaviour of parallel lines.

**★ Width opportunity:** Linear regression as a machine learning tool offers advancing students a genuine real-world connection — the gradient and intercept of a regression line have specific interpretable meanings that extend the Year 8 graphing work into data science.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: Generalising about linear functions requires encountering the pattern across multiple parameter combinations. Students who only investigate one direction miss the interaction between m and b.

**Minimum width:** Vary b in y = mx + b; describe the effect on the graph and state what b controls.

**Typical width:** Vary both m and b systematically; generalise that lines with the same m are parallel; investigate integer solutions to two-variable equations using digital tools.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how linear functions are used in linear regression models in machine learning — describe what the gradient and intercept represent in this context.
- Explore integer solutions to 2x + 3y = 48 — use digital tools to find all solutions and represent them on the Cartesian plane.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly states the effect of varying b on the graph.
- Identifies that lines with equal gradient are parallel.
- States a generalisation supported by evidence from their investigation.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8A03 — Model with linear functions
- AC9M7A06 — Manipulate formulas
