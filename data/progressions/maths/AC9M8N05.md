# AC9M8N05 — Mathematical modelling with percentages

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving rational numbers and percentages, including financial contexts; formulate problems, choosing efficient calculation strategies and using digital tools where appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model

## Prior Knowledge

Students can model practical problems with rational numbers and percentages (from AC9M7N09). Students can apply four operations to integers and rationals (from AC9M8N04).

## Progression Endpoint

Students formulate and solve practical models involving percentage increase/decrease, financial contexts including tax, and problems with negative quantities; review model appropriateness and communicate solutions.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Modelling percentage increase and decrease ⚑ ★

Students model situations involving percentage increase and decrease, including mark-ups, discounts, and GST, and explain why the result is an increase or decrease.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 financial modelling includes percentage-based tax and interest calculations — students who cannot reliably apply percentage increase/decrease across varied contexts cannot set up the financial models that depend on this reasoning.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Percentage increase and decrease problems span a wide range of structures and contexts. Students who only practise one type (e.g. discount) fail to recognise the same structure in other contexts (e.g. tax, population change).

**Minimum width:** Model a percentage increase or decrease problem (e.g. discount, mark-up) and explain why the result is higher or lower.

**Typical width:** Apply percentage modelling to mark-ups, discounts, GST, and market trends; explain the direction of change; use multiplier approach for efficiency.

**Wider:**
- Investigate successive percentage changes — why does a 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease not return to the original? Develop a general model.
- Model environmental or population contexts involving percentage change over extended time periods — connect to exponential growth informally.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly models a percentage increase or decrease problem.
- Explains why the change is an increase or decrease.
- Uses an efficient multiplier strategy where appropriate.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N09
- AC9M7N06

#### 2. Modelling financial and real-world contexts ★

Students formulate and solve models for financial problems including personal income tax, and interpret and communicate solutions including review of model appropriateness.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Financial modelling requires simultaneous application of percentage operations, negative number handling, and communication of results in context — the integration of all prior number strand work at Year 8.

**Minimum width:** Model a simple financial problem (e.g. tax bracket, pay rate) and interpret the solution in context.

**Typical width:** Apply modelling to income tax, comparing taxation brackets; model weather/environmental contexts with positive and negative quantities; review whether the model is appropriate for the situation.

**Wider:**
- Model personal income tax across multiple tax brackets — calculate effective tax rate and compare to marginal rate.
- Investigate modelling of sea depth or temperature changes using negative quantities — apply to real environmental data.

**Success criteria:**
- Formulates a model correctly from a financial or real-world context.
- Interprets the solution with appropriate units and contextual meaning.
- Reviews whether the model captures the situation accurately.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8N04
- AC9M7N09
