# AC9M8M01 — Area and perimeter of composite shapes

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

solve problems involving the area and perimeter of irregular and composite shapes using appropriate units

## Prior Knowledge

Students can apply area formulas for triangles, rectangles and parallelograms (from AC9M7M01). Students can calculate circumference of circles (from AC9M7M03).

## Progression Endpoint

Students decompose or compose irregular and composite shapes to find their area and perimeter using appropriate formulas and units.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Decomposing composite shapes for area ⚑

Students decompose a composite shape into known shapes and calculate the total area by summing component areas.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 requires the same dimension-identification discipline for irregular shapes — students who have not internalised the decomposition strategy in Node 1 apply it inconsistently to irregular shapes where the component boundaries are less obvious.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Decomposition requires an explicit strategy: identify the component shapes, identify dimensions from the diagram, calculate each area, combine. Students who proceed without this strategy make errors in dimension identification.

**Minimum width:** Decompose a composite shape made of two rectangles or a rectangle and triangle; calculate total area.

**Typical width:** Decompose and calculate area for a range of composite shapes including those with subtracted regions (e.g. shape with a hole); select appropriate decomposition strategy.

**Wider:**
- Investigate floor plan problems requiring area calculation for irregular rooms — apply to real building design contexts.
- Explore shapes requiring both addition and subtraction of areas (e.g. a frame) and generalise the approach.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies a valid decomposition of the composite shape.
- Calculates each component area accurately.
- Combines areas correctly (adding or subtracting as required).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M01
- AC9M8M03

#### 2. Perimeter and area of irregular shapes ★

Students estimate and calculate the perimeter and area of irregular shapes using measurement approximation strategies.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Irregular shape problems require judgment about appropriate estimation methods. Multiple practical contexts (land parcels, natural features, council maps) build the judgment to select appropriate precision.

**Minimum width:** Estimate the area of an irregular shape using a grid overlay; calculate perimeter by summing measured segments.

**Typical width:** Use increasingly accurate approximation methods (grid, line segments) to estimate area and perimeter; discuss precision and error; apply to practical contexts.

**Wider:**
- Apply area estimation to a real context: estimate the area of an irregularly shaped land parcel from a map or aerial image.
- Investigate how Simpson's rule or similar numerical integration methods formalise the estimation approach — preview of Y11/12 mathematics.

**Success criteria:**
- Estimates the area of an irregular shape using a systematic method.
- Calculates or estimates the perimeter accurately.
- States the precision of the estimate and its limitations.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8M03
