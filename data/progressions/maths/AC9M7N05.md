# AC9M7N05 — Rounding and estimation

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

round decimals to a given accuracy appropriate to the context and use appropriate rounding and estimation to check the reasonableness of solutions

## Prior Knowledge

Students can round whole numbers and simple decimals. Students understand decimal place value and can multiply/divide decimals by powers of 10 (from AC9M6N04, AC9M6N06).

## Progression Endpoint

Students round decimals to a specified accuracy appropriate to the context and use estimation to judge whether solutions are reasonable.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 0 hinges)

#### 1. Rounding decimals to specified places

Students round decimals to a given number of decimal places using standard rounding conventions.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students who only practise decontextualised rounding often fail to select appropriate accuracy in context. Varied practical contexts are needed to build judgment about what 'appropriate' means.

**Minimum width:** Round a decimal to 1, 2 or 3 decimal places as specified.

**Typical width:** Round to specified decimal places across a range of contexts; explain the rounding rule and why the convention exists.

**Wider:**
- Investigate contexts where rounding up or rounding down is more appropriate than standard rounding (e.g. buying enough paint — always round up).
- Explore the effect of rounding at each step of a multi-step calculation vs rounding only at the end.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly rounds a decimal to a specified number of decimal places.
- Applies the 'round half up' convention consistently.
- Does not confuse rounding to 2 decimal places with rounding to the nearest 100.

#### 2. Context-appropriate accuracy ★

Students choose an appropriate level of rounding for a given context and explain why that accuracy is suitable.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Judging appropriate accuracy requires a reasoning process students rarely apply spontaneously: What is the purpose of the answer? What precision is the measurement tool capable of? Does the context require over- or under-estimation?

**Minimum width:** Given a practical problem (e.g. measuring fabric, calculating cost), state appropriate rounding and justify the choice.

**Typical width:** Compare the effect of different levels of rounding across several contexts and evaluate which is most appropriate for each.

**Wider:**
- Investigate situations where over-precision misleads (e.g. reporting a population estimate to the nearest unit).
- Discuss how measurement accuracy relates to rounding: a ruler accurate to 1mm supports reporting to 1dp in cm.

**Success criteria:**
- Selects a contextually appropriate level of decimal accuracy and justifies it.
- Recognises that different contexts require different precision.
- Can explain why rounding to too many places can give a false sense of precision.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N09

#### 3. Estimation to check reasonableness

Students use estimation strategies (front-end, rounding, compatible numbers) to check whether a calculated solution is reasonable.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Estimation as a reasonableness check only becomes habitual across a wide variety of problem types. Students who practise estimation only in drill exercises rarely apply it spontaneously in problem-solving.

**Minimum width:** Estimate the answer to a calculation and compare with the computed result to check reasonableness.

**Typical width:** Apply multiple estimation strategies; identify when a computed answer is unreasonable and diagnose the likely error.

**Wider:**
- Use estimation as a first step in multi-step problems to set bounds on the expected answer before computing.
- Investigate how a calculator error (e.g. missing a decimal point) is caught by estimation.

**Success criteria:**
- Produces a reasonable estimate before or after computing.
- Identifies whether a given answer is unreasonable based on estimation.
- Explains the estimation strategy used.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N06
- AC9M7N09
