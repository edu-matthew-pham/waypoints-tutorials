# AC9M8SP01 — Congruence and similarity

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Space

## ACARA Descriptor

identify the conditions for congruence and similarity of triangles and explain the conditions for other sets of common shapes to be congruent or similar, including those formed by transformations

## Prior Knowledge

Students can classify triangles, perform transformations, and understand that transformations preserve congruence (from AC9M7SP02, AC9M7SP03). Students can identify equal angles and sides.

## Progression Endpoint

Students identify and apply the conditions for triangle congruence (SSS, SAS, AAS, RHS) and similarity (AA, SAS, SSS), and explain these conditions for other shapes including those formed by transformations.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 2 hinges)

#### 1. Congruence conditions for triangles ⚑

Students identify and apply the four conditions for triangle congruence (SSS, SAS, AAS, RHS) and explain why each is sufficient.

**⚑ Hinge:** Congruence conditions are the prerequisite for Node 2 (similarity conditions) and directly feed AC9M8SP02 — students who cannot identify and apply congruence conditions cannot access similarity reasoning or establish quadrilateral properties via congruent triangles.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Congruence proofs require an explicit three-step scaffold: identify the condition, state the matching pairs, conclude congruence. Students who proceed informally miss required elements and cannot generalise.

**Minimum width:** State the four congruence conditions and identify which applies to a given pair of triangles.

**Typical width:** Apply congruence conditions to prove triangles congruent; explain why each condition is sufficient; investigate whether SSA is a valid condition.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why SSA is not a sufficient congruence condition — construct two non-congruent triangles with the same SSA combination.
- Apply congruence proofs to geometric contexts: show two triangles formed by diagonals of a rectangle are congruent.

**Success criteria:**
- States all four congruence conditions correctly.
- Identifies which condition applies to a given pair of triangles.
- Explains why SSA is not a valid congruence condition.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7SP02
- AC9M8SP02

#### 2. Similarity conditions for triangles ⚑ ★

Students identify and apply the conditions for triangle similarity (AA, SAS, SSS) and understand similarity as a scaled version of congruence.

**⚑ Hinge:** Similarity conditions feed directly into AC9M8SP04 where students design algorithms to classify shapes as congruent, similar, or neither — thin treatment of similarity conditions means the algorithm design task in SP04 cannot be completed.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Similarity in practical contexts (maps, shadows, enlargements) requires recognising the similar triangle structure in non-obvious configurations. Multiple contexts build this recognition.

**Minimum width:** State the AA condition and use it to identify similar triangles; calculate a missing side using the scale factor.

**Typical width:** Apply all three similarity conditions; calculate scale factors; use similarity to find unknown side lengths in practical contexts.

**Wider:**
- Investigate indirect measurement using similarity: measure the height of a building or tree using shadow lengths and similar triangles.
- Apply similarity to map scale problems — connect the mathematical similarity to AC9M8M07 ratio modelling.

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies similar triangles and states the condition used.
- Correctly calculates a missing side using the scale factor.
- Maintains consistent matching of corresponding sides.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7SP03
- AC9M8SP04

#### 3. Congruence and similarity in other shapes and transformations

Students explain the conditions for congruence and similarity in other common shapes and connect congruence to transformations.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Connecting congruence/similarity to transformations integrates the spatial reasoning from Y7 transformations with the new congruence/similarity concepts — a genuine cross-topic integration.

**Minimum width:** Explain what must be equal for two rectangles to be congruent; explain what makes two rectangles similar but not congruent.

**Typical width:** Apply congruence and similarity reasoning to quadrilaterals and other polygons; connect congruence to translations, reflections and rotations; connect similarity to enlargement.

**Wider:**
- Compare angle and side measurements of shapes under each transformation type — systematically determine what changes and what stays the same.
- Investigate: is every square similar to every other square? Is every rectangle? Justify using the conditions.

**Success criteria:**
- States conditions for congruence and similarity for a given shape type.
- Connects congruence to rigid transformations and similarity to enlargement.
- Correctly identifies whether two given shapes are congruent, similar, or neither.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7SP03
