# AC9M8M02 — Volume and capacity of right prisms

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

solve problems involving the volume and capacity of right prisms using appropriate units

## Prior Knowledge

Students can calculate volume of rectangular and triangular prisms using V = Ah (from AC9M7M02). Students understand unit conversion between cubic units and litres.

## Progression Endpoint

Students solve problems involving volume and capacity of right prisms, selecting appropriate units and applying reasoning about optimal dimensions.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Unit relationships between volume and capacity ⚑

Students understand and apply the relationships 1 cm³ = 1 mL, 1000 cm³ = 1 L, 1 m³ = 1000 L, and use these to convert between volume and capacity units.

**⚑ Hinge:** Unit conversion errors propagate directly into Node 2 — students who confuse cm³ and mL or m³ and L produce answers in the wrong unit or off by a factor of 1000 in practical capacity problems.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Unit conversion between volume and capacity is a precision skill that students frequently get wrong under pressure. Fluency here is prerequisite to problem-solving.

**Minimum width:** Convert between cm³ and mL, and between m³ and L, for given volumes.

**Typical width:** Apply unit conversion in practical problem contexts; choose appropriate units for the context; explain the relationship between cubic centimetres and millilitres.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how unit choice affects the numerical value of a measurement — when is it more practical to use litres vs cubic metres in real contexts?
- Explore the volume of a cubic metre using 1000 × 1000 mL = 1000 L as a concrete model — connect to large-scale water storage contexts.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly converts between cm³, mL, L and m³.
- Selects appropriate units for a given practical context.
- Does not confuse cm³ and cm (volume vs length).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M02

#### 2. Solving practical volume and capacity problems ★

Students solve practical problems involving volume and capacity of right prisms, including optimisation and multi-step contexts.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Practical volume problems vary significantly in what reasoning is needed — capacity problems, packaging optimisation, and dimension-finding all require recognising the same underlying formula but in different configurations.

**Minimum width:** Solve a practical problem requiring volume calculation and capacity interpretation.

**Typical width:** Apply volume reasoning to optimal packaging, production and dimension problems; interpret capacity in context; select which measurements matter for the problem.

**Wider:**
- Investigate optimal packaging: for a fixed volume, what dimensions minimise surface area? Explore patterns for rectangular prisms.
- Apply volume and capacity to a production context: given a required capacity and one fixed dimension, find the remaining dimension.

**Success criteria:**
- Selects and applies the correct formula for the prism type.
- Interprets the result in context with appropriate units.
- Identifies which measurements are relevant to the problem.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M02
- AC9M8M01
