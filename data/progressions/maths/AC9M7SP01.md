# AC9M7SP01 — 2d representations of 3d objects

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Space

## ACARA Descriptor

represent objects in 2 dimensions; discuss and reason about the advantages and disadvantages of different representations

## Prior Knowledge

Students can compare parallel cross-sections and recognise their relationship to right prisms (from AC9M6SP01). Students have informal experience with drawings of 3D objects.

## Progression Endpoint

Students represent 3D objects using nets, isometric drawings, and plan/elevation views, and reason about the advantages and disadvantages of each representation.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Nets of prisms and other 3D objects

Students identify, draw and construct nets of prisms and other common 3D objects.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students develop spatial reasoning for nets by working with multiple 3D objects. Restricting practice to cubes gives a false sense of mastery that fails with other prisms.

**Minimum width:** Identify which nets will fold to make a cube or rectangular prism; draw a net for a triangular prism.

**Typical width:** Test multiple possible nets for the same object; identify which are valid; construct 3D objects from nets.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how many valid nets a cube has (11) — systematically find them all.
- Design and construct nets for less familiar solids (square pyramid, cylinder).

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies valid nets for a cube and rectangular prism.
- Draws a net that folds correctly to produce a given 3D object.
- Does not include overlapping faces in a net.

#### 2. Isometric and plan/elevation drawings ⚑

Students draw and interpret isometric drawings and plan/elevation views (front, back, side, top) of 3D objects.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who produce inconsistent views cannot reason about representation trade-offs in Node 3 — they cannot evaluate whether a net or isometric view is more useful without being able to correctly produce and read both.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Interpreting and drawing 2D representations of 3D objects requires systematic reasoning: which face is visible from each view? Students who proceed intuitively produce inconsistent views.

**Minimum width:** Construct at least two views (e.g. top and front) of a given 3D object; interpret an isometric drawing.

**Typical width:** Draw all standard views of a given object; build an object from its views; use isometric paper to draw 3D objects.

**Wider:**
- Interpret aerial/isometric views of real buildings or structures and identify the 3D object they represent.
- Investigate 2D representations in First Nations Australian art — discuss what spatial information is conveyed.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly draws at least two views of a 3D object.
- Reconstructs a 3D object from its views.
- Does not confuse view orientation (e.g. top vs front).

#### 3. Advantages and disadvantages of different representations ★

Students reason about when each type of 2D representation is most useful and what information each preserves or loses.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Reasoning about representation trade-offs requires familiarity with all representation types simultaneously — this node integrates the prior two nodes.

**Minimum width:** Identify one advantage and one disadvantage of using a net vs an isometric drawing for the same object.

**Typical width:** Compare representations for a range of objects and purposes; match the representation to the task (e.g. net for construction, isometric for communication).

**Wider:**
- Investigate professional contexts: architects use plans, packaging designers use nets, engineers use orthographic views — discuss why.
- Evaluate: which representation best communicates a given 3D design decision?

**Success criteria:**
- States at least one advantage and one disadvantage for two different representations.
- Selects an appropriate representation for a given purpose and justifies the choice.
- Recognises that no single representation shows all information about a 3D object.
