# AC9M7N04 — Rational numbers on a number line

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Number Concepts (Number)
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

find equivalent representations of rational numbers and represent rational numbers on a number line

## ACARA Elaborations

- investigating equivalence of fractions using common multiples and a fraction wall, diagrams or a number line
- expressing a fraction in simplest form using common divisors
- applying and explaining the equivalence between fraction, decimal and percentage representations of rational numbers; for example, 16%, 0.16, 4/25 are equivalent
- representing positive and negative fractions and mixed numerals on various intervals of the real number line, including intervals that are not symmetric about zero

## Prior Knowledge

Students can compare and order fractions using equivalence, add and subtract fractions, and convert between simple fractions and decimals (from AC9M6N03, AC9M6N04, AC9M6N07). Students understand percentage as parts per hundred.

## Progression Endpoint

Students find equivalent representations of rational numbers across fraction, decimal and percentage forms, simplify fractions, and place positive and negative fractions and mixed numerals on a number line.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Find equivalent fractions using common multiples, simplify fractions to lowest terms, and convert between fraction, decimal and percentage for familiar values.

**Typical width:** Apply equivalence across fraction, decimal and percentage forms for a range of rational numbers; represent positive and negative fractions and mixed numerals on a number line across intervals that are not symmetric about zero.

**Wider:**
- Investigate repeating decimals (1/3 = 0.333..., 1/7 = 0.142857...) as rational numbers — explore the repeating pattern and what makes it terminate or repeat.
- Explore the density of rational numbers — between any two rationals, find another — and discuss informally what this implies about the number line.
- Apply fraction-decimal-percentage conversion in consumer contexts: comparing discounts, nutrition labels, and interest rates presented in different forms.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge, 1 width opportunity)

#### 1. Equivalence and simplification of fractions ⚑

Students find equivalent fractions using common multiples and express fractions in simplest form using common divisors.

**⚑ Hinge — ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth:** Simplification errors propagate into every subsequent conversion and operation with rational numbers — Node 2 conversions, Node 3 number line placement, and all of AC9M7N06 depend on fractions being in comparable forms.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth: Fraction equivalence and simplification underpin every subsequent operation with rational numbers. Precision here matters — small errors in simplification compound.

**Minimum width:** Find two equivalent fractions for a given fraction and simplify a fraction to its lowest terms.

**Typical width:** Use the fraction wall and number line to verify equivalence; apply simplification in computation contexts.

**Wider:**
- Investigate what the simplest form of a fraction tells you about the relationship between numerator and denominator (coprime numbers).
- Connect simplification to GCD from AC9M7N02: simplest form means GCD(numerator, denominator) = 1.

**Success criteria:**
- Generates multiple equivalent fractions for a given fraction.
- Correctly simplifies a fraction to lowest terms.
- Can explain why the fractions are equivalent (multiplying/dividing numerator and denominator by the same value).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02 — Prime factorisation
- AC9M7N06 — Four operations with rational numbers

#### 2. Converting between fraction, decimal and percentage ★

Students convert fluently between fraction, decimal and percentage representations of rational numbers.

**★ Width opportunity:** Repeating decimals and the density of rationals are genuinely rich extensions — advancing students who encounter 1/3 = 0.333... and reason about what this means are engaging with number structure beyond procedural conversion.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: Students often have strong conversion skills in one direction (fraction to decimal) but poor skills in others. Encountering the same rational number across varied contexts and conversion directions resolves partial understanding.

**Minimum width:** Convert a given rational number between all three representations (fraction, decimal, percentage) for common values.

**Typical width:** Explain the conversion process for each direction; identify equivalent representations for less familiar fractions (e.g. 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%).

**Wider:**
- Investigate repeating decimals (e.g. 1/3 = 0.333...) and understand these as rational numbers.
- Explore how percentage, decimal and fraction choices affect ease of computation in different contexts.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly converts a given rational number between fraction, decimal and percentage forms.
- Identifies when representations are equivalent without performing full conversion.
- Does not confuse 0.5 with 5% (confusing decimal and percentage forms).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N05 — Round decimals appropriately
- AC9M7N06 — Four operations with rational numbers

#### 3. Rational numbers on the number line

Students place positive and negative fractions, decimals and mixed numerals accurately on a number line, including intervals other than 0–1.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly: Placing negative fractions requires a reasoning scaffold: students must convert to a comparable form and reason about which direction is more negative. Without explicit steps, students often mirror positive fractions incorrectly.

**Minimum width:** Plot a given set of positive and negative fractions and mixed numerals on a number line with appropriate scale.

**Typical width:** Construct a number line for a given interval, select appropriate scale, and place a mixed set of rational numbers including fractions, decimals and percentages on it.

**Wider:**
- Given a number line with some rational numbers marked, identify and justify the positions of unlabelled points.
- Explore density of rational numbers: between any two rational numbers, find another — repeat and discuss what this implies.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly places a given set of rational numbers (including negatives and mixed numerals) on a number line.
- Selects and labels appropriate scale for the interval.
- Correctly orders the set from least to greatest.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N07 — Add and subtract integers
