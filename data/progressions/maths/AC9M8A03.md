# AC9M8A03 — Model with linear functions

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Algebra
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve applied problems involving linear relations, including financial contexts; formulate problems with linear functions, choosing a representation; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model

## ACARA Elaborations

- modelling situations involving linear functions, including practical contexts such as taxi fares involving flag fall fees, motion in a straight line
- modelling problems in practical situations and interpreting solutions within the context of the problem, including giving attention to all units of measurement
- modelling financial problems involving pay rates, using a table of values to represent the pay amounts and hours worked using an hourly rate of pay
- modelling patterns on Country/Place and exploring their connections and meaning to linear equations, using the model as a predictive tool

## Prior Knowledge

Students can graph linear relations and solve linear equations (from AC9M8A02). Students have experience with mathematical modelling (from AC9M7N09).

## Progression Endpoint

Students formulate linear function models for applied problems, choose appropriate representations, solve and interpret solutions, and review model appropriateness.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Formulate a linear function for a practical context with a constant rate and fixed component; use it to answer a specific question; identify one limitation of the model.

**Typical width:** Formulate and solve linear models for varied applied contexts (taxi fares, pay rates, motion); choose appropriate representation (table, graph, equation); interpret solutions; review model appropriateness including domain and linearity assumptions.

**Wider:**
- Compare two linear models on the same axes (e.g. two taxi companies) — find the break-even point algebraically and interpret in context.
- Model patterns on Country/Place with linear functions — investigate Indigenous knowledge connections and use the model as a predictive tool.
- Apply linear modelling to financial contexts comparing different pay structures (base + commission vs flat rate) — model, compare and advise.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge, 1 width opportunity)

#### 1. Formulating linear models ⚑ ★

Students identify a linear relationship in a practical context, formulate it as a function y = mx + b, and identify the meaning of gradient and intercept in context.

**⚑ Hinge — ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here:** Node 2 requires solving and interpreting the model — students who cannot formulate the linear function from context have no model to solve, interpret, or review for appropriateness.

**★ Width opportunity:** Comparing two linear models and finding the break-even point, or connecting linear modelling to Country/Place contexts, offers advancing students a rich applied and cultural extension within the Year 8 band.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: Formulating a linear model requires recognising the constant rate structure across varied contexts. Students who only practise with one context type fail to identify the linear structure in novel situations.

**Minimum width:** Given a practical context with a constant rate and fixed component, formulate the linear function and identify m and b in context.

**Typical width:** Formulate linear models for varied contexts (taxi fares, pay rates, motion); identify gradient as rate of change and intercept as fixed value; choose table, graph or equation as appropriate.

**Wider:**
- Model patterns on Country/Place with linear functions — investigate Indigenous knowledge connections to linear relationships and use the model as a predictive tool.
- Compare two linear models on the same axes (e.g. two taxi companies) and find the break-even point algebraically.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies gradient and intercept from a practical context.
- Formulates the linear function in equation form.
- Interprets m and b in terms of the problem context.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8A02 — Graph and solve linear equations
- AC9M8A04 — Experiment with linear functions

#### 2. Solving, interpreting and reviewing the model

Students solve a linear model for a given value, interpret the solution in context, and evaluate whether the model is appropriate for the situation.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point — components come together here: Solving, interpreting and reviewing simultaneously requires students to coordinate graphical, algebraic and contextual reasoning — the integration across all three representations is the learning.

**Minimum width:** Use a linear model to answer a specific question; state the solution in context; identify one limitation of the model.

**Typical width:** Solve practical problems using the model; interpret solutions including units and contextual meaning; review model appropriateness considering domain, linearity assumption, and real-world constraints.

**Wider:**
- Investigate where a linear model breaks down — at what point does a taxi fare model, pay rate model, or motion model stop being realistic?
- Apply linear modelling to a financial context involving different pay structures (base + commission vs flat rate) — model, compare and advise.

**Success criteria:**
- Uses the linear model correctly to answer a specific question.
- States the solution in contextually appropriate terms.
- Identifies at least one limitation or domain restriction of the model.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N09 — Model with rational numbers
- AC9M8A02 — Graph and solve linear equations
