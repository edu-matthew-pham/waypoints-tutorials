# AC9M8M07 — Modelling with ratios and rates

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems involving ratios and rates, including financial contexts; formulate problems; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, reviewing the appropriateness of the model

## Prior Knowledge

Students can solve ratio and rate problems (from AC9M7M06, AC9M8M05). Students have modelling experience from prior years.

## Progression Endpoint

Students formulate and solve models for practical problems involving ratios and rates in financial and scientific contexts, review appropriateness and communicate solutions.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Formulating and solving ratio/rate models ⚑ ★

Students identify whether a problem requires a ratio or rate model, formulate it, and solve in context.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 requires evaluating model appropriateness — students who have only formulated one model type cannot identify when the chosen model oversimplifies or when a different structure would be more appropriate.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Ratio and rate modelling problems span an unusually wide range of contexts at Year 8. Multiple varied contexts are needed to build the judgment to select the right model structure.

**Minimum width:** Formulate and solve a practical problem using either a ratio or rate model; state which is appropriate and why.

**Typical width:** Apply modelling to scales and plans, chemical mixing, currency conversion, and income tax; justify model choice; select efficient calculation strategy.

**Wider:**
- Investigate radiocarbon dating as a ratio application: model the ratio of carbon-14 to carbon-12 isotopes in organic material to estimate age.
- Model the ratio applications in First Nations Australians' string and cordage making — investigate the ratio of fibre lengths and twisting rates involved.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies whether the problem requires a ratio or rate model.
- Formulates and solves the model correctly.
- Justifies the model choice and interprets the solution in context.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8M05
- AC9M7M06
- AC9M7N08

#### 2. Reviewing model appropriateness

Students evaluate whether a ratio or rate model is appropriate for a given situation, identify assumptions, and suggest improvements.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Reviewing model appropriateness requires coordinating modelling experience, real-world knowledge, and mathematical reasoning simultaneously — the integration of the whole measurement modelling strand.

**Minimum width:** State one assumption in a given model and explain how it might limit the model's accuracy.

**Typical width:** Review the appropriateness of a model for varied practical contexts; identify when a simple ratio/rate model oversimplifies; suggest model refinements.

**Wider:**
- Compare simple and refined models for the same situation (e.g. constant vs variable exchange rate) — evaluate which is more appropriate and in what circumstances.
- Investigate a financial model (income tax brackets) and evaluate where the 'step function' model differs from a smooth rate model — discuss the implications.

**Success criteria:**
- Identifies at least one assumption made in a given model.
- Explains how the assumption affects the model's accuracy.
- Suggests a reasonable refinement to the model.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8N05
- AC9M8A03
