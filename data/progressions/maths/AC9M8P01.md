# AC9M8P01 — Complementary events

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Probability

## ACARA Descriptor

recognise that complementary events have a combined probability of one; use this relationship to calculate probabilities in applied contexts

## Prior Knowledge

Students can assign probabilities to single-stage events and verify they sum to 1 (from AC9M7P01). Students understand the sample space.

## Progression Endpoint

Students recognise complementary events, use P(A) + P(not A) = 1 to find probabilities, and apply this to practical contexts including AI applications.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Recognising complementary events ⚑

Students recognise complementary events as pairs that together cover all outcomes, and state that P(A) + P(not A) = 1.

**⚑ Hinge:** Confusion between complementary and mutually exclusive events causes systematic errors in Node 2 — students who cannot reliably identify complements apply P(not A) = 1 − P(A) to the wrong events.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Recognising complementary events requires reliable identification before the relationship can be applied strategically. Students who confuse complementary with mutually exclusive make systematic errors in node 2.

**Minimum width:** Identify the complement of a given event and verify that P(A) + P(not A) = 1 for a specific example.

**Typical width:** Identify complementary pairs across varied contexts (dice, spinners, weather); state the relationship; distinguish complementary from mutually exclusive events.

**Wider:**
- Investigate events that are not easily described by name — construct the complement explicitly from the sample space.
- Explore whether two events can be both complementary and mutually exclusive — discuss what this tells you about the sample space.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies the complement of a given event.
- States P(A) + P(not A) = 1 and verifies it for a specific case.
- Does not confuse complementary events with mutually exclusive events.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7P01

#### 2. Using the complement relationship to calculate probabilities ★

Students use P(not A) = 1 − P(A) to calculate probabilities in applied contexts, including cases where finding the complement is more efficient than direct calculation.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The strategic insight — that finding the complement is sometimes easier — only develops across varied problem types. Students who only practise direct complement calculation miss the efficiency judgment that makes the relationship powerful.

**Minimum width:** Given P(A), find P(not A); apply to a practical problem where the complement is given and the event probability is required.

**Typical width:** Apply the complement relationship to a range of practical contexts; recognise when finding the complement is the more efficient strategy; apply to simple 'at least one' problems.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how AI applications use complementary event probabilities when assessing binary outcomes — e.g. spam vs not spam, fraud vs not fraud.
- Explore P(at least one) = 1 − P(none) for simple cases — apply to problems where direct calculation would require listing many outcomes.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly calculates P(not A) given P(A) and vice versa.
- Identifies at least one context where finding the complement is more efficient.
- Applies the complement relationship correctly to a practical problem.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7P01
- AC9M8P02
