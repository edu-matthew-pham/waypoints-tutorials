# AC9M8ST04 — Statistical investigations with samples

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Statistics

## ACARA Descriptor

plan and conduct statistical investigations involving samples of a population; use ethical and fair methods to make inferences about the population and report findings, acknowledging uncertainty

## Prior Knowledge

Students can plan and conduct statistical investigations (from AC9M7ST03). Students understand sampling methods and variation (from AC9M8ST02, AC9M8ST03).

## Progression Endpoint

Students plan and conduct investigations using ethical and fair sampling methods, make inferences about the population, and report findings while explicitly acknowledging uncertainty.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Planning an ethical investigation with sampling ⚑

Students plan a statistical investigation including ethical and fair sampling methods, anticipate sources of uncertainty, and identify how findings will be used to make inferences.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 requires conducting the investigation and reporting with acknowledged uncertainty — without a valid, ethically-designed plan from Node 1, there is no investigation to conduct and no basis for making or communicating inferences.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Planning an ethical investigation requires explicit reasoning about who is included, who might be harmed by the data collection, and how to minimise bias — students need a planning scaffold that surfaces these considerations.

**Minimum width:** Plan a statistical investigation specifying: the population, the sampling method, why it is ethical and fair, and what inference is being sought.

**Typical width:** Design an investigation with explicit ethical consideration; identify potential sources of bias and uncertainty; plan how to communicate uncertainty in the report.

**Wider:**
- Investigate progress in reconciliation between First Nations Australians and non-Indigenous Australians — evaluate the ethical considerations in sampling and reporting for such sensitive topics.
- Explore how businesses use electricity consumption data to draw conclusions about event impacts (e.g. pandemic effects) — evaluate the ethical data use.

**Success criteria:**
- Specifies the population, sample, and sampling method.
- Identifies at least one ethical consideration and addresses it in the plan.
- States what inference will be drawn from the investigation.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST01
- AC9M8ST02
- AC9M7ST03

#### 2. Conducting, inferring and reporting with uncertainty ★

Students conduct the investigation, make inferences about the population from the sample, and report findings while explicitly acknowledging the uncertainty introduced by sampling.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Conducting and reporting with acknowledged uncertainty integrates sampling method choice, distribution analysis, inference-making, and communication — the full statistics strand at Year 8.

**Minimum width:** Conduct a sample investigation; make one inference about the population; state one source of uncertainty in the conclusion.

**Typical width:** Conduct a full investigation cycle; make multiple inferences; report findings including confidence language; connect sample size to the degree of uncertainty acknowledged.

**Wider:**
- Compare the inferences drawn from two different sample sizes for the same population — discuss how the larger sample changes confidence in the inference.
- Explore how statistical uncertainty is communicated in public health or environmental reporting — compare with how uncertainty is often omitted in media reports.

**Success criteria:**
- Makes a valid inference about the population from the sample.
- Explicitly acknowledges uncertainty in the report.
- Connects the degree of uncertainty to the sample size and method.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8ST03
- AC9M7ST03
