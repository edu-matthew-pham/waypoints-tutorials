# AC9M7M02 — Volume of right prisms

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

solve problems involving the volume of right prisms including rectangular and triangular prisms, using established formulas and appropriate units

## Prior Knowledge

Students can calculate area of triangles, rectangles and parallelograms (from AC9M7M01, AC9M6M02). Students understand volume as the measure of 3D space and have worked with rectangular prisms using unit cubes.

## Progression Endpoint

Students solve problems involving the volume of right prisms (rectangular and triangular) using V = Ah with appropriate units.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Volume as cross-section area times height ⚑

Students understand that the volume of any right prism equals the area of its cross-section multiplied by its height.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who don't generalise beyond V = lwh cannot calculate the volume of triangular prisms in Node 2 — they lack the cross-section principle that makes V = Ah work for any right prism.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students who learn V = lwh as a formula for rectangular prisms only often fail to generalise to triangular prisms. The cross-section principle requires reinforcement across multiple prism types.

**Minimum width:** Explain and demonstrate why V = Ah for a rectangular prism using unit cubes; state that the same principle applies for triangular prisms.

**Typical width:** Connect the area of the base (cross-section) to the volume; explain why the cross-section must be consistent (uniform) throughout the height.

**Wider:**
- Build rectangular prisms from unit cubes and verify V = l × w × h = Ah for various dimensions.
- Explore why V = Ah only holds for prisms with uniform cross-section — investigate what changes for a pyramid.

**Success criteria:**
- States that V = Ah for right prisms and explains what A and h represent.
- Correctly identifies the cross-section and its area for a given prism.
- Does not confuse the total surface area with the cross-sectional area.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M01

#### 2. Calculating volume of rectangular and triangular prisms ★

Students calculate the volume of rectangular and triangular right prisms using V = Ah with appropriate units.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Volume problems in varied practical contexts (construction, storage, water capacity) build the habit of appropriate unit selection and conversion, which students neglect in decontextualised practice.

**Minimum width:** Calculate the volume of a rectangular prism and a triangular prism given all necessary dimensions.

**Typical width:** Solve practical volume problems including selecting appropriate units (cm³, m³, litres); convert between units.

**Wider:**
- Solve real-world problems: volume of concrete for a triangular ramp, volume of a storage container — with unit conversion.
- Investigate the volume of composite prisms: e.g. an L-shaped prism decomposed into two rectangular prisms.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly calculates volume for both rectangular and triangular prisms.
- Uses appropriate cubic units.
- Converts between units where required (e.g. cm³ to litres).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M06
- AC9M7N06
