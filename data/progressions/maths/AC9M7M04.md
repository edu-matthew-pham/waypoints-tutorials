# AC9M7M04 — Angles with parallel lines

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Measurement
**Planning mode:** A — Concept-certifying

## ACARA Descriptor

identify corresponding, alternate and co-interior relationships between angles formed when parallel lines are crossed by a transversal; use them to solve problems and explain reasons

## ACARA Elaborations

- constructing a pair of parallel lines and a pair of perpendicular lines using their properties, a pair of compasses and a ruler, set squares or using geometry software
- using dynamic geometry software to identify relationships between alternate, corresponding and co-interior angles for a pair of parallel lines cut by a transversal
- using dynamic geometry software to demonstrate how angles and their properties are involved in the design and construction of scissor lifts, folding umbrellas
- using geometric reasoning of angle properties to generalise the angle relationships of parallel lines and transversals

## Prior Knowledge

Students can identify angle relationships on lines and at points and determine unknown angles (from AC9M6M04). Students understand that angles on a straight line sum to 180° and angles at a point sum to 360°.

## Progression Endpoint

Students identify corresponding, alternate and co-interior angle relationships and use them with geometric reasoning to find unknown angles.

## Width Tiers

**Width varies by:** phenomena and contexts

**Minimum width:** Identify corresponding, alternate and co-interior angle pairs for parallel lines cut by a transversal; use one angle relationship to find a missing angle.

**Typical width:** Apply all three angle relationships (corresponding equal, alternate equal, co-interior supplementary) to find unknown angles in diagrams; give geometric reasons for each step; apply across varied diagram orientations.

**Wider:**
- Use dynamic geometry software to explore how the angle relationships change when lines are not parallel — identify when the properties no longer hold.
- Apply angle reasoning to real design contexts: analyse angles in scissor lifts, folding umbrellas, or roof truss designs.
- Construct parallel and perpendicular lines using compasses and ruler — connect the construction process to the angle properties used to verify parallelism.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 2 hinges, 2 width opportunities)

#### 1. Parallel lines and transversals ⚑

Students identify parallel lines and transversals in diagrams and name the eight angles formed.

**⚑ Hinge — ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth:** Students who confuse angle pair types (corresponding, alternate, co-interior) apply the wrong property in Nodes 2 and 3 — they may apply the equal-angles rule to co-interior pairs (which are supplementary), producing wrong answers throughout.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here — fluency matters more than breadth: Correct identification of angle pairs is prerequisite to all reasoning in this descriptor. Students who confuse corresponding with alternate make systematic errors in every subsequent problem.

**Minimum width:** Identify and label all eight angles formed by a transversal crossing two parallel lines.

**Typical width:** Draw parallel lines with a transversal; identify corresponding, alternate, and co-interior pairs by position.

**Wider:**
- Investigate designs and structures where parallel lines and transversals appear (roof trusses, railway tracks, tiled floors).
- Use dynamic geometry software to verify that corresponding angles remain equal as the transversal angle changes.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies and labels the eight angles formed.
- Correctly identifies at least one example of each: corresponding, alternate, co-interior pair.
- Does not confuse vertically opposite angles with the parallel line angle relationships.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M05 — Angle sum of triangles

#### 2. Properties of corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles ⚑ ★

Students state and apply the angle properties: corresponding angles are equal, alternate angles are equal, co-interior angles are supplementary.

**⚑ Hinge — ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here:** The co-interior/alternate confusion produces persistent errors in Node 3 multi-step reasoning — students who apply the wrong property at step 1 propagate wrong values through the entire chain, with no self-correction possible without restating geometric reasons.

**★ Width opportunity:** Understanding why the angle properties hold (via translation/rotation argument) is richer than applying them — advancing students who can construct an informal justification develop genuine geometric reasoning.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts — more examples resolve misconceptions here: Students frequently misapply the properties, especially confusing co-interior (supplementary) with alternate (equal). Multiple diagram orientations — not just the standard vertical layout — resolve this.

**Minimum width:** State each property and calculate a missing angle using one of the three relationships.

**Typical width:** Apply multiple properties in sequence to find all unknown angles in a parallel lines diagram; give reasons for each step.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why these properties hold — informally, by translating or rotating the transversal to show correspondence.
- Apply properties to real-world geometric problems: angles in roof pitch design, alignment of structures.

**Success criteria:**
- States each angle property correctly.
- Calculates missing angles using the correct property.
- Does not apply the equal-angle property to co-interior pairs.

#### 3. Using angle reasoning to solve problems ★

Students solve multi-step angle problems involving parallel lines, giving geometric reasons for each step.

**★ Width opportunity:** Multi-step geometric proof design is a rich task for advancing students — creating problems that require chaining three or more angle facts develops systematic reasoning within the Year 7 band.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning — unpack the logic explicitly: Multi-step geometric reasoning requires an explicit scaffold: identify what you know, select the applicable property, state the reason. Students who calculate without reasons cannot self-check and lose marks systematically.

**Minimum width:** Solve a problem requiring two steps of angle reasoning and state the reason for each step.

**Typical width:** Solve problems combining parallel line angle properties with straight line and vertically opposite angle facts; write full geometric reasons.

**Wider:**
- Design a problem requiring at least three angle reasoning steps — write the solution with full justification.
- Investigate how angle properties are applied in geometric proofs (informal): show that a given pair of lines must be parallel.

**Success criteria:**
- Solves a multi-step angle problem correctly.
- States a geometric reason for each step.
- Does not assume angle values without justification.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M05 — Angle sum of triangles
- AC9M7SP02 — Classify polygons
