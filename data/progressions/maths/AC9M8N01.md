# AC9M8N01 — Irrational numbers

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

recognise irrational numbers in applied contexts, including square roots and π

## Prior Knowledge

Students can locate non-perfect square roots between consecutive integers and understand π as the constant ratio C/d (from AC9M7N01, AC9M7M03). Students understand the real number line and rational number representation.

## Progression Endpoint

Students recognise irrational numbers in applied contexts, understand that they cannot be expressed as exact fractions, and approximately locate them on the real number line.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Irrational numbers as non-terminating, non-recurring decimals ⚑ ★

Students understand that irrational numbers cannot be expressed as fractions and have decimal expansions that neither terminate nor recur.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who conflate 'irrational' with 'very large' or 'impossible to use' cannot reason correctly in Node 2 about locating irrationals on the number line — they treat approximations as exact values and cannot describe the real number system accurately.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The concept of irrationality is abstract — students need multiple concrete examples (√2, √3, π, φ) and contexts to build a stable understanding that goes beyond memorising which numbers are irrational.

**Minimum width:** State that √2 and π are irrational; explain what makes a number irrational.

**Typical width:** Distinguish rational from irrational numbers across a range of examples; use digital tools to explore the decimal expansion of irrational numbers; explain why they cannot be written as exact fractions.

**Wider:**
- Investigate historical approximations to π in different societies (Egyptian, Chinese, Indian, Greek) and evaluate the accuracy achieved.
- Explore the golden ratio φ = (1 + √5)/2 in art and design — verify it is irrational and locate it approximately on the real number line.

**Success criteria:**
- States that irrational numbers cannot be expressed as fractions and have non-terminating non-recurring decimal expansions.
- Correctly classifies given numbers as rational or irrational.
- Does not confuse 'irrational' with 'very large' or 'impossible to use in calculation'.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8N02
- AC9M8M06

#### 2. Locating irrational numbers on the real number line

Students approximately locate irrational numbers on the real number line and understand the real number system as including both rational and irrational numbers.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Locating irrational numbers requires explicit reasoning: identify bounding perfect squares, take their roots, narrow the interval. Without the scaffold students place irrationals arbitrarily.

**Minimum width:** Locate √2 and √3 approximately on the real number line by identifying the integers they fall between.

**Typical width:** Locate a range of irrational numbers (square roots, π) on the number line using digital tools; describe the real number system as the union of rationals and irrationals.

**Wider:**
- Use digital tools to zoom into the real number line and observe the density of both rational and irrational numbers in any interval.
- Investigate applied contexts where irrational numbers arise: hypotenuse lengths from Pythagoras, circumference calculations — connect to where exact values are needed vs approximations.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly locates an irrational number between two consecutive integers on the number line.
- Describes the real number system as including both rationals and irrationals.
- Does not treat the decimal approximation as the exact value.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N01
- AC9M8M06
