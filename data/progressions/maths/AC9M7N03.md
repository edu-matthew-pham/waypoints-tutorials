# AC9M7N03 — Expanded notation and powers of 10

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

represent natural numbers in expanded notation using place value and powers of 10

## Prior Knowledge

Students understand place value for large whole numbers and can multiply and divide by powers of 10 (from AC9M6N06). Students can write numbers in expanded form using place value (e.g. 7000 = 7 × 1000).

## Progression Endpoint

Students represent natural numbers in expanded notation using powers of 10, and explain the connection between place value and exponent notation.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Powers of 10 as place value columns ⚑

Students connect each place value column to a power of 10 and write powers of 10 in exponent notation (10⁰=1, 10¹=10, 10²=100, etc.).

**⚑ Hinge:** Students with a patchy place-value-to-power mapping produce systematic errors in Node 2 — wrong exponents mean wrong expanded forms, and the entire purpose of this descriptor is lost.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: The mapping of place value to powers of 10 must be automatic before expanded notation is learnable. Students who have this mapping patchy make systematic errors in node 2.

**Minimum width:** Match place value columns (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) to their corresponding power of 10 and write each in exponent notation.

**Typical width:** Extend to at least 10⁶; explain why 10⁰ = 1; connect the exponent to the number of zeros.

**Wider:**
- Investigate 10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³ as a preview of decimal place value extending the pattern.
- Connect to scientific notation contexts: the diameter of Earth is approximately 1.27 × 10⁷ metres.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly states the power of 10 corresponding to each place value column up to millions.
- Writes 10⁰ = 1 and explains why.
- Does not confuse the exponent with the value (e.g. does not say 10³ = 30).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02

#### 2. Expanded notation using powers of 10

Students write any natural number in expanded notation using powers of 10 (e.g. 7000 = 7 × 10³) and convert back from expanded to standard form.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students who only see standard expanded form (7×1000 + 3×100) often fail to make the connection to exponent notation. Multiple representations across the same numbers are needed.

**Minimum width:** Write a given 4–5 digit number in expanded notation using powers of 10 and reconstruct a number from expanded notation.

**Typical width:** Work fluently with expanded notation for numbers up to 7 digits; identify the value of each digit expressed as a power of 10.

**Wider:**
- Compare expanded notation with and without exponents (e.g. 7×1000 vs 7×10³) and explain what the exponent adds.
- Explore how expanded notation makes computation transparent: why is 6300 × 100 = 6300 × 10² easy to compute mentally?

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly writes any given natural number in expanded notation using powers of 10.
- Reconstructs a number correctly from its expanded notation.
- Explains what each term in the expanded form represents.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02
