# AC9M8M03 — Circumference and area of circles

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

solve problems involving the circumference and area of a circle using formulas and appropriate units

## Prior Knowledge

Students understand π and can calculate circumference using C = πd (from AC9M7M03). Students can calculate area of rectangles and triangles.

## Progression Endpoint

Students derive and apply A = πr² to solve problems involving circle area and circumference, including composite shapes with circular components.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Deriving the area of a circle formula ⚑

Students understand why A = πr² using at least one informal derivation method.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who only memorise A = πr² without understanding cannot reliably choose between area and circumference formulas in Node 2 — they conflate the two formulas under pressure, particularly when asked to find radius from area (requiring rearrangement of a formula they don't understand).

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students who only memorise A = πr² without any derivation experience cannot explain why the formula works or recover it when forgotten. Multiple derivation approaches build robust understanding.

**Minimum width:** State A = πr² and explain informally why it is reasonable (e.g. the area is between 2r² and 4r²).

**Typical width:** Investigate the area using a square grid or sector rearrangement method; connect the result to A = πr²; verify the formula is consistent with known approximations.

**Wider:**
- Use the sector rearrangement method — divide circle into increasingly smaller sectors arranged into a near-rectangle — and show why the rectangle's dimensions give πr × r = πr².
- Investigate the connection between area and circumference: A = ½ × C × r — explain this geometrically.

**Success criteria:**
- States A = πr² and can explain the formula using at least one informal argument.
- Explains why the area must be between 2r² and 4r².
- Does not confuse area formula with circumference formula.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M03

#### 2. Applying circle area and circumference formulas ★

Students apply A = πr² and C = 2πr to calculate area and circumference, find radius/diameter given area or circumference, and solve composite shape problems.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Circle measurement problems require recognising which formula applies and which quantity is unknown. Varied practical contexts build the judgment to select the right formula and direction of calculation.

**Minimum width:** Calculate area given radius; find radius given area; solve one practical problem.

**Typical width:** Apply both formulas in practical contexts; find any unknown (r, d, C, or A) given another; solve composite shape problems including semicircles.

**Wider:**
- Explore traditional weaving designs by First Nations Australians and investigate the significance and use of circles — calculate areas and circumferences in context.
- Investigate composite shapes combining circles with other polygons — e.g. area of a stadium or athletic track.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly calculates area given radius or radius given area.
- Solves a composite shape problem involving circular components.
- Uses an appropriate approximation for π and states it.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8M01
- AC9M7M03
