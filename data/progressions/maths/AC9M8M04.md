# AC9M8M04 — Duration and time zones

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

solve problems involving duration, including using 12- and 24-hour time across multiple time zones

## Prior Knowledge

Students can calculate elapsed time and convert between 12- and 24-hour time. Students understand the concept of time zones informally.

## Progression Endpoint

Students solve problems involving duration and time zone conversion, including multi-zone itinerary and scheduling problems.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Converting between time zones ⚑

Students convert times between time zones using UTC offsets and apply 12- and 24-hour time conventions.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 multi-zone scheduling requires chaining multiple individual time zone conversions — students who cannot reliably handle day changes or offset arithmetic in Node 1 produce cascade errors across the multi-zone itinerary.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Time zone problems vary significantly: some cross midnight, some cross the date line, some involve daylight saving. Multiple real-world contexts are needed to develop reliable conversion reasoning.

**Minimum width:** Convert a given time from one time zone to another using a UTC offset.

**Typical width:** Convert between multiple time zones; handle day changes; apply to real contexts (flight arrival times, international calls, Perth vs eastern states).

**Wider:**
- Investigate why time zones exist and how the International Date Line creates calendar day differences.
- Explore daylight saving — how does it affect time zone offsets between Australian states, and why is it not observed uniformly?

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly converts a time between two given time zones.
- Handles day changes correctly when conversion crosses midnight.
- Uses 24-hour time appropriately to avoid AM/PM ambiguity.

#### 2. Solving multi-zone scheduling and itinerary problems ★

Students solve practical problems involving scheduling across multiple time zones, including travel itineraries and virtual meeting planning.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Multi-zone scheduling requires coordinating UTC offsets, duration calculation, date change reasoning, and 12/24-hour conversion simultaneously — a genuine integration of all time concepts.

**Minimum width:** Plan a meeting time that works for two people in different time zones; show the time in both zones.

**Typical width:** Plan an international travel itinerary across multiple time zones; calculate local arrival and departure times; identify scheduling challenges for a multi-national team.

**Wider:**
- Plan an international travel itinerary across at least three time zones in Asia; calculate all local times; identify the best transit options.
- Investigate the practical challenge of regular virtual meetings for a company with staff in different Australian states and an international office — propose a fair meeting time.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly plans a meeting or itinerary accounting for all time zone offsets.
- Identifies and resolves day-change issues.
- Communicates the solution clearly showing local times in each zone.
