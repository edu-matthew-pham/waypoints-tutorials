# AC9M8M06 — Pythagoras' theorem

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Measurement

## ACARA Descriptor

use Pythagoras' theorem to solve problems involving the side lengths of right-angled triangles

## Prior Knowledge

Students can identify perfect squares, square roots, and right-angled triangles (from AC9M7N01, AC9M7SP02). Students have informal exposure to the 3-4-5 triangle.

## Progression Endpoint

Students state and apply Pythagoras' theorem to find unknown side lengths in right-angled triangles in practical and geometric contexts.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 2 hinges)

#### 1. Understanding and stating Pythagoras' theorem ⚑ ★

Students state Pythagoras' theorem (a² + b² = c²) and understand it as a relationship between areas of squares on the sides of a right-angled triangle.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 application requires students to identify the hypotenuse and set up a² + b² = c² correctly — students who have only memorised a formula without understanding the area relationship misidentify the hypotenuse or set up the equation incorrectly, producing systematically wrong answers.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Proofs from multiple cultural traditions enrich understanding of the theorem and demonstrate it is not exclusively a Western mathematical result. Multiple verification methods also build confidence before applying the theorem.

**Minimum width:** State the theorem and identify which side is the hypotenuse; verify the theorem holds for a 3-4-5 triangle.

**Typical width:** Verify the theorem using area diagrams or proofs from multiple cultural traditions; state the converse; identify Pythagorean triples.

**Wider:**
- Investigate different applications, demonstrations and proofs of Pythagoras' theorem from multiple cultures: Egypt, Mesopotamia, India, China, and Greece — compare the approaches.
- Identify Pythagorean triples systematically: (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (7,24,25), (8,15,17) — investigate how to generate new triples from known ones.

**Success criteria:**
- States Pythagoras' theorem correctly and identifies the hypotenuse.
- Verifies the theorem for at least one specific case.
- States the converse: if a² + b² = c² then the triangle is right-angled.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N01
- AC9M8N01

#### 2. Finding unknown side lengths using Pythagoras ⚑ ★

Students apply Pythagoras' theorem to find the hypotenuse or a shorter side in right-angled triangles, including in practical and coordinate geometry contexts.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 3 triangle classification requires comparing a² + b² with c² — students who cannot fluently apply the theorem to find unknown sides (and who misidentify c as the longest side) apply the classification test to the wrong quantities.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Applying Pythagoras requires first identifying the right-angled triangle within a diagram. Students who only practise decontextualised calculations fail to identify the relevant triangle in applied problems.

**Minimum width:** Find the hypotenuse and find a shorter side given the other two sides for at least two examples.

**Typical width:** Apply the theorem to varied practical problems; identify right-angled triangles within diagrams; apply to distance between two points on the Cartesian plane.

**Wider:**
- Apply the theorem to 3D problems: find the diagonal of a rectangular prism using the theorem twice.
- Investigate how Pythagoras' theorem can determine distance between two points on a Cartesian plane — connect to applications in predictive text and mapping.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies the hypotenuse in a given right-angled triangle.
- Finds an unknown side correctly using the theorem.
- Applies the theorem to a practical problem context.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8N01
- AC9M7SP03

#### 3. Classifying triangles using side lengths

Students use the relationship between a² + b² and c² to classify triangles as right-angled, acute or obtuse.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Triangle classification requires comparing a² + b² with c² and reasoning about the direction of the inequality. Students need an explicit reasoning scaffold to apply this reliably.

**Minimum width:** Given three side lengths, determine whether the triangle is right-angled, acute or obtuse.

**Typical width:** Apply the classification rule across a range of triangles; explain the reasoning for acute (a² + b² > c²) and obtuse (a² + b² < c²) cases.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why the inequality changes direction for acute and obtuse triangles — connect to the geometric argument about how the angle affects the opposite side length.
- Apply classification to survey and construction contexts: verify whether a corner is square using the converse of Pythagoras.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly classifies a triangle given three side lengths.
- States the rule for each classification (right, acute, obtuse).
- Identifies the longest side as c before applying the test.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7SP02
