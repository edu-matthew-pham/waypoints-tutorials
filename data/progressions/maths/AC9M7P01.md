# AC9M7P01 — Sample space and probability

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Probability

## ACARA Descriptor

identify the sample space for single-stage events; assign probabilities to the outcomes of these events and predict relative frequencies for related events

## Prior Knowledge

Students can assign probabilities on a 0–1 scale using fractions, percentages and decimals (from AC9M6P01). Students have experience with probability language and listing outcomes.

## Progression Endpoint

Students identify sample spaces for single-stage events, assign probabilities to outcomes, and use these to predict relative frequencies.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Identifying and listing sample spaces ⚑

Students identify the sample space for a single-stage event and list all possible outcomes systematically.

**⚑ Hinge:** An incomplete sample space produces wrong probability fractions for all outcomes in Node 2 — if an outcome is missing from the sample space, every other outcome gets an inflated probability and the probabilities cannot sum to 1.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Systematic, complete listing of a sample space is prerequisite to assigning probabilities. Students who list incomplete sample spaces assign wrong probabilities to all outcomes.

**Minimum width:** List the sample space for a coin toss and a standard die roll; explain what sample space means.

**Typical width:** List sample spaces for spinners, lucky dips, and card draws; verify the sample space is complete by confirming all outcomes are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

**Wider:**
- Investigate sample spaces with unequal likelihood outcomes — can outcomes still be listed even if not equally likely?
- Compare sample spaces for different versions of the same game (6-sided vs 8-sided die).

**Success criteria:**
- Lists a complete sample space for a given single-stage event.
- Explains what sample space means.
- Verifies completeness (all outcomes accounted for).

#### 2. Assigning probabilities to outcomes

Students assign theoretical probabilities to outcomes of a single-stage event using P = favourable/total and express these as fractions, decimals or percentages.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students often believe probability only applies to fair devices. Encountering non-fair devices (biased coins, unequal spinners) generalises their understanding.

**Minimum width:** Assign the probability of each outcome for a fair die roll; express as a fraction.

**Typical width:** Assign probabilities for events with unequal outcomes (e.g. spinners with unequal sectors); express in fraction, decimal and percentage forms; verify probabilities sum to 1.

**Wider:**
- Investigate non-fair devices: if a spinner has sectors of 1/4, 1/4, and 1/2, assign probabilities and verify.
- Connect probability fractions to relative frequency: a probability of 1/6 means in the long run about 1 in every 6 outcomes.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly assigns the probability to each outcome in a sample space.
- Verifies that all probabilities sum to 1.
- Expresses probability in the most appropriate form for the context.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N04
- AC9M7N06

#### 3. Predicting relative frequencies ★

Students use theoretical probability to predict how often an outcome will occur in a given number of trials.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Predicting relative frequencies requires connecting probability, multiplication, and the meaning of the sample space — integrating all prior nodes.

**Minimum width:** Predict the number of times an outcome will occur in 60 trials given its probability.

**Typical width:** Predict relative frequencies for multiple outcomes; compare predictions to simulated results; explain discrepancies.

**Wider:**
- Investigate how the accuracy of predictions improves with more trials — connect to AC9M7P02.
- Apply relative frequency prediction to real decisions: fairness of a game, likelihood of an event occurring.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly predicts the relative frequency for a given number of trials.
- Understands that predictions are expected values, not certainties.
- Can adjust predictions for non-fair devices.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7P02
