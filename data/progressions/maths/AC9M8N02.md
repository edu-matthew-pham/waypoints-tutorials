# AC9M8N02 — Exponent laws

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

establish and apply the exponent laws with positive integer exponents and the zero-exponent, using exponent notation with numbers

## Prior Knowledge

Students can write numbers using exponent notation and connect powers of 10 to place value (from AC9M7N02, AC9M7N03). Students understand prime factorisation.

## Progression Endpoint

Students establish and apply the product, quotient, and power-of-a-power rules, and the zero exponent, using numerical examples and exponent notation.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 0 hinges)

#### 1. Establishing the exponent laws from expanded form

Students derive the product of powers, quotient of powers, and power of a power rules from expanded form, and state each rule.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Deriving the laws from expanded form requires an explicit step-by-step process. Students who jump to applying rules without derivation have fragile understanding and apply laws incorrectly to different bases.

**Minimum width:** Derive and state the product rule (aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ) using at least two numerical examples.

**Typical width:** Derive all three laws from expanded form; state each rule; verify using digital tools; establish that a⁰ = 1 for any non-zero base.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why a⁰ = 1 using the quotient rule: aⁿ/aⁿ = a⁰ = 1 — connect to the pattern of decreasing exponents.
- Explore the pattern extending to negative exponents: what would a⁻¹ mean, following the sequence pattern?

**Success criteria:**
- Derives at least one exponent law from expanded form.
- States all three laws and the zero exponent correctly.
- Explains why a⁰ = 1.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02

#### 2. Applying exponent laws in combination ★

Students apply the exponent laws individually and in combination to simplify numerical expressions, checking that bases are the same before applying.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Applying the laws in combination requires procedural fluency with each law before they can be chained. Errors typically arise from applying laws across different bases or confusing additive and multiplicative rules.

**Minimum width:** Apply the product and quotient rules to simplify expressions with the same base.

**Typical width:** Apply all three laws in combination to simplify multi-step numerical expressions; identify when the zero exponent arises naturally; use digital tools to verify.

**Wider:**
- Investigate expressions where multiple laws apply in sequence — develop a systematic order of operations for exponent simplification.
- Explore how exponent laws apply in scientific notation contexts: multiply and divide numbers in scientific notation using the product and quotient rules.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly applies each exponent law individually.
- Applies laws in combination without confusing different-base expressions.
- Does not apply exponent laws when bases differ.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8N01
