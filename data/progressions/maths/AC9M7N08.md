# AC9M7N08 — Ratios

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

recognise, represent and solve problems involving ratios

## Prior Knowledge

Students can find fractions and percentages of quantities (from AC9M6N07). Students have experience with part-whole relationships and multiplicative thinking (from AC9M6N08, AC9M6N09).

## Progression Endpoint

Students recognise and represent ratios, distinguish part-part from part-whole relationships, share quantities in given ratios, and apply ratios to practical problems.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Ratio as a relationship between quantities ★

Students recognise that a ratio expresses a multiplicative relationship between two quantities and can represent ratios using diagrams and notation.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students frequently confuse part-part and part-whole ratio interpretations. Multiple contexts where both interpretations are valid (e.g. 2 red, 3 blue beads: 2:3 part-part; 2:5 red-to-total) are needed to resolve this.

**Minimum width:** Represent a given ratio using diagrams (e.g. bar model) and write it in ratio notation a:b.

**Typical width:** Distinguish part-part and part-whole interpretations of the same situation; represent both and explain the difference.

**Wider:**
- Investigate equivalent ratios (simplifying and scaling) and connect to equivalent fractions.
- Explore ratios in design contexts: aspect ratios of screens, mixing colours, recipe scaling.

**Success criteria:**
- Writes a given ratio correctly in a:b notation.
- Distinguishes between part-part and part-whole interpretations of a ratio.
- Represents a ratio accurately using a diagram.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N04
- AC9M7M06

#### 2. Equivalent ratios and simplification ⚑

Students find equivalent ratios by scaling up and down, and express a ratio in simplest form.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who cannot generate equivalent ratios and simplify cannot execute the sharing algorithm in Node 3 — finding the value of one part requires the ratio to be in a form they can work with.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Equivalent ratios underpin sharing and all ratio problem types. Students need fluency before applying ratios in multi-step problems.

**Minimum width:** Generate two equivalent ratios for a given ratio; simplify a ratio to its simplest form.

**Typical width:** Use equivalent ratios to solve scaling problems (e.g. scale up a recipe); verify equivalence using unit rates.

**Wider:**
- Connect equivalent ratios to the concept of a unit rate (1:n form) and use unit rates for comparison.
- Investigate how ratios are used in maps and scale drawings.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly simplifies a ratio to lowest terms.
- Generates equivalent ratios by multiplying or dividing both parts by the same value.
- Does not confuse simplifying a ratio with subtracting the same value from both parts.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02

#### 3. Sharing in a given ratio ★

Students share a quantity in a given ratio and solve related problems.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Sharing in ratio requires a systematic approach (find total parts, find value of one part, multiply). Students who try to proceed intuitively make errors with non-obvious ratios. An explicit scaffold is necessary.

**Minimum width:** Divide a given quantity in a given two-part ratio (e.g. share $60 in ratio 2:3).

**Typical width:** Solve sharing problems in two- and three-part ratios; work backwards from a share to find the total.

**Wider:**
- Investigate profit-sharing problems involving three or more partners in different ratios.
- Apply sharing in ratio to real proportional contexts: mixing paint, sharing resources on Country.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly shares a quantity in a two-part ratio.
- Identifies the total number of parts in the ratio.
- Can work backwards from a share to find the total quantity.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N06
- AC9M7M06
- AC9M7N09
