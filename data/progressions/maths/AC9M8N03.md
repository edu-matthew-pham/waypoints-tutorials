# AC9M8N03 — Terminating and recurring decimals

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

recognise terminating and recurring decimals, using digital tools as appropriate

## Prior Knowledge

Students can convert fractions to decimals and understand that some fractions produce repeating decimals (from AC9M7N04). Students understand prime factorisation (from AC9M7N02).

## Progression Endpoint

Students recognise terminating and recurring decimals and identify from the prime factorisation of the denominator whether a fraction produces a terminating decimal.

## Waypoint Sequence (2 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Identifying terminating and recurring decimals ⚑

Students distinguish terminating from recurring decimals and use correct notation for recurring decimals.

**⚑ Hinge:** Node 2 requires students to predict termination from prime factorisation — students who cannot reliably classify a decimal as terminating or recurring by inspection cannot verify whether their prime factorisation prediction is correct.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students often believe all fractions terminate or that recurring means 'goes on forever randomly'. Multiple examples across different denominators resolve these misconceptions.

**Minimum width:** Classify given fractions as terminating or recurring by division; write recurring decimals using dot or bar notation.

**Typical width:** Identify terminating, recurring and non-terminating (irrational) decimals; use digital tools to explore the decimal expansion of fractions; apply correct notation.

**Wider:**
- Investigate the length of the repeating cycle for unit fractions (1/7, 1/11, 1/13) and look for patterns — connect to divisibility.
- Explore why 0.999... = 1 using the recurring decimal as an argument — discuss what this tells us about real number representation.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly classifies a fraction's decimal expansion as terminating or recurring.
- Writes a recurring decimal using correct notation.
- Does not confuse recurring with irrational (non-terminating, non-recurring).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M8N01
- AC9M7N04

#### 2. Predicting termination from prime factorisation of denominator ★

Students identify whether a fraction terminates by examining whether the prime factorisation of the denominator contains only factors of 2 and/or 5.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: The connection between prime factorisation and decimal behaviour requires an explicit logical chain that students rarely construct spontaneously. A scaffold linking the denominator structure to the decimal base is needed.

**Minimum width:** State the rule and apply it to determine whether a given fraction terminates, without performing the division.

**Typical width:** Apply the rule across a range of fractions including those in non-simplest form; explain why denominators with only 2s and 5s produce terminating decimals.

**Wider:**
- Investigate why only factors of 2 and 5 produce terminating decimals in base 10 — connect to the structure of our number system and how this would differ in base 12.
- Explore mixed examples where simplification first is required before applying the rule.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly predicts whether a fraction terminates from its denominator's prime factorisation.
- Explains why the rule works (connection to base 10).
- Simplifies fractions first where necessary before applying the rule.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N02
- AC9M8N01
