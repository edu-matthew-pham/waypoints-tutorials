# AC9M7N09 — Mathematical modelling with rational numbers

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Number

## ACARA Descriptor

use mathematical modelling to solve practical problems, involving rational numbers and percentages, including financial contexts; formulate problems, choosing representations and efficient calculation strategies, using digital tools as appropriate; interpret and communicate solutions in terms of the situation, justifying choices made about the representation

## Prior Knowledge

Students can apply the four operations to rational numbers, work with percentages, and solve ratio problems (from AC9M7N06, AC9M7N08). Students have experience with mathematical modelling in prior years (from AC9M6N09).

## Progression Endpoint

Students formulate, solve and interpret mathematical models involving rational numbers and percentages in practical contexts, justifying representation choices and communicating solutions in terms of the situation.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 0 hinges)

#### 1. Formulating a model from a practical problem

Students identify the mathematical structure of a practical problem and choose an appropriate representation (fraction, decimal, percentage, or ratio) to model it.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Formulating a model is the step students most commonly skip, jumping to calculation. An explicit scaffold — identify known, unknown, relationship — is needed before students apply it spontaneously.

**Minimum width:** Given a practical problem, identify what operation and representation are needed and set up the calculation.

**Typical width:** Identify when a problem requires a model rather than direct calculation; articulate assumptions made in setting up the model.

**Wider:**
- Compare how different representations model the same situation (e.g. profit as percentage increase vs dollar value) and discuss what each makes visible.
- Investigate how simplifying assumptions affect the model's accuracy.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly identifies the operation and representation needed for a given practical problem.
- States any assumptions made in setting up the model.
- Does not start calculating before identifying the model structure.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N06
- AC9M7N08

#### 2. Solving and interpreting in context ★

Students solve a mathematical model and interpret the result in terms of the original situation, checking reasonableness.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Interpretation in context is a distinct skill from calculation. Students who can compute correctly frequently give answers without units, in wrong form, or without checking reasonableness. Varied practical contexts build the habit of contextual interpretation.

**Minimum width:** Solve a modelled problem correctly and express the answer in contextually appropriate terms.

**Typical width:** Solve multi-step modelling problems in financial and proportional contexts; use digital tools where appropriate; verify reasonableness with estimation.

**Wider:**
- Investigate financial modelling: simple budget scenarios involving income, expenses, profit and loss with percentage components.
- Model proportion problems using First Nations Australians' contexts (land area proportions, resource allocation).

**Success criteria:**
- Expresses the solution in contextually appropriate terms (correct units, reasonable precision).
- Checks the reasonableness of the solution using estimation.
- Does not present a raw number without contextual interpretation.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N05
- AC9M7ST03

#### 3. Justifying representation choices ★

Students justify why they chose a particular representation and calculation strategy, and communicate this reasoning clearly.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Justification draws on all prior number work simultaneously — representation choices, operation strategies, contextual meaning. The integration and articulation of these is the learning at this node.

**Minimum width:** Explain in words why they chose a particular representation (fraction, decimal, percentage) for a given problem.

**Typical width:** Compare two possible representations for the same problem, evaluate which is more efficient, and justify the choice.

**Wider:**
- Critique a given solution that uses an inefficient or inappropriate representation — identify the problem and rewrite.
- Investigate: when would a percentage model be misleading? (e.g. 50% increase then 50% decrease ≠ original value).

**Success criteria:**
- Justifies representation choices with reference to the problem context and computation efficiency.
- Communicates the solution process, not just the answer.
- Can identify a more efficient representation when presented with an inefficient one.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7N06
- AC9M7M06
