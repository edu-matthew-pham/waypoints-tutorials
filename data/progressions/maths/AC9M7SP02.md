# AC9M7SP02 — Classifying triangles and polygons

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Space

## ACARA Descriptor

classify triangles, quadrilaterals and other polygons according to their side and angle properties; identify and reason about relationships

## Prior Knowledge

Students have informal familiarity with shape names and basic properties. Students can measure angles and sides.

## Progression Endpoint

Students classify triangles and quadrilaterals using side and angle properties and reason about hierarchical relationships between shape families.

## Waypoint Sequence (3 waypoints, 1 hinge)

#### 1. Classifying triangles by sides and angles

Students classify triangles as scalene, isosceles or equilateral (by sides) and as acute, right-angled or obtuse (by angles), and understand that these classifications are independent.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Students often treat side classification and angle classification as mutually exclusive. Encountering diverse triangles that require both labels simultaneously resolves this.

**Minimum width:** Classify a given triangle by both its side and angle properties.

**Typical width:** Classify a range of triangles; identify that a triangle can be e.g. isosceles and right-angled simultaneously; investigate which combinations are possible.

**Wider:**
- Construct triangles with given side lengths and determine which combinations are possible (triangle inequality).
- Investigate which combinations of side-type and angle-type are possible vs impossible (e.g. can a triangle be equilateral and obtuse?).

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly classifies a triangle by both side and angle properties.
- Understands that the two classifications are independent.
- Does not assume equilateral means right-angled.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M05

#### 2. Classifying quadrilaterals and their relationships ⚑ ★

Students classify quadrilaterals (square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, kite, trapezium) using side and angle properties, and identify hierarchical relationships.

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who classify by appearance rather than properties cannot design reliable classification algorithms in AC9M7SP04 — the algorithm requires specifying testable property conditions, not visual recognition rules.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Hierarchical classification requires deliberate reasoning about necessary and sufficient conditions. Students who classify by appearance alone fail with non-standard orientations and confuse the hierarchy.

**Minimum width:** Classify a given quadrilateral and state the properties used.

**Typical width:** Describe, compare and contrast all quadrilateral types; explain hierarchical relationships (e.g. all squares are rectangles, not all rectangles are squares).

**Wider:**
- Construct a hierarchy diagram (Venn or tree) of quadrilaterals showing subset relationships.
- Investigate which quadrilateral properties are necessary vs sufficient for classification.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly classifies any standard quadrilateral using its properties.
- States at least two hierarchical relationships between quadrilateral types.
- Does not rely on orientation to classify (e.g. recognises a rotated square).

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7SP04

#### 3. Classifying other polygons and reasoning about properties

Students classify polygons by number of sides and by regularity, and reason about properties such as line symmetry and convexity.

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Classifying polygons draws together side properties, angle sum knowledge, and symmetry reasoning simultaneously.

**Minimum width:** Name and classify polygons up to at least octagons; distinguish regular from irregular and convex from concave.

**Typical width:** Reason about properties of specific polygons: angle sums, number of diagonals, symmetries.

**Wider:**
- Investigate tessellation properties: which regular polygons tessellate alone, and why? Connect to interior angles.
- Explore polygon properties in architecture, design, and cultural patterns.

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly classifies polygons by name, regularity, and convexity.
- Explains what makes a polygon regular.
- Can identify whether a polygon is convex or concave and explain the difference.

**Related standards:**
- AC9M7M05
- AC9M7SP04
