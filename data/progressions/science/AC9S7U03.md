# AC9S7U03 — Earth-sun-moon system

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Understanding

## ACARA Descriptor

model cyclic changes in the relative positions of the Earth, sun and moon and explain how these cycles cause eclipses and influence predictable phenomena on Earth, including seasons and tides

## Prior Knowledge

Students can describe day/night as caused by Earth's rotation and identify moon phases from observation. They have not modelled 3D spatial relationships or explained tides and eclipses mechanistically.

## Progression Endpoint

Students model and explain how cyclic changes in Earth-sun-moon positions cause eclipses, seasons and tides.

## Waypoint Sequence (5 waypoints, 3 hinges)

#### 1. Earth's rotation, revolution and axial tilt ⚑

Students can distinguish Earth's rotation from revolution and explain how axial tilt relates to observable changes across a year

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who cannot distinguish rotation from revolution and build the 3D spatial model cannot explain eclipses or seasons in Nodes 3-4 — both require coordinating Earth's axial tilt with its orbital position simultaneously.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Students commonly confuse rotation and revolution or understand each separately without being able to explain their combined observable effects. Scaffolding the spatial reasoning — what does an observer on Earth experience as a result of each cycle, and how do the two cycles combine — builds the 3D mental model that is prerequisite for eclipse and season explanations in later nodes.

**Minimum width:** Distinguish rotation from revolution; link rotation to day/night and revolution to the year; identify that Earth's axis is tilted

**Typical width:** Use a physical or digital model to demonstrate both cycles simultaneously; explain what an observer on Earth would experience across a year

**Wider:**
- Use physical models or virtual simulations to explain how Earth's tilt and position relative to the sun causes differences in light intensity
- Investigate First Nations Australians' calendars and how they are used to predict seasonal changes
- Explain why the length of daylight changes across a year at different latitudes using a model

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly distinguishes rotation from revolution
- Links rotation to day/night and revolution to the year
- Identifies that Earth's axis is tilted

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I04
- AC9S7H02

#### 2. Moon phases and the lunar cycle ⚑ ★

Students can explain moon phases as a result of the moon's position relative to Earth and sun and sequence them correctly

**⚑ Hinge:** The shadow misconception — phases caused by Earth's shadow — persists directly into eclipse explanation in Node 3. Students who hold it will explain lunar eclipses as moon phases and cannot correctly distinguish the two phenomena.

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Sequencing all 8 moon phases correctly and explaining the position-based cause is a precision task that students commonly get partially right. The common error — confusing phases with Earth's shadow — is persistent and needs targeted correction through repeated modelling practice, not more contexts.

**Minimum width:** Correctly sequence all 8 moon phases; explain that phases result from the moon's position relative to Earth and sun, not Earth's shadow

**Typical width:** Model moon phases using a light source and spheres; predict the phase visible from Earth at a given moon position

**Wider:**
- Analyse a month of real moon phase data — identify the pattern, calculate the period, identify anomalies
- Research knowledges held by First Nations Australians regarding the phases of the moon and the lunar cycle
- Investigate how lunar calendars from different cultures use moon phases differently and why

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly sequences all 8 moon phases
- Explains phases as caused by the moon's position, not Earth's shadow
- Does not confuse moon phases with eclipses

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I03
- AC9S7I04
- AC9S7I05
- AC9S7H02

#### 3. Eclipses: solar and lunar ★

Students can explain the conditions required for solar and lunar eclipses and why eclipses do not occur every month

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Eclipse explanation requires 3D spatial reasoning about three bodies simultaneously — the hardest conceptual step in this standard. Students who rely on 2D diagrams alone consistently misexplain why eclipses are rare. Broader contexts — historical records, cultural accounts, simulation — help students build the 3D mental model that 2D diagrams cannot provide.

**Minimum width:** Explain the conditions required for a solar and lunar eclipse; correctly distinguish umbra from penumbra; explain why eclipses don't occur every month

**Typical width:** Model both eclipse types using a light source; explain the role of the moon's orbital tilt in eclipse frequency

**Wider:**
- Use physical models or virtual simulations to explain cyclic patterns of lunar and solar eclipses
- Research First Nations Australians' oral traditions and cultural recordings of solar and lunar eclipses; compare with contemporary understandings
- Analyse historical eclipse records — explain why ancient cultures found eclipses unpredictable and what understanding was missing

**Success criteria:**
- States the conditions required for both solar and lunar eclipses
- Correctly distinguishes umbra from penumbra
- Explains why eclipses don't occur every month

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I01
- AC9S7I04
- AC9S7H01
- AC9S7H02

#### 4. Seasons ⚑ ★

Students can explain seasons as caused by axial tilt and correctly relate tilt to solar angle and day length at different latitudes

**⚑ Hinge:** The distance misconception — seasons caused by proximity to the sun — is explicitly noted as assessed and extremely persistent. Students who hold it produce confidently wrong season explanations and cannot correctly account for opposite hemispheric seasons.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The distance misconception — seasons caused by proximity to the sun — is extremely persistent and directly assessed. Without resolving it here, students produce confidently wrong answers. High width investment across multiple latitude contexts (equator, temperate, polar) and cultural calendar comparisons forces students to confront the misconception repeatedly until the tilt explanation displaces it.

**Minimum width:** Explain that seasons are caused by Earth's axial tilt, not distance from the sun; correctly link tilt to solar angle and day length

**Typical width:** Use a model to demonstrate why the Southern and Northern hemispheres experience opposite seasons simultaneously

**Wider:**
- Compare seasonal patterns at the equator, temperate zones, and poles — explain differences using the tilt model
- Investigate First Nations Australians' seasonal calendars (e.g. six-season calendars) and compare with the four-season European model
- Analyse real insolation data for two locations at different latitudes across a year; explain the patterns using the tilt model

**Success criteria:**
- Explains seasons as caused by axial tilt, not distance from the sun
- Links tilt to solar angle and day length
- Correctly identifies opposite seasons in Northern and Southern hemispheres

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I04
- AC9S7I06
- AC9S7H02

#### 5. Tides

Students can explain tides as caused by the moon's gravitational pull and distinguish spring from neap tides by moon position

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: Tides integrate the lunar cycle (Node 2), the Earth-moon-sun positional model (Node 3), and gravitational force concepts from AC9S7U04 into a single explanatory account. Students who understand each component separately but cannot coordinate them into a tidal explanation have not reached this node's checkpoint. Analysing tide charts requires all three components to work together.

**Minimum width:** Explain that tides are caused by the moon's gravitational pull; identify spring and neap tides and the moon positions that cause them

**Typical width:** Analyse a tide chart for a coastal location — identify spring and neap cycles and connect them to the lunar calendar

**Wider:**
- Examine the gravitational attraction of the moon and sun on Earth's oceans; explain how relative positions result in tidal variations
- Research how First Nations Australians used tidal patterns and connection between lunar cycle and ocean tides for resource management
- Investigate how tidal patterns differ between locations and explain using the moon-sun-Earth model

**Success criteria:**
- Explains tides as caused by the moon's gravitational pull
- Correctly identifies spring and neap tides
- Links spring and neap tides to moon positions

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I05
- AC9S7I07
- AC9S7H02
- AC9S7H04
