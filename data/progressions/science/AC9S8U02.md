# AC9S8U02 — Structure, function and organ systems

**Year 8**
**Strand:** Biological Sciences

## ACARA Descriptor

analyse the relationship between structure and function of cells, tissues and organs in a plant and an animal organ system and explain how these systems enable survival of the individual

## Prior Knowledge

Students can describe organelle functions and cell specialisation (AC9S8U01) and explain how structural features enable survival at the organism level (AC9S5U01). They have not yet encountered the tissue or organ as organisational levels, or analysed how structure at sub-organism levels enables system-level survival functions.

## Progression Endpoint

Students analyse structure-function relationships across the cell → tissue → organ → organ system hierarchy in one plant and one animal system, explaining how integrated system function enables individual survival.

## Waypoint Sequence (4 waypoints, 2 hinges)

#### 1. Cell specialisation — same DNA, different structure and function ⚑

Students can explain how cells with the same genetic information become specialised for different functions, and describe structural features of two or more specialised cell types.

**⚑ Hinge:** Cell specialisation is the conceptual mechanism that explains tissue formation. Students who cannot explain why cells differ structurally cannot understand why similar cells group into tissues in N2, and cannot analyse organ structure in N3 in terms of the specialised cells that compose it.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: The structure-function reasoning method must be practised at the cell level before it is applied at the tissue and organ level. Students who learn specialised cell features as a list without the reasoning framework ('this feature enables this function') cannot transfer the analysis to novel cell types or to tissues.

**Minimum width:** Students describe two structural features of one specialised cell type and explain how each feature serves the cell's function.

**Typical width:** Students analyse: red blood cells (biconcave, no nucleus — maximises haemoglobin, flexible for capillaries); xylem cells (hollow, thick walls — water transport); nerve cells (long axon, many dendrites — signal transmission over distance); muscle cells (elongated, many mitochondria — contraction); explain that specialisation means organelle complement and shape are matched to function; connect to N5 of AC9S8U01.

**Wider:**
- Compare the organelle complement of a secretory cell (many ribosomes, large Golgi) to a muscle cell (many mitochondria) — explain each difference using function
- Investigate how an artificial organ mimics real cell specialisation — what cell-level properties does the artificial version replicate or approximate?

**Success criteria:**
- Describes at least two structural features of a specialised cell and explains how each serves the cell's function
- Explains that cells with the same DNA can be specialised for different functions
- Connects organelle complement to cell specialisation for at least one cell type

**Related standards:**
- AC9S8U01
- AC9S5U01

#### 2. Tissues — groups of specialised cells performing a shared function

Students can define a tissue as a group of similar specialised cells performing a shared function, and give examples from plant and animal systems.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: The tissue concept extends specialisation to the collective level — similar cells acting together. Students need to understand that tissue function emerges from cell arrangement and cooperation, not just from individual cell properties. This is the building block for organ composition in N3.

**Minimum width:** Students define tissue and give one plant and one animal tissue example with the shared function of each.

**Typical width:** Students describe: muscle tissue (muscle cells — contraction); epithelial tissue (epithelial cells — lining/protection); xylem tissue (xylem cells — water transport); phloem tissue (sieve tube cells — sugar transport); explain that tissues perform functions individual cells cannot perform at scale; explain that tissue identity is defined by cell type and function, not just location.

**Wider:**
- Examine prepared slides of muscle, epithelial and plant vascular tissue — describe how the arrangement of cells in each tissue reflects its function
- Research how a tissue disorder (e.g. fibrosis — abnormal connective tissue) affects the function of the organ it composes

**Success criteria:**
- Defines a tissue as a group of similar specialised cells performing a shared function
- Names at least one animal and one plant tissue with the function of each
- Explains how tissue function emerges from the collective action of its component cells

**Related standards:**
- AC9S8U01

#### 3. Organs — tissues working together for an integrated function ⚑

Students can describe an organ as a structure composed of multiple tissue types, each contributing to the organ's overall function.

**⚑ Hinge:** The organ-as-multi-tissue-structure concept is the prerequisite for organ system analysis. Students who think of organs as single-tissue structures cannot explain why organ function depends on the coordination of component tissues, and cannot analyse how organ failure propagates from tissue disorder.

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Students commonly think of organs as single things rather than integrated multi-tissue structures. Making the tissue composition of organs explicit — and explaining why multiple tissue types are required — is the conceptual move that connects tissue-level analysis to organ-level function.

**Minimum width:** Students name two tissue types in one organ and explain how each contributes to the organ's function.

**Typical width:** Students analyse: the heart (cardiac muscle tissue, epithelial lining, connective tissue — contraction, smooth flow, structural integrity); a leaf (epidermis, mesophyll, vascular tissue — gas exchange, photosynthesis, water/sugar transport); explain that organs require multiple tissue types because no single tissue can perform all the functions an organ needs; compare 2D and 3D representations of organs to understand spatial relationships.

**Wider:**
- Compare 2D and 3D representations of the same organ — what spatial relationships are visible in 3D that flat diagrams cannot show?
- Investigate how a disorder at the tissue level affects organ function — e.g. how atherosclerosis (connective tissue thickening in arteries) impairs the circulatory organ's function

**Success criteria:**
- Names at least two tissue types in a named organ and explains the contribution of each
- Explains why organs require multiple tissue types
- Analyses how a tissue-level change affects organ function

**Related standards:**
- AC9S8U01

#### 4. Organ systems — coordinated organs enabling survival ★

Students can describe how organs within a system coordinate to perform a survival function, applying this to one animal and one plant system.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Applying the organ system analysis to both a plant and an animal system — and comparing them as analogous solutions — builds the generalised structure-function analytical capability the descriptor's 'analyse' demand requires.

**Minimum width:** Students describe the organs of one system and explain how the system as a whole enables a named survival function.

**Typical width:** Students analyse one animal system (circulatory — heart, blood vessels, blood; or digestive — mouth, stomach, intestines, liver) and one plant system (vascular — xylem, phloem, roots, leaves); explain how each organ's structure contributes to the system's survival function; explain that the system fails if any organ fails; connect system function to individual survival (oxygen delivery, nutrient transport, water uptake).

**Wider:**
- Compare the vascular system of a plant to the circulatory system of an animal as analogous solutions to the same survival problem — what structural differences reflect different organism constraints?
- Investigate how an artificial organ (dialysis machine, pacemaker) mimics or augments the function of a real organ — what structural principles does it replicate?

**Success criteria:**
- Names the organs of one animal and one plant system and explains the function of each
- Explains how the coordinated system enables a specific survival function
- Compares one plant and one animal system as solutions to a shared survival problem

**Related standards:**
- AC9S8U01
- AC9S5U01
