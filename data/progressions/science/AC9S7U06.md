# AC9S7U06 — Pure substances and mixtures

**Year 7**
**Strand:** Understanding

## ACARA Descriptor

use a particle model to describe differences between pure substances and mixtures and apply understanding of properties of substances to separate mixtures

## Prior Knowledge

Students can classify materials as solids, liquids or gases and have an introductory particle model from AC9S7U05. They have not formally distinguished pure substances from mixtures at the particle level or applied separation techniques systematically.

## Progression Endpoint

Students use a particle model to describe the difference between pure substances and mixtures, and select and apply appropriate separation techniques based on properties of substances.

## Waypoint Sequence (5 waypoints, 2 hinges)

#### 1. Pure substances vs mixtures at the particle level ⚑

Students can distinguish pure substances from mixtures using particle diagrams and classify familiar materials correctly

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who cannot distinguish pure substances from mixtures at the particle level cannot select appropriate separation techniques in Nodes 3-5 — technique selection requires knowing what type of mixture is present.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: The pure/mixture distinction at the particle level is prerequisite for all separation technique selection. Students who cannot make this distinction select inappropriate techniques and cannot explain their choices. Seeing the distinction across multiple material types — solutions, alloys, everyday materials — forces students to apply the particle-level definition rather than relying on visual appearance.

**Minimum width:** Distinguish pure substance from mixture using particle diagrams; classify 5 familiar materials correctly with justification

**Typical width:** Draw particle diagrams for a pure substance, a homogeneous mixture and a heterogeneous mixture; explain the key differences

**Wider:**
- Use representations of particles to show the difference between samples of pure substances and mixtures; identify examples of each in everyday contexts
- Examine different solutions and identify the solvent and solute; represent at the particle level
- Investigate a borderline case (e.g. alloy, saltwater at different concentrations) — is it pure or a mixture? What does this reveal about the definition?

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly distinguishes pure substance from mixture using particle diagrams
- Classifies 5 familiar materials correctly with justification
- Does not confuse homogeneous mixtures (e.g. solutions) with pure substances

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I04

#### 2. Properties as the basis for separation ⚑

Students can explain that separation techniques exploit differences in physical properties and match property differences to techniques

**⚑ Hinge:** Students who cannot map property differences to techniques apply techniques by rote and cannot justify choices or adapt when scenarios change. This mapping is the conceptual engine for all technique selection nodes.

**Response modes:**
- ↔ Widen contexts: Understanding that techniques exploit property differences (not just 'removing things') is the conceptual key. Without this, students apply techniques by rote and cannot explain or adapt when scenarios change. Seeing the property-technique connection across multiple real-world contexts — home, industry, water purification — forces students to apply the principle rather than memorise a technique-mixture pairing.

**Minimum width:** State that separation techniques exploit differences in physical properties; match at least three property differences to a separation technique

**Typical width:** Analyse a mixture — identify relevant property differences and justify which technique is most appropriate, including why others would not work

**Wider:**
- Analyse how physical properties of substances in mixtures (particle size, density, volatility) determine the separation technique used
- Explore and compare separation methods used in a variety of situations such as in the home, recycling industries and water purification
- Evaluate a separation scenario with multiple possible techniques — rank by efficiency, cost and practicality

**Success criteria:**
- States that separation exploits differences in physical properties
- Correctly matches at least 3 property differences to separation techniques
- Explains why a chosen technique would work for a given mixture

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I01
- AC9S7I05

#### 3. Filtration and evaporation ★

Students can perform or describe filtration and evaporation and justify their use for appropriate mixtures

**Response modes:**
- ◆ Practise here: Performing or describing filtration and evaporation correctly requires procedural precision — students commonly describe steps in the wrong order, omit key steps, or confuse the two techniques. Practice with feedback on procedural accuracy is what helps, not more separation technique contexts.

**Minimum width:** Correctly perform or describe filtration and evaporation; identify a mixture each technique is appropriate for and explain why using property differences

**Typical width:** Design a procedure to separate a given mixture using filtration and/or evaporation; predict and explain the result at the particle level

**Wider:**
- Investigate and use filtration and evaporation in a range of practical contexts; evaluate effectiveness
- Investigate separation techniques used by First Nations Australians such as hand-picking, sieving, winnowing and filtering — explain the property differences each exploits
- Investigate the effect of a variable (e.g. filter paper grade, temperature) on separation efficiency; analyse results using particle theory

**Success criteria:**
- Correctly describes or performs filtration and evaporation
- Identifies a mixture appropriate for each technique with property reasoning
- Explains why the chosen technique works using property differences

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I02
- AC9S7I03
- AC9S7I06
- AC9S7H02

#### 4. Distillation and chromatography ★

Students can describe the principles of distillation and chromatography and identify mixtures each technique is appropriate for

**Response modes:**
- ◎ Scaffold reasoning: Students commonly confuse distillation with evaporation — both involve heat and phase change — without reasoning through the key difference: distillation collects the vapour, evaporation does not. Scaffolding the comparison explicitly (same property exploited, different outcome sought, different equipment needed) is what resolves the confusion.

**Minimum width:** Describe the principle of distillation and chromatography; identify a mixture each is appropriate for and explain why

**Typical width:** Compare distillation and evaporation — explain when each is appropriate and why, using particle theory and property differences

**Wider:**
- Investigate crystallisation, chromatography and distillation; explain the property differences each exploits
- Investigate separation techniques used by First Nations Australians such as yandying, cold-pressing and steam distilling — connect to property principles
- Analyse a chromatography result — calculate Rf values, identify components, evaluate what the result reveals about mixture composition

**Success criteria:**
- Describes the principle of distillation and chromatography
- Identifies a mixture appropriate for each technique
- Distinguishes when distillation is preferred over evaporation

**Related standards:**
- AC9S7I02
- AC9S7I03
- AC9S7I04
- AC9S7I05
- AC9S7H02

#### 5. Selecting and justifying separation techniques ★

Students can select and justify an appropriate separation technique for an unfamiliar mixture using property differences

**Response modes:**
- ⊕ Integration point: This node integrates pure/mixture distinction (Node 1), property-technique mapping (Node 2), and procedural knowledge of all four techniques (Nodes 3-4) into a coordinated selection and justification task for unfamiliar mixtures. Students who can handle each technique separately but cannot select and justify for a novel mixture have not integrated the property-based framework.

**Minimum width:** Given an unfamiliar mixture, select an appropriate technique and justify the choice using property differences

**Typical width:** Design a multi-step separation procedure for a mixture with three components; justify each step and predict the outcome

**Wider:**
- Evaluate a real-world separation process (e.g. water purification, mining, food processing) — identify techniques used and property differences exploited
- Compare industrial and First Nations separation techniques for the same type of mixture — analyse efficiency, sustainability and cultural significance
- Design and evaluate an original separation procedure for a novel mixture; assess against criteria of efficiency, cost and practicality

**Success criteria:**
- Selects an appropriate technique for an unfamiliar mixture
- Justifies the choice using property differences
- Identifies at least one technique that would not work and explains why
