Our shared example is [Renewable, non-renewable or continuous?](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/geography/water-7/resource-types/), the first waypoint in **Water as a resource**, AC9HG7K01. Keep this guide open and try the example in another tab. The classroom situation below is made up.

## Think first: what did the answer reveal?

```step
stage: predict
insight: "A wrong classification can point to an underlying rule a student is using."
check:
  type: predict
  q: "A student puts sunlight and timber in the renewable group because both are “there tomorrow”. What would you focus on next?"
  options: ["How the student decided to group the resources", "Whether the final labels match the answer key", "How many examples the student has completed"]
  actual: "Ask how the student chose the group. They may be treating renewable and continuous as the same idea, but you need to hear their explanation to find out."
```

## Try: the real lesson

```step
stage: info
insight: "The live example connects a prediction to reasoning, feedback and a smaller step."
check:
  type: reflect
  text: "I tried the prediction, one wrong answer and its feedback, then Build up to it and the original Nib task in the real lesson."
```

In the [live resource-types lesson](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/geography/water-7/resource-types/):

1. Choose a prediction and press **Lock in my prediction**. Read the explanation under **Try** and compare it with your choice.
2. In **Worked example: two ways of coming back**, choose **Fish are a continuous resource** for the fishing question, then **Check answer**. Read the feedback before correcting it.
3. Open **2 · Find Nib's mistake**. Nib treats renewable and continuous as the same because both “come back”. Look at the reason Nib gives for putting them in the same group.
4. Open **Build up to it**. Answer the question about which resource people can use up, then return to Nib's explanation and find the faulty line.
5. Set **Width** to **Minimum**, then **Typical** to try **Typical: more resources**. The extra examples ask you to use the same idea. **Wider** adds a discussion you could have with your class. Choose which examples to use and how long to spend.

Tick the box when you have tried those steps. It is a reminder for you; this guide cannot see what you did in the other tab.

## The idea: what feedback adds

```step
stage: investigate
insight: "Feedback can explain a difference the student has missed."
check:
  type: question
  q: "After a student chooses “Fish are a continuous resource”, what makes the feedback useful?"
  options:
    - "It explains why fish and sunlight belong in different groups."
    - "It identifies the student’s exact misunderstanding without a follow-up question."
    - "It lets the student skip the original task once the smaller question is right."
    - "It confirms understanding as soon as the student switches to the right answer."
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - "The result tells you which answer they chose. Ask how they arrived at it."
    - "A smaller question helps the student return to the original task."
    - "Ask the student to explain the difference or try another example to see whether it now makes sense."
  explain: "The feedback explains why fish and sunlight belong in different groups. Ask the student to explain that difference in their own words."
hint:
  - "Read the feedback again. What does it explain that a “wrong” label would leave out?"
```

## Check: support, minimum or width?

```step
stage: practise
insight: "A smaller step helps with the original task. Width adds examples of the same idea."
check:
  type: match
  q: "Match what you saw to its purpose."
  pairs:
    - ["Build up to it", "A smaller question, then back to the original task"]
    - ["The main lesson at Minimum", "The understanding everyone is working towards"]
    - ["Typical: more resources", "More examples of the same idea"]
  explain: "Minimum covers the main understanding. Typical and Wider offer extra examples. You decide who would benefit from them and how long to spend."
```

## Apply: ask one follow-up

```step
stage: make
insight: "A teacher checks whether the learner can now use the distinction."
check:
  type: reflect
  text: "I wrote or discussed one follow-up question that would show whether a student now distinguishes renewable from continuous."
```

For example, ask the student to name another resource and explain which group it belongs in. Discuss what their explanation tells you. The site does not mark this response.
