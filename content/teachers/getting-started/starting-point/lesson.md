Learning Waypoints helps teachers unpack curriculum into connected conceptual steps, then provides tools to plan, teach and check understanding.

A **waypoint** is an important understanding on the way to a broader curriculum expectation. It connects an idea to prerequisites, possible misconceptions and evidence that students have grasped it. The progression is a professional interpretation grounded in ACARA descriptors; ACARA does not prescribe every waypoint or teaching method.

This complements familiar Learning Intentions and Success Criteria by making conceptual dependencies and teaching supports visible. Your unit and your judgement remain central.

## Think first: tomorrow's lesson

```step
stage: predict
insight: "The starting point depends on the teacher's immediate need."
check:
  type: predict
  q: "In this invented situation, you already know the concept and need slides for tomorrow. Where would you look first?"
  options: ["Unpack", "Prepare", "Tutorials"]
  actual: "Prepare is the direct starting point for teaching resources. Unpack could help revisit the concept, and Tutorials could supply a useful checkpoint; neither is a required first step."
```

## The idea: three ways in

```step
stage: investigate
insight: "The three tools serve different needs and can be used independently."
```

- [**Curriculum: Unpack**](https://acara.learningwaypoints.com/): explore curriculum expectations and the conceptual steps and prerequisites behind them.
- [**Planning and preparation: Prepare**](https://prepare.learningwaypoints.com/): work with slide decks and printable teaching resources.
- [**Tutorials**](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/geography/water-7/resource-types/): try activities and checks that surface understanding, individually or together.

For the opening scenario, Prepare directly serves the need for slides. Unpack could help revisit the concept, and Tutorials could add a checkpoint.

Open each tool. You may move between them, but there is no compulsory order. The Geography example is a preview, not yet tried with classes.

## Check: match the need

```step
stage: practise
insight: "Match an immediate need to what the tool actually offers."
check:
  type: match
  q: "These teacher situations are invented. Match each to the most direct starting point."
  pairs:
    - ["Clarify the prerequisite behind a Year 7 Geography idea", "Unpack"]
    - ["Prepare slides for tomorrow's lesson", "Prepare"]
    - ["Try a check about confusing renewable and continuous resources", "Tutorials"]
  explain: "Unpack makes the curriculum progression visible; Prepare works with teaching resources; Tutorials offers activities and feedback. The best first choice depends on the need."
hint:
  - "Ask whether the teacher needs to understand the progression, prepare a resource, or try a check."
```

## Check: is there a required order?

```step
stage: practise
insight: "A tool choice is not a mandated teaching sequence."
check:
  type: question
  q: "A colleague already has a unit plan and wants one misconception check. Which advice fits?"
  options:
    - "Use the relevant Tutorials check alongside the unit."
    - "Complete Unpack and Prepare before opening Tutorials."
    - "Use Unpack because its curriculum map is a ready-made slide deck."
    - "Use Tutorials only for homework; classroom teaching needs another tool."
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - "The tools are independent entry points, not three required stages."
    - "A curriculum progression helps unpack an idea; Prepare is the resource tool."
    - "A tutorial check can also support a whole-class prediction or discussion."
  explain: "Choose the tool that serves the teaching move you need now."
```

## Apply: your next starting point

```step
stage: make
insight: "Teachers choose a starting point and can explain when another tool might help."
check:
  type: reflect
  text: "I chose one tool for a lesson I am planning and explained, in my own notes or to a colleague, when a second tool might also help."
```

Choose one tool for a lesson you are planning. Explain in your own notes or to a colleague when a second tool might help. No response is sent or automatically assessed. Next, try a real conceptual check.
