You choose the concept and teaching move. Tutorials can support discussion, investigation and writing; teachers still select sources, interpret reasoning, guide inquiry and assess learning.

## Think first: a useful checkpoint

```step
stage: predict
insight: "A teaching move should respond to evidence of need."
check:
  type: predict
  q: "In this invented situation, students have discussed water resources, but you are unsure whether they distinguish renewable and continuous. What would you try next?"
  options: ["One check followed by a discussion of reasons", "Restart the entire unit in Tutorials", "Assign wider work as a speed group"]
  actual: "One check can give you evidence to discuss. A full restart is not required; width adds contexts rather than pace groups."
```

## Check: choose a teaching move

```step
stage: practise
insight: "Whole-class, checkpoint and catch-up uses address different evidence of need."
check:
  type: match
  q: "These classroom situations are invented. Match each to a suitable use."
  pairs:
    - ["You want competing initial explanations from the whole class", "Project a prediction, then discuss reasons together"]
    - ["After discussion, you need evidence about one distinction", "Use one relevant check and ask why"]
    - ["One student needs a prerequisite while the class applies the idea", "Offer a catch-up or smaller step, then return to the main task"]
  explain: "These are choices alongside your unit. None requires teaching the whole course or replacing the investigation."
```

## The idea: keep the teacher decisions

```step
stage: investigate
insight: "The site checks structured answers; teachers retain inquiry and assessment decisions."
check:
  type: question
  q: "Which plan uses the tutorial appropriately?"
  options:
    - "Use a classification check, discuss the reasons, then continue the class investigation."
    - "Replace the whole inquiry because the site marks extended reasoning."
    - "Give optional width only to the quickest finishers as their permanent track."
    - "Treat a correct click as enough evidence for every curriculum expectation."
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - "The site does not automatically assess extended reasoning. The full investigation stays with the class."
    - "Width offers additional contexts on the same concept, not a permanent ability or pace track."
    - "A structured answer is one piece of evidence. Discussion, application and writing still matter."
  explain: "Use the resource where it adds useful evidence, then make the next teaching decision yourself."
```

## Apply: choose one thing to try

```step
stage: make
insight: "A small pilot fits a specific concept and moment in an existing unit."
check:
  type: reflect
  text: "In my own planning I chose one progression, planning resource or strategy, or tutorial check, when I would use it in an existing unit, and what it would help me understand or do."
```

Choose one progression, planning resource or strategy, or tutorial check. Name where it fits your existing unit and what you would learn from trying it. Write in your own notes or discuss with a colleague. No student information is needed. For subject starting points and the three reflection prompts, open the [Humanities pilot companion](https://tutorials.learningwaypoints.com/humanities-pilot/).
