title: Levers and pulleys in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

A **lever** is a bar that turns on a **pivot**. A force's **turning effect** (its moment) is the force × its distance **from the pivot**. A lever balances when the turning effects on both sides are equal: a 600 N rock 0.5 m from the pivot balances a 200 N push 1.5 m away.

Pushing far from the pivot makes the push stronger, by the same factor that the hand moves farther: 3 times the force, 3 times the distance. There are three kinds: the pivot in the middle (class 1: crowbar, scissors), the load in the middle (class 2: wheelbarrow, nutcracker) and the push in the middle (class 3: tweezers, your forearm). Class 3 always needs more force than the load, and gains speed and reach.

A single **fixed pulley** changes the direction of a pull, not its size (like an idler gear). Ropes that hold up a **moving** block share the load: with 4 of them you pull a quarter of the load, and pull 4 times as much rope.

Gears, levers and pulleys all make the same trade: a bigger force for a longer distance (or a lower speed). With no friction, **work in = work out** (force × distance).

## What students get wrong

- **Distances from the end of the bar.** Every distance starts at the pivot.
- **A lever (or pulley) gives force for free.** Less force always means more distance.
- **One pulley halves the pull.** It changes only the direction.
- **Counting the rope you pull.** Count only the ropes holding the moving block.

## The words

**lever**, **pivot (fulcrum)**, **load**, **effort (push or pull)**, **turning effect (moment)**, **mechanical advantage**, **fixed pulley**, **block and tackle**, **work**.
