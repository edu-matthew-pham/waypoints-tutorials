## The idea in plain words

Motors turn. Machines often need something else: back and forth, up and down at set times, or a swing. Three mechanisms change one motion into another.

A **crank and slider**: a pin on a turning wheel, a rod, and a slider on a track. One turn is one stroke out and back; the **stroke is twice the crank's radius**. Pumps use it one way, engines the other.

A **cam and follower**: a shaped wheel and a rod resting on it. The cam's **shape decides the follower's path**, shown on a **displacement graph** (height against angle). Eccentric: smooth wave, lift = 2 × offset. Pear: rests half a turn. Snail: slow rise, sudden drop (one way only). Heart: steady rise and fall.

A **four-bar linkage**: a frame, a crank, a coupler and a rocker. The crank turns all the way only if **shortest + longest ≤ the other two** (Grashof's rule) and the crank is the shortest. A longer crank gives a bigger swing, up to that limit.

## What students get wrong

- **The stroke equals the radius.** It is twice the radius.
- **Reading a displacement graph as a picture of the cam.** It is height against angle.
- **Adding the wrong two bars.** Sort first; add the shortest and the longest.
- **A longer crank always swings farther.** Only until it jams.

## The words

**crank**, **connecting rod**, **slider**, **stroke**, **cam**, **follower**, **dwell**, **displacement graph**, **linkage**, **coupler**, **rocker**, **frame**.
