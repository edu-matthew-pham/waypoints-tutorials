title: Frames and trusses in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

A **truss** is a frame of straight members joined by **pins**. Each member only **pulls** (tension) or **pushes** (compression) along its length. Slanted members carry more than their share; flatter frames have bigger forces.

A **triangle** can't change shape; a square on pins leans over. **Brace** squares with diagonals. For a flat frame on a pin and a roller, **members + 3 = 2 × joints**.

A pull only fails when a member snaps. A push **buckles** a long thin member at a much smaller force, falling with length squared. Good trusses keep pushed members short and make long members pull. **Testing to failure** gives the **safety factor** (failure load ÷ working load).

## What students get wrong

- **Loads only squash.** The bottom of a roof truss pulls.
- **Stronger members fix a wobbly frame.** Shape (triangles) does.
- **Members = 2 × joints + 3.** It is − 3.
- **A member fails only when pulled too hard.** Long pushed members buckle first.

## The words

**truss**, **member**, **joint (pin)**, **support (pin, roller)**, **tension**, **compression**, **brace**, **buckling**, **safety factor**.
