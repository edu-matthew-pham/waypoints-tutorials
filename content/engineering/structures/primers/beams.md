## The idea in plain words

A **beam** carries a load across a gap. Its **supports share the load** like a lever: each holds load × the far distance ÷ the span, so the nearer support holds more.

A beam **sags** less the stiffer its **cross-section** is, and stiffness grows with **depth cubed** (a rectangle: width × depth³ ÷ 12). The same strip on its edge is 100 times stiffer than flat. **I-beams** and **tubes** put material far from the middle, where it adds most stiffness.

## What students get wrong

- **The same material sags the same.** Shape matters more.
- **Twice as deep is twice as stiff.** It is 8 times.
- **The nearer support holds less.** It holds more.

## The words

**beam**, **span**, **support**, **reaction**, **sag (deflection)**, **cross-section**, **I-beam**, **stiffness**.
