## The idea in plain words

A battery has a **voltage** (V: how hard it pushes) and a **capacity** (mAh: how much charge it holds). Run time is roughly capacity ÷ current: 2000 mAh at 500 mA lasts about 4 hours. Don't plan to use all of it: voltage drops as a battery empties, and rechargeables are damaged by running flat, so plan on about 80%. Weight matters on anything that moves.

AA alkaline packs are cheap and safe; NiMH rechargeables are 1.2 V a cell, not 1.5. **LiPo** packs are light and can give large currents, which is why drones and fast robots use them, but they store a lot of energy in a soft pouch: damaged, over-charged or over-discharged cells can swell and catch fire. They need a balance charger, supervised charging in a fire-safe bag, a low-voltage cutoff, storage at storage charge, and retiring puffy cells.

## What students get wrong

- **mAh is power or strength.** It is how long, not how hard.
- **A LiPo is just a rechargeable AA.** It needs its own rules.

## The words

**voltage**, **capacity (mAh)**, **current draw**, **run time**, **NiMH**, **LiPo**, **2S** (two cells, 7.4 V), **balance charger**, **storage charge**.
