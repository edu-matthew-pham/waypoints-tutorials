## What is here

About 250 lessons for Years 7 to 10, written against the Australian Curriculum (version 9). There is one course per strand per year, as the curriculum sets it out, so a Year 8 class works in the Year 8 courses. Faster students go broader within their year, not on to the next. Each lesson teaches one step of the curriculum's own learning progression, and lists earlier steps under **What you should already know**, so a student can go back without being sent ahead.

- **Number**: [Year 7](../maths/number-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/number-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/number-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/number-10/). Integers, powers, fractions, decimals and percentages, ratios; recurring and irrational numbers and index laws; the real numbers; approximation.
- **Algebra**: [Year 7](../maths/algebra-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/algebra-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/algebra-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/algebra-10/). Expressions, rules and graphs, equations; brackets, inequalities and straight lines; index laws, coordinate geometry and quadratics; quadratics by hand, two variables, exponentials and models.
- **Measurement**: [Year 7](../maths/measurement-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/measurement-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/measurement-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/measurement-10/). Area, volume, circles and angle facts; Pythagoras, time zones and rates; scientific notation, surface area, error and trigonometry in use; composite solids, bearings, error, scale and log scales.
- **Space**: [Year 7](../maths/space-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/space-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/space-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/space-10/). Classifying shapes, nets, views and transformations; congruence and similarity; the trigonometric ratios, enlargement and geometric algorithms; proof, networks and algorithms.
- **Statistics**: [Year 7](../maths/statistics-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/statistics-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/statistics-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/statistics-10/). Averages and displays; samples and bias; distributions and a whole investigation; misleading graphs, boxplots, association and two-way tables.
- **Probability**: [Year 7](../maths/probability-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/probability-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/probability-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/probability-10/). Sample spaces and experiments; Venn diagrams, two-way tables and trees; two-step events and simulations; conditional chance.

A few topics sit where the curriculum puts them, which may not be where you expect: Year 7 angle facts (parallel lines, angle sums) are in Year 7 Measurement; rates and ratio models are in Measurement; Year 9 and 10 index laws are in Algebra; and Year 9 trigonometry starts in Year 9 Space (naming the sides, the ratios) and carries on in Year 9 Measurement (finding sides and angles).

Some courses are small (Year 7 Probability has 3 lessons, Year 10 Number has 3). They stay as their own course rather than being merged into another strand.

**Looking back over a year.** Each year has a look-back page that gathers the recap cards from all six of its courses: [Year 7](../maths/look-back/year-7/) · [Year 8](../maths/look-back/year-8/) · [Year 9](../maths/look-back/year-9/) · [Year 10](../maths/look-back/year-10/). Nothing on it is marked, and it never counts towards Done.

**Before Year 7.** Short optional catch-up lessons for the primary ideas Year 7 relies on are in [Before Year 7](../maths/before-year-7/).

The old course addresses (Number Foundations, Algebra Foundations, Linear relations, Angles and shapes, and so on) still work and forward to the new courses. Progress is saved per lesson address, so a lesson started before the change shows as not started.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson has the same six steps, and takes about 40 to 50 minutes.

1. **Predict.** Students commit to an answer before they see anything, which brings their current idea into the open.
2. **Try.** An interactive board (algebra tiles, a number line, fraction bars, a factor tree, parallel lines, a shape you cut and rearrange, a circle you roll out, a graph, a dot plot, a spinner) lets them test the prediction.
3. **Worked example.** One new move, set out step by step, then a planted mistake to find: a common misconception, met after the method.
4. **Vary.** The same idea, changing one thing at a time, ending with an edge case.
5. **Practise.** Mixed questions, with a review question from an earlier lesson.
6. **Make.** Students work backwards: build a problem that has a given answer.

Answers are marked kindly. An equal answer in an unexpected form is accepted, or told what is left to do ("collect the like terms", "simplify"). In reasoning tasks, students write their working their own way and it is never marked; the answer and the reason at the key step are, and then students compare their working with a model solution.

Each lesson has an **Explain it to me** drawer with a short voiced clip and a summary, opened from the step bar once a student has made their first prediction.

## For you

At the bottom of each course page there is a **Teacher guide** link. It opens:

- a **teacher guide** for each lesson: goals, timing, what to say at each step, the misconceptions and what to do about them, stretch and support, and an exit ticket;
- a **unit primer** ("in 5 minutes") for each unit;
- **printable cheat sheets** and a slide deck and worksheet for each lesson, which load into Prepare.

There is nothing to install and no accounts. Progress is kept in each student's own browser; we do not track anyone.

## A ten-minute tour

Try these as if you were a student, then open the teacher guide for one of them.

1. [**Reasoning with angles**](../maths/measurement-7/angle-reasoning/) (Year 7): the parallel-lines board, a planted mistake, and a reasoning task where students write their own working.
2. [**Expanding brackets**](../maths/algebra-8/expanding/) (Year 8): algebra tiles, answers marked by form, and working checked one line at a time.
3. [**Fractions, decimals and percentages**](../maths/number-7/fractions-decimals-percentages/) (Year 7): fraction bars and a hundred grid.
4. [**Inequalities**](../maths/algebra-8/inequalities/) (Year 8): building a solution on the number line.
5. [**Many trials**](../maths/probability-7/many-trials/) (Year 7): thousands of coin flips, and a mystery bag to estimate.
