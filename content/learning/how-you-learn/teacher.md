## What this course is for

An optional, discussion-led introduction to **self-regulated learning**: how students plan, check and look back on their own learning. It's for any class, Years 6 to 8, and takes four lessons of about 40 minutes. It gives students the ideas and a shared language (before, during and after). The habit itself is built afterwards, in every subject, with the strategy cards and occasional prompts. See For Learning skills teachers for how to do that.

## How the workshops run

- **Explore before naming.** Each workshop starts with a made-up task that has a trap where planning or checking pays off. The class compares approaches, and the idea is named afterwards.
- **Discussion carries it.** The class plans have a debate or discussion in each lesson; several questions have more than one good answer.
- **Nib makes the mistakes.** Nib, the site's novice, makes one slip per lesson for students to find, so no real student's thinking is on show.
- **The last workshop is the transfer.** Students plan to use one strategy on a real task from another subject that week. Following up later ("did it help?") matters more than anything in the lessons.

## Nothing personal is asked

Students never share how they study, their marks, or what they find hard. Each lesson ends with a private think that isn't written down or collected. The "own task" in workshop 4 is written for the student only.

## Width

Each lesson has a **Typical** step for Years 7 and 8 (a science report, a long project, an AI explanation). For Year 6, add `?width=minimum` to the lesson link. Typical steps aren't needed to finish.

## The research behind it

- Self-regulated learning as a cycle of forethought, performance and self-reflection (Zimmerman); the EEF's plan, monitor and evaluate is the same idea, simplified. The EEF finds these strategies work best taught explicitly and applied to normal subject content, not as standalone thinking-skills lessons: that's why this course is short and optional, and the cards are the part to keep using.
- Understanding the task first (Winne and Hadwin), metacognitive monitoring and calibration (Dunlosky; Nelson and Narens), illusions of competence and desirable difficulties (Bjork), and the review of study techniques by Dunlosky and colleagues (2013).
- Self-directed learning (Knowles) as choosing goals and resources, separate from self-regulated learning.
- Teaching a strategy explicitly (NEMO-T, Shyam Barr, *Educate to Self-Regulate*, 2024; SRSD, Harris and Graham), and the gap between knowing a strategy and using it (Veenman; Barr).

The lessons simplify these ideas for Years 6 to 8 and say "usually" and "often" where the evidence has limits.
