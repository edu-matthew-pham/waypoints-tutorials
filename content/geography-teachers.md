## What is here

**This is a preview.** One lesson is built, and none of it has been tried with a class.

- [**Water as a resource**](../geography/water-7/): five lessons are planned for AC9HG7K01. The first, *what kind of resource water is*, is built: students sort resources by two questions (does it come back by itself, and can our use make it run out?) and apply the same reasoning to water in its three forms. It is a hinge lesson, so it carries an easier check you can set before moving on.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where Nib makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## Real places

Real places appear only for settled facts that will not date (a river is a river, a quoted line from a named source). Everything else, the people and the cases, is made up, so nothing dates and nothing leans.

## First Nations connection to Country

We do not write explanations of First Nations values or connection to Country ourselves. Teacher notes start from ACARA's own elaborations and ask you to build on them with local, community-approved material. Those standards will be pointers to First Nations-led resources.

## What is planned

These are plans, not promises. The rest of the water course (movement, effects, changes to the land), then Water in Australia, the values of water and water hazards, then liveability. The Geography skills (interpreting data, drawing conclusions, strategies for action) come after.
