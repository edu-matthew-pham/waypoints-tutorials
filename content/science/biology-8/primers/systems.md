title: From cells to organs in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

Nearly every cell in your body has the **same DNA**, but cells **specialise**: they switch on different instructions and develop a shape and contents that suit one job.

| Cell | Feature | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Red blood cell | biconcave disc, no nucleus, full of haemoglobin | more room for oxygen, quick exchange, squeezes through capillaries |
| Nerve cell | long axon, many dendrites, myelin | carries signals a long way, fast |
| Muscle cell | long, many mitochondria, protein fibres | contracts, with plenty of energy |
| Root hair cell | long extension, no chloroplasts | huge surface for absorbing water underground |

Similar cells working together form a **tissue** (muscle tissue, epithelial lining). Several tissues working together form an **organ** (the heart: cardiac muscle, lining, connective tissue, nervous tissue; a leaf: epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, xylem and phloem). Organs work together in **organ systems**.

## Where the picture breaks

- The heart diagram is a strip of the wall, not the whole heart. The leaf diagram is one slice.
- Specialised cells are drawn as single examples; real tissues contain many cells and other cell types.

## Common misconceptions

- Different cells have different DNA.
- An organ is made of one kind of tissue.
- Specialised cells are simply "bigger" or "smaller" versions of a general cell.
