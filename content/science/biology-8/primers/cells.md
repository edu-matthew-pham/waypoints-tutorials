## The idea in plain words

**Cell theory:** all living things are made of one or more cells; the cell is the smallest unit that carries out all life processes; cells come from other cells. That includes plants, fungi and bacteria, not just animals.

- **Single-celled** organisms (bacteria, yeast, amoebas) are one cell doing everything.
- **Multicellular** organisms (plants, animals, mushrooms) are organised collections of many cells.

Almost every cell has three parts:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Cell membrane** | A thin, flexible barrier that controls what enters and leaves |
| **Cytoplasm** | Jelly-like fluid where most chemical reactions happen |
| **Nucleus** | The control centre, holding the DNA (bacteria have no nucleus: their DNA is loose) |

Plant cells also have a **cell wall** (support), **chloroplasts** (photosynthesis) and a large **vacuole** (keeps the cell firm). Both have **mitochondria** (release energy).

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's diagrams are flat, coloured models. Real cells are 3D, mostly transparent, and need stains to see under a microscope.
- Sizes are not to scale: a bacterium is about a tenth of the width of a cheek cell.

## Common misconceptions

- Only animals have cells (plants are "made of plant stuff").
- The cell wall and the cell membrane are the same thing.
- The nucleus is a "brain" that thinks; it holds instructions.

## Cellular scale (the first lesson)

Most cells are 0.01 to 0.1 mm across, below the eye's limit of about 0.1 mm; a person has about 37 trillion. Magnification = image size ÷ actual size. Cells were discovered with the microscope (Hooke 1665, Leeuwenhoek). Misconceptions: cells are about the size of sand grains; bigger animals have bigger cells.

## Plant cells and other organelles (nodes 4 and 5)

Only plant cells have a cell wall (support), chloroplasts (photosynthesis) and a large vacuole (firmness, storage). Both have mitochondria (respiration) and ribosomes (proteins); the numbers follow the cell's job. Misconceptions: plants don't need mitochondria; all plant cells are green; animal cells have walls.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does a stain change how many cell parts we can see?** Change: onion skin unstained or with iodine. Measure: parts identified (wall, membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm). Keep the same: onion, magnification, slide preparation.
- **Does magnification change how many cells fit across the field of view?** Change: objective lens (×4, ×10, ×40). Measure: cells across the field. Keep the same: slide, specimen. *(Measure the field width with a clear ruler at low power, then estimate one cell's size.)*
- **Do all plant cells have chloroplasts?** Change: plant part (Elodea leaf, onion bulb, celery stalk). Measure: cells with chloroplasts out of 10. Keep the same: magnification, stain (none). *(Tackles "all plant cells are green".)*

**Safety and ethics:** slides and coverslips are glass: handle by the edges and report breakages. Never aim a microscope mirror at the Sun. Iodine stains skin and clothes.
