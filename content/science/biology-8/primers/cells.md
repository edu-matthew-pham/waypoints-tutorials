## The idea in plain words

**Cell theory:** all living things are made of one or more cells; the cell is the smallest unit that carries out all life processes; cells come from other cells. That includes plants, fungi and bacteria, not just animals.

- **Single-celled** organisms (bacteria, yeast, amoebas) are one cell doing everything.
- **Multicellular** organisms (plants, animals, mushrooms) are organised collections of many cells.

Almost every cell has three parts:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Cell membrane** | A thin, flexible barrier that controls what enters and leaves |
| **Cytoplasm** | Jelly-like fluid where most chemical reactions happen |
| **Nucleus** | The control centre, holding the DNA (bacteria have no nucleus: their DNA is loose) |

Plant cells also have a **cell wall** (support), **chloroplasts** (photosynthesis) and a large **vacuole** (keeps the cell firm). Both have **mitochondria** (release energy).

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's diagrams are flat, coloured models. Real cells are 3D, mostly transparent, and need stains to see under a microscope.
- Sizes are not to scale: a bacterium is about a tenth of the width of a cheek cell.

## Common misconceptions

- Only animals have cells (plants are "made of plant stuff").
- The cell wall and the cell membrane are the same thing.
- The nucleus is a "brain" that thinks; it holds instructions.
