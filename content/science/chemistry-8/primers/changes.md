title: Chemical change in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

A **chemical change** makes at least one **new substance**. At the particle level, the atoms in the starting substances (the **reactants**) separate and join up in new arrangements (the **products**). No atoms are made or lost; they are rearranged.

A **physical change** (melting, boiling, dissolving, cutting) changes the arrangement or spacing of the particles, but every particle stays the same particle: no new substance.

**The test is "is there a new substance?", not "can it be reversed?"** Dissolving salt can be reversed by evaporating the water, and it is physical. Some chemical changes can be reversed too (charging a battery). Reversibility is a clue, not the test.

## Clues that a new substance has formed

A colour change, a gas given off (bubbles), a solid forming in a liquid, light, or a temperature change that keeps going. Each is a clue only: boiling water makes bubbles and is physical.

## Common misconceptions

- If it can be reversed, it must be physical; if it can't, it must be chemical.
- Dissolving is a chemical change because the solid "disappears".
- In burning, the fuel is destroyed or "turns into energy".
- Mixing two substances is always a chemical change.
