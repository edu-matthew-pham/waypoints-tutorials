## The idea in plain words

A **chemical change** makes at least one **new substance**. At the particle level, the atoms in the starting substances (the **reactants**) separate and join up in new arrangements (the **products**). No atoms are made or lost; they are rearranged.

A **physical change** (melting, boiling, dissolving, cutting) changes the arrangement or spacing of the particles, but every particle stays the same particle: no new substance.

**The test is "is there a new substance?", not "can it be reversed?"** Dissolving salt can be reversed by evaporating the water, and it is physical. Some chemical changes can be reversed too (charging a battery). Reversibility is a clue, not the test.

## Clues that a new substance has formed

A colour change, a gas given off (bubbles), a solid forming in a liquid, light, or a temperature change that keeps going. Each is a clue only: boiling water makes bubbles and is physical.

## Common misconceptions

- If it can be reversed, it must be physical; if it can't, it must be chemical.
- Dissolving is a chemical change because the solid "disappears".
- In burning, the fuel is destroyed or "turns into energy".
- Mixing two substances is always a chemical change.

## Physical change first

The unit now opens with physical change (node 1): melting, boiling and dissolving rearrange the same particles, so no new substance forms and the mass stays the same. Steam is still H₂O. Chemical change (node 2) then contrasts with it on the same boards.

## Signs and energy (nodes 3 to 5)

Signs of a chemical change (colour, gas, precipitate, light, smell, temperature change) are clues, not proof; the test is a new substance. Exothermic reactions give energy out to the surroundings (hotter); endothermic ones take it in (colder). There is no 'cold energy'.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does the mass change when ice melts?** Change: state (frozen, then melted). Measure: mass of a sealed zip-lock bag of ice cubes. Keep the same: the bag stays sealed and is dried before weighing. *(Expect no change: physical change, same particles.)*
- **Does more bicarbonate of soda make vinegar colder?** Change: mass of bicarb (1, 2, 3, 4 g). Measure: temperature drop. Keep the same: 50 mL of vinegar, starting temperature, insulated cup. *(Endothermic: a few degrees colder; the bubbles are a new substance, carbon dioxide.)*
- **Does rinsing steel wool in vinegar change how much it warms as it rusts?** Change: dipped in vinegar or left dry. Measure: temperature rise in 5 minutes, steel wool wrapped round the thermometer bulb in a closed jar. Keep the same: mass of steel wool, jar. *(Exothermic: expect a rise of a few degrees for the dipped wool.)*

**Safety and ethics:** safety glasses with vinegar; gloves for steel wool, which splinters.
