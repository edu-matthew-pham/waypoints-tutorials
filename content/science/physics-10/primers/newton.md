## The idea in plain words

- **First law (inertia):** an object keeps doing what it is doing (staying still, or moving at a constant velocity) unless a **net force** acts on it. A moving object does **not** need a force to keep moving; it needs a force to **change** its motion. Things slow down on Earth because of friction and air resistance, forces that are easy to forget.
- **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity: a = (change in velocity) ÷ (time taken), in **m/s²**. It has a direction: speeding up, slowing down (acceleration opposite to the motion) and turning are all acceleration. On a speed–time graph, acceleration is the **gradient**.
- **Second law:** **F = ma**, where F is the **net** force (N), m the mass (kg) and a the acceleration (m/s²), in the direction of the net force. Double the net force, double the acceleration; double the mass, half the acceleration.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board runs motion in one line (left and right). Turning is acceleration too, but the board can't show it.
- Friction on the board is a fixed size while sliding; real air resistance grows with speed.

## Common misconceptions

- Motion needs a continuous force (the Aristotelian view: "the force of the throw stays in the ball").
- Acceleration means "going fast"; a fast object at a steady speed has zero acceleration.
- In F = ma, F is any force on the object (it is the net force).
- Mass and weight are interchangeable in F = ma.
