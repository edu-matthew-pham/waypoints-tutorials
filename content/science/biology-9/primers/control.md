## The idea in plain words

Every response follows one pathway: **stimulus → receptor → command centre → effector → response**.

- A **stimulus** is a change. It can be **external** (heat, light, a sound) or **internal** (blood sugar rising, body temperature falling, too little water).
- **Receptors** detect it; the **command centre** (the brain or spinal cord, or a gland such as the pancreas) decides; **effectors** (muscles and glands) carry out the response.
- The **nervous system** sends electrical impulses along nerves: fast, short-lived, to one exact place. Reflexes go through the spinal cord without waiting for the brain.
- The **endocrine system** releases **hormones** into the blood: slower, longer-lasting, reaching every cell but acting only on target cells.
- **Homeostasis** keeps internal conditions (temperature, blood sugar, water) near a **set point** by **negative feedback**: a change away from the set point triggers a response that reverses it.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's pathways are one line each; real responses involve many receptors, nerves and both systems at once.
- The temperature model is a toy: the numbers are not a real body's, and it uses only sweating, shivering and blood flow.
- The race graph is schematic: real nerve and hormone timings vary hugely.

## Common misconceptions

- Only external changes are stimuli.
- The brain controls every response (reflexes and many hormones don't wait for it).
- Hormones travel along nerves.
- Homeostasis keeps things exactly constant, rather than correcting changes.
