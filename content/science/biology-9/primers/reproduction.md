## The idea in plain words

- **Gametes** are sex cells: sperm and egg in animals; pollen (carrying the male nucleus) and the ovule's egg in flowering plants. Each has **half** the usual number of chromosomes (23 in humans, not 46), so fertilisation restores the full number.
- Gametes are **specialised**: a sperm has a tail, a midpiece full of mitochondria and an enzyme cap; an egg is large, with a food store and a coat that lets only one sperm in.
- **Sexual reproduction**: two parents, gametes fuse, offspring get a random half of each parent's genes, so they **vary**.
- **Asexual reproduction**: one parent, no gametes, offspring are **clones** (genetically identical), unless a mutation occurs.
- Variation helps a population survive a changing environment; asexual reproduction is faster and needs no partner.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's creatures have three genes, each with two forms and one simple effect. Real traits often involve many genes and the environment.
- Gamete diagrams are not to scale: a human egg is about 20 times the width of a sperm's head.

## Common misconceptions

- Offspring are a blend, or an exact half-and-half mix of their parents' looks.
- Brothers and sisters should look the same because they have the same parents.
- Gametes have the same number of chromosomes as body cells.
- Asexual reproduction only happens in "simple" organisms (strawberries, potatoes, aphids and some lizards do it too).
