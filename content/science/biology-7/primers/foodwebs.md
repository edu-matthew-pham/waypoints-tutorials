## The idea in plain words

A **food chain** shows one path of who eats whom: grass → grasshopper → lizard → kookaburra. A **food web** joins all the chains in an ecosystem. **Arrows point the way energy flows: from the food to the eater.**

- **Producers** (plants, algae) make their own food using sunlight.
- **Consumers** eat other organisms: **primary** consumers eat producers, **secondary** consumers eat primary consumers, and so on.
- **Decomposers** (fungi and bacteria, which are microorganisms) break down dead plants and animals and wastes, returning nutrients to the soil.

**Energy flows one way.** At each step up, most of the energy (about 90%) is used for living (moving, keeping warm) or lost as heat and in parts not eaten; only about 10% is passed on. That is why there are few top predators. **Matter cycles**: decomposers return the atoms to the soil and air, where producers use them again.

A **biotic factor** is a living part of the ecosystem (predators, prey, competitors, disease). Changing one population ripples through the web: a **trophic cascade**.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's cascade model follows each feeding link once, nearest effects first, and says "could go either way" when effects disagree. Real populations also depend on how much each species relies on each food, on competition and on time.
- The 10% rule is an average; real values range from about 5% to 20%.

## Common misconceptions

- Arrows mean "eats" and point from the eater to the food.
- Energy is recycled like matter.
- Removing a predator is good for everything below it.
- Decomposers aren't part of the food web.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does light intensity change how fast pondweed makes oxygen?** Change: lamp distance. Measure: bubbles per minute. Keep the same: the piece of pondweed (Elodea), water temperature, a pinch of bicarbonate of soda. *(Use an LED lamp so the water doesn't warm.)*
- **Does temperature change how fast decomposers break down food?** Change: fridge, room or warm spot. Measure: how much of an apple slice is covered in mould after a week. Keep the same: fruit, slice size, moisture, sealed bags.
- **Are there more small animals under leaf litter than on bare ground?** Change: site. Measure: animals counted in a 30 cm square in 5 minutes. Keep the same: square size, time, weather.

**Safety and ethics:** keep water away from the lamp's power; never open mould bags. Observe small animals gently and return them where found. Use school grounds; for sites on Country, check with local Traditional Owners or your school's First Nations contacts first.
