## The idea in plain words

**Igneous** rocks form when molten rock cools and sets solid. Underground it is called **magma**; when it reaches the surface it is **lava**.

- **Intrusive** igneous rock cools **slowly**, underground (over thousands to hundreds of thousands of years). Particles have time to join the few crystals that start, so the crystals grow **large**: you can see them (granite).
- **Extrusive** igneous rock cools **quickly** at the surface (days to years). Many crystals start at once and none has time to grow: crystals are **small** (basalt).
- Cooled almost instantly (lava into water), particles freeze where they are, with no pattern: **volcanic glass** (obsidian).

So **crystal size is a record of cooling rate**: a geologist can read where a rock formed from its crystals.

## Where the picture breaks

- Granite and basalt also differ in what they are made of (granite is rich in silica; basalt in iron and magnesium). The simulation shows only the effect of cooling speed.
- Real minerals are several different crystals growing together; the simulation colours each crystal differently to show its size.

## Common misconceptions

- Large crystals form because there is more magma.
- Fast cooling means the crystals are smaller because they "shrink".
- All volcanic rock is the same.
