title: Atom rearrangement in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

In a chemical reaction the atoms of the **reactants** separate and join up in new arrangements, the **products**. The **types** and **numbers** of atoms do not change: only how they are joined. So the total mass does not change either (**conservation of mass**).

That is why an equation must **balance**: each side must show the same atoms. In methane burning, CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, both sides have 1 C, 4 H and 4 O.

## Word equations first

Before the atoms: a **word equation** names what reacts and what is made, reactants → products (methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water). The common slips are leaving out water when a fuel burns, leaving out oxygen, and putting heat or light in the equation. Word equations show no amounts or atoms; that is what formulas and balancing add.

## Why mass seems to change

When something burns in the open, gases (carbon dioxide, water vapour) escape into the air, so the ash weighs less than the wood. In a sealed container nothing escapes, and the mass stays the same. Rusting seems to gain mass because oxygen from the air joins the iron.

## Where the picture breaks

- The simulation shows a reaction with more than two reactant particles happening at once. Real reactions like burning methane happen in several quick steps.
- Molecules are drawn as balls stuck together, in 2D.

## Common misconceptions

- Atoms are destroyed when something burns, or "turn into energy".
- Balancing means changing the small numbers in formulas (changing H₂O to H₂O₂ makes a different substance).
- Gases have no mass, so a reaction that makes a gas loses mass.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does sealing the flask change the mass after bicarbonate of soda reacts with vinegar?** Change: open flask, or a balloon over the neck. Measure: total mass before and after. Keep the same: amounts of bicarb and vinegar, the balance. *(Open loses mass as carbon dioxide escapes; sealed stays almost the same. An inflated balloon can read a little light because the air pushes it up.)*
- **Does steel wool gain mass as it rusts?** Change: time (days 0 to 5). Measure: mass of a damp pad of steel wool on a watch glass. Keep the same: the pad, where it sits, weighing it dry each time. *(A small gain from oxygen joining the iron; a 0.01 g balance helps.)*

**Safety and ethics:** safety glasses with vinegar; never cap a fizzing bottle, use a balloon. Gloves for steel wool.
