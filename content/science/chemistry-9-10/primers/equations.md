## The idea in plain words

In a chemical reaction the atoms of the **reactants** separate and join up in new arrangements, the **products**. The **types** and **numbers** of atoms do not change: only how they are joined. So the total mass does not change either (**conservation of mass**).

That is why an equation must **balance**: each side must show the same atoms. In methane burning, CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, both sides have 1 C, 4 H and 4 O.

## Why mass seems to change

When something burns in the open, gases (carbon dioxide, water vapour) escape into the air, so the ash weighs less than the wood. In a sealed container nothing escapes, and the mass stays the same. Rusting seems to gain mass because oxygen from the air joins the iron.

## Where the picture breaks

- The simulation shows a reaction with more than two reactant particles happening at once. Real reactions like burning methane happen in several quick steps.
- Molecules are drawn as balls stuck together, in 2D.

## Common misconceptions

- Atoms are destroyed when something burns, or "turn into energy".
- Balancing means changing the small numbers in formulas (changing H₂O to H₂O₂ makes a different substance).
- Gases have no mass, so a reaction that makes a gas loses mass.
