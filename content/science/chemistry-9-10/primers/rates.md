## The idea in plain words

Particles react only when they **collide**, and only if the collision has **enough energy** (the **activation energy**). Most collisions just bounce. The **rate** of a reaction depends on how many collisions happen each second and how many of those have enough energy.

| Change | What it does to collisions |
|---|---|
| Higher temperature | Particles move faster: more collisions, and many more with enough energy |
| Higher concentration | More particles in the same space: more collisions |
| Larger surface area (smaller pieces) | More particles exposed: more collisions |
| A catalyst | Gives the reaction an easier path with a lower activation energy: more collisions have enough energy. It is not used up. |

Temperature has the biggest effect because it changes **both** how often particles collide and how hard.

## Where the picture breaks

- The simulation is 2D and has only a few dozen particles, so run times vary from run to run, as real ones do with small amounts. Compare several runs.
- In the simulation the catalyst simply halves the activation energy for every collision. A real catalyst provides a different route, often on its surface.

## Common misconceptions

- Heat makes particles "want" to react, or makes them bigger.
- Every collision causes a reaction.
- A catalyst is used up, or adds energy.
