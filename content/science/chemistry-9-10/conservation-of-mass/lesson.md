A short lesson with no simulation. You can balance equations. Now check what a balanced equation says about mass.

## Engage: the fizzing flask

```step
stage: predict
insight: "Students see a falling mass reading in an open vessel as mass being destroyed. The mass is in the gas that escaped."
check:
  type: predict
  q: "An antacid tablet is dropped into water in an open beaker on a balance. It fizzes and the balance reading falls. Where has the missing mass gone?"
  options: ["Into the carbon dioxide gas that escaped into the air", "It was destroyed in the reaction", "It turned into energy", "The balance is faulty"]
  actual: "Into the carbon dioxide gas that escaped. If the same reaction were done in a sealed flask, the reading wouldn't change. The mass is conserved: the gas just left the balance."
```

## Explain: counting everything

```step
stage: investigate
insight: "Closed-system results, open-system corrections and the link to the balanced equation meet the three success criteria."
tasks:
  - check:
      type: question
      q: "Why is the total mass the same before and after a reaction in a sealed flask?"
      options: ["Atoms are only rearranged: none are made or destroyed, so the total mass stays the same", "Any gas made has no mass, so only the solids and liquids count", "The flask absorbs the difference"]
      answer: 0
      feedback: [null, "Gases have mass too. In a sealed flask they stay on the balance, so the reading holds.", "Nothing absorbs mass."]
      explain: "The same atoms are present before and after, just joined in new ways. Same atoms, same total mass."
  - check:
      type: number
      q: "In a sealed flask, 12.0 g of magnesium reacts with 8.0 g of oxygen. What mass of magnesium oxide forms (g)?"
      answer: 20
      explain: "The total mass of products equals the total mass of reactants: 12.0 + 8.0 = 20.0 g."
  - check:
      type: number
      q: "In an open dish, 2.4 g of magnesium burns and the white ash weighs 4.0 g. What mass of oxygen from the air joined in (g)?"
      answer: 1.6
      explain: "4.0 − 2.4 = 1.6 g. The ash is heavier because it contains oxygen from the air."
  - check:
      type: question
      q: "A log burns in an open fire and the ash is much lighter than the log. Is mass conserved?"
      options: ["Yes: most of the mass left as carbon dioxide and water vapour, which spread into the air", "No: burning destroys mass", "Yes: because the ash is the same thing as the log"]
      answer: 0
      feedback: [null, "Mass isn't destroyed: it left as gases.", "Ash is a different substance from wood."]
      explain: "Count the gases, and the total mass of reactants (wood and oxygen) equals the total mass of products (ash, carbon dioxide and water vapour)."
  - check:
      type: question
      q: "The balanced equation 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O has 4 hydrogen atoms and 2 oxygen atoms on each side. What does this tell you about mass?"
      options: ["The same atoms are on each side, so the mass is the same before and after", "Water is lighter than hydrogen and oxygen together", "Nothing: equations don't show mass"]
      answer: 0
      feedback: [null, "Water has the same mass as the hydrogen and oxygen that make it.", "A balanced equation is a record of the atoms, and so of the mass."]
      explain: "Balancing is the same idea as conservation of mass: each atom appears on both sides the same number of times."
  - check: { type: reflect, text: "I can explain why mass is conserved in a reaction and why it can seem to change in an open system." }
```

**Revision summary**

- In a chemical reaction atoms are **rearranged, not made or destroyed**, so the total mass is **conserved**.
- In a **closed system** the mass stays the same.
- In an **open system** it can seem to change because a **gas escapes** or **joins in from the air**; counting the gas shows the mass is still conserved.
- A **balanced equation** has the same atoms on both sides.
