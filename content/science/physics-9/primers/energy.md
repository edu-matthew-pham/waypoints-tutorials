title: Conduction and convection in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

Heat is energy moving from hotter to cooler places. The particle model explains two ways it travels through matter.

- **Conduction** (mostly in solids): particles at the hot end vibrate harder and bump their neighbours, which then vibrate harder and bump theirs. The **energy** passes along; the **particles stay in place**. Metals conduct well (and they have free electrons that carry energy fast, Year 10); most non-metals, and gases, conduct poorly: they are **insulators**.
- **Convection** (only in liquids and gases): the heated part of the fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks to take its place. The **particles themselves move**, carrying the energy with them in a loop called a **convection current**.

Trapped air is an excellent insulator: it conducts poorly and, if it is trapped in small pockets, it can't form convection currents. That is how wool, fur, feathers, foam and double glazing work.

## Where the picture breaks

- The simulation pushes hot particles up directly. In reality hot fluid rises because its particles spread out, making it less dense.
- Radiation (the third way, which needs no particles) is the next node.

## Common misconceptions

- Heat rises (hot fluid rises; heat goes in every direction).
- Cold flows into things ("close the door, you're letting the cold in").
- In conduction the particles travel along the material.
- Metals feel cold because they are colder than wood at the same temperature.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does the material of a rod change how fast heat travels along it?** Change: the rod (copper, aluminium, steel, glass). Measure: the time until a drawing pin stuck on with petroleum jelly drops off. Keep the same: rod size, water temperature, the pin's distance from the water.
- **Does wrapping a cup change how fast hot water cools?** Change: the wrapping (none, foil, bubble wrap, a wool sock). Measure: the temperature drop over 10 minutes. Keep the same: cup, volume, starting temperature. *(The best wrappings trap air.)*
- **Does hotter water rise faster?** Change: the temperature of coloured water released at the bottom of a beaker of cool water. Measure: the time for the colour to reach the top. Keep the same: the beaker, the amount of dye, the depth.

**Safety and ethics:** use water no hotter than about 60 °C (from a kettle, not boiling), stand beakers on a mat, and report broken glass.
