title: Wave and particle models in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

The **particle model** explains how energy moves through matter: **conduction** and **convection** (collisions and bulk movement), and **electricity** (static charge is electrons moved from one material to another; a current is free electrons pushed round a loop by a battery).

The **wave model** explains **sound** and **light**. Sound is a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions that needs a medium; the pitch is set by frequency and the loudness by amplitude. Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave that needs no medium, and sits in a spectrum from radio to gamma: shorter wavelength, higher frequency, more energy. The wave model explains reflection and refraction.

A **model is a tool**, with a domain of usefulness. Light also behaves as photons when it hands energy to single electrons.

## Where the picture breaks

- Boards slow waves down and show only a few particles; real sound waves have vastly more particles per wavelength.
- The wave in the spectrum board is a schematic: real wavelengths span about fifteen powers of ten.
- The current board exaggerates the electrons' drift speed; real drift is a fraction of a millimetre per second.
- Colours of light and their different bending (dispersion) are not modelled.

## Common misconceptions

- Air particles travel from the speaker to the ear.
- Sound travels through space or faster in lighter media.
- Rubbing makes charge, or moves protons.
- Electrons are used up in the bulb, or the battery makes them.
- X-rays are faster than light, or microwaves have more energy per wave than X-rays.
- Light bends because glass pushes it; it speeds up in glass.
- One of the wave and particle models must be the true one.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does a longer vibrating ruler make a lower note?** Change: the length of ruler over the edge of the desk. Measure: the frequency (a data logger sound sensor), or rank the pitch by ear. Keep the same: the ruler, how far you flick it.
- **Does the angle light hits a glass block change how much it bends?** Change: the angle of incidence (0°, 20°, 40°, 60°). Measure: the angle of refraction with a protractor. Keep the same: the block, the ray box. *(At 0° the ray goes straight through.)*
- **Does rubbing a balloon more give it more charge?** Change: the number of rubs on wool (5, 10, 20). Measure: the number of small paper pieces it picks up. Keep the same: the balloon, the cloth, the paper pieces. *(Humid days spoil static.)*

**Safety and ethics:** ray box lamps get hot; check for latex allergies before using balloons.
