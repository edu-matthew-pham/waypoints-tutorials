title: Conservation of energy and efficiency in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

**Conservation of energy**: energy is never made or destroyed, only transferred or transformed, so total energy in = total energy out. Energy that seems lost has gone to the surroundings, usually as heat and sound.

**Efficiency** = useful energy out ÷ total energy in × 100%. It is a proportion, and no real device reaches 100%, because some energy always becomes by-products.

A **Sankey diagram** shows the flows as bands with widths to scale: the useful output goes straight on, wasted outputs bend away, and the outputs add up to the input.

## Where the picture breaks

- The boards show one split from input to outputs; real systems have several stages, each with its own losses.
- Figures are rounded and typical (filament bulb 10%, LED 40%, car engine 25%, coal station 35%, hydro 90%).

## Common misconceptions

- Energy is used up or disappears.
- "Conserve energy" in the everyday sense (save it) is the same as the law.
- The device with the biggest useful output is the most efficient.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Which ball keeps the biggest share of its energy after a bounce?** Change: the ball (tennis, ping-pong, rubber, squash). Measure: bounce height ÷ drop height × 100%. Keep the same: drop height, floor. *(For one ball, gravitational energy is proportional to height, so this is the efficiency of the bounce.)*
- **Does a filament bulb or an LED give off more heat?** Change: the bulb, at similar brightness. Measure: a thermometer 2 cm away after 5 minutes. Keep the same: distance, time, power supply. *(A warmer thermometer means more by-product heat and a less efficient bulb.)*
- **Does a heavier pendulum bob keep swinging longer?** Change: the mass of the bob (same size). Measure: swings until the swing is half as wide. Keep the same: string length, starting angle.

**Safety and ethics:** filament bulbs get hot enough to burn: don't touch them until they have cooled.
