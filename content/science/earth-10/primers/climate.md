title: Earth's energy balance in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

- The sun delivers on average about **340 W/m²** to the top of the atmosphere. About **30% is reflected** (clouds, ice, bright surfaces): this fraction is the **albedo**. The rest is **absorbed** and warms the surface, oceans and air.
- Earth gives energy back to space as **infrared** radiation. When energy out equals energy in, Earth is in **energy balance** and its temperature stays steady. If in exceeds out, Earth warms until the extra outgoing infrared restores balance.
- Energy moves between the spheres by radiation, conduction and convection (warm air rising, evaporation). The **oceans store** most of the extra heat and move it around the world.
- **Greenhouse gases** (water vapour, CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide) absorb infrared from the surface and send some back down. They slow the energy going **out**, not the sunlight coming in. Without them Earth's average surface would be about −18 °C instead of about 15 °C (the **natural greenhouse effect**). Adding more (the **enhanced greenhouse effect**) creates an imbalance, so Earth warms.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's model has one atmosphere layer and one surface temperature; real climate has clouds, winds, currents, ice and feedbacks.
- In the model, sunlight passes straight through the air; really about a fifth is absorbed on the way down.
- The model reaches balance in a few decades; the real deep ocean takes centuries.

## Common misconceptions

- The greenhouse effect is the ozone hole.
- Greenhouse gases trap incoming sunlight (they slow outgoing infrared).
- The greenhouse effect is entirely bad (without it Earth would be frozen).
- Energy balance means nothing is happening (huge flows in and out are equal).
