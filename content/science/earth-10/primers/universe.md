## The idea in plain words

- The **big bang theory** says space, time, matter and energy began about **13.8 billion years ago** in an extremely hot, dense state, and the universe has been **expanding and cooling** ever since. It wasn't an explosion into empty space: space itself expands.
- Stages: inflation (a tiny fraction of a second); protons and neutrons (a millionth of a second); hydrogen and helium nuclei (3 minutes); atoms, and light set free (380 000 years: the cosmic microwave background); the dark ages; the first stars (about 200 million years); galaxies (about 1 billion years); the sun and Earth (9.2 billion years).
- Heavier elements (carbon, oxygen, iron) were made later, inside stars and in supernovae.
- **Redshift:** light from distant galaxies is shifted to longer (redder) wavelengths, so they are moving away from us. Hubble found that **farther galaxies move away faster**: what you'd see from *any* galaxy if space is expanding. Run the expansion backwards and everything was once together.

## Where the picture breaks

- The timeline board spaces the stages evenly; the real times run from a tiny fraction of a second to billions of years.
- The expanding-space board is 2D and its redshifts are drawn 20 times bigger. Real galaxies also have their own small motions (nearby Andromeda is blueshifted, moving towards us).
- Strictly, cosmological redshift is space stretching the light on its way, not just a Doppler effect; at Year 10 the Doppler picture is a good model.

## Common misconceptions

- The big bang was an explosion at one point in space, and we are near the centre.
- Galaxies are flying through space away from a centre (space itself is stretching; there is no centre).
- The universe started from a tiny speck sitting in empty space.
- All elements were made in the big bang.
