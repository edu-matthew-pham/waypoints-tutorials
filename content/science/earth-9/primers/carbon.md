title: The carbon cycle in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

- Earth has four **spheres**: the **atmosphere** (air), **biosphere** (living things), **hydrosphere** (water) and **geosphere** (rock, soil, magma). They overlap and interact: soil is rock and living things; a cloud is water in the air.
- Carbon is stored in every sphere: CO₂ in the air, living things and soil, dissolved in the oceans, and rocks and fossil fuels in the geosphere. It moves between stores.
- **Photosynthesis** takes CO₂ from the air into living things. **Respiration** (by plants, animals and decomposers) returns it. These two flows are huge (about 120 billion tonnes of carbon a year each way) and nearly balance.
- **Combustion** of fossil fuels moves carbon from the geosphere, where it was stored over millions of years, into the air in a few years. The air's CO₂ has risen from about 280 ppm (before 1800) to about 420 ppm.
- When flows in and out of a store balance, the store stays steady. When they don't, it grows or shrinks.

## Where the picture breaks

- The board's model is simplified: four stores, a few flows, and simple rules for how plants and oceans respond. Real numbers vary between sources.
- The geosphere store shows only fossil fuels; rocks such as limestone hold far more carbon but move it very slowly.
- In the board, switching off a whole process is a "what if": real processes never stop entirely.

## Common misconceptions

- Plants get their mass from the soil (most of a plant's mass is carbon taken from the air).
- Only animals respire (plants and decomposers respire too).
- Carbon is used up or destroyed when fuel burns (it moves to the air as CO₂).
- Humans' extra CO₂ is small next to natural flows, so it can't matter (the natural flows balance; the extra doesn't).
