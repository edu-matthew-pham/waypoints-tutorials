## The idea in plain words

- **DNA** is a long molecule that carries the instructions for building and running an organism. It is coiled into **chromosomes** in the nucleus (46 in human body cells, in 23 pairs: one of each pair from each parent).
- A **gene** is a section of DNA that codes for one protein, which affects a characteristic. Different versions of a gene are **alleles**.
- **Meiosis** is the cell division that makes gametes: the pairs separate, and each gamete gets one chromosome from each pair, chosen at random. With 23 pairs there are over 8 million combinations, before crossing over adds more.
- Each characteristic in the board's examples is controlled by one gene with two alleles. A **dominant** allele (B) shows whenever it is present; a **recessive** allele (b) shows only when there are two (bb). The pair of alleles is the **genotype**; the characteristic you see is the **phenotype**.
- A **Punnett square** shows every way the parents' gametes can combine, so it gives the **probability** of each genotype, not a guaranteed count.

## Where the picture breaks

- The zoom is not to scale; a real chromosome is far longer than drawn, and genes are scattered with non-coding DNA between them.
- Single-gene, two-allele, fully dominant traits are the simple case. Eye colour, height and skin colour are controlled by many genes (and affected by environment), so real eye colour does not follow a 3 : 1 rule.
- The meiosis board shows 3 pairs and leaves out crossing over.

## Common misconceptions

- A gene is the same thing as a trait ("the blue-eyes gene" as if there were one per colour).
- Dominant alleles are more common, or "stronger", or better.
- A 3 : 1 ratio means exactly 3 of 4 children will show the dominant trait.
- Meiosis makes identical gametes.
