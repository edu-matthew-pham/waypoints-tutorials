## The idea in plain words

- Individuals in a population **vary**: measure any characteristic and you get a spread of values. Much of that variation is inherited (genes), some comes from the environment.
- **Natural selection:** individuals vary; more are born than survive; those whose characteristics suit the environment are more likely to **survive and reproduce**; they pass on their alleles; over many generations, the population's characteristics shift.
- Individuals don't change to suit the environment: the **population** changes, because survivors have more offspring.
- Selection needs variation that already exists; new variation comes from mutations and sexual reproduction.

## Where the picture breaks

- The beetles inherit colour as the average of their parents plus a small random change: a simplification of many genes.
- Birds hunt by colour contrast alone; real predators use more cues, and real selection acts on many characteristics at once.
- Each generation is a hunt then a breed; real generations overlap.

## Common misconceptions

- Organisms change because they need to ("the beetles turned dark to hide").
- Individuals evolve during their lifetime.
- Natural selection creates the variation.
- The fittest are the strongest, rather than the best suited to that environment.
