## The idea in plain words

Everything is made of **particles**, far too small to see. The particle model has four key ideas:

1. All matter is made of particles.
2. There is **empty space** between them (nothing at all, not even air).
3. They are always **moving**.
4. They **attract** each other.

Solids, liquids and gases are the same particles arranged and moving differently:

| | Arrangement | Motion | Attraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid | close, fixed pattern | vibrate in place | strong |
| Liquid | close, no pattern | slide past each other | weaker |
| Gas | far apart | fast, in all directions | very weak |

## Why it matters

Properties come from these three features: a gas squashes because its particles are far apart; honey flows slowly because its particles attract strongly; a gas is much less dense because far fewer particles fit in each cm³. Every separation technique in the next unit is explained the same way.

## Where the picture breaks

- Particles are drawn as circles, but real particles are not little balls and are much further apart in a gas than any diagram shows.
- Particles never change size. Students who say "particles expand when heated" are describing the spaces, not the particles.
- Ice is an exception: it is less dense than liquid water because its particles lock into an open pattern.

## Common misconceptions

- Matter is continuous, or particles float in something (air, water).
- Particles in a solid don't move.
- Gases rise because they are light.
- Particles squash, shrink or grow.

## Energy and changes of state; the model's limits (nodes 4 and 5)

Melting and boiling take energy in; freezing and condensing give it out. While a substance changes state its temperature stays constant: the energy separates the particles (the flat parts of a heating curve). The particle model explains states, diffusion and pressure but not colour, reactions or electricity, because its particles have no parts.

## Investigate in class

Optional ideas if you run a practical for this unit: pick one, adapt it to your equipment, or use your own.

- **Does the temperature of water change how fast a drop of food colouring spreads?** Change: water temperature (cold, room, warm). Measure: time until the colour looks even. Keep the same: volume of water, size of drop, no stirring. *(Warm tap water is enough; a phone video from above makes timing fair.)*
- **Does the temperature rise while ice is still melting?** Change: time (read every 30 seconds). Measure: temperature of crushed ice in a beaker, stirred. Keep the same: amount of ice, the room. *(Expect a flat stretch near 0 °C until the ice is gone: the heating curve's plateau.)*
- **Does air squash more than water?** Change: what fills a sealed syringe (air, water, sand). Measure: how far the plunger moves under a firm push. Keep the same: syringe, starting volume, push. *(No needles; a finger or cap seals the tip.)*

**Safety and ethics:** warm water only, never boiling; take care with glass thermometers.
