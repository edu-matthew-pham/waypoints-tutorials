---
title: Unit 1 — What does it mean to learn?
---

## Purpose

Students investigate "Does AI help us learn?" without being led to a predetermined verdict. Each lesson complicates the preceding interpretation.

## Three teaching moves

1. **Provocation:** A successful game may not tell us what its creator learned. Ask students to distinguish the product from the evidence about its creator.
2. **Experiential contrast:** Compare supported performance with a fresh attempt. Ask what each shows and what remains uncertain. The classroom exercise is illustrative, not research evidence.
3. **Perspective-taking:** Compare independent skill, design productivity, collaborative participation and informed agency. Ask which purposes of education each position values.

## Concepts worth checking

Performance, understanding, transfer, retention, agency and sociocultural participation are distinct but related. A student can learn through assistance; a successful outcome can also occur without the intended learning. Neither observation alone resolves the course question.

## Dialogue moves

Ask students to state a view charitably before challenging it. Distinguish empirical claims, interpretations and educational values. Revisit earlier reasoning without requiring students to change their opinions.

## Optional artefacts

Refer to the course-level teacher guidance for optional journals, forums, collaborative argument maps and final position statements. This unit does not depend on any of them.
