## The idea in plain words

A **prompt** is the request you give an AI assistant. Saying your goal, the useful context and the kind of help you want (an explanation, steps, a question first, or a hint) changes what comes back. A conversation can also be steered: if the first reply misses, a **follow-up** can fix the specific problem.

## Why this is useful

Students learn to name what is wrong with a reply before reacting to it: it gave the answer away, assumed things that aren't true, used words they don't know, or made a claim that isn't well supported. Each problem has a different fix.

## Where the picture breaks

- Both boards use prepared replies, not a live AI. Real assistants vary and do not always follow instructions.
- A clear prompt cannot guarantee a correct answer, and asking an AI to check itself is not independent checking.
- Longer is not better: extra detail helps only when it would change what good help looks like.

## Common misconceptions

- "There is a magic prompt that always works."
- "If I ask it to be sure, it will be right."
- "More context fixes every problem." It doesn't fix jargon or a shaky claim.
- "Personal details help." They are rarely needed and should stay out.

## Try it yourself (5 minutes)

On lesson 2's board, open "Studying for a test". Try "Give more context" first, then "Question an assumption". Notice which one exposes the shaky claim.

## Questions students ask

**Do I have to be polite to AI?** Politeness is fine, but it doesn't tell the assistant anything about your task.

**What if it ignores my follow-up?** Say so again, more plainly, or use another source. Notice what it actually does.
