---
title: Unit 1 — What do we want from AI?
---
## Purpose

Before judging any tool, a committee agrees what it wants from AI. The unit produces a one-page **charter** (five to seven principles, each with a way to check it's working) and a broad **first policy** for students and staff. Every later decision in the course is tested against the charter, and Unit 4 refines the policy.

## The lessons

1. **What is school for?** Name what learning and school life AI should serve, and sort tasks into "AI can help", "keep the thinking" and "depends on the goal". The same task can sit in different groups for different years.
2. **What would we never accept?** Turn values into principles a decision can be checked against, and test each on quick cases. Wider: the six principles of the Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools.
3. **Write the charter.** Critique a made-up school's charter, then write your own.
4. **A first policy.** A charter says what matters; a policy says what happens, for students and staff. Version 1 is broad on purpose and says how it will be refined. A rule that relies on catching AI use mostly punishes the honest, so "check" means checking the policy works.

## The made-up school

Westbrook School has three aims (independent thinkers; everyone belongs and is treated fairly; care for each other and the world) and a one-paragraph AI policy with gaps on purpose: no principles, no one who checks it, no review date, and nothing about AI that arrives on its own. Students meet it in lesson 1 and keep returning to it.

## Everyday picture

A team's values ("we play fair, everyone gets a game") before choosing tactics. Tactics change every week; the values help you choose them.

## Where the picture breaks

A team can pick its players; a school can't always pick its AI. Unit 3 is about that.

## Cautions

Students may want verdicts ("ban it", "allow everything"). Keep asking "which principle is that, and how would we check it's working?". Steer away from enforcement by detection: schools can't see home use, and AI detectors are unreliable. Don't survey students' private AI use; talk about tasks and tools, not individuals.

## Staff committee

The four sessions work as they are for staff; for lesson 4, start from the school's existing policy. Use the school's real aims and AI policy from the start (the made-up school is optional), and bring the national framework into lesson 2.
