## Purpose

The committee refines its first policy (lesson 4) into version 2, using the evidence from Units 2 and 3. The product is a **policy proposal** for whoever owns the school's policy.

## The lesson

11. **Refine the policy.** Sort draft lines into too vague, about right and too prescribed. Move fast-changing detail out of the policy into termly guidelines or task instructions. Rewrite lines that rely on catching people so they work through task design and honest acknowledgement.

## Two balances

- **Vague vs prescribed.** Too vague can't decide a case; too prescribed (named apps, versions, every allowed use) is out of date within weeks and removes teacher judgement. Three layers solve most of it: **policy** (stable), **guidelines** (updated each term: approved tools, settings, year-level expectations), **task instructions** (set by the teacher: how much AI this task allows).
- **Checkable vs policed.** A school can check that its policy works (learning holds up without AI, reports come in, nobody is treated unfairly). It mostly can't catch individual AI use: home use is invisible and detectors are unreliable, wrongly flagging second-language writers most. A workable policy relies on task design, rewarding honesty, and students understanding why the thinking matters, with enforcement kept for clear cases.

## Everyday picture

A road: the law says "drive to the conditions" (policy), the signs change with roadworks (guidelines), and the driver judges this corner (task). Police can't watch every corner, so roads are designed to make the safe choice easy.

## Where the picture breaks

Students don't need a licence, and a school's "road design" is mostly the tasks teachers set.

## Cautions

Keep real detector product names out of student discussion. If students raise their own experience of being accused, keep it general and follow up privately.

## Staff committee

This is the drafting session for staff: work line by line on the real policy, with whoever owns it in the room.
