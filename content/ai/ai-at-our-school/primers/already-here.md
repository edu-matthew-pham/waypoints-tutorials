title: Unit 3 — When AI is already here
---
## Purpose

Most AI reaches students without a committee choosing it: an AI answer at the top of a search page, a new button in an app the school already uses, an app from home. A yes-or-no on each tool doesn't fit. The committee needs a way to **respond**, and to notice what arrives next. The unit ends with a **response plan** and a review cycle.

## The lessons

8. **Map it.** Sort Westbrook's tools: we chose it, it arrived (a feature switched on in a tool we use), or students bring it.
9. **Respond, not just gatekeep.** Choose a response for each item (teach a habit, a class norm, change the task, change a setting, offer an alternative, ask the vendor, restrict, or wait and review by a date) and check it against the charter. A made-up search page with an AI answer above the results, for Year 7 and for Year 10.
10. **Keep it under review.** Who notices new AI, how students report problems, when everything is revisited, who holds the map, and whether a problem is a rule problem (fix the rule) or a know-how problem (show people how).

## Everyday picture

Weather, not a gate. You can't stop rain; you choose a coat, change the plan, or stay in, and you check the forecast again tomorrow.

## Where the picture breaks

People decide how AI features behave, and a school can ask for changes. Rain can't be asked.

## Cautions

Map tools and tasks, not people. Never survey individual students' private AI use; gather reports from teachers and from students about tools in general. Real controls (what an admin can switch off) change fast: check them on the vendor's own pages and date them.

## Staff committee

This unit is often the most useful one for staff: the map and the response plan sit naturally beside the school's policy review.
