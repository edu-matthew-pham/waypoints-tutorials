## Purpose

Someone proposes a tool. The committee judges it against its charter and recommends: adopt, trial, change something first, or don't use it, with conditions and what would change its mind. The unit ends with a one-page **recommendation**.

## The lessons

5. **Does it help learning?** What problem it solves, what kind of AI it is, and whether the evidence shows learning (compared with what, measured when). StudyPal's pitch and its one small study.
6. **Can we trust it, and who does it affect?** StudyPal's privacy page, settings (chats used to train the AI, on by default) and terms (13 and over); students it may help or leave out.
7. **Recommend.** A decision with conditions, checked principle by principle.

## The made-up tool

**StudyPal**, an AI study app pitched to Westbrook. Its claim ("students scored 30% higher") comes from one small study of homework scores while using the app, with students choosing whether to use it. Its terms say 13 and over, so some Year 7s can't sign up. The same material runs through all three lessons.

## Everyday picture

Buying a bike for a younger sibling: does it fit them (not just you), is it safe, what does it cost to keep going, and what would make you return it?

## Where the picture breaks

A bike's effects are easy to see. Whether a tool helps learning shows up later, on a task done without it.

## Cautions

Keep real product names out of student work in class unless the teacher has checked the vendor's own pages and dated them. No student accounts are made in these lessons; any real trial follows the school's approval process and is teacher-run.
