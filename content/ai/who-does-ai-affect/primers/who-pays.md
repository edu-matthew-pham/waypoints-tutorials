title: Unit 3 — Who pays the cost?
---
## Purpose

Costs that users don't see: energy and water, the people who label data, and the people whose work trained the AI.

## The lessons

1. **Energy and water:** one prompt is small (about 0.24–0.34 Wh in 2025 estimates; images far more), totals are large and rising; water figures range from 0.26 mL to about 50 mL per prompt depending on what is counted. **Re-check the figures every January.**
2. **Who labels the data?** The TIME report on Sama workers in Nairobi, with Sama's rebuttal; "low pay" needs a comparison. Typical: Fairwork's platform ratings. Wider: how insecure work shapes labels.
3. **Whose work trained it?** Copyright and AI training is being decided now. Settled in Australia: copyright needs a human author; no text-and-data-mining exception. Typical: music creators' worries and the Bartz v Anthropic settlement.

## Everyday picture

A cheap T-shirt: you see the price, not the water, the factory workers or the designer whose idea was copied.

## Where the picture breaks

AI's costs are spread across many countries and companies, and the figures are estimates that change fast.

## Cautions

The labelled material in the Sama case was graphic: describe it only as harmful text. Every number is dated; teach "who measured it, when, and what did they count?".
