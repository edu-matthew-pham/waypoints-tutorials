title: Unit 2 — Who decides?
---
## Purpose

Three ways a system can decide or act for people, each with its own kind of failure and its own safeguard.

## The lessons

1. **When a formula marks you** (rules written by people): England's 2020 exam grades. One rule applied to everyone goes wrong for a whole group at once; people need someone who checks, a fair appeal, and someone responsible. Typical: a Services Australia promotion round (11 promotions overturned on review). Wider: Robodebt, automation without AI.
2. **Learned from the past** (predict or sort): Amazon's experimental résumé tool marked down "women's". A model trained on past decisions copies their patterns. Typical: a 2024 test of AI résumé ranking with identical résumés. Wider: a health algorithm that predicted cost instead of illness.
3. **When AI acts** (agents): Air Canada was responsible for its chatbot's advice; a made-up booking agent shows which actions need a person to confirm first. Typical: Australian guidance on human control. Wider: lab tests versus real events.

## Everyday picture

A referee (rules), a coach who picks the team from last year's stats (learned from the past), and an assistant with your bank card (acts). Each needs a different check.

## Where the picture breaks

Real systems mix kinds: a hiring process can use rules, a model and an agent at once.

## Cautions

Race, gender and money come up. Keep the focus on systems, examples and safeguards, not on any group's abilities or on individual stories.
