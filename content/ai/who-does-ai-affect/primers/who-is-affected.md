title: Unit 1 — Who is affected?
---
## Purpose

Two moves set up the whole course. First, name the kind of AI (rules written by people, learns to predict or sort, makes new content, takes actions), because each goes wrong differently. Second, the **lens**: who gains, who loses or could be harmed, who decides, and what's the catch? Every later lesson uses both.

## The lessons

1. **Which kind of AI is it?** A short recap of AI Foundations › What kind of AI is it?, with this course's cases.
2. **Who is affected, and how?** The lens on a real benefit: AI-assisted breast screening in Sweden cut doctors' reading work by about 44% with no more false alarms (one study, one country). Typical: skin-cancer AI should complement specialists. Wider: AI weather forecasting, and checking which services really use it.

## Everyday picture

The lens is like asking about a new school bus route: who gets a shorter trip, who now walks further, who chose the route, and what nobody has checked yet.

## Where the picture breaks

Real systems affect people the lens can miss (families, taxpayers, people in other countries). Ask "who's missing?" as a fifth question with older students.

## Cautions

Benefits get the lens too: "for whom, and what's the catch?" Avoid making AI the villain or the hero; the course teaches judgement, not a position.
