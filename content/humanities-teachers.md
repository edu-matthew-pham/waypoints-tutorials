## What is here

History, Geography, Civics and Economics all ask students to question, find and share. These short lessons teach those skills once, on made-up cases, so your own unit can spend its time on content.

**This is a preview.** Nothing here has been tried with a class yet.

- [**Investigate like a humanities expert**](../hass/inquiry-7/): nine lessons. *Asking questions* (what makes a question worth investigating, building one from a subject's big ideas, sizing it) and *Finding information* (where to look, what to keep, how to organise it). *Sharing what you found* adds using subject vocabulary, explaining with evidence and making an argument. Each has models, checks and writing in the student's own document.
- [**Before Year 7: HASS**](../hass/before-year-7/): two short Year 6 catch-ups, *Questions that guide an inquiry* and *Find it and sort it*, for a class that needs them first.

## How it fits your unit

- **Dip in, don't do it first.** Use a lesson when your unit reaches that stage. Each lesson has an "In each subject" note on what differs: History asks why and how we know, Geography where and why there, Economics choices and trade-offs, Civics who decides.
- **One running case per subject** (a tuckshop, a council road, a river town, a museum object), so every subject sees itself.
- **The full investigation is class work.** The site teaches the skills; the real question, real sources and finished response are yours. Printable inquiry outlines for each subject are planned, not built.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class, so how long to spend and how much extra to use is your call. Extra steps (marked typical or wider) are optional width on the same idea. Longer written tasks are done in the student's own book, not on the page, and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## Where it sits

The lessons are tagged to the Year 7 inquiry skills standards of all four subjects (questions, locating and organising, and creating descriptions, explanations and arguments), so each subject's curriculum map finds them. Skills that belong to one subject, such as History's sources or Geography's data, stay in that subject.
