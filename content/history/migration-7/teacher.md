## Deep time Australia (AC9HH7K02 to K07)

The last unit of this course lists AC9HH7K02 to K07 as taught in class. They are about the deep history of First Nations Australians. This site writes no lessons and no student explanation for them, on purpose: First Nations histories and cultures should be taught with local, community-approved material and resources led by First Nations people, and we have not yet consulted anyone to write it with them.

- **Start from ACARA.** Each of its rows links to that standard's curriculum page, which shows its waypoints and ACARA's elaborations. The elaborations are examples, not a checklist.
- **Ask your school first.** Your school's First Nations education contact, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander education officer or Reconciliation Action Plan (RAP) group is the first stop for local, community-approved material and for who to invite or consult.
- **Follow protocols.** Check local protocols before using images or voices of people who have died, information about sacred or significant sites, and anything about ancestral remains.
- **National starting points** (check with your school's contacts before using):
  - [AIATSIS: Guide to evaluating and selecting education resources](https://aiatsis.gov.au/education/guide-evaluating-and-selecting-education-resources), for judging whether a resource is respectful and accurate.
  - [Narragunnawali: Reconciliation in Education](https://www.narragunnawali.org.au/) (Reconciliation Australia), with curriculum resources and support for a school RAP.

The skills lessons elsewhere in History (sources, evidence, dating) are written so they can be used beside this work. Where they need an example from First Nations history, they use the custodians' own words and a link, not an explanation of ours. A First Nations educator should review the History courses before they are used beyond CGGS.
