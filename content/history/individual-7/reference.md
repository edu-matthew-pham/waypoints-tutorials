These lessons use **Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor of China,** as the worked example. If your class studies a different person, use their card with the same questions: **Who were they, when did they live, and what position did they hold? What did they achieve, and how did each achievement change things? What lasted after them, and how did people at the time and later writers see them?**

Each card is a short summary to start from, not a full account. Check it against your class text. Dates for the ancient world are often approximate.

## Ancient China

### Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor

- **When and position:** born about 259 BCE; King of Qin from 246 BCE, at about 13; emperor from 221 BCE, after Qin defeated the last rival kingdoms; died 210 BCE.
- **Achievements:** one standard script; standard weights, measures, cart axles and coins; roads, canals and joined northern walls; one system of laws run by appointed officials in commanderies and counties.
- **How he was seen:** his own stone inscriptions praise him for peace and order; Han writers, including Sima Qian, describe him as harsh and cruel. The story of the burning of books (213 BCE) and killing of scholars comes from Sima Qian, writing about a century later, and historians treat its details with care.
- **Legacy:** the Qin fell in 206 BCE, but the Han kept his officials, script and measures.
- **Sources to look for:** his tomb and the clay army; inscribed Qin weights; Qin bamboo-slip laws; Sima Qian's history.

### Confucius (an alternative China card)

- **When and position:** traditionally 551 to 479 BCE, in the state of Lu; held official posts in Lu, then taught students for much of his life.
- **Achievements:** taught that people should become good through learning, respect for family and proper behaviour, and that rulers should govern by example.
- **How he was seen:** his students and later followers collected his sayings in the *Analects*, which took shape over several generations after his death. Han rulers valued texts linked to him, and scholars of those texts were given government teaching posts.
- **Sources to look for:** the *Analects*; Sima Qian's account of his life. The two sometimes disagree, for example about how senior his posts were.

## Ancient Egypt

### Hatshepsut

- **When and position:** ruled about 1479 to 1458 BCE, first as regent for her young stepson Thutmose III, then as king herself; statues and reliefs showed her as a male king.
- **Achievements:** sent a trading expedition to Punt; built her temple at Deir el-Bahri, near the Valley of the Kings.
- **How she was seen:** after her death, her name and images were removed from many monuments, most likely under Thutmose III. Historians argue about why.
- **Sources to look for:** the reliefs at Deir el-Bahri (including the Punt expedition); her statues.

### Ramesses II

- **When and position:** pharaoh about 1279 to 1213 BCE, one of the longest reigns in Egyptian history.
- **Achievements:** fought the Hittites at the Battle of Kadesh (1274 BCE), which historians think was closer to a draw than the victory he claimed; made a peace treaty with the Hittites (about 1258 BCE); built the temples at Abu Simbel, the Ramesseum and a new capital, Pi-Ramesses.
- **How he was seen:** his temple walls show him as a great warrior king; later Egyptians remembered him as Ramesses the Great.
- **Sources to look for:** the Kadesh scenes and texts on his temples; the peace treaty with the Hittites.

## Ancient Greece

### Pericles

- **When and position:** about 495 to 429 BCE, in Athens; elected as one of the city's generals almost every year for many years.
- **Achievements:** led Athens at the height of its power; under his leadership the Parthenon and other temples on the Acropolis were built (the Parthenon from 447 BCE); led Athens into war with Sparta.
- **How he was seen:** the historian Thucydides, who lived at the time, admired him, and wrote that Athens was a democracy in name but in practice was led by its first citizen, Pericles. Plutarch, writing about 500 years later, told stories of his life.
- **Sources to look for:** Thucydides' history, including the funeral speech he gives to Pericles; Plutarch's *Life of Pericles*; the Parthenon itself.

### Socrates

- **When and position:** about 469 to 399 BCE, in Athens; a thinker who questioned people in public, with no official position.
- **Achievements:** taught by asking questions; his ideas reached later thinkers through his students, especially Plato.
- **How he was seen:** he wrote nothing himself. Plato and Xenophon admired him; Aristophanes mocked him in a comic play, *Clouds*. In 399 BCE an Athenian court found him guilty of disrespecting the gods and corrupting young people, and sentenced him to death.
- **Sources to look for:** Plato's dialogues; Xenophon; Aristophanes' *Clouds*. Scholars disagree about how closely Plato's Socrates matches the real man.

## Ancient Rome

### Augustus

- **When and position:** 63 BCE to 14 CE; Julius Caesar's adopted heir; given the title Augustus by the Senate in 27 BCE and seen as the first Roman emperor.
- **Achievements:** ended years of civil war; kept a long peace across much of the empire; rebuilt and built many temples and public buildings; set up a paid professional army.
- **How he was seen:** he wrote his own list of achievements, the *Res Gestae*, to be set up in bronze outside his tomb. The historian Tacitus, writing about a century later, reports harsh judgements too, including the violence of his rise.
- **Sources to look for:** the *Res Gestae* (a copy survives on a temple in Ankara); Tacitus; Suetonius; coins and statues.

### Julius Caesar

- **When and position:** 100 BCE to 44 BCE; elected consul; conquered Gaul (completed 52 BCE); crossed the Rubicon into Italy with his army in 49 BCE, starting a civil war; made dictator for life in 44 BCE.
- **Achievements:** conquest of Gaul; reformed the calendar (the Julian calendar, from 45 BCE).
- **How he was seen:** senators who feared he would rule as a king killed him on 15 March 44 BCE; his heir Octavian later had him declared a god.
- **Sources to look for:** Caesar's own account of the war in Gaul; Suetonius; Plutarch; Cicero's letters.

## Ancient India

### Chandragupta Maurya

- **When and position:** ruled about 321 to 297 BCE; founded the Mauryan Empire, the first empire to cover most of the Indian subcontinent.
- **Achievements:** built a large, centrally run empire with his adviser Kautilya (also called Chanakya); made a treaty with Seleucus, one of Alexander the Great's successors, gaining land in return for 500 elephants.
- **How he was seen:** no account from his own time survives. A Greek ambassador at his court, Megasthenes, wrote about the empire; his book is lost, but later writers quote it.
- **Sources to look for:** the *Arthashastra* (a book on running a state linked to Kautilya); Greek and Roman writers quoting Megasthenes.

### Ashoka

- **When and position:** Chandragupta's grandson; ruled about 268 to 232 BCE.
- **Achievements:** after a bloody war in Kalinga (about 260 BCE), his edicts say he regretted the killing; he promoted *dhamma*, good conduct such as kindness and truthfulness, and supported Buddhism.
- **How he was seen:** Buddhist writings kept stories about him for centuries. His own edicts, carved on rocks and pillars, could not be read until James Prinsep worked out the script in 1837, which changed how historians saw him.
- **Sources to look for:** his rock and pillar edicts; the stupa at Sanchi; Buddhist chronicles.
