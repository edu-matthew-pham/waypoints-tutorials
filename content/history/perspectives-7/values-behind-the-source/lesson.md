A source can tell you about more than its maker. It can show the **attitudes and values** of their whole society: what people thought was right, important or normal. Historians call this reading **the world behind the source**.

Values show up in three places:

- **the words** a source uses,
- **the pictures and symbols** it uses,
- **what it takes for granted**: things it doesn't bother to explain, because everyone then accepted them.

**Confucius** (551 to 479 BCE) was a teacher. After he died, his followers collected his sayings in a book, the *Analects*, and officials in Han China studied it closely. One saying, in the words the Australian Curriculum uses, is:

> "Women and underlings are especially difficult to handle."

*Underlings* means servants and people of low rank. We are reading this saying as evidence of past attitudes, not because it is right.

## Predict: what can it show?

```step
stage: predict
insight: "Committing first shows whether students stop at judging the saying or go on to ask what it shows about the society."
check:
  type: predict
  q: "What can a saying like this tell a historian?"
  options:
    - "What some people at the time thought about who should be in charge"
    - "Nothing, because it is unfair by today's standards"
    - "Probably how most women and servants really behaved at home"
    - "What Confucius thought, and nothing about anyone else then"
  actual: "It shows attitudes of the time about rank and who should manage whom. It was collected, taught and studied for centuries, so it tells us about more than one man. Today we can also say it is unfair; both can be true."
```

## Worked example: words, pictures, what it takes for granted

| Where to look | In the Confucius saying |
|---|---|
| The words | "Handle": women and servants are to be managed |
| Who is speaking, to whom | A respected teacher, to male students |
| What it takes for granted | A household where a man is in charge and everyone has a rank |
| So the value it shows | Order by rank, with men of rank at the top |

Pictures work the same way. In 1972, archaeologists opened a Han tomb at **Mawangdui**, in Changsha. It held a T-shaped painting on silk, about 2 metres long, made about 2,200 years ago for a noblewoman, Lady Xin Zhui. From top to bottom it shows:

- **Heaven**: the sun (with a crow in it) and the moon (with a toad and a hare)
- **The lady**: with attendants around her; below, her family make offerings for her
- **The world below**: strange creatures that were believed to guard the body

You can see the painting on the [Hunan Museum's page](https://web.hnmuseum.com/en/node/7370).

## Vary: one source, everyone?

```step
stage: modify
insight: "The opposite slip, one source standing for a whole society, checks that students keep a value to the people who held it."
check:
  type: question
  q: "Mei says: 'Confucius said it, so every person in Han China believed women and servants were hard to handle.' What is the best reply?"
  options:
    - "It shows a view taught to officials, not what everyone believed"
    - "That is right, because Confucius was the most famous teacher"
    - "No, because Confucius lived before the Han, so it tells us nothing"
    - "No, because sayings never show what a society valued"
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - "Being famous and studied makes it important, not shared by everyone."
    - "Han officials studied the Analects closely, so it does tell us about Han values."
    - "Sayings can show values. The question is whose."
  explain: "A source shows values held by some people at the time. How widely they were held needs more evidence."
```

## Typical: a face on a coin

Roman coins usually carried the emperor's head and his titles. Everyone who paid for bread held one.

```step
stage: make
tier: typical
insight: "A coin shows values through a symbol in another society, and makes 'what it takes for granted' concrete."
check:
  type: question
  q: "What does putting the emperor's head on everyday coins suggest about Roman values?"
  options:
    - "Rulers wanted everyone, every day, to know who was in charge"
    - "Romans thought coins had to show a person to be worth money"
    - "Every Roman agreed the emperor was a good ruler"
    - "Coins were made only for the emperor's own use"
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - "Coins could show other things too. Why choose the ruler?"
    - "A coin shows what the ruler wanted people to see, not what they all thought."
    - "Ordinary people used them every day; that's the point."
  explain: "Symbols on everyday things show what a society's rulers wanted taken for granted."
```

## Wider: judging the past

  options:
    - "Describe the attitude in its own time first, then judge it"
    - "Judge it by today's standards, since that is what matters now"
    - "Never judge the past, because people then knew no better"
    - "Change the words so the saying sounds fair today"
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - "That tells us about us. What about them?"
    - "Historians can judge; they describe first, so the judgement is fair to the evidence."
    - "Changing a source destroys the evidence."
  explain: "Describe first, in its own time, then judge, so the judgement rests on an accurate picture."
