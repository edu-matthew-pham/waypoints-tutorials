title: "People and power: swap-in cards"
---
These lessons use **ancient China in the Qin and Han period** as the worked example. If your class studies a different society, use its card with the same questions: **What were the layers? How did the people at the top use their power? What did women do, and what evidence survives? Who did the work at the bottom, and what changed?**

Each card is a short summary to start from, not a full account. Check it against your class text.

## Ancient China (Qin and Han)

- **Layers:** the emperor; nobles (relatives and allies with titles, and in the early Han, kingdoms); officials appointed by the emperor; farmers (most people); craftworkers, merchants and soldiers; convicts and slaves.
- **How power worked:** commanderies and counties run by officials; household registers; grain tax; written laws; called-up labour and army service.
- **Women:** most evidence was written by men at court. Ban Zhao (scholar, about 45 to 117 CE) and Empress Lü (regent, 195 to 180 BCE) are exceptions; tomb figures show women's work.
- **Change:** the conscripts' revolt of 209 BCE (according to Sima Qian); heavy taxes on merchants and the salt and iron monopolies under Emperor Wu; Wang Mang's ban on buying and selling land and slaves (9 CE), cancelled in 12 CE after powerful families opposed it.
- **Sources to look for:** Qin bamboo-slip laws; Sima Qian's history; Ban Zhao's *Lessons for Women*.

## Ancient Egypt

- **Layers:** the Pharaoh; viziers (chief officials); priests; nomarchs (regional governors); scribes; soldiers; craftworkers and merchants; farmers; slaves.
- **How power worked:** scribes recorded harvests, taxes and workers; officials organised labour on temples and tombs.
- **Women:** some women owned property and made contracts; many paintings and funerary texts show women of wealthy families.
- **Change:** power shifted between the Pharaoh and regional governors over the centuries.
- **Sources to look for:** tomb paintings; funerary texts; scribes' own writings, such as the *Instruction of Amenemope*.

## Athens and Sparta

- **Athens:** adult male citizens could vote in the assembly and owed military service; women, slaves and foreigners living there could not vote. Many women of wealthy families were expected to stay mostly at home.
- **Sparta:** Spartiates (full citizens, trained as soldiers); perioikoi (free but not citizens, often traders and craftworkers); helots (an enslaved farming population who did the farm work). Spartan women were said to exercise and to manage property.
- **Sources to look for:** pottery paintings; writers such as Xenophon and Plutarch, who were outsiders or wrote much later.

## Ancient Rome

- **Layers:** patricians (old noble families) and plebeians (most citizens); women of different classes; slaves; freedmen (former slaves).
- **How power worked:** the Senate and elected officials in the Republic; later the emperor.
- **Slavery:** many slaves were captured in wars and worked as farm labourers, household servants and gladiators; some were freed, and some freedmen became wealthy.
- **Sources to look for:** inscriptions on tombs of freedmen; Roman law; wall paintings.

## Ancient India

- **Layers as ancient texts describe them:** four varnas: Brahmins (priests and teachers), Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (merchants and artisans) and Shudras (labourers and peasants). This is the ideal order the texts set out; historians compare it with other evidence of how people actually lived, which was more varied.
- **Women:** texts describe roles in marriage, family life, work and education, which varied by period and group.
- **Sources to look for:** ancient law and religious texts; inscriptions; Greek visitors' accounts, such as Megasthenes (preserved by later writers).
