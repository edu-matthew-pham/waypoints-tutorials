**Stratigraphy** means studying the layers at a site. Where layers have stayed in place, lower layers formed before the ones above them. That gives a relative order: older or younger, without a calendar date. Archaeologists record where things were found as well as the things themselves.

**A real source:** UNESCO's [account of the First Qin Emperor's mausoleum](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/441) describes terracotta figures, horses, chariots and weapons. These physical finds can help investigate early society. Our layer exercise below is invented; it does not represent that site's excavation record.

## Predict: commit to a claim

```step
stage: predict

  q: Which layer formed first?
  options:
    - C, the top layer

```



## Try: sort the evidence

```step
stage: run
insight: Separate the kinds of evidence or claims before using them together.
check:
  type: sort
  q: What does each statement claim?
  options:
    - Relative order
    - Exact date
  items:
    - - A formed before B
      - Relative order
      - It compares two layers.
    - - The fire happened in 220 BCE
      - Exact date
      - That needs a calendar date, which the layers alone do not supply.
    - - C formed after B
      - Relative order
      - The new floor is above the burnt material.
```

Compare the result with your prediction. What would you keep or change?

## Worked example: follow the reasoning

```step
stage: investigate
insight: Test the main inference, then repair the planted misconception before carrying it into a new case.
tasks:
  - check:
      type: question
      q: Which account fits the sequence best?
      options:
        - The new floor was laid before the earlier floor
        - A fire may have happened between two periods of use
        - An invading army certainly caused the fire
      answer: 1
      explain: A, B and C support a sequence of use, burning and later use. The cause of the burning is still open.
      feedback:
        - A lies below C in an undisturbed sequence.
        - null
        - Charcoal does not identify an attacker.
  - check:
      type: question
      q: "Nib writes: “B is deeper than C, so B is exactly 500 years older.” What needs changing?"
      options:
        - Keep the order but remove the exact time gap
        - Reverse the order because deeper means newer
        - Keep the time gap because three layers prove it
      answer: 0
      explain: Depth can establish relative order here. It does not measure a number of years.
      feedback:
        - null
        - The layers are undisturbed, so the lower one formed first.
        - Layers can accumulate at different rates.
      fault: logic
      misconception: layers-give-exact-date
    smaller:
      check:
        type: question
        q: Can a stack of layers alone tell you how many years passed?
        options:
          - Yes, count the layers
          - No, it gives an order
        answer: 1
        explain: The order is useful even when the time gap is unknown.
        feedback:
          - Look at what the evidence actually records.
          - null
    hint:
      - Check whether Nib is comparing order or measuring time.
      - What would you need to attach years to this sequence?
```

The earlier floor lies below the burnt material. A later floor lies above it. A fire between two periods of use is one interpretation. The burnt material alone does not show who started a fire or whether there was an attack.

## Vary: change one condition

```step
stage: modify
insight: Keep the same idea while testing a changed condition.
check:
  type: question
  q: Which detail should you record before dating that coin from its depth?
  options:
    - Its colour alone
    - Its position inside the later pit
    - How heavy it feels
  answer: 1
  explain: The pit disturbed the original sequence. Depth alone would give the wrong order.
  feedback:
    - Colour does not show whether the layer was disturbed.
    - null
    - Weight does not establish the sequence.
```

Change one condition: a later pit cuts through C and B into A. A recent coin falls into its filling. The coin is deep, but the pit is later than the layers it cuts.

## Practise: a fresh case

```step
stage: practise
insight: Apply the same test independently to another case.
check:
  type: question
  q: At a second made-up, undisturbed site, a floor lies below flood mud and a rebuilt wall lies above it. Which account is supported?
  options:
    - Flooding came before any floor existed
    - The wall proves why the flood happened
    - A period of use was followed by flooding and rebuilding
  answer: 2
  explain: The positions give a sequence. They do not give the cause or an exact year.
  feedback:
    - The floor is below the flood deposit.
    - A wall does not explain the cause of a flood.
    - null
```
