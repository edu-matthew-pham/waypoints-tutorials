## The idea in plain words

A **ratio** compares quantities in a stated order: 2 red to 3 blue is $2 : 3$. **Part to part** (red to blue) is $2 : 3$; **part to whole** (red to all) is $2 : 5$, the same as the fraction $\tfrac{2}{5}$.

Two ratios are **equivalent** when both terms are multiplied (or divided) by the same number: $2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9$. **Simplest form** divides both terms by their highest common factor: $12 : 18 = 2 : 3$.

To **share a total in a ratio**, add the parts ($2 + 3 = 5$), divide the total by the parts to get one part ($\$60 \div 5 = \$12$), and multiply for each share ($\$24$ and $\$36$). The shares add back to the total.

## Why it matters

Ratios are everywhere: mixes, maps and scale models, recipes. They lead to rates, proportion and similar shapes.

## What students get wrong

- **Part to whole as part to part.** 4 apples and 3 pears is $4 : 7$ for apples to all the fruit, not $4 : 3$.
- **Adding to scale.** $2 : 3$ as $4 : 5$. Both terms are multiplied, not added to.
- **Dividing by one term.** Sharing $60 in 2 : 3 as 60 ÷ 2 and 60 ÷ 3. Add the parts first.

## The words

**ratio**, **term**, **part to part**, **part to whole**, **equivalent ratios**, **simplest form**, **share**, **scale**.
