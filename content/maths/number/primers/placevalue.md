title: Place value and estimating in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

Each place value column is ten times the one on its right, so each is a **power of 10**: ones $10^0 = 1$, tens $10^1$, hundreds $10^2$, thousands $10^3$. The exponent counts from 0 at the ones column. **Expanded notation** writes a number as digit times power of 10, added up: $4052 = 4 \times 10^3 + 5 \times 10^1 + 2 \times 10^0$.

To **round** a decimal to a number of decimal places, find the two numbers with that many places it sits between and take the nearer. Look at the one digit straight after the place: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less leaves it; halfway rounds up. How much to round depends on the job: to the cent for money, up when you need enough (tins of paint), to what the ruler can show for a measurement.

An **estimate** rounds each number to its first digit, keeping its size: $38 \times 52 \approx 40 \times 50 = 2000$. Compare it with the exact answer to check it is reasonable.

## Why it matters

Place value as powers of 10 leads to scientific notation and to decimals (the columns continue as $10^{-1}$, $10^{-2}$). Rounding and estimating are the checks that catch a decimal point in the wrong place.

## What students get wrong

- **Counting the exponent from 1.** The tens as $10^2$. Count from 0 at the ones.
- **Cutting digits off.** 3.746 to 2 places as 3.74. Look at the next digit.
- **Rounding twice.** 2.349 to 1 place as 2.35 then 2.4. Only the digit straight after the place counts.
- **Losing the size when estimating.** 19.6 as 2, not 20.
