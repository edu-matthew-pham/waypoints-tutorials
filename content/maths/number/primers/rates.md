## The idea in plain words

A **rate** compares two quantities measured in **different units**: 60 km per hour, $4.50 per kilogram, 80 beats per minute. A **ratio** compares quantities of the **same** kind (2 cups to 3 cups). The **unit rate** is the amount for one of the second quantity: divide the first by the second, and keep both units.

A rate works both ways: total = rate × amount, and amount = total ÷ rate. For speed, distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. Writing the units in each step (km ÷ km/h = h) shows whether to multiply or divide. Speeds convert with 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s, so divide km/h by 3.6 to get m/s.

## Why it matters

Prices, speeds, fuel use, exchange rates and heart rates are all rates. Comparing unit rates is how to find the best buy.

## What students get wrong

- **Dividing the wrong way round** ($0.25 per kg for 3 kg at $12).
- **Multiplying distance by speed to find a time.**
- **Multiplying by 3.6 to get m/s.** m/s is the smaller number.
- **Calling a rate a ratio.** Different units make it a rate.

## The words

**rate**, **unit rate**, **per**, **km/h**, **m/s**, **best buy**, **exchange rate**.
