## The idea in plain words

Fractions are **equivalent** when they name the same amount: multiply or divide the top and the bottom by the same number ($\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{8}{12}$). Dividing by the highest common factor gives the **simplest form** ($\tfrac{12}{18} = \tfrac{2}{3}$). A percentage is out of 100 and a decimal counts tenths and hundredths, so one amount has three names: $\tfrac{3}{4} = 0.75 = 75\%$. Divide the top by the bottom for a decimal; × 100 for a percentage.

Every fraction, decimal and mixed numeral has one place on the number line. To find it, split each unit into equal parts (quarters for $\tfrac{3}{4}$, tenths for $0.6$) and count from zero: right for a positive, left for a negative.

A negative mixed numeral or decimal is a whole number and a part, **both** below zero: $-1.5$ is one unit left of zero and then half a unit further left, so it sits between $-2$ and $-1$. Different names can share a place: $-\tfrac{2}{4} = -\tfrac{1}{2} = -0.5$.

Calculating comes next. To **add or subtract**, cut both fractions into the same parts first (a common denominator, the LCM of the bottoms): $\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{4}{12} + \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac{7}{12}$. To **multiply**, multiply the tops and the bottoms ($\tfrac{3}{4}$ of $\tfrac{2}{3}$ is $\tfrac{6}{12}$); to **divide**, count how many fit ($3 \div \tfrac{1}{4} = 12$). A **percentage of an amount** is a multiplication too: 15% of 80 is $0.15 \times 80 = 12$, and 15% off leaves 85%, so $\times 0.85$. The unit ends by choosing a quick strategy: change the order, change the form ($0.75 = \tfrac{3}{4}$), or split a number.

## Why it matters

Scales on thermometers, rulers and graphs are number lines in parts. Ordering mixed fractions and decimals, and later adding and subtracting them, all rest on knowing where each one sits.

## What students get wrong

- **Adding the same number to the top and the bottom.** $\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{3}{4}$. Compare the fraction bars: the amount changes.
- **Reading the digits as the percentage.** $0.5 = 5\%$. 0.5 is 50 hundredths: 50%.
- **Counting the part back towards zero.** −1.5 placed at −0.5, between −1 and 0. Ask whether −1.5 °C is colder or warmer than −1 °C.
- **Adding the tops and the bottoms.** $\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{2}{7}$, which is less than $\tfrac{1}{3}$ alone. Cut both into twelfths.
- **Multiplying always makes bigger.** $\tfrac{3}{4} \times 20$ judged more than 20. A multiplier less than 1 takes part: 15.
- **Multiplying by the part taken off.** 20% off $50 worked out as $50 × 0.2 = $10, the discount. What is left is 80%: × 0.8.
- **Comparing decimals by digits.** −0.3 judged greater than −0.25 because 3 < 25. Write both with the same number of places.

## The words

**fraction**, **decimal**, **mixed numeral** ($1\tfrac{3}{4}$): ways of writing numbers between the whole numbers. **equivalent**: two names for the same number, at the same place on the line.
