title: Classifying shapes in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

Shapes are named by their **properties**, not by how they look.

A triangle gets two names: by its **sides** (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and by its **biggest angle** (acute, right-angled, obtuse). Equal sides sit opposite equal angles, so an equilateral triangle has three 60° angles and can never be right-angled.

A quadrilateral's name lists properties: **trapezium** (at least one pair of parallel sides), **parallelogram** (two pairs), **rhombus** (four equal sides), **rectangle** (four right angles), **square** (both), **kite** (two pairs of equal neighbouring sides). A shape with all of a name's properties belongs to that family: a square is a rectangle and a rhombus.

A **polygon** is named by its number of sides, and is **regular** only when its sides **and** angles are all equal. It is **concave** when a corner points in.

## Why it matters

Definitions by properties are where mathematical reasoning starts: "it is a rectangle, so its diagonals are equal" is an argument. Pythagoras (next in Measurement) needs right-angled triangles named reliably.

## What students get wrong

- **Calling a triangle acute because it has an acute angle.** Every triangle has at least two. The biggest angle decides.
- **A square is not a rectangle.** Its extra property (equal sides) does not remove the rectangle's.
- **Equal sides make a polygon regular.** A rhombus has equal sides, but its angles differ.

## The words

**scalene**, **isosceles**, **equilateral**, **acute**, **right-angled**, **obtuse**, **trapezium**, **parallelogram**, **rhombus**, **kite**, **regular**, **convex**, **concave**, and the polygon names pentagon to decagon.
