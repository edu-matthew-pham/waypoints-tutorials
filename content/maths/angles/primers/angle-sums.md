## The idea in plain words

The three angles of **any** triangle add to 180°. Change the shape and one angle grows while the others shrink by the same amount; change the size and the angles do not change at all. Why: tear off the three corners and put their points together, and they fill a straight line, which is 180°.

Any polygon splits into triangles from one corner: join that corner to every corner not next to it. A shape with $n$ sides makes $n - 2$ triangles, and their angles fill the polygon's corners exactly, so the angle sum is $(n - 2) \times 180°$: 360° for a quadrilateral, 540° for a pentagon, 720° for a hexagon.

## Why it matters

Angle sums give reasons in geometry: a missing angle can be worked out, not measured, and the answer is exact. They also explain which regular shapes tile a floor, and link to the turtle in the coding pathway, which turns 360° in total round any closed shape.

## What students get wrong

- **Bigger triangle, bigger angles.** Size changes the sides, not the angles. The board's angles stay the same when a triangle is enlarged on the grid.
- **Splitting from the middle.** Lines from a middle point make $n$ triangles, and 360° round the middle point gets counted. Split from a corner instead.
- **Measured totals of 179° or 181°.** A protractor reads to about a degree. The true total is exactly 180°.

## The words

**interior angle**: an angle inside a shape at one of its corners. **angle sum**: the interior angles added together. **polygon**: a closed shape with straight sides. **regular**: all sides and all angles equal.
