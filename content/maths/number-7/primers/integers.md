## The idea in plain words

The **integers** are the whole numbers, zero and the negative whole numbers: …, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, … On a number line, negatives sit to the left of zero.

Two ideas carry the unit:

- **Further left is less.** −7 < −2 because −7 is further left, even though 7 is more than 2. Zero is greater than every negative.
- **Adding and subtracting are moves.** Start at the first number. Adding moves the way the second number points: right for a positive, left for a negative. Subtracting moves the opposite way to adding. So 5 − (−3): adding −3 would go left 3, so subtracting it goes right 3, to 8.

## Why it matters

Temperatures, heights above and below sea level, money owed and changes in a score all use negatives. In algebra, every table of values with negative inputs, and every graph in four quadrants, needs these two ideas.

## What students get wrong

- **Comparing by size without the sign.** −8 > −2 "because 8 > 2". The board writes the comparison in words: "−8 is further left, so it is less".
- **Taking the number's sign as the move.** 5 − (−3) = 2, moving left because of the minus sign on 3. The board draws the adding move faintly above a subtraction, so students see the jump turn round.

## The words

**integer**: a whole number, zero, or a negative whole number. **negative**: below zero. **less than** ($<$), **greater than** ($>$): the wide end of the sign faces the greater number. **further from zero**: the size without the sign (later, the absolute value).
