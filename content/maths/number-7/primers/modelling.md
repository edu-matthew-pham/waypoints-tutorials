## The idea in plain words

**Modelling** turns a real problem into maths, solves it, and turns the answer back into the situation. Three steps: **formulate** (what is known, what is unknown, what is the relationship, and which representation: fraction, decimal, percentage or ratio?), **solve** (estimate first, then calculate efficiently, with a calculator where it helps), and **interpret and justify** (the answer in the situation's units, sensibly rounded, and why that representation).

For money, a percentage is always of the **original** amount: profit is a percentage of the cost. Percentages of different amounts do not add: 20% off then 10% off $200 is $200 × 0.8 × 0.9 = $144, not 30% off. For measurement, a mix is shared in parts, and a scale such as $1 : 50$ multiplies from the drawing to the real thing and divides the other way.

## Why it matters

Modelling is where Number meets the real world: shopping, savings, building, maps. The skill that matters is the first step, deciding what the problem is, before any calculation.

## What students get wrong

- **Calculating before formulating.** Ask for known, unknown and relationship first.
- **The wrong base.** Profit out of the selling price, not the cost.
- **Adding percentages of different amounts.**
- **Bare numbers.** 33.6 what? Write $33.60.

## The words

**model**, **formulate**, **interpret**, **justify**, **representation**, **profit**, **markup**, **discount**, **scale**.
