## The idea in plain words

**Volume** counts unit cubes: cm³, m³. A box fills up in layers. Each layer covers the base, so it holds as many cubes as the base has squares; the height counts the layers. So $V = A \times h$, and for a box $V = l \times w \times h$.

Every **right prism** works the same way: it has the same **cross-section** all the way along, like a loaf of bread. Volume = area of the cross-section × length. A triangular prism's cross-section is a triangle, so halve first.

**Capacity** is how much a container holds: 1 cm³ holds 1 mL, and 1000 cm³ = 1 L.

## Why it matters

Water tanks, concrete, packaging and medicine doses are volumes and capacities. The cross-section idea carries straight on to cylinders in Year 9.

## What students get wrong

- **Counting only the outside.** A 4 by 3 by 2 box counted as 26 visible squares. It is full: 2 layers of 12.
- **Forgetting the half for a triangular prism.** Tent end 2 m by 1.5 m worked out as 3 m². It is a triangle: 1.5 m².
- **Calling the face it stands on the cross-section.** A tent stands on a rectangle, but its slices are triangles.
- **Dividing by 100 for litres.** 1000 mL = 1 L.

## Year 8: capacity and prism problems

1 cm³ holds 1 mL, 1000 cm³ holds 1 L and 1 m³ holds 1000 L; a cubic metre is 100 × 100 × 100 cm³, not 100. For any right prism V = A × h (the end's area times the length), and a missing length is V ÷ A. The common slips: converting a cubic unit with the length factor once, and using length × width × height on a triangular prism.

## The words

**volume**, **cubic units** (cm³, m³), **prism**, **cross-section**, **capacity**, **millilitre** (mL), **litre** (L), **kilolitre** (kL), **right prism**.
