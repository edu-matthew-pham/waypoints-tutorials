## The idea in plain words

In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse** $c$, the longest side. Draw a square on each side: the two smaller squares together have exactly the area of the biggest. That is **Pythagoras' theorem**, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.

To find the hypotenuse, square the two shorter sides, add, and take the square root. To find a shorter side, subtract: $b^2 = c^2 - a^2$. Check that the hypotenuse is still the longest.

It also works backwards as a test. With $c$ the longest side: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ means right-angled; smaller means obtuse; bigger means acute. Whole numbers that fit, like 3-4-5 and 5-12-13, are **Pythagorean triples**.

## Why it matters

Ladders, ramps, screens, diagonal paths and checking a corner is square all use it. It leads to distance on a graph, trigonometry and 3D measurement.

## What students get wrong

- **Adding the sides.** 6 + 8 = 14 for the hypotenuse. The squares add: 36 + 64 = 100, c = 10.
- **Stopping at c².** 25 is an area; the side is √25 = 5.
- **Adding for a shorter side.** That gives a side longer than the hypotenuse. Subtract.
- **Testing with the wrong side as c.** c must be the longest side.

## The words

**hypotenuse**, **right-angled triangle**, **Pythagoras' theorem**, **Pythagorean triple**.
