title: Time zones in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

Every place keeps its own clock, set by its **time zone**: an offset from UTC (Coordinated Universal Time). Sydney is UTC+10, or UTC+11 during daylight saving; London is UTC+0, or +1 in its summer.

A city with a **bigger offset is ahead** (its clock is later); a smaller offset is behind. To change a time, find the difference between the offsets, add or take it away, and **change the day** when you cross midnight. For a flight, add the flight time to the departure time (in the departure zone), then change to the arrival zone. **24-hour time** avoids mixing up am and pm.

## Why it matters

Calls to family overseas, online meetings with other states, flights and sports broadcasts all cross time zones. It is also a good workout in adding and taking away times past midnight.

## What students get wrong

- **Adding for a place that is behind.** A smaller offset means an earlier time.
- **The right hour, the wrong day.** Past 24:00 is the next day; below 00:00 is the day before.
- **Adding the flight time but not changing zones.** The answer is still on the departure clock; change it to the arrival zone.
- **Daylight saving.** Offsets change through the year, and not every state uses it: check before planning.

## The words

**time zone**, **UTC**, **offset**, **ahead**, **behind**, **24-hour time**, **duration**, **daylight saving**, **International Date Line**.
