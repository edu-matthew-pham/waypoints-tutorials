title: Straight-line graphs in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

A rule such as $y = 3x + 1$ turns each value of $x$ into a value of $y$. Plot the pairs as points, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 4)$, $(2, 7)$, and they lie on a **straight line**. Any rule of the form $y = mx + c$ gives a straight line.

The two numbers do different jobs:

- $m$, the number in front of $x$, is the **gradient**: how far the line goes up for each step of 1 to the right. A bigger $m$ is steeper; $m = 0$ is flat; a negative $m$ goes down.
- $c$, the number on its own, is where the line crosses the $y$-axis (the value of $y$ when $x = 0$).

## Why it matters

Steepness is a rate: dollars per hour, metres per second, litres per minute. Reading $m$ as "how much more for each one more" is the idea students will use in science graphs, budgets and modelling.

## What students get wrong

- **Swapping m and c.** Reading the number on its own as the steepness, or expecting a bigger $m$ to lift the line. The board shows the line turning about the same point on the $y$-axis as $m$ changes.
- **Any minus sign means "down".** $y = 3x - 2$ still goes up: only a negative $m$ makes the line go down.

## The words

**gradient** ($m$): up for each step right. **$y$-intercept** ($c$): where the line crosses the $y$-axis. **steeper**: goes up (or down) more for each step right.
