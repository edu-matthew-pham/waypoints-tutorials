title: Classification algorithms in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

An **algorithm** is a precise set of steps and decisions that gives the right result every time. A classification algorithm sorts shapes by asking yes/no questions about their sides and angles.

Two things make it work. **Order**: ask the most specific question first, or an equilateral triangle is called isosceles and a square is called a parallelogram. **Coverage**: every yes and every no must lead on to another question or a name.

To check one, **trace** it on one shape of every class. A flowchart, a decision tree and numbered steps are three ways to show the same algorithm.

## Why it matters

Every `if` and `else` in a program is a decision like these. Writing the steps clearly first is how programmers avoid bugs.

## What students get wrong

- **Asking the wider question first**, so specific shapes get a general name.
- **Testing on one shape**, which hides a missing branch.
- **Leaving a branch with no ending.**

## The words

**algorithm**, **attribute**, **decision**, **flowchart**, **decision tree**, **trace**.
