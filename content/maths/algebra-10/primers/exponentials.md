## The idea in plain words

A straight-line (linear) table **adds** the same amount each step. An exponential table **multiplies** by the same number each step. That number is the **ratio** (or base, $b$). The rule is $y = a(b^x)$: $a$ is the starting value (where the graph crosses the $y$-axis) and $b$ is what you multiply by.

If $b$ is more than 1 the graph grows; if $b$ is between 0 and 1 it decays. A decay graph gets closer and closer to the $x$-axis but never reaches it: that line, $y = 0$, is the **asymptote**. To solve $b^x = $ a number, write both sides as powers of the same base and then the indices must match.

## Why it matters

Anything that grows or shrinks by the same share each time follows this pattern: germs on food, a cooling cup of tea, money in a savings account, a social media post being shared. The last hours of growth add far more than the first.

## What students get wrong

- **Checking differences.** An exponential table has a constant *ratio*, not a constant difference.
- **Decay reaches zero.** Halving forever never gets to 0.
- **Reading $b$ as the next value.** $b$ is the ratio, $y(1) \div a$, not $y(1)$.
- **Dividing by the base.** In $3^x = 81$ the 3 is not multiplying $x$, so $x \ne 81 \div 3$.
