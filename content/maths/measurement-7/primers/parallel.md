title: Parallel lines in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

A transversal crossing two parallel lines makes eight angles, four at each crossing. Because the lines go the same way, the bottom crossing is a copy of the top one, so the angles come in pairs:

- **corresponding** (the same place at each crossing, an F shape): equal;
- **alternate** (between the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal, a Z shape): equal;
- **co-interior** (between the lines, on the same side, a C shape): add to $180^\circ$.

With vertically opposite angles and angles on a straight line (Year 6), one angle gives all eight. Each angle found comes with a reason.

## Why it matters

The pairs are the first real geometric reasoning students do: a chain of facts, each justified. They also prove that a triangle's angles add to $180^\circ$ (draw a line through one corner, parallel to the opposite side: two alternate pairs), and they return in construction, surveying and anything built with parallel parts (scissor lifts, roof trusses, shelving).

## What students get wrong

- **Co-interior angles are equal**: the most common slip, because the other two pairs are. The board shows them adding to $180^\circ$; ask "are both the same size?"
- **Vertically opposite named as alternate**: a pair at one crossing is not a parallel-lines pair. Each pair takes one angle from each crossing.
- **Using the rules when the lines are not parallel**: look for the arrows first. The board's tilt slider shows the rules failing.
- **No reason**: an angle without a reason cannot be checked. Ask for the rule's name every time.

## The words

**parallel**: going the same way, never meeting (marked >>). **transversal**: a line crossing two or more lines. **corresponding**, **alternate**, **co-interior**: the three pairs above. **supplementary**: adding to $180^\circ$. **vertically opposite**: opposite each other at a crossing.
