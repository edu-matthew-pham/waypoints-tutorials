title: Irrational numbers in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

A **rational** number is a fraction of integers (whole numbers or their negatives); its decimal stops or repeats. An **irrational** number cannot be written as a fraction; its decimal never stops and never repeats. √2, √3, √5 and π are irrational. The square root of a whole number is irrational unless the number is a perfect square (√16 = 4).

Together the rational and irrational numbers make the **real numbers**: every point on the number line. To place a root, find the perfect squares either side (25 < 30 < 36, so 5 < √30 < 6), then square decimals to get closer. A rounded value (1.414) is an approximation; √2 is exact.

## Why it matters

Exact answers from Pythagoras and circles are often irrational. Knowing when a decimal is only an approximation matters for measuring, building and calculating.

## What students get wrong

- **Every root is irrational.** √16 = 4.
- **π = 22/7 or 3.14 exactly.** Both are rational approximations.
- **Halving to find a root.** √50 is just over 7, not 25.

## The words

**rational**, **irrational**, **real number**, **perfect square**, **approximation**, **exact value**.
