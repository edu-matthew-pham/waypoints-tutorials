title: Index laws in 5 minutes

## The idea in plain words

A power is repeated multiplication: 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 (base 2, index 3). Writing powers out as factors gives the laws for powers of the **same base**: multiplying adds the indices (aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ), dividing subtracts them (pairs cancel), and a power of a power multiplies them ((aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ). Dividing a power by itself gives 1, and the quotient law calls it a⁰, so a⁰ = 1.

Simplify one law at a time: powers of powers first, then multiply and divide from left to right.

## Why it matters

The laws make very large and very small numbers manageable (scientific notation in science), and they are the base for algebra with powers in Years 9 and 10.

## What students get wrong

- **Multiplying the bases.** 3² × 3⁴ = 3⁶, not 9⁶.
- **Joining different bases.** 2³ × 3² stays as 8 × 9 = 72.
- **a⁰ = 0.** It is 1.
- **Adding powers.** 2³ + 2³ = 2⁴, not 2⁶.

## The words

**base**, **index** (exponent, power), **product law**, **quotient law**, **power of a power**, **zero index**, **scientific notation**.
