title: Recurring decimals in 5 minutes
---
## The idea in plain words

A fraction is a division, and dividing it out gives a decimal that either **terminates** (3/8 = 0.375) or **recurs** (1/7 = 0.142857142857…). In long division every remainder is less than the denominator, so either a remainder is 0 (it stops) or one comes back (the digits repeat from there). The repeating block gets a **bar** or **dots**.

You can predict which without dividing: write the fraction in simplest form and break the denominator into primes. Only 2s and 5s: it terminates, because terminating decimals are fractions over 10, 100, 1000… and 10 = 2 × 5.

## Why it matters

It is the first step to the real number system: every fraction is a decimal that stops or repeats, which is what makes irrational numbers (which do neither) different.

## What students get wrong

- **Calling a recurring decimal irrational.** It repeats, so it is a fraction.
- **Not simplifying first.** 3/12 = 1/4 terminates even though 12 has a 3.
- **Bar over the wrong digits.** Only the repeating block.

## The words

**terminating**, **recurring**, **repeating block**, **remainder**, **prime factor**, **simplest form**.
