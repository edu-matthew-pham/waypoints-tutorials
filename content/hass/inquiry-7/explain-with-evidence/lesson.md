An **explanation** answers why or how. Make a claim. Name evidence from a source. Then explain **how the evidence supports the claim**. Don't claim more than the evidence shows.

Every case, number and source card in this lesson is made up for practice. Parkside School's tuckshop can't stock everything. Riverbend Council is thinking about closing Mill Road outside the school. Wattle Flat is a town on the Kilby River in a dry year. The Hartley Museum has a bronze mirror it labels as Han. None of these records tells you anything about a real council, river or ancient society.

## Think first

```step
stage: predict
insight: An explanation connects information to why or how.
check:
  type: predict
  q: Which sentence explains, rather than just reports, the tuckshop's choice?
  options:
    - The fridge costs $600 and the budget is also $600.
    - The fridge uses the whole budget, so there's no money for an oven.
    - The tuckshop chose a fridge, and it also looked at an oven.
    - The tuckshop has a budget of $600 to spend on new equipment.
  actual: The sentence about the whole budget explains. It links the price to the budget and says why there is no oven. The others report facts but don't link them.
```



## The idea: claim, evidence, explain

```step
stage: investigate
insight: A claim, evidence and reasoning have different jobs.
check:
  type: match
  q: Match each part of a Kilby River explanation to its job.
  pairs:
    - - Less rain helped cause less water to flow into the dam.
      - "Claim: the answer being offered"
    - - Record G1 shows 20 mm of rain this month and 60 mm in the same month last
        year. Record G2 shows about 3 ML a day flowing in this month, and 8 ML a
        day last year.
      - "Evidence: named findings from records"
    - - Less rain could mean less water running off the land into the dam. The
        lower flow fits this, but other causes need checking.
      - "Reasoning: the connection and its limits"
```

ML means megalitres. One megalitre is a million litres. G1 and G2 compare the same month in two years. They show a difference, but not the whole cause.

**Worked example: why can't Parkside buy both?** Quote E1 (made up) lists a fridge at $600 and an oven at $500. Budget record E2 sets a limit of $600. In this practice case these are the only two options. So if the tuckshop picks the fridge, the oven is the next best thing it gives up.

| Item | Cost in E1 |
|---|---:|
| Fridge | $600 |
| Oven | $500 |
| Both | $1,100 |

*Scarcity means the tuckshop has to choose. Quote E1 shows that both cost $1,100, which is more than the $600 budget in E2. Buying the fridge uses all the money, so its opportunity cost is the oven.* The table shows the numbers side by side. The explanation says why they matter.

## Check: connect both findings

```step
stage: practise
insight: A geography explanation links both findings to how water moves, without claiming they prove the only cause.
check:
  type: question
  q: Using G1 and G2 above, which conclusion fits the evidence best?
  options:
    - The records prove that less rain was the only reason less water flowed in.
    - Less rain probably meant less runoff, which fits the lower flow into the dam.
    - Less water flowed in, so the town must have used more water this year.
    - Rain and flow both went down, but there's no way to link the two.
  answer: 1
  feedback:
    - Two records can't rule out every other cause, such as water taken upstream.
    - null
    - The records don't say anything about the town's water use. That is a guess.
    - There is a link. Rain runs off the land into the river and then into the dam.
  explain: The best conclusion links both records through how water moves, and says "probably" because other causes could also play a part.
smaller:
  check:
    type: question
    q: Which record measures water entering the dam?
    options:
      - G1, the rain record
      - G2, the flow record
    answer: 1
    feedback:
      - G1 measures rain falling on the land, not water flowing into the dam.
      - null
    explain: G2 measures water flowing into the dam. G1 helps explain why that flow might have dropped.
hint:
  - Name what each record measures.
  - Could another influence also fit the findings?
```



## Vary: civic and historical explanations

```step
stage: modify
insight: Across subjects, an explanation connects specific evidence to an answer.
tasks:
  - check:
      type: question
      q: Notice C1 (made up) asks for comments on Mill Road before a decision. Why is
        this consultation?
      options:
        - The council will do whatever most comments say
        - People can give their views before the council decides
        - The council has told everyone what it has decided
        - The notice is printed in the paper for everyone to see
      answer: 1
      feedback:
        - Asking for views doesn't promise to follow them. The council still decides.
        - null
        - That is an announcement. Consultation happens before the decision.
        - Where the notice appears doesn't make it consultation. What matters is that it asks for views.
      explain: C1 lets people take part by giving their views before the council decides.
  - check:
      type: question
      q: Museum card M1 records a polished face. Conservation note M2 says wear may
        have reduced the shine. Which explains why the mirror might look different now?
      options:
        - M1 shows it was shiny. M2 says wear may have dulled it.
        - M1 says polished and M2 says wear. Both are facts.
        - M2 proves every Han mirror lost its shine in this way.
        - The mirror is old, so it was always dull like it is now.
      answer: 0
      feedback:
        - null
        - Those are two facts side by side. An explanation links them to the change.
        - M2 says "may", and it is about this one made-up mirror, not every mirror.
        - M1 records a polished face, so the mirror probably wasn't always dull.
      explain: This answers why. It links both records to a possible cause of change.
    smaller:
      check:
        type: question
        q: Which word names a possible cause in M2?
        options:
          - Wear
          - Polished
        answer: 0
        feedback:
          - null
          - Polished describes how the surface looked, not what changed it.
        explain: Wear is the possible cause. Use it to explain the change.
```

## Apply: write and compare

```step
stage: make
insight: Produce explanations with the evidence required by each subject.
check:
  type: reflect
  text: In my own document I wrote an explanation for my current subject using its checklist below, named
    evidence and connected it to my claim. I compared it with the model and
    asked a partner or teacher to check the reasoning.
```

Choose **your current subject’s** task below, using the supplied records or real sources approved by your teacher. Meet its evidence requirements; another case is optional practice on the same move.

- **Economics:** explain why Parkside cannot buy both. Include the cost table, refer to E1 and E2 and use *scarcity* and *opportunity cost*. Compare with the worked example.
- **Civics:** explain how C1 enables participation. Model: *Notice C1 invites comments before a decision, so people can participate by contributing views during consultation.*
- **Geography:** explain how less rain could lead to less water flowing into the dam. Use **both G1 and G2**. Link each one to how water moves through the catchment, and say what else might have played a part. Compare with the matching example.
- **History:** explain why the mirror might look different now. Use **both M1 and M2**, name *cause* or *change*, and explain rather than list. Model: *M1 records a polished face. M2 says wear may have dulled it, so wear could explain the change. We can't be sure exactly how it first looked.*

Real History work needs verified sources and a historical content check. Write in your own book or document; the site does not collect or mark it. For paragraph organisation, try English's [Inside a paragraph](../../../english/cohesion/inside-a-paragraph/).
