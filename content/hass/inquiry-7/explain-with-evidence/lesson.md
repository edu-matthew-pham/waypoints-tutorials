An **explanation** answers why or how. Make a claim, name evidence from a source and explain **how it supports the claim**. Evidence sets limits on what you can say.

All cases, figures and source cards here are invented practice material. Parkside School's tuckshop cannot stock everything; Riverbend Council is considering closing Mill Road outside the school; Wattle Flat is on the Kilby River in a dry year; Hartley Museum labels a bronze mirror as Han. The records do not establish facts about a real council, river or ancient society.

## Think first

```step
stage: predict
insight: An explanation connects information to why or how.
check:
  type: predict
  q: Which sentence explains rather than just reports the tuckshop choice?
  options:
    - The fridge costs $600 and the budget is $600.
    - The tuckshop chose a fridge.
    - The fridge uses the whole $600 budget, leaving no funds for the oven, so
      scarcity forces a choice.
  actual: The explanation about the whole budget connects the budget and price to why the tuckshop must
    choose.
```



## The idea: claim, evidence, explain

```step
stage: investigate
insight: A claim, evidence and reasoning have different jobs.
check:
  type: match
  q: Match each part of a Kilby River explanation to its job.
  pairs:
    - - Less rain contributed to less water entering the dam.
      - "Claim: the answer being offered"
    - - Rain record G1 shows 20 mm this month versus 60 mm in the same month
        last year; flow record G2 shows mean daily inflow of 3 ML this month
        versus 8 ML in that same comparison month.
      - "Evidence: named findings from records"
    - - Less rain in the catchment could mean less runoff entering storage; the
        lower inflow fits this, although other causes need checking.
      - "Reasoning: the connection and its limits"
```

ML means megalitres, a measure of water volume. G1 and G2 cover the same two months; they show a comparison, not a complete account of the cause.

**Worked example: why can Parkside not buy both?** Invented quote E1 lists a fridge at $600 and an oven at $500. Budget record E2 sets a $600 limit. These are the only two options in this practice decision, so the oven is the next best alternative if the fridge is chosen.

| Item | Cost in E1 |
|---|---:|
| Fridge | $600 |
| Oven | $500 |
| Both | $1,100 |

*Scarcity forces the tuckshop to choose. Quote E1 shows both goods cost $1,100, above the $600 budget in E2. Buying the fridge uses all available funds, so its opportunity cost is the oven it gives up.* The table makes the comparison visible; the explanation says why it matters.

## Check: connect both findings

```step
stage: practise
insight: A geographical explanation connects both findings to the process
  without claiming they prove the only cause.
check:
  type: question
  q: Using G1 and G2 above, which conclusion is best supported?
  options:
    - The records prove rainfall was the only influence on inflow.
    - Lower rainfall could reduce runoff; lower measured inflow is consistent
      with this, though other influences need checking.
    - Rainfall and inflow are interesting numbers with no connection.
  answer: 1
  feedback:
    - These records do not eliminate other influences.
    - null
    - Explain how water could move from the catchment into storage.
  explain: Connect both records through the water process and preserve uncertainty.
smaller:
  check:
    type: question
    q: Which record measures water entering the dam?
    options:
      - G1, rainfall
      - G2, inflow
    answer: 1
    feedback:
      - Rainfall measures water falling, not inflow itself.
      - null
    explain: G2 measures inflow; G1 helps explain a possible rainfall connection.
hint:
  - Name what each record measures.
  - Could another influence also fit the findings?
```



## Vary: civic and historical explanations

```step
stage: modify
insight: Across subjects, an explanation connects specific evidence to an answer.
tasks:
  - check:
      type: question
      q: Invented notice C1 invites comments on Mill Road before a decision. Why is
        this consultation?
      options:
        - The council has agreed with every comment
        - The notice is printed
        - People can contribute views before the council decides
      answer: 2
      feedback:
        - Inviting views does not promise agreement.
        - The format does not explain the process.
        - null
      explain: C1 creates an opportunity for participation before deciding.
  - check:
      type: question
      q: Museum card M1 records a polished face. Conservation note M2 says wear may
        have reduced the shine. Which explains why the appearance might have
        changed?
      options:
        - M1 records a reflective surface; M2 identifies wear as a possible
          cause of reduced shine.
        - M1 says polished; M2 says wear. Those are facts.
        - M2 proves every Han mirror lost its shine in the same way.
      answer: 0
      feedback:
        - null
        - The facts still need connecting to the change.
        - The note says may, and concerns this invented object.
      explain: This answers why, uses cause and change, and connects both named records.
    smaller:
      check:
        type: question
        q: Which word names a possible cause in M2?
        options:
          - Wear
          - Museum
        answer: 0
        feedback:
          - null
          - The location is not the proposed process.
        explain: Use the possible process to explain the change.
```

## Apply: write and compare

```step
stage: make
insight: Produce explanations with the evidence required by each subject.
check:
  type: reflect
  text: In my own document I wrote an explanation for my current subject using its checklist below, named
    evidence and connected it to my claim. I compared it with the model and
    asked a partner or teacher to check the reasoning.
```

Choose **your current subject’s** task below, using the supplied records or real sources approved by your teacher. Meet its evidence requirements; another case is optional practice on the same move.

- **Economics:** explain why Parkside cannot buy both. Include the cost table, refer to E1 and E2 and use *scarcity* and *opportunity cost*. Compare with the worked example.
- **Civics:** explain how C1 enables participation. Model: *Notice C1 invites comments before a decision, so people can participate by contributing views during consultation.*
- **Geography:** explain how reduced rain could contribute to lower inflow. Use **both G1 and G2**, connect each to the catchment process and acknowledge other possible influences. Compare with the matching example.
- **History:** explain why the mirror's appearance might have changed. Use **both M1 and M2**, name *cause* or *change* and explain rather than list. Model: *M1 records a polished face; M2 identifies wear as a possible cause of change, so wear could explain reduced shine, although the original appearance remains uncertain.*

Real History work needs verified sources and a historical content check. Write in your own book or document; the site does not collect or mark it. For paragraph organisation, try English's [Inside a paragraph](../../../english/cohesion/inside-a-paragraph/).
