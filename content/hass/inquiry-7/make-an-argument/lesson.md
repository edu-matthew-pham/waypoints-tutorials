An **argument** takes a position on a question someone could reasonably disagree about. Give supporting points, name evidence and explain how it supports your position. Consider an alternative fairly.

All cases, figures and source cards here are invented practice material. Parkside School's tuckshop cannot stock everything; Riverbend Council is considering closing Mill Road outside the school; Wattle Flat is on the Kilby River in a dry year; Hartley Museum labels a bronze mirror as Han. The records do not establish facts about a real council, river or ancient society.

This lesson tags Civics and History only. Economics' responses and Geography's action stay in those subjects.

## The idea: support a position

```step
stage: investigate
insight: Each source belongs because it supports a particular point.
check:
  type: match
  q: Match each part of the Mill Road argument to its job.
  pairs:
    - - Ask the council to trial a closure at arrival time, then review it.
      - "Position: what the audience is asked to accept"
    - - Count C2 records 40 vehicles crossing the entrance during arrival time.
      - Evidence for examining conflicts at the entrance
    - - Shop survey C3 says 6 of 8 shops receive deliveries later.
      - Evidence that a timed trial could avoid many delivery periods
    - - Two shops may still need access; check their arrangements.
      - "Counter-consideration: an issue to address"
```

**Invented source cards:** C2 and C3 are as above. Neither records crashes or proves a closure will be safe. Notice C1 invites comments before a decision.

**Model addressed to Riverbend Council:**

*Please consider a trial closure of Mill Road during arrival time. First, C2 records 40 vehicles crossing the school entrance then; reducing those movements could reduce opportunities for conflict, though the count does not measure crashes. Second, survey C3 places six shops' deliveries later, suggesting a timed trial could avoid many delivery periods. Consultation under C1 should include the other two shops so their access needs shape the trial. This offers participation before deciding rather than assuming everyone agrees.*

The points do different work: one concerns the entrance, the other deliveries. Each names evidence and explains the connection.

## Vary: argue about a display

```step
stage: modify
insight: A historical position also needs evidence and limits.
check:
  type: question
  q: M1 records a polished mirror face; M2 says wear may have reduced the shine.
    Which position is supported for a display about change?
  options:
    - Include the artefact to discuss possible change, using both records and
      acknowledging uncertainty.
    - Use it to prove what every person in Han society looked like.
    - Leave out M2 because uncertainty weakens every argument.
  answer: 0
  feedback:
    - null
    - These records cannot support a claim about everyone.
    - Acknowledging uncertainty makes the position more accurate.
  explain: Use both records and acknowledge what they cannot settle.
smaller:
  check:
    type: question
    q: Does 'may have' mean the cause is certain?
    options:
      - Yes
      - No
    answer: 1
    feedback:
      - The wording leaves uncertainty.
      - null
    explain: Keep that uncertainty in the argument.
```

**History model:** *For the display about change, include the mirror as an artefact showing how a surface may change. First, catalogue source M1 records a polished face, giving evidence of a reflective surface. Second, conservation source M2 identifies wear as a possible cause of reduced shine, helping explain change over time. However, neither settles its original appearance. Present this interpretation as possible, rather than as a complete account of life in Han times.*

For a real historical topic, your teacher supplies or approves real sources and checks historical knowledge.

## Apply: plan two arguments

```step
stage: make
insight: A plan connects a position to distinct points and evidence for an audience.
check:
  type: reflect
  text: In my own document I planned a civic argument and a historical argument,
    each with a position, two supported points, an audience and an alternative.
```

Address the Mill Road argument to the council and the mirror argument to museum visitors. You may disagree with the models if the evidence supports your position. For real class work, use your teacher's sources.

| Part | What to put in your plan |
|---|---|
| Question and audience | What is being debated, and who needs to understand it? |
| Position | One clear answer |
| Point 1 | A reason, named evidence and its connection to the position |
| Point 2 | A different reason, named evidence and its connection |
| Alternative | A fair objection or interpretation and your response |
| Close | Return to the position within the evidence's limits |

## Apply: write and check

```step
stage: make
insight: A finished argument connects evidence and reasoning for its audience.
check:
  type: reflect
  text: I wrote both arguments in my own document, compared them with the models,
    and asked a partner or teacher to check the criteria below.
```

Turn each plan into a short argument. Check:

- **Civics:** a clear claim, **two distinct supporting points** with referenced evidence and at least two accurate civic terms, such as *consultation* and *participation*.
- **History:** a clear position supported by **at least two pieces of evidence**, at least two historical terms, such as *artefact*, *source*, *change* or *interpretation*, and a counter-consideration.
- **Both:** evidence connected to reasons, language suitable for the audience and uncertainty preserved.

Writing stays in your own book or document. A partner or teacher reads it; the site does not collect or mark it.
