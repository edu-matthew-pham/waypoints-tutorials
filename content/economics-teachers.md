## What is here

Two courses are built (a preview), and neither has been tried with a class yet.

- [**Scarcity and opportunity cost**](../economics/scarcity-7/): four lessons for AC9HE7K01. *Needs and wants* (and why wants never run out), *Scarcity* (and how it differs from a shop running out), *Opportunity cost* (what is given up when we choose), then *Putting it together*: the whole chain, with a sustainability question. Lessons 1 and 2 are hinges, so each carries an easier check you can set before moving on.
- [**Types of businesses**](../economics/businesses-7/): three lessons for AC9HE7K02. *Goods, services and why businesses exist* (reasons beyond profit, hinge), *Ways a business can be set up* (sole proprietorship, partnership, cooperative, corporation, franchise and not-for-profit), and *Matching the structure to the job* (how purpose and structure shape what is provided and how it reaches consumers across markets, shopping centres, online and remote stores).

All the scenarios are made up (a school tuckshop, a community garden, a council budget, a corner bakery, a valley growers' cooperative, a community food pantry), so nothing dates and nobody's real money is involved.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where Nib makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea: the *typical* steps add more decision contexts, and the *wider* steps stretch the same idea, never faster. Opportunity cost and business structures are worth giving their typical steps, because meeting a range of examples is what makes the categories stick. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## First Nations content

We do not write explanations of First Nations economic life ourselves. The teacher notes quote ACARA's elaborations for each standard (exchange systems and partnerships for AC9HE7K01; First Nations-owned businesses and why they were started for AC9HE7K02) and ask you to build on them with local, community-approved material or a business's own published words.

## What is planned

These are plans, not promises. The rest of the Year 7 knowledge standards — entrepreneurs (AC9HE7K03), work and income (AC9HE7K04), and consumer rights and responsibilities (AC9HE7K05) — then Economics' own skills: interpreting economic data and trends (AC9HE7S03) and developing a cost-benefit response to an economic issue (AC9HE7S04).
