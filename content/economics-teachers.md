## What is here

Six courses are built, covering every Year 7 Economics and Business standard.

- [**Scarcity and opportunity cost**](../economics/scarcity-7/): four lessons for AC9HE7K01. *Needs and wants* (and why wants never run out), *Scarcity* (and how it differs from a shop running out), *Opportunity cost* (what is given up when we choose), then *Putting it together*: the whole chain, with a sustainability question. Lessons 1 and 2 are hinges, so each carries an easier check you can set before moving on.
- [**Types of businesses**](../economics/businesses-7/): three lessons for AC9HE7K02. *Goods, services and why businesses exist* (reasons beyond profit, hinge), *Ways a business can be set up* (sole proprietorship, partnership, cooperative, corporation, franchise and not-for-profit), and *Matching the structure to the job* (how purpose and structure shape what is provided and how it reaches consumers across markets, shopping centres, online and remote stores).
- [**Entrepreneurs and business success**](../economics/entrepreneurs-7/): three lessons for AC9HE7K03. *What entrepreneurs know and do* (recognising opportunities, a shared vision, running decisions), *Values and the decisions entrepreneurs make* (opportunities, stakeholders and compliance; hinge), and *Explaining why a business succeeds* (initiative, innovation and evidence). The last lesson treats "characteristics explain success" as one way to explain it, with luck, timing and starting money kept in view. Students bring a real local business into each Make step, so build a class set of entrepreneur profiles first.
- [**Work, income and identity**](../economics/work-7/): three lessons for AC9HE7K04. *Types of work* (paid and unpaid, full-time to volunteer; hinge), *How people earn an income* (wages, salaries, profit, dividends, rent and social security), and *Why people work* (identity, self-esteem, community, material and non-material living standards). Students are never asked about their own family's work or income. Lesson 1 shows an unpaid carer on purpose; its teacher notes ask you to check it suits your class and swap it if not.
- [**Consumer rights and responsibilities**](../economics/consumers-7/): four lessons for AC9HE7K05. *Rights and responsibilities* (both sides of a sale; hinge), *Your rights when you buy* (consumer guarantees, warranties, change of mind, cooling-off, returns), *What businesses must do* (mandatory and voluntary standards, recalls), and *Plan before you buy* (a weekly budget on the spreadsheet board, and a pay-later deal checked against it). The law stated is real Australian consumer law, each claim checked against an official source and listed with its date in the teacher notes; check the ACCC site if the page is old. Debt is touched, not taught: paying later still has an opportunity cost, and borrowing "can be useful or costly", with the later standards named for you.
- [**Responding to an economic issue**](../economics/issues-7/): five lessons for Economics' own skills, AC9HE7S03 and S04. *From information to an issue* (hinge), *Reading a trend* and *Cause and effect in data* (both on the graph board, with made-up data), *Costs and benefits* (hinge), and *Developing a response* (two phone plans on the spreadsheet board; the student's own response is unmarked). Lesson 3 says that two things going together doesn't prove a cause, and its teacher notes name where students meet the same idea in science, English, ICT and Year 10 maths. The shared skills (questions, information, presenting) are in Humanities skills.

All the scenarios are made up (a school tuckshop and its oven, a community garden, a council budget, a town bakery, a valley growers' cooperative, a community food pantry, and the businesses of Riverbend, a made-up town), so nothing dates and nobody's real money is involved.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where Nib makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea: the *typical* steps add more decision contexts, and the *wider* steps stretch the same idea, never faster. Opportunity cost and business structures are worth giving their typical steps, because meeting a range of examples is what makes the categories stick. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## First Nations content

We do not write explanations of First Nations economic life ourselves. The teacher notes quote ACARA's elaborations for each standard (exchange systems and partnerships for AC9HE7K01; First Nations-owned businesses and why they were started for AC9HE7K02; First Nations entrepreneurs and community-run companies for AC9HE7K03; continuity of cultural practices and caring for Country for AC9HE7K04) and ask you to build on them with local, community-approved material or a business's own published words.
