## What is here

One course is built, and none of it has been tried with a class.

- [**Scarcity and opportunity cost**](../economics/scarcity-7/): four lessons for AC9HE7K01. *Needs and wants* (and why wants never run out), *Scarcity* (and how it differs from a shop running out), *Opportunity cost* (what is given up when we choose), then *Putting it together*: the whole chain, with a sustainability question. Lessons 1 and 2 are hinges, so each carries an easier check you can set before moving on.

All the scenarios are made up (a school tuckshop, a community garden, a council budget), so nothing dates and nobody's real money is involved.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where Nib makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea: the *typical* steps add more decision contexts, and the *wider* steps stretch the same idea, never faster. Opportunity cost is worth giving its typical step, because many different choices are what separate it from "the price". Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## First Nations content

We do not write explanations of First Nations economic life ourselves. The teacher notes quote ACARA's elaboration for this standard, which names exchange systems and partnerships used to decide about limited resources sustainably, and ask you to build on it with local, community-approved material.

## What is planned

These are plans, not promises. The rest of Year 7 knowledge standards (AC9HE7K02 to K05), then Economics' own skill, developing a response to an economic issue (AC9HE7S04).
