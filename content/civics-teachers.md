## What is here

Six courses are built (twenty-three lessons in total).

- [**How Australia is governed**](../civics/government-7/): five lessons for AC9HC7K01, in the order the ideas depend on each other: *The Constitution* (hinge), *Representative and responsible government* (hinge), *Power shared, power checked*, *One country, several governments (federation)*, and *Borrowed from Britain and America*.
- [**Freedoms and taking part**](../civics/democracy-7/): four lessons for AC9HC7K02, in waypoint order: *The five democratic freedoms* (speech, association, assembly, religion and movement, hinge), *Within the bounds of law* (why legal limits such as defamation and public safety exist), *Ways to take part in democracy* (comparing voting, petitions, committee inquiries, volunteering, community groups and peaceful protest), and *Formal and active citizenship* (legal status versus active participation, and historical exclusion and change in Australia).
- [**Law and justice**](../civics/law-7/): four lessons for AC9HC7K03, in waypoint order: *Where our law comes from* (Acts, common law and precedent, independent judges, Magna Carta as later read; hinge), *The same law for everyone* (the rule of law, presumption of innocence, burden of proof; hinge), *Courts and a fair trial* (the court ladder, appeals, judge and jury), and *Fair courts and democracy* (access to justice and why it matters for democracy). Every case and person is made up.
- [**A diverse democracy**](../civics/diversity-7/): three lessons for AC9HC7K04: *Secular, pluralist, multi-faith* (secular describes the government, not the people; section 116; hinge), *Christian and Western heritage* (traditions in Parliament and law, and why a tradition isn't an official religion), and *Many origins, one democracy* (migrant communities and real, dated ABS Census data on a small spreadsheet). The third idea, First Nations histories and cultures, is taught by you: the course lists it as *Taught in class* and the teacher guide links its curriculum page with ACARA's elaborations. No step asks about a student's religion, background or family.
- [**Whose view is it?**](../civics/perspectives-7/): three lessons for Civics' own skill AC9HC7S03, on one made-up local decision (closing Main Street for a Saturday market): *Whose view is it?* (who made a source, what they want, what's left out; hinge), *Why views differ* (agree, disagree, and why: values, interests, information, role), and *Why it's hard to decide* (competing values and interests, missing evidence, and why majority support doesn't settle it alone). Swap in your own local issue with Make your own version.
- [**Values and living together**](../civics/values-7/): four lessons for AC9HC7K05: *Values we name* (values often named as Australian, and how they show up in public life), *Do we live our values?* (hinge: judging with real, dated survey evidence from the Scanlon Institute), *Values across traditions* (one shared value, giving, in each tradition's own word; religious freedom), and *Why people come to Australia* (migrants, refugees and the citizenship pledge). No made-up hardship stories; nothing personal asked.

## What is real and what is made up

The institutions are the content, so they are real: the Constitution, Parliament's two houses, the Governor-General, the courts. Everything else is made up: the people, the proposals, the results, a club. The lessons name no current office holders and no parties, so they do not date and do not lean. The House board in *Representative and responsible government* uses a made-up House with two sides named by letter; its teacher notes offer the 2010 hung parliament as a real example for you to use if you choose.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where Nib makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## Sensitive topics

Where the standards reach religion, heritage, values, migrants and refugees (AC9HC7K04 and K05), the lessons are factual and neutral: terms and dated public data on the site, values questions as discussion with no marked answer, and nothing personal asked.

## Year 6 catch-up and what comes next

Every Year 7 Civics standard has a course. Both skills are covered too: *Whose view is it?* teaches AC9HC7S03, and AC9HC7S04 (explaining ways to take part) is taught in *Ways to take part* and *Formal and active citizenship*. For students who missed Year 6, *Three levels of government* in [Before Year 7: HASS](../hass/before-year-7/three-levels/) covers local, state and federal government, and *The Constitution* and *One country, several governments* link to it. Years 8 to 10 are not planned yet.
