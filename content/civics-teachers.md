## What is here

**This is a preview.** One lesson is built, and none of it has been tried with a class.

- [**How Australia is governed**](../civics/government-7/): five lessons are planned for AC9HC7K01, in the order the ideas depend on each other: the Constitution, representative and responsible government, shared and checked power, federation, and what Australia borrowed. The first, *the Constitution*, is built, using a made-up club's rules as the model before the real Constitution. It is a hinge lesson, so it carries an easier check you can set before moving on.

## What is real and what is made up

The institutions are the content, so they are real: the Constitution, Parliament's two houses, the Governor-General, the High Court. Everything else is made up: the people, the proposals, the results, a club. The lessons name no current office holders and no parties, so they do not date and do not lean.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where Nib makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## Sensitive topics

Where the standards reach religion, heritage, values, migrants and refugees (AC9HC7K04 and K05), the plan is factual and neutral: terms and data on the site, and values questions left to class discussion.

## What is planned

These are plans, not promises. The rest of the government course, then the characteristics of Australia's democracy, the legal system, a secular and pluralist society and values and social cohesion. A Year 6 catch-up, *Three levels of government*, is planned in [Before Year 7: HASS](../hass/before-year-7/).
