This lesson is mostly a class discussion. Your teacher leads it, and the class writes its agreement together. You do not need to share anything about yourself.

## Engage: rules or agreement?

```step
stage: predict
insight: "Committing to a guess: a short agreement the class writes is easier to keep than a long list of rules from someone else."
check:
  type: predict
  q: "Which is people most likely to keep?"
  options: ["Five short lines the class wrote and agreed together", "25 rules written by someone else", "No agreement at all"]
  actual: "Usually the short one the class wrote. People keep what they helped make and can remember."
```

## Explore: what makes a good line?

```step
stage: run
insight: "Sorting draft lines shows what a good agreement line looks like: short, positive, about what we do."
check:
  type: sort
  q: "Is each draft line a good line for a class agreement, or does it need work?"
  options: ["Good line", "Needs work"]
  items:
    - ["We pause before we post.", "Good line", "Short, positive, and something we do."]
    - ["Never ever do anything bad online or you'll be in big trouble!!!", "Needs work", "It's a threat, and 'anything bad' doesn't say what to do."]
    - ["We ask before we share a photo of someone.", "Good line", "Clear and kind."]
    - ["We tell a trusted adult when something worries us, and we help friends to tell.", "Good line", "It names what to do and who helps."]
    - ["Don't use phones.", "Needs work", "Nobody will keep it, and it isn't about how we treat each other."]
    - ["If someone in our class is having a hard time online, we don't add to it.", "Good line", "It's about what bystanders do."]
```

Was your guess right? Good lines are **short**, **positive** (what we do, not what we don't), and **something everyone can keep**.

## Explain: a made-up class's agreement

```step
stage: investigate
insight: "Reading a finished example gives the class a model before writing its own."
check:
  type: question
  q: "Read the made-up class's agreement below. Which course habit is missing from it?"
  options: ["Slowing down when something pushes you to hurry", "Pausing before you post", "Asking before sharing a photo of someone", "Telling a trusted adult"]
  answer: 0
  feedback: [null, "Line 1 is about pausing.", "Line 2 is about asking first.", "Line 4 is about telling."]
  explain: "Nothing in it is about pressure and tricks. A class could add one, or decide five lines is enough."
```

**Room 12's online agreement** (made up)

1. We pause before we post, and choose the kinder version.
2. We ask before we share a photo or message about someone else.
3. We keep personal details for people we know in real life.
4. We tell a trusted adult when something worries us, and help friends to tell.
5. If someone is having a hard time online, we don't add to it, and we check on them.

Every line came from a lesson in this course. Your class's agreement can look quite different. What matters is that **you write it together** and that **everyone can keep it**.

## Make: our agreement

```step
stage: make
insight: "The class writes and agrees its own lines, led by the teacher; the site holds a tick, not the agreement."
check: { type: reflect, text: "I helped my class write our agreement, and I can say one line from it." }
```

Your teacher will lead this part. In small groups, write one or two lines, then the class chooses and agrees five or so. Use the course's habits if they help:

- **A day's trail:** can I take it back, does it say where I am, is it about someone else?
- **Who can see this?** The smallest audience that does the job.
- **Pause before you post:** how will they read it?
- **Who is on the other end?** Friendly is not the same as known.
- **Screens and me:** notice, then choose.
- **Pressure and tricks:** stop, check, ask.
- **When something goes wrong:** stop, keep it, tell, block and report.


**A private think** (just for you): which line of the agreement will be easiest for you to keep? Which will be hardest?
