## Before you use this course

**⚑ Draft for the school's wellbeing team.** These eight lessons and their teacher notes have not been read by a wellbeing team or tried with a class. Share them with your school's wellbeing lead (counsellor, pastoral lead or child-protection contact) before teaching, agree the support pathway and any local wording, and fit the lessons to the school's own policies on bullying, devices and reporting.

**If a student discloses something.** No lesson asks students to share their own experiences, but a story can bring one up. Follow your school's child-protection procedure. Listen, don't probe, don't promise to keep it secret (say you may need to tell someone whose job is to help), and pass it on as the procedure says. Thank the student, and steer the class back to the made-up story.

**Ground rules for every lesson.**
- Every story, name, app and message is made up. Keep the talk about the characters, not about students, classmates or real apps.
- Never ask students what has happened to them, what apps they use or how long they spend on screens.
- Private thinking stays private: the "private think" at the end of each lesson is not written down, collected or shared.
- No fear tactics and no real incidents. The message is "here is a habit that helps", never "this will happen to you".

## Grading by age (width)

Each lesson has a minimum path that suits Years 5 and 6, and a **Typical** step with situations for Years 7 and 8 (group chats with a bigger audience, older games, peer pressure). For Years 5 and 6, add `?width=minimum` to the lesson link you give students; for Years 7 and 8, give the plain link. Typical steps are not needed to finish a lesson.

## Rules, laws and ages

The lessons state no laws and no age rules (such as minimum ages for social media accounts), because these change. For the current rules, point to the eSafety Commissioner: [esafety.gov.au](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) (its pages for kids are at [esafety.gov.au/kids](https://www.esafety.gov.au/kids), and reporting at [esafety.gov.au/report](https://www.esafety.gov.au/report); addresses checked 26 Oct 2026). Kids Helpline is free and for young people: 1800 55 1800, [kidshelpline.com.au](https://kidshelpline.com.au/) (checked 26 Oct 2026; check the number is still current each term). eSafety also has pages for young people and for educators; we could not open those from here, so find them from the eSafety home page.

## Where it fits

How computers work › [Cyber safety and privacy](../../../systems/cyber/) teaches passwords, second factors, phishing, the digital footprint and the Privacy Principles; these lessons link there instead of repeating it. [AI in your life](../../../ai/ai-in-your-life/) covers feeds, sticky app design and companion chatbots for Years 7 to 10. This course adds the everyday and relational side: audience, kindness, trust, pressure, wellbeing and what to do when something goes wrong.

Lesson 8, the class agreement, is a short teacher-led lesson: the site gives students a sort and an example to think with, and the class writes its own agreement together. It is a lesson rather than only a class plan so that it sits in the course order, gets a guide and slides like the others, and students can come back to the example later.
