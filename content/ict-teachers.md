## What is here

ICT skills are the everyday tools every subject uses: spreadsheets, documents, slides and files. This is separate from Digital Technologies, which is about how computers work and how to make things with code. It is for students in any class, and for teachers of any subject who want a class to get better at a tool they use.

**This is a pilot.** Four courses are built, none tried with a class yet: [Working with data](../ict/data/) (nine lessons on spreadsheets), [Documents and slides](../ict/documents/) (nine lessons on headings, lists, layout, slides and credits), [Files and systems](../ict/files/) (four lessons on folders and names, copy and move, versions and backup, and fixing problems) and [Staying safe and well](../ict/safe/) (eight short workshops on made-up stories: your trail, who can see it, kind chats, who is on the other end, screens, pressure tricks, a plan for when something goes wrong, and a class agreement). All usually suit Years 5 to 8. We'd like a colleague to try one with a class before we build more, so please tell us what worked and what didn't.

Each course also has a **Reference** page of cards: one screen for each skill, with steps for any app and a note for each common app, to look up when you need it. Lessons count toward progress; cards do not.

**Staying safe and well needs your wellbeing team first.** Please share its lessons and teacher notes with the school's wellbeing lead before teaching it. Every lesson's notes say what to do if a student discloses something (follow the school's child-protection procedure, and never promise secrecy). No lesson asks students about their own experiences, reflections are private, and no laws or age rules are stated: the notes point to the eSafety Commissioner for those.

## How it teaches

- **The idea first, the tool second.** A lesson teaches what every spreadsheet shares (cells have names, a formula uses them and keeps up when a number changes) on a small sheet on the page. It then asks students to do the same in the app your school uses, whether that's Excel, Sheets or something else, so it keeps working when the buttons move.
- **Core functions only.** Nothing that changes from year to year: no pivot tables or macros, and no AI buttons.
- **Real data.** Lessons use data from other subjects, such as a Year 7 science investigation.
- The lesson ends with an optional step in your school's own app. Students need no other software, and you don't need to know their app well.

## Where it sits

ICT skills maps loosely to the Digital Literacy general capability, at pathway level only (not to individual standards). It overlaps a few Digital Technologies descriptors, which the course names in text. For how data is stored and moved, see [How computers work](../systems-teachers/). For coding with data, see Code's [Data detective](../code/data/) and [SQL and databases](../code/sql/).
