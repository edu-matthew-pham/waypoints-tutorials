## What is here

ICT skills are the everyday tools every subject uses: spreadsheets, documents, slides and files. This is separate from Digital Technologies, which is about how computers work and how to make things with code. It is for students in any class, and for teachers of any subject who want a class to get better at a tool they use.

**This is a pilot.** One course is started, [Working with data](../ict/data/) (usually Years 6–8), and it has one lesson so far: [A formula keeps up](../ict/data/a-formula-keeps-up/). It hasn't been tried with a class yet. We'd like a colleague to try it before we build more, so please tell us what you find.

## How it teaches

- **The idea first, the tool second.** A lesson teaches what every spreadsheet shares (cells have names, a formula uses them and keeps up when a number changes) on a small sheet on the page. It then asks students to do the same in the app your school uses, whether that's Excel, Sheets or something else, so it keeps working when the buttons move.
- **Core functions only.** Nothing that changes from year to year: no pivot tables or macros, and no AI buttons.
- **Real data.** Lessons use data from other subjects, such as a Year 7 science investigation.
- The lesson ends with an optional step in your school's own app. Students need no other software, and you don't need to know their app well.

## Where it sits

ICT skills maps loosely to the Digital Literacy general capability, at pathway level only (not to individual standards). It overlaps a few Digital Technologies descriptors, which the course names in text. For how data is stored and moved, see [How computers work](../systems-teachers/). For coding with data, see Code's [Data detective](../code/data/) and [SQL and databases](../code/sql/).

## What is planned

These are plans, not promises. We'll propose each pathway for your approval before building it.

1. Working with data: more lessons (adding up and averaging a column, sorting and filtering, one chart that fits the data).
2. Documents and presentations.
3. Files and systems.
4. Investigating and judging information, and staying safe and well, as short workshop-style lessons.

Touch typing is a link out to existing tools, not something we'll rebuild.
