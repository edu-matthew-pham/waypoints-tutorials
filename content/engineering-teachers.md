## What is here

An elective layer on top of the core curriculum, for schools with kits, a makerspace or a STEM elective. Each lesson teaches one engineering idea on a board you can change and watch: gears, levers, linkages, trusses, circuits, microcontrollers, CAD, circuit boards. Lessons follow the maths-style shape: Predict, Try on the board, a worked example, vary it, practise, make.

This is not core Digital Technologies, and we don't claim a curriculum node for it. Design and Technologies (Engineering principles and systems) is the home. Ratio from Maths, forces from Science and loops from Code are applied here, not taught again.

## Where to start, and what you need

- **No kit needed, everything in the browser:** [Mechanisms](../engineering/mechanisms/) (gears, levers, pulleys) then [Linkages and cams](../engineering/linkages/); and [Structures](../engineering/structures/) (triangles, bridges, beams).
- **Physical computing:** [Circuits with the micro:bit](../engineering/circuits/), then [Arduino](../engineering/arduino/). Each circuits lesson has a "Build it in Tinkercad" step; the Arduino course runs a simulator first and ends with real hardware (an Uno, a breadboard, an optional Pico). Students should have done [micro:bit Foundations](../code/microbit/) first.
- **Power and motors (Years 9–10):** [Power and motors](../engineering/power/) covers pin limits, motor drivers and batteries. The LiPo battery safety sign-off is for you to run with the class, not for students to pass alone.
- **CAD and fabrication:** [Design and print](../engineering/cad/) uses Tinkercad 3D; [Parametric design](../engineering/parametric/) uses Onshape. A 3D printer or laser cutter is only needed if you want students to make the part.
- **Your own board:** [Your own board](../engineering/pcb/) is browser only until you order.

## Design projects

[Capstone projects](../engineering/projects/) are eight optional projects that follow a design cycle (Inquire, Develop, Create, Test, Evaluate), with MYP Design criteria A to D noted in the teacher notes. Three suit Years 8–9, three Years 9–10, and two are stretch projects (soldering a protoboard, designing a PCB).

## For you

Each course page links a **Teacher guide** for each lesson (goals, timing, what to say, misconceptions, exit ticket), a deck and a worksheet. Hardware steps are marked as optional (`typical`), so a class without kits can still finish every lesson on the board.
