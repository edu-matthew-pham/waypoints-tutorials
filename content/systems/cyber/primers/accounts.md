## The idea in plain words

Passwords fail in predictable ways: sites leak their password lists, people reuse one password everywhere (so one leak opens every account), and common or personal passwords are on the lists people try first. Long passphrases (four random words), a different one for every site, kept in a password manager, fix most of it.

**Multi-factor authentication** (MFA) adds something you *have* (a phone, a security key) or *are* (a fingerprint) to something you *know*. A leaked password alone is no longer enough. Codes can still be tricked out of people by a fake sign-in page; a security key only answers the real site.

**Phishing** messages pretend to be someone you trust to get a password, money or a click. The signs: a sender address that isn't quite right, pressure or a deadline, a request for a password or payment, and a link whose text and destination differ. When unsure, go to the site or person yourself.

Your **digital footprint** is the data systems collect about you. A system should collect only what its purpose needs; settings such as location sharing and public profiles add to it.
