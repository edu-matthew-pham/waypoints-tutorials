## The idea in plain words

A **network** is computers joined so they can send each other data. They can be joined by a **cable**, by **Wi-Fi** (radio waves to a router) or by **mobile data** (radio waves to a phone tower). Cables are fastest and most reliable; Wi-Fi lets you move around but gets slower further from the router and through walls; mobile data works almost anywhere. The **internet** is many networks joined together.

Data doesn't travel in one piece. A message is cut into small **pieces** (packets), each with a number. The pieces pass through several devices on the way (routers), and can take different paths and arrive in a different order. The computer at the end uses the numbers to put them back in order.

## Teaching it

- Unplugged: pass a sentence across the room cut into numbered strips, by different routes; reassemble it at the far end.
- On the board, speeds are words (very fast, fast, medium, slow). The Year 7–8 board shows the same model in Mb/s and ms.
- Year 7–8 teachers: a student who can say why a message arrives in order even though its pieces took different paths can start Networks.
