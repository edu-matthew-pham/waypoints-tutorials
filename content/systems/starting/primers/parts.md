## The idea in plain words

Every computer, from a games console to a phone to a laptop, has the same five kinds of parts:

- **Input** takes in what you do: buttons, a keyboard, a touch screen, a microphone.
- The **processor** follows the instructions and works out what happens next.
- **Memory** holds what the computer is using right now. It empties when the power goes off.
- **Storage** keeps things when the power is off: saved games, photos, apps.
- **Output** shows or plays the result: a screen, a speaker.

They **take turns**. Press A in a game: the buttons send the press to the processor, the processor changes the score in memory, then sends it to the screen. Save: the processor copies the score from memory into storage. Turn off and on: memory is empty, storage still has the save.

## Why it matters

AC9TDI6K01 (Years 5–6). Year 7–8's Inside a computer (8K01) assumes it: it starts at specifications (cores, GB of RAM) and treats RAM and storage as different parts.

## Teaching it

- The board is a simplified console: one processor, one memory, one storage, and no operating system. Say so if a student asks where the game's code lives (in storage, loaded into memory to run).
- Memory versus storage is the confusion that lasts into Year 9. Use the "turn off and on" button every time it comes up.
- Year 7–8 teachers: a student who can say which part keeps a saved game, and which empties when the power goes off, can start Inside a computer.
