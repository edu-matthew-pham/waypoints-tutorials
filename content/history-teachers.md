## What is here

Twelve Year 7 courses and a catch-up cover every Year 7 History standard, in three pathways: Sources and evidence, The ancient world, and Thinking historically. None has been tried with a class yet.

- [**Source origin and purpose**](../history/sources-7/): four lessons for AC9HH7S03, with an optional warm-up on primary and secondary sources (AC9HH7S02). Who made a source and what it shows, why it was made and what was going on, then all the questions together on one source and what it can and cannot tell us.
- [**Source usefulness**](../history/usefulness-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7S04, in waypoint order: separating factual claims from opinions and applying the four accuracy questions (hinge), distinguishing accuracy from usefulness for a specific inquiry question (hinge), and putting both together to judge what a source can tell you.
- [**How we know**](../history/evidence-7/): four lessons for AC9HH7K08, in waypoint order: layers and physical evidence, DNA and dating, written sources, and conservation. DNA is on the required path. Layers and written sources have hinge checks with smaller-step support. The course includes a reference and recap.
- [**Early human migration**](../history/migration-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7K01: what the Out of Africa theory says and what 'theory' means; how changes in rainfall, deserts and sea levels are thought to have moved people over many generations; and how several kinds of evidence build an interpretation. Arrival on the Australian continent (AC9HH7K02) is not taught here; it stays with First Nations-led resources.
- [**Shaped by the land**](../history/land-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7K09, the land, water and climate where ancient China grew; how farming produced a surplus that fed people who did not farm; and how the same features gave opportunities and set limits. The Reference page has swap-in cards for Egypt, Greece, Rome and India.
- [**People and power**](../history/people-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7K10: the layers of Qin and Han society and how officials carried the emperor's power; what women did and why the evidence about them is thin; and the groups whose work the society rested on, and how the order changed. Swap-in cards for Egypt, Athens and Sparta, Rome and India.
- [**Beliefs and practices**](../history/beliefs-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7K11: how beliefs about ancestors, Heaven and the afterlife fitted together and led to practices; what Han tombs (Mawangdui, tomb models, the terracotta army) show about funerary customs, and whose burials they show; and what beliefs reveal about what a society valued and how it was organised, compared with Egypt. Swap-in cards for Egypt, Greece, Rome and India.
- [**Contact and conflict**](../history/contact-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7K12: why the Qin, the Han and the Xiongnu met and fought, usually for several reasons at once; what conquest, trade and the heqin peace agreements changed, for both sides; and how contact, even indirect contact between Rome and the Han, drove change. Swap-in cards for Egypt, Greece, Rome and India.
- [**A significant individual**](../history/individual-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7K13: who Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor, was and three tests of significance; his achievements and how each one changed the empire through officials and labourers (with the burning of books told according to Sima Qian); and his legacy, how his inscriptions and Han writers saw him, and why significant does not mean good. Swap-in cards for people from China, Egypt, Greece, Rome and India.
- [**Time, cause and change**](../history/time-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7S05, in the Thinking historically pathway: BCE, CE and BP dates and timelines, with a timeline board (hinge); several causes of one event, long-term, short-term and the trigger, and its effects (hinge); and what changed and what stayed from the Qin to the Han, shown with dated sources.
- [**Past perspectives**](../history/perspectives-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7S06: what a source describes and the perspective of its maker, with a source-colouring board (hinge); the attitudes and values behind a source, using ACARA's Confucius saying (introduced as a perspective to analyse) and the Mawangdui silk painting; and whose voices are missing from the record, and what thin evidence can still show.
- [**Historical interpretations**](../history/interpretations-7/): three lessons for AC9HH7S07, all on why the Qin dynasty fell: fact or interpretation (hinge); why Han writers and some historians today tell it differently (evidence, questions, values); and making your own argument about why, written in the student's book. No interpretation is given as the answer.
- [**Before Year 7 History**](../history/before-year-7/): one short Year 6 catch-up, *Can you trust this source?*, for a class that needs it before the Year 7 lessons.

**Source origin and purpose** and **Source usefulness** use ancient China in the Qin and Han period as their model. **How we know** uses labelled, made-up excavations and reports, with a sourced Qin conservation example and an optional Rosetta Stone example. The questions carry into the society your class studies.

## How a lesson runs

Most lessons use the same kinds of step, usually in this order: predict, try, a worked example (where a character, Nib, makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. Some put a practise step on a board before the worked example. Every class takes the same steps. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## How it fits your unit

Use the lessons when your class first meets sources, then return to them with the sources in your own depth study. The questions of origin, purpose and usefulness are the same for any society, so they carry into whichever ancient world you teach.

## First Nations history

We do not write explanations of First Nations history or culture ourselves. Teacher notes start from ACARA's own elaborations and ask you to build on them with local, community-approved material. The deep-time Australian standards (AC9HH7K02 to K07) are listed in the last unit of [Early human migration](../history/migration-7/): each is taught in class with local, community-approved material and First Nations-led resources, following your school's protocols. That course's teacher guide has starting points.

## What comes next

Trials with classes, and fixes from what teachers see. Some facts in the ancient-world lenses still rest on encyclopedia summaries or on books not checked online; they are being replaced with museum and scholarly sources.
