## What is here

**This is a preview.** One course is built so far, and none of it has been tried with a class.

- [**Source origin and purpose**](../history/sources-7/): four lessons for AC9HH7S03, with an optional warm-up on primary and secondary sources (AC9HH7S02). Who made a source and what it shows, why it was made and what was going on, then all the questions together on one source and what it can and cannot tell us.
- [**Before Year 7 History**](../history/before-year-7/): one short Year 6 catch-up, *Can you trust this source?*, for a class that needs it before the Year 7 lessons.

The worked example throughout is **ancient China in the Qin and Han period**, the society many Year 7 classes study. Every lesson is also written so the same questions work on any source.

## How a lesson runs

Every lesson follows the same steps in the same order for every class: predict, try, a worked example (where a character, Nib, makes a common slip for students to find), vary, practise, make. How long to spend is your call. Extra steps are optional width on the same idea. Written tasks are done in the student's own book and checked by you or a partner. Each lesson has teacher notes and a class plan.

## How it fits your unit

Use the lessons when your class first meets sources, then return to them with the sources in your own depth study. The questions of origin, purpose and usefulness are the same for any society, so they carry into whichever ancient world you teach.

## First Nations history

We do not write explanations of First Nations history or culture ourselves. Teacher notes start from ACARA's own elaborations and ask you to build on them with local, community-approved material. The deep-time Australian standards (AC9HH7K02 to K07) will be a single page pointing to First Nations-led resources, and follow your school's protocols and local community guidance.

## What is planned

These are plans, not promises. *How we know* (AC9HH7K08, historical and archaeological evidence) is the gateway to build next, then source usefulness (AC9HH7S04) and the ancient-world lenses (AC9HH7K09 to K13), each with the Qin and Han worked example and swap-in cards for Egypt, Greece, Rome and India.
