## What is here

Five courses, 57 lessons, for Years 7 to 10. They build understanding of how AI works, test whether and when it helps learning, and look at who AI affects. They are classroom lessons for you to lead: every lesson has a class plan, and every activity works on paper. Each is a short 5E inquiry (Engage, Explore, Explain, Practise, Elaborate, Evaluate). None has been tried with a class yet, so please tell us what you find.

## Where to start

1. **[AI Foundations](../ai/foundations/)** (13 lessons) first: prediction, a dice and word generator, where the table breaks, training, mistakes and bias, prompting and follow-ups, and what to hand over to AI and what to keep.
2. Then any of these, in any order; none needs another:
   - [Does AI Help Us Learn?](../ai/does-ai-help-us-learn/) (12): what counts as learning and what the evidence shows.
   - [Who Does AI Affect?](../ai/who-does-ai-affect/) (11): real cases through one lens: who gains, who loses, who decides, what's the catch.
   - [AI in your life](../ai/ai-in-your-life/) (10): the AI in your apps and what it wants from you. The chatbot, attention and fakes lessons carry a **wellbeing note** in their teacher notes; please read it before you teach them.
3. **[AI at our school](../ai/ai-at-our-school/)** (11) last, after both society courses: students act as a school AI committee and write a charter of principles.

Year 7 can do the core steps. For older classes, add `?width=wider` to a lesson link to start them at the wider steps.

## Using real AI in lessons

Our rules, so you know what students will and won't meet:

- **The core is scripted.** Where a lesson shows an AI's reply, it is labelled "scripted example reply, not a real AI". Students never need an AI account.
- **Real AI is optional.** It only appears in `prompt:` cards on steps marked typical, each with a no-AI alternative, and no step needs it to finish. The page says "only if your teacher says you can". Whether your students use a real AI tool is your decision.
- **Claims about studies keep their limits** (who was studied, on what) and are checked against the full papers. Figures are dated and sourced.
