## What is here

Short, self-paced coding lessons for Years 6 to 10, in three pathways that share the same big ideas. Each lesson runs in the browser with a live playground, so nothing is installed. Students keep their own progress in their browser; there are no accounts, and you cannot see their progress from here.

## Which course do you start with?

Pick the pathway that matches what you want students making. Any of the three is a complete start; none needs another first.

- **[Python](../code/paths/python/)**: type real code. Start with [Python Foundations with Turtle](../code/python-foundations/) (drawing: commands, loops, variables, decisions, lists), then [Python functions and events](../code/python-functions/) (ending in a game), then the [console bridge](../code/console/). The extensions (data, machine learning, SQL, games, web apps) all come after the console bridge.
- **[Web](../code/paths/web/)**: build pages. [Structure and style a page](../code/html/), then [Layout and components](../code/css/). JavaScript extensions follow (p5.js, the DOM, Vue, Bootstrap).
- **[MakeCode](../code/paths/makecode/)**: blocks first, then Python, for younger students and anyone new to code. Start with [micro:bit Foundations](../code/microbit/) or [Arcade Foundations](../code/arcade/). Robotics, micro:bit and the computer, and Secret messages build on micro:bit.

Each course page says "Usually Years 6–8" (or 7–9, or 9–10). That is a guide to typical fit, not a gate; a keen Year 7 can go further and an older class can start at the beginning.

## How a lesson runs

Code lessons follow PRIMM: **Predict** (commit to an answer first), **Run**, **Investigate**, **Modify**, **Make**. The site is a complete self-paced lesson. If you teach it as a class, each lesson has a deck with the talk for Predict and Investigate (whole class), then students work through Modify and Make at their own pace. Each lesson also has a short "Explain it to me" clip and escalating hints.

Every lesson names the hard part up front, so students know where to expect effort. Courses end with a challenge that uses what the course taught, with milestones that fade their support.

## For you

At the bottom of each course page is a **Teacher guide** link. It opens a guide for each lesson (goals, what to say, where students get stuck, an exit ticket, and solutions), a deck for Prepare, a worksheet, and printable cheat sheets for each unit and for the whole course. Open projects (briefs with a skills tracker and a "show your teacher" card) are on each course's Projects page.

See also [Big ideas across the pathways](../teach/code/ideas/) for how loops, variables and the rest recur from course to course.

## What you need

- **Python and Web:** a modern browser. Nothing else.
- **MakeCode:** needs access to makecode.com. The micro:bit, Robotics and AI robots courses have hands-on steps marked as optional, so a class without devices can use the simulator.
- **Python in a locked-down school:** [Check your school](../check/) once on a student device.
