Year 10 Number

Exact or approximate?

Try firstIs 7.07 the exact value of √50? Why or why not?

Check your answer

No. 7.07 squared is 49.9849, not 50.

$\sqrt{50}$ is exact. 7.07 is a different number: close, but $7.07^2 = 49.9849$. A calculation done with rounded values gives an answer for the rounded numbers, not for the exact ones.

Combine exact roots first: $\sqrt{50} \times \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{100} = 10$.

Chop or round?

Try firstChop $3.3490 to the cent. Then round it.

Check your answer

Chopped: $3.34. Rounded: $3.35 (the next digit is 9).

To chop a price, cut it off after the cent. To round it, look at the digit after the cent: 5 or more goes up.

Chopping always makes a number smaller, so over thousands of prices the errors add up in one direction. Rounding goes up and down about equally, so the errors mostly cancel.

Rounding over and over

Try firstDoes the gap between 1.05^n and 1.046^n times $1000 grow by the same amount each year? Why?

Check your answer

No. Each year's extra also earns, so the gap grows faster each year.

Money in an account is multiplied by the same factor each year, so after $n$ years it's $1000 \times 1.046^n$.

If you round the factor to 1.05, the error isn't added each year: it is multiplied in, so the gap grows faster and faster. Keep the exact factor all the way and round once, at the end.

Year 10 Algebra

Powers of terms

Try firstSimplify (3x²)³.

Check your answer

27x⁶.

A power outside a bracket applies to every part inside: $(2k)^3 = 2^3 \times k^3 = 8k^3$.

Divide terms by dividing the numbers and subtracting the indices. If the index comes out negative, write the letter underneath: $4k^2 \div 8k^3 = \dfrac{1}{2k}$.

Special products

Try firstExpand (x − 6)².

Check your answer

x² − 12x + 36.

A bracket squared is the bracket times itself: $(x + 3)^2 = (x + 3)(x + 3) = x^2 + 3x + 3x + 9 = x^2 + 6x + 9$. There are four pieces: a square, two strips and a corner. It isn't $x^2 + 9$.

With a minus sign, the strips are red and the corner is still positive: $(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9$.

When the brackets differ only in their sign, the strips cancel: $(x + 3)(x - 3) = x^2 - 3x + 3x - 9 = x^2 - 9$.

Factorising when x² has a number

Try firstFactorise 2x² + 11x + 5.

Check your answer

(2x + 1)(x + 5).

When there's a number in front of $x^2$, the two numbers for the strips multiply to $a \times c$, not $c$. For $2x^2 + 7x + 3$: $2 \times 3 = 6$, and $1$ and $6$ add to $7$.

Split the middle term and group: $2x^2 + 6x + x + 3 = 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = (2x + 1)(x + 3)$. Check by expanding.

Completing the square

Try firstWrite x² + 6x + 1 as (x + 3)² + k. What is k?

Check your answer

−8: the corner needs 9 and there is 1, so k = 1 − 9 = −8.

A square with sides $x + 3$ is an $x$ by $x$ square, two strips of $3x$, and a corner of 9. That is $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$.

If you have $x^2 + 6x + 5$, you have the square and the strips but only 5 ones for a corner that needs 9. So it is the square, with 4 ones taken away: $x^2 + 6x + 5 = (x + 3)^2 - 4$.

A square is never below zero, so the lowest value of $(x + 3)^2 - 4$ is $-4$, when $x = -3$.

Solving by reshaping

Try firstA rectangle's length is 2 m more than its width, and its area is 15 m². Find the width.

Check your answer

3 m: w(w + 2) = 15, so (w + 5)(w − 3) = 0 and w = 3 (w = −5 is thrown out).

A photo 30 cm by 20 cm with a border $x$ all the way round has sides $30 + 2x$ and $20 + 2x$, because the border is at both ends.

The framed area is $(30 + 2x)(20 + 2x) = 4x^2 + 100x + 600$. Setting it to 1200 and moving everything across gives $4x^2 + 100x - 600 = 0$, which is $x^2 + 25x - 150 = 0$ after dividing by 4. That factorises as $(x + 30)(x - 5) = 0$.

Both $x = -30$ and $x = 5$ solve the equation, but a border can't be negative, so the answer is 5 cm.

Solving inequalities

Try firstYou have $50 and a day costs $14 entry plus $3.50 a ride. What is the most whole rides?

Check your answer

10: 14 + 3.5r ≤ 50, so r ≤ 10.28, and 10 whole rides.

An entry of $12 and $4.50 a ride costs $12 + 4.5r$ for $r$ rides. With $40 to spend, $12 + 4.5r \le 40$.

Solve it like an equation: $4.5r \le 28$, so $r \le 6.2$. If you divide by a negative, reverse the sign.

The solution is a range, but rides come whole, so the context picks the values: the most is 6 rides, which costs $39. Seven would cost $43.50.

Regions from a budget

Try firstTickets cost 10 dollars and snacks 5 dollars. The budget is 60 dollars. Where does the boundary of 10t + 5s ≤ 60 meet each axis?

Check your answer

t-axis (s = 0): 10t = 60, t = 6. s-axis (t = 0): 5s = 60, s = 12. The line joins (6, 0) and (0, 12).

A budget with two kinds of item is an inequality in two letters: $24p + 12d \le 120$.

To graph it, put each letter equal to 0 in turn. With $p = 0$: $12d = 120$ so $d = 10$. With $d = 0$: $24p = 120$ so $p = 5$. Draw a solid line through $(5, 0)$ and $(0, 10)$, then test $(0, 0)$ to shade the side that fits.

Every point in the region is a plan within the budget. Only the whole-number points are plans the shop can sell.

Solving by substitution

Try firstSolve y = 3x and x + y = 20.

Check your answer

x + 3x = 20, so 4x = 20, x = 5 and y = 15. Check: 5 + 15 = 20.

When one equation says what a letter equals, substitute it into the other equation. With $c = 12 + 4g$ and $c = 30 + g$, both rules equal $c$, so $12 + 4g = 30 + g$. Solve to get $g = 6$, then put it back in a rule to find $c = 36$.

Put the replacement in brackets: for $5c - 2g = 21$ with $c = g + 3$, write $5(g + 3) - 2g = 21$, not $5g + 3 - 2g$.

A false result such as $10 = 14$ means the lines are parallel and there is no solution. A true result such as $0 = 0$ means the lines are the same and every point is a solution.

Solving by elimination

Try firstSolve 2x + y = 11 and 2x + 3y = 19.

Check your answer

Subtract: 2y = 8, so y = 4. Then 2x + 4 = 11, so x = 3.5. Check: 7 + 12 = 19.

Elimination adds or subtracts whole equations so a letter cancels. For $4v + 2k = 44$ and $4v + 5k = 62$, subtract the first from the second: $3k = 18$, so $k = 6$. Put $k$ back to find $v = 8$. Check in the other equation.

If no coefficients match, multiply an equation (both sides) first. If the letter's terms have opposite signs, add.

A false result such as $0 = 4$ means no solution (parallel lines). A true result such as $0 = 0$ means infinitely many (the same line).

Setting up equations

Try firstA plan is 90 cents a day plus 30 cents per kWh. Write the cost c for a day with k kWh, and find c when k = 10.

Check your answer

c = 90 + 30k. For k = 10: 90 + 300 = 390 cents, which is 3.90 dollars.

To set up a problem from a situation:

  1. Name the letters, with units: $k$ is the kWh used in a day, $c$ is the cost for the day in cents.
  2. Write each rule in one unit: Plan A is $c = 120 + 24k$, Plan B is $c = 72 + 28k$.
  3. Solve: set the rules equal, $120 + 24k = 72 + 28k$, so $k = 12$ and $c = 408$.
  4. Say it in the situation's words: at 12 kWh a day the plans cost the same, 408 cents a day. Above that Plan A is cheaper.
  5. Check it makes sense: no negative kWh and no half tickets.

Same ratio each time

Try firstA table goes 3, 12, 48, 192. Is it linear or exponential? What is the next value?

Check your answer

Exponential: each value is 4 times the one before. The next value is 768.

A table is exponential when each value is the same number of times the one before. That number is the ratio: divide a value by the one before it.

A linear table has a constant difference instead: the same amount added each step. $5, 10, 15, 20$ is linear. $5, 10, 20, 40$ is exponential, with ratio 2.

A ratio below 1 is still exponential: $80, 40, 20, 10$ has ratio 0.5.

Exponential graphs

Try firstFor y = 10(0.5ˣ): where does it cross the y-axis, does it grow or decay, and what line does it get close to but never reach?

Check your answer

At 10; decay; y = 0.

In $y = a(b^x)$, $a$ is where the graph crosses the $y$-axis, because $b^0 = 1$. $b$ is what you multiply by each step.

If $b > 1$ the graph grows. If $0 < b < 1$ it decays: the values get smaller but never reach 0. The line $y = 0$ is the asymptote: the graph gets as close as you like but never touches it.

Same base both sides

Try firstSolve 9ˣ = 81, then solve 4ˣ = 8.

Check your answer

9ˣ = 81: x = 2. 4ˣ = 8: 2²ˣ = 2³, so x = 1.5.

To solve an equation with $x$ in the power, write both sides as powers of the same base. If $2^x = 2^6$ then $x = 6$.

The base isn't multiplying $x$, so you can't divide by it. $3^x = 81$ means 3 multiplied $x$ times, which is $3^4$.

If the bases differ, find a base that makes both. $8^x = 32$: $8 = 2^3$, $32 = 2^5$, so $2^{3x} = 2^5$ and $x = \dfrac{5}{3}$.

Which model?

Try firstA table goes 80, 100, 125, 156.25. Linear, quadratic or exponential? Show the test.

Check your answer

Exponential: 100 ÷ 80 = 1.25, 125 ÷ 100 = 1.25 and 156.25 ÷ 125 = 1.25. The differences 20, 25, 31.25 are not equal.

To choose a model from a table, test it three ways, in order:

  1. First differences. If they are equal, it's a line.
  2. Second differences (differences of the differences). If they are equal, it's a quadratic.
  3. Ratios. Divide each value by the one before. If they are equal, it's an exponential.

Say which test worked, and show the numbers.

Writing the model

Try firstA phone loses 20% of its value each year and costs 900 new. Write the model and find its value after 3 years.

Check your answer

A = 900(0.8ⁿ). After 3 years: 900 × 0.8³ = 460.80 dollars.

A rate a year changes the balance by the same share each year, so the model multiplies.

  • Growth of r%: factor $b = 1 + r/100$, for example 5% is 1.05.
  • Decay of r%: factor $b = 1 - r/100$, for example 8% is 0.92.

The model is $A = a(b^n)$: $a$ is the starting amount and $n$ is the number of years. Always give the answer in dollars (or the unit in the question).

Circles as relations

Try firstA circle has equation (x − 2)² + (y + 5)² = 36. What are its centre and radius? Is it a function?

Check your answer

Centre (2, −5), radius 6. It isn't a function: a vertical line through the centre meets it twice.

A circle is every point a fixed distance, the radius $r$, from a centre $(h, k)$. Its equation is

$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$

  • The centre is where each bracket is zero: $(x - 3)$ gives $h = 3$, and $(y + 1)$ gives $k = -1$.
  • The right side is the radius squared.
  • A circle is a relation, but not a function: most $x$ values have two $y$ values.

Judging the model

Try firstA model for a colony says 200 × 1.5ʰ cells after h hours. A dish holds 10,000 cells. In which whole hour does the model first pass the dish, and what should you say about hours after it?

Check your answer

200 × 1.5¹⁰ = 11,533 and 200 × 1.5⁹ = 7,689, so hour 10. After it the model is not reliable, because the dish is full.

A model is judged by what it says, whether it matches data, what it assumes and how far out it can be trusted.

  • Interpret: say what the result means in the situation, with units.
  • Test: compare the model with a real measurement; change an input such as the doubling time if it is off.
  • Assumptions: the doubling time stays the same however many cells there are.
  • Range: past the point where the food or room runs out, the model's numbers describe something that does not exist.

Make a conjecture

Try firstIn y = ax², Mia says 'if a is bigger, the curve is narrower'. Write one test that could show her wrong, and say what you'd expect to see.

Check your answer

Any negative pair: a = −1 and a = −3. −1 is bigger, but y = −x² is wider than y = −3x². One case that disagrees rejects the claim; she could change it to 'if a is further from 0'.

A conjecture is a claim about a whole family of cases that you can test: "If $a$ is bigger, then the ball lands sooner."

  • State it as if-then, so a new case could show it wrong.
  • Test it by changing one number at a time, and include a case that might break it (zero, a negative, a very small number).
  • Cases that agree support it; they don't prove it. One case that disagrees rejects it, and you can then refine the claim.

Reading log scales

Try firstHow many hours is it safe to stay at 94 dB, if 85 dB allows 8 hours and every 3 dB halves it?

Year 10 Space

Some cases or every case?

Try firstMia measured the diagonals of 6 rectangles and they were equal each time. Is that a proof that every rectangle's diagonals are equal? Say why.

Check your answer

No. Six rectangles are six cases. A proof gives a reason that works for every rectangle, such as two congruent triangles.

A demonstration checks some cases and shows the claim works for those. A proof gives a reason that covers every case at once.

Given

Try firstOf 60 students, 24 play football and 9 play football and tennis. What is the chance a football player plays tennis? What is the chance a tennis player plays football if 18 play tennis?

Check your answer

9/24 = 0.375, then 9/18 = 0.5. The group changes, so the answer changes.

A chance "given" something is found inside the group where that thing is true.

  • Spot the given: "given", "knowing that", "if … then" and "of the" point at the group.
  • Shrink the whole: only the days or people in that group are counted.
  • Divide: count what you want inside the group, then divide by the size of the group.
  • Swapping the events changes the group, so P(A | B) is usually not P(B | A).
