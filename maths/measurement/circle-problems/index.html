Circle problems

Choose the formula by what you need: C = 2πr for a length round the edge, A = πr² for the space inside. Work backwards from an area with r = √(A ÷ π). Half and quarter circles, rings and tracks are parts of circles added to or taken from other shapes.

A running track, a round garden bed, a pizza deal: is a 30 cm pizza more than twice a 20 cm one? Circle problems need the right formula, sometimes run backwards.

What you should already know:
  • Area of a circle · Cut a circle into thin slices and lay them top to tail: they make a shape close to a rectangle, πr long and r high.

Ask first: is the answer a length round the edge (circumference, C=2πrC = 2\pi rC=2πr) or the space inside (area, A=πr2A = \pi r^2A=πr2)?

How this lesson works: Predict, Try, Investigate, Modify, Practise, Make

  • Try: Try it on the board and compare the result with your prediction.
  • Investigate: Work out why it happens, one step at a time.
  • Modify: Change the problem on purpose and see what happens.
  • Practise: A few on your own, to check you have it.
  • Make: Make your own problem using what you have learned. Expect a few tries; that is normal.

One large or two mediums, on paper

Do this one in your book, not on the screen. The sizes are from a typical menu; prices are left out. Write each radius, square it, and keep the units, then type the difference.

Also used in

  • Food. Menu sizes are widths, but the pizza you eat is area, so a bigger pizza is bigger than it looks. Value is area per dollar.
  • Farming. From the air, dry-country farms are green circles watered by an arm turning about a centre. The arm is the radius, and the corners of the paddock are what is left.
  • Garden beds. The edging is a circumference and the soil is an area.

For teachers

Students choose between circumference and area, find a radius from an area, and solve problems with semicircles, quarter circles, rings and tracks (AC9M8M03, node 2). About 40 minutes.

Open the teacher guide: answers, solutions, slides and a worksheet.

Students will be able to

  • I can choose the circumference or the area formula for a problem.
  • I can find the radius from an area.
  • I can solve problems with parts of circles.

Before class

Do the Run board yourself first. Students need the area of a circle (previous lesson) and square roots.

The one idea: ask whether the answer is a length (C = 2πr) or a space (A = πr²); to go back from an area, divide by π and take the square root. The common mistake is stopping at r² (Leo's mistake).

Answers accept π from the calculator or 3.14; ask students to say which they used.

Wider: composite shapes with circles (a stadium, a keyhole), and circles in First Nations Australian weaving designs, where the context is shared respectfully with local knowledge.

Running the lesson

  1. Predict: ask for the two steps of πr² in order.
  2. Run: ask what area radius 1.5 gives, before moving the slider.
  3. Investigate: ask Leo to check his radius forwards.
  4. Modify: ask how the two semicircles of the track make one circle.
  5. Make: ask which formula the edging needs.
  6. Paper: one large against two mediums is worked in books; the page marks the difference and the reason for halving each width, then shows a model. Books out, and look for two radii squared, not the widths.

The hard part (on purpose)

Working backwards from an area. Ask students to write the two steps of πr² forwards before they undo them.

When students get stuck

  • A student stops at r² when working backwards. Ask: Is 15.9 the radius, or the radius squared? Fix: Take the square root.
  • A student finds a semicircle's perimeter as half the circumference only. Ask: Walk round the semicircle: what straight part is there? Fix: Add the diameter.
  • A student uses the diameter in πr². Ask: Radius or diameter? Fix: Halve the diameter first.

Stretch and support

Stretch: Is a 30 cm pizza more than twice as much as a 20 cm pizza? Compare their areas and prices from a real menu.

Support: A two-column card: 'length round the edge' (C = 2πr) and 'space inside' (A = πr²).

Where it leads

This completes AC9M8M03.

Where it leads: cylinders (Year 9), sectors and arc lengths (Year 10).

What each step is for

  • a round rug: Committing to a radius from an area shows whether they undo π and the square, or divide only.
  • find the radius: Searching for the radius that gives the area shows the two steps that undo A = πr².
  • backwards and in parts: Undo π then the square (Leo forgets the square root); a semicircle is half the area.
  • tracks and rings: Composite problems with circles: a semicircle, a running track's area and perimeter, and a target area on the board.
  • circle problems: Radius from area, parts of circles, a ring and a semicircle's perimeter, mixed; forgetting the square root is the planted slip.
  • a garden bed: Choosing a radius to meet an area range uses the formula backwards as a prediction, checked on the board.
  • one large or two mediums, on paper: Widths on a menu are diameters; the area of each pizza needs the radius squared, so the large is much bigger than it looks and two mediums can still beat it.
