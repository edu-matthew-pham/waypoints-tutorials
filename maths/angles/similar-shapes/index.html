Similar shapes

Similar shapes are the same shape but can be different sizes, like a photo and its enlargement. Find the scale factor, use it to work out missing sides, and learn the conditions that make two triangles similar.

How can you find the height of a tree without climbing it? Its shadow and your own shadow make two triangles of the same shape.

Explain it to me

Similar shapes are the same shape, maybe a different size. Every length is multiplied by the same scale factor k = copy ÷ original, and the angles stay the same. Congruent is similar with k = 1.

Triangles are similar by AA, SSS (sides in one ratio) or SAS (two sides in one ratio, the angle between them equal). For other shapes, check both the angles and the ratios of the sides.

Double the sides

In an enlargement, every length is multiplied by the same scale factor kkk, and every angle stays the same. Congruent shapes are similar with k=1k = 1k=1.

k=copy lengthmatching original lengthk = \frac{\text{copy length}}{\text{matching original length}}k=matching original lengthcopy length​

Triangles are similar when any one of these holds:

Condition What matches
AA two angles equal (so the third is too)
SSS all three pairs of sides in the same ratio
SAS two pairs of sides in the same ratio, and the angle between them equal

Translations, reflections and rotations move a shape without changing its size, so they make congruent shapes. An enlargement makes a similar shape.

For teachers

Students scale a triangle by a scale factor, find missing sides of similar shapes, and compare rectangles that are similar, congruent or neither (AC9M8SP01, nodes 2 and 3). About 40 minutes.

Open the teacher guide: answers, solutions, slides and a worksheet.

Students will be able to

  • I can find a scale factor from a pair of matching sides.
  • I can use a scale factor to find a missing side.
  • I can say whether two shapes are congruent, similar or neither, and why.

Before class

Do the Run board yourself first. Students need congruent triangles (last lesson) and multiplying by decimals.

The one idea: similar means every length times the same scale factor, with the angles unchanged. The common mistake is adding a fixed amount to every side (Mia's mistake).

Similarity conditions for triangles: AA, SSS (all sides in one ratio) and SAS (two sides in one ratio, the included angle equal). Only AA is practised much here; SSS appears in Practise. The link to transformations: translations, reflections and rotations give congruent shapes; an enlargement gives a similar shape.

Wider: measure something tall outside with shadows and a metre ruler.

Running the lesson

  1. Predict: ask what doubling does to a corner's angle.
  2. Run: ask what each copy side ÷ original side gives.
  3. Investigate: ask Mia whether her photo would look stretched.
  4. Modify: ask why two rectangles with all 90° angles may not be similar.
  5. Make: ask which original side matches y.

The hard part (on purpose)

Matching the sides. Ask students to say which side matches which (shortest with shortest) before they divide.

When students get stuck

  • A student adds the same amount to every side. Ask: Would the picture look the same shape? Fix: Find k by dividing, then multiply every side by k.
  • A student divides the wrong way (original ÷ copy). Ask: Is the copy bigger or smaller? Should k be more or less than 1? Fix: k = copy ÷ original.
  • A student says all rectangles are similar because the angles match. Ask: Is a 4 by 2 rectangle the same shape as a 4 by 3? Fix: For shapes other than triangles, check the sides' ratios too.

Stretch and support

Stretch: A similar triangle has scale factor 3. How many times bigger is its area? Test it with the 3, 4, 5 triangle.

Support: A table with original sides in one column and copy sides in the next, with a ÷ column for k.

Where it leads

This covers AC9M8SP01 nodes 2 and 3.

Where it leads: proving quadrilateral properties with congruent triangles (next lesson), congruence algorithms, and trigonometry in Year 10 (which rests on similar right-angled triangles).

What each step is for

  • double the sides: Committing to what happens to the angles when sides double shows whether they confuse lengths with angles.
  • scale a triangle: Changing the scale factor shows every side multiplied by the same number and the shape unchanged.
  • scale factors: Find k from a matching pair and multiply; adding a fixed amount is the slip (Mia); AA is enough because the third angle follows.
  • rectangles and shadows: Rectangles can have equal angles and still not be similar; similar triangles measure a tree from shadows; all squares are similar but not all rectangles.
  • similar shapes: Finding k, missing sides and reductions, mixed; adding instead of multiplying is the planted slip; transformations link congruence and similarity; the congruence review links the two ideas.
  • find the scale factor: Working back from one copy side to k shows they can find a scale factor and use it.
