Solving harder equations

Get the x terms on one side and the numbers on the other, doing the same to both sides; expand any brackets first. Then check by substituting into the first equation.

Two phone plans: 7 dollars plus 3 a gigabyte, or 5 a gigabyte minus a 9 dollar credit. When do they cost the same? 3x + 7 = 5x − 9.

In Year 7 you solved equations with the balance method, even with xxx on both sides. Year 8 equations add negatives, brackets and fractions, where blocks on a scale no longer help. The rule is the same: do the same to both sides, until xxx is on its own.

Then check: put your answer back into the first equation. If both sides are equal, it is right.

  • Predict: Read the problem and decide what you think before you try anything. A wrong guess is fine: it shows you what to look for.
  • Try: Try it on the board and compare the result with your prediction.
  • Investigate: Work out why it happens, one step at a time.
  • Modify: Change the problem on purpose and see what happens.
  • Practise: A few on your own, to check you have it.
  • Make: Make your own problem using what you have learned. Expect a few tries; that is normal.

Explain it to me

To solve an equation, do the same to both sides until xxx is on its own:

  1. Expand any brackets.
  2. Take the smaller xxx term from both sides, so xxx is on one side only.
  3. Undo the numbers with the inverse operation (add to undo −-−, subtract to undo +++).
  4. Divide (or multiply) to get xxx.

3x+7=5x−9→7=2x−9→16=2x→x=83x + 7 = 5x - 9 \to 7 = 2x - 9 \to 16 = 2x \to x = 83x+7=5x−9→7=2x−9→16=2x→x=8. Check: 3(8)+7=313(8) + 7 = 313(8)+7=31 and 5(8)−9=315(8) - 9 = 315(8)−9=31. On a graph, x=8x = 8x=8 is where the two sides meet.

          For teachers

          Students solve linear equations with x on both sides, brackets, negative answers and fractions by doing the same to both sides, writing one step per line, and verify every answer by substitution; a graph shows the solution as where the two sides meet (AC9M8A02, node 2). About 50 minutes.

          Open the teacher guide: answers, solutions, slides and a worksheet.

          Students will be able to

          • I can solve an equation with x on both sides, including negatives.
          • I can solve an equation with brackets by expanding first.
          • I can check my solution by substituting it into the first equation.

          Before class

          Do the working check yourself first. Students need The balance method (Year 7), Expanding brackets, and adding and multiplying negatives.

          This is a hinge node: inequalities (next) use the same moves, and sign slips carry over. The common mistake is undoing a term with its own sign (taking 7 away to undo − 7); it is planted in Ben's working and again in Practise.

          The working check marks each line against the one before, so students see the first step that went wrong; the last line must be x = …

          Running the lesson

          1. Predict: take a show of hands, then ask how students chose: trying values is fine, and leads to checking.
          2. Run: ask what P's y-coordinate means (the value of both sides).
          3. Investigate: model the worked example, saying why the smaller x term moves (so x stays positive).
          4. Modify: for the no-solution task, ask what the graph would look like.
          5. Make: collect students' equations and solve one together.

          When students get stuck

          • A student takes 7 away to undo − 7 (Ben's mistake). Ask: What undoes taking 7 away? Fix: Use the inverse: add 7 to both sides.
          • A student leaves x on both sides after the first step. Ask: Which x term will you remove from both sides? Fix: Take the smaller x term from both sides first.
          • A student expands only the first term of a bracket. Ask: What does the number outside multiply? Fix: Every term inside: 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6.

          Stretch and support

          Stretch: Solve (x + 4)/2 = (x − 1)/3. (Multiply both sides by 6: 3x + 12 = 2x − 2, x = −14.)

          Support: Use the Run graph to check answers: drag P to the meeting point.

          Where it leads

          Next: Inequalities (node 3), which solve the same way except when multiplying or dividing by a negative.

          Where it leads: simultaneous equations (Year 9 and 10), and rearranging formulas with the subject in two places.

          What each step is for

          • when do they cost the same?: Committing to a solution of 3x + 7 = 5x − 9 before solving shows whether they can reason about both sides; the graph then shows the solution as where the two sides are equal.
          • where both sides are equal: Each side of the equation is a line; the solution is where they meet, because there both sides have the same value.
          • same to both sides: Move the smaller x term (so the x term stays positive), then the numbers, then divide; substitute to check. Ben's mistake takes 7 away to undo − 7, instead of adding it.
          • one change at a time: One change at a time: a bracket, a negative answer, a fraction, brackets on both sides, then the edge case of an equation with no solution.
          • solve and check: Equations with x on both sides, negatives and a geometry context, mixed; the inverse-sign slip is planted again; the review keeps expanding fresh.
          • build an equation: Choosing a second line that meets y = 2x + 1 at x = 3 builds an equation with that solution, reversing the lesson: from the answer to the equation.
