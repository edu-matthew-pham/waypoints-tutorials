Using rates

Use a rate to find a total (rate × amount), or the amount (total ÷ rate). For speed: distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. Show the units at every step, and check the answer makes sense.

How much will it cost to fill the car? How long is the drive to the coast? How many dollars for your holiday money? Each answer comes from one rate, used the right way round.

What you should already know:
  • Rates · A rate compares two quantities with different units: 60 km per hour, $4 per kg, 80 beats per minute.

A rate links two quantities, so you can go either way:

  • total = rate × amount: 30 L of petrol at $1.80 per L costs 30×1.8030 × 1.8030×1.80 = $54.00;
  • amount = total ÷ rate: $54.00 at $1.80 per L buys 54÷1.80=3054 \div 1.80 = 3054÷1.80=30 L.

For speed, distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. Write the units at each step: they tell you whether to multiply or divide (km ÷ km/h = h). Then check: is the answer about the right size? The petrol prices and the exchange rate in this lesson are made up for the task.

How this lesson works: Predict, Try, Investigate, Modify, Practise, Make
  • Predict: Read the problem and decide what you think before you try anything. A wrong guess is fine: it shows you what to look for.
  • Try: Try it on the board and compare the result with your prediction.
  • Investigate: Work out why it happens, one step at a time.
  • Modify: Change the problem on purpose and see what happens.
  • Practise: A few on your own, to check you have it.
  • Make: Make your own problem using what you have learned. Expect a few tries; that is normal.

For teachers

Students use rates both ways (total = rate × amount, amount = total ÷ rate) for petrol, speed, water and exchange rates, letting the units decide multiply or divide (AC9M8M05, node 2). About 35 minutes.

Open the teacher guide: answers, solutions, slides and a worksheet.

Students will be able to

  • I can use a rate to find a total or an amount.
  • I can use distance, speed and time in any order.
  • I can check an answer with its units and its size.

Before class

Do the Run board yourself first. Students need rates and unit rates (previous lesson).

The one idea: a rate works both ways; the units decide whether to multiply or divide. The common mistake is multiplying distance by speed to find a time (Leo's mistake).

The exchange rate in the lesson (0.65 US dollars per Australian dollar) is a round example, not today's rate.

Wider: plan holiday spending with a current exchange rate (and its fees); compare two phone plans.

Running the lesson

  1. Predict: ask what 40 ÷ 2.05 would mean.
  2. Run: ask how much one more litre adds.
  3. Investigate: ask Leo whether 9000 hours makes sense.
  4. Modify: ask why the gap grows by the same amount each hour.
  5. Make: ask what the model leaves out.
  6. Paper: the drip-rate task in Make is worked in books; the page marks the answer (50) and the reason for multiplying by the drop factor. Books out, then look for the units written beside each step.

The hard part (on purpose)

Choosing to multiply or divide. Ask students to write the units into the calculation (km ÷ km/h = h) before they press a key.

When students get stuck

  • A student multiplies to find a time. Ask: What units does your answer have? Fix: km ÷ km/h gives hours: divide.
  • A student writes 2.5 hours as 2 h 50 min. Ask: What is half an hour in minutes? Fix: 0.5 h = 30 min.
  • A student converts money the wrong way. Ask: Should you get more or fewer US dollars than Australian dollars? Fix: Check the size: 1 AUD buys less than 1 USD here.

Stretch and support

Stretch: Plan a trip budget overseas: look up today's exchange rate, convert three prices, and include a 3% card fee.

Support: A card: total = rate × amount; amount = total ÷ rate; units in every line.

Where it leads

This completes AC9M8M05.

Where it leads: rate models (next lessons), gradient as a rate (Linear relations), and speed in science.

What each step is for

  • fill the tank: Committing to a cost from a price per litre shows whether they multiply the rate by the amount.
  • the petrol bowser: Moving the litres along the double number line shows the cost growing by the rate for every litre.
  • multiply or divide: Units decide multiply or divide (Leo multiplies to find a time); currency conversion is a rate too.
  • race, shower, money back: Comparing two rates over time, working backwards from a total, and converting money both ways.
  • using rates: Totals, distances, times and a conversion, mixed; multiplying when the units say divide is the planted slip; the speed conversion review keeps units fresh.
  • plan a drive: Choosing a speed and time for a fixed distance uses distance = speed × time in both directions.
  • a drip rate, on paper: Two rates chained: mL per hour, then drops per hour, then drops per minute. The units say what to multiply and what to divide.

  • Use distance, speed and time in any order.
  • Check an answer with its units and its size.
