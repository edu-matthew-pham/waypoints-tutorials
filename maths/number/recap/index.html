Number Foundations: recap

Answer each question in your head or on paper first, then check it.

Integers

1. Comparing integers

Try firstPut in order from least to greatest: 4, −6, 0, −1, −10.

Check your answer

−10, −6, −1, 0, 4.

On a number line, the number further left is less. That is true below zero too.

$-7$ is further left than $-2$, so $-7 < -2$ ("$-7$ is less than $-2$"), even though 7 is more than 2. Think of temperatures: $-7$ °C is colder than $-2$ °C.

To put integers in order, place each on the line and read from left to right. Zero is greater than every negative number.

2. Adding and subtracting integers

Try firstWork out −3 + 7, 2 − 9 and 4 − (−5).

Check your answer

4, −7 and 9.

To add or subtract integers, start at the first number and move along the number line.

  • Adding a positive moves right: $-3 + 5 = 2$.
  • Adding a negative moves left: $4 + (-6) = -2$.
  • Subtracting moves the opposite way to adding. Adding $-3$ would move left 3, so subtracting $-3$ moves right 3: $5 - (-3) = 8$.

So subtracting a negative gives the same answer as adding the positive.

3. Multiplying and dividing integers

Try firstWork out −7 × (−4), 24 ÷ (−6) and (−1)⁴.

Check your answer

28, −4 and 1.

$a \times b$ is $a$ jumps of $b$ from zero. $3 \times (-2)$ is 3 jumps of $-2$: left to $-6$.

A negative number of jumps goes the opposite way: $-3 \times (-2) = 6$. The pattern agrees: $3 \times (-2) = -6$, $2 \times (-2) = -4$, $1 \times (-2) = -2$, $0 \times (-2) = 0$, so $-1 \times (-2) = 2$.

The rule: same signs give a positive, different signs give a negative. Dividing follows the same rule, because it undoes multiplying: $-12 \div 3 = -4$, since $3 \times (-4) = -12$.

Squares, powers and primes

4. Squares and square roots

Try firstA square has area 81 cm². Find its side and its perimeter.

Check your answer

Side √81 = 9 cm; perimeter 4 × 9 = 36 cm.

A square number is a number times itself: $6^2 = 6 \times 6 = 36$. It is the area of a square with side 6. The perfect squares to 144 are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121 and 144.

The square root undoes squaring: $\sqrt{36} = 6$ because $6 \times 6 = 36$. It is the side of a square with area 36. (Squared is not doubled: $5^2 = 25$, not 10.)

5. Between the squares

Try firstBetween which two whole numbers is √55? Give a reason.

Check your answer

7 and 8, because 7² = 49 and 8² = 64, and 55 is between them.

For a number that is not a perfect square, find the perfect squares either side: $36 < 43 < 49$. Their roots give the whole numbers either side: $6 < \sqrt{43} < 7$.

To get closer, square decimals: $6.5^2 = 42.25$ and $6.6^2 = 43.56$. The closer one wins: $\sqrt{43} \approx 6.6$. (A square root is not a half: $\sqrt{50}$ is about 7, not 25.)

6. Powers

Try firstWork out 3³ and write 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 as a power.

Check your answer

3³ = 27; 5⁴.

Exponent notation is short for repeated multiplication: $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2^4$. The base (2) is multiplied; the exponent (4) counts how many times. So $2^4 = 16$, not $2 \times 4 = 8$.

Squares are powers with exponent 2 ($5^2 = 25$) and cubes exponent 3 ($2^3 = 8$). Powers of 10 count zeros: $10^3 = 1000$.

7. Prime factors

Try firstWrite 84 as a product of prime factors, with exponents.

Check your answer

84 = 2² × 3 × 7.

A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself (1 is not prime). A factor tree splits a number into a factor pair, then splits every branch that is not prime, until all the branches are primes.

The prime factorisation collects them with exponents: $36 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 2^2 \times 3^2$. Every tree for 36 ends with these primes. Check by expanding: $4 \times 9 = 36$.

8. HCF and LCM

Try firstFind the HCF and LCM of 20 and 30.

Check your answer

20 = 2² × 5 and 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. HCF = 2 × 5 = 10; LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60.

Write both numbers as products of primes: $12 = 2^2 \times 3$ and $18 = 2 \times 3^2$.

The HCF is the product of the primes they share (each to its lower power): $2 \times 3 = 6$. The LCM is the product of all the primes, each shared one counted once (each to its higher power): $2^2 \times 3^2 = 36$. Multiplying the two numbers ($12 \times 18 = 216$) counts the shared primes twice, so it is a common multiple but not usually the lowest.

Fractions and decimals

9. Equivalent fractions

Try firstWrite 20/32 in its simplest form, and give one other fraction equal to it.

Check your answer

5/8 (÷ 4). For example 10/16 or 15/24.

Fractions are equivalent when they name the same amount. Multiply or divide the top and the bottom (the numerator and the denominator) by the same number: $\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \tfrac{8}{12}$. (Adding the same number to both changes the amount.)

A fraction is in simplest form when the top and the bottom share no factor but 1. Divide both by their highest common factor: $\tfrac{12}{18} = \tfrac{2}{3}$ (÷ 6).

10. Fractions, decimals and percentages

Try firstWrite 3/5 as a decimal and as a percentage, and 0.08 as a percentage.

Check your answer

0.6 and 60%; 0.08 = 8%.

Percent means out of 100, so a percentage is a fraction over 100: $75% = \tfrac{75}{100} = \tfrac{3}{4}$. A decimal counts tenths and hundredths: $0.75$ is 75 hundredths.

  • Fraction to decimal: divide the top by the bottom ($\tfrac{3}{8} = 0.375$).
  • Decimal to percentage: × 100 ($0.5 = 50%$, not 5%).
  • Percentage to fraction: over 100, then simplify ($16% = \tfrac{4}{25}$).

Some decimals never end: $\tfrac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$

11. Fractions and decimals on the line

Try firstPut in order from least to greatest: 0.4, −1 1/4, −0.8, −1/2.

Check your answer

−1 1/4, −0.8, −1/2, 0.4.

To place a fraction or decimal, split each unit into equal parts and count from zero. Quarters: split each unit into 4. Tenths: into 10.

A negative number counts to the left of zero. $-\tfrac{3}{4}$ is three quarters left of 0. A negative mixed numeral or decimal is further left still: $-1.5$ is one unit left, then half a unit further left, halfway between $-2$ and $-1$.

Once they are on the line, read from left to right to put them in order.

12. Adding and subtracting fractions

Try firstWork out 2/3 + 1/5 and 1 1/4 − 2/3.

Check your answer

10/15 + 3/15 = 13/15. 15/12 − 8/12 = 7/12.

Fractions can be added only when the parts are the same size. Cut both into the same parts with equivalent fractions, using a common denominator (the LCM of the bottoms is the lowest):

$\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{4}{12} + \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac{7}{12}$. Then add the tops and keep the bottom. (Adding across gives $\tfrac{2}{7}$, which is less than $\tfrac{1}{3}$ alone.)

For mixed numerals, change them to improper fractions first. For decimals, line up the points so tenths go with tenths.

13. Multiplying and dividing fractions

Try firstWork out 3/5 × 10/12, and 2 ÷ 1/3.

Check your answer

30/60 = 1/2. 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6 (six thirds in 2).

To multiply fractions, multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms, then simplify: $\tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{6}{12} = \tfrac{1}{2}$. It is $\tfrac{3}{4}$ of $\tfrac{2}{3}$: in a square, 3 of 4 rows of 2 of 3 columns is 6 of 12 parts.

Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes a number smaller ($\tfrac{3}{4} \times 20 = 15$). Dividing asks how many fit: $3 \div \tfrac{1}{4} = 12$, the same as $3 \times 4$. To divide by a fraction, multiply by it flipped (top and bottom swapped: $\tfrac{1}{6}$ becomes $\tfrac{6}{1} = 6$).

14. Percentages of amounts

Try firstA $40 shirt is 35% off. Find the sale price.

Check your answer

35% of $40 = $14, so $26. Or 40 × 0.65 = 26.

A percentage is hundredths: $15% = 0.15$. So 15% of $80 is 0.15 × 80 = $12 (or 10% is $8 and 5% is $4).

For a decrease, take it off or multiply by what is left: $80 \times 0.85 = 68$. For an increase, add it on or multiply by $1.15$. To find what percentage 18 is of 24, divide and $\times 100$: $18 \div 24 = 0.75 = 75%$.

15. Choosing a strategy

Try firstWork out 5 × 3.6 × 2 and 40% of 15, each in a quick way. Say which move you used.

Check your answer

5 × 2 = 10, then × 3.6 = 36 (any order). 40% = 2/5: 15 ÷ 5 × 2 = 6 (change the form).

A quick strategy usually comes from one of three moves:

  • Change the order: multiplying can be done in any order, $4 \times 2.7 \times 25 = 100 \times 2.7 = 270$.
  • Change the form: $0.75 = \tfrac{3}{4}$, so $0.75 \times 36 = 27$; $25% = \tfrac{1}{4}$.
  • Split a number: $12 \times 2.5 = 12 \times 2 + 12 \times 0.5 = 30$.

Then check with an estimate: is the answer about the right size?

16. Calculating with negative fractions and decimals

Try firstWork out −3/5 × 10/9 and −4 × (−2.25). Show one strategy.

Check your answer

−2/3. 9 (−4 × −2 = 8, −4 × −0.25 = 1, so 8 + 1 = 9).

The sign rules for integers work for every fraction and decimal: same signs give a positive, different signs a negative (for × and ÷). Work out the sign first, then the size: $-\tfrac{3}{4} \times \tfrac{2}{5} = -\tfrac{6}{20} = -\tfrac{3}{10}$.

Make the size easy: multiply in any order ($25 \times (-3.7) \times 4 = 100 \times (-3.7) = -370$), or split a number ($6 \times (-2.5) = 6 \times (-2) + 6 \times (-0.5) = -15$).

Place value and estimating

17. Place value and powers of 10

Try firstWrite 7 304 in expanded notation using powers of 10.

Check your answer

7 × 10³ + 3 × 10² + 4 × 10⁰. (The 0 in the tens column is left out.)

Every place value column is ten times the one on its right, so each is a power of 10: ones $10^0 = 1$, tens $10^1$, hundreds $10^2$, thousands $10^3$. The exponent counts the zeros in the value, and it counts from 0 at the ones column.

A number in expanded notation is the sum of each digit times its power of 10: $4052 = 4 \times 10^3 + 5 \times 10^1 + 2 \times 10^0$. A column holding 0 adds nothing, so it is left out.

18. Rounding decimals

Try firstA stick is measured as 83.46 cm with a ruler marked in millimetres. Round it sensibly, and say why.

Check your answer

83.5 cm: the ruler shows millimetres, which is 1 decimal place of a centimetre.

To round to a number of decimal places, find the two numbers with that many places that your number sits between, and take the nearer. Look at the one digit straight after the place: 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less leaves the digit as it is. Halfway rounds up. So 3.746 to 2 places is 3.75.

How much to round depends on the job: money to the cent, a ruler measurement to what the ruler can show, tins of paint up so there is enough. More digits than the instrument measures only gives a false sense of accuracy.

19. Estimating to check

Try firstEstimate 4.8 × 31. The calculator shows 148.8. Is it reasonable?

Check your answer

5 × 30 = 150, so yes.

An estimate is a quick, close answer from rounding. Round each number to its first digit and keep its size: $38 \times 52 \approx 40 \times 50 = 2000$. If the exact answer is near the estimate it is reasonable; if it is out by a factor of ten, check the decimal point.

Rounding can go up or down, so an estimate can be above or below the exact answer. What matters is that it is close and quick.

Ratios

20. What is a ratio?

Try firstA jar has 4 black and 5 white beads. Write the ratio of black to white, and of black to all the beads.

Check your answer

4 : 5 and 4 : 9.

A ratio compares quantities in a stated order. With 2 red and 3 blue counters, red to blue is $2 : 3$ (part to part). The whole is $2 + 3 = 5$ counters, so red to all is $2 : 5$ (part to whole), which is the fraction $\tfrac{2}{5}$.

The order matters: $2 : 3$ is not $3 : 2$. The first thing named is the first number.

21. Equivalent and simplest ratios

Try firstSimplify 24 : 36, and find the missing term: 3 : 5 = 12 : ?

Check your answer

24 : 36 = 2 : 3. 3 → 12 is × 4, so 5 × 4 = 20.

Two ratios are equivalent when both terms of one are the other's both terms multiplied (or divided) by the same number: $2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9$. Picture it as copies of the same pattern of blocks.

To simplify, divide both terms by their highest common factor: $12 : 18 = 2 : 3$ (÷ 6). Adding the same number to both terms changes the ratio.

22. Sharing in a ratio

Try firstShare $84 between Sam and Tia in the ratio 3 : 4. How much does Tia get?

Check your answer

3 + 4 = 7 parts. 84 ÷ 7 = 12. Tia has 4 parts: $48.

To share a total in a ratio: add the parts ($2 + 3 = 5$), find one part (total ÷ parts: $$60 \div 5 = $12$), and multiply for each share ($2 \times 12 = 24$, $3 \times 12 = 36$). The shares add back to the total.

To work backwards, use a known share to find one part: if 3 parts are $24, one part is $8.

Rates

27. Rates

Try firstA cyclist rides 45 km in 1.5 hours. Find her speed in km/h and m/s.

Check your answer

45 ÷ 1.5 = 30 km/h; 30 ÷ 3.6 ≈ 8.3 m/s.

A rate compares two quantities with different units (km per hour, $ per kg); a ratio compares the same kind of quantity.

The unit rate is the amount for one of the second quantity: divide the first by the second, and keep both units. To convert km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6.

28. Using rates

Try firstA plane flies 2100 km at 700 km/h. How long does it take?

Check your answer

2100 ÷ 700 = 3 hours.

Use a rate both ways: total = rate × amount and amount = total ÷ rate. For speed, distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed.

Write the units at each step (km ÷ km/h = h); they tell you whether to multiply or divide. Then check the size makes sense.

Modelling

23. Modelling with money

Try firstA $80 jacket is marked up 25% then sold at 25% off the new price. Is the final price more than, less than or equal to $80? Work it out.

Check your answer

80 × 1.25 = 100, then 100 × 0.75 = 75: less than $80.

Modelling with money: formulate (known, unknown, which representation: fraction, decimal, percentage or ratio), solve (estimate, then calculate efficiently), interpret and justify (the answer in dollars, rounded to the cent, and why that representation).

A percentage is always of something. Profit is a percentage of the cost. Percentages of different amounts do not add: 20% off $200 then 10% off the new price is $200 × 0.8 × 0.9 = $144, not 30% off.

24. Modelling with ratios

Try firstA recipe mixes juice and water 2 : 7. How much juice is in a 900 mL drink?

Check your answer

2 + 7 = 9 parts. 900 ÷ 9 = 100 mL a part. Juice is 200 mL.

Modelling with ratios: formulate (what is known, what is unknown, what relationship?), solve, then interpret (the answer in the situation's units, and does it make sense?).

25. Percentage change

Try firstA $60 item is discounted 25%, then the discounted price goes up 25%. What is the final price?

Check your answer

60 × 0.75 × 1.25 = $56.25.

An increase of $p%$ multiplies by $1 + \frac{p}{100}$; a decrease multiplies by $1 - \frac{p}{100}$.

For one change after another, multiply the multipliers: each percentage is of the current amount. Up 20% then down 20% is $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$: 4% less.

26. Money models

Try firstTax is 0% on the first $18 200 of income, 15% on the next part up to $45 000, and 30% on the part from $45 000 to $135 000. Find the tax on $50 000.

Check your answer

15% of (45 000 − 18 200) = 15% of 26 800 = $4020, plus 30% of 5000 = $1500: $5520 in all.

To model a money problem: decide what to find, choose the calculation, work it out, and check it makes sense.

Income tax is worked out in brackets: each part of the income is taxed at its own rate. The marginal rate is the rate on the last dollar; the effective rate is total tax ÷ income, which is lower (for incomes above $18 200).
