## Decided (18 Oct 2026, Matthew)
- **Two parallel courses**, both after AI Foundations, neither a prerequisite for the other or for Does AI Help Us Learn?:
  - **AI in your life** (working title): feeds and attention, what you give away (AI and your data only), is it real?, fakes that hurt (deepfake bullying, voice-clone scams), talking to AI (companions).
  - **Who Does AI Affect?**: the lens with benefits, who decides (automated decisions), when AI acts (agents), jobs, energy and water, who labels the data, whose work trained it (copyright), making rules (policy, wider steps).
  - The lesson lists in brief Part 4 › Proposed structure are a starting point, not fixed.
- **Topic 1 narrowed** to AI and your data (training on chats, a consent screen, the Children's Online Privacy Code); link to Cyber safety for the digital footprint and the APPs.
- **Sensitive lessons** (companions, deepfake bullying): publish, but flag each for review with the school's wellbeing lead (say so in the lesson's teacher notes and in the plan). Teacher notes only for lawsuits and individual tragedies; never ask students about their own use.
- **Kinds of AI** (rules, predictive ML, generative, agents) are named in AI Foundations (a separate refinement, not this task); each new course opens with a short "which kind is it?" recap that links back.
- **Existing resources:** write our own lessons. Credit an idea's source (for example "idea from Day of AI") and link their lessons in teacher notes as optional extras; don't copy their text or materials. Free tools they use (for example Google's Quick, Draw!) may be used after checking each tool's terms and what it collects. The Digital Technologies Hub (CC BY 4.0) may be copied with attribution.
- **Attacks on AI** (malicious models, voice-clone scams, prompt injection) go to Cyber safety as new examples: propose them in the plan as a separate change; the new course keeps only the scam-on-families case.

## Matthew's preferences
Plain words for Year 7; core ideas at Year 7 with `tier: typical | wider` steps for older students; cases, not lectures; platform-agnostic; no phones in class; students own their working, light checks; not too much AI encouragement; every activity works offline with paper and talk; a solo, self-paced version of every lesson.
